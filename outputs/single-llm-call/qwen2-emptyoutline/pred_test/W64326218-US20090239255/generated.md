# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and medical diagnostics, specifically to the identification and characterization of Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) and its use as a biomarker for various epithelial malignancies. The invention encompasses methods for detecting the presence of ODAM in biological samples, as well as the use of ODAM-specific antibodies for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) is a protein encoded by a gene located within the secretory calcium-binding phosphoprotein (SCPP) cluster on chromosome 4q13. This cluster is known to specify elements involved in bone and tooth development, as well as mineralization. Studies have shown that ODAM is highly expressed by mature ameloblasts and is present in the enamel organ and junctional epithelial cells. Additionally, ODAM expression has been found to be up-regulated in various human cancers, including cervical and gastric cancer.

The initial discovery of ODAM's role in odontogenesis and its presence in calcifying epithelial odontogenic tumors (CEOT) provided the first evidence of its expression. Chemical analyses of amyloid fibrils isolated from CEOT specimens revealed that these components were composed of N-terminal fragments of a 153-residue hypothetical protein specified by the FLJ20513 gene, which is a short form of ODAM cDNA cloned from the Kato III human signet-ring gastric carcinoma cell line. Subsequent studies identified varying amounts of a second ODAM-related protein with residues originating from the fourth exon, providing conclusive evidence for the transcription of a longer ODAM product.

Further investigations using anti-ODAM specific polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies have confirmed the presence of ODAM in ameloblasts and certain normal and neoplastic human epithelial tissues. These findings suggest that ODAM may serve as a novel cancer biomarker and provide a therapeutic target for patients with certain epithelial malignancies.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for detecting and characterizing Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) in biological samples. The invention encompasses the use of ODAM-specific antibodies for diagnosing and monitoring various epithelial malignancies, as well as for therapeutic purposes.

### EXAMPLE 1

#### Detection of ODAM in Biological Samples Using RT-PCR

To detect the presence of ODAM in biological samples, RNA was extracted from Kato III cells using guanidine isothiocyanate. cDNAs were prepared by a two-step RT-PCR procedure using Superscript II reverse transcriptase and Taq polymerase. For ODAM synthesis, both coding exon 2 sense and exon 10 antisense primers were used, yielding a product encoding amino acid residues 25-279. To obtain a cDNA specifying the shorter (153-residue) FLJ20513 product, exons 5 sense and 10 antisense primers were utilized. The reactions were run on an MJ Research model PTC-200 PCR apparatus for up to 40 cycles of amplification, each consisting of 1 minute at 94°C, 62°C, and 72°C, followed by a single 15-minute incubation cycle at 72°C prior to agarose gel analysis.

### EXAMPLE 2

#### Synthesis of Recombinant ODAM-Related Polypeptides

To provide the requisite material for immunochemical studies, recombinant products representing the coding sequences of either the Kato III-derived FLJ20513 or exons 2-10 of full-length ODAM were synthesized. Analysis of the two 35S-labeled in vitro translated products by SDS/PAGE indicated molecular weights of approximately 21,000 and 38,000, respectively. These masses were higher than those predicted from their primary structure and may have resulted from protein misfolding or other factors that impeded gel migration. Mass spectrometric analyses revealed molecular weights consistent with the expected values, and the long ODAM product was comparable to that reported for the rat homolog. Immunoprecipitation and Western blot studies indicated that the products contained the C-terminal FLAG epitope. Shorter, immunoreactive proteins corresponding in size to that encoded by exons 5-10 were consistently observed in both in vivo and in vitro products obtained from the larger rODAM construct.

### EXAMPLE 3

#### Generation of Anti-ODAM Antibodies

As part of an effort to develop antibodies that would recognize ODAM-related molecules, synthetic peptides and protein were used as immunogens. A polyclonal antiserum was obtained from a rabbit immunized with a 12-mer peptide (residues 67 to 78). In addition, two IgG monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were generated: one, designated 5A-1, resulted from immunizing mice with a peptide encompassing residues 52 to 90 in coding exon 4, and the other, 8B-4, was produced against the 153-amino acid FLJ20513 form of rODAM. Immunoprecipitation and Western blot studies indicated that mAb 8B-4 immunoprecipitated both exons 2-10 and 5-10 rODAM runoff translation products. The 5A-1 mAb did not react with the shorter rODAM molecule corresponding to FLJ20513. To localize the region of ODAM recognized by mAb 8B-4, proteolytically-derived rODAM fragments were tested by Western blotting. The reagent reacted only with the 178-279 component, indicating that the epitope was located between ODAM residues 178 and 216.

### EXAMPLE 4

#### ODAM Expression by Normal and Malignant Dental Tissue

The monoclonal anti-ODAM antibody 5A-1 immunostained CEOT and ameloblastoma cells, as well as dental epithelial cells present in unerupted human tooth follicles and ameloblasts in ten-day-old mouse teeth. Similar results were obtained with the polyclonal anti-ODAM antiserum. These findings indicate that ODAM has an essential role in normal and tumor-related odontogenesis.

### EXAMPLE 5

#### ODAM Expression by Non-Dental Normal and Malignant Tissue

Hybridization of a 70-member human tissue array using a 32P-labeled cDNA probe that encodes the FLJ20513 form of ODAM revealed a strong signal with tracheal and salivary gland-derived RNA, but only a weak reaction with stomach and fetal lung. ODAM expression in the two major sites was evidenced by the demonstration that the epithelial cells were immunostained by mAb 8B-4, as well as mAb 5A-1 and the polyclonal anti-ODAM antiserum. Immunohistochemical analyses of 35 other normal tissues revealed comparable staining of the bronchus and, to a lesser extent, ovary, bladder, ureter, endometrium, and skin. Given the detection of ODAM in the human gastric carcinoma Kato III cell line, human tumor tissue-specific arrays were analyzed, and moderate to intense immunostaining of the nucleus and/or cytoplasm with mAb 8B-4 was found in 46 and 48 of the 60 breast and gastric samples, respectively, and in three of six lung specimens contained in a multi-specific array. This reactivity was confined principally to adenocarcinomas and could be inhibited by the presence of the immunogen. Comparable results were obtained using mAb 5A-1, albeit with varying degrees of intensity, as well as with 8B-4 and the anti-Gli-1 antibody. The presence of ODAM within neoplastic breast cells was also shown by in situ hybridization using a biotin-labeled oligonucleotide probe specific for exons 2-10-encoded ODAM proteins. This reactivity could be diminished by competition with the corresponding unlabeled specific ODAM oligonucleotide, but not with a non-corresponding ODAM antisense oligonucleotide. In contrast, the probe did not appear to hybridize with normal breast tissue.
