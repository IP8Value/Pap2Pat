# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for testing a sample derived from the blood of a test subject to determine the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). More specifically, the invention provides a method for detecting specific microRNAs (miRNAs) in the plasma of a test subject, which can serve as diagnostic biomarkers for RA. The invention also encompasses a diagnostic reagent for determining the pathology of RA based on the detection of these miRNAs.

## BACKGROUND ART

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a systemic, chronic inflammatory disease characterized by joint destruction, deformity, and disability. The disease is heterogeneous, and untreated patients often experience progressive symptoms leading to significant disability. Early and aggressive treatment has been shown to yield better clinical outcomes compared to delayed therapy. The American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and the European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) developed new classification criteria for RA in 2010 to facilitate early diagnosis and treatment. However, these criteria can sometimes lead to false diagnoses in patients with self-limiting diseases, particularly when anti-citrullinated antibodies (ACPA) and rheumatoid factor (RF) are negative.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNA molecules that play a crucial role in post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression. They are involved in various cellular processes, including lipid metabolism, apoptosis, differentiation, organ development, and the progression of malignant tumors. Recent studies have shown that patients with RA exhibit alterations in their cellular miRNAs, particularly in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, T lymphocytes, synovial fibroblasts, and osteoclasts. These alterations contribute to inflammation, degradation of the extracellular matrix, and invasive behavior of resident cells.

Circulating miRNAs in plasma and other biological fluids have been shown to be stable and resistant to endogenous RNase activity. They have been explored as potential biomarkers for various diseases, including cancers and tissue injuries. Previous research has identified miR-132 as a potential biomarker for RA, but it does not differentiate RA from osteoarthritis (OA). Therefore, there is a need for novel and specific miRNA biomarkers that can accurately diagnose RA and differentiate it from other conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of specific and reliable biomarkers for the early and accurate diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Current diagnostic methods, such as the 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria, can sometimes lead to false diagnoses, particularly in patients with self-limiting diseases. Additionally, existing biomarkers like ACPA and RF are not always present in RA patients, making it challenging to diagnose the disease in its early stages. The invention aims to provide a method and diagnostic reagent that can detect specific miRNAs in plasma, which can serve as robust biomarkers for RA, thereby improving the accuracy and timeliness of diagnosis.

### Means of Solving the Problems

The present invention provides a method for testing a sample derived from the blood of a test subject to determine the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). The method involves detecting the presence and/or concentration of specific microRNAs (miRNAs) in the plasma of the test subject. The specific miRNAs of interest include miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and a combination of miR-24, miR-30a-5p, and miR-125a-5p (referred to as ePRAM for "estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs"). These miRNAs have been identified as being significantly elevated in the plasma of RA patients compared to healthy controls (HCs) and patients with other conditions such as osteoarthritis (OA) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE).

The method comprises the following steps:
1. Collecting a blood sample from the test subject.
2. Isolating plasma from the blood sample.
3. Extracting total RNA from the plasma.
4. Detecting the presence and/or concentration of the specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) or another suitable method.
5. Comparing the detected levels of the specific miRNAs to predetermined threshold values to determine the presence or absence of RA.

The invention also provides a diagnostic reagent for determining the pathology of RA, comprising primers and probes for detecting the specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) in a plasma sample.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention offers a significant improvement in the early and accurate diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). By detecting specific miRNAs in plasma, the method and diagnostic reagent provided by the invention can distinguish RA from other conditions with high sensitivity and specificity. This allows for timely and appropriate treatment, which can significantly improve clinical outcomes and reduce the risk of disability. The invention also addresses the limitations of existing diagnostic methods, such as the 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria, by providing a more reliable and specific biomarker for RA.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Test Method of a Sample Derived From the Blood of a Test Subject for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The present invention provides a method for testing a sample derived from the blood of a test subject to determine the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). The method involves the following steps:

1. **Collection of Blood Sample**: A blood sample is collected from the test subject using an EDTA-2K containing tube to prevent coagulation. The sample is then processed to separate the plasma.

2. **Isolation of Plasma**: The blood sample is centrifuged at 1400 g for 7 minutes to separate the plasma. The plasma is then stored at -80°C until further analysis.

3. **Extraction of Total RNA**: Total RNA is extracted from the plasma using a modified version of the EXIQON protocol. The plasma is mixed with TriPure Isolation Reagent, and the mixture is processed to isolate the RNA. Synthetic C. elegans miRNA (cel-miR-39) is added to the sample to normalize possible sample-to-sample variation.

4. **Detection of Specific miRNAs**: The presence and/or concentration of specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) are detected using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). The qRT-PCR is performed using primers and probes specific to the target miRNAs. The data are normalized using the synthetic cel-miR-39 and analyzed to determine the levels of the specific miRNAs.

5. **Comparison to Threshold Values**: The detected levels of the specific miRNAs are compared to predetermined threshold values. If the levels of the specific miRNAs exceed the threshold values, the test subject is determined to have rheumatoid arthritis (RA).

### 2. Diagnostic Reagent for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The present invention also provides a diagnostic reagent for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis (RA). The diagnostic reagent comprises primers and probes for detecting the specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) in a plasma sample. The reagent is designed to be used in conjunction with the method described above to detect the presence and/or concentration of the specific miRNAs.

The diagnostic reagent includes the following components:
- Primers and probes for detecting miR-24.
- Primers and probes for detecting miR-125a-5p.
- Primers and probes for detecting miR-30a-5p (for the calculation of ePRAM).
- Synthetic C. elegans miRNA (cel-miR-39) for normalization.
- Reagents for RNA extraction and qRT-PCR.

## EXAMPLES

### Method

#### Collection and Processing of Blood Samples
Blood samples were collected from 102 patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and 104 healthy controls (HCs). The samples were collected using EDTA-2K containing tubes and processed to isolate plasma. The plasma was stored at -80°C until further analysis.

#### RNA Extraction
Total RNA was extracted from the plasma using a modified version of the EXIQON protocol. The plasma was mixed with TriPure Isolation Reagent, and the mixture was processed to isolate the RNA. Synthetic C. elegans miRNA (cel-miR-39) was added to the sample to normalize possible sample-to-sample variation.

#### Detection of Specific miRNAs
The presence and/or concentration of specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) were detected using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR). The qRT-PCR was performed using primers and probes specific to the target miRNAs. The data were normalized using the synthetic cel-miR-39 and analyzed to determine the levels of the specific miRNAs.

### Results

#### Detection of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p
The levels of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p were significantly higher in the plasma of RA patients compared to HCs. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) for miR-24 was 0.81, with a sensitivity of 63.7% and a specificity of 89.5%. The AUC for miR-125a-5p was 0.89, with a sensitivity of 64.7% and a specificity of 89.5%.

#### Calculation of ePRAM
The ePRAM score was calculated using the combination of miR-24, miR-30a-5p, and miR-125a-5p. The ePRAM score showed an AUC of 0.89, with a sensitivity of 78.4% and a specificity of 92.3%.

#### Differentiation of RA from Other Conditions
The levels of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM were significantly lower in patients with osteoarthritis (OA) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) compared to RA patients. The percentage of true negatives for miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and the ePRAM test in patients with OA was 79%, 79%, and 75%, respectively. In patients with SLE, 91%, 64%, and 64% were diagnosed negative for RA.

#### Correlation with Clinical Variables
The levels of miR-24 and ePRAM correlated with disease activity parameters such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28). However, miR-125a-5p did not correlate with any evaluated clinical parameters, suggesting that it is not influenced by general inflammation.

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE

A comparative study was conducted to evaluate the performance of the specific miRNAs (miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and ePRAM) against traditional biomarkers such as ACPA and RF. The results showed that the specific miRNAs provided higher sensitivity and specificity for the diagnosis of RA, particularly in ACPA-negative patients. The sensitivity of detection for ACPA-negative RA patients was 73% for the ePRAM test, compared to 47% for miR-24 and 67% for miR-125a-5p.
