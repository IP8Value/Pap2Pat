# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy, specifically to the discovery and development of novel, fully human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) targeting the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2). The invention provides methods for identifying and characterizing these antibodies, as well as their use in the treatment of cancers overexpressing ROR2.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, and despite recent advancements in targeted therapies, including antibody- and cell-based treatments, broadly effective and curative options remain limited. Classical chemotherapy, which relies on cytotoxic or cytostatic small molecules, continues to be the standard of care in many cancer treatments. However, these treatments often result in dose-limiting toxicities and limited selectivity against cancer cells, leading to only partial clinical efficacy.

To enhance the anti-tumor efficacy and reduce toxicity, targeted delivery of cytotoxic agents to tumors has been a long-standing goal. Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have emerged as a promising tool for this purpose due to their ability to specifically bind to tumor-associated antigens (TAAs). Naked antibodies can interfere with cancer cell growth or recruit immune system components to inhibit tumor growth, but these activities are often insufficient for complete eradication of targeted tumor cells. Therefore, the development of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) has gained significant attention.

ADCs consist of an antibody conjugated via a covalent linker to a potent cytotoxic payload, combining the high selectivity of the antibody moiety to a TAA with the otherwise intolerably high cytotoxic potential of the payload. However, the development of safe and effective ADCs is challenging due to the structural complexity of these molecules and the high functional requirements necessary to achieve a favorable therapeutic index. Only a few ADCs have been approved for clinical use, while many others are in various stages of clinical trials.

ROR2, a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor (ROR) family, is overexpressed in several human cancers, including renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, and breast, colorectal, oral, and pancreatic cancers. ROR2 plays a role in mediating pro-tumorigenic activity through noncanonical Wnt signaling pathways. Given its tumor-selective expression and involvement in cancer progression, ROR2 is a promising target for the development of ADCs.

The present invention addresses the need for novel, fully human mAbs and ADCs targeting ROR2, which can be used to develop more effective and safer cancer therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel, fully human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) targeting the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2). The invention encompasses methods for identifying and characterizing these antibodies, as well as their use in the treatment of cancers overexpressing ROR2.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for identifying novel, fully human mAbs targeting ROR2, comprising:
1. Immunizing transgenic mice expressing human variable heavy (VH) and light (VL) chain gene segments with a recombinant human ROR2 extracellular domain (hROR2-ECD).
2. Enriching hROR2-specific B lymphocytes from the spleens of immunized mice.
3. Amplifying and cloning the coding regions for human VH and VL variable domains from the enriched B lymphocytes into transposable expression vectors.
4. Generating cellular libraries by stably transposing the expression vectors into immortalized murine pro-B cells.
5. Screening the cellular libraries for surface IgG expression and binding to hROR2-ECD.
6. Single-cell sorting of hROR2-binding cellular clones.
7. Functionally screening the clonal supernatants for binding to hROR2-ECD and in vitro cell killing activity using a secondary ADC assay.

In another aspect, the invention provides novel, fully human mAbs targeting ROR2, characterized by their high affinity, cross-reactivity to cynomolgus monkey and mouse ROR2, and potent in vitro cell killing activity as ADCs.

In a further aspect, the invention provides ADCs comprising the novel, fully human mAbs conjugated to a cytotoxic payload, such as a derivative of the strong cellular toxin PNU-159682, via a site-specific conjugation method.

The invention also encompasses methods for using the novel mAbs and ADCs in the treatment of cancers overexpressing ROR2, including renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, and breast, colorectal, oral, and pancreatic cancers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

- **Monoclonal Antibody (mAb):** A type of antibody that is made by identical immune cells that are all clones of a unique parent cell.
- **Antibody-Drug Conjugate (ADC):** A biopharmaceutical that combines a monoclonal antibody with a cytotoxic payload via a linker.
- **Receptor Tyrosine Kinase-like Orphan Receptor 2 (ROR2):** A transmembrane protein involved in cell signaling pathways, particularly in cancer progression.
- **Transpo-mAb Display:** A mammalian cell-based platform for the discovery and functional screening of antibodies.
- **Human Ig Transgenic Mice (H2L2):** Mice engineered to express human variable heavy (VH) and light (VL) chain gene segments.
- **Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR):** A technique used to measure the binding affinity and kinetics of interactions between biomolecules.
- **Sortase-Mediated Antibody Conjugation (SMAC):** A method for site-specific conjugation of antibodies to cytotoxic payloads.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

#### Immunization of H2L2 Ig Transgenic Mice

The invention begins with the immunization of human Ig transgenic mice (H2L2) with a recombinant human ROR2 extracellular domain (hROR2-ECD) containing a C-terminal Twin-Strep-tag. The immunization strategy involves a primary immunization followed by two boost immunizations. The Twin-Strep-tag facilitates the purification of the recombinant hROR2-ECD protein and is used for the enrichment of hROR2-specific B lymphocytes.

#### Enrichment of hROR2-Specific B Lymphocytes

Splenocytes from immunized H2L2 mice are enriched for hROR2-specific B lymphocytes using magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS). The enriched B lymphocytes are then used for the amplification and cloning of the coding regions for human VH and VL variable domains.

#### Cloning and Library Construction

The coding regions for human VH and VL variable domains are amplified by RT-PCR using specific primers and cloned into transposable expression vectors. The VH amplicons are cloned into vectors encoding human IgG1 heavy chain constant domains, while the VL amplicons are cloned into vectors encoding human Igκ light chain constant domains. This results in libraries of expression vectors for fully human IgG1 heavy chains and Igκ light chains.

#### Generation of Cellular Libraries

The HC and LC expression libraries are co-electroporated into immortalized murine pro-B cells (L11) to generate cellular libraries. The L11 cells are capable of efficiently expressing IgG1/κL antibodies as both membrane-bound and secreted forms. The cellular libraries are then screened for surface IgG expression and binding to hROR2-ECD using flow cytometry (FACS).

#### Functional Screening of Clonal Supernatants

Clonal supernatants from single-cell sorted Transpo-mAb clones are functionally screened for binding to hROR2-ECD and in vitro cell killing activity using a secondary ADC assay. The secondary ADC assay involves incubating clonal supernatants with hROR2-overexpressing cells followed by the addition of an anti-human Fcγ IgG coupled to a cytotoxic payload. The cell viability is then assessed using a luminescence-based assay.

#### Recovery and Characterization of Novel mAbs

The VH and VL coding regions from selected hROR2-reactive clones are recovered by RT-PCR and cloned into an EBNA-based expression vector. The recombinantly expressed mAbs are characterized for their binding affinity to hROR2-ECD using Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) and for their cross-reactivity to cynomolgus monkey and mouse ROR2 using ELISA.

#### Generation and Evaluation of ADCs

The novel, fully human mAbs are conjugated to a cytotoxic payload, such as a derivative of PNU-159682, using sortase-mediated antibody conjugation (SMAC). The cell killing activity of the resulting ADCs is evaluated on hROR2-overexpressing and hROR2-negative cell lines using a luminescence-based cell viability assay.

#### Therapeutic Applications

The novel, fully human mAbs and ADCs targeting ROR2 have the potential to be used in the treatment of cancers overexpressing ROR2, including renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, and breast, colorectal, oral, and pancreatic cancers. The high affinity, cross-reactivity, and potent in vitro cell killing activity of these mAbs and ADCs make them promising candidates for the development of effective and safer cancer therapies.
