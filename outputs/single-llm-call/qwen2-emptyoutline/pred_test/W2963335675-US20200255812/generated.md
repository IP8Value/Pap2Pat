# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and enzymology, specifically to the structure and function of the propeptide of the lipase from the fungus Rhizomucor miehei (RmL). The invention provides novel insights into the mechanism by which the propeptide inhibits the activity of the mature lipase, thereby ensuring proper maturation and activation of the enzyme during its expression pathway.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Lipases are enzymes that catalyze the hydrolysis of ester bonds in triglycerides, and they belong to the family of triglyceride lipases (EC 3.1.1.3). Class 3 lipases, which include the lipase from Rhizomucor miehei (RmL), are members of the α/β hydrolase superfamily and are characterized by a catalytic triad composed of Ser, His, and Asp. These enzymes exist in both closed and open conformations, with the closed form preventing substrate access to the active site and the open form allowing substrate access.

The propeptide of RmL plays a crucial role in the proper folding and activation of the mature enzyme. It is initially co-translated with the mature enzyme and is cleaved off during the maturation process. The propeptide has been shown to inhibit the activity of the mature lipase, ensuring that the enzyme remains inactive until it is properly folded and secreted. Despite the importance of the propeptide, its structure and the mechanism of inhibition have not been fully elucidated.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides the first detailed structure of the propeptide of RmL and its interaction with the mature enzyme. The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Structural Determination**: The invention discloses the three-dimensional structure of the propeptide of RmL, both in its wild-type form and in a deletion variant (ProRmL-del), which lacks two residues at the beginning of the mature enzyme domain. The structure reveals how the propeptide wraps around the mature enzyme, covering the active site and preventing its opening.

2. **Inhibitory Mechanism**: The invention demonstrates that the propeptide inhibits the activity of the mature lipase by burying the active site and interacting with the lid region of the enzyme. This interaction maintains the enzyme in a closed, inactive conformation.

3. **Propeptide-Mature Enzyme Complex**: The invention provides a detailed description of the interactions between the propeptide and the mature enzyme, including hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions. These interactions are critical for the stability of the propeptide-mature enzyme complex and the inhibition of lipase activity.

4. **Industrial Applications**: The invention has significant implications for the industrial production of lipases. By understanding the structure and function of the propeptide, it is possible to design strategies to improve the stability and activity of lipases in various applications, such as detergents, food processing, and biofuel production.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials

The materials used in the examples include the following:

- **Plasmids**: Plasmids containing the gene for the wild-type ProRmL and the ProRmL-del variant.
- **Host Cells**: Aspergillus oryzae for expression of the propeptide and the propeptide-mature enzyme complex.
- **Reagents**: Buffers, salts, and other reagents for protein purification and characterization.
- **Instruments**: HPLC, mass spectrometry, and X-ray crystallography equipment for structural analysis.

### Example 1: Variant Generation, Transformation and Expression

#### Variant Generation

The ProRmL-del variant was generated using PCR-based site-directed mutagenesis. The forward primer was designed to introduce the desired deletion, and the PCR product was treated with DpnI to digest the methylated parental DNA. The resulting plasmid was transformed into E. coli, and the presence of the desired mutation was confirmed by sequencing.

#### Transformation and Expression

The plasmid containing the ProRmL-del variant was transformed into Aspergillus oryzae ToC1512 by the protoplast method. Transformants were screened for protein expression on a small scale by inoculating spores in 2 mL of expression media in 96-well culture plates and grown for 4 days at 34°C. Protein expression was confirmed by SDS-PAGE. Large-scale fermentation was carried out in 1 L baffled shake flasks for 4 days at 34°C and 180 rpm.

#### Purification

The culture supernatant was clarified by vacuum filtration and sterile filtration. The sample was then subjected to hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) using a decylamine column. The inactive lipase (ProRmL-del) was collected from the flow-through fractions and further purified using ion exchange chromatography (IEX). The purified ProRmL-del was concentrated and used for further experiments.

### Purification of RmL WT Controls: RmL WT and RmL WT Pur

The mature RmL without the propeptide (RmL WT) was purified from the decylamine column by eluting with a step gradient of 100% buffer B (Milli-Q water). The purified RmL WT was characterized by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry. The RmL WT Pur was further purified using gel filtration chromatography to remove any remaining impurities.

### Propeptide Variant Generation

The propeptide was separated from the mature enzyme using a two-step purification process. The starting material was diluted in ethanol and formic acid and applied to a C8-silica resin. The propeptide was eluted with a linear gradient of ethanol and formic acid. The propeptide-containing fractions were further purified using a C5-silica resin and characterized by mass spectrometry.

### Example 2: Native PAGE

Native PAGE was performed to demonstrate the loss of activity in the propeptide-containing sample. The resolving gel was composed of 12% acrylamide-bis-acrylamide, and the stacking gel was composed of 5.1% acrylamide-bis-acrylamide. The gel was stained with brilliant blue, and a zymogram was run to visualize the activity of the lipase.

### Example 3: Temperature Stability Assay (TSA)

The temperature stability of the propeptide-mature enzyme complex was assessed by incubating the samples at different temperatures and measuring the residual activity. The samples were incubated at 40°C, 50°C, and 60°C for 1 hour, and the activity was measured using the GLAD assay.

### Example 4: Zymogram Analysis

Zymogram analysis was performed to visualize the activity of the lipase in the presence and absence of the propeptide. The native PAGE gel was placed on an agarose/brilliant blue plate containing olive oil and brilliant green. The gel was incubated at room temperature, and the activity was visualized by the appearance of clear zones.

### Example 5: Decylamine Agarose Chromatography

Decylamine agarose chromatography was used to separate the active lipase (mature RmL without propeptide) from the inactive lipase (ProRmL including the propeptide). The culture supernatant was applied to the decylamine column, and the inactive lipase was collected from the flow-through fractions. The active lipase was eluted from the column and characterized by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry.

### Example 6: Relative Wash Performance

The relative wash performance of the lipase in the presence and absence of the propeptide was evaluated using a detergent model. The lipase was incubated with the propeptide at a molar ratio of 1:64, and the activity was measured using the GLAD assay. The results showed a significant reduction in activity in the presence of the propeptide, confirming its inhibitory effect.
