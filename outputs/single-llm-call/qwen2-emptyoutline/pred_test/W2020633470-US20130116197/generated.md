# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and, more specifically, to novel therapeutic agents for the treatment of ocular diseases characterized by abnormal angiogenesis and vascular leakage. The invention provides designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) that specifically bind to vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) and inhibit its biological activity. These DARPins exhibit high affinity and potency, making them suitable for both intravitreal and topical administration.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) plays a critical role in the regulation of angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis. Among the VEGF family, VEGF-A is a major regulator of angiogenesis, primarily signaling through VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), a type III receptor tyrosine kinase. Blocking VEGF-A, particularly its predominant splice variants VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, has been shown to nearly completely inhibit blood vessel growth. This inhibition is therapeutically beneficial in conditions such as tumor growth and ophthalmic disorders, where uncontrolled blood vessel growth and vascular leakage lead to significant morbidity.

Currently, several anti-VEGF drugs are approved for clinical use, including ranibizumab and pegaptanib, which are used to treat wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD). However, these treatments often require frequent intravitreal injections, which can increase the risk of complications such as endophthalmitis and retinal tears. Additionally, the need for frequent injections represents a significant burden for patients and incurs substantial healthcare costs.

There is a pressing need for new VEGF inhibitors that can be delivered more safely and effectively. Novel therapeutic agents should ideally be highly potent, stable, and amenable to both intravitreal and topical administration. Designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) are a promising class of binding proteins that meet these criteria. DARPins are derived from naturally occurring ankyrin repeat proteins and can be engineered to bind specific target molecules with high affinity and specificity. They exhibit excellent thermal and thermodynamic stability, high solubility, and robustness, making them suitable for high-concentration formulations and various application routes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel DARPins that specifically bind to VEGF-A and inhibit its biological activity. These DARPins are characterized by their high affinity, potency, and stability, making them suitable for both intravitreal and topical administration. The DARPins of the invention are useful for treating ocular diseases characterized by abnormal angiogenesis and vascular leakage, such as wet AMD and diabetic macular edema.

In one aspect, the invention provides a DARPin that specifically binds to VEGF-A and inhibits its biological activity. The DARPin comprises a sequence of ankyrin repeats and exhibits a binding affinity for VEGF-A with a dissociation constant (KD) of less than 25 pM.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a DARPin of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition is suitable for intravitreal or topical administration.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating an ocular disease characterized by abnormal angiogenesis and vascular leakage, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a DARPin of the invention.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a DARPin of the invention.

In still another aspect, the invention provides a method for preventing or reducing corneal neovascularization in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a DARPin of the invention.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Rapid Generation of a Panel of Anti-VEGF-A DARPins

The DARPins of the invention were generated using ribosome display, a technique that allows for the rapid enrichment of target-binding proteins from naïve libraries. Three standard selection rounds were followed by three consecutive off-rate selection rounds and a final collection round. The resulting DNA pools were screened for VEGF-A binders using a crude extract ELISA. Binders with strong ELISA signals were further characterized.

The identified DARPins were expressed in E. coli and purified from the soluble fraction. Expression levels were comparable to those of previously published DARPins, yielding approximately 200 mg of expressed protein per liter of shake-flask culture. The purified DARPins were characterized using various ELISAs and cellular assays. All DARPins tested exhibited strong binding to VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, with no binding to VEGF-C. The affinity of the DARPins was confirmed using a Quantikine sandwich ELISA, which showed that all DARPins tested induced strong signal suppression of more than 50%, even at a VEGF-A concentration of 25 pM. The IC50 value for one of the DARPins, DARPin #4, was determined to be 10 pM. Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) further confirmed the low picomolar affinity of the DARPins.

### Functional In Vitro Assays Confirming Highly Potent VEGF-A Antagonism

The potency of the DARPins was confirmed using in vitro assays. In a VEGF-R2 phosphorylation inhibition assay, the DARPins were able to suppress VEGF-A-induced phosphorylation of VEGFR-2, demonstrating their ability to inhibit VEGF-A signaling. In a human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) spheroid sprouting assay, the DARPins potently suppressed endothelial cell sprout formation, confirming their anti-angiogenic activity. Some of the DARPins exhibited potency comparable to or better than the clinically used anti-VEGF drugs ranibizumab and bevacizumab.

### Ocular Penetration and Retention of Intravitreally Injected DARPins

The ability of the DARPins to penetrate into different compartments of the eye after intravitreal injection was assessed in a mouse model. Alexa-labeled DARPins were injected intravitreally, and their distribution was analyzed at various time points. The DARPins were detected throughout all retinal layers, demonstrating their suitability for targeting retinal disease.

### Vascular Leakage Inhibition by Intravitreal DARPin Application

The efficacy of the DARPins in inhibiting VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage was evaluated in a rabbit model. Intravitreal injection of 50 μg of DARPin #4 significantly inhibited VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage compared to a control group. The fluorescence leakage ratio was 0.8 ± 0.1 in the DARPin-treated eyes, compared to 6.9 ± 3.1 in the control group, indicating almost complete inhibition of vascular leakage.

### Angiogenesis Inhibition by Topical DARPin Application

The potential of the DARPins for topical anti-angiogenic treatment was assessed using a rabbit model of suture-induced corneal angiogenesis. Topical application of DARPin #4 eye-drops reduced the length and density of blood vessels infiltrating the cornea by 31% and 43%, respectively. In a rat model of laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (CNV), topical application of DARPin #6 inhibited CNV formation to a similar extent as the positive control Triamcinolone. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of topically applied DARPins in inhibiting angiogenesis.

### The Next Generation of Ophthalmic Biologics

The DARPins of the invention represent the next generation of VEGF inhibitors for ocular applications. They offer significant advantages over currently licensed products, including higher potency, stability, and the potential for both intravitreal and topical administration. The robust nature of DARPins allows for simple liquid formulation and was well tolerated in animal experiments, making them attractive candidates for the development of novel anti-VEGF drugs.
