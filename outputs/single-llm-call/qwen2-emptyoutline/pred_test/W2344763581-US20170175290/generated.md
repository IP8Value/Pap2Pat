# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for the direct growth of single-crystalline III-V semiconductors on amorphous substrates. More specifically, the invention pertains to a technique known as Transient Liquid Phase (TLP) crystal growth, which enables the formation of high-quality, single-crystalline III-V semiconductors in user-defined geometries on a variety of substrates, including silicon dioxide (SiO2), glass, and plastic. The method is particularly advantageous for the scalable and controllable fabrication of optoelectronic and electronic devices.

## BACKGROUND

III-V semiconductors, such as indium phosphide (InP), gallium phosphide (GaP), and indium antimonide (InSb), are widely used in the fabrication of high-performance electronic and optoelectronic devices due to their superior material properties. However, the growth of these materials typically requires lattice-matched substrates, which limits the choice of substrates and complicates the fabrication process. Traditional methods, such as molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) and metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD), often suffer from issues related to cost, complexity, and scalability.

The Transient Liquid Phase (TLP) crystal growth technique offers a promising alternative. In TLP growth, a thin layer of a group III metal, such as indium (In), is patterned onto a substrate and then encapsulated by a dielectric layer, such as silicon dioxide (SiO2). The substrate is then heated in the presence of a group V precursor, such as phosphine (PH3), causing the group III metal to transform into a liquid phase. The group V element diffuses through the dielectric layer and reacts with the liquid metal to form the desired III-V semiconductor. This process allows for the growth of single-crystalline III-V materials in user-defined geometries, with high crystalline quality and minimal defects.

Despite the potential of TLP growth, there are several challenges that need to be addressed to make it a viable and scalable manufacturing process. These include controlling the nucleation and growth of the III-V crystals, achieving uniform and reproducible doping, and ensuring the compatibility of the process with a wide range of substrates. The present invention provides solutions to these challenges, enabling the direct growth of single-crystalline III-V semiconductors on amorphous substrates with high quality and scalability.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for the direct growth of single-crystalline III-V semiconductors on amorphous substrates using Transient Liquid Phase (TLP) crystal growth. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Substrate Preparation**: A clean substrate, such as a silicon wafer with a thermal oxide layer or a glass slide, is prepared. The substrate is lithographically patterned with the desired shape for the III-V semiconductor.

2. **Nucleation Layer Deposition**: A thin nucleation layer, such as molybdenum oxide (MoOx), is deposited on the patterned substrate. This layer promotes the nucleation of the III-V semiconductor.

3. **Group III Metal Deposition**: A layer of a group III metal, such as indium (In), is deposited on the nucleation layer. The thickness of the metal layer is controlled to achieve the desired final thickness of the III-V semiconductor.

4. **Dielectric Encapsulation**: A dielectric layer, such as silicon dioxide (SiO2), is deposited over the group III metal layer. This layer serves to confine the liquid metal during the growth process and to control the diffusion of the group V element.

5. **Growth Conditions**: The substrate is heated in a low-pressure furnace at a temperature ranging from 500 to 535°C in the presence of a group V precursor, such as phosphine (PH3). The partial pressure of the group V precursor is controlled to achieve the desired growth rate and crystal quality.

6. **In Situ Doping**: Optionally, a dopant gas, such as germane (GeH4), can be introduced during the growth process to achieve controlled n-type doping of the III-V semiconductor.

7. **Post-Growth Processing**: After the growth is complete, the substrate is cooled, and the III-V semiconductor is characterized using techniques such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD).

The method of the present invention enables the growth of single-crystalline III-V semiconductors with high crystalline quality, uniform doping, and user-defined geometries. The process is scalable and compatible with a wide range of substrates, including amorphous materials, making it suitable for the fabrication of advanced electronic and optoelectronic devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Substrate Preparation

The first step in the TLP crystal growth process is the preparation of the substrate. The substrate can be a silicon wafer with a thermal oxide layer, a glass slide, or any other suitable amorphous material. The substrate is cleaned to remove any contaminants and then lithographically patterned with the desired shape for the III-V semiconductor. The patterning can be done using standard photolithographic techniques, such as spin coating a photoresist, exposing the photoresist to UV light through a mask, and developing the photoresist to create the desired pattern.

### Nucleation Layer Deposition

After the substrate is patterned, a thin nucleation layer is deposited on the patterned areas. The nucleation layer is typically a metal oxide, such as molybdenum oxide (MoOx), and serves to promote the nucleation of the III-V semiconductor. The thickness of the nucleation layer is typically in the range of 1 to 10 nm. The nucleation layer can be deposited using physical vapor deposition (PVD) techniques, such as thermal evaporation or sputtering.

### Group III Metal Deposition

Next, a layer of a group III metal, such as indium (In), is deposited on the nucleation layer. The thickness of the metal layer is controlled to achieve the desired final thickness of the III-V semiconductor. The metal layer can be deposited using PVD techniques, such as thermal evaporation or sputtering. To ensure a smooth and uniform metal film, the substrate can be cooled to a temperature below 150 K during the deposition process using liquid nitrogen.

### Dielectric Encapsulation

After the group III metal layer is deposited, a dielectric layer, such as silicon dioxide (SiO2), is deposited over the metal layer. The dielectric layer serves to confine the liquid metal during the growth process and to control the diffusion of the group V element. The thickness of the dielectric layer is typically in the range of 10 to 100 nm. The dielectric layer can be deposited using PVD techniques, such as thermal evaporation or sputtering. Additionally, angled evaporation can be used to coat the exposed side regions of the metal with the dielectric layer, ensuring complete encapsulation.

### Growth Conditions

The substrate is then placed in a low-pressure furnace and heated to a temperature ranging from 500 to 535°C in the presence of a group V precursor, such as phosphine (PH3). The partial pressure of the group V precursor is controlled to achieve the desired growth rate and crystal quality. The growth process is typically carried out for 10 to 20 minutes at a pressure ranging from 100 to 300 Torr. The partial pressure of the group V precursor can be adjusted to control the number of grains per feature, with lower partial pressures leading to fewer grains and larger grain sizes.

### In Situ Doping

Optionally, a dopant gas, such as germane (GeH4), can be introduced during the growth process to achieve controlled n-type doping of the III-V semiconductor. The partial pressure of the dopant gas is controlled to achieve the desired doping concentration. The dopant concentration can be determined by analyzing the photoluminescence spectra of the grown III-V semiconductor. The photoluminescence peak energy shifts to higher energies as the doping concentration increases, indicating increased electron concentrations.

### Post-Growth Processing

After the growth is complete, the substrate is cooled, and the III-V semiconductor is characterized using various techniques. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) is used to examine the morphology and shape of the III-V semiconductor. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is used to confirm the crystalline quality and stoichiometry of the III-V semiconductor. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) is used to determine the crystal orientation and grain structure of the III-V semiconductor. Photoluminescence spectroscopy is used to measure the optical properties of the III-V semiconductor, including the photoluminescence peak energy and the Urbach tail parameter, which indicates the quality of the crystal and the presence of defects.

### Applications

The TLP crystal growth method of the present invention has a wide range of applications in the fabrication of electronic and optoelectronic devices. For example, the method can be used to grow single-crystalline InP in the shape of microwires, which can be used to fabricate high-performance metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs). The method can also be used to grow single-crystalline InP in the shape of complex 3D architectures, such as multilayer structures, which can be used in the design of monolithic 3D electronics. Additionally, the method can be used to grow single-crystalline InP on flexible substrates, such as polyethylene terephthalate (PET), for applications requiring flexible electronics.

### Scalability and Substrate Compatibility

One of the key advantages of the TLP crystal growth method is its scalability. Unlike traditional III-V growth methods, which require both group III and group V elements to be introduced in the vapor phase, the TLP method only requires the group V element to be in the vapor phase, with the geometry fixed by the template. This allows for simple reactor designs that can be easily scaled up to large scales, such as 4-inch wafers. Furthermore, the growth process is self-limiting, preventing unwanted thickness or compositional variation across a wafer.

The TLP crystal growth method is also compatible with a wide range of substrates, including amorphous materials. The relatively low growth temperature (500-535°C) allows for the use of substrates that cannot withstand higher temperatures, such as glass and plastic. This opens up new possibilities for the integration of III-V semiconductors in a variety of applications, including flexible electronics and large-area displays.
