# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND ART

Schizophrenia is a severe neuropsychiatric disorder characterized by disturbances in cognitive, emotional, and motor processes. Dysfunction of the dopaminergic system is considered one of the primary causes of this disease. Previous studies have reported the expression of neurotransmitters, neuropeptides, and the D2 subclass of dopamine receptor genes (D2, D3, D4 subtypes) in immune cells, particularly in peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL). These findings suggest that the expression patterns in PBL may reflect the status of corresponding brain receptors. Recent research has identified the upregulation of D3 subtypes of D2 subclass of dopamine receptor genes (DRD3) in schizophrenic PBL, proposing these as useful peripheral markers for the identification and follow-up of schizophrenia. Additionally, decreased mRNA levels of the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7AChR) gene in PBL of schizophrenic patients have been reported. These studies primarily utilized dopaminergic ligand binding assays or quantitative RT-PCR to analyze gene expression.

In recent years, advanced molecular methods, such as high-throughput microarray technology and quantitative real-time PCR (QRT-PCR), have been developed to analyze gene expression profiles more comprehensively and precisely. Several groups have reported gene expression changes in postmortem schizophrenic brain tissue using microarray technology. The identification and use of multiple peripheral molecular genetic markers would significantly aid in the early diagnosis and follow-up of schizophrenia, facilitating the implementation of effective therapy. Therefore, the present invention aims to identify genes with differential expression in schizophrenic PBL using both high-throughput microarray analysis and QRT-PCR techniques, focusing on drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients to exclude the influence of medication on gene expression.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for identifying and quantifying the expression levels of specific genes in peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL) of drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients. The method involves the use of high-throughput microarray analysis and quantitative real-time PCR (QRT-PCR) to screen and validate the differential expression of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes. The invention provides a reliable and accurate means to detect these genes as potential peripheral genetic markers for the early diagnosis and follow-up of schizophrenia. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Patient Selection**: Recruiting drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients and healthy control individuals, ensuring informed consent and thorough medical examinations.
2. **Sample Preparation**: Isolating PBL and RNA from the collected blood samples.
3. **Microarray Analysis**: Performing high-throughput microarray analysis to pre-screen the expression patterns of genes in PBL.
4. **QRT-PCR Validation**: Conducting QRT-PCR to validate the differential expression of DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes.
5. **Statistical Analysis**: Analyzing the data to determine the significance of the expression changes and classifying the samples into affected and non-affected groups.

## DEFINITIONS

- **Peripheral Blood Lymphocytes (PBL)**: White blood cells isolated from peripheral blood, primarily consisting of T cells, B cells, and natural killer cells.
- **High-Throughput Microarray Analysis**: A technique that allows the simultaneous measurement of the expression levels of thousands of genes using a microarray chip.
- **Quantitative Real-Time PCR (QRT-PCR)**: A molecular biology technique used to amplify and simultaneously quantify a targeted DNA molecule, providing a measure of the initial amount of specific DNA sequences.
- **DRD2 Gene**: A gene encoding the D2 subtype of dopamine receptors, which are part of the D2 subclass of dopamine receptors.
- **Kir2.3 Gene**: A gene encoding an inwardly rectifying potassium channel, specifically a member of the Kir2 family of constitutively active inward rectifier K+ channels.
- **Drug-Naive**: Individuals who have never received pharmacological treatment for their condition.
- **Drug-Free**: Individuals who have not received pharmacological treatment for a specified period.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Patient Selection and Sample Collection

The method begins with the recruitment of drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients and healthy control individuals. Each participant undergoes a comprehensive medical examination, including physical and neurological assessments, routine laboratory tests, and psychiatric evaluations using standardized tools such as the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview Plus (MINI Plus) and the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS). Informed consent is obtained from all participants, and the study adheres to the ethical guidelines outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Blood samples are collected in EDTA-treated tubes, and the lymphocyte fraction is isolated by washing the blood with a red blood lysis buffer to lyse erythrocytes. The isolated PBLs are then used for RNA extraction.

### RNA Isolation and Quality Control

RNA is extracted from the isolated PBLs using a commercial RNA isolation kit, and the quality and quantity of the RNA are verified by electrophoresis and spectrophotometry. The RNA samples are stored at -80°C in the presence of an RNAse inhibitor to maintain their integrity.

### Microarray Analysis

The microarray analysis is performed using a custom human cDNA microarray containing 3200 human cDNA clones. The microarray is prepared by amplifying cDNA inserts from a human lymphocyte cDNA library and sequence-verified clones from a mixed human library. The amplified cDNA is spotted onto FMB cDNA slides using a MicroGrid Total Array System. For probe preparation, total RNA from the pooled schizophrenic patients is reverse transcribed using a poly-dT-primed Genisphere Expression Array 350 Detection Kit. The resulting cDNA is hybridized onto the microarray, and the hybridization is carried out in a Ventana hybridization station. After hybridization, the slides are washed and scanned to obtain the expression data. The data are analyzed to identify genes with more than two-fold changes in expression levels compared to the control samples.

### QRT-PCR Validation

To validate the differential expression of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes identified by microarray analysis, QRT-PCR is performed on individual RNA samples from schizophrenic patients and control individuals. The RNA samples are reverse transcribed using SuperScript II RNase H-Reverse transcriptase, and the cDNA is used as a template for QRT-PCR. The reactions are performed using either SybrGreen dye or TaqMan probes, and the relative quantification of the target genes is determined using the Pfaffl method. The expression levels of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes are normalized to internal control genes (β-actin or hypoxanthine phosphoribosyltransferase (HPRT)) and compared to the average normalized Ct values of the control samples.

### Statistical Analysis

The statistical significance of the expression changes is determined using the unpaired Student's t-test. Agglomerative hierarchical clustering is applied to classify the samples into affected and non-affected groups based on the expression data. The Euclidean metric is used to calculate the dissimilarities between observations, and the group average method is employed for clustering. The results are analyzed to confirm the overexpression of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes in schizophrenic PBL and to establish these genes as potential peripheral genetic markers for schizophrenia.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Identification of DRD2 and Kir2.3 Gene Overexpression in Schizophrenic PBL

In a study involving 13 drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients and 10 healthy control individuals, microarray analysis was performed to screen the expression patterns of genes in PBL. The microarray data revealed a 2.56 ± 0.26-fold increase in the expression level of the DRD2 gene and a 6.75 ± 2.3-fold increase in the expression level of the Kir2.3 gene in schizophrenic patients compared to healthy controls. These preliminary findings were validated using QRT-PCR with SybrGreen dye and TaqMan probes.

#### Example 2: QRT-PCR Validation of DRD2 and Kir2.3 Gene Expression

QRT-PCR was performed on individual RNA samples from 13 affected and 10 non-affected individuals to determine the expression levels of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes. The results showed a significant overexpression of the DRD2 gene in 6 male and 6 female schizophrenic patients, with an average increase of 21.4 ± 18.5-fold using the SybrGreen method and 7.8 ± 6.3-fold using the TaqMan probe method. Similarly, the Kir2.3 gene was overexpressed in 6 male and 6 female schizophrenic PBL, with an average increase of 22 ± 34.7-fold using the SybrGreen method and 8.2 ± 4.8-fold using the TaqMan probe method. These findings confirm the overexpression of DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes in schizophrenic PBL and support their potential as peripheral genetic markers for the diagnosis and follow-up of schizophrenia.

#### Example 3: Hierarchical Clustering Analysis

Agglomerative hierarchical clustering was applied to the gene expression dataset to classify the samples into affected and non-affected groups. The clustering analysis correctly classified the non-affected control and affected samples into two separate clusters, with the exception of one schizophrenic sample (F5). The results demonstrate the robustness of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes as markers for distinguishing between affected and non-affected individuals.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for identifying and quantifying the expression levels of the DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes in peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL) of drug-naive or drug-free schizophrenic patients. The method combines high-throughput microarray analysis and quantitative real-time PCR (QRT-PCR) to accurately detect the overexpression of these genes, which can serve as reliable peripheral genetic markers for the early diagnosis and follow-up of schizophrenia. The use of drug-naive or drug-free patients ensures that the observed gene expression changes reflect the disorder itself rather than the effects of medication. Further validation and high-throughput analysis of additional affected PBLs are recommended to confirm the utility of these genes as diagnostic markers.
