## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of tissue processing and antigen retrieval has seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for accurate and reliable methods to study biological samples. Traditional techniques, such as formaldehyde fixation, have been widely used for preserving tissue morphology but often result in the cross-linking of proteins, which can obscure or mask antigens. This limitation has prompted researchers to develop novel methods to enhance antigen retrieval and improve the quality of immunohistochemical and other molecular analyses.

Formaldehyde, a common fixative, introduces cross-links between proteins, which can hinder the detection of specific antigens. Various methods have been explored to reverse these cross-links, including heat-induced epitope retrieval (HIER) and enzymatic digestion. However, these methods often suffer from low efficiency and can cause damage to the tissue structure. The development of effective formaldehyde scavengers that can break these cross-links without compromising tissue integrity is a critical area of research.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel class of formaldehyde scavengers that function as efficient antigen retrieval agents. These scavengers are capable of reversing formaldehyde-induced cross-links, thereby enhancing the detection of antigens in fixed tissues. The invention has broad applications in histopathology, immunohistochemistry, and other areas of biomedical research where the preservation and detection of antigens are crucial.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for enhancing antigen retrieval in formaldehyde-fixed tissues using formaldehyde scavengers. Specifically, the invention provides a composition comprising one or more formaldehyde scavengers selected from the group consisting of 2-imidazolidinone, ascorbic acid, and tris, buffered to a pH of 3-4. The method involves treating formaldehyde-fixed tissue samples with the scavenger composition to reverse formaldehyde-induced cross-links and improve the accessibility of antigens.

The invention further includes a method for preventing the transfer of formaldehyde labels from formaldehyde-treated proteins to other biomolecules. This method involves treating the formaldehyde-treated protein with the scavenger composition prior to mixing with other biomolecules, thereby reducing the risk of cross-contamination and improving the accuracy of downstream analyses.

The invention also encompasses the use of the scavenger composition in various applications, including but not limited to immunohistochemistry, mass spectrometry, and proteomics. The scavenger composition can be applied to a wide range of tissue types and is particularly useful for enhancing the detection of antigens in formaldehyde-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Compounds

The invention utilizes a composition comprising one or more formaldehyde scavengers selected from the group consisting of 2-imidazolidinone, ascorbic acid, and tris, buffered to a pH of 3-4. These compounds are known for their ability to react with formaldehyde and break the cross-links formed during the fixation process. The buffering to a pH of 3-4 is crucial for optimizing the scavenging activity of these compounds.

2-imidazolidinone, also known as urea, is a versatile compound that can effectively scavenge formaldehyde. It forms stable adducts with formaldehyde, thereby neutralizing its cross-linking potential. Ascorbic acid, a potent antioxidant, not only scavenges formaldehyde but also reduces oxidative stress, which can further enhance the preservation of tissue integrity. Tris, a commonly used buffer in biochemical applications, is effective in maintaining the pH at an optimal level for scavenging activity.

The scavenger composition can be prepared by dissolving the selected scavenger(s) in a suitable solvent, such as water, and adjusting the pH to 3-4 using a suitable acid, such as hydrochloric acid. The concentration of the scavenger(s) can vary depending on the specific application, but a typical concentration range is 5-10% (w/v).

### EXAMPLE 2

To evaluate the effectiveness of the scavenger composition in enhancing antigen retrieval, a series of experiments were conducted using formaldehyde-fixed brain tissue sections. Adult (6 mo.) non-transgenic C57BL6/J mice were used in this study. The animals were euthanized with CO2, and their brains were harvested and fixed with formaldehyde. Paraffin sections (10 μ) were prepared and subjected to antigen retrieval using the scavenger composition.

The paraffin sections were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through graded alcohols. For antigen retrieval, the sections were heated in the scavenger composition (5% w/v 2-imidazolidinone, pH 3-4) using a Decloaking Chamber (Biocare Medical, Concord, CA). The sections were heated initially at 80 °C and then raised to 95 °C, held for 45 min, and cooled back down to 80 °C for a total of 60 min.

Following antigen retrieval, the sections were subjected to immunofluorescence using antibodies against Type IV Collagen and Claudin 5. The sections were incubated with biotinylated anti-rabbit goat antibody and visualized using an avidin-linked fluorophore. The slides were imaged using an Olympus IX61 DSU confocal microscope.

The results showed a significant enhancement in the detection of Type IV Collagen and Claudin 5 in the scavenger-treated sections compared to the untreated controls. The enhanced signal intensity and clarity of the immunofluorescence images demonstrated the effectiveness of the scavenger composition in breaking formaldehyde-induced cross-links and improving antigen accessibility.

### EXAMPLE 3

To further validate the scavenger composition, a series of experiments were conducted using model peptides. Human angiotensin I and ACTH (18–39) were treated with formaldehyde and then subjected to scavenging using the scavenger composition.

10 μl of a solution of human angiotensin I (5 mg/ml water) or ACTH (18–39) (5 mg/ml water) was added to 40 μl formalin (pH 6.8). The mixture was kept at 4 °C for 48 h prior to mass spectrometry. To the formalin-treated peptide (2 μl) was added 98 μl of the scavenger composition (5% w/v 2-imidazolidinone, pH 3-4). The mixture was heated at 95-98 °C for 45 min. The reaction was monitored by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry at 3 time points (0 min, 20 min, and 45 min).

The mass spectrometry results showed a significant reduction in the intensity of the formaldehyde-crosslinked peaks and a corresponding increase in the intensity of the native peptide peaks. This indicated that the scavenger composition effectively reversed the formaldehyde-induced cross-links and restored the native state of the peptides.

### EXAMPLE 4

To assess the ability of the scavenger composition to prevent the transfer of formaldehyde labels from treated proteins to other biomolecules, a series of experiments were conducted using equine myoglobin and human ACTH (18–39).

Equine myoglobin (200 μg) was treated with formaldehyde (47%, pH 6.8) for 48 h at 4 °C. The formylated myoglobin was concentrated, washed with water, lyophilized, and taken up in 200 μl of water. Human ACTH (18–39) (1 μl from 0.5 mg/ml stock) was added to the formylated myoglobin solution (20 μl) and 1 μl of water. Similarly, human ACTH (18–39) (1 μl) was added to formylated myoglobin (20 μl) and 1 μl of the scavenger composition (5% w/v ascorbic acid, pH 3-4). The mixtures were heated at 95 °C for 1.5 h and monitored by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry.

The mass spectrometry results showed a significant reduction in the transfer of formaldehyde labels from the formylated myoglobin to the ACTH (18–39) in the presence of the scavenger composition. This demonstrated the effectiveness of the scavenger composition in preventing cross-contamination and improving the accuracy of downstream analyses.

### EXAMPLE 5

To evaluate the applicability of the scavenger composition in a broader range of tissue types, a series of experiments were conducted using formaldehyde-fixed liver, kidney, and lung tissues. Paraffin sections (10 μ) of these tissues were prepared and subjected to antigen retrieval using the scavenger composition.

The sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, and antigen retrieval was performed using the scavenger composition (5% w/v 2-imidazolidinone, pH 3-4) as described in Example 2. Following antigen retrieval, the sections were subjected to immunofluorescence using antibodies against specific antigens relevant to each tissue type.

The results showed a consistent enhancement in the detection of antigens across all tissue types. The enhanced signal intensity and clarity of the immunofluorescence images demonstrated the versatility and effectiveness of the scavenger composition in improving antigen retrieval in a variety of tissue types.

### EXAMPLE 6

To assess the long-term stability of the scavenger composition, a series of experiments were conducted over a period of 6 months. The scavenger composition (5% w/v 2-imidazolidinone, pH 3-4) was prepared and stored at 4 °C. At regular intervals (0, 1, 3, and 6 months), the composition was tested for its scavenging activity using the model peptide experiments described in Example 3.

The mass spectrometry results showed no significant loss in scavenging activity over the 6-month period. This demonstrated the long-term stability of the scavenger composition, making it suitable for use in routine laboratory settings.

### EXAMPLE 7

To evaluate the compatibility of the scavenger composition with automated staining systems, a series of experiments were conducted using a Ventana Benchmark Ultra automated staining system. Formaldehyde-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections were processed using the scavenger composition as part of the automated staining protocol.

The sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, and antigen retrieval was performed using the scavenger composition (5% w/v 2-imidazolidinone, pH 3-4) as described in Example 2. Following antigen retrieval, the sections were stained with specific antibodies and visualized using the automated staining system.

The results showed that the scavenger composition was fully compatible with the automated staining system and produced high-quality immunohistochemical images. This demonstrated the practicality and ease of use of the scavenger composition in a clinical and research setting.

### EXAMPLE 8

To assess the impact of the scavenger composition on the preservation of tissue morphology, a series of experiments were conducted using formaldehyde-fixed brain tissue sections. The tissue architecture remained intact, and the cells retained their normal appearance. This demonstrated the gentle and non-destructive nature of the scavenger composition, making it suitable for use in applications where the preservation of tissue morphology is critical.
