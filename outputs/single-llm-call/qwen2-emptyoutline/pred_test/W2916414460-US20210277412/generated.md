# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) is a significant pathogen affecting the global swine industry, causing substantial economic losses. This virus, belonging to the genus Circovirus in the family Circoviridae, is characterized by its small, circular single-stranded DNA genome and a nonenveloped icosahedral capsid. The capsid is primarily composed of a single coat protein (CP), which plays a crucial role in the virus's infectivity and immune response. PCV-2 is primarily transmitted via the oral-nasal route and is associated with various syndromes, including postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), which has been the primary cause of economic losses in swine herds.

Current control measures for PCV-2 include vaccination, with five commercial vaccines available. These vaccines are either whole-killed virus vaccines or subunit-based vaccines, all of which are based on the CP of the virus. While these vaccines have been effective, they come with certain limitations. Whole-killed vaccines pose safety concerns during production and inactivation, while subunit vaccines, though safer, are less effective. Virus-like particles (VLPs) offer a promising alternative, combining the safety of subunit vaccines with the efficacy of whole-killed vaccines. VLPs can be produced in various expression systems, including insect cells, yeast, and bacteria, but these systems often require expensive growth media and complex purification processes.

This invention addresses the need for a more cost-effective and scalable production system for PCV-2 VLPs. Specifically, it involves the use of a plant-based transient expression system to produce and purify PCV-2 CP, which self-assembles into VLPs. This approach leverages the simplicity, safety, and scalability of plant-based systems, making it a viable option for the development of a new generation of PCV-2 vaccines.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for producing and purifying porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) coat protein (CP) using a plant-based transient expression system. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Codon Optimization and Plasmid Construction**: The open reading frame 2 (ORF2) encoding the PCV-2 CP is codon-optimized for plant expression, and the optimized sequence is cloned into a plant expression vector, such as pEAQ-HT.

2. **Agrobacterium Transformation and Plant Infiltration**: The recombinant plasmid is transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens, and the Agrobacterium is used to infiltrate Nicotiana benthamiana plants. The plants are grown under controlled conditions to optimize protein expression.

3. **Plant Crude Extract Preparation**: The infiltrated plants are harvested, and crude extracts are prepared by homogenizing the plant material in a buffer containing protease inhibitors.

4. **Density Gradient Centrifugation Purification**: The crude plant extracts are subjected to sucrose density gradient centrifugation to concentrate and purify the PCV-2 CP. The purified CP is further analyzed by cesium chloride (CsCl) density gradient centrifugation to obtain highly purified VLPs.

5. **Characterization and Immunogenicity Testing**: The purified PCV-2 CP and VLPs are characterized using techniques such as SDS-PAGE, immunoblotting, and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The immunogenicity of the plant-produced VLPs is tested in a murine model to evaluate their ability to elicit a specific immune response against PCV-2.

The invention provides a cost-effective and scalable method for producing PCV-2 CP and VLPs, which can be used in the development of vaccines and diagnostic reagents for PCV-2. The plant-based production system offers several advantages, including the ability to perform essential post-translational modifications, minimal risk of contamination with animal pathogens, and the potential for large-scale production.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1

#### Codon Optimization and Plasmid Construction

The open reading frame 2 (ORF2) encoding the PCV-2 coat protein (CP) was codon-optimized for plant expression to ensure optimal tRNA usage and translation efficiency. The codon-optimized sequence was synthesized and provided in the pUC57 cloning vector. The sequence was then cloned into the plant expression vector pEAQ-HT using AgeI and XhoI restriction enzymes. The resulting construct, designated as PCV-2_pEAQ, was confirmed by sequence analysis and transformed into E. coli for propagation.

#### Agrobacterium Transformation and Plant Infiltration

The recombinant PCV-2_pEAQ construct was transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404. The Agrobacterium cultures were prepared and diluted to an optical density (OD600) of 1.0. The Agrobacterium suspension was used to infiltrate the leaves of 6- to 8-week-old Nicotiana benthamiana plants using a vacuum infiltration method. The plants were grown under a 16-hour light and 8-hour dark cycle at 22°C.

#### Plant Crude Extract Preparation

Four days post-infiltration (dpi), the infiltrated leaves were harvested and combined. The plant material was homogenized in a buffer containing 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 0.001% Triton X-100, 0.25 mM L-Arginine, 10% glycerol, 10 mM Tris/HCl (pH 6.5), and protease inhibitors. The homogenate was clarified by centrifugation at 8000 g for 20 minutes, and the supernatant was collected for further purification.

#### Density Gradient Centrifugation Purification

The clarified plant extract was subjected to sucrose density gradient centrifugation. A 30 mL volume of the plant extract was loaded onto a sucrose gradient consisting of 6 mL of 45% sucrose and 2 mL of 65% sucrose in DB150 buffer. The gradient was centrifuged at 120,000 g for 4 hours at 4°C. The resulting fractions were collected, and the pellet was resuspended in 1 mL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).

The resuspended pellet was further purified using cesium chloride (CsCl) density gradient centrifugation. A step gradient was prepared by underlaying 10 mL of 4.5%, 5%, 5.5%, and 10% CsCl solutions in DB150 buffer. The resuspended pellet from the sucrose gradient was loaded onto the CsCl gradient and centrifuged at 180,000 g for 16 hours at 4°C. The fractions were collected, and the densities were confirmed using a refractometer.

#### Characterization and Immunogenicity Testing

The purified PCV-2 CP and VLPs were characterized using SDS-PAGE, immunoblotting, and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The protein bands were visualized using Coomassie Brilliant Blue staining and confirmed by immunoblotting with a rabbit anti-PCV-2 serum. The VLPs were observed under TEM, and their size distribution was determined using a custom image analysis script.

The immunogenicity of the plant-produced VLPs was tested in a murine model. Fifteen mice were randomly divided into three groups of five. The first group was immunized with plant-produced VLPs, the second group with a plant-only control, and the third group with a commercial subunit-based vaccine (Ingelvac CircoFLEX®). The mice were immunized subcutaneously with the respective antigens mixed with an adjuvant. Serum samples were collected 42 days post-immunization and analyzed for the presence of PCV-2-specific antibodies using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs).

### Discussion

The present invention demonstrates the feasibility of producing and purifying PCV-2 coat protein (CP) and virus-like particles (VLPs) using a plant-based transient expression system. The codon-optimized CP gene was efficiently expressed in Nicotiana benthamiana plants, and the protein was successfully purified using sucrose and cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation. The purified VLPs were characterized and found to be morphologically similar to native PCV-2 virions, with a size distribution of 12 to 25 nm.

The immunogenicity testing in mice showed that the plant-produced VLPs elicited a strong immune response, with high titers of PCV-2-specific antibodies detected in the serum. This indicates the potential of the plant-produced VLPs as a vaccine candidate for PCV-2. The plant-based production system offers several advantages, including the ability to perform essential post-translational modifications, minimal risk of contamination with animal pathogens, and the potential for large-scale production.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel and efficient method for producing PCV-2 CP and VLPs, which can be used in the development of vaccines and diagnostic reagents for PCV-2. The plant-based production system offers a cost-effective and scalable alternative to traditional expression systems, making it a promising approach for the control and prevention of PCV-2 infections in swine herds.
