# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The prevalence of obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) has dramatically increased over the past few decades, affecting a significant portion of the global population. Obesity is characterized by an excessive accumulation of adipose tissue, which can lead to a variety of metabolic disorders, including T2DM. T2DM is a complex metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia, which arises from impaired insulin secretion, increased hepatic glucose production, and decreased insulin-mediated glucose uptake (insulin resistance). Insulin resistance is a critical factor in the development of T2DM and is closely linked to the dysfunction of adipose tissue.

Adipose tissue expansion in obesity can occur through hypertrophy (increase in adipocyte size) or hyperplasia (increase in the number of adipocytes). Hypertrophic adipocytes are associated with systemic insulin resistance and metabolic dysfunction, whereas hyperplastic adipocytes tend to retain insulin sensitivity. Therefore, promoting adipocyte hyperplasia has been proposed as a potential therapeutic strategy to improve insulin sensitivity and treat metabolic disorders.

Monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) is a member of the SLC16a solute carrier gene family and is responsible for the bidirectional transport of monocarboxylates such as lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies across the plasma membrane. MCT1 is highly expressed in proliferating cells and is a target of the oncogenic transcription factor c-Myc. In adipocytes, MCT1 expression increases during differentiation, and lactate metabolism is closely linked to adipocyte function and insulin sensitivity.

This invention relates to a method for inducing hyperplasia in mature adipocytes by inhibiting MCT1 activity. By modulating MCT1-mediated lactate transport, the invention aims to promote the re-entry of mature adipocytes into the cell cycle, leading to increased adipocyte proliferation and improved metabolic health.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for inducing hyperplasia in mature adipocytes by inhibiting the activity of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1). The method involves treating mature adipocytes with a small molecule inhibitor of MCT1, such as AZD3965, to reduce MCT1 activity. This inhibition leads to a re-entry of mature adipocytes into the cell cycle, resulting in increased adipocyte proliferation and improved insulin sensitivity.

The invention further encompasses compositions comprising MCT1 inhibitors and methods for their use in treating metabolic disorders associated with obesity and insulin resistance. The compositions and methods of the invention are designed to promote adipocyte hyperplasia, thereby redistributing lipid content and enhancing the overall metabolic health of adipose tissue.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

- **Adipocyte**: A specialized cell that stores energy in the form of lipids (triglycerides) and plays a crucial role in energy homeostasis and metabolism.
- **Hyperplasia**: An increase in the number of cells in a tissue or organ, leading to an overall increase in tissue mass.
- **Hypertrophy**: An increase in the size of individual cells in a tissue or organ, without an increase in the number of cells.
- **Insulin Resistance**: A condition in which the body's cells do not respond effectively to insulin, leading to elevated blood glucose levels.
- **Monocarboxylate Transporter 1 (MCT1)**: A protein that facilitates the transport of monocarboxylates such as lactate and pyruvate across the plasma membrane.
- **Proliferation**: The process by which cells divide and multiply in number.
- **Re-entry into the Cell Cycle**: The process by which quiescent cells resume cell division and enter the cell cycle.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Inhibition of MCT1 in 3T3-L1 Cells Hinders Adipocyte Lipid Accumulation

**Objective**: To determine the effect of pharmacological inhibition of MCT1 on adipocyte differentiation and lipid accumulation in 3T3-L1 cells.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Culture and Differentiation**: Murine 3T3-L1 cells were cultured and differentiated into mature lipid-laden adipocytes using a well-established protocol. The cells were treated with or without 1 µM AZD3965, a potent and selective small-molecule MCT1 inhibitor, during the differentiation process.
2. **Oil Red O Staining**: Lipid accumulation was assessed by Oil Red O staining and quantification of the lipid content.
3. **Immunoblotting**: Protein expression of adipogenic markers such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ), fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4/aP2), and hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) was analyzed by immunoblotting.

**Results**:
- Treatment with AZD3965 significantly reduced lipid accumulation in 3T3-L1 cells, as evidenced by a decrease in the Oil Red O staining area.
- The expression levels of PPARγ, FABP4, and HSL were significantly attenuated in cells treated with AZD3965, indicating impaired adipocyte differentiation.

#### Example 2: MCT1 Inhibition Suppresses Induction of Adipogenic Gene Expression in Preadipocytes

**Objective**: To determine the effect of MCT1 inhibition on the induction of adipogenic gene expression in preadipocytes.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Culture and Differentiation**: 3T3-L1 cells were differentiated into adipocytes with or without 1 µM AZD3965.
2. **RT-qPCR and Immunoblotting**: The expression of adipogenic markers such as PPARγ, FABP4, and HSL was analyzed by RT-qPCR and immunoblotting.

**Results**:
- The expression of PPARγ, FABP4, and HSL was significantly reduced in 3T3-L1 cells treated with AZD3965, indicating that MCT1 inhibition suppresses the induction of adipogenic gene expression.

#### Example 3: Mature Adipocytes Show Reduced Lipid Content but Remain Differentiated After MCT1 Inhibition

**Objective**: To assess the impact of MCT1 inhibition on fully differentiated adipocytes.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Culture and Treatment**: Fully differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes were treated with 1 µM AZD3965 for up to 72 hours.
2. **Lipid Content and Metabolism**: Changes in lipid content, NADP+/NADPH bioavailability, and intracellular glycerol levels were monitored.
3. **RT-qPCR and Immunoblotting**: Transcript and protein expression levels of adipogenic markers were analyzed by RT-qPCR and immunoblotting.
4. **Adipokine Secretion**: Adipokine secretion was assessed using an adipokine antibody microarray.

**Results**:
- Treatment with AZD3965 led to a significant decrease in lipid content, which was not due to cytotoxicity.
- There was a subtle yet significant decrease in NADP+/NADPH bioavailability and a significant increase in intracellular glycerol levels, indicating increased lipolysis.
- Transcript levels of PPARγ were significantly decreased, but protein levels remained unchanged.
- FABP4 mRNA and protein levels were initially increased but returned to baseline by 72 hours.
- HSL expression was significantly decreased.
- Adipokine secretion was not significantly affected, suggesting that adipocytes remained differentiated.

#### Example 4: RNA Sequencing Reveals Transcriptional Consequences of MCT1 Inhibition in Adipocytes

**Objective**: To investigate the transcriptional changes induced by MCT1 inhibition in mature adipocytes.

**Methods**:
1. **RNA Isolation and Sequencing**: RNA was isolated from 3T3-L1 adipocytes treated with or without 1 µM AZD3965 for 24 hours and subjected to RNA sequencing.
2. **Pathway Analysis**: Differentially expressed genes were identified, and pathway analysis was performed using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA).

**Results**:
- 277 genes were significantly differentially expressed following MCT1 inhibition.
- Pathway analysis revealed that these genes were largely implicated in the regulation of the cell cycle and mitosis.
- Genes involved in cell cycle progression were upregulated, while genes that inhibit cell cycle progression were downregulated.

#### Example 5: Adipocytes Re-Enter the Cell Cycle Following MCT1 Inhibition

**Objective**: To validate the transcriptional changes observed in RNA sequencing and confirm the re-entry of adipocytes into the cell cycle.

**Methods**:
1. **RT-qPCR and Immunoblotting**: The expression of cell cycle-promoting genes such as CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, and Cyclin B was analyzed by RT-qPCR and immunoblotting.
2. **Proliferation Assay**: A fluorescent DNA dye-based assay was used to determine changes in cell number.
3. **Confocal Microscopy**: Ki67 expression, a marker of proliferation, was visualized by confocal microscopy.

**Results**:
- RT-qPCR confirmed a significant upregulation of CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, and Cyclin B in MCT1 inhibitor-treated adipocytes.
- Immunoblotting showed a significant decrease in CDK1 phosphorylation at Tyr15, indicating an increase in the active form of CDK1.
- The proliferation assay demonstrated a significant increase in the total cell number in MCT1 inhibitor-treated adipocytes.
- Confocal microscopy revealed a significant increase in Ki67 expression, confirming the re-entry of adipocytes into the cell cycle.

#### Example 6: MCT1 Inhibition Enhances Adipocyte Glucose Uptake in a CDK1-Dependent Manner

**Objective**: To evaluate the impact of MCT1 inhibition on insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in mature adipocytes.

**Methods**:
1. **Glucose Uptake Assay**: Insulin-stimulated glucose uptake was measured in 3T3-L1 adipocytes treated with or without 1 µM AZD3965 for up to 72 hours.
2. **CDK1 Inhibition**: The effect of MCT1 inhibition on glucose uptake was assessed in the presence of the CDK1 inhibitor RO-3306.

**Results**:
- Treatment with AZD3965 significantly increased insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in mature adipocytes.
- The effect of AZD3965 on glucose uptake was attenuated by the addition of RO-3306, indicating a CDK1-dependent mechanism.

#### Example 7: MCT1 Inhibition Enhances Adipocyte Lipid Storage Capacity

**Objective**: To assess the ability of MCT1 inhibition to enhance the lipid storage capacity of adipocytes.

**Methods**:
1. **Triglyceride Assay**: Intracellular triglyceride content was measured in 3T3-L1 adipocytes pretreated with or without 1 µM AZD3965 for up to 72 hours and cultured with or without 100 nM insulin for an additional 72 hours.

**Results**:
- Adipocytes pretreated with AZD3965 accumulated significantly more triglycerides than adipocytes not exposed to the MCT1 inhibitor.
- The effect was more pronounced in adipocytes pretreated for 48 hours, suggesting that MCT1 inhibition promotes hyperplasia and enhances the lipid storage capacity of adipocytes.

### Conclusion

The present invention demonstrates that inhibiting MCT1 activity in mature adipocytes can promote re-entry into the cell cycle, leading to increased adipocyte proliferation and improved insulin sensitivity. This method has the potential to be developed into a novel therapeutic strategy for treating metabolic disorders associated with obesity and insulin resistance. Further in vitro and in vivo studies are warranted to fully elucidate the systemic metabolic benefits of MCT1 inhibition and to optimize the therapeutic potential of this approach.
