## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The development of lactic acidemia (LA) in septic shock (SS) is associated with a poor prognosis. Blood concentrations of lactate depend on its rate of production and utilization by various organs. Uptake of lactate by the liver occurs via a membrane-associated, pH-dependent, bidirectional facilitative antiport transport system known as the monocarboxylate transporter. Once transported into the hepatocyte, lactate can be converted into glucose by the gluconeogenic pathway or can be converted to pyruvate to enter the mitochondria to be metabolized to CO2 and water. The accumulation of lactate is usually associated with acidosis, which may reflect ATP hydrolysis, extracellular transport of protons, and other mechanisms. Hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction has been postulated as an explanation for the development of LA in SS, although extra-hepatic mechanisms also contribute.

In mitochondria, pyruvate undergoes conversion to acetyl CoA, after which oxidative phosphorylation occurs. Electrons are transferred primarily by reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) to mitochondrial complexes I, III, and IV to generate a proton (H+) gradient (the proton motive force; pmf) across the mitochondrial inner membrane. The free energy accumulated as the pmf is then used to drive ATP synthesis through the F1FoATP synthase activity (Complex V), allowing protons to return to the mitochondrial matrix.

The variable reports pertaining to hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction in SS may be a consequence of the different methodology and experimental protocols used in different studies. The recent development of the Seahorse XF24 extracellular flux analyzer allows for precise measurements of mitochondrial oxygen consumption under various conditions and has not previously been used to investigate hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction in SS.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for treating septic shock (SS) and lactic acidemia (LA) using gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS). The method involves administering GSS to a subject suffering from SS to improve systemic hemodynamics, reduce blood lactate concentrations, and enhance hepatic mitochondrial function. GSS is effective in reversing the systemic vasodilation and low mean arterial pressure (MAP) that develop in SS, and it also improves hepatic mitochondrial function, which is often impaired in SS. The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising GSS and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for the treatment of SS and LA.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### RESULTS

In a canine model of SS, the administration of GSS was found to significantly improve systemic hemodynamics, reduce blood lactate concentrations, and enhance hepatic mitochondrial function. Specifically, GSS was capable of increasing mean arterial pressure (MAP) to baseline levels, reducing heart rate (HR), and decreasing blood lactate concentrations. In contrast, norepinephrine (NE), while effective in increasing MAP, did not reduce blood lactate concentrations. Furthermore, GSS was found to improve hepatic mitochondrial function, as evidenced by increased maximal oxygen consumption rates (OCR) and coupled respiration, as measured using the Seahorse XF24 extracellular flux analyzer.

### METHODS

#### Animal Preparation

In this study, a bacteremic model of Pseudomonas aeruginosa was used to measure hepatic mitochondrial function and LA under conditions of intense inflammatory response. The animals (18 to 26 kg) were studied under sufentanil citrate (0.05–0.3 μg/kg/min) and midazolam (5 μg/kg/min) anesthesia, with ventilation maintained at an arterial PO2 of ~400 mmHg. Two vascular catheters were inserted under sterile conditions to measure hemodynamic parameters, including MAP, pulmonary arterial pressure (Ppa), wedge pressure (Pwp), and right atrial pressure (Rap), as well as heart rate (HR) and cardiac output (CO). Blood samples were taken for analyses of PO2, PCO2, and pH, as well as for serum liver function tests, troponin, lactate concentrations, hematocrit (Hct), and white blood cell (WBC) counts.

#### Experimental Protocols

Five groups of animals were included in the study: a septic control group, a non-septic control group, a norepinephrine (NE) septic group, a gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS) septic group, and a GSS non-septic group. Baseline measurements of hemodynamics and blood parameters were made after a 1-hour period of stability. In the septic groups, after the initiation of bacterial infusion, measurements were repeated in the septic shock condition, defined as a MAP decrease to 60–65 mmHg. NE and GSS were then administered to maintain MAP at baseline levels. In the non-septic groups, measurements were made at comparable baseline and sham shock intervals, followed by the administration of GSS or a placebo (D5W).

#### Mitochondrial Preparation

After in vivo measurements, a piece of liver was harvested and mitochondria were isolated using a dounce homogenizer. The mitochondrial preparation was diluted in cold mitochondrial assay solution (MAS) and plated onto a custom Seahorse 24-well culture dish for OCR measurements.

#### Mitochondria Measurement of Oxygen Consumption Rates (OCR)

Hepatic mitochondrial function was examined using the Seahorse XF24 extracellular flux analyzer. Basal OCR was measured by adding pyruvate (10 mM) and malate (2 mM). ADP (4 mM) was then added to allow ATP synthase to function, producing an increase in OCR (coupled OCR). Oligomycin (2.0 μM) was added to terminate coupled respiration, followed by the protonophore FCCP (4 μM) to measure uncoupled respiration. Finally, rotenone (2 μM) and antimycin A (2 μM) were added to inhibit the electron transport chain.

#### To Determine Whether MCT1 Protein is Altered in Septic Shock

The expression and activity of the monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) protein were evaluated to determine its role in lactate transport and metabolism in septic shock. Western blotting and immunohistochemistry were used to assess MCT1 protein levels in liver tissues from the different groups.

#### Gallic Acid Septic Group

In a separate set of experiments, gallic acid was administered to a septic group to compare its effects with those of GSS. Gallic acid is a phenolic compound with similar antioxidant properties to GSS. The effects on systemic hemodynamics, blood lactate concentrations, and hepatic mitochondrial function were measured using the same methods as described above.

#### Statistics

Hemodynamic measurements were analyzed using a two-way ANOVA (split-plot) with a Student Newman Keuls (SNK) multiple comparison test. Results are reported as mean ± 1 SD, with significance set at P < 0.05. Mitochondrial OCR parameters were analyzed using a one-way ANOVA with a SNK multiple comparison test.
