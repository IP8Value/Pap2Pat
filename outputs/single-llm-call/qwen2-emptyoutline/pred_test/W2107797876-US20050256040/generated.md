# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel synthetic protein, designated as Synthetic Glycopeptide (SGP), and methods for its use in the treatment of various types of cancer. Specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for the administration of SGP to inhibit tumor growth and metastasis in vivo. The invention further encompasses the use of SGP in combination with extracellular matrix components to modulate its activity and enhance its therapeutic efficacy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, with numerous challenges in developing effective treatments. Traditional cancer therapies, such as chemotherapy and radiation, often come with significant side effects and limited efficacy, especially in advanced stages of the disease. There is a pressing need for new therapeutic agents that can selectively target and destroy cancer cells while minimizing harm to healthy tissues.

Synthetic Glycopeptide (SGP) is a novel protein designed to disrupt cell membranes, leading to cell death. Initial studies have shown that SGP exhibits potent anti-tumor activity in vitro and in vivo. However, the mechanism of action and the factors influencing its selectivity and efficacy have not been fully elucidated. Understanding these aspects is crucial for optimizing the therapeutic potential of SGP and developing it into a viable cancer treatment.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a synthetic protein, Synthetic Glycopeptide (SGP), and methods for its use in the treatment of cancer. SGP is a designed protein with a unique structure that enables it to disrupt cell membranes, leading to cell death. The invention encompasses the following aspects:

1. **Composition of SGP**: SGP is a synthetic protein composed of specific amino acid sequences that confer its membrane-disrupting properties. The protein is synthesized using standard peptide synthesis techniques, such as the Fmoc procedure.

2. **Methods of Synthesis**: The invention includes methods for synthesizing SGP, including the use of automated peptide synthesizers and purification techniques such as HPLC chromatography.

3. **Anti-tumor Activity**: SGP demonstrates potent anti-tumor activity in vitro and in vivo. Local administration of SGP to tumor xenografts in nude mice results in significant reduction in tumor volume and inhibition of metastasis.

4. **Mechanism of Action**: SGP disrupts cell membranes, leading to cell death. The mechanism of action is influenced by the presence of extracellular matrix components, which can modulate SGP's activity.

5. **Therapeutic Applications**: The invention provides methods for using SGP in the treatment of various types of cancer, including breast carcinoma, Kaposi's sarcoma, prostate carcinoma, and lung carcinoma. SGP can be administered locally to the tumor site to minimize systemic side effects.

6. **Safety Profile**: SGP does not produce significant toxicity when injected subcutaneously or intradermally, making it a promising candidate for cancer therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Composition of SGP

Synthetic Glycopeptide (SGP) is a designed protein with a unique structure that confers its membrane-disrupting properties. The protein is synthesized using the Fmoc procedure starting from Fmoc-Leu-PEG (polyethylene glycol) resin. The synthesis is carried out using an automated peptide synthesizer, such as the Miligen 9050, to monitor the de-protection of the Fmoc group by UV absorbance. After cleavage from the resin by trifluoroacetic acid, the crude peptide is purified by HPLC chromatography with an ODS column, 20 ϫ 250 mm, using a gradient system of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. The purity and molecular weight of SGP are confirmed by amino acid analysis and fast atom bombardment mass spectroscopy.

### Methods of Synthesis

The synthesis of SGP involves the following steps:

1. **Resin Preparation**: Fmoc-Leu-PEG resin is prepared and loaded into the peptide synthesizer.
2. **Amino Acid Coupling**: The desired amino acids are sequentially coupled to the resin using the Fmoc procedure.
3. **Deprotection**: The Fmoc group is removed after each coupling step by UV absorbance monitoring.
4. **Cleavage and Purification**: The peptide is cleaved from the resin using trifluoroacetic acid and purified by HPLC chromatography.
5. **Characterization**: The purity and molecular weight of the final product are confirmed by amino acid analysis and mass spectroscopy.

### Anti-tumor Activity

SGP demonstrates potent anti-tumor activity in vitro and in vivo. In vitro studies show that SGP can induce rapid cell death in various human cell lines, including Kaposi's sarcoma-derived cells (KS1767), breast carcinoma cells (MDA-MB-435), and microvessel endothelial cells. The mechanism of cell death varies depending on the concentration of SGP. At high concentrations (˃10 M), SGP leads to rapid non-necrotic, non-apoptotic cell death. At lower concentrations (5-10 M), SGP induces necrosis, and at even lower concentrations (˂5 M), it induces apoptosis.

In vivo studies using nude mice bearing human tumor xenografts have shown that local administration of SGP results in significant reduction in tumor volume and inhibition of metastasis. Tumors treated with SGP were significantly smaller compared to those treated with PBS. Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated tumors revealed widespread cell death, confirming the anti-tumor activity of SGP.

### Mechanism of Action

The mechanism of action of SGP involves the disruption of cell membranes, leading to cell death. The potency of SGP is influenced by the presence of extracellular matrix components. In vitro, SGP shows no selectivity toward normal or malignant cells. However, in vivo, the activity of SGP is limited to tumors due to the protective effect of the extracellular matrix. Matrigel and polymeric fibronectin, which mimic the extracellular matrix, can modulate the activity of SGP, reducing its cytotoxic effects on normal cells.

### Therapeutic Applications

The invention provides methods for using SGP in the treatment of various types of cancer, including:

1. **Breast Carcinoma**: SGP can be administered locally to breast carcinoma xenografts to reduce tumor volume and inhibit metastasis.
2. **Kaposi's Sarcoma**: SGP is effective in treating Kaposi's sarcoma, a type of cancer associated with HIV infection.
3. **Prostate Carcinoma**: SGP can be used to treat prostate carcinoma, a common type of cancer in men.
4. **Lung Carcinoma**: SGP is effective in reducing the size of lung carcinoma xenografts and inhibiting metastasis.

### Safety Profile

SGP does not produce significant toxicity when injected subcutaneously or intradermally. Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated mice showed no overt pathology in major organs, confirming that SGP treatments do not affect sites other than the injected tumor area. This safety profile makes SGP a promising candidate for cancer therapy.

### EXAMPLE 1

**Synthesis of SGP**

SGP was synthesized using the Fmoc procedure starting from Fmoc-Leu-PEG resin. The synthesis was carried out using an automated peptide synthesizer (Miligen 9050) to monitor the de-protection of the Fmoc group by UV absorbance. After cleavage from the resin by trifluoroacetic acid, the crude peptide was purified by HPLC chromatography with an ODS column, 20 ϫ 250 mm, using a gradient system of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. The purity and molecular weight of SGP were confirmed by amino acid analysis and fast atom bombardment mass spectroscopy.

### EXAMPLE 2

**In Vitro Cytotoxicity Assay**

The cytotoxicity of SGP was evaluated using the Kaposi's sarcoma-derived cell line KS1767. Cells were incubated with different concentrations of SGP (10 M, 5 M, 3 M, and 1 M) for 24 hours. Cell viability was determined by morphology using acridine orange and ethidium bromide staining. Results showed that SGP induced rapid cell death at high concentrations (˃10 M), necrosis at intermediate concentrations (5-10 M), and apoptosis at low concentrations (˂5 M).

### EXAMPLE 3

**In Vivo Anti-tumor Activity**

Nude mice bearing MDA-MB-435 xenografts were treated with local injections of SGP (100 M or 1 mM) or PBS. Tumor volumes were measured weekly for 8 weeks. Results showed that SGP-treated mice had significantly smaller tumor volumes compared to PBS-treated mice. Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated tumors revealed widespread cell death, confirming the anti-tumor activity of SGP.

### EXAMPLE 4

**Effect of SGP on Different Tumor Types**

Nude mice bearing xenografts of prostate (PC-3) and lung carcinoma (H358) cell lines were treated with local injections of SGP (100 M or 1 mM) or PBS. Tumor volumes were measured weekly for 8 weeks. Results showed that SGP was effective in reducing the size of prostate and lung carcinoma xenografts and inhibiting metastasis.

### EXAMPLE 5

**Effect of Extracellular Matrix Components on SGP Activity**

The effect of extracellular matrix components on SGP activity was evaluated using KS1767 cells. Cells were incubated with SGP (1 mM) in the presence or absence of matrigel or polymeric fibronectin. Results showed that SGP was ineffective in the presence of matrigel and polymeric fibronectin, indicating that these components can modulate SGP activity.

### EXAMPLE 6

**Histopathological Analysis of SGP-Treated Tumors**

Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated MDA-MB-435 and KS1767 xenografts was performed. Tumors were fixed in Bouin solution, embedded in paraffin, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Results showed widespread cell death in SGP-treated tumors, with many condensed nuclei and no effect on the extracellular matrix.

### EXAMPLE 7

**Safety Profile of SGP**

The safety profile of SGP was evaluated by injecting SGP (100 M) subcutaneously or intradermally into nude mice. The injected areas were monitored for 2 weeks. Results showed no surface effects or overt pathology in major organs, confirming the safety of SGP.

### EXAMPLE 8

**Mechanism of SGP Action**

The mechanism of SGP action was investigated using a combination of in vitro and in vivo studies. In vitro, SGP was shown to disrupt cell membranes, leading to cell death. In vivo, the activity of SGP was limited to tumors due to the protective effect of the extracellular matrix. These findings suggest that SGP is a potent membrane-disrupting agent with therapeutic potential in cancer treatment.

This detailed description and the accompanying examples provide a comprehensive understanding of the invention and its potential applications in cancer therapy. The invention offers a novel approach to cancer treatment by targeting cell membranes with a designed protein, SGP, and modulating its activity using extracellular matrix components.
