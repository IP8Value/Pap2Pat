# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of superconducting materials and, more specifically, to the fabrication of magnesium diboride (MgB2) thin films with high transition temperatures (Tc) using magnetron sputtering techniques. These films are particularly useful in the development of superconducting microdevices for various applications, including high-frequency superconducting photon detectors, transmission lines, and quantum computing qubits.

### 2. Description of the Related Art

Superconducting materials, characterized by zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature (Tc), have found extensive applications in various fields, including electronics, energy, and quantum computing. Magnesium diboride (MgB2) is a promising superconducting material due to its relatively high Tc of around 39 K and its ease of fabrication compared to other high-temperature superconductors. However, the practical realization of MgB2 thin films with high Tc and desirable properties has been challenging due to issues such as oxidation, small grain size, and off-stoichiometry during fabrication.

Magnetron sputtering is a widely used technique for depositing thin films due to its scalability and ability to produce uniform films. However, in-situ sputtered MgB2 films often exhibit low Tc due to oxidation, small grain size, and off-stoichiometry, particularly at elevated temperatures. Post-annealing of these films can improve Tc but often results in increased surface roughness. Room temperature deposition followed by post-annealing has been explored to address these issues, but challenges remain in achieving smooth and stoichiometric films.

Previous attempts to improve the quality of MgB2 thin films have involved various strategies, such as optimizing the deposition conditions, using different substrates, and employing capping layers to prevent magnesium evaporation during annealing. Despite these efforts, achieving high Tc and smooth films on a large scale remains a significant challenge.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for fabricating ultra-smooth, wafer-scale superconducting MgB2 thin films with high Tc using magnetron sputtering and post-annealing techniques. The method involves depositing a Mg-B composite film at room temperature, followed by post-annealing in a controlled environment to achieve high Tc while maintaining smoothness and uniformity. A key aspect of the invention is the use of a boron capping layer to prevent magnesium evaporation during annealing, ensuring the formation of large MgB2 grains and high Tc.

The invention also includes the use of a silicon nitride buffer layer on silicon substrates to prevent reactions between magnesium and silicon, which can degrade the superconducting properties of the films. The method allows for precise control of the magnesium to boron ratio, enabling the tuning of resistivity and kinetic inductance, which are crucial parameters for the performance of superconducting microdevices.

The resulting MgB2 thin films exhibit high Tc, low surface roughness, and excellent uniformity, making them suitable for a wide range of applications, including high-frequency superconducting photon detectors, transmission lines, and quantum computing qubits. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of superconducting materials, providing a reliable and scalable method for producing high-quality MgB2 thin films.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Description

The present invention provides a method for fabricating ultra-smooth, wafer-scale superconducting MgB2 thin films with high Tc. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Deposition of Mg-B Composite Film**: A Mg-B composite film is deposited on a substrate using magnetron sputtering at room temperature. The substrate can be silicon, sapphire, or other suitable materials. To prevent reactions between magnesium and silicon, a thin silicon nitride buffer layer is deposited on the silicon substrate using low-pressure chemical vapor deposition (LPCVD). The Mg-B composite film is deposited using radio frequency (RF) magnetron sputtering of elemental magnesium and boron targets. The sputtering conditions, including the power and substrate bias, are optimized to achieve a smooth and uniform film.

2. **Application of Boron Capping Layer**: A thin layer of boron is deposited on top of the Mg-B composite film to serve as a capping layer. The boron capping layer prevents magnesium evaporation during the subsequent annealing process, ensuring the formation of large MgB2 grains and high Tc. The boron capping layer also forms a viscous, crack-free oxide cap during annealing, which helps maintain the smoothness of the film.

3. **Post-Annealing**: The Mg-B composite film with the boron capping layer is annealed in a rapid thermal processor (RTP) to convert the composite film into MgB2. The annealing temperature and time are carefully controlled to achieve the desired Tc while minimizing surface roughness. The annealing temperature is kept below the melting point of magnesium (650 °C) to prevent magnesium evaporation and surface roughening. The optimal annealing conditions depend on the specific deposition parameters and can be fine-tuned to achieve the highest Tc.

4. **Removal of Boron Capping Layer**: After annealing, the boron capping layer is removed using an argon ion mill. The removal of the capping layer is essential for measuring the superconducting properties of the MgB2 film and for device fabrication. The ion milling process is carefully controlled to avoid damaging the underlying MgB2 film.

5. **Device Fabrication**: The MgB2 thin films are used to fabricate superconducting microdevices, such as planar quarter-wave resonators patterned using a coplanar waveguide (CPW) architecture. The devices are fabricated using standard photolithography and etching techniques. A tri-purpose titanium-gold bilayer is used as a passivation layer during fabrication, a hardmask, and electrical contact pads. The devices are then passivated with a dielectric layer to protect the MgB2 film from oxidation and other environmental factors.

6. **Characterization**: The superconducting properties of the MgB2 thin films and devices are characterized using various techniques, including X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and electrical measurements. The XPS is used to analyze the composition and stoichiometry of the films, while the TEM is used to examine the microstructure and interfaces. Electrical measurements, such as DC resistance and current density, are performed using a four-point method, and RF measurements are conducted using a vector network analyzer in a liquid helium Dewar.

The method of the present invention addresses the challenges associated with fabricating high-quality MgB2 thin films by optimizing the deposition and annealing conditions and using a boron capping layer to prevent magnesium evaporation. The resulting films exhibit high Tc, low surface roughness, and excellent uniformity, making them suitable for a wide range of applications in superconducting technology.

## CONCLUSION

The present invention provides a robust and scalable method for fabricating ultra-smooth, wafer-scale superconducting MgB2 thin films with high Tc. The method involves depositing a Mg-B composite film at room temperature, applying a boron capping layer, and post-annealing in a controlled environment. The use of a silicon nitride buffer layer on silicon substrates and the careful optimization of deposition and annealing conditions ensure the formation of high-quality MgB2 films with desirable properties. The resulting films are suitable for various applications, including high-frequency superconducting photon detectors, transmission lines, and quantum computing qubits. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of superconducting materials, providing a reliable and efficient method for producing high-performance MgB2 thin films.
