# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics, particularly to the inhibition of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) heterodimerization. More specifically, the invention provides a novel cyclic peptidomimetic compound that inhibits the protein-protein interaction (PPI) of EGFR and HER2, thereby modulating cell signaling pathways involved in cancer cell growth and proliferation.

## BACKGROUND ART

Epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFR) play a crucial role in cell growth and differentiation in normal physiological processes. Dysregulation of EGFR signaling is a significant factor in the development and progression of various cancers, including breast, lung, and ovarian cancers. The human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family, comprising four homologous members (HER1-4), is involved in cell growth and differentiation. Binding of ligands to the extracellular domains of these receptors triggers a conformational change that initiates intracellular signaling through the transmembrane helix, cytoplasmic kinase domain, and regulatory region. Dysregulation of homo- or heterodimerization processes of these receptors or their overexpression is a key factor in tumor progression. Among the possible dimers of these receptors, EGFR-HER2 and HER2-HER3 are particularly significant in the development and progression of different types of cancer.

Monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab, which target the HER2 extracellular domain, are currently used for treating breast cancer. Trastuzumab binds to domain IV, while pertuzumab binds to domain II of the extracellular region of HER2. Despite their effectiveness, antibodies have limitations in terms of shelf-life, delivery, and immunogenicity. Therefore, there is a need for alternative therapeutic agents that can effectively inhibit EGFR and HER2 heterodimerization and overcome the limitations associated with antibodies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel cyclic peptidomimetic compound that inhibits the heterodimerization of EGFR and HER2, thereby modulating cell signaling pathways involved in cancer cell growth and proliferation. The compound, designated as Compound 18, is a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic with a β-turn/β-hairpin structure that specifically binds to the extracellular domain (ECD) of HER2, particularly domain IV, and inhibits the protein-protein interaction (PPI) of EGFR and HER2.

The invention includes:
1. A cyclic peptidomimetic compound having the formula:
   \[
   \text{Cyclo}[-\text{Arg}-\text{Anapa}-\text{Phe}-\text{Asp}-\text{L-Pro}-\text{D-Pro}-\text{Arg}-\text{Anapa}-\text{Phe}-\text{Asp}-]
   \]
   where Anapa is a β-amino acid.

2. A method for inhibiting the heterodimerization of EGFR and HER2 in a cell, comprising contacting the cell with an effective amount of the cyclic peptidomimetic compound of the invention.

3. A pharmaceutical composition comprising the cyclic peptidomimetic compound of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

4. A method for treating a cancer characterized by overexpression of HER2, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of the cyclic peptidomimetic compound of the invention.

5. A method for inducing apoptosis in HER2-positive cells, comprising contacting the cells with an effective amount of the cyclic peptidomimetic compound of the invention.

6. A method for delaying the progression of breast cancer tumors in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the cyclic peptidomimetic compound of the invention.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Definitions/General Discussion

The term "cyclic peptidomimetic" refers to a synthetic molecule that mimics the structure and function of a natural peptide but is cyclized to enhance stability and reduce susceptibility to enzymatic degradation. The term "protein-protein interaction (PPI)" refers to the physical contact between two or more proteins that occurs in a living cell. The term "heterodimerization" refers to the process by which two different proteins form a complex. The term "extracellular domain (ECD)" refers to the portion of a transmembrane protein that extends outside the cell membrane.

### Example 1. Compound 18: Synthesis and Characterization

The cyclic peptidomimetic compound 18 was synthesized using standard solid-phase Fmoc chemistry. The synthesis began with the loading of Fmoc-Pro-OH onto 2-chlorotrityl chloride resin. The resin was then coupled with the remaining amino acids in the sequence using HCTU as the coupling reagent. After the final amino acid was added, the Fmoc-protecting group was removed, and the peptide was cleaved from the resin using a deprotection cocktail. Cyclization was achieved by treating the linear peptidomimetic with PyAOP and DIEA in THF/DMF. The resulting cyclic peptidomimetic was purified by HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry, circular dichroism spectroscopy, and NMR.

### Example 2. Inhibition Testing

The antiproliferative activity of Compound 18 was evaluated in various cancer cell lines, including HER2-overexpressing breast cancer cell lines (SKBR-3, BT-474) and lung cancer cell lines (Calu-3, NCI-H1975). The IC50 values were determined using a luminescence-based cell viability assay (CellTiter-Glo®). Compound 18 exhibited potent antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing cell lines with IC50 values in the low nanomolar range. In contrast, the compound showed minimal activity in cell lines that do not overexpress HER2.

### Example 3. Competitive Binding Assays

Competitive binding assays were performed to demonstrate that Compound 18 binds to the HER2 extracellular domain (ECD). FITC-labeled Compound 5, a known binder to HER2 ECD, was used as a competitor. Varying concentrations of Compound 18 were incubated with HER2-expressing BT-474 cells, and the fluorescence intensity was measured. The results showed a concentration-dependent decrease in fluorescence, indicating that Compound 18 competes with FITC-Compound 5 for binding to HER2 ECD.

### Example 4. Testing Compound 18 Analogs for PPI Inhibition

To investigate the effect of chirality on the biological activity of Compound 18, several analogs were synthesized by altering the chirality of the β-amino acid and the Pro-Pro sequence. The antiproliferative activity of these analogs was evaluated in HER2-overexpressing cell lines. The results showed that the most active compound was the one with the L-Pro-D-Pro sequence and (R) anapa, consistent with the findings for Compound 18.

### Example 5. Dose-Response Curves for Heterodimerization

Dose-response curves were generated to evaluate the ability of Compound 18 to inhibit the heterodimerization of HER2:HER3 and EGFR:HER2. Proximity ligation assays (PLA) and enzyme fragment complementation assays (PathHunter®) were used to assess the inhibition of heterodimerization. The results showed that Compound 18 inhibits the heterodimerization of HER2:HER3 and EGFR:HER2 in a concentration-dependent manner, with significant inhibition observed at concentrations as low as 0.5 µM.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel cyclic peptidomimetic compound, Compound 18, that effectively inhibits the heterodimerization of EGFR and HER2, thereby modulating cell signaling pathways involved in cancer cell growth and proliferation. The compound exhibits potent antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing cancer cell lines and has the potential to be developed as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of cancers characterized by HER2 overexpression. The specific chirality of the β-amino acid and the Pro-Pro sequence is crucial for the biological activity of the compound, highlighting the importance of structural optimization in the design of peptidomimetic inhibitors.
