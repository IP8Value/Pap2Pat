# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of quantum computing has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly with the development of ion trap technology. Ion traps are a promising platform for quantum computing due to their ability to store and manipulate high-fidelity quantum states. Atomic ions, when confined in these traps, provide fundamentally stable qubits, especially when hyperfine levels are utilized. High-fidelity state preparation and detection, as well as logic gates driven by both lasers and microwave fields, are readily available in these systems. However, the practical implementation of ion trap quantum computers faces several challenges, primarily related to the stability and reliability of the experimental setups.

Traditional ion trap experiments often involve complex assemblies of tabletop optical components, which are subject to drift due to temperature changes and require frequent adjustments to maintain optimal alignment. This instability hampers the transformation of ion trapping experiments into reliable quantum computers. To address these issues, a novel system design approach is needed to create a more stable and reliable environment for trapped ions. This invention introduces a compact ultra-high vacuum (UHV) assembly for traps operating at cryogenic temperatures, along with a stable optical system to deliver the necessary laser light for manipulating ion qubits.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a compact ultra-high vacuum (UHV) assembly and a stable optical system for ion trap quantum computing. The UHV assembly, referred to as the "trap cryopackage," is designed to minimize the volume of the UHV environment and contains only the essential components for trapping ions: the ion trap, the atomic source, and an activated carbon getter. The trap cryopackage is cooled to cryogenic temperatures to achieve the pressure levels required for stable ion trap operation.

The invention also includes a modular optical system where each optical function is designed onto a customized breadboard, referred to as an "optical block." These optical blocks are precision-mounted to a predesigned reference point and can be temperature-stabilized to eliminate optical misalignment due to thermal drifts. The modular design allows for each optical block to be tested independently before incorporation into the complete system.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Compact UHV Assembly**: The trap cryopackage is a compact UHV assembly that minimizes the volume of the UHV environment and contains only essential components.
2. **Cryogenic Cooling**: The trap cryopackage is cooled to cryogenic temperatures to achieve the necessary pressure levels for stable ion trap operation.
3. **Modular Optical System**: The optical system is modular, with each function designed onto a customized breadboard (optical block) that can be precision-mounted and temperature-stabilized.
4. **Stable Optical Alignment**: The modular design and temperature stabilization ensure stable optical alignment, reducing the need for frequent adjustments.
5. **Integrated Optomechanical Structure**: The optomechanical structure is tightly integrated into the surrounding system, ensuring mechanical and thermal stability.
