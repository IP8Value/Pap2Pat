# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for detecting residual undifferentiated induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) in differentiated cell populations. More specifically, the invention pertains to the identification and use of marker genes that are highly sensitive and robust for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in cells derived from all three germ layers: endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm.

## BACKGROUND ART

### PRIOR ART LITERATURE

Induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technologies have revolutionized the field of regenerative medicine and therapeutic applications. iPSCs, which can be derived from adult somatic cells, possess the ability to differentiate into any cell type in the human body, making them invaluable for tissue engineering, drug discovery, and personalized medicine. However, the safety of iPSC-derived cells is a critical concern, particularly the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs, which can form teratomas upon transplantation.

Several methods have been explored to detect and eliminate residual undifferentiated iPSCs. These methods include the use of cell surface markers, growth medium modifications, and genetic markers. For instance, LIN28A has been used to detect residual undifferentiated iPSCs in retinal pigment epithelial (RPE) cells. However, LIN28A is not universally suitable for all cell types, as it can be expressed in certain differentiated cells, leading to false positives.

Other approaches, such as the re-seeding method, involve culturing differentiated cells in iPSC maintenance conditions to observe the formation of undifferentiated cell colonies. While effective, these methods can be time-consuming and may not be sensitive enough to detect very low levels of contamination.

### Problem for Solution by the Invention

Despite the progress in iPSC technology, there remains a significant challenge in ensuring the safety of iPSC-derived cells for clinical applications. Specifically, the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations poses a risk of teratoma formation, which can be life-threatening. Current methods for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs are either not universally applicable to all cell types or lack the sensitivity required for clinical use.

Therefore, there is a pressing need for a robust and sensitive method to detect residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations derived from all three germ layers. Such a method would significantly enhance the safety and reliability of iPSC-based therapies, paving the way for broader clinical applications.

### Means to Solve the Problem

The present invention addresses the aforementioned problem by providing a method for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs using novel marker genes. These marker genes are specifically and highly expressed in undifferentiated iPSCs and are downregulated during differentiation, making them ideal for sensitive and reliable detection.

The invention involves the following steps:
1. **Identification of Marker Genes**: Utilizing single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) and quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), we identified several genes that are highly and specifically expressed in undifferentiated iPSCs. These genes include ESRG (Embryonic Stem Cell Related), CNMD (Chondromodulin, also referred to as LECT1), and SFRP2 (Secreted Frizzled Related Protein 2).
2. **Validation of Marker Genes**: We validated the suitability of these marker genes by correlating their expression levels with the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations. The marker genes ESRG and CNMD were found to be highly correlated with the number of residual undifferentiated iPSCs and had low detection limits.
3. **Application to Different Germ Layers**: The marker genes were tested in cells derived from all three germ layers: endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm. The results showed that ESRG and CNMD are robust markers for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in all three germ layers.
4. **Detection Method**: The invention also includes a method for detecting the expression of these marker genes using quantitative PCR (qPCR) and single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH) followed by flow cytometry.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention provides a rapid, sensitive, and robust method for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations. By using the novel marker genes ESRG and CNMD, the invention ensures the safety and reliability of iPSC-derived cells for clinical applications. This method can be applied to cells derived from all three germ layers, making it a versatile tool for a wide range of regenerative medicine and therapeutic applications.

## BEST MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Identification of Marker Genes

To identify marker genes suitable for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs, we utilized single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) and quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qPCR). The criteria for selecting marker genes were:
1. Specific expression in the iPSC stage to exclude genes expressed during directed differentiation.
2. High expression in iPSC to facilitate high-level and sensitive detection even at low levels of undifferentiated iPSC contamination.
3. Considerable downregulation in differentiated cells to ensure a clear distinction between undifferentiated and differentiated cells.

From our scRNAseq data, we identified twelve genes that met these criteria. These genes were further validated using qPCR, and the following genes were selected for further analysis: ESRG, CNMD, SFRP2, SOX2, THY1, USP44, VSNL1, and SPP1. The expression levels of these genes were confirmed to be downregulated significantly following the stimulation of differentiation in several iPSC lines.

### Validation of Marker Genes

To validate the suitability of the selected marker genes, we correlated their expression levels with the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations. We used the re-seeding method, where differentiated cells were cultured in iPSC maintenance conditions to observe the formation of undifferentiated cell colonies. The marker genes ESRG, CNMD, and SFRP2 were found to be well-correlated with the number of residual undifferentiated iPSCs, with detection limits of 0.005%, 0.025%, and 0.025%, respectively. In contrast, SOX2, NANOG, and OCT4 had higher detection limits, and LIN28A was not well-correlated with the number of residual iPSCs.

### Application to Different Germ Layers

To ensure the versatility of the marker genes, we tested their applicability in cells derived from all three germ layers: endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm. The marker genes ESRG and CNMD were downregulated significantly in hepatic endoderm (HE), pancreatic cells, septum transversum mesenchyme (STM)/mesenchymal cells (MC), endothelial cells (EC), neural crest cells, and neural stem cells. No residual undifferentiated iPSCs were detected in these differentiated cell populations using the re-seeding method.

### Detection Method

The invention includes a method for detecting the expression of the marker genes ESRG and CNMD using quantitative PCR (qPCR) and single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH) followed by flow cytometry. The qPCR method involves isolating RNA from the differentiated cell population, converting it to cDNA, and amplifying the target genes using specific primers and probes. The smFISH method involves hybridizing branched DNA probes to the target mRNA molecules and detecting the hybridized probes using fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Identification of Candidate Genes for Detecting Undifferentiated iPSCs

We performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) on iPSCs and differentiated cells to identify genes that are highly and specifically expressed in undifferentiated iPSCs. The scRNAseq data were analyzed to select genes that met the following criteria: specific expression in the iPSC stage, high expression in iPSC, and significant downregulation in differentiated cells. Twelve candidate genes were identified, and their expression levels were confirmed using qPCR. The genes ESRG, CNMD, SFRP2, SOX2, THY1, USP44, VSNL1, and SPP1 were selected for further analysis.

#### Example 2: Validation of Marker Genes in Hepatic Differentiation

We evaluated the expression levels of the candidate genes in hepatic endoderm (HE) cells and correlated them with the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs using the re-seeding method. The marker genes ESRG, CNMD, and SFRP2 were found to be well-correlated with the number of residual undifferentiated iPSCs, with detection limits of 0.005%, 0.025%, and 0.025%, respectively. In contrast, SOX2, NANOG, and OCT4 had higher detection limits, and LIN28A was not well-correlated with the number of residual iPSCs.

#### Example 3: Application to Different Germ Layers

We tested the marker genes ESRG and CNMD in cells derived from all three germ layers: endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm. The marker genes were downregulated significantly in hepatic endoderm (HE), pancreatic cells, septum transversum mesenchyme (STM)/mesenchymal cells (MC), endothelial cells (EC), neural crest cells, and neural stem cells. No residual undifferentiated iPSCs were detected in these differentiated cell populations using the re-seeding method.

#### Example 4: Detection of Residual Undifferentiated iPSCs Using smFISH

We applied the single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH) method to detect the expression of the marker gene ESRG in differentiated cell populations. Branched DNA probes were hybridized to the target mRNA molecules, and the hybridized probes were detected using fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry. The results showed that the smFISH method was effective in detecting ESRG-positive cells in the differentiated cell populations.
