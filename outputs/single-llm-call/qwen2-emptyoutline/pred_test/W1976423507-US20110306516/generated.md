# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION
The present invention relates to the field of stem cell biology and, more specifically, to methods for the rapid and efficient production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and the isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSC colonies. The invention provides a method for purifying iPSCs using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) based on a combination of positive and negative surface markers, thereby enabling the early identification and isolation of fully reprogrammed cells.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
The discovery that differentiated somatic cells can be reprogrammed to an embryonic stem cell-like state by the forced expression of specific transcription factors (Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, cMyc) has revolutionized the field of stem cell biology. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) exhibit remarkable similarities to embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and hold significant promise for in vitro disease modeling, drug discovery, and therapeutic applications. However, the initial derivation of stable iPSC clones through viral transduction of dermal fibroblasts is a slow and inefficient process, typically taking 4-6 weeks with a success rate of ≤0.01%.

Current methods for identifying and isolating colonies of bona fide iPSCs early in the reprogramming process (2-3 weeks post-infection) rely on light microscopy and manual isolation, which require specialized skills and advanced cell culture techniques. There is a critical need for standardized and validated methods to identify, isolate, and purify reprogrammed cells efficiently and reliably. Previous studies have shown that early reprogramming events involve changes in cell proliferation rates and morphology, downregulation of the mesenchymal marker CD13, and upregulation of pluripotency markers SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. However, these markers alone are insufficient to purify fully reprogrammed iPSCs, especially when using integrating vectors for reprogramming.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a method for purifying fully reprogrammed iPSCs using FACS based on a combination of positive and negative surface markers. This method significantly enhances the purity and efficiency of iPSC derivation, making it suitable for clinical and research applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
The present invention provides a method for the rapid and efficient production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and the isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSC colonies. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Reprogramming**: Reprogramming somatic cells, such as dermal fibroblasts, using viral vectors (e.g., retroviral or Sendai viral vectors) to express the transcription factors Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and cMyc.
2. **FACS Sorting**: Enriching for fully reprogrammed iPSCs by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) based on the expression of positive markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60) and the absence of the negative marker CD13.
3. **Culture and Expansion**: Culturing the sorted cells on feeder layers or feeder-free conditions to form iPSC colonies.
4. **Characterization**: Verifying the pluripotency and stability of the derived iPSCs through various assays, including karyotypic analysis, DNA fingerprinting, pluripotent surface marker expression, quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), and in vitro and in vivo differentiation potential.

The invention further includes the use of this method to derive iPSCs from a variety of cell populations, including cells reprogrammed with DNA-integrating or non-integrating viruses, and fibroblasts harvested from healthy or disease patients. The method ensures the early isolation of a highly purified population of fully functional iPSCs, reducing the appearance of transformed cells and improving the overall quality of the derived iPSC lines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies
The method of the present invention involves the rapid and efficient production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and the isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSC colonies. The process begins with the reprogramming of somatic cells, such as dermal fibroblasts, using viral vectors to express the transcription factors Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and cMyc. The reprogramming can be achieved using either DNA-integrating vectors (e.g., retroviral vectors) or non-integrating vectors (e.g., Sendai viral vectors).

Once the cells are infected with the viral vectors, they are cultured under conditions that promote reprogramming. The cells are then subjected to fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to enrich for fully reprogrammed iPSCs. The FACS sorting is based on the expression of positive markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60) and the absence of the negative marker CD13. This combination of markers ensures the isolation of a highly purified population of fully functional iPSCs, free from contaminating non-reprogrammed or partially reprogrammed cells.

The sorted cells are then cultured on feeder layers or feeder-free conditions to form iPSC colonies. The colonies are monitored for their morphology and surface marker expression to ensure they meet the criteria for fully reprogrammed iPSCs. The method allows for the rapid and efficient production of iPSCs, reducing the time required for derivation from weeks to days.

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy
Immunofluorescence and microscopy are essential tools for characterizing the derived iPSCs. The iPSCs are stained with antibodies against pluripotency markers such as SSEA4, TRA-1-60, and Nanog to verify their pluripotent state. The cells are also examined for the absence of the mesenchymal marker CD13, which indicates successful reprogramming. Confocal microscopy is used to visualize the expression of these markers and to assess the morphology of the iPSC colonies.

In addition to immunofluorescence, other microscopy techniques such as phase contrast microscopy and bright-field microscopy are used to monitor the growth and development of the iPSC colonies. These techniques help to ensure that the derived iPSCs are morphologically consistent with normal human embryonic stem cells (hESCs).

### Primers
Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) is used to verify the expression of pluripotency genes and the silencing of viral transgenes in the derived iPSCs. Specific primers are designed to amplify the endogenous genes Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and cMyc, as well as the viral transgenes. The qRT-PCR analysis helps to confirm that the iPSCs have activated the endogenous pluripotency genes and silenced the viral transgenes, indicating successful reprogramming.

The qRT-PCR data are normalized to housekeeping genes to account for variations in RNA input and amplification efficiency.

### Comparison of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors in Reprogramming
The present invention compares the use of retroviral and Sendai viral vectors for reprogramming somatic cells. Retroviral vectors are known to integrate into the host genome, which can lead to insertional mutagenesis and genetic instability. In contrast, Sendai viral vectors are non-integrating, reducing the risk of genetic alterations in the derived iPSCs.

The method of the present invention demonstrates that both retroviral and Sendai viral vectors can be used to reprogram somatic cells, but the efficiency and purity of the derived iPSCs differ. Time course analysis by flow cytometry shows that SSEA4POSTra-1-60POS cells are detectable as early as 7 days post-infection (dpi) and their proportion increases up to 21 dpi. However, the proportion of CD13POSSSEA4POSTra-1-60POS cells decreases more rapidly in retroviral infections compared to Sendai viral infections.

The FACS sorting strategy is optimized to enrich for fully reprogrammed iPSCs by selecting the highest Tra-1-60POS expressing cells within the CD13NEGSSEA4POS population. This strategy effectively removes contaminating partially reprogrammed or transformed cells, regardless of the reprogramming vector used.

The derived iPSCs are characterized using a battery of assays, including karyotypic analysis, DNA fingerprinting, pluripotent surface marker expression, qRT-PCR, and in vitro and in vivo differentiation potential. The results show that the FACS-derived iPSCs are pluripotent and stable, with comparable levels of pluripotency markers and differentiation potential to manually derived iPSCs.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a robust and efficient method for the production and isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSCs, suitable for a wide range of applications in stem cell biology and regenerative medicine.
