## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biochemistry and molecular biology, specifically to engineered transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) monomers that function as dominant negatives to block TGF-β signaling. These monomers are designed to inhibit the signaling pathways of TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, which are involved in various physiological and pathological processes, including cancer progression and fibrosis.

## BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) is a multifunctional cytokine that plays a crucial role in various biological processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Dysregulation of TGF-β signaling is implicated in numerous human diseases, such as cancer, fibrosis, and autoimmune disorders. Despite extensive research, no specific inhibitors of TGF-β signaling have been approved for clinical use due to challenges in achieving high specificity and efficacy.

The TGF-β family consists of three major isoforms: TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3. These isoforms form homodimers that bind to type I and type II receptors (TβRI and TβRII) to initiate signaling cascades. The signaling capacity of TGF-β monomers arises from their ability to non-covalently dimerize and bind their receptors. The structural motif that contributes significantly to the self-association of the monomers is the "heel" α-helix, α-helix 3, which forms a large portion of the binding surface for TβRI.

Previous studies have shown that the elimination of α-helix 3 should interfere with both self-association of the monomers and binding of TβRI, while not impairing TβRII binding. This hypothesis forms the basis for the design of engineered TGF-β monomers that can function as dominant negatives to block TGF-β signaling.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an engineered transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) monomer that functions as a dominant negative to block TGF-β signaling. The engineered monomer lacks the Cys-77 residue and the heel α-helix (α3), which are critical for dimer formation and TβRI binding. The engineered monomer retains high affinity for TβRII, enabling it to bind and block TβRII, thereby inhibiting TGF-β signaling.

In one embodiment, the engineered TGF-β monomer is derived from TGF-β2 and includes seven amino acid substitutions to enhance its affinity for TβRII. The engineered monomer, designated mmTGF-β2-7M, is highly soluble, stable, and effective in inhibiting TGF-β signaling with IC50 values in the range of 20-70 nM.

The invention also encompasses methods for producing the engineered TGF-β monomer, pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered monomer, and methods of using the engineered monomer to treat or prevent diseases associated with TGF-β signaling, such as cancer, fibrosis, and autoimmune disorders.

### II. Terms and Methods

- **Engineered TGF-β Monomer**: A TGF-β monomer that has been modified to lack the Cys-77 residue and the heel α-helix (α3). The monomer may also include additional amino acid substitutions to enhance its affinity for TβRII.
- **Dominant Negative**: A molecule that, when present, interferes with the normal function of a signaling pathway by competing for binding sites or disrupting complex formation.
- **High Affinity Binding**: The ability of a molecule to bind to its target with a high degree of specificity and strength, typically characterized by a low dissociation constant (KD).
- **Solubility**: The ability of a substance to dissolve in a solvent, typically water, at a given concentration and pH.
- **Stability**: The resistance of a molecule to degradation or denaturation under various conditions, such as temperature, pH, and storage time.
- **Pharmaceutical Composition**: A formulation containing the engineered TGF-β monomer and one or more pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, carriers, or adjuvants.

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments

The present invention provides an engineered TGF-β monomer that functions as a dominant negative to block TGF-β signaling. The engineered monomer is derived from TGF-β2 and includes the following features:

1. **Deletion of Cys-77 and α-helix 3**: The Cys-77 residue, which forms the inter-chain disulfide bond, and the heel α-helix (α3) are deleted to prevent dimer formation and TβRI binding.
2. **Seven Amino Acid Substitutions**: Seven amino acid substitutions are introduced to enhance the affinity of the monomer for TβRII. These substitutions include K25R, I92V, N94R, R26K, L89V, T95K, and I98V.
3. **High Solubility and Stability**: The engineered monomer is highly soluble in water at neutral pH and exhibits high intrinsic stability due to the presence of four intramolecular disulfide bonds.
4. **Inhibitory Activity**: The engineered monomer effectively inhibits TGF-β signaling with IC50 values in the range of 20-70 nM.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling

#### Background

The TGF-β family of cytokines plays a critical role in various physiological and pathological processes. Dysregulation of TGF-β signaling is implicated in numerous human diseases, including cancer, fibrosis, and autoimmune disorders. The present example describes the design, production, and characterization of an engineered TGF-β monomer, mmTGF-β2-7M, that functions as a dominant negative to block TGF-β signaling.

#### Discussion

The engineered TGF-β monomer, mmTGF-β2-7M, was designed to lack the Cys-77 residue and the heel α-helix (α3) to prevent dimer formation and TβRI binding. Additionally, seven amino acid substitutions were introduced to enhance the affinity of the monomer for TβRII. The engineered monomer was produced in Escherichia coli and purified to homogeneity. The physical and biochemical properties of mmTGF-β2-7M were characterized using various techniques, including NMR spectroscopy, X-ray crystallography, and surface plasmon resonance (SPR).

The NMR analysis confirmed that mmTGF-β2-7M was natively folded and retained the expected secondary structure, particularly in the TβRII-binding finger region. The three-dimensional structure of mmTGF-β2-7M was determined by X-ray crystallography to a resolution of 2.75 Å, showing that the overall fold was preserved relative to TGF-β2, with the only difference being a slight hinge bending of the monomer.

The SPR experiments demonstrated that mmTGF-β2-7M bound TβRII with high affinity, comparable to that of TGF-β1 and TGF-β3. The engineered monomer was also shown to be highly soluble in water at neutral pH and exhibited high intrinsic stability due to the presence of four intramolecular disulfide bonds.

The inhibitory activity of mmTGF-β2-7M was assessed using a luciferase reporter assay in HEK293 cells stably transfected with a TGF-β-responsive luciferase reporter. The results showed that mmTGF-β2-7M potently inhibited TGF-β signaling with IC50 values in the range of 20-70 nM. The engineered monomer was also shown to function as a competitive inhibitor against TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3, with measured IC50 values within a factor of 2-3 of that measured for TGF-β1.

The findings of this example demonstrate that the engineered TGF-β monomer, mmTGF-β2-7M, functions as a dominant negative to block TGF-β signaling and has several attributes that may overcome limitations encountered with other classes of inhibitors, such as high specificity, small size, and high solubility. These properties make mmTGF-β2-7M a promising therapeutic candidate for the treatment of diseases driven by hyperactive TGF-β signaling.
