## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of virology, specifically to methods and systems for producing infectious hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in non-hepatic mammalian cells. More particularly, the invention provides a method for producing infectious HCV particles of diverse genotypes, including the clinically problematic genotype 1, using a cell line harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon and a plasmid-based system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA.

## BACKGROUND

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) is a major global health problem, infecting approximately 2–3% of the world's population. Chronic HCV infection can lead to serious liver complications, including cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. HCV belongs to the genus Hepacivirus in the family Flaviviridae, and at least six genotypes have been identified. Genotypes 1a and 1b account for more than two-thirds of HCV infections worldwide. Despite significant advances in understanding HCV biology, there is no approved vaccine, and current treatments are less effective against genotype 1 compared to other genotypes.

The limited availability of animal models, such as chimpanzees, and the difficulties in reproducing true HCV infection in small animals have significantly hindered the study of HCV biology. In vitro studies have been marked by the development of subgenomic replicons and full infectious cycles in cell culture, but these models are complicated by the high error rate of the HCV RNA replicase and the emergence of cell-adaptive mutations. These mutations often result in the production of HCV particles with impaired infectivity or fitness in vivo.

Flaviviruses and HCV share common features in their replication and assembly processes. Flaviviruses can produce infectious particles when their structural genes are provided in trans, suggesting that a similar approach might be feasible for HCV. However, attempts to produce infectious HCV particles by providing structural proteins in trans in HCV replication-permissive cell lines have been unsuccessful. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel method for producing infectious HCV particles in non-hepatic mammalian cells.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for producing infectious hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in non-hepatic mammalian cells. The method involves the use of a cell line harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon and a plasmid-based system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA. Specifically, the invention includes the following steps:

1. Providing a non-hepatic mammalian cell line harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon.
2. Transfecting the cell line with a plasmid encoding HCV genomic RNA under the control of a bacteriophage T7 promoter.
3. Transfecting the cell line with a plasmid encoding bacteriophage T7 and SP6 RNA polymerases.
4. Allowing the cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA and the production of HCV structural proteins.
5. Harvesting the infectious HCV particles from the cell culture supernatant.

The method of the present invention allows for the production of infectious HCV particles of diverse genotypes, including the clinically problematic genotype 1, without the occurrence of cell-adaptive mutations. The produced HCV particles are capable of infecting target cells, such as Huh-7.5 cells, and can be used for various applications, including drug screening, vaccine development, and basic research.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

### Overview

The present invention provides a method for producing infectious hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in non-hepatic mammalian cells. The method utilizes a cell line harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon and a plasmid-based system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA. This approach circumvents the limitations associated with existing in vitro systems, such as the high error rate of the HCV RNA replicase and the emergence of cell-adaptive mutations.

### Cell Line and Flavivirus Subgenomic Replicon

The non-hepatic mammalian cell line used in the present invention is preferably a BHK-21 cell line harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon. The flavivirus subgenomic replicon can be derived from West Nile virus (WNV) or another flavivirus, such as dengue virus. The subgenomic replicon is designed to express a reporter gene, such as Renilla luciferase, under the control of the flavivirus RNA-dependent RNA polymerase. The presence of the flavivirus subgenomic replicon in the cell line creates a permissive environment for the production and release of HCV particles.

### Plasmid-Based System for Cytoplasmic Transcription

The plasmid-based system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA includes two components:

1. **Plasmid Encoding HCV Genomic RNA**: This plasmid contains the full-length HCV genomic sequence under the control of a bacteriophage T7 promoter. The 3′ end of the HCV genomic sequence is flanked by a hepatitis delta virus (HDV) antisense ribozyme, which ensures the correct processing of the HCV RNA transcript.
2. **Plasmid Encoding Bacteriophage RNA Polymerases**: This plasmid encodes both bacteriophage T7 and SP6 RNA polymerases. The T7 RNA polymerase drives the high-level cytoplasmic production of uncapped HCV genomic RNA, while the SP6 RNA polymerase is used to amplify the T7 RNA polymerase transcript.

### Transfection and Production of HCV Particles

The BHK-21 cell line harboring the flavivirus subgenomic replicon is transfected with the plasmid encoding HCV genomic RNA and the plasmid encoding bacteriophage RNA polymerases. The co-transfection results in the cytoplasmic co-amplification of both bacteriophage polymerases, leading to the high-level production of HCV genomic RNA. The HCV genomic RNA is translated into HCV structural proteins, which assemble into infectious HCV particles. The particles are released into the cell culture supernatant and can be harvested by ultracentrifugation.

### Characterization of HCV Particles

The harvested HCV particles can be characterized using various techniques, including:

1. **Electron Microscopy**: Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and negative staining electron microscopy (EM) can be used to visualize the morphology of the HCV particles.
2. **Sucrose Density Gradient Centrifugation**: Sucrose density gradient centrifugation can be used to determine the buoyant density of the HCV particles and to separate them from other cellular components.
3. **Immunogold Electron Microscopy**: Immunogold electron microscopy can be used to confirm the presence of HCV structural proteins within the particles.
4. **Infectivity Assays**: The infectivity of the HCV particles can be assessed by incubating them with target cells, such as Huh-7.5 cells, and measuring the accumulation of HCV RNA and proteins.

### Applications

The infectious HCV particles produced by the method of the present invention can be used for various applications, including:

1. **Drug Screening**: The HCV particles can be used to screen for antiviral compounds that inhibit HCV replication and infection.
2. **Vaccine Development**: The HCV particles can be used to develop vaccines against HCV, particularly for the clinically problematic genotype 1.
3. **Basic Research**: The HCV particles can be used to study the mechanisms of HCV entry, replication, assembly, and release in different cell types.

### Advantages

The method of the present invention offers several advantages over existing in vitro systems for producing HCV particles:

1. **Avoidance of Cell-Adaptive Mutations**: The method does not involve HCV RNA replication, thus avoiding the high error rate of the HCV RNA replicase and the emergence of cell-adaptive mutations.
2. **Production of Diverse Genotypes**: The method can be used to produce infectious HCV particles of diverse genotypes, including the clinically problematic genotype 1.
3. **High Yield and Quality**: The method produces HCV particles with high yield and quality, making them suitable for various applications.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

#### Cell Cultures

BHK-21 cells were grown in Eagle's Minimum Essential Medium (E-MEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), GlutaMax-I, and non-essential amino acid mix. BHK-21 cells harboring a WNV lineage II subgenomic replicon encoding Renilla luciferase (BHK-WNV cells) were propagated in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (D-MEM) supplemented with 10% FBS, GlutaMax-I, and 5 µg/mL blasticidin. Huh-7.5 cells and Huh-7.5 cells harboring an HCV subgenomic replicon of genotype 1a (Huh-7.5-SG 1a rep) were maintained as described. HepG2 cells were grown in E-MEM supplemented with 10% FBS, GlutaMax-I, and non-essential amino acid mix. All cells were cultured in an incubator with a 95% air/5% CO2 atmosphere saturated in humidity.

#### Plasmid Constructs

A plasmid-based system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA was designed. The system consists of a set of two plasmids:

1. **pCR-T7p/SP6pol**: This plasmid encodes the bacteriophage SP6 RNA polymerase (SP6pol) gene under the control of the T7 promoter.
2. **pSL-SP6p/T7pol**: This plasmid encodes the bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol) gene under the control of the SP6 promoter.

The plasmid encoding HCV genomic RNA (p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV) contains the full-length HCV genomic sequence of the H77 strain under the control of the T7 promoter and flanked by a hepatitis delta virus (HDV) antisense ribozyme at the 3′ end.

#### Production of HCV Particles

BHK-WNV cells were transfected with the plasmid encoding HCV genomic RNA (p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV) and the plasmid encoding bacteriophage RNA polymerases (pCR-T7p/SP6pol and pSL-SP6p/T7pol) using Lipofectamine LTX and Plus reagent. The culture medium was harvested at day 3, centrifuged at 30,000 × g for 30 minutes at 4°C to remove cell debris, and the clarified supernatants were centrifuged at 100,000 × g for 3 hours at 4°C. The pellets were resuspended in culture medium and filtered through a 0.45 µm PVDF membrane.

#### Electron Microscopy

BHK-WNV cells transfected with the HCVbp-coding plasmid were fixed in 2% glutaraldehyde in 0.1 M sodium cacodylate for 1 hour at room temperature, then overnight at 4°C. The cells were processed for transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and negative staining electron microscopy (EM). The pooled sucrose fractions containing HCVwt were diluted with PBS, pelleted, and analyzed for negative staining EM. Serum from a cured HCV patient previously infected with genotype 1a was used to detect HCVwt in the immuno-EM analysis.

#### Infectivity Assay with HCVbp or HCVwt

The virus-containing supernatant from BHK-WNV cells was clarified at 30,000 × g in a SW28 Beckman rotor for 30 minutes, filtered through a 0.45 µm PVDF membrane, and concentrated (60-fold) with 106 MWCO Vivaspin filters. Huh-7.5 cells were seeded in an 8-well chamber coverglass and incubated with HCVbp for 2 hours at 37°C. After virus inoculum removal, cells were grown for another 48 hours to analyze the expression of HCV NS5A protein by immunofluorescence. To test the infectivity of HCVwt (1a, 1b, and 2a) produced by BHK-WNV cells, HepG2-CD81 cells were seeded on 24-well collagen plates and incubated with particles in the presence or absence of IFN-α and ribavirin. Total RNA was harvested daily and intracellular HCV RNA was measured by RT-Taqman PCR.

### Example 2

#### Production of HCV Bicistronic Particles in BHK-WNV Cells

To produce HCV bicistronic particles (HCVbp), a plasmid encoding HCV 5′-UTR to NS2 sequence upstream of the encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV) IRES of a Huh-7.5 cell-adapted HCV subgenomic replicon of subtype 1a was used. BHK-WNV cells were co-transfected with this plasmid and the p2B system. The formation of large vesicles filled with 50–60 nm particles was observed by transmission electron microscopy. The HCV E1 glycoprotein and other structural proteins were detected across the sucrose density gradient, confirming the production of HCV particles.

### Example 3

#### Infectivity of HCVbp for Huh-7.5 Cells

The infectivity of HCVbp in Huh-7.5 cells was tested using RT-qPCR for the 5′-UTR. The amounts of HCV RNA in target cells increased substantially by day 4 post-infection. The infectious titers of HCVbp in the supernatants of BHK-WNV cells were measured in Huh-7.5 cells, with TCID50 values ranging from 0.6 × 10^4 units/mL at day 3 to 2.5 × 10^5 units/mL at day 4. The infectivity of HCVbp was spread over a broad range of buoyant densities, with a peak of infectivity between 1.08–1.13 g/cm³.

### Example 4

#### Receptors Involved in HCVbp Entry

The entry of HCVbp into target cells was tested using a reporter system. The dependence of HCVrp entry on surface molecules previously implicated as essential entry receptors was assessed by pretreating Huh-7.5 cells with siRNA pools targeting SR-B1, CD81, ASGP-R subunits 1 and 2, and claudin-1. Inhibition of the Firefly luciferase signal generated by HCVrp entry occurred when Huh-7.5 cells were pretreated with these siRNAs, confirming the involvement of these receptors in HCV entry.

### Example 5

#### Production of JFH-1 Strain-Based HCV by BHK-WNV Cells

The production of infectious particles based on the JFH-1 strain was tested by transfecting BHK-WNV cells with plasmids encoding the genomic RNA of JFH-1 or a Con1-JFH1 (1b-2a) chimera. The harvested particles were incubated with Huh-7.5 cells, and the detection of viral RNA in the target cells confirmed the infectivity of the produced HCV particles.

### Example 6

#### BHK-WNV Cells Produce Authentic Infectious HCV

To produce authentic infectious HCV particles, BHK-WNV cells were co-transfected with the p2B system and a plasmid encoding a full-length genomic RNA with the consensus sequence of a strain of genotype 1a (H77). The harvested HCVwt particles were analyzed by sucrose density gradient centrifugation and electron microscopy. The particles were positive by immuno-gold electron microscopy, indicating their recognition by immunoglobulins from an HCV-cured patient.

### Example 7

#### HCV Produced in BHK-WNV Cells Infect HepG2-CD81 Cells

The infectivity of HCVwt produced in BHK-WNV cells was tested in HepG2-CD81 cells. The incubation of HepG2-CD81 cells with HCVwt of subtypes 1a, 1b, and 2a resulted in high readings starting at day 0. Although the detected amounts of HCV RNA sharply decreased during the first 24–48 hours, it increased afterward, confirming the infectivity of the produced HCV particles.

### Example 8

#### Infection of Cultured Human Liver Slices

The infectivity of HCVwt produced in BHK-WNV cells was tested in cultured human liver slices. The liver slices were incubated with HCVwt (H77 strain) or Huh-7.5 cell-produced HCVcc (JFH-1 strain). Specific staining by anti-HCV antibodies was observed within the slices at various locations, confirming the infectivity of the produced HCV particles in a physiologically relevant context.
