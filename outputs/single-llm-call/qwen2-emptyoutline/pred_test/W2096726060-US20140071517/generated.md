## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of optical upconversion, particularly to the synthesis and functionalization of lanthanide-doped sodium yttrium tetrafluoride (NaYF4) nanocrystals. More specifically, the invention pertains to the encapsulation of these nanocrystals with a silica shell and the attachment of infrared-absorbing dyes to the silica-coated nanoparticles to enhance the upconversion efficiency and broaden the spectral range of the upconversion process.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Optical upconversion is a multiphoton process wherein two or more low-energy photons are absorbed and a higher-energy photon is emitted. This phenomenon has significant applications in various fields, including solid-state lasers, luminescent bioassays, next-generation lighting, enhanced photovoltaic response, and imaging technologies. The upconversion process in lanthanide-doped nanocrystals involves three key components: the host material, the sensitizing ion, and the activator ion. The host material provides a low phonon energy environment to minimize nonradiative relaxation, while the sensitizing ion absorbs the incoming radiation and transfers energy to the activator ion, which then emits the upconverted photon.

Despite the potential of lanthanide-doped nanocrystals, their practical application is limited by the narrow absorption cross-section and low molar absorptivity of the lanthanide ions. To overcome these limitations, researchers have introduced organic dyes as optical antennas to capture incoming radiation more efficiently and transfer the energy to the sensitizing ions via Förster Resonance Energy Transfer (FRET). This approach significantly enhances the upconversion efficiency and broadens the spectral range of the upconversion process.

However, the successful implementation of this strategy requires precise control over the synthesis and functionalization of the nanocrystals, as well as the attachment of the dye molecules to the nanoparticle surface. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a method for synthesizing lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanocrystals, encapsulating them with a silica shell, and attaching infrared-absorbing dyes to the silica-coated nanoparticles.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for enhancing the upconversion efficiency and broadening the spectral range of lanthanide-doped sodium yttrium tetrafluoride (NaYF4) nanocrystals. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Synthesis of Lanthanide-Doped NaYF4 Nanocrystals**: Stoichiometric quantities of metal cations (Na+, Y3+, and trivalent lanthanide dopants) are converted to oleate salts and mixed with octadecene solvent and oleic acid. Ammonium fluoride is added to form amorphous sodium yttrium fluoride, which is then converted to hexagonal crystalline form by heating the reaction mixture to greater than 300°C.

2. **Encapsulation of Attached Dye**: The synthesized nanoparticles are functionalized by base-catalyzed hydrolysis and condensation of siloxane monomers containing amine groups. This forms a thin silica shell around the nanoparticles, providing a surface for dye attachment.

3. **Attachment of Dye to Encapsulated Nanoparticle**: Infrared-absorbing dyes with functional groups (carboxylic acid, isothiocyanate, or NHS ester) are attached to the amine-functionalized nanoparticles via covalent bonding, forming a stable dye-nanoparticle composite.

4. **Fabrication of Films**: The dye-nanoparticle composites can be further processed to fabricate films or incorporated into various materials for practical applications.

The invention also encompasses the resulting dye-nanoparticle composites, which exhibit enhanced upconversion efficiency and a broader spectral range compared to unmodified lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanocrystals. These composites have potential applications in areas such as photovoltaic devices, bioimaging, and laser threat detection.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### X=—NH2, —CO2M′, —SH, —OH,

The functional groups on the surface of the nanoparticles play a crucial role in the attachment of the dye molecules. The amine group (—NH2) is particularly important as it serves as a reactive site for the formation of covalent bonds with the dye molecules. Other functional groups, such as carboxylic acid (—CO2M′), thiol (—SH), and hydroxyl (—OH), can also be used to modify the surface of the nanoparticles and facilitate the attachment of the dye molecules.

### M=H2, Cu, Co, CoR3, Ni, SiR3R4, AlR3, Zn, Fe, FeR3, Mn

The choice of metal cations in the synthesis of the lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanocrystals is critical for achieving the desired upconversion properties. Commonly used metal cations include hydrogen (H2), copper (Cu), cobalt (Co), nickel (Ni), silicon (Si), aluminum (Al), zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), and manganese (Mn). The specific combination of metal cations and their ratios can be adjusted to optimize the upconversion efficiency and spectral characteristics of the nanocrystals.

### R4=—R3, —Cl, —CH3, —H, -Ph, —OCH3, —OCH2CH3

The substituents on the siloxane monomers used for the encapsulation of the nanoparticles can be varied to control the properties of the silica shell. Common substituents include alkyl groups (—R3), chlorine (—Cl), methyl (—CH3), hydrogen (—H), phenyl (-Ph), methoxy (—OCH3), and ethoxy (—OCH2CH3). The choice of substituents can affect the thickness and stability of the silica shell, as well as the reactivity of the amine groups on the surface of the nanoparticles.

### M′=K, Li, Na, H

The counterions for the carboxylic acid groups on the dye molecules can be potassium (K), lithium (Li), sodium (Na), or hydrogen (H). The choice of counterion can influence the solubility and reactivity of the dye molecules, which in turn affects the efficiency of the dye attachment process.

## Synthesis of Lanthanide-Doped NaYF4 Nanocrystals

The synthesis of lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanocrystals involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Oleate Salts**: Stoichiometric quantities of the acetate salts of the metal cations (Na+, Y3+, and trivalent lanthanide dopants) are mixed with oleic acid and octadecene solvent in a 50 mL flask. The mixture is heated under vacuum to 120°C to form a clear solution and then cooled to room temperature.

2. **Addition of Fluorine Anion**: Ammonium fluoride is dissolved in methanol and added to the flask, forming a white-colored precipitate. The solution is stirred for 30 minutes and then slowly heated to evaporate the methanol, followed by degassing with nitrogen at 100°C for 10 minutes.

3. **Formation of Hexagonal Crystalline Material**: The solution is heated to boiling at approximately 324°C and maintained for 20 minutes. After cooling, the nanocrystals are precipitated from the solution with ethanol and centrifuged at 4000 RPM. The particles are further purified by two cycles of dispersing the nanoparticles in toluene or cyclohexane, precipitating in a two-fold excess of ethanol, and centrifuging.

### Encapsulation of Attached Dye

The encapsulation of the lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanoparticles with a silica shell involves the following steps:

1. **Surface Silanization**: A volume of 0.7 mL of surfactant (Igepal CO-520) is stirred into 40 mL of cyclohexane. The mixture is added to a solution of 80 mg of lanthanide-doped NaYF4 oleic-acid-coated nanoparticles dispersed in 30 mL of cyclohexane. In a test tube, 0.6 mL of ammonium hydroxide (30% in water) is stirred into 3 mL of Igepal CO-520, and the solution of oleic-acid-capped nanoparticles is added. The resulting clear solution is sonicated for a minimum of 20 minutes to fully create the reverse microemulsion.

2. **Formation of Silica Shell**: 0.05 mMol (50 μL of 0.1 M solution in cyclohexane) of amine-containing siloxane monomer (3-aminopropyl) trimethoxysilane (APS) is added to the reverse microemulsion of nanoparticles and stirred vigorously for two hours to form a thin silica shell. The functionalized, silica-coated nanoparticles are precipitated from the reaction mix with two times excess of ethanol, centrifuged, washed with a 50:50 mixture of deionized water and ethanol, and then centrifuged again to collect the nanoparticles.

### Attachment of Dye to Encapsulated Nanoparticle

The attachment of the dye molecules to the amine-functionalized nanoparticles involves the following steps:

1. **Dye Attachment**: 100 mg of amine-functionalized nanoparticles are dispersed in 10 mL of absolute ethanol and combined with 300 μL of 5 mM dye in ethanol or dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) solution, for an initial dye molecule-to-nanoparticle ratio of approximately 200:1. The reaction mixture is stirred for 20 minutes at room temperature for NHS-functionalized dyes or for 1-2 hours at 35°C for carboxylic-functionalized dyes. The nanoparticle-dye nanocomposite is washed with ethanol until the eluent is clear of nonbound dye.

2. **Optical Characterization**: Upconversion measurements are performed on an Edinburgh Instruments model FS920 fluorimeter equipped with a cooled R928P photomultiplier. The diode laser light sources are single-mode devices purchased from QPhotonics LLC. The laser output is collimated or mildly focused by a C110TME-B aspheric lens from Thorlabs Inc. Spot sizes are measured by a scanning knife-edge technique. The continuous-wave laser power is measured on a Newport Corp. model 1918-C power meter with a model 818-SL calibrated silicon detector. Calculated power densities are in the range of 0.2 to 60 W/cm².

### Fabrication of Films

The dye-nanoparticle composites can be further processed to fabricate films or incorporated into various materials for practical applications. The fabrication of films involves the following steps:

1. **Dispersion of Nanocomposites**: The dye-nanoparticle composites are dispersed in a suitable solvent, such as ethanol or toluene, to achieve a homogeneous suspension.

2. **Film Formation**: The suspension is cast onto a substrate, such as a glass slide or a flexible polymer film, using a spin-coating or doctor-blade technique. The solvent is allowed to evaporate, leaving a thin, uniform film of the dye-nanoparticle composites.

3. **Characterization of Films**: The resulting films are characterized using techniques such as UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy, fluorescence microscopy, and X-ray diffraction to assess their optical and structural properties.

The present invention provides a method for enhancing the upconversion efficiency and broadening the spectral range of lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanocrystals. By encapsulating the nanoparticles with a silica shell and attaching infrared-absorbing dyes, the invention enables the creation of dye-nanoparticle composites with improved performance and potential applications in various fields.
