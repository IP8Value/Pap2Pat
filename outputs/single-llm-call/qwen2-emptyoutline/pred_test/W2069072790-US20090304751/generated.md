# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of mucosal immunology has long sought effective methods to stimulate robust and protective immune responses at mucosal surfaces. Mucosal tissues, such as those lining the respiratory and reproductive tracts, are particularly vulnerable to infections due to their direct exposure to the external environment. Despite significant advances in vaccine technology, there are currently no FDA-approved adjuvants capable of effectively stimulating cell-mediated immune responses within mucosal tissues. This gap in technology is partly due to the hostile nature of mucosal surfaces, which can degrade immunogenic proteins before they can be effectively presented to the immune system.

Vaults, large ribonucleoprotein particles found in nearly all eukaryotic cells, offer a promising solution to this challenge. These particles are composed of multiple copies of three protein species and several copies of a small untranslated RNA. The most abundant protein, the 97 kDa major vault protein (MVP), forms the barrel-shaped structure of the vault particle. The internal cavity of the recombinant vault is large enough to accommodate multiple immunogenic proteins, providing a protected environment for their delivery to dendritic cells and induction of immunity.

The present invention relates to the use of recombinant vault nanoparticles as a novel vaccine platform for generating effective mucosal immune responses. Specifically, the invention involves the encapsulation of immunogenic proteins within vault nanoparticles, which are then administered to mucosal surfaces to induce a robust and protective immune response. The invention addresses the need for a safe and effective method to deliver immunogenic proteins to mucosal tissues, thereby enhancing the body's ability to combat mucosal infections.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for generating mucosal immune responses using recombinant vault nanoparticles. The invention involves the encapsulation of an immunogenic protein within a vault nanoparticle, which is then administered to a mucosal surface to stimulate a mucosal immune response. The vault nanoparticles are designed to protect the immunogenic protein from degradation and to facilitate its delivery to dendritic cells, thereby enhancing the induction of a cell-mediated immune response.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a recombinant vault nanoparticle comprising a major vault protein (MVP) and an immunogenic protein encapsulated within the vault. The immunogenic protein can be any protein capable of inducing an immune response, such as the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia muridarum. The vault nanoparticle is designed to be stable, biocompatible, and capable of being efficiently internalized by dendritic cells.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for inducing a mucosal immune response in a subject. The method involves administering a recombinant vault nanoparticle to a mucosal surface of the subject, wherein the vault nanoparticle comprises an immunogenic protein encapsulated within the vault. The administration can be via intranasal, intravaginal, or other mucosal routes. The method is effective in inducing a strong and specific immune response against the encapsulated immunogenic protein, thereby providing protection against mucosal infections.

The invention also includes a kit for preparing and administering the recombinant vault nanoparticles. The kit can include components such as the recombinant vault nanoparticles, buffers, and instructions for use. The kit is designed to facilitate the preparation and administration of the vault nanoparticles in a clinical setting.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction to Recombinant Vault Nanoparticles

Recombinant vault nanoparticles are large, barrel-shaped structures composed primarily of the major vault protein (MVP). The MVP is capable of self-assembling into vault-like particles when expressed in insect cells using the baculovirus system. The internal cavity of the recombinant vault is large enough to accommodate multiple immunogenic proteins, making it an ideal platform for vaccine delivery. The vault nanoparticles are designed to protect the encapsulated proteins from degradation and to facilitate their delivery to dendritic cells, which are key players in the induction of immune responses.

### Design and Production of Recombinant Vault Nanoparticles

To produce recombinant vault nanoparticles, the MVP is expressed in insect cells using the baculovirus system. The MVP sequence alone encodes all the necessary structural information to form the vault particle. To encapsulate an immunogenic protein, the protein is fused to a targeting domain, such as the minimal interaction domain (mINT) derived from VPARP. The fusion protein is then co-expressed with the MVP in insect cells, allowing the immunogenic protein to be packaged inside the vault nanoparticles.

For example, the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia muridarum can be fused to the mINT domain and co-expressed with the MVP. The resulting vault nanoparticles, referred to as MOMP-vaults, contain the MOMP protein encapsulated within the vault structure. The MOMP-vaults are purified using standard techniques, such as sucrose gradient centrifugation, and resuspended in a suitable buffer for administration.

### Mechanism of Action

Recombinant vault nanoparticles are designed to be internalized by dendritic cells, which are professional antigen-presenting cells. Upon internalization, the vault nanoparticles release the encapsulated immunogenic protein, which is then processed and presented to T cells, leading to the activation of a cell-mediated immune response. The vault nanoparticles are also capable of activating inflammasomes, which are multiprotein complexes that play a crucial role in the maturation and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-1β. This activation of inflammasomes further enhances the immune response by promoting the recruitment of immune cells to the site of infection.

### Advantages of Recombinant Vault Nanoparticles

Recombinant vault nanoparticles offer several advantages over traditional vaccine platforms. First, the vault nanoparticles provide a protected environment for the encapsulated immunogenic protein, preventing its degradation in the hostile mucosal environment. Second, the vault nanoparticles are biocompatible and do not induce excessive inflammation, making them safe for use in mucosal tissues. Third, the vault nanoparticles are capable of activating inflammasomes, which enhances the induction of a cell-mediated immune response. Finally, the vault nanoparticles can be easily produced using the baculovirus system, making them a viable and scalable vaccine delivery platform.

### Administration and Dosage

The recombinant vault nanoparticles can be administered to mucosal surfaces using various routes, including intranasal, intravaginal, and oral. The dosage and frequency of administration can be adjusted based on the specific immunogenic protein and the desired immune response. For example, in a mouse model, intranasal administration of MOMP-vaults at 200 µg per dose, repeated every two weeks for a total of three doses, was effective in inducing a strong and specific immune response against Chlamydia muridarum.

### Examples of Use

#### Example 1: Intranasal Immunization with MOMP-Vaults

In a study involving C57BL/6 mice, MOMP-vaults were administered intranasally at 200 µg per dose, repeated every two weeks for a total of three doses. Two weeks after the last immunization, the mice were challenged intravaginally with 1.5 × 10^5 inclusion-forming units (IFU) of Chlamydia muridarum. The bacterial burden in vaginal swabs was monitored every three days. Mice immunized with MOMP-vaults showed a significant reduction in bacterial burden compared to control mice immunized with GL-vaults. The MOMP-vaults induced a strong and specific immune response, characterized by the production of anti-chlamydial IgG and the recruitment of Th1 cells to the genital tract.

#### Example 2: Dendritic Cell Uptake and Maturation

Immature bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDC) were incubated with MOMP-vaults for 30 minutes to 1 hour at 37°C. The cells were then stained with CD11c-PE, a cell surface marker of murine dendritic cells, and analyzed by flow cytometry. MOMP-vaults were efficiently internalized by the BMDC and induced the secretion of cytokines and chemokines necessary for the production of Th1 cells, including IL-12p70, IL-10, and CXCL10. The MOMP-vaults did not cause the BMDC to secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNFα, IL-6, or CXCL2, indicating that the vault nanoparticles selectively reduce general inflammation.

#### Example 3: Inflammasome Activation

Immature BMDC were incubated with MOMP-vaults for 6 hours, and the levels of IL-1β in the cell supernatants were measured using a Luminex assay. MOMP-vaults significantly increased the levels of IL-1β, indicating activation of the inflammasome. Western blot analysis confirmed that pro-IL-1β was cleaved by active caspase-1, leading to the maturation and secretion of IL-1β. These results demonstrate that MOMP-vaults activate inflammasomes independently of toll-like receptors (TLR), further enhancing the immune response.

#### Example 4: Th1 Cell Immunity in Mucosal Tissues

Mice immunized with MOMP-vaults were challenged intravaginally with Chlamydia muridarum, and the number of CD3+CD4+ cells that secrete IFNγ or IL-4 in the iliac lymph nodes (ILNs) was quantitated. MOMP-vault immunized mice produced a significant number of Th1 cells, which migrated to the ILNs during peak infection and resolution of infection. The MOMP-vaults also induced the production of cytokines and chemokines associated with the recruitment of Th1 cells, such as IL-1α and CXCL10. These findings indicate that MOMP-vaults effectively enhance Th1 cell immunity in mucosal tissues, providing protection against mucosal infections.
