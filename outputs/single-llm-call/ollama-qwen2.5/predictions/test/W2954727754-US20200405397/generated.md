- This paper introduces an AR-assisted 3D visualization system designed to enhance percutaneous cardiac interventions. The system segments the patient's 3D heart model from CT images for procedural planning and uses a Fourier-based method to register intraoperative fluoroscopic images with the 3D model, achieving high accuracy and success rates.
- Compared to feature-based methods like SIFT and SURF, the Fourier-based registration algorithm demonstrates superior performance in terms of both success rate (100% vs. 85.7-91.4%) and accuracy (0.42 mm vs. 0.88 mm). This improvement is attributed to the polar-logarithmic transform used in the frequency domain.
- The system calculates the catheter's 3D position by processing fluoroscopic images from different angles, achieving a mean standard error of 0.30 mm. This precision meets the clinical requirement for transseptal puncture procedures, where an accuracy of around 5 mm is expected.
- Despite its advantages, the system has limitations such as the static nature of the 3D-printed heart model, which does not account for cardiac and respiratory motion. Future work will focus on incorporating gated CT scans and real-time phase matching to improve dynamic rendering and accuracy.
- The AR system provides a more intuitive and user-friendly interface compared to traditional 2D visualization methods, enabling better hand-eye coordination and reducing the need for constant screen switching. This can lead to improved procedural outcomes and a lower learning curve for new techniques.
- The proposed guidance system also offers potential applications in pre-procedural planning, training, and education. It can serve as a platform for residents and fellows to practice complex procedures and provide quantitative comparisons of different PCI methods among interventionalists.
- While the current system is effective for static phantom models, future improvements will address challenges such as real-time tracking with bi-plane C-arms and advanced segmentation techniques for patient-specific fluoroscopic images. These enhancements will further enhance the accuracy and applicability of the AR-assisted visualization system in clinical settings.
