- The paper explores quantum speedup in lattice spin models using a modified parallel classical walk which applies transitions to all spins simultaneously. This contrasts with traditional Metropolis-Hastings where only one spin is updated per step. Numerical simulations show the parallelized version outperforms the original, reaching lower energies faster for equal computational resources.
- The authors construct a quantum walk unitary that implements this parallel transition in O(n) gates, matching the classical complexity. However, the parallel walk violates detailed balance and is not directly reversible, posing challenges for quantization using existing techniques like Szegedy's method which require reversibility.
- To enable a quantum implementation, the paper proposes offline compilation of rotation angles to increase gate times from picoseconds to microseconds, bringing them into a realistic range. Further work is needed on optimizing circuits and understanding the class of problems where super-quadratic speedups can be achieved for practical quantum advantage.
- The authors acknowledge discussions with Jeongwan Haah, Thomas Häner, Matt Hastings, Guang Hao Low and Guillaume Duclos-Cianci. JL received support from FRQNT scholarship programs. Overall, this work makes progress towards implementing more efficient quantum walks on lattice spin models, but faces challenges in quantizing irreversible classical processes.
- In summary, the paper presents a modified parallel walk for lattice spins that outperforms traditional Metropolis-Hastings and could enable practical quantum speedups if efficiently implemented. However, further research is needed to overcome obstacles related to irreversibility and circuit optimization before this approach can be realized on near-term quantum hardware.
