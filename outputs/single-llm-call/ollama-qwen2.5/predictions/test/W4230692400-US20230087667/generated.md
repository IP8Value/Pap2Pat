Billowing out the analysis of CUVA's performance across different initialization strategies and external resources reveals nuanced insights. When varying Gaussian Mixture initializations between HAC and KMeans, HAC consistently delivers superior results. This is likely due to HAC’s hierarchical approach, which better captures the natural clustering structure in the data compared to KMeans' centroid-based method.

The choice of knowledge graph embedding technique also impacts CUVA's performance. Using HolE for embeddings yields better Macro F1 scores than other methods like TransE or DistMult. This is because HolE effectively captures complex relationships and interactions between entities, which is crucial for accurate clustering and disambiguation in the CANONICNELL dataset.

The entity linker (EL) from Stanford CoreNLP significantly boosts CUVA's performance compared to the PPDB 2.0 resource. The EL provides high-quality entity linking, helping CUVA better understand context and resolve ambiguities. In contrast, while PPDB 2.0 offers useful paraphrases, its impact is less pronounced, likely because it primarily aids in capturing synonyms rather than resolving deeper semantic relationships.

Varying the threshold θ E for HAC initialization reveals that a lower θ E (0.2) yields better Macro F1 values on the test fold, despite higher validation performance at θ E = 0.3. This suggests that a more lenient clustering approach during initialization can lead to better generalization and performance on unseen data. However, the improvement is marginal, indicating that CUVA's robustness to small variations in initial conditions.

In summary, while HAC and HolE are optimal choices for initialization and knowledge graph embedding, respectively, the entity linker plays a crucial role in enhancing CUVA's disambiguation capabilities. These findings underscore the importance of carefully selecting and integrating external resources to maximize performance in complex natural language processing tasks.
