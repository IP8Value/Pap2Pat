- The proposed periodic artifact removal algorithm and its initialization counterpart effectively remove stimulation artifacts even with unknown phase shifts and aliasing. This is crucial for real-world applications like DBS where data can be fragmented due to missing segments of varying lengths.
- The primary tuning parameter, \(\hat{K}\) (number of harmonics), is easily interpretable and can be estimated from a spectrogram or using information criteria like AIC/BIC. A slightly higher \(\hat{K}\) often makes minimal difference in results, making it a robust choice for fixed DBS device settings.
- The algorithms' simplicity allows for efficient hardware implementation on DSPs or FPGAs, crucial for real-time applications in embedded closed-loop DBS therapies. This could enable the discovery of biomarkers and improve treatment efficacy by removing stimulation artifacts on-the-fly.
- Numerical examples demonstrate promising results but are not exhaustive. Future studies could explore semi-rectangular waves with infinitely many harmonics, a common feature in real-life DBS artifacts. Additionally, the algorithms can be extended to handle nonuniform sample times and segmentwise mean minimization for more varied applications.
