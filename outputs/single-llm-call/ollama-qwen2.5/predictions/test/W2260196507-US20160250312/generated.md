- **Citation**: Longley, R. J. et al. Comparative assessment of vaccine vectors encoding ten malaria antigens identifies two protective liver-stage candidates. Sci. Rep. 5, 11820; doi: 10.1038/srep11820 (2015).

- **Objective**: The study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of various vaccine vectors encoding ten different malaria antigens to identify those that could protect against liver-stage malaria infection. This was achieved through a comprehensive comparison of immune responses and protective outcomes in mice.

- **Methods**: Chimeric P. berghei parasites expressing P. falciparum antigens were used to challenge vaccinated mice, assessing both cellular and humoral immune responses. Intracellular cytokine staining (ICS), ELISpot assays, and LIPS assays were employed to measure T-cell and antibody responses, respectively.

- **Results**: Two liver-stage vaccine candidates, PfLSA1 and PfLSA3, were identified as protective against malaria infection. These vaccines induced robust CD8+ T-cell responses and delayed the onset of blood-stage parasitemia in challenged mice, demonstrating their potential for further development.

- **T-Cell Depletion**: To confirm the role of T cells in protection, subsets of T cells were depleted using monoclonal antibodies. Depletion of CD8+ T cells abolished the protective effect, highlighting the critical role of these cells in mediating immunity against liver-stage malaria.

- **Statistical Analysis**: Survival and median delay to infection were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves and Log-Rank tests, respectively. Significant differences were observed between vaccinated and control groups, validating the protective efficacy of the identified vaccine candidates.

- **Conclusion**: The study successfully identified PfLSA1 and PfLSA3 as promising liver-stage malaria vaccine candidates. These findings provide a strong foundation for further preclinical and clinical development of these vaccines to combat malaria.
