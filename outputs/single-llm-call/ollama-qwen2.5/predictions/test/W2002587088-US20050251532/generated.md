- The proposed content-adaptive analysis and representation framework aims to discover "interesting" sparse events from unscripted multimedia by segmenting audio into inliers (usual background) and outliers (unusual events). It uses eigenvector analysis of an affinity matrix derived from statistical models of time series subsequences. Experiments on 12 hours of sports audio across genres showed effective extraction of unusual events, though not all outliers were highlights.
- Three types of time series were used for event discovery: low-level features, frame-level classification labels, and one-second level labels. Low-level features provided a systematic way to characterize domains without prior knowledge. Frame/second-level labels required domain-specific classes but could improve precision. The choice between them depended on the application and available domain expertise.
- For surveillance audio, the framework was applied to elevator and traffic intersection recordings with no a priori knowledge of relevant sound classes. It identified key event-related sounds like banging and excited speech for elevators, and ambulance sirens/crashes for intersections. This demonstrated the ability to systematically identify distinguishable sound classes indicative of events without needing domain-specific processing upfront.
- The framework's computational complexity was analyzed, with the main bottlenecks being affinity matrix computation (O(N^2)) and eigenvector calculation. Future work will focus on reducing this while allowing graceful performance degradation. Overall, the approach provided an inlier/outlier segmentation that could be used to systematically choose key audio classes for supervised models, enabling event detection even in unfamiliar domains where all sound types cannot be anticipated.
- In conclusion, this framework offers a flexible and systematic method for discovering sparse "interesting" events from unscripted multimedia by postponing domain-specific processing as late as possible. It can characterize new domains, identify relevant sounds, and enable supervised modeling of key event classes without requiring extensive prior knowledge. This makes it well-suited for less understood or unconstrained scenarios like surveillance where all sound types cannot be anticipated in advance.
