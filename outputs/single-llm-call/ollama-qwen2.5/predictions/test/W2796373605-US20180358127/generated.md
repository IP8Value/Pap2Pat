- **Study Overview**: This study investigates whether erythrocyte fatty acid profiles can serve as biomarkers to distinguish between children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and neurotypical (NEU) controls. With a larger sample size and matched age and gender, the research aims to evaluate individual-level differences in fatty acids.

- **Methodology**: The study included all variables from univariate analysis in multivariate classification using FDA. PDFs of the FDA scores showed modest improvement over univariate results but did not achieve sufficient diagnostic accuracy for practical use.

- **Regression Analysis**: Regression of red blood cell fatty acids against seafood consumption revealed that increased seafood intake correlated with higher DHA and EPA levels in both ASD and NEU groups, supporting dietary links to these fatty acids.

- **Discussion**: The study's findings suggest that erythrocyte fatty acid profiles are not useful biomarkers for predicting ASD status. While population-level differences may be statistically significant, they do not translate to effective individual-level classification, highlighting the need for more robust biomarker research methods in ASD.

- **Conclusion**: Fatty acid profiles show no significant differences between ASD and NEU individuals, indicating limited potential as diagnostic biomarkers. The study emphasizes the importance of sharing raw data and embracing negative results to advance biomarker research in ASD and improve clinical outcomes.
