- IL/acid mixtures were explored as an alternative to 30 wt% KOH in Ni/MH batteries. Conductivity and viscosity improved with acetic acid addition to ILs. [DEMA][TfO] with 6 M acetic acid showed a conductivity of 14.3 mS•cm−1, compared to pure [DEMA][TfO]'s 9.0 mS•cm−1.

- Among eight screened ILs, acetic acid/[EMIM][Ac] performed best, demonstrating long cycle life and stable discharge voltage plateau at ~1.2 V. An AB5 anode in this electrolyte achieved a specific capacity of 161.9 mAh•g−1 with low charge/discharge overpotentials.

- CV measurements revealed a 0.6 V gap between the hydrogen cathodic peak and reduction edge, indicating potential for redox couples with higher potentials than those used in aqueous systems. This could significantly boost energy density of proton-conducting batteries.

- Challenges included lower ionic conductivity compared to KOH, absence of stable SEI, residual water leading to low coulombic efficiency, and instability of Ni(OH)2 cathode in weakly acidic environment. Ongoing research aims to address these issues for improved performance.
  
- The study concluded that IL/acid mixtures offer promising proton-conducting properties with potential for higher energy density through use of high-potential redox couples. However, further optimization is needed to overcome current limitations and fully realize the benefits.
