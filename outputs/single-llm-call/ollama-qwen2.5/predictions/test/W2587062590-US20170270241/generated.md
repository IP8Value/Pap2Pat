- Dpath is a software tool that uses single-cell transcriptome analysis to reveal hierarchical haemato-endothelial lineages of Etv2 progenitors. It employs advanced algorithms like weighted Poisson-based non-negative matrix factorization (wp-NMF) and random walk with restart (RWR) for cell clustering, metacell construction, and gene prioritization.
- The wp-NMF method decomposes the expression matrix into metagene basis and coefficients while accounting for dropout events. This allows Dpath to identify biologically meaningful metagenes that represent key cellular states or processes during lineage development.
- To cluster cells, Dpath maps them onto a self-organizing map (SOM) using mean metagene expression profiles. The SOM is then partitioned into metacells representing distinct cellular states. A directed graph captures the hierarchical relationships between these metacells based on metagene entropy.
- For gene prioritization, Dpath performs an RWR starting from specified cellular states (progenitor or committed). Genes are ranked by their correlation with steady-state probabilities of metacells. This identifies genes differentially expressed in key cell types and involved in lineage specification or maintenance processes.
- The authors validate Dpath using simulated single-cell RNA-seq data and real data from Etv2+ progenitors. They show it outperforms existing methods for clustering, gene prioritization, and revealing hierarchical relationships between cellular states. The findings provide novel insights into haemato-endothelial lineage development.
- Dpath is implemented as an R package available to the research community. It represents a powerful tool for analyzing single-cell transcriptomic data and uncovering complex cell hierarchies in developmental biology and other fields. The authors demonstrate its utility by revealing key aspects of Etv2+ progenitor differentiation into hematopoietic and endothelial lineages.
- In summary, Dpath offers an innovative computational framework to dissect cellular heterogeneity and lineage relationships from single-cell data. Its ability to account for technical noise and capture biological signals makes it a valuable resource for researchers studying cell fate decisions and tissue development processes.
