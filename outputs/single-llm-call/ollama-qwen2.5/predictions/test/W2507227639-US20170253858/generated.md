- The study utilized 3D adult cardiac extracellular matrix (ECM) to promote iPSC-derived cardiomyocyte (CM) maturation. Results showed that the age of ECM (fetal vs. adult) influenced mechanical properties and fiber architecture, affecting CM gene and protein expression.
- After 7 days in 3D adult cardiac ECM hydrogels, iPSC-derived CMs exhibited increased calcium-handling gene expression compared to 2D cultures. Calcium signaling was also enhanced in 3D, consistent with observed gene and protein changes.
- The 3D adult cardiac ECM better mimics the in vivo environment than traditional 2D or non-native matrices, promoting CM maturation for potential therapeutic applications in heart disease treatment.
- This work advances strategies to mature iPSC-derived CMs by using complex 3D scaffolds that provide biomechanical cues and a more physiologically relevant culture system. The authors thank collaborators and acknowledge funding from NIH, NSF, CIRM, and UC Irvine facilities.
