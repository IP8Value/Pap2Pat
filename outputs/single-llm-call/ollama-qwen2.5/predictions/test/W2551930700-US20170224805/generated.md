- The study utilized Radio-Controlled Capsule (RCC) technology to deliver an oral adenoviral influenza vaccine directly to the human ileum or jejunum. This approach allowed for a detailed examination of mucosal immune responses in humans, providing valuable insights into the biological performance of the vaccine and guiding the development of an effective enteric-coated tablet format.
- Higher responder rates and HA-specific ASC numbers were observed when the rAd oral vaccine was released into the ileum compared to the jejunum. This suggests that the ileum may be more efficient for immune induction, possibly due to its denser lymphoid clusters and prevalence of Peyer's patches (PPs).
- The study found significant increases in α4(high)β7(high) B cells in peripheral blood post-immunization, indicating a strong intestinal homing response. These cells co-expressed CCR9, suggesting they are likely to be recruited to the small intestinal lamina propria. This finding is consistent with the role of retinoic acid-producing dendritic cells in driving α4β7 and CCR9 expression.
- Fecal and nasal samples showed detectable increases in HA-specific IgA in several subjects post-immunization, though background levels were high due to prior influenza exposure. The study also identified a population of α4+β7(negative)/α4+β1+ B cells with the potential to home to the nose and bronchi, contributing to mucosal immunity.
- The rAd oral vaccine generated both pre-plasma and memory mucosal homing B cells, including α4(high)β7(high)CD27(intermediate) cells. This finding is significant as it suggests the vaccine may induce robust mucosal immune responses that could protect against pathogens infecting the mucosa, highlighting the potential of this platform for future vaccine development.
- The study's results underscore the importance of mucosal immunity in vaccine efficacy, particularly for pathogens that infect and proliferate in mucosal tissues. The data from human challenge studies with RSV and norovirus support the role of IgA responses in protection, emphasizing the value of developing vaccines that can effectively induce both systemic and mucosal immune responses.
- In summary, this study demonstrates that oral delivery of an adenoviral influenza vaccine to the ileum or jejunum can generate robust systemic and mucosal immune responses. The findings provide a foundation for further development of enteric-coated tablets and highlight the potential of this approach for enhancing protection against mucosally transmitted pathogens.
