- This study identified 76 miRNAs with altered expression in women with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. Four specific miRNAs (miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-223, and miR-652) were significantly dysregulated in circulation.

- The researchers used artificial neural networks (ANN) for data mining of microarray results. This approach was chosen due to its ability to handle non-linearity and complexity while identifying biologically relevant biomarkers with good predictive performance.

- Validation by RT-qPCR confirmed miR-181a and miR-652 were downregulated in both circulation and tumor tissue, while miR-29a was only dysregulated in blood. This suggests circulating profiles may not directly reflect tumor activity.

- The 3-miRNA panel (miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-652) provided a sensitivity of 74% and specificity of 70%, exceeding some current clinical biomarkers. However, larger validation studies are needed to confirm these results.

- The mechanisms by which tumor-associated miRNAs enter circulation remain unclear. They may be actively secreted or passively released from dying cells. Once in blood, they can associate with AGO proteins or microvesicles for protection and transport.

- Despite challenges like lack of standardization in quantification methods, circulating miRNAs show promise as non-invasive biomarkers for breast cancer diagnosis when used in combination with imaging modalities like mammography. However, further research is needed to elucidate their functional roles and mechanisms of action.

- The potential applications of these tumor-associated miRNAs extend beyond diagnostics to therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring depleted expression levels. This could provide new avenues for halting or reversing tumorigenesis in breast cancer patients.

- In conclusion, this study presents a promising 3-miRNA signature with the potential to improve early detection and outcomes for Luminal A-like breast cancer when used as an adjunct to current screening methods. However, large prospective studies are needed before clinical implementation can be considered.
