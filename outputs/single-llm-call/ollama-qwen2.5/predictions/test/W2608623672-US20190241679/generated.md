- This study provides an extensive characterization of a fractionative lignocellulose pretreatment using low-cost triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate [TEA][HSO₄]. The process was optimized for both pulp saccharification and lignin recovery, achieving up to 80% glucose yield and over 85% delignification.
- At mild temperatures (120 °C), the optimal pretreatment time was around 8 hours with fresh IL. Adding excess acid shifted this optimum to about 2 hours. The process effectively cleaved up to 80% of lignin ether bonds, with harsher conditions leading to lignin condensation and reduced recovery.
- Hemicellulose was primarily solubilized as monomers, converting into furfural and other degradation products. These products likely contributed to the formation of pseudolignin in the precipitate fraction. While some HMF production occurred, it did not significantly affect glucose retention in the cellulose-rich pulp.
- The [TEA][HSO₄] solution could be reused multiple times with excellent recovery rates (>99%), attributed to its non-volatility. Recycling maintained high lignin extraction and saccharification yields. No significant build-up of lignin fragments or inorganic salts was observed, indicating the solution's self-cleaning properties.
- Technoeconomic analysis suggested that capital and operating costs for this process would be lower than those for dilute acid pretreatment. Key areas for future development include optimizing pretreatment temperature, solid loadings, hemicellulose recovery, upscaling, and heat integration. The ionoSolv pretreatment system shows strong potential for industrial scale-up due to its high efficiency and low solvent cost.
