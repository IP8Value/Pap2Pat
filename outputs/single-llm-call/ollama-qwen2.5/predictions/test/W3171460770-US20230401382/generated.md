- This paper highlights the importance of dynamic modeling for continuously evolving content. It proposes a method to handle out-of-vocabulary (OOV) tokens and sub-optimal tokenization through an algorithm that updates vocabulary dynamically. Three sampling methods are introduced for incremental training using hard examples, which were found effective in experiments on two classification tasks.
- The authors recommend the "Token MLM Loss" sampling method due to its ability to integrate into online model serving for real-time monitoring of evolving content. This approach can automatically trigger incremental training when overall loss increases significantly, offering flexibility and timeliness compared to fixed interval updates.
- Experiments were conducted using Twitter data from 2013-2019 on two tasks: Country Hashtag Prediction (2014, 2017) and OffensEval 2019. Results show that incremental training with proposed sampling methods outperformed random sampling, demonstrating the importance of timely content in BERT model training for evolving data.
- The dynamic vocabulary update algorithm was applied to both base and incremental models. It involves analyzing token frequency shifts between consecutive years, updating the vocabulary by adding new popular tokens while removing less frequent ones. This ensures the model remains relevant to current language usage patterns over time.
- For pre-processing, Twitter texts were lowercased, and URLs, user mentions, emails replaced with special tokens. The maximum sequence length was set at 32 wordpiece tokens due to tweets' short nature. Pre-training used a batch size of 256 and learning rate of 1.5e-4; fine-tuning had a smaller batch size (32) and lower learning rate (5e-8).
- Sampling weights for incremental training were computed using a linear combination of the signal from the chosen method (e.g., MLM loss), normalized tweet length, and a random component. This approach balances the influence of hard examples with diversity in sampling to ensure robust model updates.
- The Country Hashtag Prediction task involved 16 popular country hashtags and associated tweets. Data was collected for two years: 2014 (472K tweets) and 2017 (407K tweets). Tweets were preprocessed by removing country hashtags and names, then split into training, development, and test sets in a 70%-15%-15% ratio.
- Results showed significant performance gains using incremental models over base models trained on previous year's data. For instance, "Token Embedding" method improved Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 by 5.4% and 10.6%, respectively, compared to weighted sampling baseline. The effectiveness of proposed methods was consistent across both tasks, despite varying levels of semantic shift between years.
- Training cost savings were substantial with the incremental training approach. Compared to retraining a base model from scratch (2.6M steps), incremental training required only 600k additional steps, resulting in a 76.9% reduction in computational cost. This efficiency is crucial for maintaining up-to-date models on evolving content.
- Future work includes exploring automatic incremental training and investigating model performance over longer periods with diverse types of social media content. The authors also plan to improve hashtag handling by preserving more hashtag information in dynamic language models, addressing limitations observed in current experiments.
