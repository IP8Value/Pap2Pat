- Dye-induced upconversion was demonstrated for two nanocomposite systems with different optical antennae and lanthanide-based pathways. Infrared dyes acted as efficient light-harvesting antennae for the upconversion process in lanthanide-doped NaYF4 nanoparticles, significantly enhancing the infrared absorption bandwidth and upconversion efficiency.
- The study used four discrete diode lasers to excite dye-attached nanoparticles at specific wavelengths where the dye absorbs but the nanoparticle does not. This demonstrated that energy transfer from the dye is the source of the upconverted signal, with a two-photon power dependence observed for both direct atomic and dye-induced upconversion.
- Practical applications include extending silicon solar cell wavelength range and providing visible laser threat warnings. The technique allows discrimination of different infrared bands by pairing dyes with specific absorption spectra to nanoparticles with distinct activation wavelengths and visibly different emissions.
