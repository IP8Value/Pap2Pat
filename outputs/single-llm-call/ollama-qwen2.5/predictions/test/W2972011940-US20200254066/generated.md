- The study aimed to reverse aspects of aging by combining recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH), dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin. It was conducted on a small group of volunteers over 12 months, with follow-up at 18 months for the first cohort.
- rhGH was administered to induce initial insulin response, while DHEA was added to suppress insulin. Metformin was introduced to optimize IGF-1 levels and minimize insulin. Doses were adjusted based on individual responses throughout the trial.
- MRI scans were used to quantify thymic fat content, providing a measure of thymus involution. The computational methodology applied for this purpose had previously been validated in bone marrow fat quantification, ensuring accurate results.
- Cryopreserved peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were analyzed using CyTOF technology to assess immune cell subsets. This involved staining with heavy metal isotope-labeled antibodies and detailed gating strategies to identify specific cell populations accurately.
- Epigenetic age was determined using four different DNA methylation clocks: Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, and GrimAge. These clocks were chosen based on their predictive power for various aging-related outcomes, providing a comprehensive assessment of biological aging.
- Linear mixed-effects regression models were used to analyze the longitudinal data, accounting for multiple measurements from each participant. Paired t-tests or appropriate alternatives were employed for specific endpoint comparisons, with results normalized to baseline values for consistent interpretation.
