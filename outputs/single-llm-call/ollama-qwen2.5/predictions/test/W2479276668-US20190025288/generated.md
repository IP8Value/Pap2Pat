- The described in vitro assay captures the hepatotoxic potential of single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) using primary mouse and human hepatocytes. This novel approach allows early detection of toxic molecules before initiating in vivo studies, potentially saving time and resources.

- A balanced set of SSO reference molecules with documented in vivo liver toxicity was used to develop the assay. Key cellular markers such as LDH, GSH, ATP, caspase-3/7 activity, cytokine release, and miR122 levels were measured over time and at different concentrations to establish a correlation with in vivo toxicity.

- The assay revealed that SSO-induced hepatotoxicity involves early activation of apoptosis, leading to cell death. Unlike previous reports using non-primary cells, the caspase-3/7 activation data here aligned well with observed in vivo hepatotoxicity, providing valuable mechanistic insights.

- Non-parenchymal cells (NPCs) are known to take up SSOs preferentially in the liver, but the assay showed that hepatocytes alone were equally sensitive to detecting toxic SSOs. This suggests a direct effect of SSOs on hepatocytes, not just an NPC-mediated response, contributing to overall hepatic toxicity.

- The established hepatocellular damage marker miR122 was increased in both hepatocyte mono-cultures and co-cultures with NPCs, further supporting the idea that SSO-induced liver injury affects multiple cell types. This finding is crucial for understanding the broader mechanisms of SSO hepatotoxicity.

- A human-relevant in vitro model using primary human hepatocytes was also developed, showing a good correlation between mouse and human hepatocyte toxicity for most tested SSOs. This cross-species comparison enhances the predictive value of the assay for clinical outcomes.

- The human hepatocyte model accurately reflected the known liver profiles of two historical SSOs that caused significant liver enzyme elevations in phase 1 clinical trials. This validation underscores the reliability and translational potential of the human in vitro system.

- Incorporating both rodent and human in vitro hepatotoxicity testing using primary hepatocytes can improve the prioritization of safer SSO candidates for further development. This approach addresses the critical need for better translation from preclinical to clinical settings, ultimately leading to more effective and safer drug candidates.
