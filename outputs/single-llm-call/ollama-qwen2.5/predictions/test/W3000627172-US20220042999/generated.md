- **Study Overview**: This study introduces a quantitative centrosomal phenotyping methodology (CAS) for predicting 10-year local recurrence (LR) risk in ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). CAS outperforms the Van Nuys score and Oncotype Dx DCIS score, providing higher concordance and better stratification of high-risk patients. The study includes two cohorts: Discovery Cohort (DC) and Validation Cohort (VC).

- **CAS Calculation**: CAS is derived from numerical and structural centrosomal abnormalities (iCTRs and mCTRs). Numerical CA (CASi) measures the severity and frequency of extra iCTRs, while structural CA (CASm) assesses the volume of enlarged mCTRs. Both components are equally weighted in the final CAStotal score.

- **Patient Cohorts**: The DC includes 154 patients with pure or mixed DCIS, while the VC comprises 137 patients. The DC has more high-grade cases and invasive recurrences, leading to higher CAS performance metrics compared to the VC, which has a balanced distribution of grades.

- **Clinical Application**: CAStotal can stratify patients into high and low-risk categories, guiding therapeutic decisions. It identifies those who may benefit from additional radiotherapy (RT) after breast-conserving surgery (BCS) or mastectomy, reducing unnecessary treatments and improving patient outcomes.

- **Comparison with VN Score**: Unlike the Van Nuys score, which relies on clinical and pathological factors, CAS provides a biological measure of centrosomal abnormalities. In multivariate analysis, CAStotal outperforms the VN score in predicting LR, particularly in high-risk subsets like patients treated with BCS alone or those receiving adjuvant RT.

- **Comparison with Oncotype Dx DCIS Score**: The Oncotype Dx DCIS score is limited to specific patient subgroups, whereas CAS is broadly applicable. CAS captures a broader range of biological pathways, including proliferation-related signals, and can be used for risk profiling in core biopsies, potentially reducing re-excisions.

- **Limitations**: The study has imbalances in patient numbers and grades between the DC and VC, leading to slightly different performance metrics. Additionally, receptor status data are missing, and the cohort does not include patients treated with endocrine therapy. External validation and mechanistic studies are needed to further assess CAS's utility.

- **Future Directions**: Future research should validate CAS in external cohorts and explore its role in tumor evolution from atypical hyperplasia to invasive disease. Understanding the molecular mechanisms of CA-associated proteins in DCIS progression could provide new therapeutic targets.

- **Supplementary Methods**: The calculation of CASi and CASm involves detailed mathematical formulas. CASi measures the average excess number of iCTRs and their frequency, while CASm assesses the volume deviation of mCTRs from normal values, scaled to ensure equal contribution to CAStotal.

- **Descriptive Statistics**: Tables provide descriptive statistics for clinicopathological characteristics based on recurrence status in both cohorts. Chi-square tests were used to determine significant differences in proportions between DCIS and invasive recurrences.

- **Bootstrap Analysis**: Univariate and multivariate bootstrap analyses show that 1000 hazard ratios are nearly normally distributed, validating the robustness of CAStotal as a predictive marker for LR in DCIS patients.
