### Synthesis and Design

In this study, a new group of analogues was synthesized based on the previously described branched H-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg-OH sequence, which is a potent ligand of the NRP-1 receptor. The main chain and the branched chain were extended with Cys-Asp or Cys fragments to explore their effects on receptor affinity.

### Affinity Enhancement

The addition of the Cys-Asp fragment at the N-terminus of the main or branched chain (α-amino group of Lys or hArg) decreased the affinity of peptidomimetics by approximately two-fold. However, elongation with a single Cys residue resulted in a significant 50- to 70-fold decrease in IC50 values for peptides 3 and 4, respectively.

### Enzymatic Stability

Enzymatic stability studies in human plasma showed that the new analogues were relatively resistant to proteolytic enzymes. After 24 hours, about 50% of each compound's initial concentration remained. Peptide 4, with Cys attached to the side chain, exhibited slower degradation compared to peptide 3.

### Literature-Based Analogues

Extended (Cys) analogues of several potent NRP-1 ligands from the literature were synthesized. The addition of Cys significantly enhanced inhibitory properties in all cases. Notably, the DKPRR peptide derived from VEGF-A165 showed a remarkable improvement, suggesting that extending the K/RXXK/R motif with Cys can enhance affinity to NRP-1.

### CysR Rule

The significant increase in inhibitory activity after adding Cys at the N-terminus may be due to additional interactions through the SH group. This enhancement could be a more general rule, named CysR, complementing the CendR rule. Further studies are needed to understand the role of Cys in ligand-protein interactions.

### Future Directions

The presented peptides have potential applications in drug design. The next step will involve determining the importance of the thiol group in ligand/NRP-1 interaction and optimizing the sulfur-containing side chain length. In vitro experiments to prove that ligands bind to NRP-1 on cell surfaces will be crucial for advancing these findings.
