- **Introduction**: This study investigates hydrogen (H2) as a potential treatment to improve sperm motility, focusing on its effects on damaged and low-motility sperm. H2 is known for its antioxidant properties, which could be beneficial in male infertility treatments.

- **Methods**: Sperm suspensions were left at room temperature for over 5 days to ensure they were highly damaged. The study compared the effects of H2 treatment with nitrogen (N2) and assessed changes in sperm motility, survival rates, and mitochondrial function.

- **Results - Initial Effects on Motility**: H2 treatment significantly increased forward sperm motility from 3.6% to 16.8%, comparable to or better than previous treatments like platelet-activating factor and exogenous pyruvate. The effect was observed within 30 minutes, suggesting a rapid stimulatory action.

- **Results - Effects on High Survival Rate Sperm**: In sperm suspensions with higher survival rates (16 out of 17), H2 treatment consistently increased forward motility. This indicates that H2 is effective in activating sperm, even under varying physiological conditions and genetic backgrounds.

- **Results - Effects on Low Survival Rate Sperm**: While H2 was less effective on nearly necrozoospermic samples, it still stimulated very sluggish motility in 3 out of 13 suspensions. This suggests that H2 can benefit both high and low survival rate sperm.

- **Results - Swimming Speed**: H2 did not enhance sperm swimming speed, indicating that the treatment does not hyperactivate sperm. This is consistent with other stimulatory reagents like caffeine, which do not affect velocity but stimulate motility.

- **Results - Motility Maintenance**: The increased motility induced by H2 was maintained for 2.5 hours, even in the absence of H2. A second H2 treatment the following day further increased motility, suggesting that repeated treatments could be beneficial in clinical settings.

- **Results - Mitochondrial Function**: H2 treatment enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential, which is crucial for sperm motility and ATP production. This supports the hypothesis that H2 improves sperm function by enhancing mitochondrial activity.

- **Results - Frozen-Thawed Sperm**: H2 treatment was effective on frozen-thawed sperm suspensions, particularly those with low initial motility. Treatment with 50% H2 significantly increased forward motility in 13 out of 15 samples, indicating potential clinical applications for asthenozoospermia.

- **Discussion - Mechanism**: The stimulatory effects of H2 on sperm motility may be due to its role as a weak scavenger of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which can damage sperm. By enhancing mitochondrial function and ATP production, H2 could improve sperm viability and motility without hyperactivating them.

- **Discussion - Clinical Implications**: The findings suggest that H2 treatment could be a promising approach for male infertility, especially in cases of asthenozoospermia. Further research is needed to assess the effects on fertilization rates, embryonic development, and safety in assisted reproductive techniques like IVF and ICSI.

- **Conclusion**: This study demonstrates that H2 treatment can activate low motility sperm, highlighting its potential as a novel therapeutic agent for male infertility. Future studies should focus on the practical applications and long-term safety of H2 in clinical settings.
