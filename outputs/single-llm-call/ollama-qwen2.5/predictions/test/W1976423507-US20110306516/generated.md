- The study validates a surface marker profile (CD13NEGSSEA4POSTra-1-60POS) for isolating early reprogrammed iPSCs via FACS after retroviral or Sendai viral 4-factor reprogramming. This approach yields high-quality, pure iPSC lines by removing non-transduced and transformed fibroblasts as early as 7 dpi.

- Using this method, the researchers generated and characterized 228 individual iPSC lines over two years from 76 fibroblast lines sourced from various individuals with healthy or diseased conditions. The FACS strategy allows for rapid isolation of fully functional iPSCs free from contamination by non-reprogrammed cells.

- FACS sorting of CD13NEGSSEA4POSTra-1-60POS cells produces independent, stable clonal cultures that maintain pluripotent marker expression over multiple passages and after adapting to feeder-free conditions. This was demonstrated for both retroviral and Sendai viral reprogramming methods.

- The derived iPSC lines exhibit proper in vitro differentiation potential across all three germ layers when assessed by EB formation and gene expression analysis. In vivo teratoma assays also confirmed the ability of FACS-derived iPSCs to form tissues from all three germ layers, validating their pluripotency.

- FACS isolation provides a significant advantage over manual picking by generating pure cultures without contaminating CD13-positive cells that can persist in manually derived lines. This purity is important for high-throughput assays and directed differentiation experiments where consistent cell populations are needed.

- The study demonstrates the utility of this FACS strategy across different reprogramming methods and somatic cell sources, suggesting it could be broadly applicable for standardized iPSC derivation workflows in research and clinical applications. Future work will explore its applicability to other cell types and reprogramming techniques.
