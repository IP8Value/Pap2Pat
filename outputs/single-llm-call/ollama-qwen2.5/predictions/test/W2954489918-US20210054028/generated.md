# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel peptide siderophore and a bacterial cell capable of producing such a peptide siderophore. Siderophores are small molecules produced by bacteria to chelate iron from the environment, facilitating its uptake into cells. The invention specifically focuses on a peptide siderophore with unique ligands that enhance its iron-chelating properties and provide additional functionalities.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Iron is an essential micronutrient for almost all living organisms, playing a critical role in various biological processes such as DNA synthesis, electron transport, and cellular respiration. However, iron is often present in environments at low concentrations or in forms that are not readily available to microorganisms and plants. Current methods of iron transport, such as synthetic chelators and naturally occurring siderophores, have limitations in terms of specificity, stability, and environmental impact. There is a pressing need for novel means of providing iron to cells, particularly in agricultural settings where iron deficiency can severely limit plant growth.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the need for an efficient and effective means of iron transport that overcomes the limitations of current methods. The solution provided by the invention is a novel peptide siderophore with N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands, which not only chelates iron but also releases nitric oxide (NO), a molecule known to enhance plant growth and stress tolerance.

The invention introduces gramibactin, a cyclic peptide siderophore produced by the bacterium *Bacillus graminis*. Gramibactin has a dual function: it efficiently chels iron and releases NO upon metal binding. The PET-CT tracer studies and supplementation experiments conducted with gramibactin have demonstrated its effectiveness in promoting plant growth and improving iron utilization.

In vitro assays and in vivo fluorescence imaging have further confirmed the efficacy of gramibactin. The ecological and agricultural implications of this invention are significant, as it offers a new approach to enhancing plant health and productivity.

The chelating properties of N-nitroso-hydroxylamines are particularly noteworthy. These ligands form strong complexes with iron, making them highly effective siderophores. A preferred embodiment of the peptide siderophore is a cyclic peptide with two side chains, one containing an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand and the other a hydroxy carboxylic acid ligand.

The invention also describes specific sequences of the peptide siderophore. For example, a peptide of the sequence X1-X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2, where X1 to X4 are amino acids and Gra1 and Gra2 are side chains, is provided. A preferred embodiment includes the peptide Asp-Thr1-Thr2-Gra1-Gly-Gra2, with specific modifications to the side chain of Thr1 and the C-terminus of Gra2.

Another embodiment is a cyclic peptide siderophore according to Formula I, which includes an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand. Additionally, a cyclic peptide siderophore according to Formula II is described, further enhancing the versatility of these molecules.

The bacterial cell producing the peptide siderophore is also part of the invention, providing a means for both natural and synthetic production of these novel compounds.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Iron is essential for numerous life processes, including oxygen transport, energy metabolism, and DNA synthesis. Siderophores are low molecular weight compounds produced by microorganisms to scavenge iron from the environment. These molecules form stable complexes with ferric iron (Fe³⁺), which can then be transported into cells via specific receptors.

The ligands in siderophores play a crucial role in their function. Common ligands include hydroxamates, catecholates, and salicylates, each forming different types of metal-ligand bonds. Siderophore production can occur through both biological processes, such as bacterial culturing, and chemical synthesis methods.

The present invention introduces peptide siderophores with unique structural features. These peptides are cyclic in nature, containing specific amino acid sequences and side chains that enhance their iron-chelating properties. The structure of these peptide siderophores is defined by the sequence X1-X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2, where X1 to X4 represent various amino acids and Gra1 and Gra2 are side chains.

### Sequences of the Invention:

SEQ ID NO: 1 represents a peptide with the general structure XXXZXZ. The positions X1 to X4 can be occupied by different amino acids, while Gra1 and Gra2 are specific side chains that enhance the siderophore's functionality. For example, in SEQ ID NO: 2 (DTTZGZ), Asp (D) and Gly (G) are fixed, while Thr1 and Thr2 have modifications to their side chains.

SEQ ID NO: 3 (XXZXZ) is another embodiment of the peptide siderophore, where X2 to X4 can vary. The nitroso group (–N=O) and hydroxylamine group (–NH–OH) are key functional groups in these peptides. N-nitroso-hydroxylamines are particularly effective at chelating iron.

Hydroxy carboxylic acids, such as lactic acid, can also serve as ligands in the peptide siderophores. Hydroxamates (–CONHOH), catecholates (–C6H4(OH)2), and salicylates (–C7H5O3) are other common ligands that can be incorporated into the peptides.

Modifications to the peptide siderophores, such as amino acid substitutions, can further enhance their properties. Conservative amino acid substitutions, where one amino acid is replaced by another with similar chemical properties, can improve stability and solubility. Alkyl (–R), alkoxy (–OR), carboxyl (–COOH), and hydroxyl (–OH) groups are common functional groups that can be introduced into the peptides.

The peptide siderophores of the invention have various functions, including promoting plant growth. Compositions containing these siderophores can be formulated for different applications, such as soil amendments or foliar sprays. The compositions can be applied to plants and plant parts to enhance iron uptake and overall health.

## Methods of Administration

The methods of administration for the peptide siderophores include treating plants and plant parts directly, as well as treating the soil in which they grow. Plant-bacterial interactions play a crucial role in the effectiveness of these treatments. Plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) are beneficial microorganisms that colonize the root zone and enhance plant growth through various mechanisms, including iron acquisition.

## EXAMPLES

The invention is further illustrated by the following examples, which describe the discovery and characterization of a novel siderophore produced by a rhizosphere bacterium. The bacterium *Bacillus graminis* was isolated from the rhizosphere of cereal plants and found to produce a unique siderophore.

### Rhizosphere Bacteria

The bacterium *B. graminis* was identified as a potential producer of novel siderophores based on its ability to grow in iron-limited conditions. The non-ribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) gene cluster responsible for siderophore biosynthesis was analyzed, and the predicted structure of the siderophore was elucidated.

### Siderophore Biosynthesis

The NRPS gene cluster in *B. graminis* was characterized using genomic sequencing. The gene cluster contained several modules that were involved in the synthesis of a cyclic peptide with N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands. Siderophore production was confirmed by culturing *B. graminis* in iron-limited media and detecting siderophore activity using chromazurol S (CAS) agar.

### Gene Cluster Responsibility

Knockout strains of *B. graminis* were created to confirm the role of the NRPS gene cluster in siderophore production. The knockout strains showed a significant reduction in siderophore activity, confirming that the gene cluster was responsible for the biosynthesis of the novel siderophore.

### Siderophore Structure Elucidation

The structure of the novel siderophore, named gramibactin, was elucidated using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS). The NMR spectra revealed that gramibactin was a cyclic peptide with an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand.

### Metal Complexation

The metal complexation properties of gramibactin were tested using iron, gallium, and other metals. The stability constants of the metal complexes were determined, and speciation diagrams were constructed to understand the binding behavior under different pH conditions. Gramibactin showed a high affinity for ferric iron and formed stable complexes.

### Plant Metal Uptake

The ability of gramibactin to promote plant growth was evaluated using PET/CT experiments. The Ga-gramibactin complex was used as a tracer to visualize metal uptake in plants. The results showed that plants treated with gramibactin had significantly higher levels of iron and improved chlorophyll content compared to control plants.

### Nitric Oxide Release

The release of nitric oxide (NO) from gramibactin was tested both in vitro and in planta. In vitro assays using the Griess test confirmed that gramibactin released NO upon metal binding. In planta experiments showed that NO was localized to the roots and shoots, enhancing plant iron homeostasis.

### Ecological Role

The ecological role of gramibactin in the rhizosphere was discussed, highlighting its potential as a natural fertilizer and plant growth promoter. The invention offers a new approach to sustainable agriculture by providing an efficient means of iron acquisition for plants.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

### Bacterial Cultures

*Bacillus graminis* was cultured in Luria-Bertani (LB) broth at 37°C with shaking. For siderophore production, the bacteria were grown in minimal media containing low concentrations of iron.

### NRPS Gene Cluster Analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted from *B. graminis* and sequenced using next-generation sequencing technologies. The NRPS gene cluster was identified and annotated using bioinformatics tools.

### Siderophore Production and Detection

Siderophore production was detected using CAS agar plates. Bacterial cultures were spotted on the plates, and siderophore activity was visualized as a color change from blue to orange.

### NMR Spectroscopy

The structure of gramibactin was elucidated using ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy. The spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance III HD spectrometer at 500 MHz for ¹H and 125 MHz for ¹³C.

### LC-MS Analysis

Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) was used to confirm the molecular weight of gramibactin. The analysis was performed on a Thermo Scientific Q Exactive Plus mass spectrometer coupled with an Ultimate 3000 UHPLC system.

### Metal Complexation Studies

The stability constants of metal complexes were determined using potentiometric titration and UV-Visible spectroscopy. Speciation diagrams were constructed to understand the binding behavior under different pH conditions.

### Plant Experiments

Arabidopsis thaliana was used as a model plant for evaluating the effects of gramibactin on plant growth. Plants were grown in hydroponic cultures with or without gramibactin, and chlorophyll content was measured using a spectrophotometer.

### Nitric Oxide Release Assays

The release of nitric oxide from gramibactin was tested using the Griess reagent. In vitro assays were performed by incubating gramibactin with ferric iron, and NO production was quantified colorimetrically.

### In Planta Experiments

Arabidopsis thaliana plants were treated with gramibactin, and NO localization was visualized using DAF-FM DA fluorescence. Confocal microscopy was used to image the distribution of NO in plant tissues.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention provides a novel peptide siderophore, gramibactin, which not only chelates iron but also releases nitric oxide, enhancing its effectiveness as a plant growth promoter. The bacterial cell producing gramibactin and methods for its administration offer new opportunities for improving agricultural practices and promoting sustainable agriculture.
