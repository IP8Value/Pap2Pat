- The study aimed to enhance meningococcal serotype A vaccine effectiveness by optimizing the conjugation of MenA CPS to CRM197. This involved preparing neoglycoconjugates with different saccharide-to-protein ratios and evaluating their immunogenicity in BALB/c mice.
- Mice were immunized three times, and antibody responses were measured using ELISA against HSA-deOAc and MenA CPS. Results showed that the optimized conjugate 8c induced higher IgG titers compared to other formulations, indicating its superior immunogenicity.
- In vitro bactericidal assays using rabbit complement demonstrated that sera from mice vaccinated with 8c effectively lysed N. meningitidis serotype A bacteria, highlighting the functional activity of induced antibodies and the potential of 8c as a more effective vaccine candidate.
- The preparation of neoglycoconjugates involved activating amino-oligosaccharides using di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate linker and conjugating them to CRM197 at specific saccharide-to-protein ratios. Conjugates were purified by tangential flow filtration and characterized for protein and saccharide content, ensuring consistent quality.
- Experiments adhered to ethical guidelines and were approved by the Italian Ministry of Health, with all procedures conducted under sterile conditions. The use of human complement samples was authorized by GSK HBS Reuse Check Team, ensuring compliance with ethical standards and participant consent.
- Statistical analysis using GraphPad Prism 7 software showed significant differences in antibody titers between groups, validating the enhanced immunogenicity of conjugate 8c. The study's findings underscore the importance of optimizing conjugation strategies to improve vaccine efficacy against meningococcal serotype A.
