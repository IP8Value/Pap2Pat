- The study utilized a combination of biochemical and biophysical methods to explore the interaction between RAS oncogenic mutants and PHB1. Techniques like bimolecular fluorescent complementation, immunoprecipitation, and lipid-binding assays were employed to characterize this interaction in detail.

- A cellular thermal shift assay demonstrated that rocaglamide stabilizes the association between PHB1 and specific lipids, suggesting a potential mechanism for modulating RAS activity through PHB1. This finding was supported by FACS analysis of BiFC-transfected cells, which showed increased fluorescence upon rocaglamide treatment.

- siRNA knockdown experiments revealed that reducing PHB1 levels significantly impaired the activation of RAS mutants in response to EGF stimulation, indicating a critical role for PHB1 in RAS signaling pathways. This was further confirmed by active RAS pulldown assays.

- Transmission electron microscopy and immunogold labeling were used to quantify the clustering of RAS mutants on plasma membrane sheets. The data showed that oncogenic RAS mutants exhibit preferential clustering, which is disrupted upon rocaglamide treatment, suggesting a potential therapeutic strategy for targeting RAS-driven cancers.

- The lipid-binding assay demonstrated that PHB1 specifically interacts with certain lipids, including phosphatidic acid and lysophosphatidylcholine. Rocaglamide was found to enhance this interaction, providing insights into how it might influence RAS membrane localization and signaling.

- In vivo studies using a mouse model of lung adenocarcinoma showed that rocaglamide treatment reduced tumor growth and improved survival rates. These findings support the potential of targeting PHB1 as a novel therapeutic approach for RAS-driven cancers.
