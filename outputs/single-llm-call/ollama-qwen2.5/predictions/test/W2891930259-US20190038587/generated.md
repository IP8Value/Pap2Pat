- **Introduction**: This study investigates whether addiction is associated with altered DNA methylation signatures in human peripheral immune cells, specifically T cells. Such biomarkers could predict addiction susceptibility and response to interventions, potentially guiding epigenetic-based treatments.

- **Methods**: The researchers used a limited number of subjects to analyze DNA methylation profiles in T cells from drug addicts compared to non-addicts. They also examined the correlation between DNA methylation and negative affect using the PANAS test.

- **Results**: The analysis revealed that addiction is associated with a significant hypomethylation signature in T cells, affecting pathways related to immune and inflammatory functions as well as neurobiological processes. Several probes correlated with negative affect scores, suggesting a link between DNA methylation and mood.

- **DHEA Treatment**: DHEA treatment in addicts reversed the differential methylation of genes relevant to brain function and behavior. Hypermethylated neuronal-related genes became less methylated, while hypomethylated genes became more methylated, indicating a potential association with addiction state.

- **Cross-Species Comparison**: The study found a significant overlap between differentially methylated genes in human T cells and those in the nucleus accumbens of cocaine-craving rats. This supports the relevance of peripheral DNA methylation markers for understanding brain-related behavioral phenotypes.

- **Limitations and Power Analysis**: Despite the small sample size, power calculations indicated that the study had sufficient statistical power to detect significant differences in DNA methylation. However, replication with larger samples is necessary to confirm findings and explore broader inclusion criteria.

- **Conclusion**: The results provide preliminary evidence of a functional role for DNA methylation in addiction and justify further studies to establish robust associations and potential therapeutic targets. Future research should aim to confirm these findings and delineate sites that predict drug craving and treatment response.

- **Data Availability**: The raw IDAT files from the study are available at GEO accession number GSE118570, allowing for independent verification and further analysis by the scientific community.
