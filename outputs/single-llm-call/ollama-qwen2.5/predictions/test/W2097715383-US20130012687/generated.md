- The study introduces a novel method for protein purification using a His6-tagged CPD (caspase protease domain) fusion system. This technique leverages the specific cleavage activity of CPD upon addition of inositol hexakisphosphate (InsP6), allowing efficient and rapid release of untagged target proteins from Ni-NTA affinity beads.

- The method was validated using various model proteins, including GFP, PfSENP1, BirA, STIM1(CAD), and mMMP12. Expression of these CPD fusion proteins in E. coli was induced with IPTG, followed by cell lysis and Ni-NTA affinity purification under standardized conditions.

- The on-bead cleavage step, where InsP6 is added to the immobilized CPD-His6 fusion proteins, typically occurs within 1-2 hours at room temperature or 4°C. This results in efficient liberation of untagged target proteins into the supernatant, which can be easily collected and further purified if necessary.

- For proteins like PfSENP1 and BirA, which showed aggregation at lower temperatures, the cleavage reactions were performed exclusively at room temperature to maintain protein solubility. The efficiency of InsP6-mediated cleavage was comparable to traditional thrombin cleavage for removing His6 tags, but with faster kinetics and fewer steps.

- The MMP12 construct required specific optimization, including induction at 16°C and the addition of TCEP to prevent misfolding during lysis. After Ni-NTA purification and on-bead cleavage with InsP6, the untagged MMP12 was further purified using gel filtration chromatography to ensure high purity and activity.

- The activity of the purified proteins was confirmed through various assays. GFP fluorescence was measured to verify protein integrity, while MMP12 activity was assessed using a fluorogenic substrate. These results demonstrated that the InsP6-mediated cleavage method not only efficiently purifies target proteins but also maintains their functional activity.

- This CPD-based purification system offers several advantages over traditional methods, including reduced purification time, fewer steps, and the ability to perform cleavage on-bead without eluting the fusion protein. The method is versatile and can be applied to a wide range of proteins, making it a valuable tool for protein expression and purification in both research and industrial settings.
