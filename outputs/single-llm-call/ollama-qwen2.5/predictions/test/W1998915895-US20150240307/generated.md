- **Introduction**: This study identifies circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) in plasma that could serve as diagnostic markers for rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Despite the stable nature of circulating miRNAs, their functions and protective mechanisms remain unclear. The research highlights the potential use of circulating miRNAs as biomarkers for various diseases, including RA.

- **Methodology**: The study used a comprehensive array approach to screen plasma from three RA patients and controls, followed by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) validation in a larger cohort. Multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to determine the diagnostic value of candidate miRNAs.

- **Results**: miR-24 and miR-125a-5p showed significant increases in RA patients compared to healthy controls. The combination of these miRNAs, along with miR-30a-5p, formed a diagnostic model called ePRAM, which demonstrated high sensitivity and specificity for RA.

- **Specificity**: Plasma miR-24 and miR-125a-5p levels did not correlate with general inflammation but were specific to RA. These miRNAs also differentiated RA from other conditions like osteoarthritis (OA) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), suggesting their potential as disease-specific biomarkers.

- **Age Considerations**: Aging significantly decreased plasma miR-125a-5p levels, but it still effectively differentiated RA patients from healthy controls across different age groups. Future studies could refine age-appropriate thresholds for these miRNAs.

- **Mechanisms and Functions**: miR-24 and miR-125a-5p may enhance inflammation by targeting key regulatory proteins like Furin and TNFAIP3, respectively. These interactions could contribute to the pathogenesis of RA, providing insights into their potential roles as biomarkers.

- **Comparative Analysis**: Other studies have identified different miRNAs associated with SLE and other conditions, but these did not show specificity for RA. The identified miRNAs in this study, particularly miR-24 and miR-125a-5p, demonstrated higher sensitivity and specificity for RA.

- **Clinical Implications**: Despite the controlled disease activity of RA patients in the study, the biomarkers showed high diagnostic accuracy. They also performed well in ACPA-negative patients, suggesting their potential for early diagnosis and treatment initiation in at-risk individuals.

- **Limitations**: The study had limitations, including the initial screening with a small sample size and manual exclusion of poor amplification data. Further validation using larger cohorts and different modalities is needed to ensure reproducibility and broader applicability.

- **Future Directions**: Future research should explore the diurnal and long-term variations in miRNA levels, as well as their utility in predicting RA prognosis and therapeutic outcomes. Identifying the cellular sources and mechanisms of circulating miR-24 and miR-125a-5p will further elucidate their roles as biomarkers.

- **Conclusion**: This study identifies miR-24 and miR-125a-5p as potential diagnostic markers for RA, with high sensitivity and specificity. The ePRAM model, incorporating these miRNAs, offers a promising tool for early diagnosis and management of RA.
