- The study developed a method to transform difficult-to-transform bacteria by in vivo methylation of plasmids using multiple methyltransferase (MTase) genes. This approach allowed successful transformation of Bacillus cereus ATCC 10987, Bacillus amyloliquefaciens TA208, and Nitrosomonas hamburgensis X14 with various shuttle and integrative plasmids.

- Electroporation conditions were optimized for B. cereus ATCC 10987 using ice-cold transformation buffer and a 2.1 kV pulse. For N. hamburgensis X14, cells were grown in DSMZ 756a medium, harvested, washed, and resuspended in 10% glycerol before electroporation at 1.2 kV.

- The upp gene of B. amyloliquefaciens TA208 was successfully knocked out using a homologous recombination plasmid pWYE748 after methylation with pM.Bam. Positive clones were verified by PCR, sequencing, and growth on MM plates supplemented with 5-FU and adenosine.

- To determine the modification sequences of cloned MTases, in vivo methylated plasmids were challenged with cognate restriction endonucleases (REases). High-copy plasmid pMK4 was used to facilitate identification. Various plasmids served as positive and negative controls depending on the specific MTase being tested.

- The method enabled successful transformation of B. cereus ATCC 10987, B. amyloliquefaciens TA208, and N. hamburgensis X14 with various shuttle and integrative plasmids, opening up new possibilities for genetic manipulation of these difficult-to-transform bacteria in biotechnology applications.
