- The study examines two reconfigurable antenna types (RCPA and RPDA) in MIMO ad hoc networks. RCPAs have 2 modes while RPDAs have 4 configurations. Both Centralized and Distributed configuration selection schemes were evaluated, with the latter showing practical advantages.

- Results show significant capacity increases when using reconfigurable antennas compared to non-reconfigurable ones. The RPDA outperformed the RCPA due to more available configurations and higher efficiency across modes, despite lower pattern diversity.

- For both antennas, the number of available configurations was found to be critical for performance gains. Even with highly correlated patterns, having multiple options provided notable capacity improvements.

- Pattern correlation between modes also impacted performance. Less correlated patterns, as seen in RCPAs, led to higher sum capacities compared to more correlated RPDA modes when normalized for efficiency differences.

- The study highlights that antenna designers should aim for a large number of diverse configurations while maintaining balanced radiation efficiencies across modes to maximize ad hoc network performance.

- Among the evaluated schemes, the Distributed Receiver Reconfiguration Approach (RXRA) showed promise by balancing capacity gains with practical constraints like channel feedback and network information requirements. This makes it a good candidate for real-world implementation in MIMO ad hoc networks.
