# Patent Application

## Title: Use of Armillaridin for Treating Esophageal Cancer

## Background
Esophageal cancer is a significant health concern with limited treatment options. Current therapies often involve surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation therapy, but these treatments have substantial side effects and varying efficacy. There is a pressing need for novel therapeutic agents that can enhance the effectiveness of existing treatments while minimizing adverse effects.

## Summary
The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating esophageal cancer using Armillaridin, a natural compound isolated from the medicinal fungus *Armillaria mellea*. The invention demonstrates that Armillaridin not only inhibits the growth of esophageal cancer cells but also enhances their radiosensitivity. This dual action makes Armillaridin a promising candidate for combination therapy with radiotherapy.

## Detailed Description

### Preparation and Administration of Armillaridin
Armillaridin can be prepared from *Armillaria mellea* using known extraction methods. The compound is then formulated into suitable pharmaceutical compositions, such as tablets, capsules, or injectable solutions. For intraperitoneal (I.P.) administration in animal models, a dose of 80 mg/kg three times per week for 12 doses has been found effective. In human clinical trials, the dosage and frequency will be adjusted based on patient response and safety profiles.

### Mechanism of Action
Armillaridin induces apoptosis in esophageal cancer cells by activating the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. This is evidenced by chromatin condensation, membrane blebbing, and a significant reduction in mitochondrial transmembrane potential. Additionally, Armillaridin causes an accumulation of cells at the G2/M phase of the cell cycle, making these cells more sensitive to radiation.

### Cell Viability Studies
Armillaridin inhibits the viability of human esophageal cancer cell lines, including squamous cell carcinoma (CE81T/VGH and TE-2) and adenocarcinoma (BE-3 and SKGT-4), in a time- and concentration-dependent manner. The estimated IC50 for these four cell lines ranges from 3.4 to 6.9 μM.

### Hypoploidy, Mitochondrial Transmembrane Potential, and Morphological Alterations
Armillaridin treatment leads to the development of a hypoploid cell population and an accumulation of cells at the G2/M phase. The percentage of sub-G1 phase increases from 4.44 ± 0.01% in control cells to 8.72 ± 2.47% in Armillaridin-treated cells, indicating apoptosis induction.

### Radiosensitizing Activity
Armillaridin enhances the radiosensitivity of esophageal cancer cells. At concentrations of 2.5 and 5.0 μM, Armillaridin increases the sensitizer enhancement ratio (SER) up to 1.6. This effect is attributed to the G2/M phase accumulation, which makes cells more susceptible to radiation damage.

### In Vivo Therapeutic Studies
In a xenograft model using nude mice, Armillaridin treatment at 80 mg/kg significantly inhibited the growth of CE81T/VGH tumors without causing significant weight loss or leukopenia. This indicates that Armillaridin has a favorable safety profile compared to conventional chemotherapeutic agents like cisplatin.

### Animal Model and Experimental Design
The animal model used in this study involved subcutaneous implantation of 106 CE81T/VGH cancer cells in the right gluteal region of nude mice. After 21 days, when tumors reached approximately 0.5 cm in diameter, the animals were subjected to treatment with Armillaridin, vehicle control, or cisplatin. Tumor size and body weight were monitored regularly.

### Statistical Analysis
Data from cell viability, apoptosis induction, and radiosensitizing activity studies were analyzed using Student's t-test or one-way ANOVA. The difference was considered significant for P < 0.05. Survival curves were fitted with a linear-quadratic model to estimate the sensitizer enhancement ratio.

### Conclusion
Armillaridin is a potent inhibitor of esophageal cancer cell growth and enhances radiosensitivity, making it a promising candidate for combination therapy with radiotherapy. Its favorable safety profile in animal models suggests potential for further clinical development.

## Claims
1. A method for treating esophageal cancer comprising administering Armillaridin to a patient in need thereof.
2. The method of claim 1, wherein the Armillaridin is administered intraperitoneally.
3. The method of claim 1, wherein the Armillaridin is administered in combination with radiotherapy.
4. A pharmaceutical composition comprising Armillaridin and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for treating esophageal cancer.
5. The pharmaceutical composition of claim 4, further comprising one or more additional therapeutic agents.

## Abstract
The invention provides methods and compositions for treating esophageal cancer using Armillaridin, a natural compound isolated from *Armillaria mellea*. Armillaridin inhibits the growth of esophageal cancer cells and enhances their radiosensitivity. The invention demonstrates that Armillaridin can be effectively used in combination with radiotherapy to improve treatment outcomes while maintaining a favorable safety profile.
