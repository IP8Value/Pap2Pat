# Patent Application for Viscosity-Based DNA Detection Using AMBR Microviscometer

## Abstract
The present invention describes a viscosity-based method using an Asynchronous Magnetic Bead Rotation (AMBR) microviscometer for label-free detection and monitoring of DNA reactions. This technique is capable of detecting changes in solution viscosity due to DNA concentration or molecular weight, making it suitable for applications such as real-time PCR monitoring and restriction digestion analysis.

## Background
### Field of the Invention
The invention pertains to the field of biotechnology and analytical chemistry, specifically focusing on methods for detecting and quantifying DNA. Traditional techniques often require labeling or complex equipment, which can be time-consuming and expensive. The present invention offers a label-free, rapid, and cost-effective alternative using an AMBR microviscometer.

### Description of Related Art
Current methods for DNA detection include gel electrophoresis, PCR, and sequencing. These methods are well-established but often require significant sample preparation and specialized equipment. Label-free techniques have gained attention due to their simplicity and reduced costs. However, existing label-free methods may lack the sensitivity and real-time monitoring capabilities needed for certain applications.

## Summary of the Invention
The invention provides a method for detecting and quantifying DNA using an AMBR microviscometer. The microviscometer measures changes in solution viscosity caused by variations in DNA concentration or molecular weight. This approach is label-free, rapid, and can be applied to various DNA reactions, including PCR and restriction digestion.

## Detailed Description of the Invention
### Introduction to AMBR Microviscometer
The Asynchronous Magnetic Bead Rotation (AMBR) microviscometer operates by measuring the rotation period of paramagnetic beads in a solution under a rotating magnetic field. The relationship between bead rotation period and solution viscosity is linear, allowing for precise viscosity measurements.

### Linear Relationship Between Viscosity and DNA Concentration
At a fixed temperature, the viscosity of common diagnostic reaction solutions increases linearly with the concentration of DNA. This relationship can be expressed as η = η0(1 + C[η]), where η0 is the solvent viscosity, C is the DNA concentration, and [η] is the intrinsic viscosity of DNA. The intrinsic viscosity increases with molecular weight, making this method suitable for detecting changes in DNA size.

### Measurement of Viscosity Using AMBR Microviscometer
The AMBR microviscometer measures the rotation period of paramagnetic beads in a solution, which is linearly proportional to the solution viscosity. By constructing a correlation curve relating bead rotation period to viscosity, the method can accurately determine the viscosity of DNA solutions.

### Influence of Bead Properties on Rotation Period
Variation in bead properties, such as size and magnetic content, can affect the measurement accuracy. However, averaging over multiple beads reduces this variability. The relative standard deviation due to bead non-uniformity is approximately 10%, with a single bead showing a much smaller variation of about 1%.

### Viscosity Measurement of DNA Aqueous Solutions
The AMBR microviscometer can detect changes in viscosity caused by DNA digestion or PCR reactions. For example, digesting lambda DNA with EcoRI yields fragments of various lengths, leading to a decrease in solution viscosity. The linear relationship between viscosity and DNA concentration is confirmed through experiments.

### Real-Time Monitoring of PCR Reactions
The AMBR microviscometer can monitor the progression of PCR reactions in real-time by detecting changes in solution viscosity. This method is approximately 5 cycles delayed compared to gel electrophoresis but offers a label-free, rapid alternative for monitoring DNA amplification.

### Potential Applications and Future Improvements
This viscosity-based method has broad applications in biotechnology, including real-time PCR monitoring, restriction digestion analysis, and other polymer reactions. Future improvements could focus on optimizing bead properties to enhance sensitivity and applicability to a wider range of DNA products.
