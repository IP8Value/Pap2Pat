- This study detailed the evaporation dynamics of blood pools, showing similarities to sol-gel transitions due to the colloidal nature of blood with RBCs and plasma. After coagulation, blood forms a gel-like system that dries at varying rates influenced by its shape and size.

- The drying front's evolution was non-uniform, affecting evaporation rates differently across the pool. By using the wet area to calculate the evaporation rate (J*), an almost constant value was observed, linking the drying front to J*.

- Incorporating various variables like temperature and humidity, a consistent diffusion coefficient for blood pools of different sizes and shapes was identified. This allowed for calculating the formation time of a pool dried under similar conditions, though further research is needed for broader applicability.

- The study used blood from healthy volunteers with varying haematocrit values, showing no significant influence on evaporation dynamics. However, physiological factors like illnesses or drug intake could indirectly affect clotting and thus evaporation, suggesting future work in this area to refine the model.

- This research is under patent application (EP19305772.6) at the European Patent Office, filed by F. Smith, C. Nicloux, and D. Brutin on June 17, 2019. The forensic interpretation section of the study outlines a practical method for estimating pool formation times using photography and a reference table.
