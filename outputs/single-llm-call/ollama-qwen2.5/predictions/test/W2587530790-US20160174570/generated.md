- Modern agriculture faces significant challenges due to climate change, necessitating improved crop resilience and yield. Despite their potential, plant endosymbiotic partners like fungal endophytes remain underexplored. These symbionts can enhance seed germination, stress tolerance, and overall plant productivity, making them crucial for sustainable agriculture.
- The concept of mycovitality, or seed endosymbiosis, is pivotal in controlling seed dormancy and alleviating environmental stress. Endophytic fungi improve plant yield and resilience by modulating acclimatization and resistance capacities, as well as indirectly through biogeochemical cycle regulation. This highlights the importance of the seed microbiome in plant development.
- Understanding the role of the spermosphere's microbiome, particularly endophytic fungi, is critical for reprogramming plant germination. These biological factors increase vigor, stress tolerance, and nutrient uptake, making them essential for plant fitness, especially in extreme environments. This understanding can lead to more predictable and robust seed germination.
- The benefits of mycovitalism extend beyond the current generation, as endophytic fungi can enhance offspring productivity through epigenetic modifications. This "prenatal care" for seeds ensures that they are well-prepared for growth, much like ensuring a healthy start for human babies. This concept is crucial for agricultural and horticultural production systems.
- Future research should focus on genomics-assisted breeding using beneficial microbiomes to improve crop varieties. By integrating endosymbiotic plant bioprotectors, resistance enhancers, and growth promoters, we can maximize the performance of plants under changing environmental conditions. This approach could lead to more sustainable and resilient agricultural practices.
- Large-scale field experiments over multiple years and sites are necessary to evaluate the contribution of endosymbiosis to plant-host genotypes. Such studies will help in understanding the natural evolution of beneficial systems and their modulation by environmental parameters, advancing endosymbiosis-based plant technology and maximizing plant performance.
