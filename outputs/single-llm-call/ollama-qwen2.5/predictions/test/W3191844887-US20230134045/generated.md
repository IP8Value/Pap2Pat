- This study evaluated PKM2 and O-GlcNAc as potential prognostic markers in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer. High expression of these markers was significantly associated with poor 10-year disease-free survival (DFS) in a Taiwanese cohort.

- Univariate Cox analyses showed O-GlcNAc, PKM2, invasive tumor size, and T stage were significantly linked to DFS outcomes. Nodal status had less impact due to the relatively benign nature of luminal A tumors.

- Multivariate analysis revealed that O-GlcNAc combined with a clinical parameter (invasive tumor size, T stage, or CTS5) had significant effects on DFS. PKM2 showed nearly significant impacts in triple-marker models.

- Logistic regression and ROC curve analyses found the T stage-O-GlcNAc-PKM2 model performed best (AUC = 0.722), suggesting these factors serve as good prognostic discriminators for ER+/HER2− patients, providing metabolic information beyond clinical data.

- The study population comprised Taiwanese women of Chinese Han ethnicity, where premenopausal breast cancer incidence is higher than in the West. This highlights potential ethnic differences and the need for tailored treatment approaches in Asian populations.

- Despite limitations such as a single-center retrospective design with 162 patients, this pilot study demonstrates the clinical utility of metabolism-based IHC markers. Further multi-center validation studies are needed to confirm these findings in diverse patient cohorts.
