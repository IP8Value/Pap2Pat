- Our study demonstrates that inhibiting the NOTCH pathway via HES1 shRNA can redifferentiate expanded human β-cells into insulin-producing cells. This process activates insulin expression and key transcription factors, primarily in BCD (eGFP+) cells, indicating their preferential capacity for redifferentiation.
- The combined treatment of HES1 shRNA and serum-free medium (SFM) significantly enhances redifferentiation compared to SFM alone, doubling the fraction of C-peptide-positive cells. This suggests a more efficient transition from a replicating state to a quiescent, differentiated state.
- Inhibition of HES1 leads to growth arrest and morphological changes, including cell clustering and nucleus size reduction, which are indicative of redifferentiation. These changes may involve a mesenchymal-epithelial transition, reversing the effects of epithelial-mesenchymal transition during dedifferentiation.
- The NOTCH pathway's role in cell survival is evident from the 5% increase in apoptosis following HES1 shRNA and RM treatment. This may be due to conflicting signals when serum is present initially. Additional differentiation cues in vitro and transplantation could help reduce cell mortality rates.
- Further research is needed to fully characterize the redifferentiated cells and compare them with adult human β-cells. Nonviral methods for HES1 inhibition will also be essential for clinical applications, but this approach holds promise for expanding β-cells from single donors for multiple recipients in diabetes therapy.
