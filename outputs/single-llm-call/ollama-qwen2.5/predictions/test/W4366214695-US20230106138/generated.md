- The study evaluated a multi-modal bio-signal acquisition system for predicting superficial incisional infections in a porcine model infected with Methicillin Susceptible Staphylococcus Aureus (MSSA). The system used machine learning to analyze tissue oxygenation, temperature, and bioimpedance.

- At non-infected sites, tissue oxygenation remained stable, temperature increased then decreased, and bioimpedance decreased then increased. Infected sites showed different trends: tissue oxygenation peaked at 25 hours post-inoculation, while temperature and bioimpedance showed higher and lower values, respectively, compared to non-infected sites.

- Digital signals indicated MSSA infection 24-31 hours before clinical signs, highlighting the system's potential for early detection. This could enable timely treatment, reducing healthcare costs, complications, and mortality rates associated with SSIs.

- The multi-modal ensemble model demonstrated promising accuracy in detecting current infections (AUC = 0.77) and predicting infections 24 hours in advance (AUC = 0.80). However, the model's performance for predicting infections 48 hours in advance was lower (AUC = 0.74), indicating a potential limit to early prediction.

- The study identified statistically significant differences between inoculated and sham sites before MSSA introduction, likely due to spurious results from limited data. Future studies with larger sample sizes could clarify these findings and improve the model's reliability.

- Integrating additional parameters like sociodemographic, clinical, peri-operative, and post-operative factors into the model could enhance its diagnostic accuracy and clinical adoption. This approach may provide a more comprehensive understanding of SSI risk and progression.

- The study used sutured sensors on porcine subjects to minimize movement-related errors. For human trials, the CrelySENSE sensors will use disposable adhesives and Bluetooth low energy (BLE) protocols, making them suitable for clinical settings.

- Future research should explore the system's effectiveness in detecting SSIs caused by other pathogens like MRSA, ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae, multidrug-resistant gram-negative bacteria, and VRE. Additionally, validating the system for deep incisional SSIs would broaden its applicability.

- In summary, the study demonstrated that the multi-modal bio-signal system can accurately detect MSSA infections in porcine subjects, providing continuous real-time monitoring of wound healing. This technology has the potential to significantly improve post-operative care and reduce complications.
