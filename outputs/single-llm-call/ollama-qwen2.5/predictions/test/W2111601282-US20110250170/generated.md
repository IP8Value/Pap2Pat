- The study evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of F16-IL2 immunocytokine and temozolomide, alone and in combination, against U87MG human glioblastoma xenografts. The combination showed superior results without additional toxicity compared to single agents.

- In subcutaneous models, the F16-IL2/temozolomide combo led to complete tumor eradication in all mice by day 40 and long-term remission for over 160 days. For intracranial xenografts, it resulted in a 73% decrease in tumor volume after 25 days.

- F16-IL2 promoted infiltration of immune effector cells into glioblastoma lesions without affecting normal organs. Selective accumulation around tumor vasculature was observed in both models, supporting the immunocytokine's anticancer role.

- The combo therapy induced increased apoptosis and complete suppression of proliferation compared to other treatments. NK cells were primarily responsible for F16-IL2's therapeutic effect, while T-cells played a lesser role.

- Differences between subcutaneous and intracranial models may explain the varied efficacy observed. IL2-based immunocytokines showed minimal solo activity but strong synergy with other regimens in multiple cancer types. Preclinical results justify clinical evaluation of F16-IL2 combined with temozolomide for glioblastoma treatment.
