- The study utilized a proteomics approach to identify potential biomarkers for early pancreatic cancer detection by analyzing the plasma proteome of genetically engineered mice that develop cancers resembling human pancreatic tumors. Through high-resolution mass spectrometry and acrylamide isotopic labeling, researchers identified 165 proteins present in greater amounts in plasma from tumor-bearing mice compared to control mice.
- These candidate biomarkers were then tested using human blood samples, including those from patients with pancreatic cancer, chronic pancreatitis, and healthy controls. Some of these proteins showed increased levels in pancreatic cancer patients compared to both healthy individuals and pancreatitis patients.
- The researchers further validated a panel of five early-stage biomarker candidates (LCN2, TIMP1, REG1A, REG3, IGFBP4) using prediagnostic sera from the Carotene and Retinol Efficacy Trial. This panel showed promise in distinguishing between individuals who later developed pancreatic cancer and matched controls up to 13 months before diagnosis.
- The study demonstrated that using genetically engineered mouse models for proteomic analysis can help overcome challenges associated with human sample variability and early-stage disease detection, potentially leading to the identification of biomarkers suitable for noninvasive screening tests for pancreatic cancer. However, further validation in larger patient cohorts is needed before clinical application.
