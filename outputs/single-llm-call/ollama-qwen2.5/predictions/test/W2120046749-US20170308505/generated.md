- The paper presents an information dynamic spectrum framework for detecting critical transitions and identifying directional influences in complex systems. It uses transfer entropy (TE) and asymmetric transfer entropy matrices (ATEM) to quantify associative, asymmetric directional influence.

- Key contributions include: (1) An effective measure of system-wide directional influence; (2) Detection of instability in systems with dynamic directional influences; (3) A probabilistic light cone method for predicting ATE trajectories and indicating critical transitions. 

- The framework was applied to various empirical datasets including non-Foster circuits, stock market indices, and Wikipedia editing activity. Results showed the ability to detect early signs of critical transitions.

- The TE/ATE curves exhibited characteristic patterns before different types of critical transitions like synchronization, abrupt falls, or exponential growth. This provides a basis for automated detection methods.

- A novel model-based prediction method was proposed using natural logarithmic curves and probabilistic light cones. It captures system-level information not discernible from individual time series alone.

- Experiments demonstrated the probabilistic light cone could detect early signs of stock market crashes, distinguishing them from other fluctuations. However, parameters may need tuning for different system regions over time.

- Future work will require a more complete analysis and development of adaptive parameter selection methods for the prediction framework. The authors are grateful to collaborators who provided helpful discussions and data.
