- **Increased Dynamic Range of RNA-seq**: RNA-seq provided a significantly higher dynamic range compared to microarray analysis, leading to the identification of more differentially expressed genes. Despite using fewer biological replicates for RNA-seq, this technology uncovered novel aspects of macrophage biology, including differential expression of gene families like apolipoprotein L and leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptors.

- **Network Analysis Insights**: A priori information-based network analysis revealed that RNA-seq data uncovered novel biological mechanisms. For example, the recognition of differential expression in the apolipoprotein L family and leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptors provided insights into their roles in macrophage function and polarization.

- **Differential Splice Variant Usage**: RNA-seq identified several genes with differential usage of alternative promoters, transcription start sites, and splice variants between M1- and M2-like macrophages. For instance, PDLIM7 showed significant differences in splice variant usage, which was not detected by microarray analysis.

- **Novel Marker Genes for Macrophage Polarization**: RNA-seq helped identify new marker genes for M1- and M2-like macrophages, such as CD120b (TNFR2) and SLAMF7. These markers can be used to better understand the biology of differentially polarized macrophages and their roles in various diseases.

- **Comparison with Microarray Data**: While RNA-seq provided more informative transcripts, microarrays still revealed 73% of the major M1-network. However, for the M2-network, only 54% was revealed by microarrays, highlighting the advantage of RNA-seq in uncovering novel biological mechanisms.

- **Human Surfaceome Analysis**: Focusing on the human surfaceome, RNA-seq identified several cell surface molecules differentially expressed between M1- and M2-like macrophages. This includes CD1a, CD1b, CD93, and CD226, which can serve as new markers to distinguish polarization states.

- **Future Implications**: The novel insights provided by RNA-seq will help deepen the understanding of transcriptional regulation during macrophage polarization. These findings introduce new cell surface markers that can aid in characterizing macrophage programs and functions in human disease, enhancing our ability to study and treat inflammatory conditions.
