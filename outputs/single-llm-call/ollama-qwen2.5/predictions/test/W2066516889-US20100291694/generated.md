- The study details the development of S77, a humanized antibody that selectively inhibits the alternative pathway (AP) of complement activation by blocking factor B binding to C3b. This mechanism potently inhibits AP convertase activity and C5 binding, preventing both formation and function of AP convertases.
- Crystal structure analysis revealed S77 binds a neoepitope created upon C3 cleavage to form C3b, selectively recognizing the active conformation of C3b over native C3. This provides therapeutic advantages by targeting only activated complement proteins.
- Unlike other inhibitors that block both pathways, S77 selectively inhibits AP while sparing CP activation. This preserves residual complement function for pathogen defense and minimizes potential side effects from complete complement shutdown.
- The binding site of S77 on C3b overlaps with regions important for interactions with factor B, factor H, CR1, and C5. This sterically blocks substrate binding to the convertase, explaining its potent inhibitory activity. 
- Overall, this study demonstrates a novel approach to generating selective complement inhibitors that target only the active form of C3b. Further studies are needed, but S77 shows promise as a therapeutic for chronic inflammatory diseases mediated by AP activation.
