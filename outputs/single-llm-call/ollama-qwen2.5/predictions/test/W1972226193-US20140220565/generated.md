- The study aimed to evaluate a novel magnetic resonance-based method (hMRS) for detecting Mycobacterium avium subspecies paratuberculosis (MAP) DNA in blood samples from Crohn's disease patients and healthy individuals. hMRS demonstrated higher sensitivity and specificity compared to nested PCR, identifying more positive cases with fewer false negatives.
- Researchers extracted pure and crude bacterial DNA from cultured bacteria and clinical isolates using standard protocols. They then performed hMRS and nPCR experiments on serial dilutions of the DNA samples to assess method performance. Specificity was tested against other bacterial strains.
- Blood samples were collected from Crohn's disease patients and healthy controls at the University of Florida after obtaining informed consent. White blood cells were isolated, and crude/pure DNA extracted for analysis by hMRS and nPCR in a blinded manner without clinical information. Results were statistically analyzed.
- The study found hMRS to be significantly more sensitive than nested PCR for MAP detection (p<0.05). hMRS identified 28/31 Crohn's disease samples as positive vs 14/31 by nPCR. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis showed hMRS had an area under the curve of 0.97, indicating excellent diagnostic performance. No healthy controls tested positive.
- Ethical approval was obtained from the University of Florida Institutional Review Board. Written informed consent was provided by all participants or their legal guardians. The study adhered to ethical guidelines for human subjects research throughout the sample collection, analysis and reporting process.
