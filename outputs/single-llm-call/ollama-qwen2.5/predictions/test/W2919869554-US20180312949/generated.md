- **Alloy Production:** Alloy ingots are produced by inductive melting of high-purity elemental constituents (Au, Cu, Ag, Pd, and Si) under inert atmosphere. Rod samples and plate coupons are formed by melting the ingots at 950°C in quartz tubes and rapidly quenching them in a water bath.

- **Sample Preparation:** Rod cross sections and plate surfaces were finely polished to achieve a 1 μm diamond mirror finish. This preparation ensures that the surface is smooth and uniform for subsequent analysis and measurement.

- **X-ray Diffraction Analysis:** The amorphicity/crystallinity of the metallic glass and crystalline phases was investigated using x-ray diffraction with CuKα radiation. This technique helps in determining the structural characteristics of the alloys, such as the presence of any crystalline phases.

- **Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC):** Phase transition thermodynamics were studied using differential scanning calorimetry at a scan rate of 20 K/min. DSC provides insights into the thermal stability and phase transitions of the materials, crucial for understanding their behavior under different conditions.

- **Color Measurement:** CIELAB color coordinates were measured on polished 20 mm × 20 mm × 1 mm plate coupons using a Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer. Measurements at four corners ensured uniformity and provided an average value for the color characteristics of each sample.

- **Hardness Testing:** Vickers hardness (HV0.5) was measured on polished plate coupons using a microhardness tester with a 40 μm indenter. Eight tests were conducted under a load of 500 g, ensuring that the indenter size was large enough to provide a representative average hardness for the composite materials.
