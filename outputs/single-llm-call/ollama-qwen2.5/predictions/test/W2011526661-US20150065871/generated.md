- The study demonstrated a single spherical transducer at 500-kHz can accurately and repeatably disrupt the blood-brain barrier in deep subcortical structures of macaque monkeys. This is an important step toward non-invasive targeted drug delivery for treating neurological conditions.

- The stereotaxic targeting system used with the portable ultrasound setup was found to be accurate and reliable, showing deviations from intended targets on the order of 1-2 mm. Real-time passive cavitation detection monitoring reliably indicated successful sonications without MRI guidance.

- Preliminary results suggest BBB openings created using typical acoustic parameters in macaques close after approximately 2 days. However, more experiments are needed to characterize the time course over a range of sonication parameters and establish if it is similar between species.

- Ongoing chronic studies will investigate safety of repeated sonications and drug delivery over 6 months in mice, as well as complete behavioral testing in monkeys. Future work will also determine whether therapeutically relevant drug levels can be maintained after crossing the BBB.
