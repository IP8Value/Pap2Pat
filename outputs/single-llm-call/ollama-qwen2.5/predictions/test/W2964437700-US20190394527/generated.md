- The proposed framework introduces a lightweight and modular method for enhancing the Quality of Experience (QoE) in cellular networks by integrating machine learning (ML) classifiers into existing base station schedulers. This approach allows for real-time detection of HTTP Adaptive Streaming (HAS) flows, enabling prioritized scheduling based on user experience metrics.
- The ML classifiers used in this framework are designed to extract simple features from standard downlink packet headers, ensuring minimal latency and ease of integration with current BS scheduler implementations. This modular design also allows for the replacement or addition of alternative schedulers without affecting other components.
- By reliably detecting HAS flows and inferring their application characteristics, such as video playback buffer state and resolution quality, the framework enables proactive scheduling decisions that significantly improve QoE metrics like initial playback delay, MOS scores, and average chunk representation quality levels.
- Simulation results under ideal flow classification show substantial improvements in QoE for HAS users, with median and worst-case users experiencing significant reductions in playback delays and increases in MOS scores. Even under misclassification scenarios, the framework demonstrates robust performance, highlighting its effectiveness in real-world conditions.
- The impact of experience-centric scheduling on File Download users is analyzed, showing a trade-off between improved UPTs for HAS users and slight degradation for File Download users. However, this degradation is mitigated by reduced chunk request frequency and idle times, maintaining overall network efficiency and traffic volume.
- Future work could extend the framework to detect and prioritize other service categories like augmented reality and social media, leveraging the modular design to incorporate new features and datasets. Additionally, there is potential for future standards to provide mechanisms for integrating higher-layer state information into physical and medium access layers, further enhancing QoE-centric scheduling.
- The proposed experience-centric scheduling framework demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of an end-to-end user application-centric approach in next-generation mobile wireless networks. By focusing on application layer metrics during downlink scheduling, this work paves the way for more proactive and efficient network management strategies that prioritize end-user satisfaction and performance.
