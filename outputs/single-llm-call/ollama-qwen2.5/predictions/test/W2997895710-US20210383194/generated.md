- We introduced a multi-scale multi-channel neural GEM with two-dimensional attentions for modeling event sequences. It exploits negative evidence of no observable events by introducing fake epochs, eliminating the need to assume specific functional forms. This approach captures hidden state variations in continuous time non-parametrically.
- Our model combines GEM frameworks and deep neural network power. It outperforms other models on synthetic and benchmark datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness for modeling event stream data. The multi-channel aspect allows separate evolution of hidden states per channel.
- An ablation study showed the importance of multi-channel modeling, attention mechanisms, and optimal memory bank sizes. Introducing one fake epoch significantly improved log-likelihoods while more epochs provided diminishing returns due to real/fake epoch imbalance. Attention enables graph visualization of event relationships.
- We tested our model on synthetic data, the ICEWS socio-political events dataset, and MIMIC-II healthcare records. MCN-GEM outperformed other models by 5-15% in most cases, with some variations based on dataset characteristics. The model's proficiency in learning from history was sometimes detrimental when train/test distributions differed.
- Possible future work includes using a parallel integral-lambda network to learn integral terms directly instead of quadrature sums, and exploring the use of negative evidence for prediction tasks where full sequence visibility may not be available. Overall, this approach shows promise for modeling complex event stream dependencies.
