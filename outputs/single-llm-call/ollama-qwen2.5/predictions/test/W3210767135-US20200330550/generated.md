- The study reveals that TCAP-1 regulates energy metabolism in skeletal muscle through an insulin-independent mechanism, affecting contractile kinetics via calcium dynamics and ATP production. This mechanism involves interactions between TCAP-1 and LPHN-1 and -3 receptors, leading to increased glucose importation and mitochondrial activity.

- TCAP-1 stimulates G-protein-coupled PLC, converting PIP3 into IP3 and DAG. Increased IP3 levels open SR channels, raising cytosolic calcium. This calcium influx into mitochondria via MCU enhances TCA cycle and ETC activity, resulting in higher ATP and NADH production and mitochondrial hyperpolarization.

- The study used siRNA and CRISPR methods to reduce LPHN-1 and -3 activities, showing that ablation of either receptor significantly inhibited TCAP-1-mediated calcium responses. This suggests potential interactions or combined actions among LPHN isoforms in TCAP-1 signaling pathways.

- Teneurins are multifunctional transmembrane proteins with TCAP at their extracellular tip. The study found teneurin-3 and LPHN-1 transcripts co-localized in the sarcolemma, supporting their role in muscle metabolism. However, this does not definitively establish them as cognitive ligand-receptor pairs.

- Future research aims to determine the trigger for TCAP release in skeletal muscle, whether by local cleavage of teneurin or independent transcription and translation. The study also plans to investigate TCAP's presence as a circulating hormone in serum, which is crucial for understanding its broader physiological roles.
