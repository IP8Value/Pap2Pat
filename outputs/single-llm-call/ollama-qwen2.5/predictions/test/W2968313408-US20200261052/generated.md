- **Subject Demographics**: The study included 33 LVO subjects (16 female), 33 IHC subjects (13 female), and 40 OHC subjects (25 female). Average ages were 66.9, 56.4, and 58.4 years, respectively. A total of 996 average beat waveforms were analyzed, with 354 from LVO, 445 from IHC, and 196 from OHC subjects. The clustering feature space had a dimension of 996 x 3.

- **Gap Analysis & Cluster Archetypes**: Gap analysis identified four optimal clusters. Type I (early onset, wide canopy, strong peaks) included 400 waveforms, mostly from controls. Type II (late onset, wide canopy, strong peaks) had 257 waveforms, also mostly from controls. Type III (early onset, narrow canopy, weak peaks) with 83 waveforms was primarily from LVO subjects. Type IV (late onset, wide canopy, weak peaks) contained 256 waveforms, evenly split between LVO and controls.

- **Comparison to TIBI Flow Grades**: Clusters I and IV aligned well with TIBI grades 5 (normal flow) and 2 (blunted flow), respectively. Cluster II, characterized by late onset but normal peaks, may reflect differences in peripheral vascular resistance. Cluster III, strongly associated with LVO, exhibited early onset but suppressed subsequent morphological structure, potentially indicating stenosis.

- **Limitations and Future Work**: The study excluded non-pulsatile flows (TIBI grades 0 and 1) due to inclusion criteria. TIBI grade 3 (Dampened) requires velocity magnitude comparison, complicating direct alignment. Future work could explore extended clustering features from waveform spectrograms and M-mode data to better differentiate clusters II and IV, enhancing LVO classification specificity.

- **Conclusions**: This study demonstrates the effectiveness of spectral clustering in identifying meaningful TCD flow types related to known morphological categories. The derived cluster archetypes can facilitate automated waveform categorization. Combining these labels with other metrics like VCI and velocity asymmetry could improve LVO classification, offering promising avenues for future research.
