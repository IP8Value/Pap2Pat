## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Histology and protein detection are fundamental techniques in biomedical research, enabling the visualization of cellular structures and proteins within tissues. Aldehyde-based cross-linking agents, such as formaldehyde and paraformaldehyde, have been widely used for tissue fixation due to their ability to preserve tissue morphology and prevent degradation. However, these agents can form stable cross-links with proteins, leading to the masking of antigenic sites and reducing the effectiveness of subsequent immunohistochemical staining. This limitation has prompted a need for improved methods to unmask antigens in fixed tissues, enhancing the detection and analysis of proteins.

The current fixation methods, while effective in preserving tissue structure, often result in reduced antigen retrieval efficiency. The formation of aldehyde-based cross-links can obscure epitopes, making it difficult for antibodies to bind to their targets. This has significant implications for diagnostic and research applications that rely on accurate protein detection. Despite numerous attempts to address these issues, existing solutions have not fully resolved the deficiencies in antigen retrieval.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for retrieving antigens from aldehyde-fixed tissues. The method involves treating the tissue with an aldehyde scavenging agent solution, which reacts with and neutralizes the aldehyde-based cross-linking agents that have formed during fixation. This reaction unmasking antigenic sites, thereby improving the detection of proteins in subsequent immunohistochemical analyses.

The invention further includes an optional heating step to enhance the effectiveness of the aldehyde scavenging agent. The formulation of the aldehyde scavenging agent solution can vary, but it typically includes one or more aldehyde scavenging agents, such as amino ethanol derivatives, amino acids and their derivatives, amine and aniline containing compounds, hydrazine and hydrazide derivatives, urea derivatives, thiol derivatives, hydroxyl compounds, and keto derivatives.

In addition to the method, the invention also encompasses a kit for retrieving proteins of interest in fixed tissues. The kit may include the aldehyde scavenging agent solution, optional nonionic surfactants, stabilizing agents, and instructions for use. The invention further provides methods for identifying effective antigen retrieval agents by testing their ability to unmask proteins in fixed tissues using techniques such as mass spectrometry.

### Unmasking

The term "unmasking" refers to the process of exposing antigenic sites in fixed tissues that have been masked by aldehyde-based cross-linking agents. This process is crucial for improving the detection and analysis of proteins in immunohistochemical studies.

### Method for Retrieving Antigens and Improving Detection

The method of the present invention involves treating aldehyde-fixed tissue with an aldehyde scavenging agent solution to unmask antigenic sites. The tissue is first deparaffinized using xylene and rehydrated through graded alcohols. The aldehyde scavenging agent solution is then applied, and the tissue is heated to enhance the reaction. This heating step typically involves raising the temperature to 95-98°C for 45 minutes.

After unmasking, the tissue can be stained with antibodies specific to the protein of interest. The staining process may involve incubating the tissue with a primary antibody, followed by a secondary antibody conjugated to a fluorophore or enzyme. This allows for the visualization and quantification of the protein in the tissue.

### Concentration of Aldehyde Scavenging Agent

The concentration of the aldehyde scavenging agent in the solution can vary depending on the specific application. Generally, a concentration of 1-5% (w/v) is effective. For example, a 5% (w/v) solution of ascorbic acid has been shown to be particularly effective in unmasking antigenic sites.

### pH and Buffering

The aldehyde scavenging agent solution may be buffered to maintain an optimal pH for the reaction. A pH range of 3-4 is typically used, as this pH facilitates the neutralization of aldehyde-based cross-links. Buffers such as citrate or phosphate can be added to achieve and maintain the desired pH.

### Nonionic Surfactants

The aldehyde scavenging agent solution may optionally include nonionic surfactants to improve the penetration of the solution into the tissue. Examples of suitable nonionic surfactants include Triton X-100, Tween 20, and NP-40. These surfactants can be added at concentrations ranging from 0.05% to 1% (v/v).

### Stabilizing Agents

Stabilizing agents may also be included in the aldehyde scavenging agent solution to enhance the stability of the reagents and improve the overall performance of the method. Examples of stabilizing agents include glycerol, sucrose, and bovine serum albumin (BSA). These agents can be added at concentrations ranging from 1% to 10% (w/v).

### Kit for Retrieving Proteins

The invention further provides a kit for retrieving proteins of interest in aldehyde-fixed tissues. The kit includes:

- Aldehyde scavenging agent solution
- Optional nonionic surfactant
- Optional stabilizing agents
- Instructions for use

The kit may also include additional reagents and materials necessary for the staining and visualization of proteins, such as primary and secondary antibodies, buffers, and mounting media.

### Method for Identifying Effective Antigen Retrieval Agents

To identify effective antigen retrieval agents, a method involving model peptides can be used. For example, human angiotensin I or ACTH (18-39) can be treated with formaldehyde to simulate fixed tissue conditions. The formylated peptides are then incubated with candidate aldehyde scavenging agents, and the reaction is monitored using mass spectrometry. Agents that effectively unmask antigenic sites will show a reduction in the molecular weight of the formylated peptides, indicating the cleavage of aldehyde-based cross-links.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Unmasking Antigenic Sites Using Ascorbic Acid

Tissue sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated. A 5% (w/v) solution of ascorbic acid was applied to the tissue, and the samples were heated at 95°C for 45 minutes. After cooling, the tissue was stained with a primary antibody specific to the protein of interest and a secondary antibody conjugated to a fluorophore. The results showed significantly improved staining intensity compared to untreated control tissues.

#### Example 2: Effectiveness of Different Aldehyde Scavenging Agents

Tissue sections were treated with different aldehyde scavenging agents, including ascorbic acid, 2-imidazolidinone, and tris. Each agent was tested at a concentration of 5% (w/v) and heated at 95°C for 45 minutes. The effectiveness of each agent in unmasking antigenic sites was evaluated by immunohistochemical staining. Ascorbic acid and 2-imidazolidinone showed the best performance, with significant improvements in protein detection.

## CONCLUSION

The present invention provides a novel method and kit for retrieving antigens from aldehyde-fixed tissues, improving the accuracy and reliability of protein detection in immunohistochemical studies. The use of aldehyde scavenging agents, optionally combined with nonionic surfactants and stabilizing agents, offers a significant advancement over existing methods, addressing the limitations of current fixation techniques.
