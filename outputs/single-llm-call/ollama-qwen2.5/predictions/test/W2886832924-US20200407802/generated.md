- The study analyzed whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) data from TCGA samples across eight cancer types to identify partially methylated domains (PMDs). An HMM-based tool called MethPipe was initially used with a 10 kb window size to detect PMDs in each sample.
- A shared set of 417 PMDs covering 13% of the genome was defined based on overlap among at least 21 of 30 tumors. The final classification of PMDs and HMDs (highly methylated domains) used a Gaussian mixture model applied to cross-sample standard deviation of methylation in 100 kb bins.
- For human samples, the SD threshold for classifying PMDs was set at 0.125, while for mouse it was lower at 0.09. More conservative "common" sets were defined with stricter SD criteria (SD>0.15 and <0.10).
- HM450 data analysis focused on solo-WCGW probes in common PMDs/HMDs to avoid confounding from globally demethylated cell types. The degree of PMD-associated hypomethylation was quantified as the difference between mean methylation of probes in common PMDs vs HMDs.
- Analysis of the IMR90 epigenome showed that solo-WCGW CpG methylation correlated with replication timing, histone marks, and gene expression. Hypomethylation-resistant CpGs were enriched at early-replicating, highly expressed genes marked by H3K36me3.
- The study provides insights into the dynamics of PMD formation in cancer genomes, highlighting the role of chromatin structure and replication timing as determinants of DNA methylation patterns. The results have implications for understanding epigenetic dysregulation in cancer development and progression.
