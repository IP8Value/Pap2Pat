- The study aimed to engineer Saccharomyces cerevisiae for high-level L-ornithine production using a modular pathway rewiring approach. Despite the complexity of the biosynthetic pathway, researchers systematically modified L-ornithine biosynthesis, regulation, and compartmentalization, as well as addressed overflow metabolism.
- The team used various strategies to enhance precursor supply, including overexpressing mitochondrial carrier proteins and introducing bacterial pathways. Notably, re-localizing a NADPH-dependent glutamate dehydrogenase to the mitochondria decreased L-ornithine production, highlighting the importance of careful pathway localization.
- To alleviate the Crabtree effect, researchers optimized glucose uptake by overexpressing a truncated form of MTH1 (MTH1-ΔT). This strategy significantly increased L-ornithine yield but reduced growth rate, making it less suitable for large-scale processes. Fed-batch fermentations were shown to be an effective alternative.
- The final engineered strain achieved a high L-ornithine titre of 192 mg/L and demonstrated the potential of using S. cerevisiae as a microbial cell factory for amino acid production. This proof-of-concept study provides valuable insights and strategies for future metabolic engineering efforts targeting complex biosynthetic pathways.
- Methods included constructing strains using plasmids or chromosomal integration, optimizing media conditions, and measuring metabolite concentrations via HPLC and GC-MS. The study highlights the importance of systematic pathway modification and careful evaluation of engineering strategies in shake-flask cultures before scaling up to fermenter processes.
