### Background of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics. Specifically, it pertains to a novel synthetic protein, SGP (Synthetic Growth Protein), designed to disrupt cell membranes and its use in treating various types of tumors.

## Summary of the Invention

SGP is a de novo designed protein with potent membrane-disrupting activity. It can be used to treat various cancers by directly injecting it into tumor sites. The invention also includes variants of SGP, such as SGP-L and SGP-E, which have reduced toxicity due to alterations in their amino acid sequences.

## SGP Effects on Cultured Cells

To evaluate the effects of SGP on cell membranes, we treated multiple human cell lines of different origins. SGP led to severe disruption of cell membranes, resulting in almost 100% loss of viability over 10 minutes in the absence of matrigel. This effect was consistent across various cell types, including malignant and non-neoplastic cells.

## LC50 Determination

The LC50 (lethal concentration) for SGP was determined by testing it on a panel of human cell lines. The LC50 values were consistently in the range of 3 μM, indicating that SGP is highly toxic to both normal and cancerous cells at similar concentrations.

## Comparison with Inactive Variants

SGP-L and SGP-E, which have altered amino acid sequences, showed significantly reduced toxicity compared to SGP. The LC50 values for these variants were increased by at least 10-fold in all cell types tested, demonstrating that the integrity of the SGP helices is crucial for its membrane-disrupting activity.

## Anti-tumor Activity in Vivo

Given the potent membrane-disrupting activity of SGP, we evaluated its anti-tumor activity in nude mice bearing human tumor xenografts. Mice were treated with local injections of SGP at concentrations of 100 μM and 1 mM. Tumor volumes were significantly smaller in SGP-treated groups compared to PBS-treated controls.

## Histopathological Analysis

Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated tumors showed widespread cell death, as evidenced by condensed nuclei and extensive apoptosis. There was no effect on the extracellular matrix, confirming that SGP specifically targets tumor cells without affecting surrounding tissues.

## Skin Toxicity Evaluation

Intradermal or subcutaneous injections of SGP did not produce any adverse effects, such as necrosis or inflammation, in normal skin. This suggests that SGP's activity is limited to the presence of lipid bilayers and is inactivated by extracellular matrix components.

## Mechanism of Action

SGP physically disrupts membrane architecture, leading to cell lysis. In vitro, this effect is potent and immediate, but in vivo, it is restricted to tumor sites due to the protective effect of the extracellular matrix. This mechanism explains the lack of toxicity seen with SGP when injected subcutaneously or intradermally.

## Conclusion

SGP represents a novel class of anti-cancer proteins with significant therapeutic potential. Its de novo design and unique mechanism of action make it a promising candidate for treating various types of cancers, particularly those that are difficult to treat with conventional therapies.

By addressing the specific needs of cancer treatment, SGP offers a new approach to combatting this devastating disease. Further research is ongoing to optimize its efficacy and explore potential applications in clinical settings.
