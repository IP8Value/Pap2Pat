- **PS as a Cancer Biomarker**: Phosphatidylserine (PS), typically found on the inner leaflet of cell membranes, becomes exposed on the surface of cancer cells. This makes PS a unique and ubiquitous biomarker for various cancers, offering a broad target for developing novel anticancer therapies. The ubiquity of PS across different types of cancer cells enhances its potential as a therapeutic and diagnostic tool.

- **Current Clinical Trials**: Several ongoing clinical trials are evaluating the efficacy of PS-targeting treatments like SapC-DOPS and bavituximab. These trials aim to improve therapeutic outcomes by leveraging the unique properties of PS. The results from these studies could pave the way for new, more effective cancer treatments that target this common biomarker.

- **Advantages in Imaging**: PS-targeting modalities offer significant advantages in imaging applications. By selectively highlighting tumors, these techniques can enhance diagnostic accuracy and guide treatment planning. The ability to image PS exposure specifically in cancer cells provides a powerful tool for early detection and monitoring of disease progression, contributing to more personalized and effective care.

- **Combination Therapies**: The highest therapeutic impact is likely to come from combining PS-targeting therapies with existing treatments. For example, using electric fields to modulate surface PS can enhance the efficacy of drugs like SapC-DOPS or bavituximab. This approach may increase tumor cell death while reducing the doses of more toxic chemotherapies, leading to better outcomes and fewer side effects.

- **Future Directions**: Future research should focus on in vivo and clinical studies to validate the use of electric fields for modulating surface PS exposure. Additionally, combining PS-targeting therapies with other treatments such as chemotherapy, radiation, and immune checkpoint inhibitors holds great promise. These combinations could significantly improve treatment outcomes and reduce the overall burden of cancer.

- **Conclusion**: The unique properties of PS make it an attractive target for both therapeutic and diagnostic applications in cancer. Emerging technologies that enhance PS-targeting efficacy have significant potential to transform cancer diagnostics and treatment. By integrating these innovative approaches with existing therapies, we can achieve more effective and personalized care for cancer patients.
