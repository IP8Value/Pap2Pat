- The study investigated the efficacy and pharmacokinetics of a novel atovaquone nanoformulation (ATQSDN) for malaria prophylaxis in mice. Mice were dosed once intramuscularly with ATQSDN or placebo, then challenged with P. berghei sporozoites 14 days later.
- Atovaquone concentration in plasma was measured by UPLC-MS/MS and showed a half-life of over 2 weeks. The nanoformulation provided complete protection against sporozoite challenge for at least 6 weeks post-dose compared to placebo controls.
- However, the ATQSDN failed to protect mice from blood stage parasite challenge, indicating its primary activity is in preventing liver-stage infection. Parasites that recrudesced after prophylaxis failure remained fully susceptible to oral atovaquone treatment.
- The study demonstrated the potential of long-acting nanoformulations for malaria chemoprevention, but also highlighted the need for combination approaches targeting both liver and blood stages of the parasite life cycle. Further studies are warranted to optimize dosing regimens.
