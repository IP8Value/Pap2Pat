SEGA (3a), a tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid, effectively prevents NSAID-induced mitochondrial pathology, apoptosis, and gastropathy by blocking MOS through scavenging intramitochondrial O₂· and free iron. This small molecule is lipophilic and stable in physiological conditions, allowing it to enter mitochondria and mitigate oxidative stress. SEGA (3a) outperforms standard antioxidants and gastroprotective agents, demonstrating superior efficacy against indomethacin-induced gastric injury.

The design of SEGA (3a) integrates the antioxidant properties of gallic acid with the lipophilic nature of tryptamine. The 5-hydroxy group in the indole moiety is crucial for its activity, as evidenced by reduced effectiveness when this group is absent or modified. This hybrid molecule targets mitochondria, addressing a key site of oxidative stress in NSAID-induced gastropathy.

Indomethacin was chosen as the representative NSAID due to its frequent use in gastrointestinal toxicity studies. The selected dose (5 mM for in vitro and 48 mg/kg b.w. for in vivo) aligns with previous research. SEGA (3a) significantly reduces indomethacin-induced mitochondrial ROS, preventing functional disruption and subsequent gastric injury. This mechanism underscores the importance of targeting mitochondria to combat NSAID toxicity.

SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior gastroprotective efficacy compared to standard iron chelators and antioxidants, with an ED₅₀ of 6.9 mg/kg b.w. It also outperforms histamine H₂ receptor antagonists like ranitidine but is slightly less effective than proton pump inhibitors omeprazole and lansoprazole. Despite this, SEGA (3a) offers advantages by targeting intramitochondrial oxidative stress without the adverse effects associated with proton pump inhibitors.

The proposed mechanism of action for SEGA (3a) involves its entry into mitochondria where it scavenges ROS and chelates free iron, preventing MOS and subsequent protein oxidation, lipid peroxidation, and thiol depletion. It also inhibits the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis by modulating caspase activation and regulating bcl-2 and bax expression. This multifaceted approach makes SEGA (3a) a promising candidate for protecting against NSAID-induced gastric injury.

In conclusion, SEGA (3a) is a novel small molecule with significant potential in mitigating NSAID-induced gastropathy. Its ability to target mitochondria and address oxidative stress through ROS scavenging and iron chelation sets it apart from existing treatments. Further research and clinical trials are warranted to explore its therapeutic applications and safety profile.
