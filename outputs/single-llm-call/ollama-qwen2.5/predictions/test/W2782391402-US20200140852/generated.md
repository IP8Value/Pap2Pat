- The study demonstrates how the N-end rule pathway applies to non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) and can be engineered for altered classification of these molecules. Posttranslational proofreading eliminates false-positive protein expression, improving OTS selectivity and biocontainment efficacy. This method enhances the ability to distinguish between nsAAs and standard AAs, which is critical for various applications like screening natural synthetases, sense codon reassignment, posttranslational modifications, and industrial production of proteins containing nsAAs.
- The researchers used a combination of orthogonal translation systems (OTSs) and engineered N-recognins to achieve selective incorporation and degradation of proteins with specific N-terminal residues. By constructing plasmids with UBP1, ClpS, and ClpP genes, they created a posttranslational proofreading system that degrades misfolded or incorrectly synthesized proteins containing nsAAs at the N-terminus.
- To evaluate and evolve OTSs, the team developed a reporter construct with ubiquitin fused to superfolder GFP (sfGFP). This allowed them to monitor protein expression levels in response to different nsAAs. They also engineered ClpS variants using rational mutagenesis to alter the recognition profile of N-end destabilizing residues.
- The study validated the proofreading method by evolving a BipA OTS, resulting in increased selectivity and biocontainment efficacy. Biocontained strains with the evolved OTS showed lower escape frequencies and improved fitness compared to those with the parental OTS. This enhancement is significant for industrial applications, as it allows safer growth of more cells without concern for accidental environmental release.

- Materials and methods included constructing orthogonal translation system plasmids with MjTyrRS-derived aaRSs and tRNA Tyr CUA opt. Posttranslational proofreading plasmids were created using an N-terminally truncated UBP1 gene from Saccharomyces cerevisiae, along with E. coli clpS and clpP genes. Rational engineering of ClpS variants involved dividing the clpS gene into two amplicons with degenerate sequences at specific codons.
- Reporter plasmids were constructed by cloning Ub-UAG-sfGFP, Ub-UAG-sfGFP_151UAG, and Ub-M-sfGFP_151UAG fusions downstream of the TET promoter in pZE21 vectors. These constructs served as templates for PCR amplification and genomic integration. The study also included detailed protocols for plasmid construction, strain generation, and experimental validation of proofreading efficacy and biocontainment.
