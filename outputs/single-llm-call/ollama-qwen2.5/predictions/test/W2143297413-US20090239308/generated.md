- Fluidigm's digital array offers a robust platform for DNA quantitation and copy number variation (CNV) studies. It is sequence-specific, easy-to-use, and flexible. Statistical sampling and estimation theories are applied to analyze CNV data from the digital array, enabling accurate computation of true target concentrations and their ratios with associated confidence intervals.
- In the spike-in experiment, a 65-base oligonucleotide identical to part of the human RPP30 gene was used as a synthetic construct. This construct was mixed with quantitated human genomic DNA at various known ratios (1:1 to 1:3.5) to simulate CNV samples.
- The digital array accurately estimated the known ratios, with 95% confidence intervals shrinking as more panels were used. At 3 or more panels, there was no overlap between confidence intervals for different ratios, demonstrating high precision and reliability of the platform for CNV studies.
- Materials and methods involved preparing a 10 µl reaction mix containing TaqMan assays for RPP30 and RNase P genes, sample loading reagent, and DNA. The mixture was partitioned into 765 chambers per panel and thermocycled on the BioMark system. FAM and VIC signals were recorded to count positive chambers.
- After PCR, Digital PCR Analysis software processed the data to count FAM-positive (RPP30) and VIC-positive (RNase P) chambers. Known ratios of RPP30 synthetic construct to genomic DNA were added to simulate CNV samples with 2-7 gene copies per diploid cell. Five panels were used for each ratio, with pooled analysis performed by summing positive chambers across different panel numbers.
- The mathematical and algorithmic solutions developed enable computing true target concentrations and ratios of multiple sequences along with confidence intervals. This provides an elegant application of statistical theories to the important real-world problem of CNV analysis on digital arrays, enhancing their utility for precise genetic studies.
