- The novel Mach-Zehnder electro-optic modulator design utilizes compact routing waveguides and high-performance modulating waveguides on a Si-LN wafer. This configuration avoids etching or bonding of thin-film LN, achieving a modulation bandwidth over 350 GHz and efficiency of 1.76 Vcm with a 5 mm interaction length.
- The proposed modulators support a bend radius of 10 μm and an edge-to-edge waveguide separation of 0.7 μm with a beat length of ∼1 cm, enabling highly compact photonic integrated circuits (PICs). These features are critical for realizing ultra-compact, large-scale PICs.
- The device fabrication process includes wafer bonding, Si patterning, etching, and metal deposition. An alternative approach without Si patterning involves using a heterogenous Si-LN wafer, leveraging robust and compatible fabrication technologies to enhance integration of high-performance electro-optic modulators in PICs.
- Simulation results show that the modulation bandwidth is sensitive to variations in device parameters such as etch depth and waveguide width. The optical group index and confinement in LN are affected by these parameters, impacting the overall performance of the modulator. Careful optimization is required for optimal device operation.
- The thickness of LN plays a crucial role in optimizing VπL and bandwidth. A 700 nm thick LN layer achieves minimal index mismatch and a large bandwidth exceeding 350 GHz with a 5 mm interaction length. Increasing LN thickness enhances optical confinement, reducing VπL but potentially decreasing bandwidth due to increased loss.
- The design demonstrates the potential for achieving impressively low Vπ values while maintaining high modulation bandwidths. For example, an interaction length of 11 mm can achieve a Vπ value of ∼1.6 V with a corresponding bandwidth of ∼75 GHz. This balance between Vπ and bandwidth is essential for various photonic applications.
- The study highlights the importance of device process control on coupling efficiency and modulation performance. Simulations show that variations in etch depth and waveguide width have minimal impact on coupling efficiency, but can significantly affect optical group index and confinement in LN, influencing overall modulator performance.
