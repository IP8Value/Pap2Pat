### Introduction

The study focuses on the characterization of a sesquiterpene synthase gene, SiTPS, from the endophytic fungus *Serendipita indica*. This enzyme was found to produce viridiflorol, a rare sesquiterpenoid with potential antimicrobial properties. The research aimed to understand the role of SiTPS in plant colonization and defense mechanisms.

### S. Indica Possesses a Viridiflorol Synthase

The in vitro and in vivo characterization of SiTPS revealed that it accepts E,E-FPP as a substrate to produce viridiflorol, a sesquiterpenoid with a 7/5/3 tricyclic scaffold. Phylogenetic analysis placed SiTPS in Clade I, indicating it performs 1,10 cyclization of E,E-FPP. This enzyme is the second fungal viridiflorol synthase to be identified.

### Overexpression of SiTPS Does Not Affect S. Indica Colonization Ability

SiTPS was found to be upregulated in colonized tomato roots at 11 dpi. However, overexpressing SiTPS in a mutant strain did not affect its colonization ability compared to the wild type or an empty vector control. This suggests that while SiTPS is induced during colonization, it may play a role beyond enhancing fungal colonization.

### Viridiflorol Could Serve as a Defense Compound

An in vitro growth assay showed that viridiflorol inhibited the germination and growth of the phytopathogenic fungus *Colletotrichum truncatum* at concentrations above 100 μg/mL. This indicates that viridiflorol could serve as a defense compound against other plant-associated fungi, providing S. indica with an advantage in competitive environments.

### Discussion

The identification and characterization of SiTPS add to the understanding of fungal terpenoid biosynthesis and their potential roles in symbiotic interactions. While overexpression did not enhance colonization, viridiflorol's antimicrobial properties suggest it could play a defensive role against pathogens. Further studies with knockout mutants and co-cultures are needed to fully understand its function.

### Patents

The authors have filed a patent application (U.S. Patent Application Serial No.: 62/899,391) titled "Method for producing the sesquiterpene viridiflorol with a fungal enzyme," which describes the function of the S. indica terpene synthase and its potential applications in producing viridiflorol.

### Conclusion

The study provides insights into the role of fungal terpenoids in plant-microbe interactions, highlighting the potential of viridiflorol as a defense compound. Understanding these mechanisms could lead to new strategies for enhancing plant health and productivity in agriculture.
