- C57BL/6 mice were inoculated with pools of three genetically characterized H. pylori clones expressing wild-type or mutant urease to assess colonization levels and immune responses over time. Stomachs were harvested, homogenized, and plated on selective media to quantify bacterial growth.

- Urease activity and stability were evaluated by measuring pH changes in phenol red solution after acid treatment and urea addition. This assay assessed the ability of urease to function under acidic conditions, reflecting its stability and activity in vivo.

- Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of mutant urease proteins in H. pylori harvested from mouse stomachs, using anti-FLAG and anti-urease antibodies. This validated successful transformation and expression of the modified urease genes.

- ELISA was used to measure urease-specific IgG levels in mouse sera at various time points, providing insights into the immune response against mutant and wild-type urease over the course of infection.
