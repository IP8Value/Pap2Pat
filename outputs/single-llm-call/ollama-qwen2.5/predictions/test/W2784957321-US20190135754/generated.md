## Summary of the Invention

The invention relates to a method for performing nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation using α-acyl lactones and lactams as prochiral nucleophiles in conjunction with allylic alcohols as electrophilic coupling partners. This method provides an efficient approach to construct quaternary stereocenters, which are valuable building blocks in organic synthesis and drug development.

## Background

The construction of quaternary stereocenters is a significant challenge in organic synthesis due to their importance in pharmaceuticals and natural products. Transition metal-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation has emerged as a powerful method for this purpose, but the use of α-acyl lactones or lactams as prochiral nucleophiles remains underdeveloped. This gap prompted our investigation into developing a nickel-catalyzed system that could efficiently and selectively produce quaternary stereocenters using easily accessible starting materials.

## Detailed Description

### Definitions
For the purposes of this invention, "α-acyl lactones" and "α-acyl lactams" refer to cyclic esters or amides with an acyl group attached at the α-position. "Allylic alcohols" are alcohols with a double bond adjacent to the hydroxyl group.

### Catalyst System
The method employs nickel(II) catalysts, specifically Ni(COD)₂, in combination with chiral bisphosphine ligands. The choice of ligand is critical for achieving high enantioselectivity. Commercially available ligands such as (R)-P-phos have been found to be particularly effective.

### Reaction Conditions
The reaction is typically carried out at low temperatures (0-10 °C) in an ethereal solvent like diethyl ether or toluene. The catalyst and ligand loadings are optimized to achieve both high yield and enantioselectivity. For example, a 5 mol% loading of Ni(COD)₂ and 12 mol% of the chiral bisphosphine ligand in Et₂O at -10 °C can provide excellent results.

### Scope and Substrate Tolerance
The method shows broad substrate tolerance. Both α-methoxycarbonyl lactones and bicyclic lactones can be used to form quaternary stereocenters with good yields and enantioselectivity. Various substituted allyl alcohols, including those with electron-rich and electron-poor aryl substituents, are compatible. Heteroaryl and aliphatic electrophiles also perform well under these conditions.

### Lactam Nucleophiles
The method can be extended to α-ester lactams, which provide even higher enantioselectivity compared to their lactone counterparts. The benzoyl-protecting group is particularly effective for lactam substrates. For example, the reaction of α-ethoxycarbonyl benzoyl-protected lactam with branched cinnamyl alcohol yields a linear product in 74% yield and 90% ee.

### Mechanistic Insights
Mechanistic studies suggest that a nickel π-allyl intermediate is likely involved in the catalytic cycle. This insight helps explain the formation of a single product from two electrophilic coupling partners, highlighting the flexibility of the catalyst system.

### Synthetic Utility
The quaternary stereocenter products can be further derivatized to access various functionalized molecules. For instance, selective reduction of lactones provides diols, and vinyl Grignard addition yields enones. Reduction of lactams with lithium aluminum hydride produces chiral piperidine derivatives, which are valuable in medicinal chemistry.

### Conclusion
In summary, the nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation method described herein offers a robust and versatile approach to constructing quaternary stereocenters. The use of commercially available ligands and easily accessible starting materials makes this method practical for synthetic applications in both academic and industrial settings.

## Claims

1. A method for performing nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation, comprising:
   - Reacting an α-acyl lactone or lactam with an allylic alcohol in the presence of a nickel(II) catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand.
   - Carrying out the reaction at a temperature between 0 °C and 10 °C in an ethereal solvent.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein the α-acyl lactone or lactam is selected from α-methoxycarbonyl lactones, bicyclic lactones, and α-ester lactams.

3. The method of claim 1, wherein the allylic alcohol is substituted with an aryl, heteroaryl, or aliphatic group.

4. The method of claim 1, wherein the nickel(II) catalyst is Ni(COD)₂ and the chiral bisphosphine ligand is (R)-P-phos.

5. The method of claim 1, further comprising reducing the lactone product to form a diol or performing a vinyl Grignard addition to form an enone.

6. The method of claim 1, wherein the α-ester lactam substrate includes a benzoyl-protecting group.

7. The method of claim 1, further comprising reducing the lactam product with lithium aluminum hydride to form a chiral piperidine derivative.

## Examples

### Example 1: Synthesis of Quaternary Lactone 3aa
**Materials:** α-Ethoxycarbonyl lactone (1a), allyl alcohol (2a), Ni(COD)₂, (R)-P-phos, diethyl ether.
**Procedure:** Combine 1a (1.0 equiv), 2a (1.2 equiv), Ni(COD)₂ (10 mol%), and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%) in diethyl ether at -10 °C. Stir for 48 hours, then quench with water and extract with ethyl acetate. Purify the product by column chromatography to obtain 3aa in 80% yield and 85% ee.

### Example 2: Synthesis of Quaternary Lactam 5aa
**Materials:** α-Ethoxycarbonyl benzoyl-protected lactam (4a), branched cinnamyl alcohol, Ni(COD)₂, (R)-P-phos, toluene.
**Procedure:** Combine 4a (1.0 equiv), branched cinnamyl alcohol (1.2 equiv), Ni(COD)₂ (5 mol%), and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%) in toluene at 0 °C. Stir for 24 hours, then quench with water and extract with ethyl acetate. Purify the product by column chromatography to obtain 5aa in 74% yield and 90% ee.

### Example 3: Reduction of Lactone 3aa to Diol 6
**Materials:** Quaternary lactone 3aa, lithium aluminum hydride, tetrahydrofuran.
**Procedure:** Dissolve 3aa (1.0 equiv) in tetrahydrofuran and add lithium aluminum hydride (1.5 equiv) at 0 °C. Stir for 2 hours, then quench with water and extract with ethyl acetate. Purify the product by column chromatography to obtain diol 6 in 88% yield.

### Example 4: Reduction of Lactam 5aa to Piperidine Derivative 9
**Materials:** Quaternary lactam 5aa, lithium aluminum hydride, tetrahydrofuran.
**Procedure:** Dissolve 5aa (1.0 equiv) in tetrahydrofuran and add lithium aluminum hydride (1.5 equiv) at 0 °C. Stir for 2 hours, then quench with water and extract with ethyl acetate. Purify the product by column chromatography to obtain piperidine derivative 9 in 74% yield.
