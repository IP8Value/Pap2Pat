- A new threshold-based system was developed to detect potentially destabilizing events in manual wheelchairs used by individuals with spinal cord injuries. The system uses wheelchair-mounted IMUs to monitor acceleration and angular velocity during collisions and turns.

- During laboratory testing, the event detection controller achieved over 90% accuracy for both collision and turn classification. Two out of three subjects experienced improved trunk stability during collisions when stimulation was applied to paraspinal and hip muscles.

- However, significant delays in detecting turns based on angular velocity limited the system's ability to effectively restore lateral trunk posture during sharp turns. Alternative sensor modalities or monitoring angular acceleration may improve performance for this application.

- This study represents a promising step towards developing an assistive device that could increase safety and independence for manual wheelchair users with spinal cord injuries by restoring trunk stability in response to destabilizing events. Further research is needed on a larger scale.

- The event detection system was tested using both implanted and surface stimulation, suggesting potential applicability beyond implant recipients. Future work will examine use outside the lab setting during activities of daily living, as well as develop algorithms for additional destabilizing scenarios like rough terrain or ramps.
