- This study demonstrated that increasing carbon source concentrations in S. cerevisiae cultures from the conventional 2-4% to 6-8% produced superior-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs after a longer fermentation period of 6 days. Higher yields and purities of L1 protein were obtained, with better antigenicity and immunogenicity in mice.
- The higher carbon concentrations allowed for prolonged culture durations, providing more time for yeast cells to produce correctly folded L1 proteins that assemble into highly immunogenic VLPs. This contrasts with typical shorter 48-72 hour fermentations used at lower carbon levels.
- Evidence suggests the redox state of host cells plays a key role in viral infection and replication. The authors found prolonged cultures producing HPV L1 had decreasing GSSG and increasing GSH, indicating significant changes to intracellular redox status over time that may impact VLP formation.
- Various culture conditions have been explored for VLP production in yeast, with this study highlighting the importance of carbon source concentration and culture duration. The results suggest more diverse criteria are needed to monitor VLP quality, as both in vivo and in vitro environments influence their properties like structural integrity and immunogenicity.
