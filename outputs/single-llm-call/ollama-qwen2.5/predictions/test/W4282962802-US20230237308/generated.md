- The document explores the calibration of clipping scalars using OCTAV versus MSE sweep methods in BERT models. It highlights that while both methods should yield similar results, OCTAV often outperforms the MSE sweep at low precision levels (4-to-6-bit). This discrepancy is attributed to non-convex behavior of the MSE function when data has large outliers.
- For most layers, the calibrated clipping scalars from OCTAV and MSE sweep are identical. However, at specific layers like 28, 33, 40, and 64, OCTAV produces smaller scalars. This is due to the presence of significant outliers in the data distributions at these layers.
- The empirical PDFs of tensors at layers 63 and 64 show a stark contrast: layer 63 has a typical distribution with density around zero, while layer 64 exhibits large outliers. OCTAV converges to the local minimum closest to zero, balancing discretization and clipping noise, whereas the MSE sweep can be skewed towards representing outliers.
- The MSE function at layer 64 has two minima: one close to zero and another near the maximum scalar value. For some tensors, the second minimum yields a lower MSE, leading the MSE sweep to select it. This results in a calibrated scalar that zeroes out non-outlier data, whereas OCTAV's solution caters to all data.
- The authors conclude that OCTAV is more reliable for calibrating clipping scalars, especially in the presence of outliers, as it converges to the minimum that balances discretization and clipping noise. This aligns with experimental results showing higher accuracy for static-OCTAV compared to the MSE sweep at low precision levels.
