- **Leakage Mechanism in Three-Dot System**: In a three-dot system, the ground state charge configuration is (1, 2, 1). The exchange Hamiltonian connects this to excited states (0, 3, 1) and (1, 3, 0) via tunnelling energies. Noise can cause decoherence, leading the system to collapse into these high-energy states, causing leakage errors. This process is significant during exchange interactions when the energy difference between states is small.

- **Perturbation Theory Analysis**: For a two-dot system, perturbation theory helps analyze the eigenstates and eigenenergies of the Hamiltonian. The dominant diagonal part \( H^{(0)} \) and the off-diagonal tunnelling part \( rH^{(1)} \) are considered. Perturbation theory shows that the ground state can mix with excited states, leading to leakage errors, while triplet and orthogonal ionized states remain unchanged.

- **Leakage Oscillation**: Starting in the singlet state \( |S \), the probability of leaking into the ionized state \( |ion^+ \) is given by perturbation theory. This probability oscillates over time as the system evolves under the Hamiltonian, and it can be calculated using the overlap between the initial and final states. The energy shift due to perturbations also affects the exchange interaction.

- **Threshold Simulation Details**: Monte Carlo simulations are used to model stabilizer checks in a surface code with errors. Error tables for X and Z stabilizers provide probabilities of error patterns. For a distance-d surface code, d rounds of measurements are performed to mitigate measurement errors, creating a 3D grid of parity check results. Minimum-weight perfect matching (MWPM) is used to correct errors, and the simulation module is available on Github.
