- A novel method for producing aligned Ni nanowires from a homogeneous solution in ambient conditions was presented. The process involves synthesizing, printing, and curing a Ni precursor ink, suitable for various applications like antennas and magnetic sensors.

- This study demonstrated aerosol printing of the ink on glass substrates, but it can be adapted to flexible and thermally sensitive materials. Different rheological properties can be developed for other printing techniques such as flexography, inkjet, and screen printing.

- Thermal reduction in a weak magnetic field produced large areas of pure aligned Ni nanowires with enhanced anisotropic electrical and magnetic properties, requiring no further processing. This is one of the first examples of printed metallic Ni from a precursor.

- The film structure and morphology can be manipulated to produce novel patterned structures with unique electronic and magnetic properties. The lowest electrical resistivity was observed for nanowire grids, showing potential for commercial applications like skin-attachable loudspeakers and microphones.

- Further research aims to reduce the electrical resistivity and explore other sintering techniques such as photonic methods to study Ni alignment, enhancing the versatility and performance of these printed structures.
