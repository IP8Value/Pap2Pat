# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Electromagnetic rotary drives are widely used in various industrial applications due to their efficiency and reliability. Conventional control systems for these drives typically rely on mechanical bearings to support the rotor, which can be problematic in high-speed or extreme environment applications. The limitations of conventional control systems include increased maintenance requirements, reduced operational lifespan, and limited performance under harsh conditions. To address these issues, a bearingless motor-generator has been developed. This innovative device integrates both motor and magnetic bearing functions into a single unit, eliminating the need for separate mechanical bearings. However, to fully realize the potential of this technology, a new control system is required.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel control system for a bearingless motor-generator. The control system enables precise control over both the motor's torque and the rotor's levitation forces by individually stimulating multiple pole pairs within the motor. This approach allows all copper and iron in the motor to contribute to either levitation or rotation, depending on the system's needs. In one embodiment, the bearingless motor-generator is configured with a cylindrical shape, where the control system manages three separate pole pairs. The invention also extends to conical motors with two stators and a rotor, allowing for full 5-axis levitation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Illustrate Cylindrical Motor Control System

The cylindrical motor control system is designed to manage the forces generated by individually stimulated pole pairs. Each pole pair can be excited independently, allowing for precise control over the rotor's position and motion. The control system utilizes a complex power electronics drive that includes 18 switches to generate arbitrary rotor reference frame d and q axis currents in each of the three individual pole-pair systems. This configuration enables the generation of different forces on the rotor, which are necessary for both levitation and torque production.

### Describe Advantages of New Control System

The new control system offers several advantages over conventional approaches. By individually stimulating pole pairs, the system can dynamically adjust the distribution of power between levitation and rotation. This flexibility reduces the need for additional components and simplifies the overall design. Additionally, the fault-tolerant nature of the 18-switch drive configuration ensures that the motor can continue to operate even if some switches fail, enhancing reliability.

### Phase of Force Vectors as a Function of Electrical Angle

The phase of the force vectors generated by each pole pair is crucial for effective control. Using magnetic circuit simulations, it has been determined that there are always six possible force vectors that can be applied to the rotor from the positive and negative rotor reference frame d-axis currents applied to each of the three pole-pairs. These forces bound six distinct regions, and a desired force within any region can be produced optimally by using the vectors bordering that region as the basis.

### Control Scheme Using Force Vectors

The control scheme involves calculating the phase of the desired force and comparing it with the available force vectors. The two force vectors bordering the region containing the desired force are then chosen as the basis. The desired force is transformed from the x, y basis to the basis containing the two vectors to be used. This approach ensures that the required rotor reference frame d-axis currents can be accurately determined to produce the desired force.

### Magnetic Circuit Model and Force Commands

The magnetic circuit model of the motor includes detailed simulations of the yoke, backiron, and each gap, tooth, and magnet. By applying arbitrary force commands and converting them to required rotor reference frame d-axis currents, the system can generate a wide range of forces. The developed rotor force using this technique has been validated through extensive simulations, demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving precise control over the rotor's position and motion.

### Conical Air-Gap for Axial Force Generation

The conical air-gap design is another innovation that enhances the capabilities of the bearingless motor-generator. By combining two conical motors on a single rotor with cones in opposite directions, it becomes possible to generate axial forces in addition to radial forces. The net z-directed force is proportional to the difference between the sums of the rotor reference frame d-axis currents in the top and bottom motors. This configuration allows for independent control over axial and radial forces, providing full 5-axis levitation.

### Axial Force Control Scheme

The axial force control scheme involves calculating the unintended axial force generated by the radial force blocks and correcting it using a z-force block. The z-force block sums all of the d-axis currents in the top and bottom motors and multiplies the difference by the axial stiffness to determine the net axial force. When an axial force command is received, the undesired axial force created by the radial control is subtracted from this command, and the required constant current is added to the top motor and subtracted from the bottom motor to prevent radial impact.

### Experimental Results and Validation

Experimental results have been obtained using a pair of prototype conical motors. The test setup included a dSPACE system with a 0.3 ms sample time. The experimental loop gain data was fit to a ratio of complex polynomials, and the control system's performance was validated through extensive testing. The results demonstrate that the new control system effectively manages both levitation and torque production, achieving precise control over the rotor's position and motion.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel control system for bearingless motor-generators that enables precise control over both levitation and rotation. By individually stimulating multiple pole pairs, the system can dynamically adjust power distribution and achieve full 5-axis levitation. The conical air-gap design further enhances the capabilities of the motor, allowing for independent control over axial and radial forces. Experimental results validate the effectiveness of the new control system, demonstrating its potential to revolutionize high-speed and extreme environment applications.
