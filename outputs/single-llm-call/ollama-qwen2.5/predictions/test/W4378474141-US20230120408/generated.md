- **Background and Challenges**: The development of large-scale MgB2 films has been hindered by magnesium's low melting point and high reactivity. This study addresses these issues by creating ultra-smooth, uniform MgB2 thin films on silicon substrates using RF sputtering and substrate biasing techniques.

- **Film Deposition and Annealing**: Precursor Mg-B composite films were deposited via RF magnetron sputtering with varying substrate biases. A boron capping layer was added to prevent magnesium evaporation during rapid thermal annealing, optimizing the critical temperature (Tc) of the films.

- **Substrate and Buffer Layer**: High-resistivity silicon substrates were used, with a low-stress silicon nitride buffer layer deposited by LPCVD. This buffer layer ensured adhesion and reduced stress, enabling the growth of uniform MgB2 films over large areas.

- **Device Fabrication Techniques**: Devices were fabricated using argon ion milling to remove the boron cap, followed by photolithography with a titanium-gold bilayer for passivation, hardmasking, and electrical contacts. This process allowed for high-yield fabrication of small critical features down to 0.5 µm.

- **Characterization Methods**: XPS depth profiling was used to analyze the chemical composition and layer structure of the films. DC resistance and current density were measured using a four-point method, while RF measurements were conducted in a liquid helium Dewar with a vector network analyzer.

- **Performance and Applications**: The developed MgB2 films demonstrated high Tc, Jc, and kinetic inductance (Lk), making them suitable for high-temperature/high-frequency superconducting devices. These properties open new possibilities for applications such as parametric amplifiers, frequency multipliers, and quantum computing qubits.

- **Conclusion**: The ultra-smooth wafer-scale MgB2 films developed in this study can be readily used for nano-and microdevice fabrication. By overcoming the challenges associated with magnesium's properties, we have achieved a technology ready for immediate implementation in various high-performance superconducting applications.
