# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biomarkers for detecting lymphoma, particularly Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL), in patients infected with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). The invention encompasses methods and systems for identifying and utilizing specific plasma proteins as biomarkers to diagnose HL at an early stage, thereby improving patient outcomes through timely intervention.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Lymphoma is a type of cancer that originates in the lymphatic system, which is part of the body's immune system. The most common forms of lymphoma are Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) and non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL). In individuals infected with HIV, the risk of developing HL is significantly higher compared to the general population, with estimates ranging from 5.6- to 14.7-fold increased risk [1]–[3]. This heightened risk is attributed to immunosuppression caused by HIV infection, which can lead to uncontrolled inflammatory responses and the activation of oncogenic viruses such as Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) [4].

Current methods for detecting HL are primarily symptom-based, leading to delayed diagnosis and more aggressive treatment regimens. Early detection of HL is crucial because it allows for less intensive treatments, reducing morbidity and mortality associated with chemotherapy and radiation therapy [6]. However, there is a significant gap in the availability of non-invasive, routine screening methods for early-stage HL, especially in HIV-infected individuals who are at higher risk.

The development of a blood-based test for lymphoma would address this critical need by providing a simple, cost-effective, and minimally invasive method for early detection. Such a test could significantly improve patient outcomes by enabling timely intervention and personalized treatment strategies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides biomarkers for the detection of Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) in HIV-infected individuals. These biomarkers are specific plasma proteins that have been identified through comprehensive proteomics analysis using accurate mass tag and time (AMT) tag technology. The method of detecting HL involves analyzing plasma samples from patients to identify the presence and levels of these biomarker proteins.

The invention further encompasses a test kit for diagnosing lymphoma, which includes reagents and materials necessary for the detection of the identified biomarkers. The diagnostic system integrates sample processing, liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS), data analysis, and statistical methods to accurately identify and quantify the biomarker proteins.

In summary, the invention offers a robust and reliable method for early detection of HL in HIV-infected patients, thereby facilitating timely and effective treatment interventions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention introduces methods and systems for detecting Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) in HIV-infected individuals by identifying specific plasma proteins as biomarkers. The methods involve comprehensive proteomics analysis using accurate mass tag and time (AMT) technology to identify and quantify these biomarker proteins.

### Sample Collection and Processing

Plasma samples are collected from HIV-infected patients, both with and without HL, for comparative analysis. The samples are processed to remove cellular debris and other contaminants, ensuring high-quality protein extracts for subsequent analysis. The sample processing steps may include centrifugation, filtration, and buffer exchange to prepare the samples for LC-MS analysis.

### Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) Analysis

The prepared plasma samples are analyzed using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS). The LC-MS system is configured to perform high-resolution separations of peptides and proteins, followed by mass spectrometric detection. The mobile phase composition is adjusted to achieve optimal separation and detection of the target biomarker proteins.

### Data Analysis

The raw data from the LC-MS analysis are processed using the AMT tag approach. This involves matching the detected peptides against a database of known tryptic peptides to identify and quantify the proteins present in the plasma samples. The peptide abundance measurements are normalized and statistically analyzed to identify peptides that show significant differences between the HL-positive and HL-negative groups.

### Statistical Methods

Statistical tests, including the non-parametric Mann-Whitney test and the modified Chi-square test (G-test), are employed to determine peptides with differential abundance due to signal intensities or presence-absence. The results of these tests are used to identify proteins that are significantly different between the two groups.

### Protein Inference and Pathway Analysis

The normalized peptide abundance values are used for protein inference, where the relative protein abundances in the two groups are calculated. Only proteins represented by two or more unique peptides are analyzed to ensure high confidence in the results. The identified proteins are further analyzed using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) software to determine common biological functions and pathways associated with HL.

### Identification of Biomarker Proteins

The proteomics analysis identified 60 proteins that met the criteria of being significantly different between the two groups and having at least a 1.5-fold difference in normalized signal intensity. These proteins include well-known acute-phase inflammatory proteins such as C-reactive protein, serum amyloid proteins, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase. The IPA analysis revealed that these proteins are associated with immune responses and cancer, suggesting their biological relevance to HL.

### Validation of Biomarkers

The identified biomarker proteins were validated by comparing them with previously reported studies on HL in the general population. Three of the proteins—C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase—were found to be increased in HL subjects without HIV, confirming their potential as reliable biomarkers for early detection.

### Test Kit and Diagnostic System

The invention further provides a test kit for diagnosing HL, which includes reagents and materials necessary for the detection of the identified biomarker proteins. The diagnostic system integrates sample processing, LC-MS analysis, data processing, and statistical methods to provide accurate and reliable results. The kit may also include controls and standards to ensure the accuracy and reproducibility of the test.

In conclusion, the present invention offers a comprehensive method for early detection of Hodgkin's lymphoma in HIV-infected individuals, utilizing specific plasma proteins as biomarkers. This approach has the potential to significantly improve patient outcomes by enabling timely intervention and personalized treatment strategies.
