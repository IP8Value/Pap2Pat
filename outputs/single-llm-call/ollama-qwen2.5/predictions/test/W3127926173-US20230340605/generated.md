- The study defines a protein signature score named ViRP that identifies good responders to neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) with chemotherapy plus bevacizumab in large HER2-negative primary breast cancers. Use of the ViRP score for selecting patients significantly enriches for those responding to treatment.
- Lasso regression was used to select proteins for the ViRP score, identifying nine key proteins. The Syk protein appears particularly important and is differentially expressed between pCR and non-pCR patients. Further studies are needed to establish causal relationships.
- Validation of the ViRP score in an independent clinical cohort (PROMIX) confirmed its robustness. While transitioning from protein to mRNA expression for a surrogate score, there was some performance drop, likely due to loss of phosphorylation information.
- The study found that estrogen receptor (ER) status did not significantly impact the response to treatment with Bev plus CTx, despite better overall response in ER-negative patients. This aligns with discrepancies seen in larger studies regarding the benefit of added bevacizumab in ER-positive or ER-negative subpopulations.
- Tumor cell content did not significantly influence the ViRP score or its individual proteins, suggesting that the score is relatively stable across different tumor purities. However, a composite tissue sample, including endothelial cells and vessels, is likely required for the score to function effectively.
- The ViRP score has potential applications beyond the neoadjuvant setting, possibly predicting responses to Bev plus CTx therapy in metastatic breast cancer. Additionally, studies are underway to investigate whether the signature can predict response in other solid cancers where bevacizumab is commonly used.
