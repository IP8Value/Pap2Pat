- The described system enables digital-to-analog conversion (DAC) of 12-lead ECG data with sufficient fidelity for most clinical needs. Reconstruction errors were highest in bundle branch blocks due to non-optimal sampling methods in ADCs, but newer devices using true simultaneous sampling may improve this.
- This DAC system offers greater flexibility and universality compared to existing commercial solutions. It allows for rapid second opinions from multiple automated interpretive programs, potentially through cloud-based servers.
- The system can enable use of less expensive or bulky ECG front ends in underserved areas or remote environments, as subsequent DAC permits use of any preferred ECG machine or program on the back end.
- Manufacturers may improve their ECG analytical software by rapidly learning from internal testing and objective competitions enabled by the DAC. This could lead to better consistency of automated interpretations within hospitals and across studies.
- A key limitation is that only a limited number of stored digital files were analyzed using hardware from two different manufacturers, requiring future validation with more data and conditions.
- The system's existence may stimulate ECG manufacturers to participate in more convenient, purely digital multi-manufacturer interchanges for 12-lead ECG second opinions, benefiting both patients and clinicians.
