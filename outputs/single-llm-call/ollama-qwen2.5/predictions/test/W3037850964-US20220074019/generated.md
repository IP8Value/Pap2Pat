- Underclay from coal formations contains REE concentrations of 250-353 ppm. Clay minerals like illite, halloysite, and kaolinite make up over 55% of the rock's bulk mineralogy. Citrate leaching is effective for extracting weakly bound REE from clay-rich sedimentary rocks.
- Bench-top and flow-through experiments show citrate is a promising leaching agent. It can selectively extract REE while minimizing release of gangue and radioactive elements, offering an environmentally friendly and cost-effective solution compared to traditional methods.
- Citrate's chelation properties allow pH-controlled selectivity against leaching unwanted elements. This method shows promise for extracting REE from various sedimentary rocks and coal mining waste. However, extraction efficiency remains low in flow-through experiments, requiring further optimization.
- Future work will focus on enhancing extraction efficiency through parameters like increasing the leach solution ratio and citrate concentration. Solid sorbent capture technology may help concentrate and purify REE from citrate-based PLS, enabling recycling of the leaching solution for multiple cycles.
- The study demonstrates that citrate leaching can recover up to 30% of total REE in bench-top experiments but remains below 1% in flow-through tests. Continued research aims to develop a technology economic evaluation and determine costs associated with scaling this process for larger operations, emphasizing the potential environmental benefits.
