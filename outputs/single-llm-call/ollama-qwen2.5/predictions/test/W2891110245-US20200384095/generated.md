### Introduction
Enteric fever, caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, remains a significant global health issue. Current vaccines offer limited protection, necessitating the development of more effective immunogens. Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) are promising candidates due to their ability to elicit robust immune responses. This study investigates a bivalent OMV-based vaccine against *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*.

### OMV Isolation and Characterization
OMVs were isolated from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* and characterized using proteomics. Both OMVs contained LPS, outer membrane proteins (OMPs), inner membrane proteins, cytosolic proteins, and virulence factors. This composition suggests that OMVs can bridge potent immunogenicity with protective immune responses against enteric fever.

### Immunogenicity of Bivalent OMV Vaccine
The bivalent OMV vaccine was administered orally to mice in three doses. Antigen-specific antibody responses, including anti-LPS, anti-Vi polysaccharide, anti-OMPs, and anti-whole cell lysate (WCL), were significantly higher compared to heat-killed immunogens. This indicates the strong immunogenic nature of OMVs.

### Humoral Immune Response
The bivalent OMV vaccine induced a robust humoral immune response. Serum from vaccinated mice showed high titers of antigen-specific antibodies, including IgG against LPS and Vi polysaccharide. These antibodies are crucial for complement fixation and opsonization, essential mechanisms for bacterial clearance.

### Cellular Immune Response
The cellular immune response was also significantly activated. Splenocytes from vaccinated mice showed increased populations of CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells. Elevated levels of Th1 and Th17 cytokines, such as IFN-γ and IL-17, indicate a strong cell-mediated immune response, crucial for preventing intracellular Salmonella infection.

### Protective Efficacy in Mice Model
In the protective study, immunized mice were challenged with lethal doses of *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*. The vaccinated group showed an 80-90% survival rate, with significantly lower bacterial burden in organs compared to non-immunized controls. This demonstrates the broad protection offered by the bivalent OMV vaccine.

### Adoptive Transfer Studies
Adoptive transfer of serum and splenocytes from immunized mice to naïve mice further confirmed the importance of both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses. Transferred serum and cells provided significant protection, highlighting the necessity of a balanced immune response for effective defense against typhoidal Salmonella.

### Mode of Action of OMV-Specific Antibodies
OMV-specific antibodies from vaccinated mice agglutinated *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*, indicating complement activation. Heat-inactivated sera also inhibited bacterial motility, crucial for mucin penetration and epithelial cell invasion. Additionally, these antibodies enhanced macrophage-mediated opsonization, contributing to the clearance of invasive strains.

### Conclusion
The bivalent OMV vaccine against *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* is immunogenic and induces both humoral and cellular immune responses, providing broad protection in a mouse model. This formulation shows promise for future clinical trials and could potentially offer long-term protection against enteric fever.
