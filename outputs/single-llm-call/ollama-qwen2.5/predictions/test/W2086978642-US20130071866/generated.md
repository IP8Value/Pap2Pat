- **Introduction**: The study aimed to evaluate blood ATP levels and A-Low Energy State (A-LES) as potential biomarkers for predicting mortality and assessing the severity of critical illness in ICU patients. The APACHE II score is commonly used but lacks real-time sensitivity.

- **Methods**: Blood ATP levels were measured using a phenol-based extraction method, and A-LES was calculated by combining ATP and lactate levels. Data from 155 healthy individuals and 42 critically ill ICU patients were analyzed.

- **Results - Normal Range**: The mean blood ATP level in healthy subjects was 0.62 (0.19) mM, with no significant effect of age or gender, though values tended to decrease slightly with age over 60 years.

- **Results - ICU Patients**: In critically ill patients, ATP levels were significantly lower, and A-LES was higher compared to healthy subjects. ATP levels normalized by total hemoglobin (tHb) were particularly low in non-survivors.

- **Prognostic Value of A-LES**: A-LES showed a clear trend, decreasing in survivors and increasing in non-survivors during ICU stay. The median A-LES at discharge was 2.7 for survivors and 38.9 for non-survivors, indicating its high sensitivity as a prognostic marker.

- **Comparison with APACHE II**: ROC analysis showed that A-LES and ATP levels were comparable to the APACHE II score in predicting mortality at ICU admission. However, A-LES provided better real-time evaluation of illness severity during ICU stay, especially for severely ill patients.

- **Discussion - Mechanism**: Blood ATP levels reflect cellular energy status, with RBCs being the primary source. A-LES combines ATP and lactate to provide a comprehensive measure of metabolic stress, making it a sensitive and real-time biomarker of critical illness.

- **Conclusion**: This study demonstrates that blood ATP levels and A-LES are valuable biomarkers for predicting mortality and assessing the severity of critical illness in ICU patients, offering advantages over traditional scores like APACHE II.
