- The study aimed to produce PCV-2 virus-like particles (VLPs) in plants through Agrobacterium-mediated transient expression. Plant-produced VLPs were purified and characterized using sucrose and CsCl gradients, SDS-PAGE, immunoblotting, and transmission electron microscopy.
- Immunogenicity of plant-made PCV-2 VLPs was evaluated in mice. Groups received either plant-made VLPs, fractionated plant protein, or a commercial subunit vaccine (Ingelvac CircoFLEX®). Mice were boosted twice at 2-week intervals before final serum collection and antibody response analysis.
- ELISA results showed that mice immunized with plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs developed specific anti-PCV-2 CP antibodies, comparable to those vaccinated with the commercial vaccine. This suggests the potential of plant-based production for developing cost-effective vaccines against PCV-2.
- The study demonstrated successful expression and purification of functional PCV-2 VLPs in plants, highlighting the feasibility of using plant-based systems for rapid and scalable vaccine production. Further research is needed to optimize yields and assess protective efficacy in target species.
