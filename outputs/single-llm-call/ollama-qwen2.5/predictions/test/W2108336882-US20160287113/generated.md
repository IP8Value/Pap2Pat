### Background and Objectives

The study aimed to evaluate the performance of three-dimensional nanostructured boron-doped diamond (3D-nanostructured BDD) for neural recording and stimulation. The researchers sought to determine if this material could offer advantages over conventional materials, such as lower impedance and higher charge injection capacity, which are crucial for effective neural interfaces.

### Methodology

The study utilized microelectrodes made of 3D-nanostructured BDD and compared their performance with conventional boron-doped diamond (BDD) microelectrodes. The electrodes were characterized using cyclic voltammetry in different electrolytes, including LiClO₄ and PBS. Additionally, the biocompatibility of the 3D-nanostructured BDD surfaces was assessed by culturing embryonic neural cells on these surfaces.

### Results

The impedance of 20-μm-diameter 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes was found to be 50 kΩ at 1 kHz, comparable to state-of-the-art neural microelectrode materials. The noise level was notably reduced, allowing the detection of small amplitude signals in the range of 10-20 mV. Cyclic voltammetry revealed a wide potential window and high charge storage capacity, indicating the material's suitability for safe charge injection.

### Biocompatibility

Embryonic neural cell cultures on 3D-nanostructured BDD surfaces showed that neurons could attach, survive, and grow neurites with no visible difference in neurite extension compared to conventional BDD. This confirms the biocompatibility of 3D-nanostructured BDD for neural cell cultures, a crucial aspect for long-term neural interfaces.

### Discussion

The 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes demonstrated excellent performance in terms of impedance, noise level, and charge injection capacity. The wide potential window and high biostability of diamond, combined with the large surface area provided by nanostructuration, make this material a promising candidate for neural interfaces. Further in vivo studies are needed to fully evaluate its potential.

### Conclusion

The study concluded that 3D-nanostructured BDD offers good performance for neural recording and stimulation. The microelectrodes' low impedance, high charge injection capacity, and biocompatibility suggest significant benefits for the development of new MEA-based neural prostheses and implants, advancing the exploration and rehabilitation of the nervous system.
