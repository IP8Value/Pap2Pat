# Identification of a Novel IDE Inhibitor

Herein, we describe the identification of a novel inhibitor of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE), a key player in glucose homeostasis and metabolic diseases. Through high-throughput screening and structure-activity relationship studies, we identified compound 6b as a potent and selective IDE inhibitor with an IC50 value of 12 nM. This compound was further optimized to yield 6bK, which demonstrated improved pharmacokinetic properties and in vivo efficacy.

## Structure and Mechanism of Action

Compound 6bK is a small molecule that binds to a unique allosteric site on IDE, distinct from the conventional substrate-binding site. Crystallographic studies revealed that 6bK occupies a hydrophobic pocket formed by residues A479 and G362, which are crucial for inhibitor binding. Docking simulations further confirmed the placement of the benzophenone and cyclohexyl groups within this pocket, contributing to the high affinity and selectivity of 6bK.

## In Vitro Validation

In vitro biochemical assays demonstrated that 6bK effectively inhibits IDE activity in a dose-dependent manner. Yonetani-Theorell double inhibitor plots showed that 6bK binds independently from the substrate mimetic Ii1, indicating non-competitive inhibition. This was further supported by the lack of competition between 6bK and insulin for binding to IDE. These findings suggest that 6bK acts through a distinct mechanism compared to traditional substrate-mimetic inhibitors.

## Small-Molecule/Enzyme Mutant Complementation

To confirm the binding site and placement of 6bK, we conducted small-molecule/enzyme mutant complementation studies. IDE mutants A479L and G362Q were significantly less inhibited by 6bK compared to wild-type IDE, validating the importance of these residues for inhibitor binding. Analogue 9, with a smaller t-butyl group substituting the p-benzoyl ring, showed comparable inhibition of both mutant and wild-type IDE. Similarly, analogue 13, with a smaller L-leucine side chain, maintained similar inhibitory activity against G362Q IDE and wild-type IDE.

## In Vivo Efficacy

In vivo studies in C57BL/6J lean mice demonstrated that a single intraperitoneal injection of 6bK (80 mg/kg) significantly improved glucose tolerance during an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT). The hypoglycaemic response to insulin injection was also enhanced in 6bK-treated mice, indicating improved insulin sensitivity. Additionally, 6bK treatment did not alter brain levels of Aβ(40) or Aβ(42) peptides, suggesting a lack of central nervous system penetration.

## Biodistribution and Pharmacokinetics

The biodistribution of 6bK was evaluated using isotope dilution mass spectrometry (IDMS). After intraperitoneal injection, 6bK was rapidly distributed to various tissues, with the highest concentrations observed in the liver and kidneys. Notably, 6bK was not detected in the brain even at tenfold concentrated samples, confirming its peripheral action. The plasma half-life of 6bK was approximately 2 hours, indicating favorable pharmacokinetic properties for sustained in vivo efficacy.

## Glucose Metabolism and Hormone Regulation

Acute IDE inhibition by 6bK led to increased insulin levels and decreased glucagon secretion following oral glucose administration. This effect was more pronounced during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) compared to an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT), highlighting the role of IDE in incretin-mediated glucose regulation. In DIO mice, 6bK treatment improved glucose tolerance and altered hepatic gene expression, with increased glucose-6-phosphatease (G6Pase) and decreased phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK) transcript levels.

## Amylin Signaling and Gastric Emptying

IDE inhibition by 6bK also affected amylin signaling, leading to slowed gastric emptying in fasted mice. This effect was abrogated in the presence of an amylin receptor antagonist, AC187, confirming the involvement of amylin signaling in the observed phenotype. These findings suggest that IDE inhibition may have therapeutic potential for conditions involving impaired glucose metabolism and gastrointestinal function.

## Glucagon Receptor Knockout Mice

To investigate the role of glucagon signaling in 6bK-mediated effects, we treated G-protein-coupled glucagon receptor knockout mice (Gcgr-/-) with 6bK. These mice displayed altered glucose tolerance when challenged with oral glucose but not intraperitoneal glucose, indicating that IDE inhibition affects glucose metabolism through both glucagon-dependent and independent mechanisms.

## Conclusion

In summary, we have identified and characterized a novel small-molecule inhibitor of IDE, 6bK, which demonstrates potent in vitro and in vivo efficacy. The unique binding site and mechanism of action of 6bK offer new insights into the regulation of insulin and glucose metabolism. These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of IDE inhibitors for treating metabolic disorders such as diabetes and obesity.
