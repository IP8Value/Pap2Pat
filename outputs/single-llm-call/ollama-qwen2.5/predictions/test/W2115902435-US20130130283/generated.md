- The study aimed to elucidate the architecture and organization of proteins within bacterial type III secretion system (T3SS) needle complexes using a multi-pronged experimental approach. This included genetic modifications, biochemical purifications, electron microscopy, and mass spectrometry.

- S. enterica serovar typhimurium strains were used to create mutants lacking or expressing modified versions of T3SS components InvG, PrgH, and PrgK. These strains allowed for the purification of needle complexes with specific protein tags or truncations for subsequent analysis.

- The research demonstrated that InvG forms the outer rings of the needle complex, extending into the periplasmic space to contact the inner rings composed primarily of PrgH and PrgK. This is the first experimental confirmation of this arrangement previously predicted by bioinformatics.

- Cryo-electron microscopy revealed the precise localization of poly-histidine tags introduced at different positions in PrgH and PrgK, confirming that the C-terminal domain of PrgH localizes to the periphery of inner ring 1 (IR1) while PrgK forms a smaller diameter ring nested within.

- Chemical crosslinking followed by mass spectrometry identified specific interactions between InvG, PrgH, and PrgK. The N-terminus of InvG was shown to directly contact the C-terminal domain of PrgH, which is critical for complex stability.

- The study also optimized methods for purifying needle complexes and separating intact particles from aggregates using sucrose gradient centrifugation. This improved sample purity for downstream structural analyses by electron microscopy and mass spectrometry.

- Overall, this work provides a detailed molecular map of the T3SS needle complex, revealing how its core components are organized to form a functional secretion apparatus. The findings lay the foundation for future studies on the mechanism of bacterial protein secretion and host cell invasion.
