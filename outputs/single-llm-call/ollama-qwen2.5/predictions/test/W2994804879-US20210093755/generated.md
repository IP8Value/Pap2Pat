# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to nasobiliary drainage, particularly to a device and method for repositioning a nasobiliary drainage tube from the mouth to the nostril during endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP). The invention aims to improve the safety, efficiency, and ease of this critical procedure.

## BACKGROUND ART

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) is a vital medical procedure used for diagnosing and treating biliary and pancreatic diseases. One common application of ERCP is endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD), which involves the placement of a drainage catheter to facilitate bile juice drainage. After completing the initial ERCP, the catheter must be repositioned from the mouth to the nostril. The conventional method for this repositioning involves inserting a nasal plastic tube, catching its tip in the pharynx and oral cavity using a finger or forceps, and then pulling it out through the nose. However, this method is time-consuming and often challenging, especially in patients with limited visibility of the oral cavity due to factors such as a high Mallampati score.

The conventional technique also poses significant risks to both the patient and the operator. For patients, the procedure can cause discomfort, mucosal scratches, or even broken teeth. For operators, there is a risk of finger injury when trying to catch the tip of the plastic tube. Additionally, the need to remove the mouthpiece and wait for the patient to wake up from sedation further complicates the process.

## TECHNICAL PROBLEM

The present invention addresses these issues by providing a device and method that simplify and expedite the repositioning of the nasobiliary drainage catheter. The primary objective is to reduce the procedural time, minimize risks of injury to both patients and operators, and enhance overall patient comfort and operator convenience.

## TECHNICAL SOLUTION

The invention comprises a novel device called the J-Loop and a method for using it to reposition the nasobiliary drainage catheter. The J-Loop is designed to be inserted into the mouth and used to drag out the plastic tube through the nostril, eliminating the need to catch the tip of the tube manually. This innovative approach significantly reduces procedural time and eliminates the risks associated with manual manipulation.

The method involves the following steps:
1. Inserting the J-Loop into the patient's mouth.
2. Guiding a plastic tube with marked depths into the nostril until it reaches the appropriate depth.
3. Pulling out the J-Loop, which drags the plastic tube along with it through the pharynx and out of the nose.

## ADVANTAGEOUS EFFECTS

The use of the J-Loop for nasobiliary drainage repositioning offers several advantages:
1. **Reduced Procedural Time**: The new technique significantly reduces the time required for repositioning, from an average of 194 seconds using the conventional method to approximately 44 seconds with the J-Loop.
2. **Enhanced Safety**: The risk of injury to both patients (e.g., mucosal scratches, broken teeth) and operators (e.g., finger injuries) is minimized.
3. **Improved Patient Comfort**: The procedure can be performed while the patient remains sedated, reducing discomfort and anxiety.
4. **Ease of Use**: The J-Loop simplifies the repositioning process, making it more accessible to a wider range of healthcare providers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Device Description

The J-Loop is a flexible, curved device designed for easy insertion into the patient's mouth. It has a smooth surface to prevent any irritation or injury to the oral cavity. The plastic tube used in conjunction with the J-Loop is marked at specific depths to ensure accurate placement.

### Method of Use

1. **Preparation**: Ensure the patient is sedated and the endoscope has been removed.
2. **Insertion of the J-Loop**: Gently insert the J-Loop into the patient's mouth, guiding it through the pharynx.
3. **Plastic Tube Placement**: Insert the plastic tube with marked depths into the nostril until it reaches the appropriate depth as indicated by the markings.
4. **Repositioning**: Slowly pull out the J-Loop, which will drag the plastic tube along with it through the pharynx and out of the nose.
5. **Completion**: Secure the repositioned catheter to ensure proper drainage.

### Clinical Outcomes

Clinical studies have demonstrated that the J-Loop technique is highly effective. The average time for repositioning using the J-Loop was significantly shorter compared to the conventional method, with a technical success rate of 97.3%. No complications such as oral cavity injury, tooth damage, or pharynx bleeding were reported in patients using the J-Loop.

### Conclusion

The J-Loop and its associated method provide a safer, faster, and more efficient way to reposition nasobiliary drainage catheters during ERCP. This innovation not only enhances patient comfort and safety but also simplifies the procedure for healthcare providers, making it a valuable addition to endoscopic practices.
