Dorian Gray, a young man of extraordinary beauty, sits in his uncle's London garden. Before him is Basil Hallward’s portrait of Dorian, which Hallward refuses to exhibit due to the deep personal connection it holds. Lord Henry Wotton, Dorian’s uncle, discusses Dorian with Hallward, expressing concern and offering advice. Dorian, captivated by his own beauty in the portrait, realizes he will never be able to look at himself the same way again. He becomes obsessed with preserving his youthful appearance, setting the stage for a descent into hedonism and moral decay.
