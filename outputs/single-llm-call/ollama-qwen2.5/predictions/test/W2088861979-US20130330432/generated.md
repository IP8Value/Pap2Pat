- **Introduction to the Study**: This study explores the antidiabetic and hypolipidemic effects of *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract in diabetic rats induced by high-fat diet (HFD) and streptozotocin (STZ). The research aims to understand how the extract influences glucose, lipid metabolism, insulin, adiponectin levels, and gene expression related to these processes.

- **Effects on Glucose and Lipid Levels**: *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract significantly reduced glucose and lipid levels in diabetic rats. This reduction is crucial for managing diabetes and its complications, indicating the potential therapeutic value of the extract in treating hyperglycemia and dyslipidemia.

- **Impact on Insulin and Adiponectin**: The extract increased insulin and adiponectin levels in diabetic rats. These increases are vital for improving glucose metabolism and reducing inflammation, suggesting that *C. latifolia* may help restore normal hormonal balance in diabetic conditions.

- **Gene Expression Changes**: *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract upregulated genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism, including PPARγ, IRS-1, IGF-1, GLUT4, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, PPARα, leptin, and lipase. These changes indicate a potential mechanism by which the extract exerts its antidiabetic and hypolipidemic effects.

- **Hepatotoxicity and Nephropathy**: The study also found that *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract reduced liver enzymes (ALT, GGT) and kidney markers (creatinine, urea), suggesting it may protect against hepatotoxicity and nephropathy, common complications of diabetes.

- **Mechanisms of Action**: The upregulation of PPARα and LPL genes by *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract suggests that the extract enhances fatty acid oxidation and lipid metabolism, contributing to its hypolipidemic effects. This mechanism is supported by the observed reduction in triglycerides and increase in HDL levels.

- **Conclusion**: The study concludes that *C. latifolia* fruit:root extract has significant antidiabetic and hypolipidemic properties, making it a promising therapeutic option for diabetes management. Further research is needed to explore its potential clinical applications and long-term effects.
