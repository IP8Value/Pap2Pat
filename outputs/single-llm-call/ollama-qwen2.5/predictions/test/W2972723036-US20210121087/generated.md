- The paper presents a model-based noninvasive intracranial pressure (nICP) estimation framework that addresses key challenges in existing methods. It accounts for unknown time offsets between radial arterial blood pressure (rABP) and cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) signals by forming a likelihood distribution over multiple time offsets.
- The method incorporates prior information about ICP values while retaining interpretability, unlike purely statistical approaches. An autoregressive model of ICP dynamics helps track changes without relying heavily on initial priors. This allows accurate estimation over long recording durations and large ICP swings.
- For each data window, the framework generates a probability distribution of ICP values that could be used for confidence metrics. It can also estimate ICP pulse pressures in a patient-specific manner without pre-training. No calibration to invasive measurements is required, making it useful as a screening tool.
- The approach potentially enables continuous nICP monitoring for patients excluded from invasive methods due to risks. Applications include intraoperative cerebral perfusion monitoring during surgeries that lack concurrent ICP measurement. Real-time implementation is feasible given the deterministic operations involved.
- Limitations include using a preset prior distribution, though this could be modified at the bedside by clinicians. Future work will involve validating on larger datasets with diverse pathologies and variations in ICP. Testing with noninvasive blood pressure monitors and harnessing information from estimated model coefficients are also promising directions for further research and development.
- In conclusion, this framework represents a significant step towards developing an accurate, continuous, real-time nICP monitoring device that can improve neurocritical care by enabling reliable assessment without invasive procedures. The method's ability to generate patient-specific estimates while accounting for key physiological challenges sets it apart from prior approaches and paves the way for clinical translation of noninvasive ICP monitoring technology.
- An invention disclosure covering this estimation framework was filed by MIT in May 2018, listing S. M. Imaduddin, A. Fanelli, and T. Heldt as co-inventors. This underscores the innovative nature and potential impact of the proposed method on advancing noninvasive ICP monitoring capabilities for a wide range of clinical applications.
