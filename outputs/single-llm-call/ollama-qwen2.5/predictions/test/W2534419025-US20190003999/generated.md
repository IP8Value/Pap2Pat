- The study demonstrates the selective capture of histidine-tagged proteins from cell lysates using TEM grids modified with NTA-functionalized graphene oxide. This method enables efficient affinity binding and visualization of target proteins via negative stain TEM or cryo-EM, offering a novel approach for protein analysis.

- Researchers prepared GO-NTA dispersions by monitoring UV-vis spectra to determine extinction coefficients for concentration measurements. They then deposited the material onto EM grids using L-S transfer in a controlled environment, optimizing conditions for reproducibility and consistency across experiments.

- BSA-PABA-GO-NTA modified grids were used to capture His6-T7 bacteriophage from both purified samples and bacterial lysates. The affinity grids selectively bound phage particles, which were visualized using negative stain TEM after washing steps to remove unbound material.

- For cryo-EM imaging of His6-GroEL, lysate was applied to BSA-PABA-GO-NTA grids followed by blotting and plunge-freezing in liquid ethane. Micrographs were acquired at 300 kV using a Titan Krios microscope equipped with a Gatan K2 Summit camera, producing high-quality images for single particle analysis.

- Single particle reconstruction of His6-GroEL was performed on 5363 particles manually picked from micrographs. After CTF correction and class averaging, an initial model with D7 symmetry was generated. Refinement produced a gold-standard map at 8.1 Å resolution using 0.143 criteria, validating the method's effectiveness for structural studies.

- Fourier shell correlation against an existing high-resolution cryo-EM map (EMD-500134) gave an approximate resolution of 9 Å, further confirming the quality and accuracy of the reconstructed model. This approach offers a robust platform for studying histidine-tagged proteins from complex biological samples using TEM grid-based affinity capture techniques.

- The study highlights the potential of NTA-functionalized graphene oxide as a versatile material for protein analysis, providing a simple yet powerful method to selectively isolate and visualize target proteins directly from cell lysates. This could have significant implications for structural biology and drug discovery applications.
