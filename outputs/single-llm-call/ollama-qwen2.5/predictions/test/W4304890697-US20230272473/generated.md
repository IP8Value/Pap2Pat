For fetal fraction (FF) calculation and chromosome aneuploidy screening, the method utilizes a combination of read depth (RD) analysis and allele frequency (AF) assessment. This dual approach enhances detection accuracy by leveraging both the quantitative and qualitative aspects of circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA). The FF is estimated using SNPs with known maternal genotypes, ensuring reliable quantification even at low fetal fractions.

Chromosome aneuploidy screening involves segmenting chromosomes into bins and comparing the RD of each bin to a reference set. This helps in identifying significant deviations indicative of trisomies or other chromosomal abnormalities. The method also incorporates statistical models to filter out technical noise and biological variability, improving the specificity and sensitivity of the test.

For monogenic variant detection, a modified BWA-GATK pipeline is employed to identify fetal single nucleotide variants (SNVs) in regions of interest. Repeat region filters are applied to exclude problematic areas marked by RepeatMasker. SNVs with sufficient depth and allele frequency thresholds are considered for further analysis. Paternally inherited or de novo fetal SNVs are identified using the ACD filter, which assesses the likelihood of a variant being of non-fetal origin based on alternative allele counts.

The FMID filter is used to distinguish maternal- and fetal-origin fragments by comparing fragment lengths of reference and alternative allele supporting reads. Insert sizes are smoothed and compared under four statistical hypotheses, including Welch’s t-test, Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, Kruskal–Wallis H-test, and Mann–Whitney U-test. Variants with significantly shorter fragment lengths in the alternative allele group are considered potentially positive.

Maternal CNV detection is based on both RD and SNP AF data. Normalized RD values are used to calculate Z-scores, which are smoothed using a mean-shift algorithm. Potential CNVs are called when the Z-score exceeds ±3. Conflicting results from SNP AF data, such as heterozygous SNPs with unexpected allele frequencies, can lead to rejection of the CNV call. This method helps in identifying maternal deletions and duplications while minimizing false positives.

Dizygotic twin pregnancies are identified by an increased number of fetal SNPs detected and variations in the AF of fetal SNPs. The presence of multiple fetal genotypes and a broader range of allele frequencies compared to singleton pregnancies is indicative of dizygotic twins. This approach helps in accurately distinguishing between monozygotic and dizygotic twin pregnancies, ensuring appropriate clinical management.

Meiotic homologous recombination for aneuploidies is detected by analyzing the pattern of fetal SNP genotypes at maternal heterozygous loci. In trisomies caused by maternal meiotic errors, consecutive fetal homozygous SNPs are indicative of MII non-disjunction (NDJ), while a lack of such stretches suggests MI NDJ. The presence of both MI and MII NDJ patterns is suggestive of a recombinant event. A cut-off of 10 continuous fetal heterozygous loci is used to determine the presence of MI NDJ.

The lower detection limit of the test was determined using mixed maternal and fetal DNA samples with known fetal percentages ranging from 4.0% to 5.8%. The overall detection rate for target diseases at the case level was 99.1%, indicating that the test can reliably detect chromosomal abnormalities and monogenic variants even at low fetal fractions. This information is crucial for establishing the clinical utility of the test in a broad range of prenatal screening scenarios.

The analysis workflow involves sequencing cfDNA samples on MGISEQ-2000 platforms followed by secondary analysis on high-performance computing servers. The process includes quality control, deduplication through UMI processing, alignment, consensus read generation, BAM file processing, variant calling, filtering, and annotation. Variant interpretation is performed according to guidelines from the American College of Medical Genetics, ensuring that only pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants are reported. Maternal genotypes are obtained from maternal cfDNA testing, while paternal testing is not required for reporting results in this study.
