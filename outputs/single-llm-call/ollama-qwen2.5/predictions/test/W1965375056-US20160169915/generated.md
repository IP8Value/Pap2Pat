- The study aimed to identify metabolic signatures in blood plasma that could distinguish individuals at high risk for developing ASD. Two independent statistical methods (PLS and SVM) were used, both yielding similar results with about 81% prediction accuracy on a 21-sample validation set.

- Key features like homocitrulline were common between the two classification methods, highlighting their importance for future blood-based diagnostics. However, larger sample sizes are needed to further validate these findings and confirm metabolite annotations.

- Several confirmed metabolites associated with ASD are relevant to mitochondrial biology, oxidative stress, and energy production - processes previously proposed to be involved in ASD etiology. This supports existing theories about autism's biological mechanisms.

- Novel metabolic alterations were identified, including changes in aspartate, citrate, creatinine, DHEA-S, hydroxyphenyllactate, indoleacetate, isoleucine, glutamate and glutarate levels between ASD and TD individuals. These had not been previously reported in other metabolomics studies of urine.

- A new potential biomarker homocitrulline was identified - it was decreased in ASD patients and ranked highest in both classification models. Additional research is needed to understand its role in ASD development, but it shows promise as a diagnostic marker.

- While the study provides proof-of-concept for metabolic biomarkers of ASD, limitations include small sample size and inability to conclusively analyze potential confounding covariates like medication or diet. Larger studies are currently underway to confirm these initial findings.

- Future efforts will focus on confirming results in larger and younger patient populations, discovering additional diagnostic metabolites, improving phenotyping of ASD subtypes, and analyzing the impact of various covariables to develop more accurate metabolic profiles for evaluating ASD risk.
