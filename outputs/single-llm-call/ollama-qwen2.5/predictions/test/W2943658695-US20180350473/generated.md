- **NRU Reactor**: The National Research Universal (NRU) reactor at Chalk River Laboratories was a heavy water-cooled and moderated facility with online re-fueling capability. It operated at a maximum power of 135 MW, featuring various rod types including driver fuel rods enriched to 19.8% U-235.

- **Neutron Detectors**: Two large-area neutron detectors were used: a boron-coated straw (BCS) detector and a B10+ stainless steel tube detector. The BCS detector had an active area of 1 × 0.18 m, while the B10+ detector presented the same active area but was filled with 3He gas.

- **Data Acquisition**: Binary list mode files from both detectors were processed offline into time-series histograms using C++ routines. These plots recorded detector counts over time, enabling comparison with NRU's measured thermal reactor power, which was derived from water flow and temperature differences.

- **Environmental Factors**: The local environment significantly influenced neutron detection, as demonstrated by the B10+ detector's ability to detect online refueling events at Location B, while the BCS detector at Location A remained unaffected due to fixed shielding. Multiple detectors are crucial for reliable monitoring.

- **Safeguards and Interference**: Potential interference with neutron detection, such as placing additional moderator or neutron sources, can be mitigated through coordinated networks of detectors and random in-person inspections. These measures ensure the reliability of neutron signatures and support IAEA safeguards goals.

- **Summary**: This study demonstrated the effectiveness of large-area neutron detectors for monitoring nuclear reactor power and isotopic inventory at stand-off distances. The technique's potential for non-invasive tracking of reactor operations supports economical and practical means to achieve IAEA safeguards objectives.
