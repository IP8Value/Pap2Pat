# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer diagnosis and more specifically to a method and device for detecting cancer-involved lymph nodes based on pH sensing. This innovative approach provides a real-time, non-invasive, and reliable means to identify metastatic lymph nodes during surgical procedures.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer diagnosis often involves the identification of metastatic lymph nodes, which are critical in determining the stage and treatment plan for various cancers, particularly breast cancer. Traditional methods such as frozen section pathology and histopathological analysis, while effective, have limitations including delays in results, potential inaccuracies, and the need for invasive procedures that can lead to unnecessary removal of healthy tissue. There is a significant need for a real-time, non-invasive method to accurately diagnose metastatic lymph nodes during surgery.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses this need by providing a method and device for detecting cancer-involved lymph nodes based on pH sensing. The invention utilizes a Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD) that measures the pH of lymph node fluid using a litmus paper embedded in a syringe needle. Cancer cells, when metastasized to lymph nodes, exhibit altered metabolism characterized by fatty acid oxidation, leading to an acidic environment within the lymph node. This change in pH can be detected rapidly and accurately using the MMLD.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Fabrication of MMLD for In Vitro Assays

The Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD) is fabricated using a micro-syringe with an 18-gauge needle and an inner diameter of 838 µm. The syringe is filled with 100 µl of injectable sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). A thin litmus paper, capable of indicating pH changes, is embedded within the needle. The mechanism involves inserting the needle into the lymph node, injecting PBS to rub against the lymph node tissue, and observing the color change of the litmus paper to indicate the acidity or basicity of the lymph node fluid.

### In Vitro Sample Collection from Patients

In vitro fresh lymph node samples were collected from 25 patients undergoing breast cancer surgery. The samples were collected under an ethically approved protocol (IR.TUMS.VCR.REC.1397.532) at our breast cancer central clinics and assistant hospitals between October 2018 and November 2019. Each sample was tested using the MMLD to determine its pH.

### Staining Process Using Hematoxylin and Eosin

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining is a standard histopathological technique used to visualize the nucleus and cytoplasmic inclusions in tissue samples. The process begins with deparaffinizing the tissue section, followed by hydration through decreasing concentrations of alcohol baths and water. The sample is then stained with hematoxylin for 3-5 minutes, washed in running tap water until sections 'blue' for 5 minutes or less, and treated with 1% acid alcohol for 5 minutes. After washing again, the sample is placed in an alkaline solution to turn blue, followed by staining with eosin for 10 minutes and final dehydration through increasing concentrations of alcohol and clearing in xylene.

### Metabolism of Cancer Cells in Lymph Nodes

Recent studies have shown that cancer cells metastasized to lymph nodes undergo a metabolic shift from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO). This change is characterized by the selective activation of FAO-related genes, particularly those regulated by Yes-Associated Protein (YAP). The oxidation of fatty acids results in the production of carbon dioxide and hydrogen ions, leading to an acidic environment within the lymph node. This metabolic alteration provides a basis for using pH sensing as a diagnostic tool.

### Mechanism of MMLD

The MMLD works by injecting PBS into the lymph node, which mixes with the lymphatic fluid. The litmus paper embedded in the needle changes color based on the pH of the mixture. A pH value below 6 indicates an acidic environment, suggesting the presence of metastatic cancer cells. Conversely, a pH above 7 suggests a healthy lymph node.

### Clinical Testing and Results

The MMLD was tested on 65 lymph nodes from 25 breast cancer patients immediately after dissection. The results showed that the pH of the lymph node fluid in cancerous nodes was significantly lower (less than 6) compared to healthy nodes (7-10). These findings were validated against frozen section and permanent H&E staining, demonstrating approximately 90% selectivity and 92% sensitivity in detecting metastatic lymph nodes.

### Advantages of MMLD

The MMLD offers several advantages over traditional methods. It provides real-time results, is non-invasive, and does not require extensive preparation or processing. This tool can help surgeons make accurate decisions during surgery, potentially reducing the need for unnecessary removal of healthy tissue and minimizing the risk of secondary surgeries.

### Conclusion

In summary, the MMLD represents a significant advancement in the diagnosis of metastatic lymph nodes during surgical procedures. By leveraging the metabolic changes in cancer cells, this device offers a reliable, rapid, and non-invasive method to identify cancer-involved lymph nodes, improving patient outcomes and reducing post-surgical complications.

## CLAIMS

1. A method for detecting cancer-involved lymph nodes comprising:
   - Inserting a syringe needle embedded with a pH-sensitive litmus paper into a lymph node.
   - Injecting phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) into the lymph node to mix with the lymphatic fluid.
   - Observing the color change of the litmus paper to determine the pH of the mixture.
   - Identifying the presence of metastatic cancer cells based on a pH value below 6.

2. A device for detecting cancer-involved lymph nodes comprising:
   - A micro-syringe with an 18-gauge needle and an inner diameter of 838 µm.
   - The syringe filled with 100 µl of injectable sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).
   - A pH-sensitive litmus paper embedded within the needle.

3. The method of claim 1, wherein the lymph node is from a patient undergoing breast cancer surgery.

4. The device of claim 2, wherein the litmus paper is capable of indicating pH changes in the range of 6 to 7.

5. A process for fabricating the device of claim 2 comprising:
   - Embedding a thin pH-sensitive litmus paper into an 18-gauge syringe needle.
   - Filling the syringe with 100 µl of injectable sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).

The Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD) uses a litmus paper embedded in a syringe needle to measure the pH of lymph node fluid.
