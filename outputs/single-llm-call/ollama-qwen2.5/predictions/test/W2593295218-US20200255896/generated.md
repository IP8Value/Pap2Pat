- The QNatal Advanced™ assay shows significant advancements in Non-Invasive Prenatal Screening (NIPS) through improved separation of affected and unaffected pregnancies. This is evidenced by Z scores where no affected pregnancy had a score <8 and no unaffected pregnancy >4, enabling the detection of false positives from maternal microduplications and global copy number abnormalities.

- These refinements have led to high positive predictive values (PPVs) for trisomy 21, 18, and 13 in clinical practice. The PPV for trisomy 21 was 98%, with one false-positive case linked to maternal genetic abnormalities. With current reporting criteria, the PPV would be 100%.

- For trisomy 18, the PPV was 92%, dropping to 96% when excluding a twin gestation with a teratoma. Trisomy 13 had a PPV of 69%, with four false-positive cases, one associated with uterine fibroids and others unexplained.

- The assay also detected sex chromosome aneuploidies with high accuracy, including Turner syndrome (45,X) and Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY). A single case of DiGeorge syndrome was confirmed by amniocentesis. These findings highlight the assay's broad applicability beyond common trisomies.

- Despite these improvements, NIPS remains a screening test due to potential false positives from biological and technical issues. In clinical practice, 10% of women terminated their pregnancies following a high-risk NIPS result for trisomy 21 without invasive testing, underscoring the need for proper counseling.

- The study's limitations include low follow-up rates and the lack of sensitivity data. Future efforts will focus on collecting more comprehensive follow-up information to better assess the assay's performance and ensure its reliability as a screening tool in various patient populations.
