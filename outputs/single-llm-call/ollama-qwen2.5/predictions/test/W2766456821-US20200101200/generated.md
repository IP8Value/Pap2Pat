- The study comprehensively profiled promoter activities in human corneal endothelial cells (CECs), identifying 45 promoters at 28 gene loci specifically expressed in both CEC tissues and cultured CECs. This included genes encoding three transcription factors: TFAP2B, LMX1B, and POU6F2.
- The researchers used Cap Analysis of Gene Expression (CAGE) to compare promoter activities between CECs and other samples from the FANTOM5 atlas, ensuring consistency in protocols for accurate comparison. They identified 137 and 206 promoters strictly specific to CE tissues and cultured CECs, respectively.
- Validation experiments using qRT-PCR confirmed the specificity of TFAP2B, LMX1B, and POU6F2. These transcription factors are crucial for early development and differentiation of neural crest-derived periocular mesenchyme, with mutations in related genes causing syndromes affecting multiple tissues.
- POU6F2 showed a unique expression pattern, being highly specific to CECs and upregulated during their maturation. This suggests it could serve as a marker for CEC differentiation or maturation, making it valuable for assessing stem cell-derived CECs in regenerative medicine applications.
- The study also found that cell cycle-related genes were downregulated in differentiated human corneal endothelial progenitors (dHCEPs), consistent with the limited proliferative capability of mature human CECs. This supports the use of HCEPs and dHCEPs as a suitable in vitro model for studying human CEC differentiation.
