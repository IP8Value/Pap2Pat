- **Study Design and Objectives**: MHS and DR designed the study to develop an efficient algorithm for identifying and quantifying regional wavefront discontinuities in atrial fibrillation (AF). The primary goal was to explore whether wave break and high RDF sites could serve as potential targets for ablation, potentially improving outcomes in persistent AF treatment.

- **Data Acquisition and Analysis**: Data were collected using a spiral catheter with high electrode density, focusing on the left atrium. Averaging pre-processed signals from the catheter provided regional wavefront analysis. The study demonstrated that 4 seconds of data collection is sufficient for RDF reproducibility, while 25 seconds are needed to include WBR.

- **Results and Interpretation**: The results showed that sites with high RDF and low WBR (↑RDF,↓WBR) were often associated with AF termination during ablation. These findings align with experimental data on AF sources. In patients where ablation successfully terminated AF, ↑RDF,↓WBR sites were frequently present and targeted.

- **Clinical Implications**: The study suggests that the WBR metric could enhance the identification of critical areas for AF perpetuation, potentially improving ablation outcomes. By providing a more informed choice of ablation targets, this method may offer a significant advancement over current empirical techniques, particularly in persistent AF cases.

- **Limitations and Future Directions**: Averaging signals reduces spatial resolution, and shorter recording times are needed to improve efficiency. The study's reliance on termination as a surrogate marker for source identification is a limitation. Further research is necessary to validate the WBR metric and establish its relationship with long-term ablation outcomes.
