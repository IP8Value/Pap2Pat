- We introduced an innovative method to enhance color rendition in RGB-LED-based virtual production stages. Our technique focuses on improving the appearance of materials lit by out-of-camera-frustum content while maintaining in-camera color accuracy. It uses four calibration images and three 3 × 3 matrices for correction, significantly outperforming previous methods.
- The approach requires minimal setup with just four calibration images: one showing each LED's appearance to the camera and another showing how each spectral channel illuminates a color chart. These images enable derivation of three separate color transforms, streamlining implementation in virtual production systems.
- We demonstrated our method’s effectiveness across various lighting environments, including RGB LED, sodium vapor, and broad-spectrum illumination. Theoretical results aligned closely with practical outcomes, highlighting the technique's robustness. However, limitations exist, particularly for monochromatic sources like sodium vapor, where 3 × 3 matrices reach their limits.
- Future work could explore joint optimization of M and Q matrices to further refine color accuracy, evaluate performance across diverse skin tones, and integrate practical lights with virtual production. Additionally, leveraging machine learning might help recover missing spectral information, enhancing material appearance under limited lighting conditions.
- Our technique is applicable beyond real-world HDR IBL environments, extending to rendered or CG scenes. For CG environments, a spectral rendering of a color chart or a photograph from a similar real-world setting can serve as the target color rendition reference. This opens opportunities for bridging the gap between synthetic and real-world lighting in virtual production.
