- **Introduction**: The Gen 2 Sensor Fish is an advanced tool developed to enhance hydropower development by providing detailed in situ measurements of environmental conditions affecting fish passage through turbines and other hydraulic structures. It measures 3D linear accelerations, rotational velocities, orientation, pressure, and temperature at a high sampling frequency.

- **Development Background**: The need for the Gen 2 Sensor Fish arose from the limitations of previous models, particularly in environments with high-pressure changes and rapid decompression typical of hydro turbines. This new model is designed to be more durable and accurate, making it suitable for evaluating fish passage conditions in various hydropower settings.

- **Key Features**: The Gen 2 Sensor Fish features a robust design that includes sensors for 3D linear accelerations, rotational velocities, orientation, pressure, and temperature. It operates at a sampling frequency of 2048 Hz, ensuring high-resolution data collection. The device also includes a recovery module to facilitate retrieval after deployment.

- **Calibration and Evaluation**: Each sensor in the Gen 2 Sensor Fish was individually calibrated and evaluated both before and after assembly. This process involved using specialized equipment such as pressure chambers, linear acceleration test tracks, and high-speed videography to ensure accuracy and reliability.

- **Pressure Calibration**: The pressure sensors were calibrated using a HOBO R U20-001-02 water level logger in a pressure chamber. Post-assembly, the device was tested in a rapid-decompression chamber, with results showing relative errors of less than 2% compared to a Honeywell TJE Pressure Transducer.

- **Acceleration Calibration**: Acceleration measurements were calibrated on a linear acceleration test track using a Brüel & Kjaer accelerometer and a National Instruments data acquisition card. Post-assembly tests confirmed relative errors of less than 5%, with most cases under 2%.

- **Rotational Velocity Calibration**: Rotational velocity was calibrated using a high-speed videography setup. The Gen 2 Sensor Fish was mounted in a rotation test fixture, and its measurements were compared to motion analysis from the videos. Relative errors were determined to be less than 5%.

- **Orientation Calibration**: The electronic compass was calibrated using a slow-speed rotation test fixture. The device's heading accuracy was verified by aligning it with magnetic north, showing an accuracy within ±4°.

- **Temperature Calibration**: The temperature sensor was calibrated in ice water and at room temperature using a DT8852 digital thermocouple thermometer. Results showed that the Gen 2 Sensor Fish measurements were accurate within ±2°C.

- **Durability Testing**: Durability tests involved subjecting the Gen 2 Sensor Fish to 100 impact tests of up to 600 g acceleration. Both measurement sensors and the recovery module remained fully functional, confirming the device's robustness.

- **Field Evaluation**: Field testing was conducted at Ice Harbor Dam on the Snake River. Three Gen 2 Sensor Fish were released from a depth of 15.2 m upstream of Spillbay 6 and successfully recovered in the tailrace. All sensors provided accurate data, validating the device's performance in real-world conditions.

- **Conclusion**: The Gen 2 Sensor Fish is a significant advancement in hydropower development, offering precise measurements to evaluate fish passage conditions. Its robust design and high accuracy make it a valuable tool for researchers and engineers in multiple countries. Funding and support from various organizations have been crucial to its development and deployment.
