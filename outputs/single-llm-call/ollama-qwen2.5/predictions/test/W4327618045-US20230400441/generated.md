- **Introduction to VOCs in Donkey Meat**: Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) play a crucial role in the flavor and aroma of meat. In this study, we used gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (GC–IMS) combined with chemometrics analysis to identify and compare VOCs in raw donkey meat from two different strains: Sanhe (SF) and West African Donkey (WT).

- **Methodology**: The study involved the collection of raw donkey meat samples from both SF and WT strains. GC–IMS was employed to analyze the VOC profiles, while chemometrics methods such as principal component analysis (PCA), partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were used for data interpretation.

- **Results of VOC Identification**: A total of 38 VOCs belonging to four categories—alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, and heterocycles—were identified in the raw donkey meat samples. Ketones, alcohols, and aldehydes were found to be the most abundant VOCs, with significant differences observed between SF and WT strains.

- **VOC Profiles and Multivariate Analysis**: The GC–IMS spectra and fingerprints revealed distinct differences between the two strains. Ethanol and hexanal-d showed clear distinctions in their profiles. PCA, PLS-DA, and OPLS-DA confirmed these findings, with 17 differential VOCs identified as potential markers to distinguish SF from WT donkey meat.

- **Discussion on Breed Dependency**: The study demonstrated that the VOC composition of donkey meat is strain-dependent, similar to previous findings in other meats. This is likely due to differences in lipid composition and degradation pathways. For instance, fatty acids at different positions in lipid molecules can influence the formation of specific VOCs, contributing to unique flavor profiles.

- **Conclusion**: The comprehensive analysis using GC–IMS and multivariate methods provided valuable insights into the characteristic VOCs of donkey meat from different strains. These findings may enhance our understanding of meat flavor development and offer a novel approach for authenticating donkey meat products. Further research is needed to clarify the specific mechanisms underlying these differences.
