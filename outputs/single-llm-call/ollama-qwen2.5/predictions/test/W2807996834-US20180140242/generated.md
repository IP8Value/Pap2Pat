### Summary of Key Points:

- **Study Overview**: The study analyzed the trajectory of PANSS item scores in patients with schizophrenia who experienced relapse during the double-blind phase of three clinical trials comparing paliperidone formulations to placebo. PANSS assessments were frequent, and patients were stable before randomization.
  
- **PANSS Assessment**: PANSS is a 30-item scale grouped into positive, negative, and general psychopathology subscales. Scores range from 1 to 7 for each item, with higher scores indicating more severe symptoms.

- **Relapse Definition**: Relapse was defined by hospitalization, self-injury, aggressive behavior, significant increases in PANSS total or specific item scores, and CGI-S score (for the oral ER study). The criteria were standardized across studies.

- **Statistical Analysis**: Linear and non-linear mixed effect models were used to model the trajectories of individual PANSS items from a stable state to relapse. Time was modeled as days relative to relapse, and polynomial and exponential functions were applied to describe the changes in scores.

- **Data Source**: The data supporting the findings are available from the Yale University Open Data Access (YODA) Project with specific identifiers for each study. All analyses were conducted using SAS 9.4.
  
- **Key Findings**: The analysis identified which PANSS items had the most significant increases at relapse, and the non-linear mixed effect model provided parameters indicating the speed and timing of symptom worsening before relapse.

- **Implications**: Understanding the trajectory of symptom changes can inform the development of early intervention strategies for preventing relapse in patients with schizophrenia. The findings highlight the importance of monitoring specific PANSS items closely.
  
- **Supplementary Material**: Additional details, including differences in study designs and detailed findings from each trial, are provided in supplementary materials.
