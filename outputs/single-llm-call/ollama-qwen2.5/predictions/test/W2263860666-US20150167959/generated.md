- The study explores the use of charged surfactants to control bubble formation during boiling, demonstrating on-off switching of bubbles through applied voltage. This technique enables spatial and temporal control over boiling processes without complex surface structures.

- Experiments were conducted using a pool boiling rig with a roughened silver foil as the boiling surface. Voltage was applied between the foil and a titanium counter electrode while measuring temperature and heat flux to generate boiling curves.

- For spatial control, a silicon wafer with patterned gold electrodes and platinum heaters was fabricated. Each electrode could be independently switched between -0.1 V and -2.0 V to selectively activate or deactivate bubble formation in specific regions.

- Contact angle measurements were performed on a copper block using the advancing contact angle of sliding bubbles. A high-speed camera captured images, which were analyzed with custom software to determine the contact angles for different surfactant solutions.

- Surfactant solutions of NaBr, MEGA-10, S10S, SDS, and DTAB were prepared at 2.6 mM concentration by dissolving stock solutions in deionized water. The solutions were sonicated to ensure complete dissolution before use in boiling experiments.
