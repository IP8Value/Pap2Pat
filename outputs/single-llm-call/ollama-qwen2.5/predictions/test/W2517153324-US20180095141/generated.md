- The study introduces a novel behavioral model for Li-S batteries, addressing the unique challenges they pose compared to conventional Li-ion batteries. This includes predicting voltage response, capacity, power capability, and degradation. The proposed structure is robust and easy to tune.

- Parameters of a Thevenin equivalent circuit model were identified using the PEM method across different temperatures. A mixed pulse discharge profile revealed current dependencies, enhancing the model's accuracy in real-world conditions.

- Polynomial functions were used to simplify the battery model parameters, with interpolation for varying temperatures. Despite Li-S batteries' complexity, validation showed low estimation error, indicating potential simplifications without significant loss of precision.

- Model validation using a realistic NEDC drive-cycle and different discharge hardware demonstrated an RMSE of 32 mV. While some deviations were noted in specific regions due to self-discharge and OCV definition issues, the overall fit was excellent, supporting its use in BMS systems.

- Future research aims to improve the model by incorporating self-discharge effects, current profile dependencies, and charge behavior. This will enhance its applicability for state-of-charge and state-of-health estimation in electric vehicles, a highly demanding environment requiring precise battery management.
