- In this work, GDSSMs are proposed where latent dynamics of agents are coupled via GNNs to capture multi-agent interactions. Moment matching rules for GNN layers enable deterministic inference and a GMM prior on initial states allows multimodal predictions. This leads to an efficient algorithm producing complex predictive distributions.
- The method shows strong empirical performance on autonomous driving datasets, with sparse covariance approximations proposed for real-world vehicle control units. Performance increases when training on more diverse environments, demonstrating generalization capabilities.
- For evaluation, RMSE and NLL metrics are used. Variants like Bayes predictor (model averaging), Gibbs predictor (risk function expectation) and Min predictor (minimum error over samples) are discussed. The method supports multimodal predictions with decreasing minRMSE as modes increase.
- Training details are provided for the rounD and NGSIM datasets, including update steps, data splits, and validation procedures. For out-of-distribution testing, models were trained on subsets of the rounD dataset to evaluate generalization capabilities across different traffic environments.
- The embedding function processes 3 seconds of history for M agents, producing a GMM output with V mixture components. Mean and variance update functions use neural networks with message passing. The emission model maps agent states back to observed space without interactions. An approximate posterior is used for inference in the GDSSM framework.
