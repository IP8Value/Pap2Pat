- The study demonstrates that maize-produced S antigen administered orally to pregnant sows provides passive immunity against PEDV to their suckling piglets. This approach offers a practical and cost-effective vaccine option with high efficacy, especially at low doses.

- Following PEDV challenge, piglets from litters fed the low-dose oral vaccine showed significantly higher survival rates compared to control animals. The results align with previous work showing that oral S protein administration increases serum neutralizing antibodies (NABs) in piglets post-challenge.

- Interestingly, high oral doses of the vaccine provided less protection than lower doses. This may be due to the onset of oral tolerance, though further research is needed to confirm this hypothesis and understand the underlying mechanisms.

- The study found that both serum and milk NABs increased in sows fed a low dose of the oral vaccine compared to controls. However, colostrum NAB levels did not differ between high-dose, low-dose, and control groups, suggesting limited transfer from colostrum to piglets.

- Milk GM-CSF levels were inversely correlated with piglet survival rates, indicating a potential immunomodulatory effect of this cytokine in neonatal protection. The observed decrease in serum GM-CSF levels in vaccinated sows suggests that the S antigen may have a downregulatory effect on GM-CSF production.

- This maize-based vaccine platform presents a convenient and effective alternative to parentally delivered vaccines, reducing costs and logistical challenges. The identification of NABs and GM-CSF as correlates of protection offers valuable insights for future vaccine development and understanding of mucosal immunity against coronaviruses.
