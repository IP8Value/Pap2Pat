- Human antibodies specific for Influenza A M2 protein were isolated using Sindbis-based mammalian cell display, demonstrating potent prophylactic and therapeutic activity in a mouse model. This highlights the utility of this method for isolating natural human antibodies with therapeutic potential.

- PBMCs from blood samples were pre-incubated with labeled Qβ and human gamma globulin, then stained with M2e-Qβ, anti-human IgM/D/CD14/CD3, and CD19. 334 M2-specific B cells were sorted by flow cytometry for library production.

- A Sindbis virus-based scFv display library was generated from sorted B cells. BHK cells infected with the library were stained with RNase-M2e and anti-RNase or Qβ-M2e and anti-M2, then FACS-sorted to isolate M2-specific clones.

- ScFv coding regions were PCR amplified, cloned into an expression vector, and transfected into HEK-293T cells. Stable pools of antibody-expressing cells were selected with puromycin, and antibodies were purified from supernatants by protein A affinity chromatography.

- ELISA plates coated with RNase-M2e-cons or RNase-M2e-VN were used to detect M2-binding activity of scFv-Fc fusion proteins. Antibodies were detected using HRP-labeled anti-mouse IgG and developed with OPD substrate, then read at 450 nm.

- Affinity constants (Kd) for M2e binding were determined by incubating antibodies with serial dilutions of M2e peptide, followed by ELISA detection. Kd values were calculated as the EC50 of the signal vs M2e concentration in solution binding reactions.

- Prophylactic and therapeutic activity was tested in a mouse model of influenza A infection. Antibodies were injected intraperitoneally 2 days before or 1-3 days after infection with lethal dose of m.a. PR8 virus. Weight loss, fever, and survival were monitored closely post-infection.
