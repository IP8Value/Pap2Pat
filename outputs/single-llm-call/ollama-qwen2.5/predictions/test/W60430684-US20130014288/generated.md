- The study introduces a novel fluorescent detection platform using genetically encoded Fluorogen Activating Protein (FAP) tags for rapid and selective labeling of Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator (CFTR) at the cell surface. This overcomes limitations of traditional methods like biotinylation, which are time-consuming and labor-intensive.
- FAP-tagged CFTR constructs were successfully expressed in stable HEK293 cell lines and showed proper trafficking to the plasma membrane when visualized using confocal fluorescence microscopy. Importantly, mutant F508del-CFTR was undetectable at the surface under normal conditions but became visible after corrector treatment.
- Immunoblot analysis revealed that N-terminal FAP-CFTR WT fusion proteins exhibited distinct core and mature glycosylation bands similar to untagged CFTR. However, CFTR EL4-FAP constructs showed impaired glycosylation, suggesting potential issues with glycan modification or accessibility at the fourth extracellular loop.
- The study evaluated several correctors for their ability to rescue F508del-CFTR trafficking and function. C18 emerged as the most potent single compound, showing robust rescue in both HEK293 cells expressing FAP-CFTR constructs and primary human airway epithelial (HBE) cells expressing endogenous F508del-CFTR.
- Combinations of correctors, particularly CFFT-002 and C18, significantly improved F508del-CFTR trafficking and function compared to single corrector treatments. This suggests that these compounds may have distinct mechanisms of action or target different structural features of the misfolded protein, highlighting the potential benefits of combination therapies.
- The FAP reporter system demonstrated high fidelity in recapitulating the behavior of untagged F508del-CFTR across different cell types and assays. This validates its use as a sensitive tool for evaluating corrector efficacy and provides a platform that could be adapted for high-throughput screening of new compounds.
- Alternative tagging strategies, including N-terminal and EL4 constructs, were used to minimize artifacts due to the fluorescent reporter position. The N-terminal fusion was found to be superior in terms of expression levels, glycosylation state, and anion transport capabilities, making it a preferred choice for future studies.
- The development of this FAP-based detection platform addresses a significant need in cystic fibrosis research by enabling rapid and sensitive detection of CFTR at the cell surface. This technology has the potential to streamline drug discovery efforts and could be adapted for studying other diseases involving protein folding defects or plasma membrane trafficking issues.
