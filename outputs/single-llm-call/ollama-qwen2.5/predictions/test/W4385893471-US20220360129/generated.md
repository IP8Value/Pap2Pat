# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to electrical machines, particularly those used in electric transportation systems. These systems require high efficiency, compact design, and effective thermal management to meet stringent power requirements.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Electric transportation, such as electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft, demands compact, high-power, and reliable electric motors and drives. During critical phases like take-off and landing, the power demand can be 3-10 times higher than during cruise, leading to increased copper loss and temperature rise in the motor's conducting regions. Conventional active cooling systems are often insufficient for managing these high thermal loads efficiently. Phase change materials (PCMs) have been introduced as a means to store and manage heat, but they alone do not address all thermal management challenges.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention motivates the use of interleaved ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials in the stator body of electrical machines. The stator is designed with laminated layers that improve thermal conductivity. Nonferrous materials with high thermal conductivity are introduced, and their volume ratio to ferrous material is optimized. Examples of suitable nonferrous materials include copper and aluminum. This design ensures efficient heat dissipation while maintaining the magnetic properties necessary for motor operation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Interleaved Ferromagnetic and Nonferromagnetic Layers

The stator body of the electrical machine is constructed with interleaved layers of ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials. This design enhances thermal management by allowing heat to be more efficiently conducted away from the motor's active components. The ferromagnetic layers ensure that the magnetic field required for motor operation is maintained, while the nonferromagnetic layers, which have higher thermal conductivity, facilitate better heat dissipation.

### Laminated Stator Design

The stator body consists of multiple laminated layers, each layer being either ferromagnetic or nonferromagnetic. The thickness and arrangement of these layers are optimized to balance magnetic performance with thermal management. For example, a 20% volume ratio of nonferrous material (such as copper) to ferrous material (such as steel) has been found to be effective in reducing thermal resistance without significantly impacting the motor's torque production.

### High Thermal Conductivity Materials

Nonferrous materials with high thermal conductivity, such as copper and aluminum, are used in the interleaved layers. Copper, with its excellent thermal conductivity, is particularly advantageous for this application. The use of these materials ensures that heat generated during operation is quickly conducted away from the motor's active components, preventing overheating and extending the motor's lifespan.

### Volume Ratio Optimization

The volume ratio of nonferrous to ferrous material in the stator body is optimized to achieve the best thermal management while maintaining the necessary magnetic properties. A 20% copper stacking factor has been found to reduce thermal resistance by up to 50% without significantly affecting torque production. This ratio ensures that the motor remains efficient and reliable under high power demand conditions.

### Example Materials

Suitable nonferrous materials for this application include Copper 110 alloy, which is known for its high thermal conductivity and mechanical strength. Ferromagnetic materials such as HF-10 C5 insulated iron laminations are used to maintain the magnetic field required for motor operation. The combination of these materials in an optimized ratio provides a robust solution for thermal management in electric motors.

### Prototype Development

A prototype of the stator with interleaved layers was developed to demonstrate the effectiveness of the design. The prototype included 0.010" thick HF-10 C5 insulated iron laminations and 0.005" thick Copper 110 alloy laminations, arranged in a 20% copper stacking factor configuration. The layers were bonded using EB-548 bonding epoxy to ensure structural integrity.

### Experimental Results

Preliminary experiments compared the thermal performance of the interleaved stator with a baseline pure iron yoke. A simple test setup applied heat to the inner surface of each sample, and a thermal camera monitored the outer surface temperatures. The results showed faster heat spread, more uniform temperature distribution, and lower hotspot temperatures in the interleaved stator, demonstrating its superior thermal management capabilities.

### Conclusion

The invention provides an effective solution for thermal management in electrical machines used in electric transportation systems. By using interleaved ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic layers, the motor's efficiency and reliability are significantly improved. The optimized design ensures that heat is efficiently conducted away from active components, preventing overheating and extending the motor's lifespan. Future work will focus on further refining the design and integrating it with advanced cooling systems to achieve even greater performance improvements.

## CLAIMS

1. An electrical machine comprising a stator body with interleaved layers of ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials.
2. The electrical machine of claim 1, wherein the nonferromagnetic material is copper.
3. The electrical machine of claim 1, wherein the volume ratio of nonferrous to ferrous material is approximately 20%.
4. The electrical machine of claim 1, wherein the stator body includes 0.010" thick HF-10 C5 insulated iron laminations and 0.005" thick Copper 110 alloy laminations.
5. The electrical machine of claim 1, further comprising a bonding epoxy to secure the interleaved layers.

## ABSTRACT

The invention provides an electrical machine with improved thermal management through the use of a stator body constructed with interleaved ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic layers. The design optimizes the volume ratio of these materials to enhance heat dissipation while maintaining magnetic performance, making it particularly suitable for high-power applications in electric transportation systems.
