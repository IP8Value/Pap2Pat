- The study explores enhancing ionic selectivity and conductivity in membranes using layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolyte layers with cross-linking agents. This method allows for precise control over membrane properties, enabling tailored applications.
- LbL deposition involves alternating positively and negatively charged polyelectrolytes to build up nanostructured layers. Cross-linking with glutaraldehyde (GA) or ethyl(dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) was used to modify the charge density and stability of the polyelectrolyte films.
- The ionic selectivity increased with more bilayers, especially when cross-linked with GA. This is due to GA selectively reacting with primary amines in the polyelectrolyte layers, shifting the net fixed charge negatively and favoring cation transport.
- Conductivity initially decreased with polyelectrolyte coatings but partially recovered upon cross-linking. GA-crosslinked membranes showed higher conductivities than EDC-crosslinked or uncrosslinked membranes, while maintaining good ionic selectivity.
- The study demonstrated that increasing the number of bilayers and using appropriate cross-linking agents can independently control ionic selectivity and conductivity. This provides a flexible approach to design ion-selective and chemically robust membranes for various applications.
