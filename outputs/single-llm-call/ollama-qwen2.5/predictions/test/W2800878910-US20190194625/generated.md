- The study developed a method to create brain organoids using multiple human cell types, including neurons, astrocytes, endothelial cells, and pericytes. These multi-cellular organoids self-assembled into complex structures that mimicked key aspects of brain tissue organization.

- Organoids containing 3 or 6 cell types were generated using a hanging drop culture method. The 6 cell type organoids included neurons, astrocytes, endothelial cells, pericytes, oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs), and microglia in specific ratios to promote self-assembly.

- Immunohistochemistry was used to characterize the cell types present within the organoids. Markers for neurons, astrocytes, endothelial cells, pericytes, OPCs, mature oligodendrocytes, and microglia were all detected, confirming the presence of multiple brain cell populations.

- The integrity of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) was assessed by evaluating permeability to FITC-labeled IgG. Organoids treated with histamine showed increased permeability compared to untreated controls, demonstrating that the BBB could be disrupted in a controlled manner.

- Hypoxia experiments revealed that 24 hours of oxygen deprivation led to disruption of tight junction proteins ZO-1 and β-catenin at the BBB interface. This suggests the organoids can model BBB dysfunction under hypoxic conditions relevant to ischemic stroke or other brain injuries.

- Neurotoxicity was assessed by treating organoids with MPTP, MPP+, or HgCl2 for 6 days. ATP production was significantly reduced in drug-treated groups compared to controls, indicating these compounds induced neuronal and glial toxicity that could be detected using this model system.

- The study demonstrated the utility of multi-cellular brain organoids as a platform to investigate BBB function, neurotoxicity, and other aspects of human brain biology. This approach may enable more physiologically relevant studies compared to traditional 2D cell cultures or animal models.
