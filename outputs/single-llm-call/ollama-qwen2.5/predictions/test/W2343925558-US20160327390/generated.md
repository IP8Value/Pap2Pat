The paper introduces a high-frequency mode-matched axis-symmetric gyroscope that demonstrates superior environmental performance. A novel stress decoupling system isolates the second elliptical modes of a BAW resonator from its substrate, minimizing external error sources affecting sensitivity and offset. The current gyro's performance is limited by temperature-dependent feed-through (FT) signals in the top metal traces connecting to electronics. Reconfiguring the pin-out could significantly reduce these errors.

The SD-BAW gyroscope features a Q value near 62,000, closely matching the predicted total Q of 68,000. Temperature variations show a well-behaved 1/T^1.65 response for both modes, ensuring no environmentally dependent damping coupling. This stability contrasts sharply with a poorly isolated BAW disk resonator, whose Q values fluctuate erratically over temperature due to substrate boundary conditions.

The SD-BAW gyroscope was interfaced with an ASIC and discrete electronics for system-level characterization. The device achieved an angle-random walk (ARW) of 0.39°/√h and a bias instability (BI) of 10.5°/h, limited by thermal and 1/f noise in the ASIC. Optimizing electronics could reduce ARW to 0.074°/√h and BI to below 1°/h.

Temperature performance evaluations from -40°C to 85°C showed a residual sensitivity change of ±2.9% after linear compensation, reducible to ±0.25% with second-order compensation. The total offset change was ±4°/s, primarily due to temperature-dependent FT signals. Rearranging the sensor pin-out could reduce this variation to ±1°/s.

The SD-BAW gyroscope exhibited robustness under random vibrations and shocks. Exposure to a 14-gRMS vibration signal resulted in a sensitivity of 0.012°/s/gRMS, showing minimal performance degradation. In contrast, a commercial TFG's noise performance degraded significantly. The device also endured a 40-g half-sine shock with only a 1.75°/s peak-to-peak offset shift, highlighting its superior resilience in real-world conditions.

The paper concludes by summarizing the design, fabrication, and characterization of a high-performance SD-BAW gyroscope. Key performance metrics include low ARW, stable temperature response, and robustness to vibrations and shocks. Further improvements are anticipated through simple modifications to the sensor pin-out, potentially enhancing bias instability and temperature stability.
