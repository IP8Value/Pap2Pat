- The study explores the impact of chemically adsorbed oxygen on magnetic domain walls in Ni/Co/Pd/W thin films. Oxygen exposure was found to induce a large Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) at room temperature, as evidenced by the transformation of domain wall types from Bloch-like to Néel-like with increasing oxygen coverage.
- The strength of the induced DMI was estimated using a combination of spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy (SPLEEM) imaging and micromagnetic simulations. The results indicate that even sub-monolayer amounts of oxygen can significantly enhance the DMI, with a saturation value comparable to that observed in heavy metal interfaces.
- Density functional theory calculations were performed to understand the microscopic origin of this effect. The calculations suggest that oxygen adsorption modifies the electronic structure at the Co/Pd interface, leading to an enhanced spin-orbit coupling and thus a stronger DMI.
- This work demonstrates a novel approach for tuning magnetic interactions in thin films via surface chemistry. The ability to control DMI through oxygen exposure could have important implications for the development of next-generation spintronic devices, such as racetrack memory or skyrmion-based logic circuits.
- The findings highlight the potential of chemically active interfaces for manipulating magnetic properties and open new avenues for exploring magneto-ionics, where electric fields can be used to control oxygen migration and dynamically tune DMI in complex oxide heterostructures.
