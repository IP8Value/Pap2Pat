- The study aimed to develop a cell sorting method for isolating beneficial cells from whole blood using CD31+ as an example. An aptamer-based approach was used to create a bead-free enriched population of CD31+ cells.

- Commercial aptamers showed high sensitivity and specificity for CD31+ PBMCs, comparable to antibody isolation methods. This is the first validation of this aptamer for PBMCs specifically.

- Cell release from the enrichment system utilized mechanical forces alone, without additional chemicals or temperature changes. A compromise between yield, purity, and viability was achieved by optimizing aptamer concentration, bead number, and combining coated/uncoated beads.

- The released cells were free of both beads and residual aptamers due to size differences. Regulatory concerns about bead contamination are avoided with this larger bead approach compared to nanoparticle methods.

- The enriched CD31+ population showed increased angiogenic and osteogenic potential in vitro. There was particular enrichment of monocytes, which have known bone-forming capabilities. Naive T cells were also enriched, potentially contributing to the regenerative effects.

- This system has therapeutic potential for isolating cell populations from blood draws. It could be adapted to target other surface antigens for enrichment or depletion using aptamers generated through in vitro selection methods.
