- The study identified significant upregulation of IP-10 and I-TAC chemokines in dengue infection across cell lines and patient samples. These chemokines recruit T cells and NK cells to the site of infection, potentially playing a role in host defense.

- NF-κB activation appeared to be the primary driver of IP-10 and I-TAC upregulation rather than IFN induction. Inhibiting NF-κB reduced chemokine levels but had no effect on viral replication, suggesting a more complex role in vivo.

- Viperin was highly upregulated during dengue infection and shown to directly inhibit viral replication when overexpressed. This is the first association of viperin with an antiviral response against dengue.

- Several ubiquitin-proteasome system genes were upregulated during dengue replication. Use of proteasome inhibitors significantly reduced viral release, indicating this pathway may play a role in virus assembly and maturation.

- Comparison with another microarray study revealed overlap in ER-expressed genes and interferon-related genes, suggesting these pathways are fundamental to the host antiviral response across different systems.

- The combined approach of gene array analysis and functional studies proved useful for identifying novel markers like IP-10/I-TAC and potential therapeutic targets such as viperin and the ubiquitin-proteasome system in dengue infection. Further investigation is warranted.
