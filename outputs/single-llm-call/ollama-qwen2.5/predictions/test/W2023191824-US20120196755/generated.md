- A new genome-wide method for mapping chromosome breakage was developed and applied to checkpoint-deficient mec1-1 cells exposed to and recovering from HU. This versatile technique can identify DSBs in any system where they persistently occur, providing direct evidence that replication fork destabilization causes chromosome fragility when the nucleotide pool is reduced or depleted.
- The study revealed ssDNA formation precedes chromosome breakage sites. A conceptual model proposes that different forms of replication stress cause cells to generate ssDNA at replication forks as a consequence of uncoupled DNA synthesis or nucleolytic processing, leading to chromosome fragility. This suggests ssDNA detection can identify fragile sites and signal fork instability.
- Using the temperature-sensitive mec1-4 allele, which shows chromosome fragility without external stress, researchers detected ssDNA at eventual breakage sites after only 40 minutes, despite breakage not occurring until much later. This supports the hypothesis that ssDNA formation is a common precursor to replication stress-induced chromosome fragility.
- The research demonstrates that chromosome breakage correlates with replication fork progression in checkpoint-deficient cells. Allowing forks more time and distance before HU exposure shifted break sites away from origin-proximal regions, indicating that ssDNA formation at stalled forks predicts subsequent DSBs during recovery from replication stress.
