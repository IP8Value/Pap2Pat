- The study establishes the DKAT cell line from a patient with triple-negative breast cancer, showcasing its aggressive nature and potential for rapid growth in vitro and in vivo. This cell line serves as a valuable model to investigate mechanisms of tumor progression and therapeutic resistance.

- DKAT cells exhibit significant plasticity, capable of undergoing epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) when cultured in specific conditions. This EMT induction leads to changes in morphology, gene expression, and functional properties, such as increased migration and invasion capabilities.

- Gene expression analysis reveals distinct profiles between DKAT cells and other breast cancer cell lines, highlighting unique molecular signatures that may contribute to the aggressive phenotype of triple-negative breast cancer. These findings provide insights into potential therapeutic targets.

- The role of Zeb1 in EMT is explored through overexpression and knockdown experiments. Zeb1 induction promotes EMT, while its suppression reverses this process, underscoring the importance of transcription factors in regulating cellular plasticity and cancer progression.

- Xenograft experiments demonstrate that DKAT cells can form tumors in immunocompromised mice, with varying efficiency depending on the number of cells injected and the presence of matrigel. These results validate the tumorigenic potential of DKAT cells and their utility in preclinical studies.

- Tumorsphere assays reveal the ability of DKAT cells to form spheres under stem cell conditions, indicating the presence of cancer stem-like cells. This property is crucial for understanding tumor heterogeneity and resistance to therapy, and may have implications for developing targeted treatments.

- Immunofluorescence staining of xenografts shows co-expression of p53 and keratin 14, suggesting the presence of both epithelial and mesenchymal markers in the tumors. This finding supports the plastic nature of DKAT cells and their potential to adopt multiple cellular states during tumor development.

- The study's comprehensive approach integrates cell biology, molecular genetics, and in vivo modeling to provide a detailed characterization of DKAT cells. These findings contribute to our understanding of triple-negative breast cancer and offer new avenues for investigating therapeutic strategies.
