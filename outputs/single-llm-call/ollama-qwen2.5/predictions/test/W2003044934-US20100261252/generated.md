- The serine-type IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* is a complex protein with multiple domains. Previous attempts to produce it in its native form resulted in very low yields and activity. This study aimed to develop a method for high-yield production of the active protease domain alone, which could be crucial for therapeutic applications.

- Four constructs (pET-S-IGAN, pET-IGAN, pCold-S-IGAN, and pCold-IGAN) encoding only the protease domain were created and expressed in various *E. coli* strains under different conditions. The goal was to identify the optimal construct and conditions for producing soluble and active IgA1 proteases.

- ELISA screening showed that the pET-IGAN construct produced higher levels of soluble IgA1 protease, especially when induced at 20°C with 0.4 mM IPTG in *E. coli* C41 (DE3) cells. This strain and condition combination yielded approximately 100 mg/L of soluble protease.

- The soluble IgA1 protease from *E. coli* C41 (DE3) was purified to over 95% purity, with a final yield of 20-40 mg/L. A novel activity assay using an Experion automated electrophoresis system measured the proteolytic activity at approximately 46 ng/μL/min per ng of IgA1 protease, significantly higher than previously reported yields and activities.

- Unlike previous attempts that struggled with low yields and activity, this method successfully produced high levels of soluble and active IgA1 protease by expressing only the protease domain. This approach avoids the complexities associated with full-length precursor expression and cleavage, making it a promising method for producing therapeutic quantities of IgA1 proteases.

- The use of *E. coli* instead of pathogenic bacteria like *H. influenzae*, *N. gonorrhoeae*, and *N. meningitidis* simplifies the production process and enhances safety. The significantly increased yield also makes it feasible to produce enough IgA1 protease for therapeutic applications, such as treating IgA nephropathy and other diseases involving IgA1 deposition.
