- The study confirms ODAM's presence in odontogenic tissues like ameloblasts, tooth follicles, and ameloblastomas, indicating its role in normal and tumor-related tooth formation. It also highlights the gene's location within the SCPP cluster on chromosome 4q13, suggesting involvement in dental development.
- ODAM is expressed in non-odontogenic tissues such as trachea, salivary glands, lacrimal gland, and breast ducts, indicating broader functions beyond tooth formation. This expression may be due to the protein's multi-functional properties conferred by disorder-promoting amino acids like proline and glutamine.
- The short 153-residue ODAM protein, initially identified in a gastric cancer cell line, lacks a secretory signal sequence and likely has intracellular functions distinct from the larger ODAM product. This suggests alternative splicing or untranslated regions may play a role in ODAM's functional diversity.
- ODAM is overexpressed in various epithelial cancers, including breast, lung, and gastric cancers, indicating potential involvement in carcinogenesis. Its co-expression with Gli-1, a member of the Shh pathway, further supports its role in tumor development and progression.
- The presence of serum anti-ODAM IgG antibodies in patients with breast, lung, and gastric cancer suggests an immune response to this protein. These autoantibodies could serve as potential biomarkers for early detection or prognosis in epithelial cancers, highlighting the diagnostic and therapeutic relevance of ODAM research.
