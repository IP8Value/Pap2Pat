- This study found significantly higher CSF isocitrate levels in bipolar disorder (BD) patients compared to healthy controls. The increase was unrelated to medication.

- mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B genes were lower in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of BD patients than controls, suggesting altered mitochondrial function.

- Protein levels of IDH3A were also reduced in cerebellar tissue from BD patients compared to controls. 

- Genetic analyses found no significant associations between ACO/IDH genes and BD in a Swedish cohort.

- Chronic lithium or valproate treatment did not alter rat CSF isocitrate levels or brain mRNA expression of related genes.

- The findings suggest mitochondrial dysfunction may be involved in BD pathogenesis, with IDH3 playing a key role. Isocitrate could serve as a potential biomarker for BD.

- Limitations include the male-only sample and need to confirm results in medication-free patients. Associations may differ in other populations.

- This study provides novel evidence that altered isocitrate metabolism via mitochondrial IDH3 may be involved in BD pathogenesis, suggesting a new therapeutic target.
