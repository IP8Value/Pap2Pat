- A novel methodology for assessing breast implant mechanical properties was developed and validated. It measures multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli from compression testing data to evaluate shape stability and deformation characteristics.
- The IDEAL IMPLANT demonstrated lower multi-dimensional strains and higher tangent moduli compared to similar-sized silicone gel implants, especially under loads exceeding 100 N. This indicates greater shape stability for the IDEAL IMPLANT.
- Shape stability metrics may help explain clinical outcomes like higher ultimate strength and lower deflation/rupture rates observed with the IDEAL IMPLANT. The dual lumen structure likely contributes to its unique mechanical properties.
- Despite higher shape stability, the IDEAL IMPLANT exhibits similar softness to gel implants under low localized compressive loads in a simple "pinch" test. This supports clinical observations of its gel-like feel.
- Results suggest implant softness does not necessarily correlate with low shape stability. Shape stability metrics could provide additional insight into mechanical properties important for safety and performance, beyond current standards.
- Lower multi-dimensional strains and higher tangent moduli may reduce dynamic interaction between the implant surface and capsule wall, potentially explaining lower capsular contracture rates observed clinically for the IDEAL IMPLANT.
- The similar shape stability of cohesive gel and conventional gel implants was unexpected. It suggests cross-linking degree may have limited impact on shape stability under compressive loads, contrary to conventional thought about cohesive gels.
- This new methodology could help optimize current and future implant designs towards improved clinical outcomes by providing additional mechanical characterization beyond traditional testing methods.
- The study demonstrates that silicone-free structured implants like the IDEAL IMPLANT can achieve high shape stability while maintaining a soft feel comparable to gel implants, challenging assumptions in breast implant design.
