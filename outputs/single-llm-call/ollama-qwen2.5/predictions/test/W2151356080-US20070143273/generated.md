- The provided document details the creation and evaluation of Relemed, a sentence-level search engine with relevance scoring for MEDLINE. It aims to improve precision and efficiency over existing systems like PubMed by focusing on relationships between query terms within individual sentences rather than entire articles.

- Relemed sorts results into 8 relevance levels based on how the query terms appear together in sentences. The first level shows articles where all terms are adjacent, while lower levels allow for more distance or sentence boundaries between terms. This allows users to quickly identify the most relevant articles.

- Two case studies comparing Relemed and PubMed were presented. For a Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) query, Relemed's precision started at 100% compared to 39.3% for PubMed. A "health literacy" query showed similar gains, with Relemed starting at 100% vs 39.3% for PubMed in the first 28 results.

- The authors note limitations like potential issues with very long queries and small result sets. Future improvements could include showing total matches per relevance level, adding field-specific searches, and refining scores through natural language processing. Overall, initial results suggest Relemed improves precision and efficiency over PubMed for multi-term biomedical queries.
