- The experimental setup for this research was conducted in a Bluefors LD-250 dilution refrigerator, with specific configurations for flux and charge inputs. These inputs were attenuated at multiple stages to minimize noise, using standard XMA attenuators and a custom bias tee. Additional low-pass filters and Eccosorb IR filters were employed to enhance qubit coherence times and reduce temperatures.
- The device was thermally anchored to the base stage of the refrigerator at 15 mK, ensuring minimal thermal interference. It was also shielded from environmental noise using an inner lead shield and two cylindrical μ-metal cans, all thermally stabilized with copper components. This setup helped in maintaining the qubit and resonator at low temperatures and reducing thermal photon effects.
- The flux and charge inputs were carefully combined and filtered to ensure high-fidelity control of the qubit. The use of commercial low-pass filters and sharp cutoff filters from K&L Microwave further minimized unwanted signals, contributing to the overall stability and performance of the quantum device during experiments.
- The experimental setup included a detailed wiring configuration that was crucial for achieving reliable results. The flux and charge lines were meticulously designed to minimize signal loss and interference, with particular attention given to the final 20 dB attenuator on the rf charge line, which was made from threaded copper for better thermal conductivity.
- The inner lead shield and μ-metal cans provided additional layers of shielding, ensuring that the sample was isolated from external electromagnetic disturbances. This setup not only improved the coherence times but also helped in maintaining a stable environment for performing delicate quantum operations, such as Ramsey and spin-echo experiments.
- The use of Eccosorb IR filters on both the flux and output lines played a critical role in reducing thermal photons, which can degrade qubit performance. These filters, along with the careful thermal anchoring of the sample shield, contributed to achieving high-quality coherence times (T1 and T2) necessary for quantum computing applications.
- The experimental setup was designed to support a wide range of measurements, including Ramsey and spin-echo experiments, by providing stable and controlled conditions. This allowed the researchers to accurately characterize the qubit's behavior under various flux and charge conditions, essential for understanding and optimizing the performance of the fluxonium device.
