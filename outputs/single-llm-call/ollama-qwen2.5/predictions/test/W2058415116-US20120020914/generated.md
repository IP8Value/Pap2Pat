# Patent Application

## Title: Method for Inhibiting HPV-16 LCR Transcription and Proliferation of HPV-Transformed Cells Using Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF)

## Background

### Field of the Invention
The present invention relates to methods for inhibiting transcription from the human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV-16) long control region (LCR) and reducing the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells. Specifically, it involves the use of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) to achieve these effects.

### Description of Related Art
Human papillomaviruses (HPVs) are a group of viruses that can cause various types of warts and cancers, including cervical cancer. HPV-16 is one of the most common high-risk types associated with malignant transformation. The long control region (LCR) of HPV-16 plays a crucial role in regulating viral gene expression, particularly for the E6 and E7 oncogenes. Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) is a cytokine known to activate the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, which can influence cell proliferation and differentiation.

## Summary of the Invention
The present invention provides a method for inhibiting transcription from the HPV-16 LCR and reducing the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells using Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF). The method involves treating HPV-16 transformed cells with LIF, which significantly reduces the expression of E6 and E7 oncogenes and decreases cell proliferation. This approach has potential therapeutic applications for managing HPV-related diseases, particularly cervical cancer.

## Detailed Description

### Introduction to HPV-16 and LCR
Human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV-16) is a well-characterized high-risk HPV that causes over 50% of cervical cancers. The long control region (LCR) of HPV-16 is a regulatory sequence that controls the expression of viral genes, particularly the E6 and E7 oncogenes. These oncogenes are essential for the transformation and maintenance of the malignant phenotype in infected cells.

### Role of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF)
Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) is a cytokine that belongs to the interleukin-6 family. It signals through the LIF receptor (LIFR) and gp130, activating the JAK-STAT pathway. This pathway is involved in various cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Recent studies have shown that LIF can modulate the expression of viral genes in infected cells.

### Inhibition of HPV-16 LCR Transcription
In this study, we compared the transcriptional activity of the HPV-16 LCR in untreated cervical SiHa cells and cells treated with various cytokines. The IL-6 superfamily member LIF reduced HPV-16 LCR expression by approximately 60% in a time- and dose-dependent manner. To verify that the observed decrease in LCR transcription is functionally significant, we examined the effect of LIF on mRNA expression using quantitative real-time PCR. CaSki cervical cancer cells were treated with different concentrations of LIF for various durations. The expression of E7 mRNA was significantly reduced over this time course, initially in a dose-dependent manner, although at three days, cells treated with low or high concentrations of LIF showed similar inhibition of E7; E6 inhibition followed a similar trend.

### Induction of Phosphorylation of STAT3
LIF is known to activate members of the JAK-STAT pathway, particularly STAT3. We evaluated the effect of LIF on STAT3 activation in SiHa cells. Following treatment with LIF, STAT3 was phosphorylated on tyrosine residue 705, indicative of activation, in a transient fashion. To determine if the observed phosphorylation was accompanied by transcriptional activation, we used a reporter plasmid and transfected SiHa cells. After treatment with LIF, the activity of the reporter plasmid was 1.8-fold higher in LIF-treated cells than in untreated cells.

### Proliferation of HPV-Transformed Cells
We also assessed the effect of LIF on the proliferation of HPV-16 transformed cervical cancer cells. CaSki cells were seeded in 96-well plates and treated with different concentrations of LIF or other cytokines. After 40 hours, cell proliferation was measured using the MTT assay. As expected, EGF promoted the proliferation of the cells relative to untreated cells. The LIF-treated cells, however, proliferated much less rapidly, only reaching a final population density approximately half that of untreated cells.

### Mechanism of Action
The inhibition of HPV-16 LCR transcription by LIF is likely mediated through the JAK-STAT pathway. STAT3 activation may lead to changes in the cellular environment that reduce the expression of viral oncogenes and inhibit cell proliferation. The absence of significant loss of membrane asymmetry suggests that LIF-treated cells undergo cell-cycle arrest rather than apoptosis.

### Potential Therapeutic Applications
The ability of LIF to inhibit HPV-16 LCR transcription and reduce the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells has significant therapeutic potential. This approach could be used in combination with other treatments, such as chemotherapy or immunotherapy, to manage HPV-related diseases, particularly cervical cancer. Further studies are needed to optimize the use of LIF for clinical applications.

## Examples

### Example 1: Effect of LIF on HPV-16 LCR Transcription
SiHa cells were transfected with a reporter plasmid containing the HPV-16 LCR and treated with different concentrations of LIF. Luciferase activity was measured to assess transcriptional activity. Results showed that LIF significantly reduced HPV-16 LCR expression in a time- and dose-dependent manner.

### Example 2: Effect of LIF on E6 and E7 mRNA Expression
CaSki cells were treated with different concentrations of LIF for various durations. RNA was extracted, and qRT-PCR was performed to measure the expression of E6 and E7 mRNAs. Results showed that LIF significantly reduced the expression of both E6 and E7 mRNAs.

### Example 3: Effect of LIF on STAT3 Phosphorylation
SiHa cells were treated with LIF, and Western blot analysis was performed to assess STAT3 phosphorylation. Results showed that LIF induced transient phosphorylation of STAT3, indicating activation of the JAK-STAT pathway.

### Example 4: Effect of LIF on Cell Proliferation
CaSki cells were treated with different concentrations of LIF or other cytokines, and cell proliferation was measured using the MTT assay. Results showed that LIF significantly inhibited the proliferation of HPV-16 transformed cells.

## Conclusion
The present invention provides a method for inhibiting transcription from the HPV-16 LCR and reducing the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells using Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF). This approach has significant therapeutic potential for managing HPV-related diseases, particularly cervical cancer. Further studies are needed to optimize the use of LIF for clinical applications.
