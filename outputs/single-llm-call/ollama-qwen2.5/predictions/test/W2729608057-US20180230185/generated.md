The synthesis and characterization of cyclic peptides targeting HER2 receptor were detailed. Compound 18 was identified as a potent inhibitor of HER2 dimerization. It demonstrated significant antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing breast cancer cell lines, with IC50 values ranging from 1 to 5 μM.

In vitro studies revealed that compound 18 effectively inhibited HER2:HER3 heterodimer formation, a key driver of HER2-mediated signaling pathways. This inhibition led to reduced activation of downstream effectors such as AKT and ERK, which are crucial for cell survival and proliferation.

NMR spectroscopy provided insights into the conformational properties of compound 18 in solution. The data indicated that the cyclic peptide adopted a compact structure with specific intramolecular interactions, consistent with its biological activity and stability.

Molecular docking studies using AUTODOCK software showed that compound 18 binds to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular region with high affinity. The binding mode involved key residues that are critical for HER2 dimerization, suggesting a mechanism by which the peptide inhibits receptor activation.

In vivo studies in athymic nude mice bearing BT-474 breast tumors demonstrated that intratumoral administration of compound 18 at 4 mg/kg twice weekly significantly reduced tumor growth compared to vehicle control. Tumor sections analyzed by Western blot and PLA assays confirmed inhibition of HER2 signaling and dimerization.

Pharmacokinetic analysis in mice showed that compound 18 exhibited good stability in mouse serum, with detectable levels up to 24 hours post-administration. The internal standard method using a cyclic peptide analog facilitated accurate quantification of compound 18, providing valuable data for dose optimization.

Toxicity studies revealed no significant adverse effects on major organs or blood parameters at the pharmacological dosage. Histopathological examination of liver, kidney, and heart tissues from treated mice showed no signs of toxicity, supporting the safety profile of compound 18 in preclinical models.

Overall, these findings highlight the potential of cyclic peptide compound 18 as a novel therapeutic agent for targeting HER2-positive breast cancer. Further optimization and clinical development are warranted to explore its efficacy and safety in human patients.
