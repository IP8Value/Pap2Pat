### Describe the Hydrogenation Process and Its Relevance

Hydrogenation is a chemical process where hydrogen gas (H₂) is added to molecules containing double or triple bonds. This reaction is catalyzed by metals like palladium, platinum, or nickel. In the context of terpene-based monomers, hydrogenation can be used to modify the structure and properties of these compounds, making them more stable and less reactive. For example, hydrogenating the exocyclic double bond in carvone-derived acrylates can prevent crosslinking and gel formation, leading to more controlled polymerization.

### Alternative Methods for Monomer Synthesis

Alternative methods for synthesizing terpene-based monomers include hydroformylation, which adds a formyl group (CHO) to an alkene, and epoxidation, which introduces an epoxy group. Hydroformylation can produce aldehydes or alcohols, while epoxidation creates cyclic ethers. These methods offer different functional groups that can be further modified to create a variety of monomers with unique properties. For instance, hydroformylation followed by reduction can yield primary alcohols, which can then be esterified to form acrylates or methacrylates.

### Catalytic One-Pot Synthesis

The catalytic one-pot synthesis of β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers is a significant advancement. This method uses palladium acetate (Pd(OAc)₂) as the catalyst, benzoquinone as both an oxidant and ligand, and methacrylic acid as both solvent and reactant. The process operates at 50°C under air, achieving high yields of regioisomeric monomers. This approach is environmentally friendly and scalable, making it suitable for industrial applications. The one-pot nature of the reaction simplifies the production process, reducing costs and waste.

### Initial Results and Optimization

Initial results using White's conditions with 10 mol% palladium acetate, 1 equivalent of benzoquinone in DMSO at 50°C yielded a 16% mix of two new monomers. Optimizing catalyst loading, benzoquinone concentration, temperature, and atmosphere improved the yield to 82%, with a 10:1 ratio of the desired regioisomers. The optimized conditions used just 2 mol% palladium acetate and 2 equivalents of benzoquinone, demonstrating the effectiveness of the catalytic system. This optimization is crucial for scaling up the process and making it industrially viable.

### Comparison with Prior Work

Comparing our method to prior work, such as Meier's coupling of acetic acid and limonene in DMSO with an iron co-catalyst, or Ferret's coupling of olefinic structures with acrylic acid and methacrylic acid, our approach stands out. Our method achieves more than double the yield (82% vs 40%) and provides a higher ratio of regioisomers (10:1 vs 1:1). The use of methacrylic acid as both solvent and reactant is also a significant advantage, simplifying the process and reducing environmental impact.

### Polymerization of New Monomers

The new β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers were polymerized using AIBN as the initiator at 65°C. Full conversion led to an insoluble polymer, confirming the ease of polymerization and demonstrating that the retained exocyclic olefinic moiety leads to crosslinking. This result is consistent with our understanding of terpene-based polymers and highlights the potential for creating high-performance materials from renewable resources.

### Properties of β-Pinene Polymers

The properties of β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate polymers were characterized using techniques such as FTIR, DSC, and mechanical testing. The acrylates showed a range of glass transition temperatures (Tg) depending on the molecular weight, with higher Tg values for methacrylates due to increased steric hindrance. The carvone-derived methacrylate polymer exhibited a high Tg of 117°C, making it suitable for powder coatings. Mechanical tests revealed properties characteristic of acrylic coatings, including high gloss, transparency, and chemical resistance.

### Application in Powder Coatings

To demonstrate the practical application of these new polymers, we prepared a carvone methacrylate-based powder coating. The mixture was melt-mixed, extruded, and ground to a fine powder. The powder was then sprayed onto aluminum panels using an electrostatic air gun and cured at 120°C for 2 minutes followed by UV irradiation. The resulting coating was transparent, highly glossy, and showed excellent adhesion and mechanical properties. Standard tests such as the acetone-rub test and pencil hardness scratch testing confirmed the high performance of the coating.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed a new catalytic one-pot approach to synthesize β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers from readily available terpenes. These monomers can be polymerized into useful materials with a wide range of physical properties. As an example, we demonstrated the preparation of a high-performance renewable acrylic powder coating using industry-standard procedures. This work opens up new opportunities for creating sustainable coatings and other advanced materials from renewable resources.
