The paper introduces OneAligner, a model designed for sentence retrieval tasks. It demonstrates strong cross-lingual transfer capabilities even when trained on a single language pair. The authors conduct extensive experiments and analyses to identify key factors influencing performance, such as monolingual data size, parallel data size, and the number of rich-resource language pairs. Despite its broad coverage, the work leaves room for future exploration in handling languages not present during pretraining and understanding the mechanisms behind cross-lingual transfer.

The model is evaluated across three primary datasets: Tatoeba-Challenge, BUCC, and XTREME. On Tatoeba-Challenge, OneAligner outperforms previous models by a significant margin, achieving high accuracy even when trained on limited language pairs. The results indicate that the model can effectively leverage rich-resource languages to benefit low-resource ones. For BUCC, the model shows strong cross-lingual transfer capabilities, performing well without seeing parallel examples for specific language pairs.

OneAligner leverages XLM-R as its base architecture and introduces several optimizations tailored for sentence alignment tasks. The authors use mean pooling followed by cosine distance for efficient retrieval and employ a fixed 1M data budget to ensure fair comparisons. They also explore the impact of different training strategies, such as combining multiple rich-resource language pairs, which leads to incremental performance gains. The model's effectiveness in cross-lingual transfer is attributed to its ability to learn robust multilingual representations during finetuning.

The authors provide detailed Tatoeba-36 results for models trained on various language pairs from the OPUS-100 dataset. They show that OneAligner consistently outperforms previous models across different language pairs, with an average accuracy of 92.4% when trained on a single rich-resource pair and up to 94.4% when trained on all language pairs under the fixed data budget. The detailed results highlight the model's robustness and versatility in handling diverse languages.

The paper also includes comprehensive New-Tatoeba results, which further validate OneAligner's performance across a broader range of languages. The detailed performance metrics for each language pair show consistent high accuracy, reinforcing the model's ability to generalize well. The authors note that adding more rich-resource pairs can lead to marginal improvements but suggest that the effort required to collect low-resource data may not justify the small gains in performance, motivating future research into more effective use of limited resources.

In conclusion, OneAligner represents a significant advancement in cross-lingual sentence alignment by demonstrating strong transfer capabilities and identifying key factors for optimal performance. The model's robustness across various languages and settings highlights its potential for practical applications in multilingual information retrieval. Future work could explore handling out-of-pretraining languages and understanding the underlying mechanisms of cross-lingual transfer, as well as extending OneAligner to other retrieval tasks beyond sentence alignment.
