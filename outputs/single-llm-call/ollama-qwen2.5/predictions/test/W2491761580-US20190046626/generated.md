- **Codon Harmonization**: By adopting codon harmonization, which matches the codon usage in E. coli closely to that of Chlamydia, we achieved significant improvement in recombinant MOMP expression. This method incorporates pauses during translation, enhancing protein folding and biosynthesis efficiency.

- **Promoters and Vectors**: Combining a moderate-strength promoter with a low-copy-number vector greatly enhanced rMOMP surface expression. Slower transcription and translation allowed more time for proper protein secretion, folding, and insertion into the outer membrane.

- **Leader Sequences**: E. coli-derived leader sequences like OmpA and OmpP drove higher levels of rMOMP surface expression compared to native Chlamydia sequences. This suggests that host-specific leaders are more efficient in guiding protein secretion and membrane insertion.

- **Cell Culture Conditions**: Optimizing cell culture conditions, including the use of suitable medium, induction timing, and temperature, was crucial for balancing high rMOMP expression with host cell viability. These adjustments helped achieve higher surface expression levels while maintaining cell health.

- **Peptidyl-Prolyl Cis/Trans-Isomerases (PPIases)**: Future efforts will involve co-expressing PPIases like SurA, PpiD, FkpA, and PpiA to potentially improve periplasmic folding of rMOMP. These enzymes can catalyze prolyl-iminopeptide bond isomerization, enhancing protein expression.

- **Disulfide Bond Formation**: Overexpressing oxidoreductases such as DsbA and DsbC in E. coli could help catalyze correct disulfide bond formation in rMOMP, a cysteine-rich protein. This might further improve its outer membrane expression and stability.

- **Secondary Structure and Immunogenicity**: OM-expressed rMOMP maintained β-strand secondary structure and elicited comparable antibody responses to native MOMP in mice. This suggests that the recombinant protein retains key epitopes in their native forms, making it a viable vaccine candidate.

- **Mouse Challenge Model**: Both native and OM-expressed rMOMP immunizations resulted in similar low post-challenge bacterial burdens using C. trachomatis serovar D. This indicates that both forms of MOMP are effective in eliciting protective immune responses in mice.

- **Future Studies**: Evaluating vaccine efficacy in a more stringent C. muridarum mouse challenge model will provide further insights into the role of adaptive immunity. The techniques used for rMOMP expression can also serve as a platform for producing other outer membrane proteins for various applications.
