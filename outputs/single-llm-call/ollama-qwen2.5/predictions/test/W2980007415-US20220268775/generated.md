- **Background**: A randomized phase II trial evaluated an autologous tumor cell vaccine (TCV) and a dendritic cell vaccine (DCV) for metastatic melanoma. The study aimed to determine if soluble PD-1 (sPD-1) levels could predict survival outcomes, particularly in patients treated with DCV.

- **Methods**: Blood samples were collected from 39 of 42 randomized patients at baseline and after three weekly vaccine doses. sPD-1 levels were measured using a multiplex ELISA. Statistical analyses included Fisher's exact test, Mann–Whitney U test, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and log-rank test for survival curves.

- **Results**: DCV-treated patients with lower baseline sPD-1 levels or a decrease in sPD-1 after vaccination had better survival outcomes. TCV-treated patients showed no significant correlation between sPD-1 levels and survival.

- **Discussion**: The findings suggest that sPD-1 levels might serve as a biomarker for predicting response to DCV in metastatic melanoma. However, the small sample size and retrospective nature of the analysis limit the conclusions, necessitating further validation in larger trials.

- **Conclusion**: Combining baseline sPD-1 levels with changes after three weekly vaccines predicted long-term survival in DCV-treated patients with metastatic melanoma. This hypothesis requires confirmation in larger prospective studies.
