# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cardiac health and specifically to methods and systems for assessing diastolic function. The invention provides a novel strain relaxation index (SRI) that can be used in conjunction with cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging to evaluate myocardial deformation during left ventricular (LV) relaxation. This index is designed to improve the accuracy and reliability of cardiac failure risk assessment, thereby enhancing patient care and management.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cardiac health is a critical area of medical research due to the high prevalence of cardiovascular diseases, including heart failure (HF) and atrial fibrillation (AF). These conditions are often associated with diastolic dysfunction, which refers to abnormalities in the relaxation and filling phases of the cardiac cycle. Traditional methods for assessing diastolic function, such as echocardiography, have limitations in terms of accuracy and reproducibility. For example, echocardiographic measurements can be influenced by a variety of factors, including operator skill and patient variability. Therefore, there is a need for more reliable and objective methods to evaluate cardiac function.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a method for assessing cardiac function using a novel strain relaxation index (SRI). The SRI is computed from myocardial deformation data obtained through cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging. Specifically, the method involves calculating the difference between post-systolic and systolic times of the strain peaks, divided by the early diastolic strain rate peak, normalized by the total relaxation time. This index provides a quantitative measure of both very early myocardial relaxation and tissue compliance, which are key indicators of diastolic function.

The invention further includes an embodiment of the imaging modality used to acquire the necessary data for SRI calculation. The preferred imaging modality is CMR, which offers high spatial and temporal resolution, allowing for precise measurement of myocardial deformation. Additionally, the invention encompasses a system for implementing the method, including hardware and software components designed to process and analyze CMR images.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present patent application provides a detailed description of a novel method and system for determining cardiac events, particularly focusing on diastolic function. The primary purpose of this invention is to improve the accuracy and reliability of cardiac failure risk assessment by introducing a strain relaxation index (SRI) that can be derived from cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging.

The motivation behind developing this method lies in the limitations of current techniques for assessing diastolic function. Traditional methods, such as echocardiography, often suffer from operator-dependent variability and may not provide a comprehensive assessment of myocardial relaxation and tissue compliance. The SRI, on the other hand, offers an objective and reproducible measure that can be used to predict the risk of heart failure (HF) and atrial fibrillation (AF).

The system and method for determining cardiac events involve several key components. First, an imaging modality is used to acquire high-resolution CMR images of the left ventricle. The preferred imaging technique involves harmonic phase analysis, which allows for the computation of mid-ventricular mid-wall circumferential strains and strain rates. These strain measurements are critical for calculating the SRI.

The cardiac image analysis process begins with the acquisition of tagged CMR images, which provide detailed information about myocardial deformation during the cardiac cycle. The method involves identifying the systolic and post-systolic times of the strain peaks, as well as the early diastolic strain rate peak (E peak). These values are then used to compute the SRI according to a specific formula: the difference between post-systolic and systolic times divided by the early diastolic strain rate peak, normalized by the total relaxation time.

The level of risk for cardiac failure is determined based on the computed SRI value. Higher SRI values indicate greater impairment in myocardial relaxation and tissue compliance, which are associated with an increased risk of HF and AF. The method also takes into account other established risk factors, such as NT-proBNP levels and coronary calcium scores, to provide a comprehensive assessment of cardiac health.

One of the limitations of current CMR methods is their inability to accurately capture early diastolic events. The SRI addresses this limitation by focusing on circumferential strain and strain rates during the relaxation phase of the cardiac cycle. Specifically, the method accounts for systolic, post-systolic, and early diastolic strain peaks, as well as the isovolumic relaxation time (IVRT). The early diastolic strain rate (E peak) is particularly important as it reflects tissue compliance and is a key indicator of diastolic function.

The development of the SRI was motivated by the need for an objective measure that could predict cardiac events more accurately than existing methods. The SRI calculation involves several steps, including the identification of relevant strain peaks and the normalization of these values to account for individual variability. The system components include a CMR imaging device, data processing software, and a user interface for displaying results. The non-transitory computer-readable medium stores the algorithms used to compute the SRI and other relevant parameters. The user interface allows clinicians to visualize deformation curves and SRI values, facilitating the interpretation of results and the formulation of treatment plans.

An example study was conducted to validate the effectiveness of the SRI as a predictor of HF and AF. The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) provided data from participants who underwent both echocardiography and tagged CMR imaging. The study involved 125 participants, with an average age of 61 years, and included a diverse population in terms of race and gender.

Data acquisition and analysis were performed using harmonic phase analysis to compute mid-ventricular mid-wall circumferential strains and strain rates. The SRI value was calculated for each participant, and risk factors such as NT-proBNP levels and coronary calcium scores were also measured. Events were classified based on established criteria for HF and AF, and a combined end-point was defined to evaluate the overall predictive power of the SRI.

Statistical analysis was performed using Cox regression to determine hazard ratios and confidence intervals. The Harrell's C-statistic and integrated discrimination index (IDI) were used to assess the performance of the SRI in predicting cardiac events. The net reclassification index (NRI) was also calculated to evaluate the improvement in risk stratification provided by the SRI.

Secondary analysis involved comparing the SRI with established risk factors, such as age, blood pressure, and cholesterol levels. Model calibration was performed using Kaplan-Meier curves to assess the concordance between predicted and observed outcomes. Baseline characteristics of the study population were described, including the incidence of HF and AF. Changes in SRI and early diastolic strain rate over time were also analyzed.

The results of the univariate and multivariate analyses demonstrated that the SRI was a significant predictor of HF and AF, with hazard ratios indicating a strong association between higher SRI values and increased risk. The IDI and NRI results further confirmed the improved predictive accuracy provided by the SRI compared to traditional methods.

In conclusion, the strain relaxation index (SRI) is a novel and effective method for assessing diastolic function and predicting the risk of heart failure and atrial fibrillation. By providing an objective and reproducible measure of myocardial relaxation and tissue compliance, the SRI has the potential to improve patient care and management in the field of cardiac health.

### EXAMPLE

The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) provided data from 125 participants who underwent both echocardiography and tagged cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging. The study population was diverse, with an average age of 61 years, 41% males, and a mix of White and African-American individuals.

Cardiac MRI studies were performed using harmonic phase analysis to compute mid-ventricular mid-wall circumferential strains and strain rates. Data acquisition involved the use of tagged CMR images, which allowed for detailed measurement of myocardial deformation during the cardiac cycle. The SRI value was calculated for each participant based on the difference between post-systolic and systolic times of the strain peaks, divided by the early diastolic strain rate peak (E peak), normalized by the total relaxation time.

Risk factors were assessed using a variety of measurements, including NT-proBNP levels, coronary calcium scores, blood pressure, and cholesterol levels. The NT-proBNP measurement was performed to evaluate biomarker levels associated with cardiac dysfunction. Coronary calcium scores were determined using computed tomography (CT) scans to assess the presence of calcified plaques in the coronary arteries.

Events were classified based on established criteria for HF and AF. Heart failure was defined as a clinical diagnosis of HF, hospitalization for HF symptoms, or use of HF medications. Atrial fibrillation was diagnosed based on electrocardiogram (ECG) findings or documentation in medical records. A combined end-point was defined to evaluate the overall predictive power of the SRI.

Statistical analysis was performed using Cox regression to determine hazard ratios and confidence intervals for the SRI as a predictor of cardiac events. The Harrell's C-statistic was used to assess the concordance between predicted and observed outcomes, while the integrated discrimination index (IDI) and net reclassification index (NRI) were calculated to evaluate the improvement in risk stratification provided by the SRI.

Secondary analysis involved comparing the SRI with established risk factors such as age, blood pressure, and cholesterol levels. Model calibration was performed using Kaplan-Meier curves to assess the concordance between predicted and observed outcomes. Baseline characteristics of the study population were described, including the incidence of HF and AF. Changes in SRI and early diastolic strain rate over time were also analyzed.

The results of the univariate and multivariate analyses demonstrated that the SRI was a significant predictor of HF and AF, with hazard ratios indicating a strong association between higher SRI values and increased risk. The IDI and NRI results further confirmed the improved predictive accuracy provided by the SRI compared to traditional methods.

In conclusion, the strain relaxation index (SRI) is a novel and effective method for assessing diastolic function and predicting the risk of heart failure and atrial fibrillation. By providing an objective and reproducible measure of myocardial relaxation and tissue compliance, the SRI has the potential to improve patient care and management in the field of cardiac health.
