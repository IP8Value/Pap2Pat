- **Introduction**: This paper introduces the concept of verifiable encryption (VE), a class of cryptosystems that allows for the verification of encrypted data without decryption. VE is particularly useful in scenarios where security and speed are critical, such as authentication protocols. The authors explore various cryptographic schemes that fit into this class, including Fiat-Shamir, Schnorr, and one-time pad.

- **Verifiable Encryption Definition**: Verifiable encryption (VE) is defined as a pair of cryptosystems (E, E') and their respective cipher text spaces. VE enables the verification of the relationship between two encrypted messages without revealing the actual content. This property is crucial for authentication protocols that need to ensure data integrity and authenticity.

- **Fiat-Shamir and Schnorr**: The paper demonstrates that specific cases of the Fiat-Shamir and Schnorr identification schemes are included in the class of VE. These schemes are well-known for their security properties and efficiency, making them suitable candidates for verifiable encryption. The authors provide a detailed analysis of how these schemes can be adapted to fit the VE framework.

- **One-Time Pad**: The one-time pad (OTP) is another cryptosystem that fits into the class of VE. The OTP is renowned for its perfect secrecy but has traditionally been limited by key management issues. The paper shows how the OTP can be used in a verifiable encryption context, particularly for authentication protocols where speed and security are paramount.

- **Authentication Algorithm**: The authors construct an authentication algorithm using the one-time pad that incorporates both speed and security without requiring public key infrastructure (PKI) or SSL/TLS. This protocol is deterministic and does not require key distribution or message exchanges typical of interactive proofs. The algorithm is designed to be efficient, with a processing time of less than 0.003 ms for keys up to 8192 bits.

- **Implementation**: An implementation of the authentication algorithm using the one-time pad is presented, demonstrating its practicality. The experiment involves a user (A), a trusted device (D), and an untrusted server (S). The device generates and manages keys, while the server performs the necessary computations to verify the user's identity without accessing sensitive information.

- **Experimental Results**: The experimental results show that the algorithm maintains consistent performance across different plaintext lengths. The encryption, verification, and decryption processes all take approximately 0.00095367 ms, regardless of the plaintext length. This efficiency makes the protocol suitable for real-world applications where speed is crucial.

- **Attacks**: The paper discusses potential attacks on the authentication algorithm, including impersonation (man-in-the-middle) and plain text attacks. While the system is vulnerable to full access control and backdoors, it remains secure against plain text attacks due to the use of one-time keys. If an attacker gains a one-time key, they cannot recover the registered plaintext or the primary encryption key.

- **Conclusion**: The paper concludes by summarizing the contributions of the research, including the definition of verifiable encryption, the inclusion of various cryptographic schemes in this class, and the development of a secure and efficient authentication algorithm using the one-time pad. The authors highlight the importance of further research to enhance security against impersonation attacks.
