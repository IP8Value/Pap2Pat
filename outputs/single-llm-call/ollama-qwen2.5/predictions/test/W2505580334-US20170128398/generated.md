- In a canine model of septic shock, hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction developed over time, primarily manifesting as reduced maximal oxygen consumption rate (OCR). Norepinephrine reversed this dysfunction but did not improve lactic acidosis, suggesting that mitochondrial impairment is not essential for lactic acidosis. Gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS) had a greater effect on reversing mitochondrial dysfunction and also reduced lactic acidosis.
- GSS may reduce lactic acidosis by increasing hepatic lactate uptake through mechanisms such as decreasing glycolysis or enhancing gluconeogenesis. Compared to norepinephrine, GSS achieved similar increases in mean arterial pressure (MAP) but with lower heart rates, potentially offering cardiovascular benefits.
- The study used a high concentration of norepinephrine to restore MAP to baseline values, which may not reflect clinical practice where lower doses are typically used. Additionally, small changes in pulmonary wedge pressure (Pwp) occurred during the experiment, though these were unlikely to significantly affect cardiac output given the relatively flat left ventricular diastolic volume-pressure relationship.
- The non-septic control groups exhibited minor changes in blood chemistries, possibly due to rhabdomyolysis from prolonged supine positioning. However, these changes were much less pronounced than those seen in septic groups and did not significantly impact the study's main conclusions. Despite these methodological considerations, GSS shows promise as a potential therapy for lactic acidosis and cardiovascular collapse in septic shock.
- The study highlights the complex relationship between mitochondrial dysfunction, lactic acidosis, and hemodynamic parameters in septic shock. While norepinephrine can improve mitochondrial function, it does not address all aspects of lactic acidosis. GSS, with its antioxidant properties, offers a more comprehensive approach by improving both mitochondrial function and reducing lactic acidosis, potentially providing a novel therapeutic option for treating sepsis-induced cardiovascular collapse and metabolic derangements.
