- ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 are potent anti-inflammatory and pro-resolution molecules that effectively inhibit angiogenesis in a model of surgically induced corneal inflammation. These lipid mediators suppress neutrophil and macrophage infiltration, downregulate cytokine expression, and reduce VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2 gene levels.
- Corneal tissues and infiltrating innate immune cells express Fpr-rs2 (LXA4 receptor) and ChemR23 (RvE1 receptor). Activating these receptors on CD11b+ immunocytes stops their migration into the cornea and reduces cytokine production, highlighting a direct mechanism for inhibiting angiogenesis.
- ATLa shows higher potency in suppressing VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis compared to RvD1 and RvE1. This may be due to its longer duration of action as a stable analog of LXA4/ATL, along with its ability to inhibit multiple steps of VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis like EC adhesion and proliferation.
- The anti-inflammatory/pro-resolution functions of RvD1 and RvE1 largely depend on suppressing TNF-α expression rather than directly regulating VEGF-A. However, ATLa can reduce both IL-1β-induced and VEGF-A-induced corneal angiogenesis, potentially by impairing early EC migration.
- Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 are expressed in epithelial cells and stromal keratocytes of the cornea under normal and inflammatory conditions. This is the first study to map their distribution, providing insights into how these receptors may regulate inflammation and angiogenesis at different layers of the cornea.
