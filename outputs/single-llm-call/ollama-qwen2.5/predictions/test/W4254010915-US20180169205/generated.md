- The study aimed to enhance the immunogenicity of dengue virus (DENV) vaccine candidates by combining the benefits of flagellin as an adjuvant and DENV envelope protein for antigen presentation. This approach sought to elicit a robust immune response, particularly in mucosal regions, which are critical for preventing viral entry.
- The researchers engineered hybrid flagellin-DENV envelope protein constructs that could self-assemble into filamentous structures. These filaments were designed to mimic the natural conformation of the DENV envelope protein, enhancing antigen presentation and recognition by the immune system.
- To assess the immunogenicity of the hybrid filaments, mice were immunized via intranasal or intraperitoneal routes. The intranasal route was chosen due to its potential for inducing mucosal immunity, while the intraperitoneal route served as a control to evaluate systemic responses.
- ELISA assays were used to quantify antibody titers in serum and feces samples collected from immunized mice at various time points. The results demonstrated that the hybrid filaments induced high levels of both systemic and mucosal antibodies, including IgA, IgG1, IgG2a, and IgM.
- B cell crosslinking experiments using a 4G2 B cell hybridoma line confirmed that the hybrid filaments effectively engaged and activated B cells. Confocal microscopy revealed that the filaments crosslinked surface-bound B cell receptors (BCRs), leading to cellular activation and subsequent antibody production.
- Immunogold labeling of the hybrid filaments further verified that the DENV envelope protein was correctly displayed on the filament surface. This confirmed that the engineered constructs maintained the necessary conformation for antigen presentation and immune recognition.
- Statistical analysis using Student's t-test and Mann-Whitney tests revealed significant differences in antibody titers between groups receiving the hybrid filaments and control groups. The results indicated a robust and specific immune response to the DENV envelope protein.
- The study concluded that the hybrid flagellin-DENV envelope constructs represent a promising vaccine platform, capable of inducing strong mucosal and systemic immune responses. This approach could potentially improve the efficacy of dengue vaccines by targeting both initial infection sites and systemic immunity.
