### Bronchoalveolar Lavage (BAL) Analysis
Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed every 6 hours during the cross-circulation procedures. BAL fluid was collected and analyzed for cellular content, protein concentration, and inflammatory markers to assess lung injury and recovery over time.

### Blood Gas Analysis
Arterial blood gas analysis was conducted at regular intervals to monitor oxygenation, carbon dioxide levels, and acid-base balance. These measurements helped evaluate the effectiveness of cross-circulation in maintaining adequate gas exchange and physiological stability.

### Hemodynamic Monitoring
Hemodynamic parameters, including mean arterial pressure, cardiac output, and pulmonary artery pressures, were continuously monitored. This data was crucial for adjusting the cross-circulation settings to optimize support and ensure stable hemodynamics throughout the 36-hour period.

### Cytokine Profiling
Cytokine levels in both plasma and BAL fluid were measured using multiplex immunoassays. These profiles provided insights into the inflammatory response and helped track the progression of lung injury and recovery during cross-circulation.

### Exhaled Breath Condensate (EBC) Analysis
Exhaled breath condensate (EBC) was collected to analyze biomarkers indicative of lung inflammation and oxidative stress. EBC analysis complemented other measures by providing non-invasive, real-time data on the respiratory system's condition.

### Microbiological Assessment
Microbiological cultures were performed on BAL fluid and bronchial brushings to detect any microbial colonization or infection. This assessment was important for identifying potential sources of secondary infections that could complicate recovery.

### Exhaled Nitric Oxide (eNO) Measurement
Exhaled nitric oxide (eNO) levels were measured as a marker of airway inflammation. Changes in eNO over time provided additional insights into the inflammatory response and the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions.

### Metabolomic Analysis
Metabolomic profiling of plasma and BAL fluid was conducted to identify changes in metabolic pathways associated with lung injury and recovery. This comprehensive analysis helped elucidate the underlying biological processes and potential targets for intervention.

### Flow Cytometry
Flow cytometry was used to analyze immune cell populations in blood and BAL fluid. This technique provided detailed information on the types and activation states of immune cells, which is crucial for understanding the immune response to lung injury.

### RNA Sequencing
RNA sequencing was performed on lung tissue samples to identify gene expression changes over time. This high-throughput approach helped uncover molecular pathways involved in lung injury and recovery, providing a foundation for developing targeted therapies.

### Protein Expression Analysis
Protein expression levels of key markers were analyzed using Western blotting and ELISA. These assays provided quantitative data on the presence and abundance of specific proteins, which are critical for understanding the functional status of the lungs.

### Exosome Profiling
Exosomes were isolated from plasma and BAL fluid to analyze their content and function. Exosome profiling helped identify potential biomarkers and mediators of intercellular communication involved in lung injury and recovery.

### Mechanical Properties of Lung Tissue
Mechanical properties of lung tissue, such as compliance and resistance, were measured using specialized techniques. These measurements provided insights into the structural changes in the lungs during cross-circulation and recovery.

### Vascular Permeability Assessment
Vascular permeability was assessed by measuring the leakage of intravascular tracers into the lung parenchyma. This assessment helped evaluate the integrity of the alveolar-capillary barrier and the degree of pulmonary edema.

### Ultrasound Imaging
Ultrasound imaging was used to visualize lung tissue and assess changes in aeration, consolidation, and fluid accumulation. This non-invasive technique provided real-time images that were useful for monitoring the progression of injury and recovery.

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed to evaluate lung structure and function at various time points. MRI provided detailed images of the lungs, helping to assess tissue damage, inflammation, and recovery over the 36-hour period.

### Positron Emission Tomography (PET) Scanning
Positron emission tomography (PET) scanning was used to assess metabolic activity in the lungs. PET imaging helped identify areas of active inflammation and cellular metabolism, providing functional data complementary to structural imaging.

### Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)
Optical coherence tomography (OCT) was employed to obtain high-resolution images of the airway walls and alveolar structures. OCT provided detailed structural information that was useful for assessing the microanatomy of the lungs during cross-circulation.

### Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) Modeling
Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modeling was used to simulate airflow patterns and gas exchange in the lungs. CFD models helped optimize ventilatory strategies and predict the impact of different interventions on lung function.

### Mathematical Modeling of Lung Injury and Recovery
Mathematical models were developed to describe the dynamics of lung injury and recovery. These models integrated data from various measurements and provided a framework for understanding the complex processes involved in lung repair.

### Data Integration and Analysis
All collected data was integrated and analyzed using advanced statistical methods. This comprehensive analysis helped identify patterns, correlations, and trends that informed the interpretation of results and guided future research directions.
