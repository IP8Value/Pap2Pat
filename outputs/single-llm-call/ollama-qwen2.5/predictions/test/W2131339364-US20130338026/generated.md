- Our study reveals that overexpression of ATAD2 in human endometrial cancers results from 8q24 amplification and correlates with activation of the MYC pathway. This association is linked to poor prognosis and disease progression.

- We identified a strong correlation between high ATAD2 expression and unfavorable outcomes in clinically characterized datasets of endometrial cancer, confirming similar findings in breast, lung, and prostate cancers.

- Analysis of TCGA data shows that relationships between ATAD2, 8q24 amplification, and MYC pathway activation are consistent across glioblastoma, breast, and ovarian cancers, suggesting a broader relevance.

- In endometrial and breast cancer cell lines, dependency on MYC predicts dependency on ATAD2. This suggests ATAD2 as a potential therapeutic target in MYC-driven cancers, where direct targeting of MYC has been challenging.

- HDAC inhibitors like Trichostatin-A show promise in inhibiting growth in MYC-dependent endometrial and breast cancer cell lines, though their efficacy is lower than that of MYC or ATAD2 knockdown. This highlights the potential for developing more specific ATAD2 inhibitors.

- The findings underscore the importance of exploring ATAD2 as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target in endometrial cancer, addressing critical gaps in targeted therapeutics and prognosis for this under-studied disease.
