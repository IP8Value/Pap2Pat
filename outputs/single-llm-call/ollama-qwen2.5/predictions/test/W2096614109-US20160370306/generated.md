- Agarose serves as an effective carrier medium for SFX data collection from both soluble and membrane protein crystals. It reduces protein consumption by two orders of magnitude compared to GDVN liquid jets, delivering at a rate of only 160 nl/min.

- The agarose stream displays better stability in vacuum conditions but can also be used at ambient pressure. This flexibility makes it suitable for various experimental setups, including those where freezing needs to be avoided.

- Agarose has a lower X-ray scattering background compared to other viscous media like LCP, mineral oil-based grease, and petroleum jelly. This is particularly advantageous for protein crystals with large unit cells and medium-to-low resolution limits.

- The agarose delivery system is compatible with a wide range of crystallization conditions, including various PEGs and high-salt environments. It can be used for both soluble and membrane proteins, making it a versatile option for SFX experiments.

- Crystals can be embedded into the agarose medium post-crystallization, which is beneficial for proteins that are difficult to express or isolate in large quantities. This feature extends the applicability of SFX to a broader range of protein complexes.

- The low-cost and readily available nature of agarose makes it an accessible choice for researchers. Its ability to reduce sample consumption while maintaining high data quality opens new possibilities for studying scarce proteins and systems challenging to crystallize in large amounts.
