- This study presents a novel approach for early-stage colorectal cancer (CRC) detection using machine learning (ML) on low-depth whole-genome sequencing of circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA). The method achieved promising results with 85% sensitivity at 85% specificity in standard k-fold cross-validation.
- To address confounding variables, the study developed tailored cross-validation techniques like k-batch and balanced k-batch. These methods help mitigate technical and biological biases that can affect ML model performance and reproducibility across different settings.
- The approach analyzes cfDNA count profiles genome-wide rather than targeting specific regions for mutation detection. This allows unbiased discovery of both tumor-derived and non-tumor-derived signals, potentially capturing more comprehensive information relevant to cancer presence.
- Key limitations include retrospective sample collection, limited follow-up on CNVs in control samples, moderate coverage sequencing for tumor fraction estimation, and the need for prospective validation studies. The study also lacks assessment of specificity for differentiating CRC from other conditions.
- Overall, this work demonstrates promising initial results for early-stage CRC detection using a minimally invasive blood test. The approach is applicable to other cancers and diseases, enabling discovery of disease-specific signals through whole-genome analysis of cfDNA profiles.
