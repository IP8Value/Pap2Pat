- The study successfully demonstrated that TEMPO-oxidized nanocellulose (NFC) films can be activated using EDC/NHS chemistry to create a robust platform for antibody immobilization. This activation process enhanced the film's water resistance and provided a hydrophilic surface suitable for biomolecule conjugation.
- Carboxylation of NFC films increased their negative charge, improving water resistance and protein adsorption control. The carboxylated surfaces showed regenerability, allowing reversible protein binding through electrostatic interactions, which is beneficial for diagnostic applications requiring surface regeneration.
- EDC/NHS activation enabled covalent antibody attachment to the NFC films. Fluorescence imaging confirmed significantly higher antihuman IgG immobilization on activated NFC compared to unmodified NFC. XPS analysis further verified the presence of antibodies by detecting increased nitrogen content, indicating successful conjugation.
- The biological activity of immobilized antihuman IgG was demonstrated using QCM-D monitoring. Antihuman IgG adsorption was followed by hIgG capture, showing specific binding with minimal non-specific adsorption after blocking treatment. This validated the NFC film's potential for use in immunoassays and diagnostic applications.
- Inkjet printing of antihuman IgG on activated NFC films demonstrated large-scale antibody attachment. Fluorescence imaging under UV light and CLSM confirmed uniform and irreversible antibody attachment. AFM analysis showed a smooth, uniform layer covering the fibrillar structure, indicating successful printing for rapid diagnostic applications.
- The study concluded that activated NFC films offer a generic, sustainable biointerface platform suitable for antibody immobilization in diagnostic applications. The robustness, hydrophilicity, and non-toxic properties of these films make them promising candidates for developing rapid diagnostic tools with high specificity and sensitivity.
