- The study focuses on the reconstruction of a mid-Cenozoic era marsupial adeno-associated virus (AAV) using germline viral "fossils" found in macropodoid species. These endogenous viral elements, termed mAAV-EVE1, were identified and analyzed from a dataset of sixteen macropodoid species.
- The researchers used advanced bioinformatics tools to align and reconstruct the ancestral mAAV-EVE1 sequence, which dates back approximately 13.8 million years. This involved correcting frameshifts, nonsense codons, and indels based on homology with extant AAV proteins.
- Phylogenetic analyses were conducted using maximum likelihood and Bayesian methods to compare the mAAV-EVE1 sequences with a concatenated set of seven exonic gene segments from the same species. The results supported the inferred ancestral sequence's placement within the macropodoid lineage.
- A 3D structure model of the mAAV-EVE1 VP3 capsid protein was generated using the SWISS-MODEL online server, with the AAV8 VP3 structure as a reference template. This model allowed for detailed comparisons of variable regions (VRs) between mAAV-EVE1 and other AAV serotypes.
- The study's findings provide insights into the evolutionary history of AAVs and demonstrate the potential of using endogenous viral elements to reconstruct ancient viruses. The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 sequence and 3D model offer a valuable resource for understanding the structural and functional properties of ancestral AAVs.
