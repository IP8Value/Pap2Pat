- PS membranes were blended with varying AG loadings and characterized using FESEM, contact angle, and zeta potential techniques. FESEM images showed visible porous structures in AG-containing membranes, enhancing total porosity, flux, hydrophilicity, and negative surface charge compared to bare PS membranes.

- The amphiphilic nature of AG contributed to the enhanced properties of PS/AG membranes. Hydrophilic residues increased wettability, while carboxylic groups provided a strong negative surface charge, both crucial for reducing bacterial attachment and biofouling.

- FESEM images revealed significant reductions in bacterial colonization on PS/AG membranes for S. aureus, E. coli, K. pneumonia, and P. aeruginosa. The highest inhibition was observed at 3 wt. % and 7 wt. % AG loadings, with minimal bacterial adhesion even after overnight incubation.

- Control membranes (neat PS) showed some bacterial inhibition, possibly due to residual DMA solvent, a known toxic agent. However, this effect was much less pronounced compared to the strong anti-adhesive properties of PS/AG membranes.

- Despite high AG loadings, a few S. aureus colonies were observed, potentially due to bacterial resistance mechanisms or uneven AG distribution within the membrane matrix. These findings highlight the importance of homogeneous additive dispersion for optimal antibacterial performance.

- The synthesized PS/AG membranes demonstrated significant reductions in biofouling for both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, indicating their potential as durable materials for water treatment applications. Enhanced hydrophilicity and negative surface charge were key factors in preventing bacterial colonization on these novel membranes.
