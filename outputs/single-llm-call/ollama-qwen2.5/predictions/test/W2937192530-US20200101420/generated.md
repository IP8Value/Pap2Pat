# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of solar desalination systems, particularly to a novel apparatus and method for membrane distillation that enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of water purification processes. This invention specifically addresses the challenges associated with conventional solar desalination methods by integrating advanced materials and design principles.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Solar desalination is a critical technology in addressing global water scarcity, offering a sustainable solution to purify saline or contaminated water using renewable energy. However, traditional direct solar desalination approaches suffer from low efficiency due to heat loss and limited evaporation rates. Recent advancements have introduced the use of porous carbon nanomaterials for water evaporation, which offer significant benefits such as high surface area, light weight, and excellent thermal conductivity. Despite these advantages, existing solar evaporation systems still face limitations in terms of scalability, cost-effectiveness, and long-term stability.

Membrane distillation (MD) is another promising technique that separates salt from water by passing water vapor through a hydrophobic membrane while maintaining a temperature gradient. However, MD also has its drawbacks, including fouling, high energy consumption, and the need for precise control of operating conditions. These limitations have hindered the widespread adoption of both direct solar evaporation and MD in practical applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel solar membrane distillation apparatus designed to overcome the limitations of existing desalination technologies. The apparatus includes a solar distillation membrane, which is composed of graphitic foam with a superhydrophobic surface. This membrane is integrated into a water chamber and a vapor chamber, facilitating efficient water evaporation and vapor transport. A condenser is also provided to collect and cool the vapor, producing purified water.

The solar distillation membrane features a tubular or planar design, depending on the application requirements. The superhydrophobic surface of the membrane is achieved through the application of a hydrophobic composition, which enhances its performance in repelling water and preventing fouling. Additionally, the apparatus includes a pre-heating chamber to enhance the temperature of feed water before it enters the distillation process.

The graphitic foam used in the membrane has a controlled pore size and bulk porosity, optimized for efficient heat transfer and water vapor permeation. The hydrophobic composition is applied to the surface cells of the foam, creating a micro/nano texture that further improves its performance. The apparatus also includes a feed water heat exchange conduit and a metal pipe pre-heater to maximize energy utilization.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a membrane distillation apparatus designed for solar desalination. The core component of this apparatus is the solar distillation membrane, which is constructed from graphitic foam. This foam has a unique structure that enhances its thermal and mechanical properties, making it ideal for use in high-temperature and high-humidity environments.

The graphitic foam used in the invention is characterized by its three-dimensional network of interconnected pores. The foam can be prepared with varying pore sizes to optimize its performance for specific applications. For example, a pore size range of 10-50 μm has been found to be particularly effective for solar desalination. The bulk porosity of the foam is typically between 80% and 95%, ensuring high surface area and efficient heat transfer.

To further enhance the performance of the graphitic foam, a hydrophobic composition is applied to its surface. This composition can be made from various materials, such as fluorosilane molecules, which are known for their low surface energy and excellent water-repelling properties. The hydrophobic coating is typically applied by immersing the foam in a solution of 0.1 wt% fluorosilane in hexane overnight. After application, the foam is oven-dried at 100°C for one hour to ensure complete bonding of the silane molecules.

The resulting superhydrophobic surface has water contact angles exceeding 150°, which significantly reduces fouling and enhances the efficiency of the distillation process. The hydrophobic composition can also be applied using other methods, such as spray coating or dip-coating, depending on the specific requirements of the application.

The membrane distillation apparatus includes a housing with a light-transmitting wall to allow solar radiation to penetrate and heat the water chamber. The water chamber is designed to hold the feed water, which is heated by the incoming solar energy. The vapor generated in the water chamber passes through the distillation membrane into the vapor chamber.

The vapor chamber is maintained at a lower temperature than the water chamber, creating a temperature gradient that drives the evaporation process. A condenser is provided to cool and collect the vapor, producing purified water. The condenser can be constructed from various materials, such as metal or plastic, depending on the desired thermal conductivity and durability.

In addition to the basic components, the apparatus may include a pre-heating chamber to further enhance the temperature of the feed water before it enters the distillation process. This pre-heating chamber can be equipped with a solar water heater or a metal pipe pre-heater to maximize energy utilization. The use of nano/micro particles in the surface cells of the graphitic foam can also improve heat transfer and water vapor permeation.

The particle size range for these particles is typically between 10-50 nm, and their concentration can be controlled to optimize performance. The pore size of the graphitic foam is carefully controlled to ensure efficient water vapor transport, with a typical range of 10-50 μm. The surface porosity of the foam can also be adjusted to enhance its hydrophobic properties.

Hydrophobic coating materials such as fluorosilane can be applied in thicknesses ranging from 10-50 nm, depending on the specific requirements of the application. The operation of the membrane distillation apparatus involves several key steps. First, the feed water is introduced into the pre-heating chamber, where it is heated by solar energy or a metal pipe pre-heater. The pre-heated water then enters the water chamber, where it is further heated by the incoming solar radiation. As the water temperature rises, vapor is generated and passes through the distillation membrane into the vapor chamber.

In the vapor chamber, the temperature gradient between the water chamber and the condenser drives the evaporation process. The vapor is cooled and collected in the condenser, producing purified water. To ensure efficient operation, air is evacuated from the distillation chamber to reduce pressure and enhance vapor transport. This can be achieved through a vapor-driven air evacuation system, which utilizes the pressure difference between the vapor chamber and the ambient environment.

The method of solar desalination using the membrane distillation apparatus involves several steps. First, the graphitic foam baseline support is prepared by machining it with an electrical saw and drills to create tubes and chambers. The properties of the graphitic foam, such as its pore size and bulk porosity, are carefully controlled during this process.

An intermediate porous surface layer is then created by coating the foam with micro-carbon particles using a wood stick knife. After drying overnight, the coated foam is thermally cured at 300°C for one hour to ensure proper bonding of the particles. A hydrophilic membrane skin layer is fabricated and coated with graphite nanoparticles to enhance its thermal properties.

The membrane is then made superhydrophobic by depositing fluorosilane molecules via solution immersion in hexane overnight. The covalent bonding of silane molecules is achieved through oven drying at 100°C for one hour. For tubular solar distillation membranes, the heat transfer and water vapor permeation properties are optimized to ensure efficient operation.

The performance of the membrane distillation apparatus can be evaluated using various tests. Total reflectance and light absorption measurements are conducted using a portable reflectometer over the 330-2500 nm spectra region. The evaporation rate and efficiency are measured using a batch membrane vessel, and temperature profiles under different solar-thermal irradiations are compared. The permeate flux under concentrated solar-thermal irradiation is also measured to assess the desalination performance.

Overall, the present invention provides a highly efficient and cost-effective solution for solar desalination, addressing the limitations of existing technologies and offering significant improvements in water purification processes.
