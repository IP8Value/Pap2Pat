- **Problem Overview**: This study introduces a novel approach to dynamically curve the line-of-sight (LOS) input in air-to-air missile guidance systems using reinforcement meta-learning. The method, termed PN-LOSC (Proportional Navigation with Line of Sight Curvature), combines traditional proportional navigation (PN) with an optimized LOS curvature policy.

- **Methodology**: The PN-LOSC guidance law is formulated by integrating the line-of-sight curvature policy with proportional navigation. The policy is optimized using proximal policy optimization (PPO) with recurrent layers, enabling it to handle complex target maneuvers and radome refraction effects. The reward function encourages efficient LOS curvature and accurate terminal performance.

- **Experimental Setup**: Experiments are conducted in a 3-DOF air-to-air missile environment, comparing PN-LOSC against standard proportional navigation (PN) and augmented proportional navigation (APN). The tests involve 5000 episodes each, with varying target acceleration capabilities and drag models. The optimization process uses 90,000 episodes to refine the policy.

- **Results**: PN-LOSC demonstrates superior accuracy and efficiency compared to PN and APN, particularly in scenarios with high target maneuverability. It achieves lower miss distances and requires less control effort, indicating a more intelligent response to target maneuvers. This is especially evident in weave maneuvers, where PN-LOSC attenuates the response at longer ranges, conserving missile acceleration capability.

- **Radome Refraction Impact**: Tests without radome refraction show that PN-LOSC's performance advantage is partly due to its increased robustness to false target motion indications caused by look angle-dependent refraction. This suggests that the guidance system can better interpret and respond to perceived target accelerations, leading to more efficient control.

- **Conclusion**: The study concludes that PN-LOSC offers significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency over traditional guidance laws, especially in high-maneuver scenarios. The approach's ability to dynamically curve the LOS input enhances its robustness to radome refraction and improves response to target maneuvers. Future work will explore the application of this method in higher-fidelity 6-DOF models and other guidance laws.

- **Future Work**: Potential future research includes extending the PN-LOSC method to six degrees-of-freedom (6-DOF) models for more realistic simulations. Additionally, the approach could be adapted to satisfy look angle and impact angle constraints, as well as constraints on load and heating rate. Investigating the integration of LOS curvature with other guidance laws may also yield further performance improvements.
