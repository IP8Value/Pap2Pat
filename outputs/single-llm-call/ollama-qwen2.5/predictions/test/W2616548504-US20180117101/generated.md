- **Study Overview**: This study explored the potential of pHENOMMenal, a genetically modified L. reuteri 100–23 strain expressing AvPAL, to reduce blood phenylalanine (phe) levels in PKU mice. The engineered microbe was administered via freeze-dried cells mixed into chow, and its efficacy was assessed through blood phe levels and immune response.

- **In Vitro Results**: In vitro studies confirmed that pHENOMMenal retained functional AvPAL and viability. However, the therapeutic effect of the probiotic was dependent on the growth phase of harvested cells, with stationary phase cells failing to reduce plasma phe despite retaining enzyme activity.

- **Control Probiotic**: Feeding PKU mice a control probiotic without AvPAL did not result in a reduction of blood phe levels. This confirmed that the observed therapeutic effect was due to the presence and function of AvPAL in pHENOMMenal, rather than any non-specific effects of the probiotic.

- **Short-Term Treatment**: A small cohort of PKU mice treated with pHENOMMenal for seven days showed a significant reduction in blood phe levels. This reduction was maintained when treatment was extended to fourteen days, indicating that the probiotic could provide sustained therapeutic benefits over time.

- **Long-Term Colonization**: Fecal samples collected four months after cessation of pHENOMMenal treatment showed growth consistent with erythromycin-resistant L. reuteri 100–23, suggesting temporary colonization of the PKU mouse gut. However, no such growth was observed eight months post-treatment, indicating that long-term colonization did not occur.

- **Immune Response**: ELISA analysis revealed no significant increase in anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies in treated mice, suggesting that AvPAL remained confined to the gut and did not elicit a systemic immune response. This is crucial for the safety and sustained efficacy of the therapy.

- **Discussion on Efficacy**: Despite reducing blood phe levels, pHENOMMenal did not achieve levels comparable to healthy mice. Factors such as higher dietary phe intake in mice compared to humans and differences in gastrointestinal transit times may affect therapeutic outcomes. Further optimization of the probiotic, including higher cfu counts or different bacterial strains, could enhance its efficacy.

- **Future Directions**: The study highlights the potential of genetically engineered microbes to treat metabolic disorders like PKU. However, for human use, concerns about permanent colonization and plasmid-based techniques will need to be addressed. A chromosomal gene insertion in a strain with limited colonization potential is being considered for future clinical applications.
