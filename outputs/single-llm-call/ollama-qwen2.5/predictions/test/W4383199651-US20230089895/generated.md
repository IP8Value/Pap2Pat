The paper explores bid optimization in real-time bidding (RTB) by incorporating both prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk tendency. It introduces a novel framework called Risk-aware Reinforcement Learning Bidding (RRLB), which adapts the ad impression value based on these factors. The core idea is to adjust bid prices using a linear combination of predicted click-through rate (pCTR) and its associated uncertainty, guided by a risk tendency function.

The authors first establish a theoretical foundation for their approach, analyzing the relationship between prediction uncertainty and risk tendency. They propose an adaptive risk-aware bidding algorithm that considers both elements in determining optimal bid prices under budget constraints. Two methods are developed to determine risk tendency: one based on expert knowledge and another using self-supervised learning from historical data.

Experimental results on real datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of RRLB compared to existing strategies. The proposed method outperforms traditional approaches by achieving higher cumulative performance, particularly in scenarios with larger budgets. An ablation study confirms the importance of both prediction uncertainty and risk tendency, showing that modeling risk tendency is even more critical for optimal bid optimization.

A hyperparameter analysis reveals that RRLB is robust to variations in the slope parameter α, which controls the influence of risk tendency on bid prices. Optimal performance is achieved with a moderate value of α = 0.1. For constant uncertainty and risk tendency settings, the experiments show that both factors significantly impact performance, with optimal values leading to better click numbers and budget consumption.

The visualization of risk tendencies for different states aligns well between the expert knowledge-based and self-supervised learning approaches. Both methods exhibit similar trends, reflecting conservative bidding in resource-limited states and more aggressive bidding in resource-rich states. This consistency validates the effectiveness of the self-supervised learning algorithm in capturing rational bidder behavior.

In conclusion, this paper addresses a critical gap in RTB by integrating risk information into bid optimization. The proposed RRLB framework, which considers both prediction uncertainty and dynamic risk tendency, demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods. Future work could explore further refinements and extensions of the approach to handle more complex market environments and bidder behaviors.
