- The study focused on identifying differentially expressed genes between stable and unstable clones in genetically modified CHO cells. DNA microarray analysis revealed 524 probes out of 61,223 that showed differential expression (corrected P ≤0.1). Further filtering narrowed this down to 14 genes with logFCabs >0.8 and corrected P <0.05.
- RT-qPCR was used to validate the microarray results for the 14 selected genes. Two reference genes (Actb, Gapdh) were used for relative expression calculations. The samples were analyzed in triplicate at two different dilutions, with only those showing ≤0.5 ΔCq deviation between target and reference gene across dilutions being included.
- Principal component analysis was performed on the RT-qPCR data of five genes most differently expressed between stable and unstable clones that were not affected by MTX presence. The three most significant principal components were visualized in a 3D plot using MATLAB, revealing clear separation between stable and unstable samples based on gene expression profiles.
- K-nearest neighbor clustering was applied to systematically evaluate the separation of stable vs unstable clones in the 3D PCA space. The optimal number of clusters (2) was determined by identifying a distinctive change in slope on a curve plotting total sum of distances vs number of clusters. This unsupervised learning approach correctly classified samples into their respective groups based solely on gene expression data.
- The study identified five potential marker genes that can distinguish stable from unstable clones in CHO cell cultures for recombinant protein production: Fgfr2, BX842664/Hist1h3c, AC115880.11, E130203B14, and Ptpre. These genes showed consistent differential expression between stable/unstable groups across multiple analyses, suggesting they could serve as useful biomarkers for clone selection in bioprocessing applications.
- The supporting data sets, including raw microarray and RT-qPCR results, are available within the article and its supplementary files. This allows other researchers to reproduce the findings and potentially apply these marker genes to their own CHO cell line development workflows to improve clone stability and productivity over time.
- In conclusion, this study provides valuable insights into gene expression changes associated with CHO clone stability. The identified markers could help accelerate bioprocess development by enabling earlier identification of optimal clones for production-scale culture, ultimately improving efficiency and reducing costs in the manufacturing of therapeutic proteins using mammalian cell systems like CHO cells.
