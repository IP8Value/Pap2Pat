- The study focuses on identifying a limiting factor in N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) overproduction in Bacillus subtilis using dynamic pathway analysis. Researchers employed targeted metabolomics and dynamic simulations to analyze metabolic pathways, revealing an unexpected futile cycle involving GlcNAc-6-phosphate.
- A futile cycle was identified where GlcNAc-6-phosphate is rapidly converted to and from its precursor, consuming energy without net product formation. This cycle limits overall GlcNAc production efficiency by diverting resources away from the desired pathway.
- Dynamic simulations were used to model different scenarios, including feedback inhibition, enzyme limitation, futile cycles, and export limitations. The futile cycle scenario best matched experimental data, indicating it as the primary limiting factor in GlcNAc overproduction.
- To validate findings, researchers conducted dynamic labeling experiments using 13C-labeled glucose. Results confirmed the presence of a rapid intracellular flux between GlcNAc-6-phosphate and its precursor, supporting the existence of the futile cycle.
- The study demonstrates how integrating metabolomics with dynamic modeling can reveal hidden metabolic bottlenecks in engineered strains. Addressing this futile cycle could significantly enhance GlcNAc production efficiency in B. subtilis, offering insights for optimizing other metabolic pathways as well.
