- The study explores radio emission spectroscopy (RES) as a non-invasive method for real-time monitoring of low pressure plasmas in semiconductor fabrication. RES can detect pressure and power variations as low as ~2.5 %/mTorr and ~3.5 %/W, respectively.

- Experiments were conducted using two commercial plasma chambers: an Oxford Instruments Plasmalab 100 capacitively coupled reactor and a modified Lam EXELAN 2300 multiple frequency chamber. The RES signal was collected via a near B-field loop antenna placed close to the plasma viewport.

- A qualitative model explains how nonlinearities in plasmas driven by two power supplies can produce intermixing products through sheath non-linear diode mixing effects, leading to observable sidebands around principal RF supply sources.

- RES offers advantages over optical monitoring techniques due to its independence from viewport transparency. The study demonstrates the potential of RES for real-time process control and diagnostics in semiconductor manufacturing environments.
