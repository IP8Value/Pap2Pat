- We demonstrated optical neural networks (ONNs) using metasurfaces capable of recognizing binary and grayscale images with high accuracy. Despite limited depth, expressive power is augmented by millions of subwavelength meta-units per layer.

- The ONN's intrinsic 2D nature and diffraction-based signal processing make it suitable for object recognition and image-based computer vision tasks. Scalability can recognize many classes of monochrome and colorful objects illuminated coherently or incoherently.

- Expressive power can be increased through phase-amplitude metasurfaces, polarization and wavelength multiplexing, arrays of distinct metasurfaces per layer, and cascading layers. This expands the range of tasks and accuracy achievable.

- Unlike biological/digital neural networks, these ONNs do not strictly implement nonlinear activation functions. Future work could introduce nonlinear materials like semiconductors to realize this capability and expand task range/accuracy.

- Advanced sensors for future applications require minimal service, resilience to interference, high energy efficiency, and information security. ONNs excel in these areas by computing directly in the physical domain without power or digital representations.

- By condensing measurement, analog-to-digital conversion, and computing into a single passive device, ONNs can fundamentally reshape data collection/analysis as "edge" perception devices that provide physics-guaranteed security and protect subject privacy.
