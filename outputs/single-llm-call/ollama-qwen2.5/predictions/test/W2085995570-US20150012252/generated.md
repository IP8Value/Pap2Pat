The study outlines a novel weighted correction strategy to address GC bias in whole genome amplification (WGA). By assigning weights to sequencing reads based on their GC content, the method normalizes read counts across genomic windows. This approach enhances the accuracy of copy number variation (CNV) detection from low-coverage sequencing data, ensuring that variations in GC content do not confound the results.

To detect CNV breakpoints, a binary segmentation algorithm was developed and applied to the normalized data. The algorithm initializes by identifying candidate breakpoints based on significant differences in read counts between adjacent windows. It then iteratively merges segments with insignificant differences until all remaining breakpoints meet a predefined significance threshold. This method ensures that only genuine CNVs are identified.

Dynamic threshold determination was implemented to minimize false signals and misdiagnosis of CNVs. The thresholds for detecting deletions and duplications were set based on the quantiles of corrected read numbers (CRNs) within regions of similar GC content. This approach leverages the central-limit theorem to ensure that variations in WGA are accounted for, thereby improving the reliability of CNV calls.

The performance of the method was evaluated using seven test samples, including single-cell and peripheral blood samples. Sensitivity and specificity were calculated by comparing the detected CNVs with those confirmed by SNP array or whole genome sequencing (WGS). The results demonstrated that the method effectively identified large CNVs (>1 Mb) with high accuracy.

To further validate the method, in silico simulations were performed on the YH genome. Candidate genomic regions were selected and CNVs of varying sizes (500 kb to 5 Mb) were simulated. The method was then applied to detect these simulated CNVs, and sensitivity, specificity, and breakpoint precision were calculated. The results showed that the method achieved high overall sensitivity and specificity on the call level.

The study also performed a breakpoint precision analysis to assess the accuracy of predicted CNV breakpoints. The minimum distance between predicted and simulated breakpoints was calculated, revealing that the method can precisely identify CNV boundaries. This analysis underscores the method's potential for accurate and reliable CNV detection in low-coverage sequencing data.
