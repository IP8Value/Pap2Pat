- For precise laser-induced collagen shrinkage in redundant conjunctival tissue, a pulsed 1460-nm diode laser focused into a 10 × 1 mm² line was used. The pulse duration was kept under 0.5 seconds to confine thermal energy within the tissue's thickness, and multiple pulses were applied at a repetition rate of 1 Hz for effective heating and cooling cycles.
- A custom 1310-nm swept-source OCT system monitored structural changes during laser treatment, capturing B-scans at 50 frames per second. This setup allowed real-time visualization of the tissue's response to laser energy, ensuring optimal collagen shrinkage without overheating or damaging surrounding areas.
- Temperature variations were recorded using a thermal camera (TiS45, Fluke) during the laser application. The camera captured images at the targeted region, providing essential data for optimizing pulse parameters and ensuring the temperature remained within the desired range for effective tissue heating.
- A 3D-printed marker created parallel lines on the conjunctiva surface to define a specific region of interest. This method ensured consistent measurement areas across multiple experiments, allowing accurate comparison of shrinkage percentages before and after laser treatment using digital microscope images.
- Porcine eyes were used as samples in this study, with each eye's conjunctiva treated 6-8 times due to the limited area and beam size. Around 30 porcine eyes were utilized, providing a robust dataset for optimizing laser parameters and validating the effectiveness of the proposed treatment method.
- The angled forceps, designed to grasp the tissue and apply the line-focused laser, ensured proper illumination angle and working distance. However, they were not used in initial experiments to avoid introducing false shrinkage due to applied force, allowing accurate measurement of laser-induced effects alone.
- Electronic supplementary material includes additional experimental details, such as the relationship between output power and driving current, spectral characteristics, and temporal features of the 1460-nm diode laser. This information supports the design choices and parameter optimization for effective conjunctival tissue treatment.
