- The study reveals that the heparan sulfate (HS) chains of glypican-3 (GPC3) are crucial for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell migration and motility through interaction with hepatocyte growth factor (HGF). This finding highlights a novel role of HS in tumor progression, suggesting targeting HS as a potential therapeutic strategy.
- The research demonstrates that the HS20 antibody, which targets the HS chains of GPC3, effectively blocks HGF-induced cell migration, motility, and spheroid formation in vitro. These results indicate that HS20 can inhibit HGF signaling by preventing HGF binding to its receptor, c-Met.
- In vivo experiments show that HS20 treatment significantly reduces the growth of both Hep3B and HepG2 liver tumors in mice. This suggests that targeting the HS chains of GPC3 could be a promising approach for treating various liver malignancies, not limited to HCC.
- The study also reveals that HS20 inhibits tumor growth independently of Wnt signaling, as it shows efficacy against HepG2 cells with constitutively active β-catenin. This broadens the potential applications of HS20 and highlights its ability to target multiple signaling pathways involved in cancer.
- Overall, this research provides valuable insights into the role of HS chains in HCC pathogenesis and supports the development of HS-targeted therapies for liver cancers. The findings suggest that antibodies like HS20 could offer a more specific and effective treatment option compared to direct targeting of growth factors or receptors.
