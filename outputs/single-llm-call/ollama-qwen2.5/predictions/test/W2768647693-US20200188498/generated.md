- The study outlines a detailed method for creating allelic exchange mutations in E. chaffeensis using electroporation and antibiotic selection. This technique allows for precise genetic modifications to study gene function.

- Linear DNA fragments containing the desired mutation are introduced into host cell-free E. chaffeensis organisms via electroporation, followed by incubation with ISE6 cells to allow for transformation and clonal growth in selective media.

- Mutant cultures are confirmed through PCR analysis targeting genomic regions flanking the insertion site, as well as sequencing of genomic junctions and Southern blot hybridization using insertion-specific probes. This ensures accurate identification of mutants.

- RNA analysis by RT-PCR is used to verify loss or restoration of transcription from the targeted gene in wild type, mutant, and restored strains. Semi-quantitative RT-PCR assesses mRNA expression levels at different cycle numbers.

- Phenotypic characterization involves complementation assays in E. coli to test functional activity of the Ech_0379 antiporter protein. Growth curves are compared between transformed E. coli strains containing wild type, mutant, or restored Ech_0379 genes using a plate reader to measure OD600 over time.

- Electronic supplementary material provides additional details on primers used, molecular cloning procedures, and growth conditions for complementation assays in E. coli. This supports the experimental methods described in the main text.
