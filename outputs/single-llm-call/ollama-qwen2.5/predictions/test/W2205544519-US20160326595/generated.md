- This study delineates genomic features of early-stage prostate cancer in African American (AA) and Caucasian American (CA) men, highlighting distinct prevalence in PTEN deletions, LSAMP locus deletions, and ERG activation. Notably, LSAMP deletion is prevalent in AA CaP and associates with rapid disease progression.
- The study found significantly higher inter-chromosomal rearrangements frequency in AA compared to CA CaPs. This suggests potential differences in chromoplexy mechanisms or tumor cell selection between these populations that warrant further investigation.
- PTEN deletions were virtually absent in early-stage AA CaP, contrasting sharply with high frequencies in CA cases. This finding was validated by FISH analysis, reinforcing the distinct genomic landscape of AA prostate cancer.
- The research highlights the importance of ethnically-informed approaches for diagnostic, prognostic markers and therapies in prostate cancer. It underscores the need to understand underrepresented populations' cancer genomes for more tailored medical strategies.
