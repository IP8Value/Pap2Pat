- The study focuses on the electrochemical exfoliation of needle coke (CK) to produce graphene for potential use in lithium-ion batteries. Needle coke materials were pre-treated, separated, and subjected to an ECE process, followed by post-reaction centrifugation to isolate the exfoliated graphene.
- Raman spectroscopy, SEM, TEM, and XPS were used to characterize the parent needle coke and the resulting exfoliated graphene. The electrical conductivity of the samples was measured using a four-point resistivity probe on buckypapers produced by vacuum filtration.
- The yield of the coke-derived graphene (CK-1d) was quantified using UV-vis absorbance spectroscopy, with an extinction coefficient of 1293 L/g/m. Annealing of CK-1d at different temperatures improved its electrical conductivity and structural properties, making it more suitable for battery applications.
- The research highlights the potential of needle coke as a cost-effective precursor for graphene production, demonstrating that the ECE process can produce high-quality graphene with good yield and enhanced properties after annealing. This could have significant implications for the development of advanced materials for energy storage devices.
