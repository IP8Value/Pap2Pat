# Patent Application

## Title: Method for Size-Selective Electrochemical Sensing of Molecular Ions Using MOSCs

## Background

### Field of the Invention
The present invention relates to a method and apparatus for size-selective electrochemical sensing of molecular ions using metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs). Specifically, it involves incorporating MOSCs into mixed matrix membranes (MMMs) for use in ion-selective electrodes (ISEs).

### Description of Related Art
Conventional ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) have limitations in detecting large molecular ions due to the lack of size-selective ionophores. The development of new ionophores that can selectively recognize and bind specific molecular ions is crucial for advancing environmental monitoring, food quality control, and biomedical surveillance.

## Summary of the Invention
The present invention provides a method for size-selective electrochemical sensing of molecular ions using metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) incorporated into mixed matrix membranes (MMMs). The unique endo- and exo-cavities of MOSCs enable locally concentrated binding sites for large molecular cations, while expressing limited sensitivity towards smaller cations. This method is applicable in both conventional and solid-contact ion-selective electrodes (ISEs).

## Detailed Description

### Introduction to MOSCs
Metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) are architecturally unique molecules with endo- and exo-cavities that can serve as binding sites for large molecular cations. These cavities fit nicely with the dimensions of specific molecular ions, enabling size-selective potentiometric responses. The 1-Co MOSC, for example, has an endo-cavity that fits well with the methylene blue (MB+) ion and exo-cavities that can bind smaller ions.

### Incorporation of MOSCs into MMMs
To incorporate MOSCs into mixed matrix membranes (MMMs), a polymeric matrix is used as the base material. The 1-Co MOSC is uniformly dispersed within this matrix to form the MMM. This process ensures that the MOSC molecules are evenly distributed, providing consistent and reliable binding sites for target molecular ions.

### Fabrication of ISEs
ISEs can be fabricated using either conventional or solid-contact methods. For conventional ISEs, the MMM is cast onto a glass electrode with an internal reference solution. For solid-contact ISEs, the MMM is applied to a pyrolyzed SU8-derived carbon (SU8-C) electrode, which serves as a solid-state interface between the membrane and the measurement circuit.

### Potentiometric Response
The potentiometric response of the MOSC-based ISEs is characterized by near-Nernstian behavior. The 1-Co·MMM ISE exhibits a near-Nernstian response to MB+, with a detection limit of approximately 30 μM. The response time for concentrations above 30 μM is on the order of 1 second, while lower concentrations show a slower response time of 50-100 seconds.

### Size Selectivity
The size selectivity of the 1-Co MOSC ionophore is demonstrated by its limited sensitivity to smaller cations such as K+, Ag+, and NH4+. The ISE shows a substantially smaller response to these ions, indicating that their sizes are too small to allow effective binding with the 1-Co MOSC. This size selectivity is crucial for distinguishing between different molecular ions in complex matrices.

### Stability of MOSCs
The stability of both the MOSC and MB+ in the MMM is exceptional, showing no sign of leaching over extended periods. A simple leaching test was performed by storing the MMMs in deionized water for over one week. The solution in contact with the 1-Co·MMM remained clear, indicating that MB+ was captured by the MOSC. In contrast, the solution in contact with the MMM without 1-Co turned blue, confirming that MB+ had leached out.

### Application in Simulated Wastewater
To demonstrate practical applicability, a simulated wastewater experiment was conducted using water collected from the Fyris River. Controlled amounts of MB+ were added to the river water, and the response curve remained near-Nernstian. However, an apparent shift in the detection limit was observed due to the reducing environment caused by microorganisms in the river water. This shift was confirmed by a control experiment using deionized water, which showed a lower concentration of MB+ in the river water.

### Conclusion
The 1-Co MOSC molecule, when incorporated into a MMM, provides a new class of size-selective ionophores for potentiometric sensing of molecular ions. The unique endo- and exo-cavities of the MOSC enable locally concentrated binding sites for large molecular cations, while expressing limited sensitivity towards smaller cations. This method is applicable in both conventional and solid-contact ISEs, offering exciting new opportunities in environmental monitoring, food quality control, and biomedical surveillance.

## Claims
1. A method for size-selective electrochemical sensing of molecular ions using metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) incorporated into mixed matrix membranes (MMMs).
2. The method according to claim 1, wherein the MOSC is 1-Co.
3. The method according to claim 1, wherein the MMM is cast onto a glass electrode for conventional ISEs.
4. The method according to claim 1, wherein the MMM is applied to a pyrolyzed SU8-derived carbon (SU8-C) electrode for solid-contact ISEs.
5. The method according to claim 1, wherein the potentiometric response of the MOSC-based ISEs is near-Nernstian.
6. The method according to claim 1, wherein the size selectivity of the MOSC ionophore is demonstrated by its limited sensitivity to smaller cations such as K+, Ag+, and NH4+.
7. The method according to claim 1, wherein the stability of both the MOSC and MB+ in the MMM is exceptional, showing no sign of leaching over extended periods.
8. The method according to claim 1, wherein the ISEs are applicable in simulated wastewater experiments.
