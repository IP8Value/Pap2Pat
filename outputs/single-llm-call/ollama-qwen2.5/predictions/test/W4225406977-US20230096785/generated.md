- **Plant Material**: Cherry tomato "Regina" and "Yellow-mimi" were grown indoors and outdoors, respectively. A branch of "Yellow-mimi" was rooted in water for 7 days. Tobacco plants were grown indoors under LED illumination. These conditions ensured healthy plant growth for subsequent experiments.

- **Absorption of Salicylic Acid**: Roots of cherry tomato "Yellow-mimi" and tobacco were exposed to a salicylic acid solution to model the absorption of water-soluble substances. Hydrogel extraction was performed on leaves, and water-immersion extraction was used as a control method to assess the efficiency of hydrogel extraction.

- **Hydrogel Film Preparation**: A 1 mm-thick agar hydrogel film was prepared by mixing agar powder with water, heating it, and pouring it into a polytetrafluoroethylene framework on a glass slide. The mixture was allowed to gel at room temperature, resulting in a uniform 2 cm × 2 cm square hydrogel film.

- **Extraction of Leaf Component**: Cherry tomato leaves were ground in methanol, centrifuged, and the supernatant collected. This process ensured that leaf components were effectively extracted for fluorescence spectroscopy analysis. The extract was diluted to a standard volume for consistent measurements.

- **Bacterial Infection with Ralstonia Solanacearum**: Ralstonia solanacearum was obtained from infected cherry tomatoes and grown in a selective broth medium. A 10-fold dilution of the bacterial broth was used to infect hydroponically grown "Regina" plants for 14 days at 35°C. Leaf samples were collected on specific days for methanol extraction and fluorescence analysis.

- **Characterization**: Fluorescence spectra of leaf extracts and hydrogel films were measured using a spectrofluorometer. LC–MS/MS was used to analyze the components of infected cherry tomato leaves, identifying candidate compounds with blue fluorescence. A mixed standard solution containing these compounds was prepared for accurate identification.
