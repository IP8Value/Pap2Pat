- In this study, human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) alone failed to reverse hyperglycemia in a mouse model of streptozotocin-induced diabetes. This contrasts with previous reports and may be due to differences in cell populations, mouse strains, models, or experimental designs. The results highlight the importance of considering these variables when interpreting outcomes.
- hBMSCs overexpressing VEGF achieved sustained recovery from diabetes through efficient engraftment, differentiation into blood vessels and β-cells, and activation of endogenous insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathways. This led to β-cell regeneration and functional recovery, suggesting a promising therapeutic approach for insulin-dependent diabetes.
- hBMSCs expressing PDX1 also differentiated into β-cells but resulted in only transient recovery due to lower engraftment efficiency and lack of necessary host environment support. This highlights the importance of angiogenesis and supportive microenvironment for long-term survival and function of transplanted cells.
- The study provides evidence that hBMSCs-VEGF induce β-cell regeneration through activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT pathway, which improves cell survival, induces differentiation and proliferation, and modulates intra-islet angiogenesis. These findings offer new insights into the mechanisms underlying β-cell recovery after injury and suggest potential clinical applications for treating diabetes.
- In conclusion, this work demonstrates that hBMSCs expressing VEGF can induce sustained recovery from insulin-dependent diabetes primarily through endogenous β-cell regeneration mediated by activation of specific signaling pathways. The results highlight the therapeutic potential of genetically modified stem cells for diabetes treatment and provide a foundation for further preclinical and clinical studies.
- Overall, the study underscores the complex interplay between transplanted cells, host environment, and molecular signaling pathways in promoting β-cell recovery after injury. It also highlights the importance of optimizing cell-based therapies to achieve long-term functional outcomes in diabetes treatment. The findings have significant implications for developing novel strategies to regenerate pancreatic islets and improve glucose homeostasis in diabetic patients.
