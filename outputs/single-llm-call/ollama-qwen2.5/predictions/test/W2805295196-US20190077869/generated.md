- The study investigates the therapeutic potential of disrupting the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Two AML patient cohorts were analyzed to assess clinical significance. In vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that blocking PVR and PVRL2 enhances immune cell activation and cytotoxicity against AML cells.
- Flow cytometry was used to measure expression levels of PVR, PVRL2, and TIGIT on AML cell lines and primary patient samples. Cytotoxicity assays evaluated the effect of blocking antibodies targeting these molecules alone or in combination with a BiTE antibody construct.
- Granzyme B ELISA measured immune activation following cytotoxicity assays. CRISPR/Cas9 was used to generate PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout MV4-11 cells for further functional studies. An AML xenograft mouse model evaluated the impact of knocking out PVR and PVRL2 on disease progression.
- RT-qPCR analyzed gene expression in cohort A samples. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS and GraphPad Prism, employing tests such as Mann-Whitney U-test, paired t-test, Pearson correlation, and Cox regression for survival analysis. All experimental procedures complied with ethical guidelines and regulations.
- The study found that both PVR and PVRL2 are highly expressed in AML cells and associated with poor patient outcomes. Blocking these molecules enhanced immune cell-mediated killing of AML targets in vitro and improved survival in xenograft models, suggesting potential for therapeutic applications targeting this pathway in AML treatment.
- Materials and methods sections provide detailed protocols for experiments including cell culture conditions, flow cytometry staining, cytotoxicity assays, Granzyme B ELISA, CRISPR/Cas9 knockout generation, animal model procedures, RT-qPCR analysis, and statistical methods. Supplemental tables and figures offer additional data supporting the main findings.
- Overall, the study provides preclinical evidence for targeting the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis as a promising immunotherapy approach in AML. The results suggest that disrupting this pathway could enhance immune responses against AML cells and improve patient outcomes when combined with other therapies like BiTE antibody constructs.
