- The document outlines a computational framework named Aldy for genotyping complex genes using high-throughput sequencing (HTS) data. It addresses challenges in copy number estimation and star allele prediction through integer programming models. Despite theoretical limitations due to short read lengths, Aldy efficiently solves many practical instances using advanced solvers like Gurobi or SCIP.
- The framework consists of three main steps: Copy Number Estimation Problem (CNEP), Major Star Allele Prediction Problem (MSAIP), and a final refinement step called GRP. Each step builds on the previous one to progressively refine the genotype call, ultimately producing a set of minor star alleles as output.
- CNEP aims to estimate gene copy numbers by solving an integer program that minimizes read count discrepancies across genomic regions. It masks identically matching regions between genes and pseudogenes to avoid misalignment issues. Multiple optimal solutions may exist due to short reads, but Aldy tries all and selects the best.
- MSAIP uses the CNEP solution to predict major star alleles by minimizing differences between observed and expected mutation frequencies. Gene-disrupting mutations are prioritized over neutral ones. If no valid solution exists, it allows novel major allele descriptions with high penalties.
- The final GRP step refines minor star alleles from MSAIP solutions by optimizing a quadratic objective that balances missing/added mutations and consistency with estimated copy numbers. It ensures all gene-disrupting mutations are assigned to major alleles while preventing over-calling of variations.
- Computational complexity is discussed, noting that the problems are generally NP-hard but often solvable in practice. Practical limitations due to short read lengths and lack of strand information are highlighted. These can sometimes prevent precise resolution of certain genotypes or structural rearrangements, especially involving distant mutations.
- Data sources used for evaluation include 1000 Genomes, Platinum Genomes, and HiSeq X CEPH sequencing data. PGRNseq data is available upon request. Aldy v1.0 relies on existing allele databases to detect fusions and categorize mutations as gene-disrupting or neutral.
- The document concludes by emphasizing that most limitations stem from inherent constraints of HTS technology rather than the Aldy framework itself. These could potentially be addressed with additional information like barcodes, which can be easily incorporated into Aldy's pipeline without altering its core mechanisms.
