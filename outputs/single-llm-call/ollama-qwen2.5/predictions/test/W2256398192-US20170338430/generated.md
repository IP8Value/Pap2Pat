# Patent Application for Substantial Improvement of Perovskite Solar Cells Stability by Pinhole-Free Hole Transport Layer with Doping Engineering

## Background

### Field of the Invention

This invention relates to the field of photovoltaic devices, specifically to perovskite solar cells. The primary focus is on enhancing the stability and efficiency of these cells through the use of a pinhole-free hole transport layer (HTL) with doping engineering.

### Description of Related Art

Perovskite solar cells have shown significant promise due to their high efficiency and low cost. However, one major challenge is their instability, particularly when exposed to environmental factors such as moisture and oxygen. Traditional HTLs often suffer from pinholes, which can lead to degradation of the perovskite layer. This invention addresses this issue by using a triple-layer HTL consisting of n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD.

## Summary of the Invention

### Objectives

The primary objective of this invention is to improve the stability and efficiency of perovskite solar cells. This is achieved by fabricating a pinhole-free HTL using vacuum evaporation techniques and doping engineering. The triple-layer structure ensures efficient hole extraction while providing a barrier against environmental degradation.

### Technical Solutions

1. **Fabrication of Pinhole-Free HTL**: A triple-layer HTL consisting of n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD is fabricated using vacuum evaporation techniques.
2. **Optimization of Doping Concentration**: The doping concentration for the n-type layer is optimized to match the HOMO level of spiro-OMeTAD with the valence band maximum of perovskite, enhancing hole extraction efficiency.
3. **Enhanced Stability**: The amorphous nature and high stability of vacuum-evaporated spiro-OMeTAD serve as a gas barrier layer, protecting the underlying perovskite material from environmental degradation.

## Detailed Description

### Fabrication Process

1. **Substrate Preparation**:
   - Fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrates are cleaned and patterned.
   - A 100 nm thick compact TiO2 layer is deposited using spray pyrolysis and post-annealed at 480°C.

2. **Perovskite Layer**:
   - A perovskite solution of MAI and PbCl2 in N,N-dimethylformamide is prepared.
   - The solution is spin-coated on the TiO2 layer and thermally annealed at 150°C for 45 minutes.

3. **HTL Fabrication**:
   - **N-type Doped Layer**: 5 mg of DMC powder is placed in a glass ampule and heated to ~100°C under vacuum conditions. The DMC vapor pressure is optimized to 1.0 × 10–5 Torr, and the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD is evaporated at a rate of 0.5 Å/s.
   - **Intrinsic Layer**: Undoped spiro-OMeTAD is deposited in the same chamber.
   - **P-type Doped Layer**: The sample is moved to a second chamber for co-evaporation with F4-TCNQ and spiro-OMeTAD at deposition rates of 0.1 Å/s and 0.5 Å/s, respectively.

### Characterization Techniques

1. **Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)**:
   - AFM measurements confirm the absence of pinholes in the vacuum-evaporated HTL layers.
2. **X-ray Diffractometer (XRD)**:
   - XRD is used to verify the crystalline structure of the perovskite layer.
3. **Ultraviolet Photoelectron Spectroscopy (UPS)**:
   - UPS measurements are performed to determine the energy levels of the HTL layers, ensuring optimal alignment with the perovskite valence band.

### Solar Cell Performance

1. **Current-Voltage (I-V) Measurements**:
   - I-V characteristics are measured under standard AM1.5G illumination using a solar simulator and source meter.
2. **Stability Testing**:
   - The stability of the devices is evaluated by measuring their performance over an extended period in both ambient and controlled environments.

## Results

### Performance Metrics

- **Efficiency**: Devices with the optimized triple-layer HTL show significant improvements in efficiency compared to reference samples with spin-coated HTLs.
- **Stability**: The vacuum-evaporated HTL devices exhibit enhanced stability, with minimal degradation over 840 hours of testing.

### Mechanism of Improvement

The amorphous nature and high stability of the vacuum-deposited spiro-OMeTAD layers serve as an effective barrier against environmental factors. Additionally, the optimized doping concentration ensures efficient hole extraction, leading to higher open-circuit voltage (Voc) and fill factor (FF).

## Conclusion

This invention provides a robust method for fabricating perovskite solar cells with improved stability and efficiency. The use of a pinhole-free triple-layer HTL with doping engineering addresses the critical issues of degradation and performance loss, making these devices more viable for practical applications.

## Claims

1. A method for fabricating a hole transport layer (HTL) for perovskite solar cells, comprising:
   - Depositing an n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer by vacuum evaporation with optimized DMC vapor pressure.
   - Depositing an intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer in the same chamber.
   - Depositing a p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer using co-evaporation of F4-TCNQ and spiro-OMeTAD.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein the DMC vapor pressure is optimized to match the HOMO level of spiro-OMeTAD with the valence band maximum of perovskite.

3. A perovskite solar cell comprising:
   - A substrate.
   - A compact TiO2 layer deposited on the substrate.
   - A perovskite layer deposited on the TiO2 layer.
   - A triple-layer HTL comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers.

4. The solar cell of claim 3, wherein the HTL is fabricated using vacuum evaporation techniques to ensure a pinhole-free structure.

5. The solar cell of claim 3, further comprising a metal contact layer deposited on the HTL.
