- **Single Impurity Model**: The single impurity model describes the behavior of a semiconductor nanocrystal with a single dopant. This hydrogenic approximation assumes that the dopant behaves like a point charge in a dielectric medium. While useful for dilute doping, it fails to capture the complexity of multiple closely spaced impurities, leading to inaccuracies in predicting spectral shifts and band structures.

- **Point Charge Assumption**: In bulk semiconductors, impurities can be treated as point charges due to their large separation compared to their effective Bohr radii. However, in nanocrystals, this assumption breaks down because the impurities are much closer together on an atomic scale. This necessitates more sophisticated models that account for the spatial distribution and interactions of impurities.

- **Phenomenological Model**: A phenomenological model is introduced to address the limitations of the point charge approximation. This model parameterizes the properties of randomly distributed impurity levels using a tight-binding approach. It incorporates parameters like binding energy, overlap energy, and length scale to describe the formation of mixed states and delocalization of impurities within the nanocrystal.

- **Impurity Binding Energy**: The impurity binding energy (ε) is a key parameter in the phenomenological model. It represents the energy required to remove an electron from the impurity state. In bulk materials, this energy converges to a well-defined value, but in nanocrystals, it can vary significantly due to confinement effects and the specific chemistry of the system.

- **Short-Range Mixing Energy**: The short-range mixing energy (γε) describes the overlap between impurity states at close distances. This parameter is crucial for understanding how impurities interact and form mixed states within the nanocrystal. In bulk materials, γε is typically equal to ε, but in nanocrystals, it can differ due to the smaller spatial scales involved.

- **Band Tailing**: Band tailing refers to the distortion of the electronic band structure caused by disorder, such as impurity doping. In nanocrystals, this can lead to a broadening and shifting of the density of states into the energy gap. This effect is particularly important for understanding red shifts in the optical spectra and must be carefully distinguished from genuine dopant-induced changes.

- **Monte Carlo Simulations**: Monte Carlo simulations are used to model the effects of disorder on the electronic structure of nanocrystals. By randomly displacing atoms and calculating the density of states (DOS), these simulations provide insights into how band tailing influences the spectral properties. The results show that smaller nanocrystals exhibit larger shifts, consistent with experimental observations.
