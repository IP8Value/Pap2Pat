- The study demonstrates for the first time that DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes are over-expressed in unmedicated schizophrenic peripheral blood leukocytes (PBL) compared to healthy individuals using microarray and quantitative real-time PCR methods.

- A key strength of this research is analyzing blood samples from drug-naive, non-medicated patients, which rules out medication as a potential cause for the observed gene expression changes. This provides clearer insight into genetic markers associated with schizophrenia itself.

- DRD2 belongs to the D2 subclass of dopamine receptors and has been linked to schizophrenia in previous brain region studies. The increased DRD2 expression in PBL aligns with reported central nervous system findings, suggesting peripheral blood cells may reflect similar disease-related changes.

- Kir2.3 is an inwardly rectifying potassium channel that can be activated by dopamine through D2 receptors. Its upregulation alongside DRD2 further supports the dopamine hypothesis of schizophrenia and highlights potential functional interactions between these genes in immune cells.

- The study confirms microarray findings using two independent real-time PCR methods (SybrGreen and TaqMan), providing strong validation for DRD2 and Kir2.3 as candidate peripheral genetic markers for schizophrenia diagnosis, though additional samples will be needed to fully establish their clinical utility.
