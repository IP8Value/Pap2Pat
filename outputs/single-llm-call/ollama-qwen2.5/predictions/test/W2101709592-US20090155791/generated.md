### Detailed Description

#### Background of the Invention

The field of epigenetics has seen significant advancements, particularly in understanding DNA methylation's role in development and diseases such as cancer. Various techniques have been developed to analyze DNA methylation, with sodium bisulfite modification followed by PCR amplification being a common approach. Two primary primer types are used: methylation-specific PCR (MSP) primers and methylation-independent PCR (MIP) primers. MSP primers target CpG sites within the primer binding site, while MIP primers amplify regions regardless of methylation status for post-PCR analysis.

#### Summary of the Invention

The invention provides a method to accurately determine DNA methylation status using optimized MIP primers and PCR conditions that minimize bias towards unmethylated sequences. The key innovation lies in the design of MIP primers, which include limited CpG residues and are used in conjunction with carefully optimized annealing temperatures to ensure proportional amplification of both methylated and unmethylated templates.

##### Methylation Independent PCR (MIP) Primers

The design of MIP primers is crucial for accurate methylation analysis. The inclusion of limited CpG residues in the primer sequences, along with optimization of annealing temperatures, helps correct for PCR bias. This ensures that both methylated and unmethylated templates are amplified proportionally.

##### Optimized Annealing Temperatures

Optimizing the annealing temperature is essential to balance the binding of primers to methylated and unmethylated templates. Lower temperatures favor amplification of both templates, while higher temperatures favor only methylated templates. An intermediate temperature can correct for PCR bias by balancing the amplification of both templates.

##### Primer Design Recommendations

To design effective MIP primers:
1. Include a limited number of CpG dinucleotides (usually one) in the primer sequence.
2. Place CpGs away from the 3' end to avoid selectivity for methylated templates.
3. Ensure the salt-adjusted melting temperature (Tm) is around 65°C.
4. Include Ts originating from non-CpG Cs at or near the 3' end.
5. Evaluate primers for secondary structure and primer dimer formation.

##### Testing Primer Sets

To test the extent of bias, PCR amplify a range of dilutions of fully methylated DNA into unmethylated DNA. Estimate the proportion of methylated to unmethylated sequences using melting analysis. Choose an annealing temperature that allows correction for PCR bias. Test primers with non-bisulfite treated DNA to ensure they do not amplify unconverted templates.

##### Applications

The invention is applicable in various methylation analysis techniques, including bisulfite sequencing, restriction digestion, DHPLC, single-strand conformation analysis, melting curve analysis, and high-resolution melting (HRM). The optimized MIP primers and PCR conditions improve the accuracy and reliability of methylation studies.

The invention provides a robust method for accurate DNA methylation analysis using optimized MIP primers and PCR conditions. ### Claims

1. A method for analyzing DNA methylation status comprising:
   - Providing a sample containing DNA.
   - Treating the DNA with sodium bisulfite to convert unmethylated cytosines to uracil while leaving methylated cytosines unchanged.
   - Amplifying the treated DNA using MIP primers, wherein the forward primer has the sequence `5'-GGG TAT TGA GTA CCG TAC-3'` and the reverse primer has the sequence `5'-GAA CTC GAG CCC TAA CGT-3'`.
   - Optimizing the annealing temperature to balance amplification of methylated and unmethylated templates.
   - Analyzing the amplified DNA using a methylation analysis technique.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein the methylation analysis technique is selected from bisulfite sequencing, restriction digestion, DHPLC, single-strand conformation analysis, melting curve analysis, or high-resolution melting (HRM).

3. A primer set for methylation-independent PCR comprising:
   - A forward primer with the sequence `5'-GGG TAT TGA GTA CCG TAC-3'`.
   - A reverse primer with the sequence `5'-GAA CTC GAG CCC TAA CGT-3'`.

4. The primer set of claim 3, wherein the primers include limited CpG residues away from the 3' end to ensure proportional amplification of methylated and unmethylated templates.

5. A method for optimizing PCR conditions in methylation analysis comprising:
   - Providing a sample containing DNA.
   - Treating the DNA with sodium bisulfite.
   - Amplifying the treated DNA using MIP primers.
   - Testing the primer set with non-bisulfite treated DNA to ensure it does not amplify unconverted templates.
   - Optimizing the annealing temperature to correct for PCR bias.
