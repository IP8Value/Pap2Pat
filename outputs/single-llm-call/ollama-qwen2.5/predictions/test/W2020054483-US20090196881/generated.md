- The study aimed to develop a reliable challenge model for assessing SVCV vaccine efficacy. Goldfish were initially tested but showed little susceptibility, likely due to their size. Koi, from a domestic US stock, proved more suitable as they reliably succumbed to both immersion and injection challenges with SVCVnc.
- A 2-week cold stress period was initially included in the challenge protocol. However, experiments revealed no significant difference in mortalities between koi challenged after slow acclimation versus rapid transfer to colder water. The final model adopted a rapid cold stressor on the day of virus challenge using koi as hosts.
- Four vaccine trials tested the pSGnc construct 28 days post-vaccination. Mortalities were significantly lower in pSGnc vaccinated fish compared to controls, with RPS values ranging from 50-88%. The vaccine provided consistent protection despite varying conditions like host age and virus dose. Survivors in pSGnc groups had low or no detectable virus.
- This study marks the first successful testing of an SVCV DNA vaccine in koi using a North American isolate. The pSGnc construct, containing the G-gene from Ia genogroup, demonstrated the highest efficacy reported for any SVCV DNA vaccine. Future work will test lower doses and cross-protection against other strains. The authors acknowledge collaborators who provided fish, rearing advice, and veterinary consultations.
- The development of this efficacious SVCV DNA vaccine offers a promising prophylactic tool to combat the spread of SVCV in North America. While factors like fish size or challenge dose did not clearly correlate with RPS variation across trials, unidentified differences between trial conditions may have influenced outcomes. Overall, the pSGnc construct shows significant potential as a protective measure against SVCV infection.
