- The study demonstrates a novel type of stretchable lithium-ion battery based on kirigami patterns. These batteries can be stretched and flexed while maintaining their performance, making them ideal for wearable devices like smart watches.

- Researchers fabricated multilayer battery structures using standard materials and processes, then applied kirigami cutting and folding techniques to create stretchability. Three different kirigami patterns were tested - Cut-N-Twist, Cut-N-Bend, and Cut-N-Fold.

- Electrochemical testing showed the kirigami batteries maintained stable performance even under repeated stretching cycles. The batteries demonstrated good rate capability and cycle life similar to conventional rigid batteries.

- A key finding was that the kirigami design allowed competing mechanisms of "crack growth" versus "plastic rolling" at the cut locations, which enabled stretchability without damaging the battery components or connections.

- The researchers integrated a kirigami lithium-ion battery into an off-the-shelf Samsung Gear 2 smart watch to demonstrate its real-world potential. The flexible battery powered all functions of the device and performed comparably to the original rigid battery in terms of standby and call times.

- Thermal testing showed the thin, stretchable kirigami battery remained at ambient temperature during discharge, unlike a conventional bulky battery that heated up significantly. This cooling advantage could improve safety and longevity for wearable devices.

- The study concludes that kirigami-based batteries have great potential to enable new form factors in wearables by providing scalable, flexible power sources using standard manufacturing processes. The concept may also be applicable to other stretchable electronic systems beyond just batteries.
