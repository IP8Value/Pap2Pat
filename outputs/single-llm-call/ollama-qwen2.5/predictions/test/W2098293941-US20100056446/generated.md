- We identified a specific substructure within the Na/K-ATPase α1 subunit that interacts with and inhibits Src. This led to the development of pNaKtide, a novel peptide inhibitor designed to target the Na/K-ATPase/Src receptor complex.

- pNaKtide effectively inhibited ouabain-induced activation of ERK1/2 in LLC-PK1 cells and primary cardiac myocytes, demonstrating its potential as an ouabain antagonist. It also blocked ouabain-induced hypertrophy in neonatal cardiac myocytes, highlighting its therapeutic relevance.

- The specificity of pNaKtide was further confirmed by showing that it did not inhibit IGF-induced ERK1/2 activation, unlike the generic Src inhibitor PP2. This suggests that pNaKtide selectively targets the Na/K-ATPase/Src complex in the plasma membrane.

- Limitations include the need to identify key amino acid residues in NaKtide and understand its binding mechanism on Src. Additionally, AP-NaKtide's potential effects on intracellular processes like vesicle trafficking remain unexplored. Finally, pNaKtide's efficacy as an ouabain antagonist in intact animals or isolated organs has yet to be evaluated.

- Despite these limitations, the development of pNaKtide represents a significant advancement in understanding and potentially modulating Na/K-ATPase-Src signaling pathways, which could have important implications for treating conditions associated with ouabain-induced cellular responses.
