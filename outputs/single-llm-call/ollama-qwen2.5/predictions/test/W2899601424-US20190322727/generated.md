- The study aimed to develop and characterize polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies against MCR-1, a critical enzyme conferring resistance to colistin, an antibiotic of last resort. Antibodies were generated using recombinant cMCR-1 as the immunogen in rabbits and mice.
- Polyclonal antibodies (pAbs) were produced by immunizing rabbits with cMCR-1. The pAb showed strong binding to cMCR-1 in ELISA, with a limit of detection (LOD) of 0.2 ng/mL. The antibody was affinity-purified and used as the capture antibody in sandwich ELISAs.
- Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were generated from immunized mice using hybridoma technology. Several mAbs were identified that bound specifically to cMCR-1, with MCR-1-7 showing the strongest reactivity and being selected for further characterization.
- The specificity of the pAb and MCR-1-7 mAb was confirmed by Western blot analysis. Both antibodies recognized a single band corresponding to the expected size of cMCR-1, demonstrating their specificity. The mAb isotype was determined to be IgG1.
- A sandwich ELISA was developed using the rabbit pAb as the capture antibody and MCR-1-7 mAb for detection. The LOD for recombinant cMCR-1 in this assay was 0.5 pg/mL, with high sensitivity and specificity. The intra-day and inter-day precision were also evaluated.
- The ELISA was applied to detect MCR-1 in meat samples inoculated with E. coli strains carrying the mcr-1 gene. Ground beef, chicken, and pork samples were spiked with low levels of bacteria (10 cfu/25 g) and enriched overnight. After lysis, MCR-1 could be reliably detected by ELISA.
- This study successfully developed highly sensitive antibodies and an ELISA for the detection of MCR-1 in complex matrices like food products. The assay has potential applications in monitoring colistin resistance in agricultural settings to prevent its spread into clinical environments.
