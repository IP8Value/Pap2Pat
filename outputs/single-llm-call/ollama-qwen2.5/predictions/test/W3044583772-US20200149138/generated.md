### Summary

- In the thin surface layer of sub-rapid-solidified low-carbon steel (0.023 wt% C), we confirmed the presence of upper bainite, acicular ferrite, and lath martensite. B1 upper bainite formed before lath martensite in the 0-50 µm depth area, while B2 upper bainite formed after acicular ferrite in the 50-200 µm depth area, with B2 being shorter than B1.

- The unexpectedly high nanohardness of martensite in this low-carbon steel was measured at 9.87 ± 0.51 GPa, comparable to steels with twenty times the carbon content. This is attributed to the fine laths of martensite, only 128 nm wide. The nanohardness for upper bainite and acicular ferrite were 4.18 ± 0.39 GPa and 2.93 ± 0.30 GPa, respectively.

- Martensite at the surface exhibited a yield strength of 2378 ± 123 MPa, significantly higher than upper bainite (1007 ± 94 MPa) or acicular ferrite (706 ± 72 MPa). This high yield strength provides exceptional abrasion resistance, unmatched by other low-carbon steels without additional heat treatment.
