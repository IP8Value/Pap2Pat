- The study introduces an efficient method called Anchored ChromPET for detecting BCR-ABL1 chromosomal translocations in CML patients. This approach uses paired-end sequencing of genomic DNA libraries with barcoded adapters and captures the major break cluster region in the BCR gene.
- Compared to karyotyping or FISH, Anchored ChromPET is highly sensitive, has high resolution, and can be automated for high-throughput analysis. The method identified exact breakpoints in BCR and ABL1 genes in two cell lines and two CML patients.
- Detection of DNA junctional fragments using this approach is comparable in sensitivity to RNA fusion transcripts by RT-PCR under controlled conditions. However, DNA fragments are more stable and detectable at higher sensitivity from formalin-fixed cells or serum samples.
- Anchored ChromPET provides a DNA-based biomarker for translocations that can be used for patient follow-up. The method has potential applications beyond BCR-ABL1 detection to identify other chromosomal translocations currently identified by cytogenetics.
- The authors note that this approach reduces sequencing costs through multiplexing and targeted capture, making it more feasible than whole-genome sequencing for identifying cancer-specific recombination biomarkers in clinical settings.
