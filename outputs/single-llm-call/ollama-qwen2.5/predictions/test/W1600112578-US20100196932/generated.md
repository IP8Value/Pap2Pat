- The study utilized a yeast-based system expressing amyloid-β protein (Aβ) fusions to investigate Aβ oligomerization and its effects on cellular function. Aβ42 peptides were polymerized in vitro and analyzed by Thioflavin T fluorescence and immunoblotting.

- Yeast strains expressing various Aβ fusion proteins were grown under controlled conditions, and their growth phenotypes were compared on agar plates with different media compositions to assess viability and pigmentation changes.

- Cell lysates from yeast cultures were prepared for immunoblot analysis using specific antibodies against Aβ, Sup35p's RF domain, and Hsp104. SDS-PAGE and agarose gel electrophoresis were used to resolve protein complexes and detect SDS-stable oligomers.

- Immunoprecipitation experiments were conducted to isolate and characterize protein interactions involving the HA-tagged Aβ fusions. Agarose beads coated with anti-HA antibodies captured target proteins, which were then analyzed by immunoblotting for co-precipitated Hsp104 and RF domain-containing proteins.
