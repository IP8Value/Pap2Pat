- In this study, researchers identified a novel antibody Fc variant (P238D) that selectively enhances binding affinity to FcγRIIb over both FcγRIIaR131 and FcγRIIaH131 through comprehensive mutagenesis. Crystal structural analysis revealed P238D induced significant changes in the recognition interface of Fc-FcγRIIb, allowing precise discrimination between receptors.
- Further optimization led to the development of V12 variant with 200-fold higher affinity for FcγRIIb compared to IgG1. Importantly, V12 maintained comparable pharmacokinetics and storage stability to wild-type IgG1, addressing concerns around reduced melting temperature.
- The study demonstrated that antibodies with increased affinity for FcγRIIaR131 have the potential to induce platelet activation/aggregation and rapid clearance from plasma in individuals with certain genotypes. This highlights both safety risks and pharmacokinetic implications of enhancing binding to specific FcγRs.
- Using an agonist anti-CD137 antibody, researchers showed that selective enhancement of FcγRIIb affinity can significantly boost therapeutic activity compared to wild-type IgG1. This supports the potential for applying engineered Fc variants with improved FcγRIIb binding in various therapeutic contexts, particularly cancer immunotherapy.
- The study concludes that the identified Fc variant (V12) represents a promising tool for enhancing antibody therapies by selectively targeting FcγRIIb. Its ability to avoid unwanted interactions with other Fc receptors while maintaining favorable properties positions it as a valuable candidate for further development and clinical evaluation.
