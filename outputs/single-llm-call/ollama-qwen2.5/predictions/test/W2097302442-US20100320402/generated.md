# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to high-dose Grid radiotherapy, particularly a novel method and system for delivering three-dimensional (3D) dose lattice radiotherapy. This advanced technique aims to improve the therapeutic outcome of radiation therapy by concentrating high doses within the tumor volume while sparing surrounding normal tissues.

## BACKGROUND

High-dose Grid radiotherapy, also known as spatially-fractionated Grid radiotherapy (SFGRT), has been a less frequently used treatment modality since its inception in the early 1930s. In its early applications, two-dimensional (2D) Grid fields were employed, typically with orthovoltage beams to create spatially alternated dose distributions. These grids were composed of shields with arrays of openings ranging from 0.5 to 1.5 cm in size. The technique was primarily used for treating advanced bulky tumors, as it allowed higher dose delivery with acceptable skin toxicity due to the alternating high and low-dose regions.

Significant and dramatic tumor regressions have been observed and reported in patients treated with GRID therapy, even though this approach does not aim to treat the entire tumor volume uniformly. Recent research in radiobiology has provided new insights into the bystander effect within the irradiated tumor volume and the abscopal effect on distant tumors or metastatic lesions. These findings suggest that high-dose Grid radiotherapy can induce rapid and higher rates of tumor cell apoptosis, particularly in bulky and hypoxic tumors.

However, traditional 2D GRID treatments still expose a considerable volume of normal tissue to high doses, often resulting in significant toxicity. The highest dose regions are frequently superficial and occur outside the tumor target, leading to suboptimal therapeutic outcomes. Therefore, there is a need for an advanced technique that can deliver high doses more precisely within the tumor while minimizing damage to surrounding tissues.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### 3D Dose Lattice Radiotherapy (LRT)

The present invention provides a method and system for delivering three-dimensional (3D) dose lattice radiotherapy. This novel approach aims to improve the therapeutic outcome of radiation therapy by creating multiple localized high-dose islands within the tumor volume, separated by low-dose regions. The 3D LRT technique can be delivered using various modalities, including intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT), volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT), and robotic radiosurgery systems.

### Method of Delivery

1. **Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT) and Volumetric Modulated Arc Therapy (VMAT):** These advanced techniques can be used to create highly heterogeneous dose distributions within the tumor volume. By modulating the intensity of radiation beams, IMRT and VMAT can deliver high doses to specific regions while minimizing exposure to surrounding normal tissues. The 3D LRT plan is generated using commercial treatment planning systems with no software or hardware modifications.

2. **Robotic Radiosurgery Systems:** Robotic radiosurgery systems, such as the CyberKnife, can deliver multiple focused non-coplanar beams to create a highly heterogeneous dose distribution within the tumor. Each beam is precisely directed to a specific high-dose vertex within the tumor volume, resulting in rapid dose fall-off between vertices.

3. **Heavy Charged Particle Beams:** Heavy charged particle beams, such as proton and carbon ion beams, can also be used to deliver 3D LRT. These particles have a well-defined range and can deposit their energy precisely at the targeted depth within the tumor. A spot-scanning technique is employed to deliver the Bragg Peak to each high-dose vertex, ensuring that the dose distribution is highly localized.

### Biological Effects

The peak-to-valley dose characteristic of 3D LRT is essential for achieving the therapeutic effect. The high-dose islands within the tumor volume are expected to trigger significant biological responses, including increased apoptosis and bystander effects. These effects can enhance the overall efficacy of radiation therapy while minimizing toxicity to surrounding normal tissues.

### Dosimetric Advantages

Compared to traditional 2D GRID therapy, 3D LRT offers several dosimetric advantages:
- **Target Coverage:** The 3D LRT technique ensures that all high-dose islands are within the tumor target, resulting in better coverage and higher maximum doses to the tumor.
- **Normal Tissue Sparing:** The rapid dose fall-off outside the high-dose islands significantly reduces exposure to surrounding normal tissues. This is particularly beneficial for critical organs such as the spinal cord and esophagus.
- **Peak-Valley Dose Differentiation:** 3D LRT creates a pronounced peak-to-valley dose profile, with similar low-dose regions but higher maximum doses within the tumor volume.

### Clinical Applications

The 3D LRT technique can be applied to various types of tumors, including lung and pancreatic masses. In both clinical examples, highly heterogeneous dose distributions were observed, with minimal exposure to surrounding normal tissues. The deliverability and accuracy of the 3D LRT plans have been verified using dosimetric measurements, demonstrating good agreement between planned and measured doses.

### Future Directions

Further biological and dosimetric exploration is needed to determine the optimal configuration of a 3D dose lattice. This includes investigating the number and locations of high-dose vertices, as well as the size of focused beams. The potential for using heavy charged particle beams and spot-scanning techniques in 3D LRT also warrants further investigation.

In conclusion, the 3D dose lattice radiotherapy technique offers a promising approach to improving the therapeutic outcome of radiation therapy while minimizing toxicity. With ongoing research and clinical trials, it is hoped that this novel method will become a standard treatment option for patients with advanced and bulky tumors.
