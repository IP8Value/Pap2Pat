- Reductide's cellular uptake and reduction depend on the cell's redox state, making it a useful probe for assessing relative redox changes in living cells. This property could be leveraged for targeted drug delivery or imaging applications.

- The development of FAM fluorescence from reductide is influenced by both cellular uptake and intramolecular disulfide bond reduction. Redox-dependent differences are more pronounced when reductide incubation follows redox modification, suggesting that cellular uptake is a critical step in signal development.

- Low doses of H2O2 (200-400 μM) stimulate an antioxidative response in human fibroblasts, increasing FAM fluorescence. Higher doses (600 μM+) cause oxidative stress and reduce FAM signal, indicating a dose-dependent cellular redox response.

- MAP uptake is not driven by concentration gradients but may be influenced by electrical potential differences across the cell membrane. Redox state affects membrane potential through regulation of ion channels, suggesting that this could play a role in MAP internalization.

- Intracellular redox state is dynamic and varies with cellular differentiation, density, and proliferation. Redox changes are linked to cell cycle progression and play key roles in various diseases, including cardiomyopathy, neurodegeneration, and cancer. Redox-based drug delivery has potential therapeutic applications.
