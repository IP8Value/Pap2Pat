# Patent Application for Synergistic Inhibition of AMPA Receptors Using Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

## Field of the Invention
The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating neurological disorders, particularly epilepsy. More specifically, it involves the synergistic use of perampanel and decanoic acid to inhibit AMPA receptors and reduce seizure activity.

## Background of the Invention
Epilepsy is a common neurological disorder characterized by recurrent seizures. Current treatments often involve antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), but many patients experience inadequate control or significant side effects. Perampanel, an AED, targets AMPA receptors, which are crucial in neuronal excitability. Decanoic acid, found in medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs), has also shown anti-seizure properties. However, the combination of these two compounds and their synergistic effect on AMPA receptors have not been fully explored.

## Summary of the Invention
The invention provides a method for treating epilepsy by administering a pharmaceutical composition comprising perampanel and decanoic acid. The combination is effective in reducing seizure activity through synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, potentially allowing for lower doses of each compound while maintaining or enhancing therapeutic efficacy and reducing side effects.

## Detailed Description of the Invention

### Small Molecule Inhibitors
Perampanel is a small molecule inhibitor that acts as an antagonist at AMPA glutamate receptors. It has been widely used to treat partial-onset seizures in adults and children. Decanoic acid, a medium-chain fatty acid, is found in MCTs and has shown promise in reducing seizure frequency.

### Synergistic Effect
Studies have demonstrated that the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid results in a synergistic effect on AMPA receptor inhibition. This synergy was observed both in vitro and in ex vivo models of epileptiform activity, where the combined treatment significantly reduced seizure-like activity compared to individual treatments.

### Mechanism of Action
Perampanel directly inhibits AMPA receptors by binding to the glutamate-binding site, reducing neuronal excitability. Decanoic acid, while not a direct receptor antagonist, modulates neuronal activity and enhances the inhibitory effect of perampanel. The exact mechanism of this enhancement is still under investigation but may involve changes in membrane potential or intracellular signaling pathways.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions
The pharmaceutical composition can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, and topical. It typically includes a therapeutically effective amount of perampanel and decanoic acid, along with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients and carriers.

### Methods of Use
The method involves administering the pharmaceutical composition to a patient in need thereof. The doses can be adjusted based on the severity of the condition and the response to treatment. For example, a typical dose might include 2-4 mg/day of perampanel and 100-300 μmol/L of decanoic acid.

### Clinical Potential
The synergistic effect of perampanel and decanoic acid offers significant clinical potential. It may allow for lower doses of each compound, reducing the risk of side effects while maintaining or enhancing therapeutic efficacy. This is particularly important in patient populations where high doses of AEDs can lead to cognitive impairment and other adverse reactions.

### Examples

#### Example 1: In Vitro Synergy
In vitro studies using Xenopus oocytes expressing AMPA receptors showed that perampanel alone inhibited receptor currents with an IC50 value of 6.2 μmol/L. When decanoic acid was added at a concentration of 100 μmol/L, the IC50 for perampanel decreased to 2.1 μmol/L, indicating a synergistic effect.

#### Example 2: Ex Vivo Seizure Model
Ex vivo hippocampal slice preparations from rats were used to induce seizure-like activity with pentylenetetrazol (PTZ). Treatment with decanoic acid alone reduced seizure activity by 30%, while perampanel alone at 100 nmol/L had no significant effect. The combination of both compounds, however, significantly reduced seizure activity by 65%.

#### Example 3: Human Brain Slice Seizure Activity
Epileptiform activity was induced in resected human neocortical tissue using a proconvulsant artificial cerebrospinal fluid. Treatment with decanoic acid (100 μmol/L) or perampanel (0.5 μmol/L) alone had minimal effects. However, the combination of both compounds virtually abolished epileptiform activity, significantly reducing the area power of local field potential activity.

### Conclusion
The invention provides a novel and effective method for treating epilepsy by combining perampanel and decanoic acid. The synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors offers improved therapeutic outcomes with reduced side effects, making it a promising approach in the management of epileptic disorders.

## Claims
1. A pharmaceutical composition comprising:
   - (a) a therapeutically effective amount of perampanel;
   - (b) a therapeutically effective amount of decanoic acid; and
   - (c) pharmaceutically acceptable excipients.
2. The pharmaceutical composition of claim 1, wherein the perampanel is present in an amount of 2-4 mg/day.
3. The pharmaceutical composition of claim 1, wherein the decanoic acid is present in an amount of 100-300 μmol/L.
4. A method for treating epilepsy comprising administering to a patient in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of perampanel and decanoic acid.
5. The method of claim 4, wherein the perampanel is administered at a dose of 2-4 mg/day.
6. The method of claim 4, wherein the decanoic acid is administered at a concentration of 100-300 μmol/L.
7. The method of claim 4, wherein the combination of perampanel and decanoic acid results in a synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors.
