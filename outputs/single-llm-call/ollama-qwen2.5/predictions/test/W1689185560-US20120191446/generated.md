- The paper presents a formalization of PEGs in Coq and development of TRX - a formally verified parser interpreter for PEGs. This allows writing PEG grammars with semantic actions in Coq and extracting certified parsers from them that provide total correctness guarantees, including termination on all inputs.
- A key challenge addressed is ensuring termination of the generated parsers. The authors present an extrinsic approach using deep embedding of parsing expressions and a certified algorithm to verify well-formedness of PEGs, justifying non-structural recursion as well-founded.
- To improve practical applicability beyond Coq experts, the authors sketch plans for bootstrapping TRX into a generic parser generator that can take textual grammar descriptions. This would result in a standalone tool not requiring Coq knowledge to use.
- Other future work identified includes implementing packrat parsing for linear time guarantees, supporting left-recursive grammars via elimination or extended memoization, and adding error reporting. The goal is to make TRX more usable on real-world problems while maintaining formal verification benefits.
- Overall this represents an important step towards certified parsers that can provide quality and security guarantees for software systems. By emphasizing practicality through case studies and future plans, the authors aim to bring these theoretical results into broader use by developers outside the theorem proving community.
