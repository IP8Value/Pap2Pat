- The study introduces VCO, a vapor derived from Litsea cubeba seed oils, as a potential anti-lung cancer agent. This volatile oil compound targets Akt deactivation in NSCLC cells, inducing apoptosis and blocking cell cycle progression by impairing cyclin D1 activity. VCO's unique property of being inhalable offers a novel delivery method for lung cancer treatment.

- Constitutive activation of Akt is crucial for NSCLC survival and proliferation. VCO effectively inhibits both Ser473 and Thr308 phosphorylation of Akt, leading to deactivation. This early event triggers downstream effects like Bad dephosphorylation, Bax aggregation on mitochondrial membranes, cytochrome c release, and caspase activation, culminating in irreversible DNA fragmentation and cell death.

- VCO's interference with NSCLC proliferation through Akt deactivation is another significant finding. By reducing Mdm2 Ser166 phosphorylation, p53 overexpression is promoted, which upregulates p21. This increased p21 associates more with cyclin D1, inhibiting G1 to S phase progression and effectively halting cell cycle advancement in these cancer cells.

- The study highlights VCO's potential advantages over existing NSCLC treatments. Unlike conventional chemotherapies that often cause toxicity and have limited effectiveness, VCO targets Akt, a key player in cancer survival and proliferation. Its inhalable nature offers direct lung delivery with minimal side effects, making it a promising therapeutic option for managing this prevalent and challenging form of adenocarcinoma.

- In conclusion, VCO demonstrates dual benefits by inducing apoptosis and inhibiting cell cycle progression in NSCLC cells through Akt deactivation. This unique approach, combined with its inhalable form, presents an encouraging possibility for effective lung cancer treatment, particularly for NSCLC where chemotherapy often fails. Further research is warranted to explore the full therapeutic potential of VCO in clinical settings.
