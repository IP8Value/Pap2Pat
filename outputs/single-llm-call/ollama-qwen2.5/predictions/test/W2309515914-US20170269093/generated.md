- **Introduction**: Lpd (lipoma preferred partner) plays a critical role in cellular processes such as membrane protrusion and invasion. This study investigates the mechanisms by which Lpd influences these processes in breast cancer cells, with a focus on its interaction with actin dynamics and signaling pathways.

- **Methods Overview**: The study employs a combination of in vitro assays, animal models, and clinical data analysis to comprehensively evaluate the role of Lpd in breast cancer. Techniques include membrane protrusion assays, barbed-end assays, micropipette manipulation, and intravital imaging.

- **Clinical Data Analysis**: Oncomine and ROCK databases were utilized to analyze Lpd mRNA expression levels in breast cancer datasets. Kaplan-Meier plots were generated to assess the correlation between Lpd expression and patient survival, with statistical significance determined using log-rank tests.

- **Tissue Microarrays (TMAs)**: TMAs of invasive breast tumor samples from 312 patients were constructed and stained with a polyclonal rabbit Lpd antibody. The intensity of Lpd in the cytoplasm and membrane was scored using the weighted histoscore method, and survival curves were generated to evaluate clinical outcomes.

- **Animal Models**: Orthotopic mouse models were used to assess tumor growth and spontaneous metastasis formation following injection of breast cancer cells into the mammary fat pad. Intravital imaging was performed to observe cell motility and protrusion dynamics in real-time within the tumor microenvironment.

- **Zebrafish Assay**: A zebrafish tumor cell dissemination assay was conducted to evaluate the role of Lpd in early metastatic events. This model allowed for the visualization and quantification of cancer cell dissemination and invasion into surrounding tissues.

- **Statistical Analysis**: Data were analyzed using ANOVA with post hoc tests or Student's t-test, as appropriate. Kaplan-Meier plots and Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to evaluate survival outcomes and the impact of Lpd expression on patient prognosis.

- **Study Approval**: Ethical approval for the use of TMAs was obtained from the NHS Research Ethics Committee. Animal experiments were approved by MIT's Department of Comparative Medicine and the Committee on Animal Care. Zebrafish studies were approved by the Northern Stockholm Experimental Animal Ethical Committee.

- **Conclusion**: The study provides insights into the role of Lpd in breast cancer progression, highlighting its importance in cellular invasion and metastasis. These findings have potential implications for therapeutic strategies targeting Lpd to improve clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients.
