- The study introduces a novel pumpless organ-on-chip device using threads to create fluid flow, enabling culture of Caco-2 and A549 cells under controlled shear stress. This approach simplifies long-term cell culture while maintaining physiological relevance.
- Caco-2 cells were cultured in the device at low shear stress (0.02 dyn/cm²), forming a confluent monolayer with tight junctions, Muc2 expression, and vacuoles characteristic of intestinal epithelium over 5 days.
- A549 lung cancer cells were cultured under higher shear stress (up to 0.75 dyn/cm²) in the Hele-Shaw device, inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) as evidenced by increased vimentin expression and decreased cytokeratin levels along the channel.
- The pumpless design allows for easy medium exchange and long-term culture, with daily refilling of the inlet reservoir. Immunofluorescence staining validated cell morphology, differentiation markers, and tight junction formation in Caco-2 monolayers.
- Confocal microscopy revealed consistent cell height across sections, confirming uniform monolayer formation. The Hele-Shaw device enabled spatially controlled EMT induction along the channel, demonstrating its utility for studying shear stress effects on cells.
- Statistical analysis showed significant differences in vimentin and cytokeratin expression between sections of the Hele-Shaw device. This pumpless organ-on-chip platform offers a simple yet powerful tool for studying intestinal and lung epithelial cell behavior under controlled fluidic conditions.
