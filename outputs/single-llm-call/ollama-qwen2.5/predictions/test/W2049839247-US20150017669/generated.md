- In this study, a multibiomarker panel was developed using MALDI-TOF MS analysis of human serum N-glycans to screen for ovarian cancer. The system showed improved classification performance compared to CA-125 in both the training and blind test sets.

- The relationship between P values from ANOVA and AUC (area under ROC curve) was systematically investigated, revealing a linear correlation that can guide biomarker selection thresholds. This approach helps identify effective biomarkers beyond just statistical significance.

- To avoid overfitting, an independent set of 60 blind samples was used to validate the screening system's predictive power. The system maintained high sensitivity and specificity across different score thresholds, demonstrating robust classification capability.

- Five out of fifteen MS peak biomarkers could not be assigned as N-glycans but were included due to consistent detection and quantitative stability in both training and test sets. This highlights the importance of clinical value over strict molecular identification in developing practical diagnostic systems.

- The screening system successfully identified all 8 recurrent ovarian cancer patients, suggesting potential for monitoring disease recurrence or prognosis. However, larger cohorts are needed to confirm these findings.

- When combined with CA-125 testing, the glycan analysis showed improved sensitivity (~15%) but lacked complete data for definitive conclusions. Future studies should explore this combination in larger sample sets.

- The developed screening system demonstrated high accuracy, particularly for early-stage ovarian cancer detection. This highlights the potential of MALDI-TOF MS-based serum glycan analysis as a valuable tool for ovarian cancer screening and management.
