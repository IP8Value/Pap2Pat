- The study investigates the role of TMT proteins in sugar transport within Arabidopsis plants. It explores how overexpression of TMT1 affects various physiological processes and subcellular sugar distribution.

- Expression of TMT1 under the 35S promoter led to increased vacuolar sugar uptake, as demonstrated by patch-clamp analyses of isolated mesophyll vacuoles from transgenic plants compared to wild-type.

- Nonaqueous fractionation revealed that overexpression of TMT1 resulted in higher vacuolar glucose and fructose levels, while cytosolic sugar concentrations were decreased.

- Gas exchange measurements showed that 35S:TMT1 plants exhibited enhanced CO2 assimilation rates during the day compared to wild-type, indicating improved photosynthetic performance.

- Analysis of phloem exudates revealed increased sugar export from source leaves in 35S:TMT1 plants, suggesting a role for TMT1 in facilitating long-distance sugar transport within the plant.

- Seed analysis demonstrated that overexpression of TMT1 led to increased seed weight and higher lipid content without affecting protein levels, indicating improved carbon allocation to seeds.

- The study concludes that TMT1 plays a crucial role in regulating intracellular sugar partitioning and transport, which has significant implications for plant growth, development, and productivity.
