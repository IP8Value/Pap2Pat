# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and device for improving the accuracy and reliability of arterial pressure measurement in critically ill patients. Specifically, it addresses the challenges associated with the dynamic characteristics of fluid-filled tubing used in invasive monitoring systems, which can distort the measured arterial pressure signal and affect derived indices such as systolic blood pressure (sAP), pulse pressure variation (PPV), and left ventricular contractility (dP/dt).

## SUMMARY

Arterial pressure measurement is a critical component of patient monitoring in intensive care units (ICUs). Current practice involves invasive methods using fluid-filled tubing connected to a catheter inserted into the radial artery. This method allows for continuous and real-time monitoring of arterial pressure, which is essential for assessing the hemodynamic status of patients and guiding therapeutic decisions. The advantages of this approach include its high temporal resolution and the ability to derive dynamic indices such as PPV and dP/dt.

However, current practice has several limitations. One significant issue is the distortion introduced by the hydraulic connection between the catheter and the pressure transducer. This hydraulic connection can alter the arterial pressure waveform due to its inherent dynamic characteristics, leading to inaccuracies in measured values. The transfer function of the hydraulic connection is influenced by factors such as the length and type of tubing used, which can cause various distortions.

These distortions can significantly impact derived indices. For example, overdamping (Ov) can lead to an underestimation of systolic pressure and overestimation of diastolic pressure, while attenuation (At) can result in a general reduction in the amplitude of the arterial waveform. Clinical staff often struggle to detect these defects through visual inspection alone, leading to potential misinterpretation of patient data.

Several proposed solutions have been suggested to address these issues, including the use of fast flushes and harmonic methods to characterize the dynamic parameters of the hydraulic connection. However, these methods have their own limitations. Fast flushes can introduce artefacts and are not always feasible in all clinical settings. Harmonic methods, while more precise, require complex equipment and may not be suitable for continuous monitoring.

There is a clear need for an improved method and device to enhance the quality of measured arterial pressure signals. The aim of the present invention is to provide a method and device that can continuously monitor and correct for distortions introduced by the hydraulic connection. This method involves determining the dynamic parameters of the hydraulic connection, analyzing the frequential content of the incident signal, and detecting the situation of the hydraulic connection.

The method includes several steps: first, it determines the dynamic parameters of the hydraulic connection using techniques such as fast flushes or harmonic analysis. Next, it analyzes the frequential content of the incident arterial pressure signal to identify any distortions. The method then detects the specific situation of the hydraulic connection, which can be categorized into three main scenarios: (a) normal operation, (b) overdamping, and (c) attenuation.

In each situation, the method applies a mechanical action on the tubing to excite the hydraulic connection. This action is synchronized with the diastolic phase of the cardiac cycle to minimize patient discomfort. Alternative mechanical actions can be used depending on the specific requirements of the clinical setting. The device also includes mechanisms for detecting decoupling between the catheter and the transducer, which can be a significant source of artefacts.

The invention further incorporates applanation tonometry to measure pulse wave velocity (PWV) propagation. This measurement is compared with a reference value to detect any decoupling or other issues. If an anomaly is detected, an alarm is generated to alert clinical staff. The device is designed for continuous monitoring and correction, integrating seamlessly into conventional measuring chains.

The device consists of a monitoring and correcting module, an actuator module, and a remote processing system. The monitoring and correcting module includes two processors: the first processor handles signal processing, while the second processor manages corrective actions. The modules communicate wirelessly or through wired connections, ensuring reliable data transfer. The device is powered by a rechargeable battery and housed in a compact casing that can be easily sterilized.

The invention offers several advantages over existing solutions. It is easy to install and use, requiring minimal training for hospital staff. The device operates transparently, allowing clinical staff to continue their usual monitoring practices without disruption. Additionally, the device can be neutralized when not needed, ensuring it does not interfere with other medical devices.

The first processor in the monitoring and correcting module performs signal processing tasks such as characterizing the dynamic parameters of the hydraulic connection and analyzing the frequential content of the incident arterial pressure signal. The second processor manages corrective actions based on the detected situation of the hydraulic connection. Redundant treatments are implemented to ensure robust performance, and the actuator module applies mechanical actions to the tubing to excite the hydraulic connection.

The remote processing system processes the measured signal and derives various indices such as sAP, PPV, and dP/dt. It displays arterial pressure plottings and records measurements for later review. The system also performs quality follow-up and calibration to ensure accurate and reliable data.

In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive solution for improving the accuracy of arterial pressure measurement by continuously monitoring and correcting for distortions introduced by the hydraulic connection. This method and device are designed to be integrated into existing clinical workflows, offering significant benefits in terms of patient safety and care quality.
