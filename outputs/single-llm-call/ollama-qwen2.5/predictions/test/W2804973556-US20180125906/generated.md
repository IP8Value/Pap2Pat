- **Introduction to AP1 and Its Significance**: An acid polysaccharide fraction (AP1) derived from ginseng extract has been identified for its potent cardioprotective effects. This study is the first to explore how AP1 protects cardiomyocytes from hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) injury by maintaining mitochondrial function and reducing oxidative stress, highlighting its potential as a novel therapeutic agent for ischemic heart disease (IHD).

- **Mechanistic Insights**: The research demonstrates that AP1 activates the RISK pathway through the upregulation of glucocorticoid receptors (GR) and estrogen receptors (ER), leading to increased phosphorylation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) and subsequent production of nitric oxide (NO). This cascade helps neutralize reactive oxygen species (ROS) and prevent mitochondrial dysfunction.

- **Mitochondrial Protection**: AP1 stabilizes the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), enhances ATP generation, and reduces the release of cytochrome c. These effects inhibit the activation of caspase-3 and -9, thereby preventing mitochondria-dependent apoptosis and maintaining cellular energy homeostasis during H/R injury.

- **Receptor-Mediated Activation**: The cardioprotective effect of AP1 is receptor-mediated, with GR and ER playing crucial roles in activating the RISK pathway. This activation leads to increased NO production, which has antioxidant properties and helps prevent mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening, further protecting mitochondria from oxidative damage.

- **Potential for IHD Prevention**: The study suggests that AP1 could be a promising candidate for preventing IHD, particularly myocardial ischemic reperfusion injury. Its ability to modulate mitochondrial function and activate protective signaling pathways makes it a valuable natural supplement for cardiovascular health, warranting further investigation in animal models and clinical settings.

- **Future Directions**: While the current study provides strong evidence of AP1's cardioprotective effects in vitro, future research will explore its efficacy in animal models of ischemic reperfusion injury. Additionally, knockdown experiments and knockout mouse models will be used to confirm the roles of GR, ER, PI3K/Akt, and ERK in this protective mechanism, providing a more comprehensive understanding of AP1's therapeutic potential.

- **Conclusion**: This study is the first to identify an acidic polysaccharide fraction (AP1) from ginseng as a potent cardioprotective agent. By activating the RISK/eNOS/NO pathway and modulating mitochondrial function, AP1 demonstrates significant potential for preventing ischemic heart disease. Further research will elucidate its mechanisms and therapeutic applications in more complex biological systems.
