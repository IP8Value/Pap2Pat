- BHK-WNV cells were transfected to produce HCVbp or HCVwt particles. Three days post-transfection, electron microscopy analysis showed virus-like particles in cells and sucrose gradient fractions. Immuno-EM with patient serum confirmed the presence of HCVwt, indicating successful particle production.
- To assess infectivity, concentrated HCVbp was incubated with Huh-7.5 cells for 2 hours, followed by NS5A protein expression analysis 48 hours later using immunofluorescence. Positive cells were counted to determine viral entry and replication efficiency.
- For live-cell imaging, Huh-7.5 cells infected with HCVbp-4cys or HCVwt-4cys were incubated with FIAsH-EDT2 dye, which binds specifically to the tetracysteine tag. Confocal microscopy revealed fluorescent signals corresponding to HCV particles, allowing real-time tracking of viral infection dynamics.
- Bromouridine (BrU) incorporation was used to label newly synthesized RNA in Huh-7.5 cells infected with HCVbp. Cells were treated with actinomycin D and BrUTP-transfected using Lipofectamine 2000. Immunostaining with anti-BrdU antibody visualized viral RNA synthesis, providing insights into the replication process.
- Total RNA from sucrose fractions was extracted for RT-TaqMan PCR analysis of HCV 5′-UTR RNA, quantifying particle-associated RNA levels. For infected cells, RNA was directly analyzed using TaqMan Gene Expression Cells-to-CT kit, with specific primers and standards to measure viral RNA kinetics over time.
- Serial dilutions of concentrated HCVbp or HCVcc were incubated with Huh-7.5 cells to determine TCID50. NS5A-positive cells were quantified using Odyssey In-cell Western system, enabling precise titration of infectious particles and assessment of viral infectivity in a dose-dependent manner.
- HepG2 cells were transduced with hCD81-expressing lentivirus to enhance HCV entry. RT-PCR confirmed CD81 expression, and infected cells were used for subsequent experiments, demonstrating the importance of CD81 in facilitating viral infection and replication in non-permissive cell types.
- Cultured human liver slices were infected with HCVcc (JFH-1) or HCVwt produced in BHK-WNV cells. Immunostaining with HCV-specific antibodies and confocal microscopy revealed widespread infection, validating the use of these models for studying HCV infection in a physiologically relevant context.
- A reporter system was used to assess HCVrp entry into Huh-7.5 cells. Cells were transfected with a luciferase-expressing construct and incubated with HCVrp particles. Luciferase activity measured viral entry efficiency, providing a sensitive and quantitative method for evaluating viral infectivity.
- siRNA-mediated gene knockdown in Huh-7.5 cells was used to investigate the role of specific host factors in HCV infection. Cells were transfected with siRNAs targeting genes of interest, followed by infection with HCV particles. Reduced viral replication confirmed the importance of these factors in the viral life cycle.
- The study complied with ethical guidelines set by the Institutional Review Board of Cochin Hospital (Paris). Human samples were obtained during routine medical care, and all procedures adhered to standard ethical practices, ensuring patient confidentiality and informed consent.
