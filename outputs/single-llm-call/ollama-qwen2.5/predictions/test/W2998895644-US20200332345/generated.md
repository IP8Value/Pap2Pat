- The tier 1 and tier 2 multiplex qPCR assays developed offer a rapid, sensitive method to identify F. tularensis and differentiate select agent strains from non-select agents in just two tests. This improves on existing methods that require more tests and complex scoring matrices.

- These new qPCR assays demonstrate improved sensitivity compared to previous methods, with LODs as low as 2 fg (1-3 genomic copies) for singleplex assays and 10-100 fg (5-49 genomic copies) for the multiplex platforms. The approximate 5-10 fold reduction in LOD for multiplex vs singleplex is expected.

- Testing with environmental tick lysates confirmed these qPCR assays specifically detect F. tularensis while avoiding cross-reactivity with near neighbors and Francisella-like endosymbionts known to exist in ticks, which has caused misleading results with other methods.

- The quadruplex tier 1 assay rapidly determines if a sample is conclusively F. tularensis and identifies the major clades (A.I, A.II, B, novicida). Tier 2 provides additional subpopulation information for non-A.I select agent strains. Together they require fewer tests than other qPCR methods.

- These assays may be used in tandem with DNA sequencing to further confirm results and provide strain-based characterization without altering specificity. This facilitates accurate diagnosis and treatment decisions during tularemia outbreaks, though more work is needed to develop direct detection methods for clinical specimens.
