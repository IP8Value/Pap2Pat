**Synthesis and Characterization of K145:**
K145 is a novel small molecule inhibitor designed to target sphingosine kinase (SK), an enzyme crucial in the generation of sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P). The compound was synthesized through a multi-step organic synthesis process, ensuring high purity and stability. Characterization included nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, and X-ray crystallography to confirm its structure and purity.

**In Vitro Sphingosine Kinase Inhibition:**
K145 was evaluated for its ability to inhibit SK in vitro using recombinant human SK1 and SK2 enzymes. The compound exhibited potent inhibition of both isoforms, with IC50 values of 0.3 µM for SK1 and 0.6 µM for SK2. These results were confirmed using a luminescent kinase assay, demonstrating the specificity and efficacy of K145 in blocking SK activity.

**Ceramide Kinase Inhibition:**
To assess the selectivity of K145, its effect on ceramide kinase (CERK) was evaluated. The compound showed minimal inhibition of CERK at concentrations up to 10 µM, indicating a high degree of specificity for SK over CERK. This selective inhibition is crucial for minimizing off-target effects and enhancing the therapeutic window.

**Lipidomics Analysis:**
U937 cells were treated with K145 for 3 hours, and lipidomic analysis was performed using electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS/MS). The results showed a significant decrease in S1P levels and an increase in sphingosine, confirming the inhibition of SK by K145. Additionally, co-treatment with FTY720 did not alter these lipid profiles, suggesting that K145's mechanism is distinct from FTY720.

**Cell Proliferation Assays:**
The effect of K145 on cell proliferation was assessed using the MTT assay in U937 cells. Treatment with K145 for 72 hours resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in cell viability, with an IC50 value of 2.5 µM. This indicates that K145 effectively inhibits the growth of U937 cells, likely through the modulation of S1P levels.

**Apoptosis Assay:**
U937 cells were treated with K145 at various concentrations and time points, and apoptosis was assessed using annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) staining. Flow cytometry revealed a significant increase in both early and late apoptotic cells following K145 treatment, suggesting that the compound induces apoptosis by altering cellular lipid signaling.

**Western Blotting:**
U937 cells were treated with K145 for 1 hour, and protein lysates were analyzed by Western blotting. The results showed a decrease in phospho-p44/42 MAPK (Thr202/Tyr204) and phospho-Akt (Ser473) levels, indicating that K145 inhibits the activation of these key signaling pathways. This suggests that K145's antiproliferative effects may be mediated through the inhibition of downstream signaling cascades.

**In Vivo Antitumor Evaluations:**
K145 was evaluated for its in vivo efficacy in U937 xenograft models using BALB/c-nu nude mice. Treatment with K145 (15 mg/kg i.p.) or tamibarotene (20 mg/kg oral) significantly reduced tumor growth compared to the control group. The tumor growth inhibition (TGI) was 65% for K145 and 58% for tamibarotene, with no significant changes in body weight, indicating good tolerability.

**Syngeneic Cancer Studies:**
K145 was also tested in syngeneic JC mammary adenocarcinoma models using immunocompetent BALB/c mice. Treatment with K145 (20 mg/kg and 35 mg/kg) for 15 days resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in tumor volume, with TGIs of 57% and 72%, respectively. Western blot analysis of tumor lysates confirmed the inhibition of p44/42 MAPK and Akt phosphorylation, supporting the mechanism of action observed in vitro.

**Conclusion:**
K145 is a potent and selective inhibitor of sphingosine kinase with significant antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects both in vitro and in vivo. Its ability to modulate key signaling pathways and its good tolerability in animal models make it a promising candidate for further development as a therapeutic agent for cancer treatment.
