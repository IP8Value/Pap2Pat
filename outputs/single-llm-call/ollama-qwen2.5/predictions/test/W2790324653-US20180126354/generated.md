- The study developed a multiple myeloma-specific capture-based sequencing platform targeting 465 genes to detect CNVs, SNVs, and translocations. It sequenced 95 tumor/normal pairs and validated the findings using exome sequencing and FISH data. This approach enabled the detection of known IGH translocations at expected frequencies and discovery of rare novel chromosomal translocations.
- The platform detected MYC translocations (both intra- and inter-chromosomal) in 13% of samples, consistent with prior studies despite some discrepancies due to sample size, disease stage, assay differences, and optimization. This frequency aligns with FISH and other sequencing-based studies in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma patients.
- IGLL5 was found to be mutated via both point substitutions and translocations in MM. The SNVs likely result from somatic hypermutation of the immunoglobulin lambda light chain locus. Non-synonymous IGLL5 mutations were mutually exclusive with RAS mutations, associated with disease progression, and present in the founding clone, suggesting a potential role in myeloma pathogenesis.
- Two patient samples had IGLL5 translocated to the IGH locus. Such translocations have been reported in B cells and associated disorders but may confer no obvious selective advantage. However, the translocation could co-opt a super-enhancer downstream of IGLL5, amplifying the effect of IGH enhancers on nearby genes like DERL3, which was upregulated in MM tumors.
- The platform's design allowed for discovery by querying 465 genes and tiling across V, D, and J regions to detect translocations involving inter-segment regions. Future versions should cover chromosome arm Yq and common SNPs to enable allele-specific ploidy detection. Targeted RNA sequencing could also provide comparative gene expression data for stratification efforts using genomic and clinical features.
- Mutation and expression data, combined with ISS stage, improve prognostic accuracy over ISS stage alone. The ease of employing a single platform to assay CNVs, SNVs, and translocations facilitates efforts to improve patient stratification using genomic, transcriptomic/expression, cytogenetic, and clinical features. This approach could support ongoing crowd-sourcing competitions aimed at enhancing multiple myeloma prognosis.
