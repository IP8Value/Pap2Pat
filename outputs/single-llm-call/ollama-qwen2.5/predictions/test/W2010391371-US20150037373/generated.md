- The study by Burrows et al. demonstrated that PPC can double the number of antigen-specific CD8+/IFNγ+ T cells in secondary responses to various vaccines. To investigate if PPC affects primary responses, researchers used the SEB model of T cell activation. They found that PPC significantly enhanced the expansion of both CD4+ and CD8+ Vβ8+ T cells during the primary response.
- By 96 hours post-SEB injection, when the contraction phase begins, CD4+ Vβ8+ T cells had contracted to levels lower than naive mice, while CD8+ Vβ8+ T cells in SEB + PPC-treated mice remained higher. This suggests PPC affects CD4 and CD8 T cell survival differently.
- PPC reduced ROS levels in activated T cells but did not prevent SEB-induced suppression of Bcl-2 expression or activation of apoptosis pathways. The lack of effect on contraction phase indicates PPC's primary impact is during the proliferative phase, possibly by enhancing dendritic cell function or migration to lymph nodes.

Further studies are needed to elucidate specific pathways and validate these findings, which could have implications for vaccine adjuvant development and immune system enhancement strategies using natural products like PPC.
