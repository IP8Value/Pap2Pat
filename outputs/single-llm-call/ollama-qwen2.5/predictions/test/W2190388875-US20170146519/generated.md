- **Introduction**: This study investigates the effects of a novel compound, G10, and DMXAA on the stimulation of the STING pathway and its potential as an antiviral agent against CHIKV. The research employs both in vitro and in vivo methods to evaluate the efficacy of these compounds.

- **Cell Culture and Treatments**: Telomerized human fibroblasts were transduced with lentiviruses carrying ISRE or ISG-luciferase reporters to monitor STING pathway activation. Cells were treated with G10, DMXAA, or 2’3’-cGAMP, and luciferase activity was measured to assess the induction of IFNβ and ISGs.

- **CRISPR/Cas9-Mediated Genome Editing**: CRISPR/Cas9 technology was used to knockout key proteins (IRF3, IPS-1, STAT1, STING) in human fibroblasts. Lentiviral vectors expressing gRNAs targeting these genes were generated and used to transduce cells, which were then selected with puromycin and verified for protein knockout by immunoblot.

- **Luciferase Reporter Assays**: Luciferase reporter assays were conducted to evaluate the activation of ISRE and ISG promoters in response to G10 and DMXAA. Cells were stimulated with these compounds, and luminescence was measured to quantify the induction of IFNβ and ISGs.

- **Thermal Shift Assays**: Recombinant human and mouse STING-CTD proteins were purified and used in thermal shift assays to determine the binding affinities of G10, 2’3’-cGAMP, and DMXAA. The Tm values were calculated to assess the stability and interactions between the compounds and the STING protein.

- **In Vivo Administration**: C57Bl/6J mice were intraperitoneally injected with 25mg/kg DMXAA or DMSO and subsequently challenged with CHIKV via footpad injection. Serum viral loads were measured at 72 hours post-infection to evaluate the antiviral efficacy of DMXAA.

- **Ethics Statement**: All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with Oregon Health and Science University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) guidelines, adhering to NIH Office of Laboratory Animal Welfare standards.
