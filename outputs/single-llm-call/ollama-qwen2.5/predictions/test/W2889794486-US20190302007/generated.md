- The study introduces a novel photoacoustic (PA) spectrophotometry method that accurately measures light absorption in highly scattering media. This technique uses optical excitation and detection, enabling homogeneous probing of samples and reducing heterogeneity effects. Calibration with known absorbers ensures absolute sensitivity.

- The PA spectrometer was tested using cupric sulfate solutions mixed with polystyrene beads of various sizes. Results showed excellent agreement between the proposed method and conventional UV-VIS spectroscopy, validating its accuracy for both pure absorptive and scattering media.

- Gold nanorods (GNRs) were used to demonstrate the method's capability in complex biological systems. Two types of GNRs—40 nm and 100 nm—were characterized using FDTD simulations, which matched experimental absorption and scattering spectra after broadening to account for size distribution.

- HUVEC cells were cultured and incubated with PEG-coated GNRs for 24 hours to study internalization. Cells were washed multiple times to remove extracellular GNRs, then fixed and imaged using both PA spectrophotometry and optical microscopy. The results showed significant changes in absorption spectra post-GNR internalization.

- Electronic supplementary material includes detailed experimental setups, calibration procedures, and additional data validating the method's performance. This comprehensive approach ensures reproducibility and reliability of the proposed technique for future applications in biomedical research.
