- **Introduction**: The study introduces a novel male sterility-fertility restoration system in plants designed for hybrid seed production. This system utilizes site-directed mutagenesis and specific promoter mutations to control gene expression, enabling the development of sterile and fertile plant lines as needed.

- **DNA Constructs and Transformation**: Four expression cassettes were constructed in plant vectors pBI101 and modified pCAMBIA1300. These constructs were transformed into Agrobacterium and used to develop transgenic tobacco plants, following previously established protocols.

- **Site-Directed Mutagenesis**: The TATATATG box region of the TA29 promoter was mutated to TGTATATG using Quik Change XL Kit. Additionally, a L105A mutation was introduced into COP1 using site-directed mutagenesis PCR, and clones were screened for the desired mutations by DNA sequencing.

- **Fluorimetric and Histochemical GUS Assay**: Fluorimetric GUS assays were performed to measure enzyme activity in transgenic lines, while histochemical GUS analysis was used to visualize gene expression patterns. Both methods provided quantitative data on the effectiveness of the promoter mutations.

- **Pollen Viability and Germination Assay**: Pollen viability was assessed using FDA/PI staining and confocal microscopy, while pollen germination was tested in an artificial liquid medium. These assays confirmed the sterility and fertility phenotypes of the transgenic plants.

- **Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**: Anther samples were prepared for TEM and SEM to analyze cellular structures at various developmental stages. The results provided detailed insights into the morphological changes associated with male sterility and fertility restoration.

- **RNA Extraction and Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR**: Total RNA was extracted from plant tissues, converted to cDNA, and analyzed by qRT-PCR. This method quantified gene expression levels, confirming the effectiveness of the promoter mutations in controlling target gene expression.

- **Microtome and Light Microscopy**: Anther sections were prepared and stained with toluidine blue O', then imaged using light microscopy. The images provided a visual confirmation of the developmental changes in anthers from different transgenic lines.

- **Crossing Strategies and F1 Screening**: Transgenic lines expressing the male sterility-fertility restoration system were crossed to generate F1 and F'1 generations. These crosses were screened for antibiotic resistance, and selected plantlets were grown in a controlled environment to evaluate their fertility and sterility phenotypes.

- **Conclusion**: The study successfully developed a novel male sterility-fertility restoration system that can be used for hybrid seed production. This system offers a robust and efficient method for controlling plant fertility, with potential applications in agriculture and biotechnology.
