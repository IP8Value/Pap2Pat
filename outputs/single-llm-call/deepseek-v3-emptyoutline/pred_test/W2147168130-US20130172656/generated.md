# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Esophageal cancer remains one of the most aggressive malignancies, characterized by poor prognosis and limited treatment options. Current therapeutic approaches, including radiation therapy combined with chemotherapy, yield unsatisfactory outcomes, with average five-year survival rates below 25%. The existing chemotherapeutic agents, such as cisplatin, often induce severe toxicity while providing only marginal survival benefits. There exists a critical unmet need for novel compounds that can effectively inhibit tumor growth while enhancing the efficacy of radiotherapy without exacerbating systemic toxicity.  

Armillaria mellea, a medicinal fungus traditionally used in herbal medicine, has demonstrated bioactive properties, including antioxidant and lymphocyte proliferation effects. Armillaridin (AM), a sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester isolated from Armillaria mellea, exhibits antibacterial activity. However, prior to the present invention, its potential as an antitumor agent, particularly in esophageal cancer, and its role as a radiosensitizer remained unexplored. The present invention addresses this gap by providing evidence that AM not only exerts cytotoxic effects on esophageal cancer cells but also significantly enhances their sensitivity to radiation therapy.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses the use of Armillaridin (AM), a sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester derived from Armillaria mellea, as a therapeutic agent for the treatment of esophageal cancer. The invention demonstrates that AM inhibits the viability of human esophageal cancer cell lines, including squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma, in a concentration- and time-dependent manner. Furthermore, AM induces apoptosis via the intrinsic mitochondrial pathway, as evidenced by caspase-3 activation, mitochondrial membrane depolarization, and characteristic morphological changes such as chromatin condensation and membrane blebbing.  

A key aspect of the invention is the radiosensitizing effect of AM, which enhances the efficacy of radiation therapy in esophageal cancer cells. The compound increases the proportion of cells arrested in the G2/M phase of the cell cycle, a phase known to be highly sensitive to radiation. This property results in a sensitizer enhancement ratio (SER) of up to 1.6, indicating a substantial improvement in radiotherapy outcomes.  

In vivo studies further validate the therapeutic potential of AM, showing significant tumor growth inhibition in xenograft models without adverse effects on body weight or hematological parameters. The invention thus provides a novel, well-tolerated treatment strategy for esophageal cancer, either as a standalone therapy or in combination with radiotherapy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention encompasses the isolation, characterization, and therapeutic application of Armillaridin (AM) for esophageal cancer treatment. AM is chemically defined as 3-chloro-6-hydroxy-4-methoxy-2-methyl-,3-formyl-2,2a,4a,5,6,7,7a,7b-octahydro-2a-hydroxy-6,6,7b-trimethyl-1H-cyclobut(e)inden-2-yl ester. The compound is preferably isolated from the mycelium of Armillaria mellea and purified to a pharmaceutical-grade standard.  

For in vitro applications, AM is dissolved in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) at a stock concentration suitable for cell culture experiments, with a final DMSO concentration not exceeding 0.1% (v/v) to avoid cytotoxicity. The working concentration ranges from 0.125 μM to 20 μM, depending on the desired therapeutic effect. Lower concentrations (0.125–5 μM) are preferred for radiosensitization, while higher concentrations (5–20 μM) are used for direct cytotoxic effects.  

The invention further details methods for assessing AM's antitumor activity, including cell viability assays (e.g., MTT assay), apoptosis detection (Annexin V/PI staining), mitochondrial membrane potential analysis (DiOC6(3) staining), and cell cycle profiling (propidium iodide staining). These methods confirm AM's mechanism of action, which involves caspase-3 activation, G2/M phase arrest, and mitochondrial dysfunction.  

For in vivo applications, AM is administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 80 mg/kg three times per week. This regimen demonstrates significant tumor growth inhibition in xenograft models while maintaining a favorable safety profile. The invention also covers combinatory therapy with radiation, wherein AM pretreatment (24 hours prior to irradiation) enhances tumor cell radiosensitivity. Radiation doses range from 0.5 Gy to 4 Gy, with 2 Gy being particularly effective for clinical relevance.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Cytotoxic Effects of AM on Esophageal Cancer Cell Lines**  
Human esophageal cancer cell lines (CE81T/VGH, TE-2, BE-3, SKGT-4) were treated with AM at concentrations ranging from 0.1 μM to 20 μM for 24–72 hours. Cell viability was assessed via MTT assay, revealing IC50 values of 3.4–6.9 μM. Morphological changes indicative of apoptosis, including chromatin condensation and membrane blebbing, were observed under light microscopy.  

**Example 2: Radiosensitization by AM**  
CE81T/VGH cells were pretreated with AM (0.125–0.5 μM) for 24 hours, followed by irradiation (0–4 Gy). Clonogenic assays demonstrated a significant reduction in surviving fractions, with an SER of 1.6 at 0.5 μM AM. Flow cytometry confirmed G2/M phase arrest, correlating with enhanced radiosensitivity.  

**Example 3: In Vivo Tumor Growth Inhibition**  
Nude mice bearing CE81T/VGH xenografts were treated with AM (80 mg/kg, 3x/week) for 4 weeks. Tumor volumes were reduced by 60% compared to controls, with no significant weight loss or leukopenia. Comparative studies with cisplatin showed superior tolerability for AM.  

**Example 4: Mechanism of Apoptosis Induction**  
AM-treated cells exhibited a 2-fold increase in caspase-3 activity, which was abolished by the pan-caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK. Mitochondrial membrane depolarization and Annexin V/PI staining confirmed apoptosis via the intrinsic pathway.  

These examples illustrate the broad utility of AM as a therapeutic and radiosensitizing agent for esophageal cancer, providing a foundation for clinical development.
