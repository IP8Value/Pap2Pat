# DESCRIPTION  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

The present invention provides a method for predicting progressive decline of kidney function in a patient with chronic kidney disease (CKD) by measuring the expression levels of versican isoforms V0 and V1 in a renal tissue sample obtained from the patient. In a cohort of 74 patients with proteinuric kidney diseases, renal expression of versican isoforms V0 and V1 was analyzed using quantitative real-time PCR. The results demonstrated that both V0 and V1 isoforms exhibited significantly higher expression levels in patients with progressive disease compared to those with stable disease. Specifically, V0 showed a 1.56-fold increase (p = 0.034) and V1 showed a 1.67-fold increase (p = 0.011) in progressive patients. Furthermore, V0 and V1 expression levels correlated significantly with serum creatinine at follow-up (V0: R = 0.337, p = 0.003; V1: R = 0.387, p = 0.001). These findings indicate that elevated renal versican V0 and V1 expression serves as a predictive biomarker for worsening renal function in CKD patients.  

### Example 2  

In another embodiment, the invention demonstrates that versican isoforms V0 and V1 enhance the predictive accuracy of conventional clinical and histological risk factors for CKD progression. Linear regression models were constructed to assess the contribution of versican expression alongside established prognostic markers such as serum creatinine at biopsy and glomerulosclerosis (GS). The combination of GS and creatinine at biopsy (Model 1) yielded an adjusted R² of 0.1619 (p = 0.0009). The addition of V0 to this model (Model 2) increased the adjusted R² to 0.2012 (p = 0.0004), while inclusion of V1 (Model 3) further improved predictive accuracy to an adjusted R² of 0.2438 (p = 0.0001). These results establish that versican V0 and V1 expression levels provide incremental prognostic value beyond traditional clinical and histological parameters in forecasting renal function decline.  

### Discussion  

The predictive utility of versican isoforms V0 and V1 was further validated in patients without acute renal failure (ARF), ensuring robustness against confounding factors. After excluding five patients with ARF, V0 and V1 expression remained elevated in progressive cases (V0: 1.52-fold, p = 0.051; V1: 1.64-fold, p = 0.018). Although serum creatinine at biopsy emerged as a strong standalone predictor (R² = 0.34), versican expression retained significant associations with future renal function. These observations underscore the reliability of versican as a biomarker, particularly in clinical scenarios where baseline renal function is compromised. The data collectively support the incorporation of versican V0 and V1 quantification into prognostic algorithms for CKD progression.  

### Example 3  

The invention further encompasses the use of versican as a biomarker in animal models of kidney disease, reinforcing its translational relevance. Renal versican mRNA expression was evaluated in three rodent models: accelerated nephrotoxic serum nephritis (mouse), adriamycin-induced nephropathy (rat), and passive Heymann nephritis (rat). Versican expression was significantly upregulated in all diseased animals compared to controls, with fold increases of 3.5 (p = 0.02), 1.5 (p = 0.03), and 8.0 (p < 0.001), respectively. Western blot analysis confirmed elevated versican protein levels in adriamycin nephropathy and passive Heymann nephritis. These findings corroborate the association between versican overexpression and renal pathology across species, highlighting its conserved role in kidney disease progression.  

### Example 4  

The cellular origins of versican expression were investigated to elucidate its mechanistic involvement in CKD. Immunohistochemical staining of human kidney biopsies revealed pronounced versican protein expression in the tubulointerstitial compartment, particularly in areas of interstitial fibrosis. In vitro studies using human renal proximal tubule cells (HK2) and fibroblasts (TK-173) demonstrated high basal expression of V0 and V1, whereas endothelial cells exhibited negligible levels. Treatment of TK-173 fibroblasts with transforming growth factor beta-1 (TGF-β1) induced a 3.5-fold upregulation of V0 and V1, suggesting a profibrotic regulatory mechanism. These results implicate versican as a mediator of tubulointerstitial injury and fibrosis, further supporting its utility as a biomarker and potential therapeutic target in CKD.  

The patent application provides comprehensive experimental validation of versican isoforms V0 and V1 as prognostic biomarkers for CKD progression, supported by clinical, histological, and mechanistic evidence. The invention enables early identification of high-risk patients, facilitating timely intervention and personalized management of kidney disease.
