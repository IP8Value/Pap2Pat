# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to improved methods for the genetic manipulation and cultivation of thermophilic bacteria belonging to the genus Thermotoga. Thermotoga species represent a deeply branching lineage of bacteria capable of growth at temperatures up to 90°C. These organisms have attracted significant scientific interest due to their potential applications in bioenergy production through hydrogen generation and polysaccharide fermentation. However, progress in understanding and exploiting these organisms has been severely limited by the lack of reliable genetic tools and cultivation methods suitable for these extreme thermophiles.  

Current limitations stem from several technical challenges. First, Thermotoga species are strict anaerobes, requiring specialized equipment and handling procedures for cultivation. Second, existing transformation methods yield extremely low efficiencies, with only one prior report of transient gene expression in these organisms. Third, the stability of introduced genetic material has not been demonstrated, preventing the establishment of engineered strains. Finally, conventional plating methods for colony isolation suffer from poor efficiency due to oxygen sensitivity, making genetic selection and screening impractical.  

There exists a significant unmet need for reliable methods to transform Thermotoga species, maintain introduced genetic elements stably, and cultivate transformants efficiently under practical laboratory conditions. The present invention addresses these needs through novel cultivation techniques, improved transformation methods, and engineered genetic systems that enable stable maintenance of foreign DNA in Thermotoga hosts.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a comprehensive system for genetic manipulation of Thermotoga species comprising: (1) novel cultivation methods enabling efficient plating and colony isolation under aerobic handling conditions; (2) optimized transformation protocols including both liposome-mediated and electroporation methods; (3) shuttle vector systems capable of stable maintenance in both Thermotoga and Escherichia coli; and (4) selection markers suitable for use in Thermotoga species.  

Key aspects of the invention include an embedded growth method using Gelrite-containing media that maintains high cell viability during aerobic handling, achieving plating efficiencies approaching 50%. The invention further provides soft SVO medium formulations that facilitate transfer between solid and liquid cultures while maintaining anaerobic conditions critical for cell viability.  

The invention demonstrates successful transformation of Thermotoga species using both liposome-mediated and electroporation methods, with transformation efficiencies sufficient for practical genetic manipulation. Engineered shuttle vectors incorporating Thermotoga plasmid elements show unexpected stability in both Thermotoga and E. coli hosts, enabling reliable maintenance of introduced genetic material.  

The methods and compositions of the invention overcome longstanding barriers to genetic manipulation of Thermotoga species, enabling metabolic engineering and other applications requiring stable genetic modification of these organisms.

### Improved Method for Cultivation of Thermotoga  

The invention provides novel cultivation methods that enable efficient handling of Thermotoga species under aerobic laboratory conditions while maintaining high cell viability. Conventional methods require strict anaerobic chambers or specialized equipment for colony isolation, significantly limiting experimental throughput.  

The embedded growth method of the invention involves mixing properly diluted liquid cultures with hot SVO medium containing 0.3% Gelrite, followed by pouring the mixture into Petri dishes where it solidifies with cells distributed throughout the matrix. This approach reduces oxygen exposure compared to surface plating methods while allowing convenient aerobic handling. The method achieves plating efficiencies of approximately 50%, representing a 10,000-fold improvement over conventional surface spreading techniques.  

For transfer of isolated colonies to liquid culture, the invention provides soft SVO medium containing 0.075% Gelrite. This formulation creates an oxygen gradient that maintains anaerobic conditions at the tube bottom where colonies are deposited, while allowing aerobic handling of the tube opening. Cultures grown in soft SVO can be subsequently transferred to liquid SVO medium using standard syringe techniques.  

The cultivation methods of the invention further enable long-term storage of Thermotoga cultures at room temperature, with viability maintained for at least two months in soft SVO medium. This represents a significant improvement over conventional storage methods requiring specialized equipment or cryopreservation.  

### Strains and Cultivation Conditions  

The invention provides optimized growth conditions for Thermotoga species including T. sp. RQ7, T. maritima MSB8, and T. neapolitana ATCC 49049. Cultures are grown at 77°C in SVO medium dispensed into serum bottles sparged with nitrogen gas to maintain anaerobic conditions. For liquid cultures, incubation is performed with shaking at 100 rpm.  

Solid media for embedded growth are prepared by mixing equal volumes of 2× concentrated liquid SVO with appropriate concentrations of gelling agents (0.3% Gelrite for embedded growth, higher concentrations for standard plates). Plates are incubated in anaerobic jars flushed with 96:4 N2:H2 mixture at 77°C, with colonies typically appearing within 24 hours.  

The invention further defines optimal antibiotic concentrations for selection in Thermotoga, with kanamycin at 150 μg/ml for liquid media and 250 μg/ml for solid media providing effective selection while minimizing spontaneous resistance. These concentrations are specifically optimized for T. sp. RQ7 and T. maritima, which show greater sensitivity than T. neapolitana.
