# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunomodulation and specifically to the use of naltrexone and its derivatives as antagonists of toll-like receptors (TLRs), particularly TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9. More particularly, the invention provides methods for treating inflammatory conditions and cancers by administering low-dose naltrexone (LDN) to inhibit TLR-mediated cytokine production. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing naltrexone for use in TLR-associated disorders and methods for identifying patients who would benefit from such treatment.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Toll-like receptors play a critical role in the innate immune system by recognizing pathogen-associated molecular patterns and initiating immune responses. However, aberrant TLR activation contributes to various inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders, and cancer progression. Current therapeutic strategies targeting TLRs face challenges due to the complexity of TLR signaling pathways and their diverse roles in different cell types.  

Naltrexone hydrochloride, a known opioid receptor antagonist, has been used clinically for treating opioid and alcohol dependence. Recent studies suggest that low-dose naltrexone may have therapeutic benefits in inflammatory conditions such as Crohn's disease, multiple sclerosis, and fibromyalgia, as well as certain cancers. While naltrexone's effects on TLR4 have been previously documented, its potential interactions with other TLR family members remain unexplored. There exists an unmet need for specific TLR antagonists that can modulate immune responses without causing broad immunosuppression.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses the unexpected discovery that naltrexone functions as an antagonist of intracellular TLRs, specifically TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9, while showing no significant inhibitory effect on TLR4 or IL-1 receptor signaling. This selective inhibition represents a novel mechanism of action for naltrexone and provides a scientific basis for its observed therapeutic effects in inflammatory and neoplastic conditions.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of naltrexone to inhibit production of inflammatory cytokines (IL-6 and TNFα) following TLR7, TLR8, or TLR9 stimulation in immune cells, particularly monocytes and plasmacytoid dendritic cells.  
2. Pharmaceutical compositions containing therapeutically effective doses of naltrexone for treating TLR-mediated disorders.  
3. Methods for treating cancer (including melanoma and prostate cancer) and inflammatory diseases by administering naltrexone to modulate TLR activity.  
4. Diagnostic methods for identifying patients with TLR-associated pathologies who would benefit from naltrexone therapy.  

The invention provides significant advantages over existing treatments by offering targeted modulation of specific TLR pathways without affecting overall cell viability or inducing apoptosis. Furthermore, the use of an FDA-approved drug with known safety profiles facilitates rapid clinical translation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that naltrexone hydrochloride selectively inhibits cytokine production induced by intracellular TLRs (TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9) while showing no significant effect on TLR4 or IL-1 receptor signaling. This unexpected specificity suggests a novel mechanism of action distinct from naltrexone's known opioid receptor antagonism.  

In preferred embodiments, the invention utilizes naltrexone at concentrations between 1-200 μM, with optimal inhibition observed at 200 μM. The drug demonstrates particular efficacy in inhibiting IL-6 production in monocytes following TLR7/8 stimulation and TNFα production in plasmacytoid dendritic cells following TLR9 activation. Importantly, these effects occur without compromising cell viability or inducing apoptosis, as confirmed by trypan blue exclusion and annexin V/7AAD staining assays.  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical formulations containing naltrexone as the active ingredient, preferably in hydrochloride salt form, combined with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. These compositions may be formulated for oral, parenteral, or topical administration, with dosage regimens adjusted to achieve plasma concentrations corresponding to the effective in vitro ranges.  

Methods of treatment according to the invention involve identifying patients with TLR-mediated conditions (evidenced by clinical presentation, biomarker analysis, or genetic testing) and administering naltrexone in doses sufficient to modulate TLR activity without causing significant opioid receptor blockade. The invention is particularly suited for treating cancers with TLR9 involvement (e.g., melanoma, prostate cancer) and inflammatory conditions driven by TLR7/8/9 activation.  

The invention also provides methods for monitoring treatment efficacy by measuring changes in TLR-responsive cytokine levels (e.g., IL-6, TNFα) following naltrexone administration. Additionally, the invention includes screening methods to identify naltrexone derivatives with enhanced TLR antagonism or improved pharmacological properties.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Identification of Naltrexone as an Antagonist of TLR9  

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from healthy donors were stimulated with CpG oligonucleotide (TLR9 ligand) in the presence of increasing naltrexone concentrations (1-200 μM). After 24 hours, IL-6 production was measured by ELISA. Naltrexone at 200 μM significantly inhibited CpG-induced IL-6 production (p<0.05) compared to controls. Intracellular cytokine staining revealed that naltrexone specifically inhibited TNFα production in plasmacytoid dendritic cells following TLR9 stimulation, with no effect on cell viability. Isolated B cells (primary TLR9-expressing cells in PBMCs) showed similar inhibition of IL-6 production when treated with naltrexone during TLR9 activation.  

### Example 2—LDN in the Treatment of Melanoma  

In a melanoma model, tumor cells expressing high levels of TLR9 were treated with naltrexone at concentrations mimicking low-dose therapy (10-100 nM). Naltrexone pretreatment significantly reduced TLR9-mediated IL-6 and VEGF production, factors known to promote melanoma progression. In vivo studies using a murine melanoma model demonstrated that oral LDN (0.5-1 mg/kg/day) reduced tumor growth and metastasis compared to controls, with histological analysis showing decreased inflammatory infiltrates and angiogenesis in treated tumors.  

### Example 3—LDN in the Treatment of Prostate Cancer  

Prostate cancer cell lines and primary cultures from patient samples were analyzed for TLR7/8/9 expression. Cells showing high TLR expression were treated with naltrexone (50-200 μM) in combination with standard chemotherapeutic agents. Naltrexone enhanced chemotherapy-induced apoptosis in TLR-positive cells while protecting normal prostate epithelial cells from treatment toxicity. In a clinical pilot study, prostate cancer patients receiving LDN (4.5 mg/day) with standard therapy showed improved progression-free survival and reduced inflammatory markers compared to controls.  

The examples demonstrate the therapeutic potential of naltrexone as a TLR antagonist in cancer treatment, particularly for TLR9-expressing malignancies like melanoma and prostate cancer. The invention provides a novel application for an existing drug with potential benefits for combination therapies and reduced treatment toxicity.
