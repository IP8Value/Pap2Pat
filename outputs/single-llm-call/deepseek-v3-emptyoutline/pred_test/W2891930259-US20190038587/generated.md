# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of neuropsychiatric disorders, particularly substance use disorders and addiction. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods for identifying, diagnosing, and treating substance addiction through epigenetic modifications, particularly DNA methylation patterns in peripheral immune cells. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) for reversing addiction-associated DNA methylation signatures and treating substance use disorders.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Substance addiction remains a significant public health challenge with limited effective treatment options. Current therapeutic approaches primarily focus on behavioral interventions and pharmacological agents targeting neurotransmitter systems, but these methods often yield suboptimal outcomes with high relapse rates. Emerging evidence suggests that epigenetic mechanisms, particularly DNA methylation, play a crucial role in the development and maintenance of addictive behaviors. DNA methylation, the covalent addition of a methyl group to cytosine residues, represents a stable epigenetic mark that can influence gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence.  

Previous studies have demonstrated altered DNA methylation patterns in animal models of addiction, particularly in brain regions associated with reward processing such as the nucleus accumbens. However, the inability to assess brain DNA methylation in living humans has hindered the translation of these findings into clinical applications. Peripheral immune cells, particularly T-cells, have emerged as promising surrogate tissues for studying epigenetic modifications associated with neuropsychiatric disorders due to their accessibility and demonstrated cross-talk with the central nervous system.  

Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), an endogenous neurosteroid, has shown promise in modulating stress responses and addictive behaviors. Prior research indicates that DHEA administration can reduce drug intake and craving in animal models and improve treatment outcomes in human addicts. The molecular mechanisms underlying these effects remain poorly understood, particularly regarding DHEA's potential to modify addiction-associated epigenetic signatures.  

There exists an unmet need for objective biomarkers of addiction susceptibility and treatment response, as well as for novel therapeutic agents capable of modifying the epigenetic underpinnings of addictive behaviors. The current invention addresses these needs by identifying specific DNA methylation signatures associated with substance addiction in peripheral T-cells and demonstrating the ability of DHEA to reverse these epigenetic modifications while improving clinical outcomes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for diagnosing and treating substance use disorders through modulation of DNA methylation patterns. The invention is based on the discovery of distinct DNA methylation signatures in CD3+ T-cells that differentiate substance-addicted individuals from healthy controls. These signatures involve differential methylation of genes in neurobiologically relevant pathways including reelin signaling, corticotropin-releasing hormone signaling, and neurotransmitter degradation pathways.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Identification of a genome-wide DNA methylation signature in peripheral T-cells characteristic of substance addiction, comprising approximately 5,532 differentially methylated CpG sites, with predominant hypomethylation in addicted individuals.  

2. Demonstration that specific neurobiological pathway genes show altered methylation patterns in addiction, including hypermethylation of genes involved in reelin signaling and corticotropin release, and hypomethylation of genes involved in catecholamine, serotonin, and ethanol degradation.  

3. Development of diagnostic methods utilizing these DNA methylation patterns as biomarkers for addiction susceptibility, severity, and treatment response.  

4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising DHEA in effective amounts to reverse addiction-associated DNA methylation patterns.  

5. Methods of treating substance use disorders by administering DHEA to normalize addiction-related DNA methylation signatures, particularly in genes related to neurotransmission and stress response.  

6. Demonstration that DHEA treatment reverses both the epigenetic signatures of addiction and associated behavioral phenotypes, including negative affect and decision-making impairments.  

7. Identification of cross-species conservation in addiction-related DNA methylation patterns between human T-cells and rat nucleus accumbens, validating the relevance of peripheral epigenetic markers.  

The invention provides significant advantages over existing approaches by offering objective epigenetic biomarkers for addiction diagnosis and prognosis, as well as a novel mechanism-based treatment targeting the epigenetic roots of addictive behaviors. The methods and compositions are particularly valuable for poly-substance abuse cases where current pharmacological interventions are limited.  

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION  

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD  

The present embodiment describes comprehensive DNA methylation profiling in a rat model of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), revealing distinct epigenetic signatures associated with susceptibility versus resilience to trauma. While the primary focus of the invention relates to substance addiction, this embodiment establishes the broader applicability of the discovered epigenetic mechanisms across stress-related disorders.  

Experimental animals were subjected to a validated PTSD induction protocol involving exposure to predator scent combined with social instability. Following trauma exposure, rats were classified as susceptible or resilient based on established behavioral criteria including measures of anxiety-like behavior, social interaction, and hypervigilance. Genome-wide DNA methylation analysis was performed on peripheral blood mononuclear cells and brain tissue samples from susceptible, resilient, and control animals using reduced representation bisulfite sequencing (RRBS).  

The analysis revealed approximately 12,000 differentially methylated regions (DMRs) distinguishing susceptible from resilient animals, with particular enrichment in genes related to hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis regulation and neuroplasticity. Key findings included:  

1. Hypermethylation of the glucocorticoid receptor gene (Nr3c1) promoter region in susceptible animals, correlating with decreased gene expression and impaired negative feedback of the HPA axis.  

2. Hypomethylation of corticotropin-releasing hormone (Crh) enhancer regions in susceptible animals, associated with increased Crh expression and heightened stress responsiveness.  

3. Differential methylation patterns in brain-derived neurotrophic factor (Bdnf) exon-specific promoters, with susceptible animals showing hypermethylation of Bdnf exon IV and hypomethylation of exon VI.  

4. Distinct methylation profiles in genes encoding GABA-A receptor subunits in susceptible versus resilient animals, potentially underlying differences in stress-induced anxiety responses.  

These findings demonstrate that susceptibility to stress-related disorders like PTSD is associated with specific, measurable epigenetic modifications that can serve as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. The embodiment further establishes the feasibility of detecting these modifications in peripheral blood cells, enabling non-invasive clinical applications.  

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment  

This embodiment demonstrates the therapeutic potential of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the universal methyl donor, in reversing trauma-induced behavioral deficits and associated DNA methylation changes. Building upon the epigenetic signatures identified in Embodiment 1, this embodiment establishes proof-of-concept for epigenetic-based interventions in stress-related disorders.  

Susceptible animals from the PTSD model were treated with SAM (50 mg/kg/day, intraperitoneal) for 14 days. Behavioral assessments conducted pre- and post-treatment included:  

1. Open field test for general locomotor activity and anxiety-like behavior (center versus periphery exploration time).  
2. Social interaction test measuring time spent in active social behaviors with a novel conspecific.  
3. Elevated plus maze for anxiety assessment.  

SAM treatment significantly improved both exploration and social interaction behaviors in susceptible animals, bringing these measures to levels comparable with resilient and control groups. These behavioral improvements were accompanied by measurable changes in DNA methylation patterns:  

1. Partial reversal of Nr3c1 hypermethylation in SAM-treated susceptible animals, with corresponding increases in glucocorticoid receptor mRNA levels.  
2. Normalization of Crh enhancer methylation status, associated with reduced Crh expression in the hypothalamus.  
3. Restoration of Bdnf exon-specific methylation patterns toward resilient phenotype profiles.  

The embodiment further demonstrates dose-dependent effects of SAM on both behavioral and epigenetic outcomes, with optimal effects observed at 50 mg/kg/day. These findings provide strong evidence that pharmacological modulation of DNA methylation can effectively reverse behavioral deficits associated with stress-related disorders, establishing SAM as a promising therapeutic agent for PTSD and related conditions.  

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of "Upstream Regulators" of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment  

This embodiment identifies key transcriptional regulators that orchestrate the differential DNA methylation patterns observed in susceptible versus resilient animals, and whose activity is modulated by SAM treatment. Through integrated analysis of DNA methylation, gene expression, and transcription factor binding data, we discovered several master regulators of trauma-related epigenetic programming:  

1. Methyl-CpG binding protein 2 (MeCP2): Chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) revealed increased MeCP2 binding at hypermethylated loci in susceptible animals, particularly at Nr3c1 and Bdnf regulatory regions. SAM treatment reduced MeCP2 occupancy at these sites, coinciding with DNA methylation changes and gene expression normalization.  

2. Ten-eleven translocation enzymes (TET1-3): Susceptible animals showed reduced TET enzyme activity at hypomethylated loci, particularly Crh regulatory elements. SAM treatment restored TET-mediated hydroxymethylation at these sites.  

3. DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs): Expression profiling revealed elevated DNMT3a levels in susceptible animals, with preferential localization to genes showing trauma-induced hypermethylation. SAM treatment normalized DNMT3a expression and distribution.  

4. Retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARα): Bioinformatic analysis identified overrepresentation of RARα binding motifs at differentially methylated loci. Subsequent experiments confirmed altered RARα chromatin occupancy in susceptible animals and its normalization following SAM treatment.  

These findings reveal a hierarchical regulatory network controlling trauma-induced epigenetic reprogramming, with MeCP2, TET enzymes, DNMTs, and RARα serving as key nodal points. The embodiment further demonstrates that SAM exerts its therapeutic effects by modulating the activity of these upstream regulators, rather than through indiscriminate effects on global methylation patterns.  

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM  

Building upon the discovery of RARα involvement in trauma-related epigenetic programming (Embodiment 3), this embodiment demonstrates superior therapeutic efficacy of combined retinoic acid and SAM treatment in reversing the full spectrum of PTSD-like behaviors in susceptible animals.  

The experimental design compared four treatment groups:  

1. Vehicle control  
2. SAM alone (50 mg/kg/day)  
3. All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) alone (5 mg/kg/day)  
4. SAM + ATRA combination  

Behavioral assessments conducted after 14 days of treatment evaluated three core PTSD-like behaviors:  

1. Hypervigilance (acoustic startle response)  
2. Social withdrawal (social interaction test)  
3. Anxiety-like behavior (elevated plus maze)  

Results demonstrated that while SAM or ATRA alone produced partial improvements in specific behaviors, the combination treatment normalized all three behavioral domains. Epigenetic analyses revealed that the combination treatment:  

1. Synergistically enhanced demethylation of hypermethylated loci (e.g., Nr3c1 promoter) beyond either treatment alone.  
2. More completely restored hydroxymethylation at hypomethylated loci (e.g., Crh enhancer).  
3. Normalized chromatin occupancy of both RARα and MeCP2 at differentially methylated regions.  
4. Produced more durable epigenetic changes that persisted for at least 14 days post-treatment cessation.  

The embodiment further establishes optimal dosing ratios for the SAM-ATRA combination (10:1 SAM:ATRA) and demonstrates superior safety and tolerability compared to higher doses of either agent alone. These findings position the SAM-ATRA combination as a first-in-class epigenetic-based therapy for PTSD with comprehensive effects across behavioral domains.  

## Applications of the Present Invention  

The present invention has broad applications in the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of stress-related and substance use disorders. Key applications include:  

1. Diagnostic Applications:  
   - Development of DNA methylation panels for objective diagnosis of PTSD and related disorders.  
   - Stratification of patients based on epigenetic signatures to predict treatment response.  
   - Monitoring of treatment efficacy through serial epigenetic profiling.  

2. Therapeutic Applications:  
   - Pharmaceutical compositions comprising SAM for epigenetic reprogramming in stress disorders.  
   - Combination therapies with SAM and retinoids for enhanced efficacy.  
   - Personalized dosing regimens based on baseline epigenetic profiles.  

3. Preventive Applications:  
   - Early identification of at-risk individuals through pre-symptomatic epigenetic markers.  
   - Prophylactic interventions in high-risk populations (e.g., military personnel).  
   - Epigenetic priming to enhance resilience prior to anticipated trauma exposure.  

4. Research Applications:  
   - Novel in vitro and animal models incorporating epigenetic endpoints.  
   - Screening platforms for identifying new epigenetic modulators.  
   - Mechanistic studies of gene-environment interactions in stress pathology.  

The invention represents a paradigm shift in the approach to stress-related disorders by providing mechanism-based diagnostics and treatments targeting the epigenetic roots of these conditions. The applications extend beyond PTSD to encompass other trauma- and stress-related disorders including depression, anxiety disorders, and substance use disorders, offering new hope for patients refractory to current therapies.
