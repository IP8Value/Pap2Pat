# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and genetic diagnostics, specifically to modified DNA polymerases with enhanced mismatch extension selectivity. More particularly, the invention pertains to mutant forms of the Klenow fragment of Taq DNA polymerase (KlenTaq) engineered to exhibit improved discrimination between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes during nucleic acid amplification. These polymerases are particularly useful in allele-specific amplification (ASA), methylation-specific PCR (MSP), and human leukocyte antigen (HLA) typing, enabling more accurate and reliable detection of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and epigenetic modifications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are the most abundant form of genetic variation among individuals and play a critical role in personalized medicine, pharmacogenomics, and disease predisposition. Accurate SNP detection is essential for applications such as genetic fingerprinting, parentage testing, and diagnostics for conditions like venous thromboembolism. Current methods for SNP analysis, including allele-specific amplification (ASA) and methylation-specific PCR (MSP), rely on the ability of DNA polymerases to discriminate between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes. However, conventional DNA polymerases often exhibit insufficient selectivity, leading to false-positive signals and reduced assay reliability.  

Similarly, epigenetic modifications such as DNA methylation are crucial biomarkers for cancer and other diseases. MSP, a widely used method for detecting methylation patterns, depends on the differential amplification of bisulfite-treated DNA using primers specific to methylated or unmethylated sequences. The success of MSP hinges on the polymerase's ability to distinguish between matched and mismatched primer-template pairs, a task for which most wild-type polymerases are suboptimal.  

Existing solutions, such as modified primers or engineered polymerases with improved fidelity, have limitations in terms of cost, efficiency, and applicability across diverse diagnostic platforms. There is thus a pressing need for DNA polymerases with enhanced mismatch discrimination capabilities that can be employed in standard laboratory settings without specialized equipment or extensive protocol modifications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the aforementioned limitations by providing mutant KlenTaq DNA polymerases with significantly improved mismatch extension selectivity. These mutants are engineered via targeted saturation mutagenesis of key amino acid residues involved in primer-template binding, particularly arginine (R) and lysine (K) residues that interact with the primer strand's phosphate backbone.  

The invention specifically discloses mutations at positions R487, K508, R536, R587, and R660 of KlenTaq, with substitutions to hydrophobic or alternative basic amino acids yielding polymerases with enhanced selectivity. Among these, the R660V mutant (arginine to valine at position 660) exhibits the most favorable combination of high activity and mismatch discrimination, making it particularly suitable for diagnostic applications.  

The mutant polymerases of the invention are characterized by:  
1. **Increased Mismatch Discrimination**: Demonstrated by delayed or absent amplification in mismatch cases compared to wild-type KlenTaq, as quantified by real-time PCR threshold cycle (Ct) values.  
2. **Retained High Activity**: Preservation of efficient amplification in matched primer-template systems, ensuring compatibility with high-throughput workflows.  
3. **Broad Applicability**: Utility in ASA, MSP, and HLA typing, as well as compatibility with whole-blood samples without prior DNA purification.  

The invention further encompasses methods for using these mutant polymerases in SNP genotyping, methylation detection, and HLA typing, including multiplex assays and direct amplification from crude biological samples.  

### Sequences  

The amino acid sequences of the mutant KlenTaq polymerases are provided, with specific substitutions at the aforementioned positions. For example, the R660V mutant sequence includes a valine substitution at position 660, while retaining the remaining wild-type sequence. Nucleic acid sequences encoding these mutants are also disclosed, along with expression vectors and host cells for recombinant production.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Saturation Mutagenesis and Screening of KlenTaq Mutants**  
A library of KlenTaq mutants was generated via saturation mutagenesis at positions R487, K508, R536, R587, and R660 using degenerate primers. Mutants were expressed in *E. coli*, and lysates were screened in a high-throughput real-time ASA assay. Active mutants exhibiting improved mismatch discrimination were identified, purified, and validated.  

### Example 2  

**Primer Extension Assays for Selectivity Assessment**  
Radioactively labeled primers were annealed to matched or mismatched templates and extended by wild-type or mutant KlenTaq. PAGE analysis revealed that mutants such as R660V showed no product formation for mismatches after 10 minutes, while producing full-length products for matches, confirming enhanced selectivity.  

### Example 3  

**Kinetic Analysis of Mutant Polymerases**  
Pre-steady-state kinetics demonstrated that mutants like R660V exhibited up to 36-fold higher mismatch discrimination (kA/kG ratio) compared to wild-type KlenTaq, with minimal loss of activity for matched substrates.  

### Example 4  

**ASA Using Human Genomic DNA**  
The R660V mutant was tested for SNP detection in the Factor II prothrombin gene using HeLa genomic DNA. The mutant showed an 18-cycle delay in mismatch amplification (ΔCt) versus 8.5 cycles for wild-type, enabling robust SNP genotyping.  

### Example 5  

**Comparison with Commercial Polymerases**  
R660V outperformed Platinum Taq and other commercial polymerases in ASA, with near doubling of ΔCt values for mismatch discrimination, as demonstrated using homozygous and heterozygous genomic DNA standards.  

### Example 6  

**Multiplex ASA from Blood Samples**  
A multiplex ASA assay for Factor II SNP detection was developed using R660V, with allele-specific primers distinguished by melting temperature analysis. Direct amplification from whole blood was achieved without DNA purification, highlighting clinical utility.  

### Example 7  

**HLA Typing with R660V**  
The mutant polymerase was compared to AmpliTaq Gold in HLA-DRB1 typing by PCR-SSP. R660V showed superior specificity, eliminating false-positive amplification observed with AmpliTaq Gold, while maintaining sensitivity.  

### Example 8  

**Error Rate Determination**  
A PCR-based assay revealed that R660V had an error rate of 2.8×10⁻⁵ mutations per base per duplication, lower than wild-type KlenTaq (8.8×10⁻⁵), with fewer transition errors.  

### Example 9  

**Methylation-Specific PCR (MSP)**  
R660V was employed in MSP to detect hypermethylation in the Septin 9 promoter, a colon cancer biomarker. The mutant showed higher activity and discrimination than a commercial MSP kit, enabling reliable methylation detection.
