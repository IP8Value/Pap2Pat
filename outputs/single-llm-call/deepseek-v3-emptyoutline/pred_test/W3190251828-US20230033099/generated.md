# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The field of cancer immunotherapy has advanced significantly with the development of dendritic cell (DC)-based vaccines, which harness the immune system's ability to recognize and eliminate tumor cells. Current DC vaccine approaches face several limitations, including reliance on single tumor antigens, HLA restriction for peptide presentation, resource-intensive neoantigen identification, and challenges in regulating cytokine delivery. These constraints reduce the broad applicability of DC vaccines, particularly for cancers lacking defined tumor antigens such as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).  

Existing methods such as Sipuleucel-T utilize fusion proteins of GM-CSF and tumor antigens to stimulate DCs ex vivo, while GVAX employs irradiated tumor cells secreting GM-CSF. However, these approaches often result in suboptimal DC activation or unintended immunosuppressive effects due to uncontrolled cytokine exposure. Tumor lysate-based vaccines show promise but require large quantities of tumor tissue, which may not be available for inoperable or recurrent cancers. There remains an unmet need for a DC vaccine platform that can efficiently deliver diverse tumor antigens while precisely controlling immunostimulatory signals to enhance anti-tumor immunity.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel dendritic cell vaccine platform utilizing tumor membrane vesicles (TMVs) incorporated with glycosyl phosphatidylinositol-anchored immunostimulatory molecules (GPI-ISMs). This platform overcomes limitations of prior art by combining three critical components: (1) TMVs containing the complete antigenic profile of tumor cells, (2) GPI-ISMs (including GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12) precisely anchored to TMV surfaces via protein transfer, and (3) ex vivo pulsed dendritic cells that efficiently present tumor antigens while minimizing systemic cytokine exposure.  

The invention demonstrates that TMVs ranging from 200-600 nm in diameter are optimally sized for DC uptake and retain tumor-specific antigens such as HER2. Incorporation of GPI-ISMs significantly enhances TMV phagocytosis by DCs while promoting DC activation through upregulated CD86 and MHC-II expression. The combined presence of GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12 induces synergistic cytokine production (IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6) critical for anti-tumor immunity.  

In therapeutic applications, the DC vaccine platform shows efficacy in both HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer models. Treatment results in significant tumor growth inhibition, extended survival, reduced lung metastasis, and improved tumor immune profiles characterized by increased infiltrating DCs and T cells. The invention provides a versatile platform adaptable to various cancer types while addressing key challenges of antigen breadth, DC activation, and controlled cytokine delivery.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Tumor Membrane Vesicles (TMVs)  

Tumor membrane vesicles are nanoscale particles derived from tumor cell plasma membranes, preserving the native antigenic profile of source tumors. TMVs are prepared through homogenization of tumor tissue or cultured cell pellets followed by ultracentrifugation over a 41% sucrose gradient. Characterization via dynamic light scattering reveals TMVs with diameters of 200-600 nm and polydispersity indices around 0.31, indicating optimal size distribution for dendritic cell uptake.  

TMVs maintain surface expression of tumor-associated antigens present on parent cells, as demonstrated by retention of HER2 on vesicles derived from D2F2/E2 cells. This antigen preservation enables broad immune recognition beyond single-epitope approaches. The membrane-bound nature of TMVs provides stability advantages over soluble tumor lysates while avoiding the resource-intensive requirements of neoantigen identification platforms.  

### TMV Preparation, Characterization, and GPI-ISM Incorporation  

The TMV preparation process involves mechanical disruption of tumor tissue followed by differential centrifugation to isolate membrane fractions. Ultracentrifugation through sucrose gradients yields purified TMVs that can be stored frozen without loss of antigenic integrity. Quality control includes Zetasizer analysis for size distribution and flow cytometry for antigen verification.  

GPI-anchored immunostimulatory molecules (GPI-ISMs) are incorporated onto TMV surfaces via protein transfer technology. This involves incubating purified GPI-ISMs (25 μg per mg TMV) at 37°C for 4 hours with constant rotation. Unbound molecules are removed through washing and ultracentrifugation. Flow cytometry with cytokine-specific antibodies confirms successful incorporation of GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12, while plain TMVs show no detectable cytokine signals.  

### TMVs Containing the Tumor Antigen HER2 and Protein Transferred GPI-ISMs on Their Surface  

In HER2-positive models, TMVs derived from D2F2/E2 cells demonstrate preserved HER2 expression matching parent cell levels. Combined with GPI-ISMs, these TMVs create a multi-functional vaccine platform delivering both tumor antigen and immunostimulatory signals. Flow cytometry analysis shows simultaneous detection of HER2 and incorporated cytokines (GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12) on individual TMVs, confirming the successful integration of all components.  

The protein transfer method enables precise control over GPI-ISM loading ratios, allowing optimization of adjuvant effects while minimizing potential toxicity. This represents a significant improvement over cytokine-secreting approaches where dosage cannot be precisely regulated. The membrane-anchored cytokines maintain bioactivity while being physically coupled to antigen sources, ensuring coordinated delivery to antigen-presenting cells.  

### Uptake of TMVs by DCs is Potentiated by GPI-ISMs  

Dendritic cell uptake studies using pH-sensitive fluorescent labeling demonstrate that GPI-ISMs significantly enhance TMV internalization. While plain TMVs show baseline uptake after 30 minutes, TMVs containing GPI-GM-CSF or GPI-IL-12 exhibit 2-3 fold greater phagocytosis by 180 minutes. The combination of both GPI-ISMs produces synergistic effects, with maximal uptake observed in DCs pulsed with dual-adjuvanted TMVs.  

Quantitative flow cytometry reveals not only increased percentages of TMV-positive DCs but also higher mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) in GPI-ISM-containing groups. This indicates both more DCs taking up TMVs and greater quantities of TMVs per cell. The enhanced uptake correlates with improved antigen presentation capacity, as demonstrated in subsequent activation and efficacy studies.  

### DC Vaccine Therapy Reduces Tumor Growth  

In HER2-positive D2F2/E2 tumor models, DC vaccines pulsed with GPI-ISM-incorporated TMVs show superior therapeutic effects compared to controls. Mice receiving DCs pulsed with plain TMVs or unpulsed DCs exhibit tumor growth kinetics and survival curves indistinguishable from PBS controls. In contrast, the GPI-ISM TMV-DC vaccine group demonstrates significant tumor growth inhibition beginning at day 14 post-treatment.  

Survival analysis reveals dramatic improvements, with median survival extending from 35 days in control groups to 57 days in vaccine-treated animals. The therapeutic effects require both TMV antigens and GPI-ISMs, as neither component alone produces significant anti-tumor responses. This confirms the critical synergy between tumor antigen delivery and precisely controlled immunostimulation in the vaccine platform.  

### DC Vaccine Inhibits 4T1 Tumor Growth, Reduces Lung Metastasis, and Improved Immune Profile of Tumors  

The invention's broad applicability is demonstrated in the 4T1 TNBC model, which lacks defined tumor antigens. DC vaccines incorporating 4T1-derived TMVs with GPI-ISMs produce significant tumor growth inhibition compared to all control groups. Metastasis assays reveal 2-3 fold reductions in lung tumor burden, highlighting the vaccine's ability to control systemic disease spread.  

Immune profiling of treated tumors shows increased infiltration of MHC-II+ CD11c+ dendritic cells (2-fold over controls) along with elevated CD4+ and CD8+ T cell populations. This modified tumor microenvironment correlates with therapeutic efficacy, suggesting the vaccine promotes both antigen presentation and effector cell recruitment. The platform's success in this antigenically undefined model underscores its potential for treating diverse cancer types.  

### Bone Marrow-Derived Dendritic Cell (BMDC) Production  

The invention utilizes bone marrow-derived dendritic cells generated through a 10-day culture protocol with recombinant murine GM-CSF. Femurs are flushed to obtain marrow cells, which undergo RBC lysis before culture in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 20 ng/mL GM-CSF. Medium is refreshed at days 3, 6, and 8 to maintain cytokine stimulation.  

Resulting BMDCs exhibit characteristic dendritic morphology and express appropriate maturation markers. These cells demonstrate superior antigen uptake capacity compared to peripheral blood-derived DCs and maintain viability through the pulsing and washing steps required for vaccine preparation. The protocol yields sufficient DC numbers for therapeutic applications while maintaining consistency across preparations.  

### 4T1 Lung Metastasis Assay  

Metastatic burden is quantified through an enzymatic digestion and selection culture method. Lungs are minced and digested in collagenase IV for 2 hours, then filtered to obtain single-cell suspensions. Tumor cells are selected using 6-thioguanine-containing medium that eliminates normal lung fibroblasts while permitting 4T1 cell growth.  

Confluent cultures are harvested and counted to determine absolute metastatic cell numbers. This sensitive assay detects differences in metastatic burden between treatment groups, with DC vaccine therapy showing consistent reduction in lung tumor cells. The method provides quantitative validation of the vaccine's systemic effects beyond primary tumor control.
