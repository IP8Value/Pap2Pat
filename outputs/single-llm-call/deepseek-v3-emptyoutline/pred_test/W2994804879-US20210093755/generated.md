# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to medical devices and procedures used in endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), specifically to a novel device and method for facilitating endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) catheter repositioning from the mouth to the nostril. The invention addresses challenges associated with conventional ENBD catheter repositioning techniques, particularly in sedated patients or those with anatomical variations in the oropharynx. The device comprises a loop-shaped holder with a curved loop head and a bar-shaped handle, along with a specially designed plastic tube featuring depth markings.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) is a critical procedure for managing biliary diseases, including cholangitis and biliary obstruction. ENBD offers advantages such as effective bile drainage, reduced risk of catheter obstruction, and the ability to obtain bile samples for diagnostic purposes. However, conventional ENBD catheter repositioning techniques present several drawbacks. The traditional method involves inserting a nasal plastic tube, manually retrieving its tip from the mouth, connecting it to the ENBD catheter, and pulling the assembly toward the nostril. This process is time-consuming and poses risks such as oral cavity injury, operator finger injuries from patient biting, and challenges in patients with high Mallampati scores (indicating difficult oropharyngeal visualization).  

Previous attempts to improve ENBD repositioning include modified techniques using guidewires or forceps. For example, Hamano et al. proposed a method involving a crossed guidewire loop. However, these approaches suffer from instability, as the guidewire loop often slips, and require additional fluoroscopic or endoscopic monitoring. There remains a need for a device that simplifies ENBD catheter repositioning, reduces procedure time, minimizes injury risks, and functions effectively regardless of oropharyngeal anatomy.  

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The conventional ENBD catheter repositioning technique is inefficient and hazardous. Key problems include prolonged procedure times due to the need to wait for sedated patients to awaken, the risk of oral injuries from forceps or manual retrieval, and difficulties in patients with unfavorable oropharyngeal anatomy (e.g., high Mallampati scores). These limitations hinder the widespread adoption of ENBD and increase procedural risks for both patients and operators.  

### Technical Solution  

The invention introduces a specialized device termed the "J-Loop," comprising two primary components: (1) a loop-shaped holder with a curved loop head and a bar-shaped handle, and (2) a plastic tube featuring depth markings. The loop-shaped holder is designed to be inserted into the oral cavity, where it captures the plastic tube inserted nasally. The curved loop head ensures secure engagement with the plastic tube, while the bar-shaped handle provides ergonomic control. The plastic tube includes markings to guide insertion depth, eliminating the need for visual confirmation of the oropharynx.  

The method involves withdrawing the ENBD catheter from the mouth, inserting the J-Loop into the oral cavity, advancing the marked plastic tube nasally until the designated depth is reached, and pulling the J-Loop to retrieve the plastic tube. The ENBD catheter is then connected to the plastic tube and repositioned nasally. This process eliminates the need for manual retrieval, reduces procedure time, and minimizes injury risks.  

### Advantageous Effects  

The J-Loop device offers significant improvements over conventional techniques:  
1. **Reduced Procedure Time**: Repositioning time is reduced by approximately 77%, from an average of 194 seconds to 44 seconds.  
2. **Enhanced Safety**: Eliminates risks of oral injuries, operator finger trauma, and tooth damage.  
3. **Anatomical Versatility**: Effective regardless of Mallampati score, making it suitable for patients with challenging oropharyngeal anatomy.  
4. **Simplified Workflow**: Does not require patient awakening or manual retrieval, streamlining the procedure.  
5. **Fluoroscopic Compatibility**: The device components are visible under fluoroscopy, aiding in precise placement.  

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The J-Loop device is constructed from medical-grade materials, such as stainless steel or biocompatible polymers, ensuring durability and patient safety. The loop-shaped holder features a curvature optimized for engaging the plastic tube, while the bar handle provides a non-slip grip. The plastic tube is marked at intervals corresponding to typical nasal-to-oral insertion depths, allowing standardized placement without direct visualization.  

In practice, the device is used as follows:  
1. After ERCP, the ENBD catheter is withdrawn from the mouth.  
2. The J-Loop is inserted into the oral cavity, with the loop head positioned near the oropharynx.  
3. The plastic tube is advanced nasally until the predetermined marking aligns with the nostril.  
4. The J-Loop is gently retracted, capturing the plastic tube within the loop head.  
5. The plastic tube is pulled orally, connected to the ENBD catheter, and repositioned nasally.  

The device is particularly advantageous in sedated patients, as it eliminates the need for mouthpiece removal or patient cooperation. Clinical studies demonstrate a 97.3% success rate, with no reported complications. The J-Loop represents a significant advancement in ENBD procedures, combining efficiency, safety, and ease of use.
