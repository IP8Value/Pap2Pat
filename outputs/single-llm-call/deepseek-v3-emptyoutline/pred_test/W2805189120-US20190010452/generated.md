# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and stem cell technology, specifically to methods for producing functional hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells. More particularly, the invention provides a novel three-dimensional culture system for efficiently differentiating human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) into vasopressin (AVP)-secreting hypothalamic neurons, along with other functionally distinct hypothalamic cell populations. The invention further encompasses the resulting cellular compositions and their applications in disease modeling, drug screening, and therapeutic interventions for neuroendocrine disorders such as central diabetes insipidus (CDI).  

## BACKGROUND ART  

The hypothalamus constitutes a critical component of the neuroendocrine system, regulating fundamental physiological processes including body temperature, circadian rhythms, hunger, thirst, and stress responses through its control of pituitary hormone secretion. Within this structure, magnocellular neurons of the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei synthesize arginine vasopressin (AVP), which is transported to and released from the posterior pituitary to regulate water balance. Dysfunction of these AVP-producing neurons leads to central diabetes insipidus (CDI), characterized by polyuria and polydipsia due to impaired water reabsorption in the kidneys.  

Current treatment for CDI relies on administration of desmopressin, a synthetic AVP analog. However, this approach carries risks of hyponatremia and fails to address cases where osmoreceptor dysfunction accompanies AVP deficiency. Cell replacement therapy using functional AVP neurons could provide physiological regulation of water balance, but generating such specialized neurons in vitro has proven challenging. Previous attempts to differentiate hypothalamic neurons from human pluripotent stem cells have achieved limited success, typically yielding low numbers of AVP-positive cells without demonstrated secretory capacity.  

Existing protocols for hypothalamic differentiation often produce heterogeneous cell populations with poor regional specificity. The neural retina and telencephalon frequently emerge as competing differentiation fates due to shared developmental origins with hypothalamic tissue. Furthermore, conventional two-dimensional culture systems fail to recapitulate the three-dimensional architecture and signaling microenvironment essential for proper hypothalamic development. There remains an unmet need for robust, reproducible methods to generate functionally mature hypothalamic neurons, particularly AVP-secreting magnocellular neurons, from human pluripotent stem cells.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The technical problems addressed by the present invention include: (1) the inability of existing protocols to efficiently direct human pluripotent stem cells specifically toward hypothalamic rather than retinal or telencephalic fates; (2) the lack of methods to separately generate dorsal and ventral hypothalamic progenitors with regional specificity; (3) the poor survival and maturation of hypothalamic neurons in conventional culture systems; and (4) the failure of previous approaches to produce AVP neurons capable of regulated peptide secretion.  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention solves these problems through a multi-step differentiation protocol that precisely recapitulates key stages of hypothalamic development. The method employs a serum-free floating culture of embryoid body-like aggregates with quick reaggregation (SFEBq) to establish three-dimensional tissue architecture, combined with timed exposure to specific morphogens that pattern the developing neuroectoderm.  

A critical innovation involves the combined use of bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4) and smoothened agonist (SAG) at defined concentrations to selectively promote hypothalamic rather than retinal or telencephalic differentiation. The invention further provides optimized conditions for generating either dorsal or ventral hypothalamic progenitors by modulating the ratio of these signaling molecules. For dorsal hypothalamic progenitors that give rise to AVP neurons, the protocol includes subsequent culture in a gas-liquid interface system followed by dissociation onto extracellular matrix-coated substrates to promote neuronal maturation.  

The addition of an Akt inhibitor during early differentiation stages enhances rostral identity, increasing the yield of AVP-positive neurons. The resulting cells demonstrate functional maturity, including depolarization-dependent AVP secretion and appropriate responses to physiological stimuli. The invention thus provides the first robust method for generating functional AVP-secreting neurons from human pluripotent stem cells, along with other hypothalamic neuronal subtypes.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### 1. Method for Producing Cellular Structure Including Hypothalamic Tissue  

The method for producing hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells comprises several key steps performed under defined culture conditions. Initially, human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) are maintained on mouse embryonic fibroblast feeder layers in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with knockout serum replacement (KSR), non-essential amino acids, L-glutamine, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and 2-mercaptoethanol. For differentiation, hESC colonies are enzymatically dissociated into single cells using TrypLE Express containing DNase I and the Rho-associated kinase inhibitor Y-27632 to prevent apoptosis.  

The dissociated cells are then reaggregated in low-cell-adhesion 96-well plates with V-bottomed conical wells at a density of 10,000 cells per well in differentiation medium containing growth factor-free chemically defined medium (gfCDM) supplemented with 5% KSR. The differentiation medium further includes non-essential amino acids, sodium pyruvate, and 2-mercaptoethanol, with half-medium changes performed every other day.  

On culture day 6, key patterning factors are added to specify hypothalamic fate. A combination of BMP4 at concentrations between 0.5-2.0 nM and SAG at 0.1-1.0 μM is introduced to the culture medium. These concentrations are critical for suppressing alternative neural fates while promoting hypothalamic differentiation. The BMP4 concentration is maintained until day 12 and then gradually reduced, while SAG treatment continues throughout the differentiation process.  

For enhanced rostral patterning, an Akt inhibitor may be added from day 6 at a concentration of 0.5 μM. This treatment increases the yield of dorsal hypothalamic progenitors that give rise to AVP neurons. The aggregates are maintained in free-floating conditions until day 30-80, with medium composition adjusted over time to promote neuronal maturation.  

### EXAMPLES  

In a representative embodiment, hESCs carrying a RAX::Venus reporter were differentiated using the described protocol. Treatment with 1.5 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG from day 6 yielded aggregates containing over 80% RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors by day 20, as confirmed by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). Immunostaining revealed mixed populations of dorsal (PAX6+/NKX2.1-) and ventral (NKX2.1+/PAX6-) hypothalamic precursors, demonstrating the protocol's capacity to generate regionally specified hypothalamic tissue.  

Further analysis showed that these progenitors expressed neural ectoderm markers including SOX1 and nestin, confirming their neural identity. Time-course studies demonstrated increasing RAX expression from day 20 onward under hypothalamic-inducing conditions, while control cultures lacking BMP4/SAG treatment showed predominant FOXG1 expression indicative of telencephalic differentiation.  

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary  

The invention further provides methods to promote functional maturation of hypothalamic neurons, particularly the AVP-producing magnocellular neurons that normally project to the posterior pituitary. At approximately day 60 of differentiation, aggregates are transferred to a gas-liquid interface culture system using Transwell porous filters in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with glucose, N2 and B27 supplements, and ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF).  

This culture modification dramatically increases the appearance of AVP neuron precursors, with over 30% of cells expressing the markers OTP and BRN2 compared to minimal expression in continuous floating cultures. Additional markers of hypothalamic differentiation including SIM1 and SIM2 become prominently expressed during this stage.  

For terminal differentiation into functional neurons, aggregates are dissociated between days 60-80 and plated on coverslips coated with Matrigel, poly-D-lysine, and laminin in medium containing fetal bovine serum, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurotrophin-3 (NT-3), and the small molecule LM22A-4. This dissociation step proves essential for obtaining mature AVP-secreting neurons, which appear between days 80-150 of culture.  

### 3. Conclusion  

The present invention establishes a robust, reproducible protocol for generating functional hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells. Key advantages include:  

1. High efficiency of hypothalamic differentiation (>80% RAX+ progenitors)  
2. Ability to generate regionally distinct dorsal and ventral hypothalamic populations  
3. Production of multiple functionally distinct hypothalamic neuron types  
4. First demonstration of AVP secretion from stem cell-derived neurons  
5. Physiological responsiveness of derived neurons to depolarizing stimuli  

### 1. Appearance of Hypothalamic AVP Neuron  

Immunocytochemical analysis of cultures differentiated using the dorsal hypothalamic protocol reveals numerous AVP-positive neurons with morphological characteristics of magnocellular neurons, including large soma size (approximately 40×20 μm) and extensive neurite outgrowth. These cells co-express the neuronal marker βIII-tubulin (TUJ1) but not corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), confirming their identity as magnocellular rather than parvocellular neurons.  

The AVP neurons demonstrate appropriate expression of processing enzymes and carrier proteins, as shown by positive staining for neurophysin II and copeptin. Functional maturity is evidenced by depolarization-dependent AVP secretion measured via radioimmunoassay. Basal AVP levels in conditioned media typically range from 1-5 pg/ml, increasing 2-3 fold upon stimulation with high potassium artificial cerebrospinal fluid.  

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary  

The invention also enables generation of ventral hypothalamic neurons, including those producing melanin-concentrating hormone (MCH). Cultures differentiated under ventral conditions (1.5 nM BMP4/1.0 μM SAG) show abundant MCH-positive neurons by day 150. These neurons demonstrate physiological responsiveness, increasing pro-MCH mRNA expression in response to nerve growth factor, dexamethasone, and lithium chloride treatment as measured by quantitative PCR.  

In addition to AVP and MCH neurons, the protocol yields cells expressing other hypothalamic neuropeptides including oxytocin, corticotropin-releasing hormone, thyrotropin-releasing hormone, neuropeptide Y, agouti-related peptide, and pro-opiomelanocortin. This diversity confirms the protocol's capacity to generate multiple functionally distinct hypothalamic cell types within the same culture system.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The hypothalamic tissue production method of the present invention has wide-ranging industrial applications across multiple sectors:  

1. **Regenerative Medicine**: The functional AVP-secreting neurons produced by this method offer a potential cell therapy for central diabetes insipidus. Transplantation of these cells could restore physiological AVP secretion and water balance regulation, overcoming limitations of current desmopressin therapy.  

2. **Disease Modeling**: The invention enables in vitro modeling of hypothalamic disorders including CDI, Prader-Willi syndrome, and hypothalamic obesity. Patient-specific induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived hypothalamic neurons will facilitate study of disease mechanisms and progression.  

3. **Drug Discovery**: The derived hypothalamic neurons provide a human cell-based platform for screening compounds targeting neuroendocrine disorders. The system is particularly valuable for identifying drugs that modulate AVP secretion or function.  

4. **Toxicology**: The technology enables assessment of compound effects on human hypothalamic function, addressing an important gap in neuroendocrine safety pharmacology.  

5. **Research Tools**: The standardized production of human hypothalamic neurons will accelerate basic research into hypothalamic development, function, and connectivity.  

The invention's commercial viability is enhanced by its scalability, reproducibility, and ability to generate functionally mature neurons. Potential business applications include licensing of the differentiation protocol, sale of pre-differentiated hypothalamic cells, and development of customized disease models or screening services for pharmaceutical partners.
