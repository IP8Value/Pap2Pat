## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to wireless communication systems, and more particularly to methods and systems for managing antenna array configurations in millimeter wave (mmWave) band communications. Specifically, the invention pertains to novel beamforming techniques that enable power-efficient operation of user equipment (UE) in 5G and beyond networks while maintaining beam correspondence between downlink and uplink transmissions. The disclosed technology addresses critical challenges related to power consumption, thermal management, and beam management in mmWave communication systems where antenna arrays are employed to achieve high-gain directional beams.  

## BACKGROUND  

In modern wireless communication systems operating in the millimeter wave spectrum, antenna arrays are commonly implemented in user equipment to generate high-gain directional beams that improve signal-to-noise ratio and throughput. Conventional implementations typically utilize complete antenna arrays with all elements active during both transmission and reception operations. However, this approach presents significant drawbacks in terms of power consumption and thermal management, particularly in mobile devices where battery life and temperature control are critical constraints.  

Existing solutions to address overheating in mmWave devices often involve falling back to lower frequency bands (such as sub-6 GHz) when thermal thresholds are approached. While this approach mitigates heating issues, it results in substantial reductions in data rates and introduces additional latency due to the need for frequent switching between frequency bands. Alternative approaches in the prior art have focused on hierarchical codebook designs that utilize partial antenna activation to create wider beams, but these implementations primarily address beamwidth requirements rather than power efficiency and thermal management concerns.  

A significant technical challenge in implementing partial antenna activation schemes involves maintaining beam correspondence between downlink and uplink transmissions. Beam correspondence refers to the condition where the optimal beam directions for reception (downlink) and transmission (uplink) remain aligned. Disruption of this correspondence necessitates additional beam management procedures, resulting in increased power consumption and potential service interruptions. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing novel methods for designing sub-chain beam codebooks that maintain beam correspondence while achieving power efficiency through intelligent antenna activation patterns.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides systems and methods for implementing power-efficient beamforming in mmWave communication systems through selective activation of antenna array elements. The disclosed technology introduces three distinct approaches for designing sub-chain beam codebooks that maintain beam correspondence while reducing power consumption: Similarity Score Maximization (Sim-Max), Spherical Coverage Maximization (SC-Max), and Beam Correspondence Spherical Coverage Maximization (BC-SC-Max).  

In one embodiment, the Sim-Max method generates sub-chain beams that closely resemble the radiation patterns of corresponding full-chain beams, preserving beam correspondence through pattern similarity. The SC-Max method optimizes the spherical coverage of sub-chain codebooks without specific consideration for beam correspondence, while the BC-SC-Max method achieves an optimal balance between spherical coverage maximization and beam correspondence maintenance.  

The invention further discloses apparatus configurations comprising dual-polarization patch antenna arrays with selective activation capabilities, where the number of activated antennas in each polarization is maintained equally. The beamforming vectors are constrained to binary magnitude values (representing antenna activation states) and discrete phase values determined by finite-resolution phase shifters. Various optimization algorithms are employed to solve the non-convex problems associated with beam codebook design, including iterative algorithms that cyclically optimize phase parameters and K-Means clustering techniques for spherical coverage maximization.  

Through comprehensive simulation results, the invention demonstrates that the BC-SC-Max method provides superior performance by maintaining beam correspondence for over 90% of operational scenarios when switching between full-chain and sub-chain configurations (3-5 active antennas), while simultaneously achieving spherical coverage comparable to dedicated coverage maximization approaches. This represents a significant advancement over conventional techniques, enabling extended mmWave operation without thermal shutdowns while maintaining high-quality beamformed communications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The detailed description of the invention begins with an explanation of the system architecture and operational principles. In a preferred embodiment, the user equipment is equipped with two mmWave antenna arrays positioned on opposite edges of the device. Each array comprises multiple dual-polarization patch antenna elements, with typical implementations ranging from 1×4 to 2×2 array configurations. The antenna elements are connected to a common radio frequency (RF) chain, allowing coordinated beamforming operations.  

The beamforming system employs digital control mechanisms to selectively activate subsets of antenna elements according to predefined codebooks. Each beamforming vector is defined with binary magnitude components (0 for deactivated, 1 for activated) and discrete phase components determined by the resolution of implemented phase shifters (typically 3-5 bits). The system maintains equal numbers of activated antennas between orthogonal polarizations to preserve polarization diversity, though the specific activated elements may differ between polarizations.  

For radiation pattern calculation, the invention utilizes a data-driven approach incorporating measured or simulated E-field response data for each antenna element. This data accounts for practical effects including housing interactions and near-field coupling. The composite beam pattern is computed through vector superposition of individual element responses, weighted by the beamforming coefficients.  

The Sim-Max codebook design method formulates the beam similarity optimization as a constrained problem maximizing pattern correlation between full-chain and sub-chain beams. The similarity metric is defined over a spherical sampling grid, with normalization ensuring consistent scoring. To solve the non-convex optimization with discrete constraints, the method employs an exhaustive search over possible antenna activations followed by iterative phase optimization for each candidate activation pattern.  

The SC-Max approach formulates the codebook design as a spherical coverage maximization problem, solved through a modified K-Means algorithm. This algorithm iteratively assigns angular regions to beams and optimizes beam patterns for their assigned regions. The initialization utilizes greedy algorithms to ensure effective starting points for the clustering process.  

The BC-SC-Max method represents a hybrid approach that maximizes coverage within the angular regions corresponding to full-chain beam patterns. This is achieved by partitioning the spherical space according to full-chain beam coverage and independently optimizing sub-chain beams for each partition. The optimization problems are solved using similar techniques to the Sim-Max method, but constrained to relevant angular regions rather than the complete sphere.  

The invention further discloses methods for evaluating and comparing codebook performance. A key metric is the best beam matching rate, which quantifies the probability that optimal beam indices align between different codebook configurations across the spherical space. Additional performance metrics include composite gain cumulative distribution functions and spherical coverage efficiency.  

Implementation aspects include dynamic switching mechanisms between codebook configurations based on thermal and power conditions. The system supports seamless transitions between full-chain and various sub-chain configurations without requiring complete beam reselection when beam correspondence is maintained. Specialized signaling protocols facilitate coordination between user equipment and base stations during configuration changes.  

The detailed description concludes with specific implementation examples derived from electromagnetic simulations. These demonstrate practical performance characteristics including pattern similarity preservation, coverage maintenance, and computational efficiency of the proposed algorithms. The results validate that the BC-SC-Max method provides optimal balance between power efficiency and communication performance, enabling practical implementation in next-generation mmWave devices.
