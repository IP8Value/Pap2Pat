## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics and prognostics, specifically to methods for assessing mortality risk in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD). More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of lipoprotein particle subfraction analysis, including low-density lipoprotein (LDL) particle diameter (LDL-Pd) and subfraction concentrations, as biomarkers for predicting survival outcomes in MHD patients.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of mortality in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients, accounting for approximately half of all deaths in this population. Despite the well-established association between conventional lipid profiles (e.g., LDL cholesterol [LDL-C] and HDL cholesterol [HDL-C]) and atherosclerotic CVD in the general population, these traditional biomarkers fail to predict mortality in MHD patients. This phenomenon, known as the "lipid paradox" or "reverse epidemiology," is characterized by an inverse relationship between serum cholesterol levels and mortality risk in chronic disease states such as chronic kidney disease (CKD) and chronic heart failure (CHF).  

Conventional lipid measurements, including total cholesterol (TC), LDL-C, and HDL-C, are derived from chemical assays that quantify cholesterol content within lipoprotein particles. However, these assays do not account for the heterogeneity of lipoprotein particles, which vary in size, density, and atherogenic potential. Each lipoprotein class comprises a continuous spectrum of particles with distinct metabolic and pathological properties. For instance, LDL particles can be subdivided into very small, small, medium, and large subfractions, with smaller LDL particles being more atherogenic due to their increased susceptibility to oxidation and enhanced endothelial penetration.  

Prior studies in the general population have demonstrated that advanced lipoprotein testing, such as measuring LDL particle concentration (LDL-Pc) and LDL particle diameter (LDL-Pd), provides superior cardiovascular risk stratification compared to conventional lipid profiles. However, such advanced lipoprotein analyses have not been systematically evaluated in MHD patients, a population with unique lipid metabolism and heightened cardiovascular risk.  

There remains an unmet need for reliable biomarkers that can accurately predict mortality risk in MHD patients, enabling targeted interventions to improve survival outcomes. The present invention addresses this need by leveraging novel lipoprotein subfraction analysis to identify high-risk individuals.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for assessing mortality risk in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients by analyzing lipoprotein particle subfractions, particularly low-density lipoprotein (LDL) particle diameter (LDL-Pd) and subfraction concentrations. The invention is based on the discovery that smaller LDL particle size and higher concentrations of very small LDL particles are associated with increased mortality, whereas larger LDL particle size and higher concentrations of large LDL particles correlate with improved survival.  

In one embodiment, the method comprises:  
1. Obtaining a biological sample (e.g., plasma or serum) from an MHD patient.  
2. Analyzing the sample to determine LDL particle diameter (LDL-Pd) and/or LDL subfraction concentrations (e.g., very small, small, medium, and large LDL particles) using ion mobility or other high-resolution techniques.  
3. Classifying the patient's mortality risk based on the measured LDL-Pd and subfraction concentrations, wherein:  
   - Smaller LDL-Pd (below a median threshold, e.g., 216.5 Å) indicates higher mortality risk.  
   - Larger LDL-Pd (above the median threshold) indicates lower mortality risk.  
   - Higher concentrations of very small LDL particles (e.g., highest quartile) indicate higher mortality risk.  
   - Higher concentrations of large LDL particles (e.g., highest quartile) indicate lower mortality risk.  

The method may further include integrating LDL-Pd and subfraction data with conventional lipid profiles (e.g., LDL-C, HDL-C) to enhance risk stratification. For example, patients with above-median LDL-C and above-median LDL-Pd exhibit the lowest mortality risk, whereas those with below-median LDL-Pd and above-median LDL-C show the highest risk.  

The invention also encompasses kits and systems for implementing the method, including reagents and devices for lipoprotein subfraction analysis.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following sections provide a detailed description of the invention, including sample collection, preparation, and analysis protocols, as well as prognostic applications.  

### Sample Collection, Preparation, and Purification  

Biological samples (e.g., plasma or serum) are collected from MHD patients under standardized conditions, preferably in a fasting state. Samples may be archived for later analysis or processed immediately. Lipoproteins are isolated and fractionated using high-resolution techniques such as ion mobility, which separates particles based on size and charge.  

Ion mobility analysis involves:  
1. Introducing the sample into a gas-phase separation chamber.  
2. Applying an electric field to drive lipoprotein particles through a neutral gas.  
3. Detecting and quantifying particles based on their mobility, which correlates with size.  

Subfractions are defined by diameter ranges (in angstroms, Å):  
- Very small LDL: 180.0–208.2 Å  
- Small LDL: 208.2–214.1 Å  
- Medium LDL: 214.1–220.0 Å  
- Large LDL: 220.0–233.3 Å  

### Determining Prognosis  

Mortality risk is assessed by comparing measured LDL-Pd and subfraction concentrations to predefined thresholds or quartiles. Key prognostic indicators include:  
1. **LDL Particle Diameter (LDL-Pd):**  
   - Below median (e.g., <216.5 Å): Higher mortality risk (HR = 2.43 for smallest quartile).  
   - Above median: Lower mortality risk (HR = 0.38 for largest quartile).  
2. **LDL Subfraction Concentrations:**  
   - Very small LDL: Highest quartile associated with 2.43-fold increased mortality.  
   - Large LDL: Highest quartile associated with 62% reduced mortality.  

Multivariate adjustments may be applied to account for confounding factors such as age, comorbidities, inflammation, and nutritional status.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: LDL Particle Diameter and Mortality  

A cohort of 235 MHD patients was followed for 6 years. LDL-Pd was measured using ion mobility, and patients were stratified into quartiles. The highest quartile (largest particles) showed a 62% lower mortality risk (HR = 0.38) compared to the lowest quartile (smallest particles; HR = 2.43).  

### Example 2: Very Small LDL Particles and Mortality  

Patients in the highest quartile of very small LDL particle concentration had a 2.43-fold higher mortality risk than those in the lowest quartile, independent of conventional lipid levels.  

### Example 3: Large LDL Particles and Survival  

Higher concentrations of large LDL particles were associated with improved survival (HR = 0.38). Patients with above-median LDL-C and large LDL-Pd exhibited the lowest mortality risk.  

### Example 4: Integrated Risk Stratification  

Combining LDL-Pd and LDL-C measurements improved risk prediction. Patients with below-median LDL-Pd and above-median LDL-C had the highest mortality risk, whereas those with above-median LDL-Pd and LDL-C had the lowest risk.  

## SUMMARY OF RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

The invention demonstrates that advanced lipoprotein subfraction analysis, particularly LDL particle diameter and subfraction concentrations, provides superior mortality risk stratification in MHD patients compared to conventional lipid profiles. Key findings include:  
1. Smaller LDL-Pd and higher very small LDL concentrations predict increased mortality.  
2. Larger LDL-Pd and higher large LDL concentrations predict improved survival.  
3. Integrating LDL-Pd with conventional lipid measures enhances prognostic accuracy.  

These findings support the clinical utility of lipoprotein subfraction analysis in MHD patients and suggest that modulating LDL particle size may improve outcomes. Further studies, including randomized trials, are warranted to validate these biomarkers and explore therapeutic interventions.
