# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The field of cerebrovascular diagnostics has long relied on Transcranial Doppler ultrasound (TCD) as a noninvasive methodology for measuring Cerebral Blood Flow Velocity (CBFV) through major cerebral arteries. The pulsatile CBFV waveform contains critical morphological information indicative of various cerebrovascular pathologies, including acute ischemic stroke caused by Large Vessel Occlusions (LVO). While existing diagnostic frameworks such as the Thrombolysis in Brain Ischemia (TIBI) flow grading system demonstrate clinical utility for LVO detection, these methods suffer from inherent subjectivity that limits their practical application. The TIBI grading scale requires expert human interpretation of subtle waveform characteristics, including peak morphology and systolic timing patterns, which cannot be reliably assessed by non-specialists. Furthermore, conventional quantitative metrics such as the Pulsatility Index lack sufficient granularity to distinguish between pathological waveform types. This creates an unmet clinical need for an objective, automated system capable of classifying TCD waveforms according to their morphological signatures while maintaining the diagnostic specificity of expert TIBI grading.  

Prior attempts at waveform classification have been constrained by reliance on predefined morphological assumptions that fail to account for the full spectrum of pathological waveform variations. Existing approaches typically require identifiable waveform peaks that may be absent or distorted in LVO cases, limiting their diagnostic scope. Moreover, current methods do not systematically evaluate whether established TIBI categories comprehensively represent the natural variance in empirical waveform data. The present invention addresses these limitations through a novel data-driven clustering methodology that objectively identifies distinct waveform archetypes without presupposing specific morphological features. This represents a significant advancement over prior art by enabling automated detection of both known and previously unclassified pathological waveform patterns.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a computerized system and method for automated classification of Transcranial Doppler (TCD) waveforms using unsupervised machine learning techniques. The system processes cerebral blood flow velocity signals to extract three key morphological features: temporal onset of maximal velocity (onset), duration of systolic complex (canopy), and peak/trough prominence (peaks). These features are derived from normalized waveform data and transformed into a three-dimensional feature space suitable for spectral clustering analysis.  

A critical innovation involves the application of gap statistic methodology to determine the optimal number of waveform clusters without relying on predefined categorical frameworks. The system automatically identifies distinct waveform archetypes through spectral clustering of the feature space, generating morphological templates that include both known TIBI analogues and novel pathological patterns. Four primary cluster types emerge from this analysis: Type I (early systolic maximum with normal peaks), Type II (delayed systolic maximum with normal peaks), Type III (early systolic maximum with suppressed peaks), and Type IV (delayed systolic maximum with suppressed peaks). These automated classifications demonstrate strong correspondence with clinical outcomes, wherein Type III waveforms show 95% association with LVO cases while Type I waveforms predominantly occur in control subjects.  

The invention further provides representative waveform archetypes for each cluster through ensemble averaging of the most characteristic member waveforms. These archetypes serve as reference templates for automated classification of new TCD recordings. The system outputs diagnostic indicators based on cluster membership probabilities, enabling objective assessment of waveform pathology without requiring expert interpretation. This represents a substantial improvement over existing methods by providing reproducible, quantitative classifications that capture the full spectrum of waveform morphology while maintaining clinical relevance.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The detailed implementation of the invention comprises several interconnected subsystems that transform raw TCD signals into classified waveform outputs. The system begins with signal acquisition using standard 2MHz handheld Doppler probes positioned to insonate the middle cerebral arteries at depths between 45-60mm. Recordings are obtained in 30-second intervals to capture sufficient cardiac cycles for analysis, typically yielding ensembles of at least 15 individual beat waveforms per recording location.  

Signal processing modules implement advanced algorithms for beat segmentation and quality control. An automated beat identification algorithm detects systolic onsets to demarcate individual waveforms, while iterative interquartile range exclusion removes artifact-contaminated beats based on cross-correlation and duration criteria. Accepted beats undergo temporal alignment at systolic upstroke followed by length normalization through zero-padding to the longest beat duration. The system generates representative average waveforms for each recording location through ensemble averaging, applying cubic spline interpolation to resample all waveforms to 100 normalized time points. Velocity normalization rescales waveforms to a [0,1] range by subtracting the diastolic minimum and dividing by the resultant systolic maximum.  

The feature extraction subsystem computes three key morphological parameters from each normalized waveform. The onset feature quantifies the normalized time at which maximal velocity occurs, calculated as the argument of the velocity maximum within the cardiac cycle. The canopy feature measures the duration of the systolic complex by counting samples where velocity exceeds 25% of the normalized range between diastolic minimum and systolic maximum. The peaks feature employs a weighted summation approach to quantify peak/trough prominence, combining binary weights for true extrema (derivative zero-crossings) with fractional weights for pseudo-extrema where the derivative magnitude falls below 0.01 of the normalized range.  

For cluster analysis, the system applies z-score normalization to each feature dimension before spectral clustering using a radial basis function kernel. The optimal cluster count is determined through gap statistic analysis comparing observed intra-cluster dispersion to reference distributions generated via singular value decomposition bootstrap sampling. Cluster assignments are validated through examination of archetype waveforms constructed by averaging the five most representative members of each cluster based on intra-cluster Euclidean distances.  

The classification output module generates diagnostic indicators by comparing novel waveforms to established cluster archetypes. The system computes feature vectors for new inputs and assigns cluster membership probabilities based on proximity in the normalized feature space. Clinical interpretations are provided according to known associations between cluster types and pathology - for example, high probability of Type III membership triggers LVO suspicion due to the 95% prevalence among confirmed occlusion cases.  

The invention further includes specialized processing for edge cases such as non-pulsatile waveforms that may indicate complete vessel occlusion. Supplemental modules can incorporate additional hemodynamic parameters including velocity asymmetries and spectral turbulence indices to enhance classification specificity. The system architecture supports continuous refinement through machine learning as additional validated cases are incorporated into the reference database.  

This comprehensive implementation provides the first objective framework for TCD waveform classification that matches the diagnostic sensitivity of expert TIBI grading while eliminating interpretation variability. The data-driven approach captures the full morphological spectrum of pathological waveforms, including previously unclassified patterns that may indicate specific stroke etiologies. By automating what has traditionally required specialized expertise, the invention dramatically expands access to advanced cerebrovascular diagnostics across clinical settings.
