# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunomodulation and allergy management. Specifically, it pertains to the use of a probiotic bacterial strain, *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 (deposited as CNCM I-4154), for the management of food allergy symptoms in sensitized individuals. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising said bacterial strain and methods for their use in reducing allergic responses mediated by Th2-associated cytokines and chemokines, particularly in the gastrointestinal tract.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Allergic diseases have seen a dramatic increase in prevalence over recent decades, with food allergies posing significant health challenges, particularly in pediatric populations. Current approaches to allergy management focus primarily on allergen avoidance and symptomatic treatment, with limited options for modulating the underlying immune dysfunction. The pathogenesis of food allergies involves a complex interplay of immune components, notably the activation of Th2-type CD4+ T cells and subsequent production of cytokines including IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13.  

While probiotics have been investigated for their potential in allergy prevention, results have been inconsistent across studies, likely due to strain-specific effects and variations in clinical settings. Moreover, the differential effects of probiotic interventions during distinct phases of allergic responses—namely prevention of sensitization versus management of established allergies—have remained poorly understood. There exists a significant unmet need for targeted interventions that can effectively modulate established allergic responses without suppressing the entire immune system.  

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:  

Allergic disease management can be approached at two distinct levels: primary prevention aimed at avoiding initial sensitization to allergens, and secondary intervention targeting the management of established allergic responses in sensitized individuals. The present invention demonstrates that *L. lactis* NCC 2287 exerts its beneficial effects specifically at the secondary intervention level, showing remarkable efficacy in managing allergic symptoms in already sensitized subjects while displaying no significant preventive effect when administered during the sensitization phase.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel use of *Lactococcus lactis* strain NCC 2287 (CNCM I-4154) for the management of food allergy symptoms in sensitized individuals. The bacterial strain demonstrates a unique capacity to reduce allergic manifestations when administered after sensitization has occurred, without affecting the sensitization process itself. This effect is mediated through localized modulation of Th2-type immune responses in the gastrointestinal tract, particularly through reduction of IL-13 production and downregulation of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC) in the ileum.  

The invention encompasses:  
1. A method for managing food allergy symptoms in sensitized subjects by administering an effective amount of *L. lactis* NCC 2287  
2. Pharmaceutical and nutritional compositions comprising *L. lactis* NCC 2287 for use in allergy management  
3. Specific dosing regimens and administration protocols for optimal therapeutic effect  
4. Methods for monitoring therapeutic efficacy through measurement of specific immunological parameters  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Effect of the Composition:  

Administration of *L. lactis* NCC 2287 to sensitized subjects results in significant reduction of allergic symptoms upon allergen challenge. This therapeutic effect is achieved without altering systemic markers of allergic sensitization such as allergen-specific IgE, IgG1, or mast cell protease (MMCP-1) levels. Instead, the composition acts through localized modulation of immune responses in the gastrointestinal tract, particularly in the ileum, where it significantly reduces:  
- Production of IL-13 by antigen-restimulated mesenteric lymph node (MLN) cells  
- Gene expression levels of IL-13  
- Expression of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC)  

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:  

The active component of the invention is *Lactococcus lactis* strain NCC 2287, deposited at the Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes (CNCM) under accession number I-4154. The composition may comprise:  
- Viable bacterial cells at a concentration of at least 1×10^8 CFU per dose  
- Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients  
- Nutritional components suitable for oral administration  
- Stabilizing agents to maintain bacterial viability  

The composition may be formulated as:  
- Lyophilized powder for reconstitution  
- Liquid suspension  
- Enteric-coated capsules  
- Food or beverage products containing the probiotic strain  

### Target Group:  

The invention is particularly suited for:  
- Individuals with established food allergies  
- Subjects sensitized to common food allergens (e.g., milk, eggs, wheat)  
- Pediatric populations with food allergy manifestations  
- Patients with gastrointestinal symptoms associated with food allergies  

## Experimental Results, Section 1:  

In vivo studies using a murine model of ovalbumin (OVA)-induced food allergy demonstrated that *L. lactis* NCC 2287 administration during the management phase (after sensitization but before challenge) significantly reduced clinical allergy scores compared to positive controls (1.3 ± 0.37 vs 2.5 ± 0.31, p = 0.038). In contrast, administration during the prevention phase (before and during sensitization) showed no significant effect on clinical scores (1.7 ± 0.42 vs 2.5 ± 0.31, p = 0.18), establishing the strain's specific efficacy in managing established allergies rather than preventing sensitization.  

## Experimental Results, Section 2:  

Immunological analyses revealed that the beneficial effects occurred without significant changes in systemic allergic markers:  
- OVA-specific IgE levels: 2054 ± 1281 pg/mL (management) vs 1674 ± 551 pg/mL (control), p ≥ 0.3  
- OVA-specific IgG1 levels: 558 ± 238 pg/mL (management) vs 535 ± 192 pg/mL (control), p ≥ 0.3  
- MMCP-1 levels: 773 ± 298 pg/mL (management) vs 1107 ± 322 pg/mL (control), p = 0.48  

These results indicate that *L. lactis* NCC 2287 acts through mechanisms distinct from modulation of humoral immunity or mast cell activation.  

## Analytical Methods:  

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:  

*L. lactis* NCC 2287 biomass was produced by culturing the strain under standard conditions, harvesting cells 3 hours after entering stationary phase, washing in PBS, and storing in 20% glycerol at -80°C. Bacterial counts were verified by plating on appropriate media.  

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:  

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from human donors by density gradient centrifugation. Cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and appropriate cytokines to induce Th2 polarization.  

### Cytokine ELISA:  

Cytokine levels (IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, IL-13) were measured in cell culture supernatants using commercial ELISA kits according to manufacturers' protocols. Optical densities were measured at 450 nm and concentrations calculated using standard curves.  

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:  

Data were analyzed using appropriate statistical software (R 2.2.1). Group comparisons were made using Kruskal-Wallis tests followed by Wilcoxon tests with Bonferroni-Sidak correction. P-values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant.  

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:  

Total RNA was extracted from intestinal tissues using SV Total RNA Isolation System. Reverse transcription was performed using Multiscribe Reverse Transcriptase. Gene expression was quantified by real-time PCR on an ABI-Prism 7900HT, normalized to housekeeping genes (β-actin, GAPDH, HPRT), and expressed as relative quantities using the 2-ΔCt method.  

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:  

### Serum MMCP-1:  

Mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1) was measured in serum by ELISA. Positive control mice showed significantly elevated MMCP-1 (1107 ± 322 pg/mL) versus negative controls (3.8 ± 2.2 pg/mL, p ≤ 0.001). *L. lactis* NCC 2287 administration did not significantly alter MMCP-1 levels (773 ± 298 pg/mL in management phase, p = 0.48 vs control).  

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:  

MLN cells were homogenized, washed, and cultured (3×10^5 cells/well) with or without OVA (1 mg/mL). After 72 hours, plates were frozen for subsequent cytokine analysis.  

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:  

MLN cells from *L. lactis*-treated mice in management phase showed significantly reduced IL-13 production (p = 0.08) compared to positive controls, while IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10 levels remained unchanged.  

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:  

Ileum samples from *L. lactis*-treated mice showed:  
- 10-fold reduction in IL-13 gene expression (124 ± 62 vs 992.6 ± 360.2 in controls, p = 0.015)  
- Significant decreases in CCL11 (5172 ± 1341 vs 24904 ± 6797, p = 0.002)  
- Significant decreases in CCL17 (1716 ± 521 vs 3943 ± 888, p = 0.04)  

No significant changes were observed in jejunum samples.
