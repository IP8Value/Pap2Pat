# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of veterinary virology and vaccinology, particularly to recombinant viral vectors derived from Gallid herpesvirus 3 (GaHV-3; MDV-2) for use as vaccines against avian diseases. More specifically, the invention provides recombinant GaHV-3 vectors engineered to express heterologous antigens, such as the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV), methods for producing such vectors, and their use in vaccines for protecting poultry against multiple pathogens.  

## BACKGROUND  

Marek's disease virus (MDV-1) or Gallid herpesvirus 2 is the causative agent of Marek's disease (MD), a highly contagious avian disease characterized by lymphomatous tumors and neurological symptoms. Control of MD has been achieved through vaccination using live attenuated strains, including MDV-1 (Rispens strain), MDV-2 (GaHV-3; SB-1 strain), and herpesvirus of turkeys (HVT). Among these, HVT has been widely utilized as a recombinant vector for expressing protective antigens against other avian pathogens, such as infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV). However, interference between multiple HVT-based vaccines limits their combined use, necessitating alternative vector platforms.  

GaHV-3 (SB-1 strain) has been used as a bivalent vaccine in combination with HVT, demonstrating synergistic effects in inducing protection against MD. Despite its proven efficacy, GaHV-3 has not been extensively explored as a recombinant vector for other avian diseases. The present invention addresses this gap by providing recombinant GaHV-3 vectors expressing heterologous antigens, such as IBDV VP2, and demonstrating their utility in inducing protective immunity without interference with existing vaccines.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 (GaHV-3; MDV-2) vector comprising an expression cassette encoding a heterologous antigen, such as the VP2 protein of IBDV. The expression cassette is inserted into one or more loci within the GaHV-3 genome, including but not limited to UL3/4, UL10/11, and UL21/22. The recombinant vector retains its ability to replicate in vitro and in vivo while expressing the heterologous antigen at levels sufficient to induce an immune response.  

The invention further encompasses methods for producing the recombinant GaHV-3 vector, including the use of bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC)-based recombineering techniques. Vaccines comprising the recombinant vector are also provided, along with methods for their use in immunizing poultry against target pathogens, such as IBDV. The vaccines may be administered alone or in combination with other avian herpesvirus vaccines, such as HVT, to induce complementary immune responses without interference.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV-3; MDV-2) Vector  

The recombinant GaHV-3 vector of the invention is derived from the SB-1 strain and engineered to express one or more heterologous antigens. The vector retains the genomic organization and essential genes of the parental virus while incorporating an expression cassette for the heterologous antigen. The expression cassette comprises a promoter, such as the murine cytomegalovirus immediate early protein 2 (mCMV IE2) promoter, the coding sequence of the heterologous antigen (e.g., IBDV VP2), and a polyadenylation signal, such as the SV40 early polyadenylation signal.  

The vector is constructed using a BAC clone of the GaHV-3 genome, enabling precise manipulation of the viral genome. The expression cassette is inserted into intergenic regions, such as UL3/4, UL10/11, or UL21/22, to minimize disruption of essential viral functions. The resulting recombinant virus is reconstituted by transfection of the modified BAC DNA into permissive cells, such as chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEFs), followed by propagation to generate high-titer virus stocks.  

### An Intermediate Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV-3; MDV-2) Vector Comprising One or More Marker(s)  

During the construction of the recombinant GaHV-3 vector, an intermediate vector comprising a selectable marker, such as the galactokinase (galK) gene, is generated. The galK cassette is inserted into the target locus using recombineering techniques, and positive clones are selected based on their ability to metabolize galactose. The galK cassette is subsequently replaced with the heterologous antigen expression cassette, and successful recombination is confirmed by PCR and sequencing.  

### Methods for Producing a Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV-3; MDV-2) Vector  

The method for producing the recombinant GaHV-3 vector comprises the following steps:  
1. **Insertion of a selectable marker**: The galK cassette is inserted into the target locus (e.g., UL3/4, UL10/11, or UL21/22) in the GaHV-3 BAC clone using recombineering.  
2. **Selection of positive clones**: Clones capable of utilizing galactose as a carbon source are selected.  
3. **Replacement with the expression cassette**: The galK cassette is replaced with the heterologous antigen expression cassette using counter-selection with 2-deoxy-galactose.  
4. **Reconstitution of the recombinant virus**: The modified BAC DNA is transfected into permissive cells (e.g., CEFs) to reconstitute the recombinant virus.  
5. **Propagation and titration**: The recombinant virus is passaged in CEFs to generate high-titer stocks, and virus titers are determined by plaque assay.  

### Vaccines  

The invention provides vaccines comprising the recombinant GaHV-3 vector and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The vaccines may be formulated for subcutaneous, intramuscular, or in ovo administration. The recombinant vector is present at a dose sufficient to induce an immune response, typically between 1,000 and 5,000 plaque-forming units (PFU) per dose. The vaccines may be used alone or in combination with other avian herpesvirus vaccines, such as HVT, to induce protection against multiple pathogens.  

### Use of the Vaccine  

The vaccine is administered to poultry, such as chickens, to induce immunity against target pathogens, such as IBDV. Vaccination may be performed in ovo or post-hatch, with a single dose sufficient to elicit protective immune responses. The vaccine induces neutralizing antibodies and cellular immune responses, providing protection against virulent challenge. The recombinant GaHV-3 vector does not interfere with the immunogenicity of other avian herpesvirus vaccines, making it suitable for use in multivalent vaccination strategies.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Cell Culture and Virus Propagation  

Primary chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEFs) were prepared from 9–10-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) embryos and maintained in E199 media supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum. DF-1 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) with 10% serum. Recombinant GaHV-3 viruses were propagated in CEFs, and virus titers were determined by plaque assay.  

### Virus Growth Curve Studies  

Confluent CEFs were infected with recombinant GaHV-3 viruses (SB-1-UL3/4VP2, SB-1-UL10/11VP2, or SB-1-UL21/22VP2) at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01. Infected cells were harvested at 0, 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, and 120 hours post-infection, and virus titers were determined by plaque assay. Growth curves revealed that all recombinant viruses replicated efficiently, with minor reductions in titer compared to the parental SB-1 virus at later time points.  

### Serum Neutralization Test  

Serum samples from vaccinated chickens were heat-inactivated and serially diluted. Dilutions were incubated with 100 TCID50 of IBDV strain D78, and the mixture was added to DF-1 monolayers. Neutralizing antibody titers were determined as the highest serum dilution preventing cytopathic effect. Vaccinated birds developed neutralizing antibodies by 2 weeks post-vaccination, with titers peaking at 4 weeks.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Virus growth kinetics and neutralizing antibody titers were analyzed using two-way ANOVA with multiple comparisons. Survival rates post-challenge were compared using the Mantel-Cox test. Significant differences (p < 0.05) were observed in virus titers at 72 and 120 hours post-infection and in neutralizing antibody titers between vaccine groups.  

### Analysis of Different Loci to Insert an Expression Cassette  

Three loci (UL3/4, UL10/11, and UL21/22) were evaluated for insertion of the VP2 expression cassette. Recombinant viruses were generated, and VP2 expression was confirmed by immunofluorescence and Western blot. The UL3/4 locus supported the highest VP2 expression, while the UL10/11 locus had the least impact on adjacent gene expression.  

### Example 2  

One-day-old SPF chicks were vaccinated with SB-1-UL3/4VP2 or SB-1-UL21/22VP2 (1,000 PFU/dose) or a commercial HVT-IBDV vaccine. Birds were challenged with virulent IBDV strain UK661 at 4 weeks post-vaccination. All vaccinated birds survived, while unvaccinated controls succumbed to infection.  

### Example 3  

Gene expression analysis by real-time PCR revealed that VP2 insertion at UL3/4 reduced UL3 and UL4 transcript levels by 4-fold, while insertion at UL21/22 increased UL21 expression 4-fold and decreased UL22 expression 2-fold.  

### Vaccine Development Against IBDV: Immunization Study  

A dose-ranging study demonstrated that 1,000 PFU of SB-1-UL3/4VP2 induced complete protection against IBDV challenge, comparable to a commercial HVT-IBDV vaccine. The recombinant GaHV-3 vector is thus a viable platform for bivalent vaccines against MD and IBDV.
