# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD  
The present invention relates generally to neural interface devices and methods for fabricating three-dimensional electrode arrays. More specifically, the invention pertains to high-resolution 3D-printed neural recording and stimulation electrodes fabricated via two-photon lithography and integrated with thin-film substrates. The invention further encompasses biomimetic electrode geometries, porous electrode structures, and methods for laser ablation of insulation layers to expose conductive surfaces at micron-scale precision.  

## BACKGROUND  
Neural interfaces capable of recording and stimulating neuronal activity are critical tools for neuroscience research and clinical applications. Conventional microelectrode arrays are typically fabricated using planar lithographic techniques, limiting their ability to conform to three-dimensional anatomical structures or achieve high-density volumetric sampling. Existing methods for creating three-dimensional electrode structures face significant challenges in resolution, fabrication speed, and integration with flexible substrates.  

Traditional approaches to neural electrode fabrication rely on silicon-based processes that produce rigid structures with limited geometric flexibility. While these devices have proven useful for certain applications, their planar nature restricts their ability to interface with complex neural architectures. Furthermore, existing methods for creating three-dimensional electrode features often require multiple alignment steps and specialized equipment, increasing fabrication complexity and cost.  

The need for high-density three-dimensional electrode arrays is particularly acute in small animal neuroscience research, where the combination of small anatomical dimensions and dense neural packing demands electrodes with micron-scale precision. Current solutions often require trade-offs between electrode density, mechanical stability, and tissue compatibility. There remains an unmet need for fabrication methods that can produce customized three-dimensional electrode geometries at high resolution while maintaining compatibility with standard microfabrication processes.  

## SUMMARY  
The present invention provides systems and methods for fabricating three-dimensional neural electrode arrays using high-resolution two-photon lithography. The invention encompasses a complete fabrication process that integrates 3D-printed polymer structures with thin-film substrates, followed by metallization and precision laser ablation to create functional electrode arrays.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A wafer-scale fabrication process that combines conventional thin-film techniques with high-speed two-photon lithography for creating three-dimensional electrode structures.  
2. Biomimetic electrode geometries, including mosquito-inspired needle designs that reduce insertion forces and enable penetration through the dura mater.  
3. Porous three-dimensional electrode structures that increase surface area for improved charge injection capacity in stimulation applications.  
4. An integrated laser ablation system that precisely removes insulation layers to expose conductive surfaces at micron-scale resolution.  
5. Methods for real-time monitoring of polymerization and surface localization during the 3D printing process.  

The fabrication process begins with standard thin-film deposition on a substrate, followed by patterning of conductive traces. Three-dimensional polymer structures are then printed directly onto the substrate using two-photon lithography with a customized photoresist formulation. After metallization of the printed structures, an insulation layer is applied and selectively removed at electrode tips using femtosecond laser ablation. The process concludes with device singulation and connector attachment.  

The resulting electrode arrays exhibit several advantages over conventional devices, including:  
- Customizable three-dimensional geometries that can be tailored to specific anatomical targets  
- High aspect ratio structures with micron-scale resolution  
- Improved tissue compatibility through biomimetic designs  
- Enhanced charge injection capacity through porous architectures  
- Seamless integration with flexible substrates for chronic implantation  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  
The present invention provides a comprehensive system for fabricating three-dimensional neural electrode arrays with micron-scale precision. The following detailed description outlines the key components and processes that enable this advanced fabrication method.  

**Substrate Preparation and Trace Patterning**  
The fabrication process begins with preparation of a substrate, typically a silicon wafer with thermal oxide layer. A base layer of polyimide is applied and cured to create a flexible foundation for the electrode array. A chromium sacrificial layer is then deposited and patterned using photolithography to define the conductive traces and electrode sites. This chromium layer serves as both an etch mask and a lift-off layer in subsequent processing steps.  

**Two-Photon Lithography System**  
The core of the invention involves a custom two-photon lithography system capable of high-speed 3D printing. The system employs a femtosecond pulsed laser (780 nm wavelength, 100 fs pulse width, 80 MHz repetition rate) focused through a high-numerical-aperture objective lens (20x, NA 0.7). A resonant scanning mirror enables rapid beam positioning, achieving print speeds approximately 10-100 times faster than conventional galvanometer-based systems.  

**Photoresist Formulation**  
A specialized photoresist formulation is used for the 3D printing process. The formulation comprises:  
- A base of OrmoComp® photoresist (Micro Resist Technology)  
- 2,4,6-trimethyl benzoyl phosphine oxide (TPO) as photoinitiator  
- 3,5-Di-tertbutyl-4-hydroxytoluene (BHT) as stabilizing agent  
- Fluorescein for in situ fluorescence imaging  

This combination provides excellent print resolution, mechanical stability, and biocompatibility while enabling real-time monitoring of the polymerization process.  

**3D Printing Process**  
Electrode structures are designed in CAD software and converted to STL files for printing. The printing process involves:  
1. Applying liquid photoresist to the prepared substrate  
2. Immersing the microscope objective in the photoresist  
3. Directly writing 3D structures by focusing the laser at programmed positions within the photoresist  
4. Real-time monitoring of polymerization through fluorescence imaging  

Following printing, the structures are developed in Ormodev solution for 12 hours to remove unpolymerized resist, then rinsed in isopropanol. A post-print UV cure (395 nm, 2.8 mW/mm² for 10 minutes) enhances crosslinking and mechanical stability.  

**Metallization Process**  
The 3D-printed polymer structures are metallized via sputter deposition of titanium (15 nm adhesion layer) and platinum (200 nm conductive layer). The sputtering process coats both the printed structures and the predefined traces simultaneously. The chromium sacrificial layer is then lifted off in heated chromium etchant, leaving behind the patterned metal traces and metallized 3D structures.  

**Insulation and Tip Exposure**  
A conformal parylene C insulation layer (3 μm thick) is deposited over the entire device. The insulation is selectively removed at electrode tips using a femtosecond laser ablation process (1035 nm wavelength, 40 μJ max pulse energy). This ablation process:  
- Precisely removes insulation from multiple electrodes simultaneously  
- Maintains micron-scale resolution  
- Minimizes thermal damage to surrounding structures  
- Can be monitored through emitted UV fluorescence  

**Biomimetic Electrode Designs**  
The invention includes several novel electrode geometries enabled by the high-resolution printing process:  

1. **Mosquito-Inspired Needle Electrodes**: These feature sharp, beveled tips with sub-micron radius of curvature, modeled after mosquito proboscis morphology. The design reduces insertion forces by an order of magnitude compared to flat-tipped electrodes and enables penetration through intact dura mater.  

2. **Porous Electrode Structures**: Three-dimensional electrodes with internal porosity ranging from 40 μm² to 400 μm² cross-sectional area. These structures provide increased surface area for improved charge injection capacity while maintaining overall device dimensions.  

3. **Custom Depth Profiles**: Electrodes can be printed with varying heights and shapes to match specific anatomical targets, such as curved cortical surfaces or layered neural structures.  

**Device Finalization**  
Completed devices are released from the substrate by laser cutting through the polyimide and parylene layers. Electrical connections are made using anisotropic conductive film to bond flex cables to standard neural interface connectors (e.g., Omnetics connectors).  

**Electrochemical Performance**  
The fabricated electrodes demonstrate excellent electrochemical characteristics:  
- Impedance of approximately 200 kΩ at 1 kHz for recording electrodes  
- Charge storage capacity increased by 2× for solid 3D structures compared to planar electrodes  
- Stable cyclic voltammetry profiles in physiological saline  

**Neural Recording Capabilities**  
Prototype devices have successfully recorded:  
- Single-unit action potentials with signal-to-noise ratios exceeding 8:1  
- Local field potentials synchronized to respiratory rhythms  
- Stable signals in both anesthetized and awake behaving animals  

The invention represents a significant advance in neural interface technology by combining high-resolution 3D fabrication with standard microfabrication processes. The resulting devices enable new capabilities in neural recording and stimulation, particularly for small animal research and potential clinical applications requiring high-density three-dimensional electrode arrays.
