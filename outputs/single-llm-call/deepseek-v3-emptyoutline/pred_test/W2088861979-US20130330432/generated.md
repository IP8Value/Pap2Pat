# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic compositions for the treatment of metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and associated hyperlipidemia. More specifically, the invention pertains to an aqueous extract derived from a combination of fruit and root of *Curculigo latifolia* (Dryand, ex W. T. Aiton), a plant belonging to the Hypoxidaceae family, for use in ameliorating insulin resistance, enhancing glucose uptake, and regulating lipid metabolism. The invention further encompasses methods of preparing the extract, pharmaceutical formulations containing the extract, and therapeutic applications thereof in the management of diabetes and related complications.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel therapeutic composition comprising an aqueous extract of *Curculigo latifolia* fruit and root, wherein the fruit and root are combined in a ratio of 1:1. The extract exhibits significant antidiabetic and hypolipidemic properties by modulating the expression of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism, including but not limited to insulin receptor substrate 1 (IRS1), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF1), glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4), peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors alpha and gamma (PPARα and PPARγ), adiponectin receptors 1 and 2 (AdipoR1 and AdipoR2), leptin, lipoprotein lipase (LPL), and lipase.  

The extract is prepared by macerating fresh *Curculigo latifolia* fruit and powdered root separately in distilled water, followed by lyophilization of the combined filtrate. The resultant composition demonstrates dose-dependent efficacy in reducing hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, and oxidative stress in diabetic subjects. Notably, the extract outperforms conventional antidiabetic drugs such as glibenclamide in improving insulin sensitivity and adiponectin secretion while mitigating adverse effects such as weight gain and hepatotoxicity.  

The invention further includes a method of treating type 2 diabetes mellitus and associated metabolic dysfunctions in a subject, comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract. The preferred dosage ranges from 50 mg/kg to 200 mg/kg body weight, with optimal results observed at 200 mg/kg. The extract may be formulated into oral dosage forms, including tablets, capsules, or syrups, optionally combined with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The following embodiments illustrate the preparation, characterization, and therapeutic efficacy of the *Curculigo latifolia* fruit:root extract. These examples are not intended to limit the scope of the invention but to provide a comprehensive understanding of its applications.  

### Example 1  

**Preparation of *Curculigo latifolia* Fruit:Root Extract**  

Fresh *Curculigo latifolia* fruits were harvested, cleaned, and mashed using a mortar and pestle. Separately, dried roots were ground into a fine powder. Fifty grams of mashed fruit was extracted with 2000 mL of distilled water under continuous stirring for 24 hours at room temperature. Concurrently, 50 g of root powder was soaked in 2000 mL of distilled water under identical conditions. The resultant mixtures were filtered through Whatman No. 1 filter paper, and the filtrates were combined in a 1:1 ratio. The combined aqueous extract was lyophilized to obtain a dry powder, which was stored at −80°C until use.  

### Example 2  

**Animal Model Induction and Treatment Protocol**  

Male Sprague-Dawley rats (160–180 g) were divided into seven groups:  
- **Group 1**: Normal rats fed a standard diet.  
- **Group 2**: Obese rats fed a high-fat diet (HFD) for 4 weeks.  
- **Group 3**: Diabetic rats induced by HFD and streptozotocin (STZ, 40 mg/kg).  
- **Groups 4–6**: Diabetic rats treated with 50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg of *Curculigo latifolia* extract, respectively.  
- **Group 7**: Diabetic rats treated with glibenclamide (10 mg/kg).  

After 4 weeks of treatment, biochemical parameters (glucose, lipids, insulin, adiponectin) and gene expression profiles were analyzed.  

### Example 3  

**Hypoglycemic and Hypolipidemic Effects**  

The extract significantly reduced fasting blood glucose levels in diabetic rats, with the highest reduction (64.5%) observed at 200 mg/kg. Plasma total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL decreased by 56.2%, 52.6%, and 75.4%, respectively, while HDL increased by 51%. These effects were superior to glibenclamide, which showed a 32.4% reduction in glucose and lesser lipid modulation.  

### Example 4  

**Gene Expression Modulation**  

The extract upregulated IRS1, IGF1, GLUT4, PPARα, PPARγ, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, leptin, LPL, and lipase genes in muscle and adipose tissues. For instance, GLUT4 expression increased by 2.5-fold in treated rats, facilitating glucose uptake. PPARγ activation mirrored the action of thiazolidinediones, enhancing insulin sensitivity.  

### Example 5  

**Safety and Hepatoprotective Effects**  

The extract normalized elevated levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), γ-glutamyltransferase (GGT), urea, and creatinine, indicating no hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity. In contrast, glibenclamide showed marginal improvements in these markers.  

### Example 6  

**Pharmaceutical Formulation**  

The lyophilized extract was formulated into 500 mg tablets containing:  
- *Curculigo latifolia* extract: 200 mg  
- Microcrystalline cellulose: 150 mg  
- Lactose: 100 mg  
- Magnesium stearate: 50 mg  

The tablets exhibited stability under accelerated conditions (40°C, 75% RH) for 6 months, with no loss of potency.
