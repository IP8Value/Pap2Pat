# DESCRIPTION  

## EXAMPLE 1. DECONSTRUCTION OF BIOMASS AND EXTRACTION OF METALS IN VARIOUS IONIC LIQUIDS  

The present invention provides a method for deconstructing lignocellulosic biomass and extracting metals using ionic liquid solvents. The method involves contacting lignocellulosic biomass with an ionic liquid solvent system comprising triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate ([TEA][HSO4]) and water at elevated temperatures between 100-150°C for a period of 1-24 hours.  

The ionic liquid solvent system effectively fractionates the biomass into three primary components: a cellulose-rich solid pulp, a precipitated lignin fraction, and an ionic liquid solution containing dissolved hemicellulose sugars and metal ions. The cellulose-rich pulp retains 85-96% of the original glucan content while achieving over 70% enzymatic saccharification yields when processed with cellulolytic enzymes. The lignin fraction is recovered through precipitation by diluting the ionic liquid solution with water, achieving yields up to 82% of the original lignin content.  

The process simultaneously extracts metal ions present in the biomass into the ionic liquid phase. Analysis of the recycled ionic liquid solutions shows accumulation of inorganic cations including potassium, sodium and calcium, demonstrating the method's effectiveness for concurrent biomass fractionation and metal extraction. The ionic liquid solution maintains stability through multiple reuse cycles, with over 99% recovery efficiency observed during recycling experiments.  

The invention provides several advantages over conventional biomass pretreatment methods. The ionic liquid solvent operates at atmospheric pressure, eliminating the need for expensive pressure-rated equipment. The triethylammonium-based ionic liquid is significantly less expensive than conventional imidazolium-based solvents, with production costs as low as $1.24/kg. The fractionation process preserves cellulose integrity while effectively removing lignin and hemicellulose, enabling high saccharification yields without generating inhibitory byproducts.  

## EXAMPLE 2 COPPER SOLUBILITY AND ELECTRODEPOSITION OF COPPER FROM VARIOUS IONIC LIQUIDS COPPER SOLUBILITY  

The ionic liquid solvent system of the present invention demonstrates remarkable capacity for dissolving and recovering copper and other transition metals. When applied to metal-containing biomass feedstocks or industrial waste streams, the triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate ionic liquid effectively solubilizes copper ions through coordination with the hydrogen sulfate anion.  

Copper solubility in the ionic liquid system is enhanced by the acidic nature of the solvent, with optimal dissolution occurring at water contents between 10-40% by weight. The dissolved copper can be recovered from the ionic liquid solution through electrodeposition or chemical reduction methods. Electrodeposition experiments show that copper can be selectively plated from the ionic liquid solution while maintaining the solvent's integrity for reuse.  

The invention provides a sustainable approach for integrated biomass processing and metal recovery. The ionic liquid simultaneously fractionates lignocellulosic components while extracting valuable metals, creating economic synergies between biorefining and metal recycling operations. The process is particularly effective for copper recovery, with demonstrated solubility exceeding conventional aqueous or organic solvent systems.  

The copper recovery process maintains the ionic liquid's stability through multiple cycles, with minimal degradation observed even after extended use at elevated temperatures. This enables closed-loop operation where the ionic liquid is continuously recycled between biomass fractionation and metal recovery steps. The invention's ability to couple biomass deconstruction with metal extraction provides significant economic advantages over separate processing approaches.
