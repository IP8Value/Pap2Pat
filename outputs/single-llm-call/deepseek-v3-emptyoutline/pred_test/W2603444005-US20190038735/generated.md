## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates generally to the field of immunology and vaccinology. More specifically, the invention pertains to virus-like particle (VLP) vaccines targeting virulence regulation in Staphylococcus aureus, particularly methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA). The invention specifically discloses compositions and methods for generating immune responses against autoinducing peptide 1 (AIP1) of the agr type I quorum sensing system through conformationally-restricted presentation on bacteriophage PP7 VLPs.  

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION  
The increasing incidence of skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs) caused by antibiotic-resistant S. aureus, particularly methicillin-resistant strains (MRSA), represents a significant public health burden. Current treatment options are becoming increasingly limited due to the rapid development of antibiotic resistance. The accessory gene regulator (agr) quorum sensing system plays a central role in S. aureus virulence by regulating the production of numerous virulence factors through secretion of autoinducing peptides (AIPs). Among the four agr types (I-IV), agr type I isolates are particularly associated with invasive infections.  

Previous attempts to develop vaccines targeting the agr system have focused primarily on agr type IV, with limited success in generating protective immunity against the more clinically relevant agr type I isolates. The small size and structural complexity of AIP1, the signaling molecule of agr type I, present particular challenges for vaccine development. The AIP1 peptide (YSTCDFIM) contains a labile thiolactone bond essential for its biological activity, making it difficult to maintain structural integrity during vaccine formulation and administration.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention provides a novel vaccine platform for generating protective immunity against S. aureus agr type I-regulated virulence. The invention discloses PP7 bacteriophage virus-like particles (VLPs) displaying a modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM, designated AIP1S) in a conformationally-restricted manner within the AB-loop of the PP7 coat protein. This presentation strategy overcomes previous limitations by:  

1) Providing multivalent display of the AIP1 epitope to enhance immunogenicity  
2) Maintaining the structural constraints of native AIP1 through insertion into the rigid β-turn structure of the PP7 AB-loop  
3) Eliminating the unstable thiolactone bond through a conservative cysteine-to-serine mutation while preserving antigenic properties  
4) Generating high-titer antibodies capable of recognizing and neutralizing native AIP1  

The vaccine demonstrates efficacy in reducing agr signaling, virulence factor production, and disease severity in a murine model of S. aureus skin infection.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Production of Virus-Like Particles  
The PP7-AIP1S VLPs of the invention are produced through recombinant expression of modified PP7 coat protein dimers in E. coli. The PP7 coat protein is engineered as a single-chain dimer containing the AIP1S sequence (YSTSDFIM) inserted into the second AB-loop. This insertion site was selected based on structural studies showing the AB-loop forms a highly constrained β-turn that maintains inserted peptides in a rigid conformation mimicking the cyclic structure of native AIP1.  

The expression construct is generated by cloning the AIP1S sequence between KpnI and BamHI restriction sites in the pDSP7K plasmid, which contains the PP7 single-chain dimer under control of a T7 promoter. Following transformation into E. coli C41 cells, expression is induced with IPTG. The VLPs self-assemble in the bacterial cytoplasm and are purified through a series of centrifugation, filtration, and size-exclusion chromatography steps using a Sephacryl S-400 HR column.  

The resulting PP7-AIP1S VLPs form icosahedral capsids approximately 30 nm in diameter, with each VLP displaying approximately 90 copies of the AIP1S epitope in a highly ordered array. This multivalent presentation is critical for generating strong immune responses against the otherwise poorly immunogenic AIP1 peptide.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Immunogenicity of PP7-AIP1S Vaccine  
BALB/c mice were immunized intramuscularly with 10 μg of PP7-AIP1S VLPs or control PP7 VLPs at weeks 0 and 4. Serum collected at 2, 4, and 8 weeks post-boost demonstrated dose-dependent binding to PP7-AIP1S but not control VLPs. Competitive ELISA showed that antibody binding to PP7-AIP1S was specifically inhibited by soluble, cyclic AIP1 but not by AIP2 (GVNACSSLF), demonstrating the vaccine generates antibodies capable of recognizing native AIP1 with high specificity.  

### Example 2: Efficacy in Murine Skin Infection Model  
Mice vaccinated with PP7-AIP1S and challenged 8 weeks post-boost with 4×10^7 CFU of USA300 LAC (agr type I MRSA) showed significantly reduced abscess formation (p<0.0042) and dermonecrosis (p=0.0177) compared to controls. Bacterial burden at the infection site on day 6 was reduced by approximately 10-fold (p=0.0001). Local levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, IL-17) were significantly reduced in vaccinated mice, consistent with reduced virulence factor production.  

### Example 3: Inhibition of agr Signaling In Vivo  
Vaccination with PP7-AIP1S significantly reduced RNAIII transcription (p=0.0029) and alpha-hemolysin (Hla) production (p=0.0025) at the infection site compared to controls. These findings demonstrate the vaccine's mechanism of action through immune interference with agr signaling.  

### ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES  

### Example 4: Structural Characterization of PP7-AIP1S  
SDS-PAGE analysis confirmed the expected size increase of the PP7 single-chain dimer following AIP1S insertion. Dynamic light scattering and size exclusion chromatography demonstrated the VLPs maintain homogeneity and proper assembly despite the insertion. Electron microscopy confirmed preservation of icosahedral symmetry.  

### Example 5: Epitope Mapping of Vaccine-Induced Antibodies  
Competition assays using synthetic peptide analogs demonstrated vaccine-induced antibodies primarily recognize conformational epitopes dependent on the cyclic structure of AIP1, rather than linear sequences. This confirms the AB-loop presentation successfully mimics native AIP1 structure.  

## DISCUSSION  
The PP7-AIP1S vaccine represents a significant advance in targeting S. aureus virulence regulation. By presenting AIP1 in a conformationally-restricted manner on a highly immunogenic VLP platform, the invention overcomes previous limitations in generating protective immunity against agr type I isolates. The conservative C4S mutation eliminates the unstable thiolactone bond while maintaining antigenic properties, addressing a major challenge in AIP-based vaccine development.  

The vaccine's efficacy in reducing disease severity and bacterial burden suggests it may have clinical utility both for prevention and as an adjunct to antibiotic therapy. The platform technology could be extended to target other agr types or similar quorum sensing systems in other pathogens.
