# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and medicine. More specifically, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC), compositions comprising such antibodies, and methods of using these antibodies for therapeutic and diagnostic purposes. The antibodies of the invention are particularly useful for treating diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC signaling, including but not limited to cancer, fibrosis, and neurological disorders involving blood-brain barrier dysfunction.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) constitute a family of growth factors that play crucial roles in embryonic development and various pathological conditions. The PDGF family includes four members: PDGF-A, PDGF-B, PDGF-C, and PDGF-D, which exert their biological effects by binding to and activating two receptor tyrosine kinases, PDGFRα and PDGFRβ. Among these, PDGF-CC has emerged as a particularly important mediator of pathological processes.  

PDGF-CC is synthesized as a latent protein that requires proteolytic cleavage to release the active growth factor domain. Unlike other PDGF family members, activation of PDGF-CC appears to be tissue-specific, with tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) being the primary activator in the central nervous system (CNS) and other proteases activating PDGF-CC in peripheral tissues. Upon activation, PDGF-CC binds to PDGFRα, inducing receptor dimerization, autophosphorylation, and subsequent activation of downstream signaling pathways that regulate cell proliferation, migration, survival, and other cellular responses.  

Dysregulation of PDGF-CC signaling has been implicated in various diseases. In cancer, PDGF-CC promotes tumor growth by recruiting cancer-associated fibroblasts and stimulating angiogenesis. Increased PDGF-CC expression has been observed in gliomas, sarcomas, leukemias, and epithelial cancers. PDGF-CC has also been shown to mediate pathological processes in fibrotic diseases of the liver, kidney, heart, and lungs. Furthermore, in the CNS, PDGF-CC has been identified as a key regulator of blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity, with its overexpression leading to BBB disruption in conditions such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.  

Despite the clear involvement of PDGF-CC in these pathological processes, there remains an unmet need for specific molecular tools to target PDGF-CC signaling. Current approaches using small molecule inhibitors such as imatinib lack specificity for PDGF-CC and may cause off-target effects. Therefore, there is a pressing need for specific inhibitors of PDGF-CC signaling, particularly monoclonal antibodies that can selectively neutralize PDGF-CC activity with high affinity and specificity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the aforementioned needs by providing isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human PDGF-CC. These antibodies are capable of neutralizing PDGF-CC activity by preventing its binding to PDGFRα, thereby inhibiting downstream signaling. The antibodies exhibit high affinity for PDGF-CC, with dissociation constants in the low nanomolar range, and demonstrate exquisite specificity for PDGF-CC without cross-reactivity with other PDGF family members.  

In one aspect, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies that bind to human PDGF-CC, wherein the antibodies comprise complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) with specific amino acid sequences. Particular embodiments include the monoclonal antibody designated 6B3, which has been shown to potently neutralize PDGF-CC activity in both in vitro and in vivo assays. The invention further encompasses chimeric and humanized versions of these antibodies, such as the chimeric 6B3 antibody (ch6B3), which retains the binding specificity and neutralizing activity of the parental murine antibody while reducing immunogenicity in human applications.  

Another aspect of the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-PDGF-CC antibodies described herein, optionally in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or other therapeutic agents. These compositions are formulated for various routes of administration, including parenteral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or intracranial delivery, depending on the intended therapeutic application.  

The invention further provides methods of using the anti-PDGF-CC antibodies for treating diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC signaling. These methods include administering a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-PDGF-CC antibody to a subject in need thereof. Target diseases include various cancers (e.g., gliomas, breast cancer, prostate cancer, renal cell carcinoma), fibrotic disorders (e.g., liver fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, renal fibrosis), and neurological conditions characterized by BBB dysfunction (e.g., stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury).  

Additional aspects of the invention include diagnostic methods using the anti-PDGF-CC antibodies to detect PDGF-CC expression in biological samples, as well as kits containing these antibodies for research or clinical use. The invention also provides methods for identifying compounds that modulate PDGF-CC signaling using the disclosed antibodies in screening assays.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations  

The anti-PDGF-CC antibodies of the present invention are useful for treating various diseases and conditions mediated by PDGF-CC signaling. Therapeutic applications include but are not limited to:  

1. Cancer treatment: The antibodies may be used to treat cancers where PDGF-CC signaling contributes to tumor growth, angiogenesis, or metastasis. These include gliomas, breast cancer, prostate cancer, renal cell carcinoma, colorectal cancer, and other malignancies shown to overexpress PDGF-CC. The antibodies may be administered alone or in combination with other anticancer therapies such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or targeted biological therapies.  

2. Fibrotic disorders: The antibodies may be used to treat fibrotic conditions of various organs including liver fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, renal fibrosis, and cardiac fibrosis. In these applications, the antibodies inhibit PDGF-CC-induced activation of fibroblasts and other mesenchymal cells that drive extracellular matrix deposition and tissue scarring.  

3. Neurological disorders: The antibodies may be used to treat neurological conditions characterized by BBB dysfunction, including stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and neurodegenerative diseases. In these applications, the antibodies prevent PDGF-CC-mediated disruption of BBB integrity, thereby reducing cerebral edema, inflammation, and secondary neuronal damage.  

Pharmaceutical formulations of the anti-PDGF-CC antibodies may be prepared according to standard methods in the art. The formulations typically comprise the antibody in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or stabilizers. Suitable formulations include solutions for intravenous or subcutaneous injection, lyophilized preparations for reconstitution, or sustained-release formulations for localized delivery.  

Dosage regimens will vary depending on factors such as the specific antibody used, the condition being treated, the severity of disease, and patient characteristics. Generally, doses may range from about 0.1 mg/kg to about 50 mg/kg of body weight, administered at intervals ranging from daily to monthly. For neurological applications, direct CNS delivery via intrathecal or intracerebroventricular routes may be employed to enhance brain penetration.  

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays  

The anti-PDGF-CC antibodies of the invention are also useful for diagnostic applications to detect and quantify PDGF-CC expression in biological samples. These assays may be used to:  

1. Diagnose diseases associated with abnormal PDGF-CC expression or activity  
2. Monitor disease progression or response to therapy  
3. Stratify patients for targeted anti-PDGF-CC therapies  
4. Conduct research on PDGF-CC biology and pathophysiology  

Diagnostic methods include immunohistochemistry (IHC), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), Western blotting, immunofluorescence, and other immunoassay formats. For IHC applications, the antibodies may be used to detect PDGF-CC expression in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections, with appropriate controls for specificity.  

In particular embodiments, the antibodies are used to assess PDGF-CC expression patterns in tumor biopsies, with elevated PDGF-CC expression serving as a biomarker for aggressive disease or as a predictor of response to anti-PDGF-CC therapy. The antibodies may also be used to monitor PDGF-CC levels in cerebrospinal fluid or blood as indicators of BBB integrity in neurological disorders.  

### Kits  

The invention provides kits comprising the anti-PDGF-CC antibodies for research, diagnostic, or therapeutic use. These kits may include:  

1. One or more anti-PDGF-CC antibodies (e.g., 6B3, ch6B3)  
2. Appropriate controls (e.g., isotype controls, positive control samples)  
3. Detection reagents (e.g., secondary antibodies, enzyme substrates, chromogens)  
4. Buffers and other necessary reagents  
5. Instructions for use  

Therapeutic kits may additionally include devices for antibody administration (e.g., syringes, infusion sets) and instructions for dosing regimens. Diagnostic kits may include standardized scoring systems for interpreting PDGF-CC expression levels in tissue samples.  

### Drug Screening Assay  

The anti-PDGF-CC antibodies of the invention may be used in screening assays to identify compounds that modulate PDGF-CC signaling. These assays include:  

1. Competition binding assays to identify compounds that disrupt PDGF-CC/antibody interactions  
2. Cell-based assays measuring PDGFRα phosphorylation in the presence of test compounds  
3. Functional assays assessing PDGF-CC-induced cellular responses (e.g., proliferation, migration)  

Such screening methods are useful for drug discovery efforts aimed at developing small molecule inhibitors of PDGF-CC signaling or for characterizing biosimilar antibodies. The antibodies may also be used as positive controls in these assays to establish baseline inhibition of PDGF-CC activity.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC  

Monoclonal antibodies against human PDGF-CC were generated by immunizing mice with recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein. Following immunization, hybridomas were produced and screened for PDGF-CC reactivity using ELISA and Western blotting. Sixteen hybridomas showing strong reactivity with PDGF-CC were selected for further characterization.  

Selected hybridomas were subcloned by limiting dilution to ensure monoclonality. Antibodies were purified from culture supernatants using protein G affinity chromatography. The purified antibodies were characterized for specificity, affinity, and neutralizing activity as described in subsequent examples.  

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies  

The complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of selected anti-PDGF-CC antibodies were sequenced to determine their variable region sequences. The CDR sequences for antibodies 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were identified, with 6B3 and 19C7 showing identical CDR sequences.  

The heavy chain variable region of 6B3 contained CDRs with the following sequences: CDR-H1 (GYTFTSYW), CDR-H2 (INPNSGYT), and CDR-H3 (ARYYGMDY). The light chain variable region contained CDRs with the sequences: CDR-L1 (RASQDISNYLN), CDR-L2 (YTSRLHS), and CDR-L3 (QQGNTLPWT). These sequences are characteristic of high-affinity antibodies specific for PDGF-CC.  

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)  

A mouse-human chimeric version of the 6B3 antibody (ch6B3) was constructed by cloning the variable regions of murine 6B3 upstream of human IgG1 constant regions. The chimeric construct was expressed in mammalian cells and purified.  

Binding assays demonstrated that ch6B3 retained the specificity and affinity of the parental murine antibody for PDGF-CC. Functional assays showed that ch6B3 effectively neutralized PDGF-CC-induced PDGFRα phosphorylation, confirming that the chimerization process did not compromise antibody function.  

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C  

The binding epitope of 6B3 on PDGF-CC was mapped using a combination of molecular modeling and peptide competition assays. Four putative binding sites were predicted based on structural analysis of PDGF-CC.  

Peptides corresponding to these regions were synthesized and tested for their ability to block 6B3 binding to PDGF-CC. Only peptide 3 (corresponding to residues 120-135 of mature PDGF-CC) effectively competed with 6B3 binding in a dose-dependent manner. This peptide was confirmed as containing the 6B3 epitope through direct binding assays using immobilized peptide.  

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity  

The specificity of anti-PDGF-CC antibodies was confirmed using surface plasmon resonance (Biacore) analysis. Antibodies were tested for binding to immobilized PDGF-CC versus other PDGF family members (PDGF-AA, PDGF-BB, PDGF-DD).  

The 6B3 antibody showed high-affinity binding to PDGF-CC (KD in the low nanomolar range) with no detectable cross-reactivity with other PDGF ligands. Similar specificity profiles were observed for other neutralizing antibodies in the panel (11F5, 19C7).  

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation  

The neutralizing activity of anti-PDGF-CC antibodies was assessed using PDGFRα-expressing porcine aorta endothelial (PAE) cells. Cells were stimulated with PDGF-CC in the presence or absence of antibodies, and receptor phosphorylation was measured by Western blotting.  

Preincubation of PDGF-CC with 6B3 or ch6B3 completely abolished PDGFRα phosphorylation, while control antibodies had no effect. Similar results were obtained in cell proliferation assays, confirming that the antibodies block PDGF-CC-induced mitogenic signaling.  

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens  

The expression pattern of PDGF-CC in human tissues was examined using 6B3 for immunohistochemistry. Both normal and tumor tissues were analyzed, with particular attention to epithelial, stromal, and vascular compartments.  

PDGF-CC showed widespread expression in normal tissues, with particularly high levels in liver, kidney, pancreas, and reproductive organs. In tumor tissues, PDGF-CC expression was significantly elevated compared to matched normal tissues, with increased staining observed in tumor cells, stroma, and blood vessels.  

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies  

The therapeutic potential of ch6B3 was evaluated in a mouse model of PDGF-CC-induced blood-retinal barrier (BRB) disruption. Mice received intraocular injections of PDGF-CC with or without prior administration of ch6B3.  

PDGF-CC injection caused significant BRB leakage as measured by dextran extravasation, while pretreatment with ch6B3 completely prevented this effect. These results demonstrate the in vivo efficacy of anti-PDGF-CC antibodies in maintaining vascular barrier integrity.  

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3  

The ability of 6B3 to modulate PDGF-CC-mediated BBB opening was assessed using established models of BBB permeability. Intraventricular injection of PDGF-CC induced BBB disruption, which was prevented by systemic administration of 6B3.  

These findings support the use of anti-PDGF-CC antibodies for treating neurological conditions involving BBB dysfunction. The results also demonstrate that circulating antibodies can effectively target PDGF-CC signaling in the CNS when the BBB is compromised.
