# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to energy storage devices, and more particularly to stretchable lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) incorporating kirigami-inspired structural designs. The invention specifically discloses novel battery architectures utilizing three distinct kirigami patterns—zigzag-cut, cut-N-twist, and cut-N-shear configurations—that enable exceptional mechanical stretchability while maintaining stable electrochemical performance. These stretchable batteries find particular utility in wearable electronic devices where conventional rigid battery form factors impose significant design constraints.  

The disclosed technology fundamentally transforms battery geometry through engineered cutting and folding patterns derived from kirigami principles, achieving stretchability exceeding 100% without compromising energy density or cycle life. The invention further provides manufacturing methods for producing these kirigami-based batteries using conventional lithium-ion battery materials and production techniques, enabling immediate commercial scalability.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Conventional lithium-ion batteries suffer from inherent rigidity due to their layered construction and brittle electrode materials, making them incompatible with emerging flexible and stretchable electronics. Prior attempts to create flexible power sources have focused primarily on material-level innovations, such as developing stretchable conductors or deformable electrolytes, but these approaches often compromise energy density or require non-standard manufacturing processes.  

Origami-inspired folding techniques have been explored for creating deformable battery structures, but these designs typically involve significant out-of-plane deformation that increases device thickness during stretching and creates mechanical instability. Existing stretchable battery designs also frequently exhibit rapid capacity fade during repeated deformation due to crack propagation in current collectors and active materials.  

There remains an unmet need for stretchable energy storage devices that: (1) maintain consistent thickness during deformation; (2) preserve high energy density comparable to conventional LIBs; (3) demonstrate mechanical durability over thousands of stretching cycles; and (4) can be manufactured using standard battery production methods. The present invention addresses these limitations through innovative kirigami patterning that controls deformation modes at the structural level rather than relying solely on material properties.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a stretchable lithium-ion battery comprising multiple layers patterned with kirigami cuts that enable controlled in-plane deformation. Three exemplary embodiments are disclosed:  

1. A zigzag-cut pattern where asymmetrical cuts between folded creases create stretchable zigzag structures that accommodate elongation through controlled buckling.  

2. A cut-N-twist pattern featuring symmetrical cuts at all creases followed by twisting to form a stable, rotation-based stretching mechanism without thickness variation.  

3. A cut-N-shear pattern combining symmetrical cutting with folding and shearing to double packing density compared to the cut-N-twist configuration while maintaining equivalent stretchability.  

The battery architecture maintains conventional LIB material composition, including graphite anodes, LiCoO₂ cathodes, polypropylene separators, and metal foil current collectors, but arranges these components in kirigami-patterned layers that are precisely aligned during assembly. Critical to the invention is the uniform cutting of all battery components—including current collectors, electrodes, separator, and packaging—using identical kirigami patterns followed by perfect lamination.  

The kirigami LIBs demonstrate exceptional performance characteristics including:  
- Over 100% stretchability without significant thickness variation  
- Stable capacity retention exceeding 85% after 100 charge/discharge cycles with intermittent stretching  
- Consistent output power (4.1W) during 3000+ stretching cycles at 90% strain  
- Energy densities comparable to conventional LIBs (~160 Wh/kg)  
- Compatibility with standard battery manufacturing processes  

A key innovation involves controlling deformation modes at kirigami cut locations to preferentially induce "plastic rolling" rather than "crack growth," as verified through finite element analysis. This is achieved by optimizing cut geometry and folding parameters to maintain stresses below critical thresholds for crack propagation.  

The invention further demonstrates practical application in powering commercial wearable electronics, exemplified by integration with a Samsung Gear 2 smartwatch. The kirigami battery replaces both the conventional battery and watch strap, providing distributed energy storage throughout the band area while withstanding repeated stretching during normal use.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The stretchable lithium-ion batteries of the present invention employ kirigami patterning to transform conventional rigid battery architectures into highly deformable structures without compromising electrochemical performance. The following sections describe three preferred embodiments along with their manufacturing processes and performance characteristics.  

**Zigzag-Cut Pattern Battery**  
This embodiment utilizes asymmetrical cutting of folded foil stacks between neighboring creases to create longitudinal zigzag patterns. When stretched, the structure accommodates elongation through out-of-plane buckling at cut locations, with stretchability determined by cut length and buckling amplitude. The zigzag pattern provides simple manufacturability and demonstrates over 100% stretchability while maintaining stable electrochemical function.  

**Cut-N-Twist Pattern Battery**  
In this preferred embodiment, symmetrical cuts are made at all creases of a folded foil stack, which is then unfolded to a planar state and twisted at both ends. The resulting structure resembles a twisted telephone cord that stretches through rotational motion at cut locations rather than buckling. This configuration eliminates thickness variation during stretching and demonstrates particular mechanical robustness, maintaining 99.8% Coulombic efficiency over 100 charge/discharge cycles with intermittent stretching.  

**Cut-N-Shear Pattern Battery**  
This embodiment builds upon the cut-N-twist design by applying shear deformation after symmetrical cutting and folding, effectively doubling the packing density. The structure stretches through rotation at cuts similar to the cut-N-twist pattern but achieves higher energy capacity per unit length. This configuration demonstrates 150% stretchability—the highest among the three embodiments—while maintaining all electrochemical advantages.  

**Manufacturing Process**  
All embodiments follow a standardized fabrication method:  
1. Precise kirigami patterning of current collectors (Cu anode, Al cathode) using customized cutting tools or laser systems  
2. Application of conventional electrode slurries (graphite anode, LiCoO₂ cathode) onto patterned current collectors  
3. Identical kirigami patterning of separator (polypropylene) and packaging (aluminized polyethylene) layers  
4. Perfect alignment and lamination of all components in Argon environment  
5. Electrolyte (1M LiPF₆ in EC:DMC:DEC) injection and final sealing  

Critical to the invention is maintaining pattern uniformity across all layers and precise alignment during assembly. The manufacturing process leverages conventional LIB materials and techniques, enabling immediate scalability without requiring specialized equipment beyond precision cutting systems.  

**Deformation Mechanism**  
Finite element analysis confirms the kirigami patterns preferentially induce "plastic rolling" deformation at folds rather than crack propagation. For a 10μm aluminum foil with bending diameters of 500-800μm, the plastic energy dissipation parameter β remains near 1MPa—well below the threshold for crack growth—ensuring mechanical durability. This explains the observed stability over 3000+ stretching cycles.  

**Performance Characteristics**  
Electrochemical testing reveals:  
- Stable charge/discharge plateaus at ~3.7V in both compact and stretched states  
- Minimal impedance change after stretching (EIS analysis)  
- 85% capacity retention after 100 cycles with periodic stretching  
- Consistent power output (4.1W) regardless of stretch state  
- High energy density (160 Wh/kg) matching conventional LIBs  

Mechanical testing demonstrates:  
- Ultimate tensile strength of 13.3MPa  
- <5% thickness variation during stretching  
- No observable cracks in current collectors after cycling (SEM verification)  

Thermal performance exceeds conventional batteries due to increased surface-area-to-volume ratio, exhibiting negligible temperature rise during operation compared to noticeable heating in standard LIBs.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Cut-N-Twist Battery for Wearable Electronics**  
A kirigami LIB using the cut-N-twist pattern was fabricated with 80mAh capacity (0.26g graphite anode, 0.65g LiCoO₂ cathode) and integrated into a Samsung Gear 2 smartwatch. The battery replaced both the original power source and watch strap, with dimensions of 51.3mm (length) × 27mm (width) × 2.6mm (thickness) in the compact state.  

Performance testing showed:  
- 24.5 hours standby time at 2.9mA discharge  
- 90 minutes calling time at 48mA discharge  
- Normal operation during 30% stretching from wrist to upper arm  
- Additional 15% strain tolerance during elbow bending  
- Perfect functionality maintenance during video playback  

**Example 2: Comparative Analysis of Kirigami Patterns**  
Controlled experiments compared all three kirigami patterns under identical conditions:  

| Parameter        | Zigzag-Cut | Cut-N-Twist | Cut-N-Shear |
|-----------------|-----------|------------|------------|
| Max Stretchability | 100%      | 100%       | 150%       |
| Thickness Variation | ±15%      | <5%        | <5%        |
| Capacity Density | 30mAh     | 30mAh      | 60mAh      |
| Cycles to Failure | 1500      | >3000      | 2500       |

The cut-N-shear pattern demonstrated superior capacity density while the cut-N-twist showed exceptional cycle life, guiding pattern selection based on application requirements.  

**Example 3: Thermal Performance Benchmarking**  
During 1-hour 48mA discharge testing:  
- Conventional smartwatch battery: Temperature rise of 8.7°C  
- Kirigami battery: No measurable temperature increase  
This thermal advantage stems from the kirigami battery's distributed architecture and high surface area, enabling passive cooling impractical in conventional designs.  

The examples demonstrate the invention's versatility across performance metrics and end-use applications, particularly in wearable electronics where stretchability and stable electrochemistry are paramount.
