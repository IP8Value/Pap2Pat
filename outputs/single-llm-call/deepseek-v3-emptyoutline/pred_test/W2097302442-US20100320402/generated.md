# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of radiation therapy, specifically to a novel method and system for delivering spatially fractionated radiation therapy in three-dimensional (3D) high-dose lattices within tumor volumes. More particularly, the invention pertains to techniques for generating highly localized, peak-to-valley dose distributions using modern radiation therapy instrumentation, including intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT), robotic-controlled linear accelerators, and charged particle beam systems. The invention addresses the limitations of conventional two-dimensional (2D) GRID radiotherapy by confining high-dose regions within the tumor volume while minimizing exposure to surrounding normal tissues.  

## BACKGROUND  

Spatially fractionated GRID radiotherapy (SFGRT) has been utilized since the early 1930s as a treatment modality for advanced bulky tumors. Traditional 2D GRID techniques employ orthovoltage or megavoltage X-ray beams with shield arrays featuring circular or square openings, typically ranging from 0.5 to 1.5 cm in size. While this approach has demonstrated significant tumor regression in clinical cases, it suffers from several drawbacks, including substantial exposure of normal tissues to high radiation doses and superficial dose deposition outside the clinical target volume.  

Recent advances in radiobiology have elucidated the bystander and abscopal effects associated with GRID therapy, wherein high-dose regions induce cell death cascades in adjacent low-dose regions and distant untreated tumors. These effects suggest that spatially fractionated radiation could enhance therapeutic outcomes, particularly for radiation-resistant tumors. However, the clinical implementation of such techniques has been hindered by the limitations of 2D GRID delivery methods.  

Modern radiation therapy technologies, including IMRT, robotic linear accelerators, and spot-scanning particle beams, offer the potential to overcome these limitations by enabling precise 3D dose lattices within tumor volumes. These techniques allow for the creation of highly concentrated dose vertices with rapid dose fall-off between vertices, thereby minimizing peripheral tissue exposure. Despite these technological advancements, no systematic method for delivering 3D lattice radiotherapy (LRT) has been established, necessitating the development of optimized delivery techniques and configurations.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a method and system for delivering 3D lattice radiotherapy (LRT) that addresses the limitations of conventional 2D GRID techniques. The invention involves generating a plurality of high-dose vertices within a tumor volume, wherein each vertex receives a dose of at least 12 Gy, with intervening low-dose regions receiving 3 cGy or less. The high-dose vertices are spatially arranged to form a 3D lattice, ensuring rapid dose fall-off between vertices and minimal exposure to surrounding normal tissues.  

The invention encompasses several delivery techniques, including:  
1. **IMRT or dynamic multileaf collimator (MLC)-based arc therapy**, wherein multiple conformal arcs or a single modulated arc are used to create the 3D dose lattice.  
2. **Robotic-controlled linear accelerators**, which employ multiple non-coplanar beams focused on predefined isocenters within the tumor volume.  
3. **Charged particle beams (e.g., protons or carbon ions)**, wherein spot-scanning nozzles deliver Bragg peaks to each high-dose vertex.  

The invention further includes methods for optimizing the lattice configuration, including the number, separation, and spatial distribution of high-dose vertices, as well as real-time tracking technologies to ensure dose delivery accuracy. By confining high-dose regions to the tumor volume and minimizing peripheral exposure, the invention enhances therapeutic efficacy while reducing treatment-related toxicity.  

## DESCRIPTION OF INVENTION  

The invention provides a comprehensive system and method for delivering 3D lattice radiotherapy (LRT), as detailed below.  

### 1. **3D Dose Lattice Configuration**  
The 3D dose lattice comprises a plurality of high-dose vertices distributed within the tumor volume, each receiving a dose of at least 12 Gy. The vertices are separated by distances sufficient to create low-dose regions (≤3 cGy) between them, ensuring a pronounced peak-to-valley dose distribution. The lattice configuration is determined based on tumor size, location, and proximity to critical structures, with vertex separation typically ranging from 1.5 to 3 cm.  

### 2. **Delivery Techniques**  
#### a. **IMRT and Dynamic MLC-Based Arc Therapy**  
The invention employs IMRT or aperture-modulated arc techniques (e.g., RapidArc) to generate the 3D dose lattice. Multiple conformal arcs or a single modulated arc are used, depending on the mechanical flexibility of the MLC. For example, in one embodiment, 20 high-dose vertices are created using six arcs with 18 MV X-rays, achieving a vertex separation of approximately 2 cm.  

#### b. **Robotic Linear Accelerators**  
The invention utilizes robotic-controlled linear accelerators (e.g., CyberKnife) to deliver multiple non-coplanar beams focused on predefined isocenters within the tumor. In one embodiment, 11 vertices are distributed throughout a pancreatic mass, with beams from 40–50 non-coplanar directions per vertex. Real-time tracking ensures precise dose delivery.  

#### c. **Charged Particle Beam Systems**  
The invention extends to charged particle beams (e.g., protons or carbon ions) with spot-scanning nozzles. Each high-dose vertex is targeted with a minimally spread-out Bragg peak, confining the high-dose region to the tumor volume.  

### 3. **Dosimetric Verification**  
The invention includes methods for verifying dose delivery accuracy using 3D quality assurance phantoms and wide-range Gafchromic films. Dosimetric measurements confirm agreement within 3% and <1 mm of planned doses.  

### 4. **Biological and Clinical Applications**  
The invention is particularly suited for induction therapy, wherein high-dose LRT is followed by conventional radiotherapy or chemotherapy. The spatially fractionated dose distribution triggers bystander and abscopal effects, enhancing tumor regression in bulky or hypoxic tumors.  

## EQUIVALENTS  

The invention encompasses all equivalent methods and systems for delivering 3D lattice radiotherapy, including but not limited to:  
1. Variations in the number, size, and spatial distribution of high-dose vertices.  
2. Alternative delivery techniques, such as helical tomotherapy or magnetic resonance-guided radiotherapy.  
3. Modifications to the peak-to-valley dose ratio, provided the high-dose vertices remain within the tumor volume.  
4. Applications to other charged particle beams or emerging radiation therapy technologies.  

The scope of the invention is defined by the appended claims and includes all modifications and equivalents falling within the spirit of the disclosure.
