# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for inhibiting the pathogenicity of bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viruses by targeting host caspase proteins. Pathogenic agents such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE), anthrax toxins, ricin, Botulinum neurotoxin A (BoNT/A), and Zika virus (ZIKV) exploit host cellular pathways to induce cytotoxicity. These pathways converge at critical hub proteins, including caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, which mediate programmed cell death. Despite the diversity of these pathogens, they share a common reliance on host caspases for their cytotoxic effects.  

Current therapeutic strategies primarily focus on neutralizing the pathogens directly, which often leads to narrow-spectrum efficacy and the rapid emergence of resistance. There remains an unmet need for broad-spectrum, host-oriented therapies that disrupt pathogenicity by targeting conserved host pathways rather than the pathogens themselves. The present invention addresses this need by identifying bithionol as a potent inhibitor of multiple caspases, thereby providing a novel therapeutic approach to mitigate the effects of a wide range of pathogens.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting the cytotoxic effects of bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viruses by administering bithionol or its derivatives. Bithionol acts as a polypharmacological agent by selectively inhibiting host caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, which are exploited by multiple pathogenic agents to induce cell death.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Combination Compositions**: Pharmaceutical formulations comprising bithionol and optionally other caspase inhibitors or antimicrobial agents.  
2. **Methods of Treatment**: Administration of bithionol to subjects exposed to or at risk of exposure to toxins or viruses that depend on caspase activation for pathogenicity.  
3. **Screening Methods**: Multiplexed cellular and biochemical assays to identify additional caspase inhibitors with broad-spectrum anti-pathogenic activity.  

The invention is supported by experimental data demonstrating bithionol's efficacy in reducing cytotoxicity induced by PE, anthrax toxins, ricin, BoNT/A, and ZIKV. Bithionol achieves this by inhibiting caspase activity without directly targeting the pathogens, thereby minimizing the risk of resistance.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Combination Compositions and Treatments  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising bithionol as the active ingredient. Bithionol (2,2'-thiobis(4,6-dichlorophenol)) is a known anti-helminthic agent that has been repurposed herein for its caspase-inhibitory properties. The compositions may further include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or additional therapeutic agents such as antibiotics, antivirals, or other caspase inhibitors.  

The compositions are formulated for oral, intravenous, or topical administration, depending on the target pathogen and clinical context. For example, oral formulations are preferred for systemic delivery against toxins like BoNT/A or ZIKV, while topical formulations may be used for localized infections.  

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition  

The invention provides methods for administering bithionol to subjects in need thereof. Effective doses range from 1 to 100 mg/kg, with preferred doses achieving serum concentrations of 10–50 μM, which exceed the half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) observed in vitro.  

Administration protocols include:  
- **Prophylactic Use**: Pre-treatment of subjects at risk of exposure to toxins or viruses (e.g., military personnel or laboratory workers).  
- **Therapeutic Use**: Post-exposure treatment to mitigate cytotoxicity and improve survival rates.  

The methods are supported by in vivo data showing that bithionol (6 mg/kg) fully protects mice from lethal doses of BoNT/A and reduces ZIKV replication in human astrocytes.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins  

Host hub proteins were identified by analyzing the sensitivity of human B-cells to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* exotoxin A (PE). Genetic variations in caspase-3 and -7 were found to significantly correlate with PE sensitivity (P < 0.05), confirming caspases as critical hubs exploited by multiple toxins, including anthrax, diphtheria, and cholera toxins.  

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to *P. aeruginosa* Exotoxin A  

B-lymphoblastoid cells from 234 individuals exhibited a 200-fold range in PE sensitivity. Mutations in *CASP3* (rs4647601, rs4647693, rs1049216) and *CASP7* (rs3814231, rs2227309) were associated with altered toxin sensitivity, demonstrating the role of caspases in mediating pathogenicity.  

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways  

A Clinical Compound Library (CCL) was screened for inhibitors of PE, cholera toxin, and diphtheria toxin cytotoxicity. Bithionol emerged as a multiplex hit, reducing cell death by all three toxins with an EC50 of 10 μM.  

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7  

Parallel FRET assays identified bithionol as an inhibitor of caspases-3, -6, and -7 (IC50 = 11–21 μM). The drug's efficacy was confirmed in dose-response studies, validating its mechanism of action.  

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases  

Bithionol protected cells from PE, anthrax toxins, and ricin by inhibiting caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9. The drug's polypharmacology was demonstrated using purified recombinant caspases.  

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins  

Bithionol (33 μM) blocked anthrax toxin-induced cell death by inhibiting caspase-1 but not furin or cathepsin B. Western blotting confirmed MAPKK2 cleavage was unaffected, ruling out direct toxin inhibition.  

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo  

Bithionol (EC50 = 10 μM) reduced ricin cytotoxicity in K562 cells and fully protected mice from lethal BoNT/A doses (6 mg/kg). FRET assays confirmed the drug acts via host caspases, not BoNT/A protease activity.  

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor  

Bithionol inhibited ZIKV replication in Vero E6 cells (EC50 = 5.5–6.7 μM) and human astrocytes (EC50 = 6.3 μM), demonstrating broad-spectrum antiviral activity.  

### Example 9. Material and Methods  

Detailed protocols for cell culture, toxin treatments, FRET assays, and animal studies are provided herein. Key reagents include:  
- **Toxins**: PE (List Biological Labs), ricin (Vector Labs), BoNT/A (Metabiologics).  
- **Assays**: Caspase activity kits (BioVision), Alamar Blue viability assays (BioRad).  
- **Animal Models**: Swiss Webster mice (oral gavage, 6 mg/kg bithionol).
