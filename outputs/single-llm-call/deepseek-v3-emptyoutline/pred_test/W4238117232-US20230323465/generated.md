# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and precision medicine. More specifically, the invention provides novel methods, compositions, and systems for the accurate preoperative diagnosis of thyroid neoplasms through detection of specific biomarker isoforms in fine needle aspiration (FNA) samples. The invention particularly addresses the critical need for improved diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing benign thyroid lesions from malignant carcinomas, including the newly classified noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP). The disclosed technology encompasses methods for detecting a panel of five specific transcript isoforms, their use in diagnostic testing, and related kits for clinical implementation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Thyroid cancer incidence has increased dramatically worldwide, with papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) showing particularly rapid growth in prevalence. Current diagnostic methods rely heavily on fine needle aspiration cytopathology, yet up to 30% of FNA samples yield indeterminate results. This diagnostic uncertainty stems from inherent limitations of visual microscopic evaluation and has been further complicated by the recent reclassification of follicular variant of PTC (FVPTC) into malignant and non-malignant (NIFTP) categories.  

Existing molecular tests for thyroid nodules, including DNA-based mutation panels and gene expression classifiers, suffer from significant limitations. These tests demonstrate poor specificity after the introduction of the NIFTP classification, frequently misclassifying these indolent lesions as malignant. Furthermore, current tests fail to adequately account for sample variability and contamination from non-thyroid cells in FNA specimens. The lack of reliable diagnostic tools for preoperative assessment of thyroid nodules leads to unnecessary surgeries and overtreatment of benign disease.  

Previous transcriptome analyses identified numerous differentially expressed genes between benign and malignant thyroid tumors. However, these findings have not been successfully translated into clinically viable diagnostic tests capable of distinguishing all relevant thyroid tumor subtypes, particularly when analyzing the limited cellular material obtained through FNA procedures. There remains an urgent unmet need for a highly specific and sensitive molecular test that can accurately classify thyroid nodules preoperatively, including proper identification of NIFTP cases.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a solution to the aforementioned problems through the development of a novel molecular diagnostic system based on detection of specific transcript isoforms. The invention discloses a panel of five biomarker isoforms selected from the genes CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 that demonstrate superior performance in distinguishing benign thyroid lesions from malignant carcinomas, including accurate identification of NIFTP cases.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. The identification and validation of specific transcript isoforms that show differential expression between benign and malignant thyroid tumors while being undetectable in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), making them particularly suitable for FNA-based testing.  

2. The development of thyroid-specific load control using the TPO1 isoform, which is stably expressed across thyroid tumor subtypes but absent in PBMCs, enabling accurate normalization of gene expression data from variable FNA samples.  

3. Methods for preparing and analyzing FNA samples to detect the biomarker panel, including RNA isolation, reverse transcription, and quantitative PCR protocols optimized for limited cellular material.  

4. Diagnostic algorithms incorporating the five-isoform expression profile to classify thyroid nodules with high specificity (91%) and sensitivity (75%), providing both positive and negative predictive values superior to existing tests.  

5. Comprehensive kits containing all necessary reagents for sample preparation, nucleic acid amplification, and detection of the biomarker panel in clinical settings.  

The invention represents a significant advance in thyroid cancer diagnostics by providing the first molecular test capable of accurately distinguishing NIFTP from malignant thyroid carcinomas preoperatively. This enables more precise surgical decision-making and reduces unnecessary interventions for benign disease.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### I. Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:  

"Thyroid fine needle aspiration (FNA) sample" refers to cellular material obtained from a thyroid nodule using fine needle aspiration techniques, whether collected intraoperatively or through percutaneous biopsy.  

"Biomarker panel" refers to the combination of five specific transcript isoforms from the genes CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 that demonstrate differential expression between benign and malignant thyroid tumors.  

"Load control" refers to the TPO1 transcript isoform used to normalize gene expression data based on thyroid cell content in a sample.  

"Noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP)" refers to the newly classified indolent thyroid tumor previously considered part of the follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma.  

"Z-ΔCt score" refers to the normalized, standardized expression value calculated for each biomarker by comparing its cycle threshold (Ct) value to that of the load control reference gene.  

"Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis" refers to the statistical method used to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the biomarker panel at different classification thresholds.  

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression  

The invention provides optimized methods for preparing thyroid FNA samples for molecular analysis. Sample collection may be performed using standard clinical FNA techniques with 25-gauge needles. Immediately after collection, samples should be preserved in 95% ethanol at -20°C or in RNA stabilization reagents such as RNALater at -80°C to prevent RNA degradation.  

Total RNA isolation from FNA samples is performed using specialized kits designed for limited cellular material, such as the GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit. Due to the variable and often minimal cellular content of FNA samples, the entire RNA eluate (typically 11 μL) should be used for cDNA synthesis. Reverse transcription is performed using high-efficiency enzymes such as SuperScript III reverse transcriptase with 500 ng of total RNA input when available, though the protocol is adaptable to lower RNA quantities.  

For quality control, samples must demonstrate detectable expression of the TPO1 load control transcript within 30 PCR cycles to be included in analysis. This threshold corresponds to approximately 370 pg of total RNA, equivalent to 12-36 thyroid cells, ensuring sufficient thyrocyte content for reliable biomarker detection.  

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid  

The invention provides specific methods for detecting the biomarker panel transcripts in prepared samples. PCR amplification is performed using gene-specific primers targeting the identified isoforms of CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1. Each reaction uses 5% of the total cDNA generated from the FNA sample with high-performance DNA polymerases such as Platinum Taq.  

Initial screening may employ semi-quantitative PCR with agarose gel electrophoresis analysis to confirm isoform-specific amplification. For diagnostic testing, real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) using SYBR Green chemistry is preferred, performed for 40 amplification cycles. All reactions should include appropriate controls for amplification efficiency and specificity.  

Expression levels of each target gene are calculated relative to the TPO1 load control using the ΔCt method (Ct(target) - Ct(reference)). These values are then z-transformed to create standardized z-ΔCt scores allowing direct comparison across genes and samples. The composite biomarker score is derived by summing the z-ΔCt values for all five transcripts.  

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)  

While the primary embodiment of the invention focuses on nucleic acid detection, alternative implementations may utilize protein-level detection of the biomarker gene products. In such embodiments, immunohistochemical or immunoassay techniques can be employed to quantify expression of the proteins encoded by CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1.  

Protein detection methods may include:  

1. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) on cytology slides prepared from FNA samples using isoform-specific antibodies.  

2. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) for quantitative measurement of protein levels in FNA lysates.  

3. Multiplex immunoassay platforms capable of simultaneous detection of multiple biomarkers.  

As with the nucleic acid-based approach, protein detection methods should incorporate normalization to thyroid-specific reference proteins such as thyroid peroxidase (TPO) to account for variable cellular content.  

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel  

The invention includes methods for guiding clinical management of thyroid nodule patients based on biomarker panel results. A composite z-ΔCt score threshold of -1 provides optimal diagnostic performance, corresponding to 75% sensitivity and 91% specificity for malignancy.  

Patients with scores above this threshold may be recommended for surgical intervention appropriate for malignant thyroid neoplasms, such as total thyroidectomy with possible lymph node dissection. Those with scores below the threshold may be candidates for more conservative management, including lobectomy or active surveillance, particularly when the cytological diagnosis suggests NIFTP.  

The test is particularly valuable for cases with indeterminate cytology (Bethesda III-V), where it can significantly reduce unnecessary surgeries. The high negative predictive value (91%) provides strong assurance that nodules classified as benign by the test are truly non-malignant, while the positive predictive value (74%) helps identify those requiring definitive treatment.  

### VI. Kits for Detection of Target Biomarkers  

The invention provides comprehensive kits for implementing the biomarker panel in clinical diagnostics. Such kits contain all necessary reagents for sample preparation, nucleic acid isolation, reverse transcription, PCR amplification, and detection.  

A typical kit may include:  

1. RNA stabilization solution for FNA sample preservation.  

2. Single-cell RNA isolation reagents and columns.  

3. Reverse transcription enzymes and buffers.  

4. Pre-optimized qPCR master mix.  

5. Primer sets specific for the five biomarker isoforms and TPO1 load control.  

6. Control RNA samples for assay calibration.  

7. Detailed protocols for sample processing and data interpretation.  

Kits may be configured for various throughput needs, from individual tests to high-throughput 96-well plate formats. Software tools for automated data analysis and result reporting may be included as part of the system.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Materials and Methods  

Clinical samples were obtained from patients undergoing thyroid surgery under Institutional Review Board approval. Intraoperative FNA samples were collected using 25-gauge needles immediately prior to resection and preserved in 95% ethanol or RNALater. Tumor tissue aliquots were snap-frozen for comparison.  

Total RNA was isolated from 80 frozen thyroid tumors (representing adenomatoid nodules, follicular adenomas, Hürthle cell adenomas, NIFTPs, FVPTCs, Hürthle cell carcinomas, follicular carcinomas, and PTCs) and PBMCs from 31 patients using Trizol and RNeasy Mini Kit. FNA samples were processed with GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit.  

Reverse transcription used 500 ng total RNA with SuperScript III. PCR amplification employed Platinum Taq with 5% of cDNA input. Real-time qPCR used Power SYBR Green Master Mix on a Bio-Rad iQ5 cycler.  

### Results  

Semi-quantitative PCR analysis identified five transcript isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1) showing maximal differential expression between cancer and non-cancer samples. These isoforms were undetectable in PBMCs. TPO1 was selected as the optimal load control due to its thyroid-specific expression and stability across tumor subtypes.  

Testing of 159 FNA samples revealed 137 (86.2%) met quality thresholds for analysis. The five-isoform panel showed significant differential expression between benign (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP) and malignant (FVPTC, PTC) tumors (p<0.0001). ROC analysis demonstrated an AUC of 0.86 for malignancy detection.  

At a z-ΔCt threshold of -1, the test achieved 75% sensitivity and 91% specificity, with NPV of 91% and PPV of 74%. The panel also significantly differentiated NIFTPs from malignant tumors (p=0.0002).  

### Discussion  

The developed five-isoform panel addresses critical limitations of current thyroid molecular tests. By focusing on specific transcript variants absent in PBMCs, the test avoids contamination artifacts common in FNA samples. The use of TPO1 as a thyroid-specific load control enables reliable quantification despite variable cellularity.  

The panel's ability to distinguish NIFTP from malignant tumors represents a major advance, as current tests typically misclassify these lesions. This capability will help reduce overtreatment of indolent neoplasms. The high NPV provides strong assurance for conservative management of test-negative nodules.  

### Conclusions  

The invention provides a robust molecular test for preoperative thyroid nodule evaluation based on detection of five specific transcript isoforms. The test demonstrates superior performance to existing methods, particularly in classifying NIFTP cases. Implementation of this test will enable more precise surgical decision-making and reduce unnecessary interventions for benign disease.  

## Example 2: Assays for Sample Preparation and Detection of 5 Biomarkers and 1 Load-Control Marker  

Detailed protocols were developed for processing FNA samples and detecting the biomarker panel:  

1. RNA Isolation Protocol:  
   - Transfer FNA sample to 500 μL lysis buffer  
   - Process through silica membrane column  
   - Wash twice with ethanol-containing buffers  
   - Elute in 11 μL nuclease-free water  

2. Reverse Transcription:  
   - Combine 11 μL RNA with 1 μL random hexamers (50 μM)  
   - Add 4 μL 5X buffer, 2 μL DTT, 1 μL dNTPs, 1 μL enzyme  
   - Incubate: 25°C 5 min, 50°C 60 min, 70°C 15 min  

3. qPCR Setup:  
   - Prepare master mix: 10 μL SYBR Green, 0.5 μL each primer (10 μM), 4 μL water  
   - Add 5 μL cDNA (1:20 dilution)  
   - Run 40 cycles: 95°C 15 sec, 60°C 60 sec  

Primer sequences for all targets were optimized for specificity and efficiency. Melting curve analysis confirmed single-product amplification for all assays.
