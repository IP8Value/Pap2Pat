# DESCRIPTION  

## STATEMENT REGARDING GOVERNMENT FUNDING

## BACKGROUND  
The field of electron microscopy (EM) has advanced significantly with the development of cryogenic EM (cryo-EM) techniques, enabling high-resolution structural determination of biological macromolecules. However, sample preparation remains a critical bottleneck, particularly in the purification and immobilization of target proteins from complex biological mixtures. Traditional methods often require extensive purification steps prior to EM analysis, which can be time-consuming and may introduce artifacts.  

Existing affinity capture techniques utilize lipid-based monolayers functionalized with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) ligands to immobilize histidine-tagged proteins. While these methods have shown promise, they suffer from limitations such as poor electron dissipation, non-specific binding, and inconsistent monolayer formation. There remains a need for improved substrates that provide enhanced electron transparency, reduced background noise, and superior affinity capture capabilities for high-resolution structural studies.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention provides a novel graphene oxide (GO) monolayer functionalized with NTA ligands (GO-NTA) for the selective capture and analysis of histidine-tagged proteins. The GO-NTA monolayers are formed at the air-water interface and transferred onto solid substrates, including transmission electron microscopy (TEM) grids, using Langmuir-Schaefer (L-S) techniques.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. The GO-NTA monolayers exhibit superior electron transparency compared to traditional carbon substrates, enabling higher contrast imaging.  
2. The planar structure of GO-NTA provides a uniform distribution of NTA ligands for efficient affinity capture of histidine-tagged proteins.  
3. Non-specific binding is minimized through chemical modification with 4-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and bovine serum albumin (BSA), enhancing specificity.  
4. The invention enables direct on-grid purification of target proteins from complex biological lysates, streamlining sample preparation for cryo-EM.  

The invention further encompasses methods for synthesizing GO-NTA, forming monolayers, and applying the monolayers to substrates for affinity capture and structural analysis.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Synthesis of GO-NTA  
Graphene oxide (GO) is synthesized using Hummer's method, followed by activation of carboxylic acid groups with thionyl chloride (SOCl2). The activated GO is then reacted with the tris-t-butyl ester of lysine-NTA to form GO-NTA-(O-t-Bu)3. Deprotection with trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) yields GO-NTA, which is characterized by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) and ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectroscopy.  

### Formation of GO-NTA Monolayers  
GO-NTA sheets are exfoliated in a methanol-water mixture using probe sonication and centrifuged to remove aggregates. The exfoliated GO-NTA is deposited at the air-water interface of a Langmuir trough, where compression yields a stable monolayer. The addition of isopropanol (IPA) to the subphase reduces surface tension, facilitating the transfer of monolayers onto substrates such as silicon wafers or TEM grids via L-S transfer.  

### Characterization of GO-NTA Monolayers  
The monolayers are characterized using atomic force microscopy (AFM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and epifluorescence microscopy. AFM reveals monolayer thicknesses of approximately 1.3 nm, consistent with single-layer GO. SEM and fluorescence imaging confirm uniform coverage and minimal multilayer formation when IPA is used in the subphase.  

### Affinity Capture Applications  
The GO-NTA monolayers are functionalized with nickel ions (Ni2+) to enable affinity capture of histidine-tagged proteins. To mitigate non-specific binding, the GO-NTA surface is chemically modified with PABA and blocked with BSA. This dual modification strategy significantly reduces background adsorption while preserving the specific binding capacity of the NTA-Ni2+ complexes.  

The invention is demonstrated through the capture of histidine-tagged T7 bacteriophage (His6-T7) and GroEL (His6-GroEL) from Escherichia coli lysates. Negative-stain TEM and cryo-EM analyses confirm the selective immobilization of target proteins on GO-NTA grids, with minimal non-specific binding.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Synthesis of GO-NTA**  
GO (335 mg) was stirred in a mixture of SOCl2 (60 mL) and dimethylformamide (DMF) (1.5 mL) at 70°C for 3 days. The reaction mixture was evaporated, washed with dichloromethane (DCM), and resuspended in acetonitrile (ACN) and triethylamine (Et3N). Tris(O-t-butyl)-Nα,Nα-dicarboxymethyllysine ester (533 mg) was added, and the mixture was stirred at 100°C for 3 days. The product was washed with tetrahydrofuran (THF) and water, dried, and deprotected with TFA in THF to yield GO-NTA.  

### Example 2  
**Exfoliation of GO-NTA**  
GO-NTA powder was suspended in a 5:1 methanol-water mixture and probe-sonicated at 150 W for five cycles (45 s sonication, 45 s rest per cycle). The suspension was centrifuged at 1200 g for 10 min, and the supernatant containing exfoliated GO-NTA was collected for further use.  

### Example 3  
**Formation of GO-NTA Monolayers**  
Exfoliated GO-NTA was deposited at the air-water interface of a Langmuir trough using a syringe pump. The monolayer was compressed to a surface pressure of 15 mN/m, and IPA was added to the subphase to facilitate L-S transfer onto silicon wafers or TEM grids.  

### Example 4  
**PABA Modification of GO-NTA**  
GO-NTA (1 mg/mL) was treated with PABA (30 mg) in aqueous solution, sonicated, and shaken for 24 h. The PABA-GO-NTA was washed and exfoliated as described in Example 2.  

### Example 5  
**BSA Blocking of GO-NTA Grids**  
GO-NTA or PABA-GO-NTA grids were incubated with BSA (0.1 mg/mL) for 5 min, followed by washing with water. The grids were dried and stored in a desiccator until use.  

### Example 6  
**Capture of His6-T7 Bacteriophage**  
GO-NTA grids were activated with NiSO4 (1 mM) and incubated with purified His6-T7 bacteriophage (10^10 particles/mL) for 2 min. The grids were washed and stained with uranyl acetate for negative-stain TEM imaging, revealing dense clusters of phage particles.  

### Example 7  
**Capture of His6-T7 from Lysate**  
Ni2+-activated GO-NTA grids were incubated with clarified E. coli lysate containing His6-T7. TEM imaging confirmed selective phage capture, with minimal non-specific binding of cellular debris.  

### Example 8  
**Capture of His6-GroEL for Cryo-EM**  
His6-GroEL was expressed in E. coli, lysed, and applied to Ni2+-activated BSA-PABA-GO-NTA grids. The grids were vitrified and imaged by cryo-EM, enabling single-particle reconstruction of GroEL at 8.1 Å resolution.  

### Example 9  
**Single-Particle Analysis of His6-GroEL**  
Cryo-EM micrographs of His6-GroEL were processed using EMAN 2.1. A total of 5363 particles were manually picked and refined to produce a density map with D7 symmetry. The reconstruction achieved a gold-standard resolution of 8.1 Å, demonstrating the utility of GO-NTA grids for high-resolution structural studies.  

This patent application provides a comprehensive description of the invention, including synthesis, characterization, and application of GO-NTA monolayers for affinity capture and structural analysis of histidine-tagged proteins. The examples demonstrate the invention's utility in streamlining cryo-EM sample preparation and improving imaging quality.
