# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and personalized medicine. More specifically, the invention provides novel methods and compositions for classifying breast cancer subtypes and predicting clinical outcomes based on gene expression profiles of normal breast tissue components. The invention particularly relates to the identification of distinct gene expression signatures in morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma that enable improved classification of breast tumors and prediction of patient outcomes.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women worldwide. Despite significant advances in diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, approximately 26% of patients with early-stage disease develop metastatic progression. Current prognostic indicators, including tumor size, grade, and hormone receptor status, fail to reliably predict outcomes for many patients. The development of more precise molecular markers capable of stratifying patients according to their likely clinical course represents an urgent unmet need in oncology.  

Recent advances in genomic technologies, particularly microarray-based gene expression profiling, have enabled comprehensive characterization of molecular alterations in cancer. Several studies have identified distinct breast cancer subtypes based on gene expression patterns, including luminal, HER2-positive, and basal-like subtypes. However, the clinical utility of these classifications remains limited by several factors, including tissue heterogeneity and insufficient understanding of the molecular differences between normal and malignant tissues.  

A critical limitation in breast cancer research has been the lack of comprehensive characterization of gene expression patterns in normal breast tissue components. The breast consists of two primary tissue compartments: the epithelium, which gives rise to carcinomas, and the stroma, which provides structural and functional support. While most studies have focused on tumor epithelium, growing evidence suggests that stromal components play an active role in cancer initiation and progression.  

Prior attempts to characterize normal breast tissue have been limited by technical challenges in isolating pure cell populations and small sample sizes. Laser capture microdissection (LCM) has emerged as a powerful tool for isolating specific cell types from heterogeneous tissues, enabling more precise molecular characterization. However, previous LCM-based studies of normal breast tissue have been constrained by limited sample numbers and failure to analyze both epithelial and stromal components comprehensively.  

There exists a pressing need in the art for improved methods of breast cancer classification and prognosis prediction based on molecular characterization of normal tissue components. Such methods would enable more accurate stratification of patients, facilitate personalized treatment approaches, and provide insights into the earliest molecular changes associated with malignant transformation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these needs by providing novel methods and compositions for breast cancer classification and prognosis prediction based on gene expression signatures derived from normal breast tissue components. Through comprehensive analysis of microdissected normal breast epithelium and stroma from a large patient cohort, the inventors have identified distinct gene expression profiles that enable improved classification of breast tumors and prediction of clinical outcomes.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Identification of a comprehensive gene expression signature distinguishing normal breast epithelium from stroma, comprising 883 differentially expressed genes.  

2. Demonstration that morphologically normal breast tissue from cancer patients shows no significant gene expression differences compared to reduction mammoplasty tissue, indicating the absence of cancer-associated field effects at the transcriptional level.  

3. Development of methods for classifying breast tumors into molecular subtypes based on their similarity to normal epithelial or stromal gene expression patterns.  

4. Identification of a distinct cluster of ER/PR/HER2-negative tumors that show molecular similarity to normal epithelium and correspond to basal-like and normal-like breast cancer subtypes with poor clinical outcomes.  

5. Provision of diagnostic and prognostic methods utilizing the normal tissue gene expression signatures to classify tumors and predict patient outcomes.  

The invention provides novel tools for precision oncology, enabling more accurate classification of breast cancer subtypes and prediction of clinical outcomes. These advances facilitate personalized treatment approaches and provide insights into the molecular basis of breast cancer heterogeneity.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Identifying Classes and Genes for Predicting Clinical Outcome  

The invention provides methods for identifying classes of breast cancer patients and specific genes predictive of clinical outcomes based on comparison with normal breast tissue gene expression profiles. Through comprehensive analysis of microdissected normal epithelium and stroma, the inventors established reference gene expression signatures that serve as benchmarks for tumor classification.  

The methods involve obtaining gene expression data from a patient's tumor sample and comparing it to the normal tissue signatures disclosed herein. Similarity to the normal epithelial signature identifies tumors with basal-like characteristics and poor prognosis, while similarity to the stromal signature characterizes luminal and HER2-positive subtypes. The degree of similarity to each normal tissue compartment provides quantitative measures for tumor classification and outcome prediction.  

### Cancers  

The methods and compositions of the invention are particularly applicable to breast cancers, including but not limited to invasive ductal carcinoma, invasive lobular carcinoma, and other histological subtypes. The invention enables classification of breast tumors into molecular subtypes including luminal A, luminal B, HER2-positive, and basal-like categories based on their gene expression similarity to normal epithelial or stromal components.  

The invention is particularly valuable for identifying and characterizing triple-negative (ER/PR/HER2-negative) breast cancers, which show strong molecular similarity to normal epithelium and are associated with poor clinical outcomes. The methods provide improved prognostic stratification for this challenging patient population.  

### Nucleic Acid Compositions  

The invention provides nucleic acid compositions useful for detecting and analyzing the normal tissue gene expression signatures. These compositions include sets of polynucleotides corresponding to the genes comprising the epithelial and stromal signatures, as well as subsets thereof suitable for specific diagnostic or prognostic applications.  

The nucleic acid compositions may include DNA, RNA, or synthetic analogs thereof, and may be provided in various forms including purified polynucleotides, arrays, primer sets, or probe collections. Particular embodiments comprise sets of polynucleotides corresponding to the 883-gene normal tissue signature or subsets thereof showing tissue-specific expression patterns.  

### SDPP Genes and Nucleic Acids  

The invention identifies specific genes demonstrating statistically significant differential expression patterns (SDPP genes) between normal breast epithelium and stroma. These genes serve as molecular markers for tissue identity and provide the basis for tumor classification methods.  

Key SDPP genes include those associated with epithelial cell-cell junctions and basal lamina (e.g., various keratins), epithelial differentiation markers, extracellular matrix components, angiogenesis-related genes, and wound response genes. The invention provides specific nucleic acid sequences corresponding to these markers and methods for their use in diagnostic applications.  

### Nucleic Acids, Primers and Probes  

The invention provides specific primers and probes for detecting and quantifying expression of the normal tissue signature genes. These include primer pairs suitable for quantitative PCR analysis, fluorescently labeled probes for in situ hybridization, and other detection reagents.  

Particular embodiments provide primer/probe sets optimized for multiplex analysis of epithelial or stromal signature genes. These enable simultaneous quantification of multiple markers in clinical samples, facilitating efficient tumor classification and prognosis prediction.  

### Polypeptide Binding Compositions  

The invention further provides polypeptide binding compositions, including antibodies and other affinity reagents, specific for proteins encoded by the normal tissue signature genes. These compositions enable protein-level detection of tissue-specific markers and complement nucleic acid-based detection methods.  

Key embodiments include antibodies against epithelial markers (e.g., Kit) and stromal markers (e.g., von Willebrand factor, CD31). These may be used individually or in panels for immunohistochemical analysis of tissue samples.  

### Microarrays  

The invention provides microarray compositions comprising probes for detecting the normal tissue gene expression signatures. These include both DNA microarrays for gene expression profiling and protein microarrays for detecting signature gene products.  

Particular embodiments comprise custom microarrays containing probes for the complete 883-gene normal tissue signature or subsets thereof selected for specific diagnostic applications. These arrays enable comprehensive analysis of tumor samples relative to normal tissue benchmarks.  

### Methods of Diagnosis  

The invention provides novel methods for diagnosing and classifying breast cancers based on comparison with normal tissue gene expression signatures. These methods involve:  

1. Obtaining gene expression data from a patient's tumor sample  
2. Comparing the expression profile to reference normal epithelial and stromal signatures  
3. Classifying the tumor based on similarity to each normal tissue compartment  
4. Assigning molecular subtype and prognostic characteristics  

The methods enable identification of high-risk basal-like tumors that show strong molecular similarity to normal epithelium. Diagnostic applications include initial tumor classification, residual disease detection, and recurrence monitoring.  

### Methods of Assigning or Selecting Treatment  

The invention further provides methods for assigning or selecting breast cancer treatments based on tumor classification using normal tissue signatures. These methods facilitate personalized therapy by:  

1. Identifying patients with basal-like tumors likely to benefit from aggressive therapy  
2. Stratifying patients for targeted treatment approaches  
3. Predicting response to specific therapeutic agents  
4. Monitoring treatment efficacy through molecular profiling  

Key applications include selection of chemotherapy regimens for triple-negative cancers and identification of patients who may benefit from novel targeted agents.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Identification of Normal Breast Tissue Gene Expression Signatures**  

Laser capture microdissection was used to isolate pure populations of epithelial and stromal cells from 34 breast cancer patients and 10 reduction mammoplasty patients. RNA was extracted, amplified, and hybridized to whole genome microarrays.  

Analysis revealed 883 genes showing statistically significant differential expression between epithelium and stroma (false discovery rate adjusted p < 1e-5). Epithelial markers included genes associated with cell junctions and differentiation, while stromal markers included extracellular matrix components and angiogenesis factors.  

Notably, normal tissue from cancer patients showed no significant differences from reduction mammoplasty tissue, indicating absence of cancer-associated field effects at the transcriptional level.  

### Example 2  

**Classification of Breast Tumors Using Normal Tissue Signatures**  

The normal tissue signatures were used to analyze gene expression data from 295 breast carcinomas. Tumors clustered into two main groups:  

1. A large cluster (257 samples) showing enrichment for stromal signature genes  
2. A small cluster (38 samples) showing enrichment for epithelial signature genes  

The epithelial-like cluster consisted entirely of ER/PR/HER2-negative tumors corresponding to basal-like and normal-like subtypes with poor survival (p = 0.000489). This demonstrates the utility of normal tissue signatures for identifying high-risk tumor subtypes.  

### Example 3  

**Immunohistochemical Validation of Tissue Markers**  

Key proteins encoded by signature genes were validated by immunohistochemistry:  

- Epithelial marker Kit showed specific expression in epithelium  
- Stromal markers von Willebrand factor and CD31 showed elevated expression in stroma  

This confirms the microarray findings at the protein level and demonstrates the utility of these markers for tissue classification.  

### Example 4  

**Prognostic Utility of Normal Tissue Signatures**  

Comparison of clinical outcomes revealed that tumors classified as epithelial-like based on normal tissue signatures had significantly worse survival than stromal-like tumors (p = 0.000489). Multivariate analysis confirmed the signature-based classification as an independent prognostic factor beyond standard clinical parameters.  

This demonstrates the clinical utility of normal tissue signatures for prognosis prediction and risk stratification.
