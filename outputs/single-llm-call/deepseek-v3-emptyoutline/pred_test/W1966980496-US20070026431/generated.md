# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cell cycle regulation and protein degradation pathways in cancer cells. More specifically, the invention discloses novel mechanisms for cyclin D1 degradation mediated through phosphorylation by MAPK signaling and subsequent ubiquitination by the FBXW8-containing SCF-like ubiquitin ligase complex.  

Cyclin D1 is a critical regulator of cell cycle progression that controls the G1 to S phase transition. Its overexpression has been observed in numerous malignancies, making it an important target for cancer therapeutics. While the transcriptional regulation of cyclin D1 has been well-characterized, the mechanisms controlling its protein degradation remain incompletely understood. Prior studies have identified Thr286 phosphorylation as being important for cyclin D1 ubiquitination and degradation, with glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK3β) proposed as the responsible kinase. However, recent evidence has questioned the role of GSK3β in this process.  

The ubiquitin-proteasome system represents the major pathway for controlled protein degradation in eukaryotic cells. Polyubiquitination requires the sequential action of three enzymes: E1 (ubiquitin-activating enzyme), E2 (ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), and E3 (ubiquitin ligase). Among E3 ligases, the SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box protein) complexes represent a well-characterized family that targets phosphorylated substrates for degradation. A related SCF-like complex containing CUL7 and FBXW8 has been identified but its physiological substrates remain largely unknown.  

There exists an unmet need in the art to fully elucidate the molecular mechanisms governing cyclin D1 degradation, particularly in cancer cells where cyclin D1 regulation is frequently dysregulated. The present invention addresses this need by identifying the MAPK signaling pathway as the kinase responsible for Thr286 phosphorylation and the FBXW8-containing SCF-like complex as the specific E3 ligase that recognizes phosphorylated cyclin D1 for ubiquitination and degradation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods relating to the regulation of cyclin D1 protein stability in cancer cells. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. The discovery that cyclin D1 is specifically destabilized during S phase in cancer cells through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, while remaining stable in normal cells.  

2. The identification of MAPK (ERK2) as the kinase responsible for phosphorylating cyclin D1 at Thr286 through interaction with a newly identified D-domain (amino acids 179-193) in cyclin D1.  

3. The demonstration that the F-box protein FBXW8 specifically recognizes phosphorylated cyclin D1 and mediates its ubiquitination through formation of either:  
   a) An SCF complex with CUL1, SKP1 and RBX1, or  
   b) An SCF-like complex with CUL7, SKP1 and RBX1.  

4. Methods for modulating cyclin D1 stability by targeting:  
   a) The MAPK phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286  
   b) The interaction between cyclin D1 and FBXW8  
   c) The assembly or activity of the FBXW8-containing ubiquitin ligase complexes  

5. Therapeutic applications for treating cancers characterized by cyclin D1 dysregulation by targeting the FBXW8-mediated degradation pathway.  

The invention further provides isolated nucleic acids encoding the relevant proteins, expression vectors, host cells, antibodies, and screening methods for identifying modulators of this pathway.

## DEFINITIONS  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"FBXW8" refers to F-Box WD-40 Domain Protein 8, an F-box protein containing WD40 repeats that serves as the substrate recognition component of SCF or SCF-like ubiquitin ligase complexes targeting phosphorylated cyclin D1.  

"MAPK" refers to Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase, specifically ERK2, which phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286 through interaction with the D-domain.  

"CULLIN 1 (CUL1)" refers to the scaffold protein that forms part of the canonical SCF ubiquitin ligase complex.  

"Cullin 7 (CUL7)" refers to the scaffold protein that forms part of the SCF-like ubiquitin ligase complex with FBXW8.  

"SKP1" refers to S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1, an adaptor protein that links F-box proteins to cullins in ubiquitin ligase complexes.  

"Cyclin D1" refers to the cell cycle regulatory protein that controls G1/S transition and is the substrate for FBXW8-mediated ubiquitination when phosphorylated at Thr286.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### FBXW8 Protein (F-BOX WD-40 Domain Protein 8)  

FBXW8 is an F-box protein containing WD40 repeat domains that serves as the substrate recognition component of SCF or SCF-like ubiquitin ligase complexes. The invention demonstrates that FBXW8 specifically recognizes cyclin D1 phosphorylated at Thr286 and mediates its ubiquitination. FBXW8 can form complexes with either CUL1 (forming a canonical SCF complex) or CUL7 (forming an SCF-like complex). The WD40 repeats of FBXW8 are responsible for phospho-dependent substrate recognition.  

### MAPK (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase)  

The invention identifies MAPK (specifically ERK2) as the kinase responsible for phosphorylating cyclin D1 at Thr286. This phosphorylation requires interaction between MAPK and a D-domain (amino acids 179-193) in cyclin D1. The D-domain contains characteristic basic and hydrophobic residues that mediate binding to MAPK. The invention demonstrates that MAPK activity is required for efficient cyclin D1 ubiquitination and that inhibition of MAPK signaling stabilizes cyclin D1 in cancer cells.  

### CULLIN 1 (CUL1)  

CUL1 serves as the scaffold protein for canonical SCF ubiquitin ligase complexes. The invention shows that CUL1 can form a complex with SKP1, FBXW8 and RBX1 that ubiquitinates phosphorylated cyclin D1. Depletion of CUL1 leads to stabilization of cyclin D1 and inhibition of cancer cell proliferation.  

### Cullin 7 (CUL7)  

CUL7 serves as the scaffold protein for SCF-like ubiquitin ligase complexes containing FBXW8. The invention demonstrates that CUL7 forms an alternative complex with SKP1, FBXW8 and RBX1 that also ubiquitinates phosphorylated cyclin D1. Like CUL1 depletion, reduction of CUL7 expression stabilizes cyclin D1 and inhibits cancer cell growth.  

### SKP1 (S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1)  

SKP1 is the adaptor protein that links F-box proteins (like FBXW8) to cullin scaffolds (CUL1 or CUL7) in ubiquitin ligase complexes. The invention shows that SKP1 is essential for cyclin D1 ubiquitination, as siRNA-mediated depletion of SKP1 leads to cyclin D1 accumulation. SKP1 interacts with both FBXW8 and the cullin scaffolds to form functional ubiquitin ligase complexes.  

### Cyclin D1  

Cyclin D1 is the key substrate in this pathway. The invention demonstrates that cyclin D1 is specifically destabilized during S phase in cancer cells through phosphorylation at Thr286 by MAPK and subsequent recognition by FBXW8. The T286A mutant of cyclin D1 is resistant to ubiquitination. The invention identifies a D-domain (amino acids 179-193) in cyclin D1 that is required for efficient MAPK phosphorylation.  

### Nucleic Acid Molecules, Polypeptide Production Methods, Expression Vectors, Fusion Proteins  

The invention provides isolated nucleic acids encoding wild-type and mutant forms of the proteins in this pathway, including FBXW8, MAPK, CUL1, CUL7, SKP1 and cyclin D1. Methods for producing these polypeptides include recombinant expression in suitable host cells using appropriate expression vectors. The invention encompasses fusion proteins such as GST-tagged versions used in the experimental demonstrations.  

### Covalently Modified Proteins  

The invention includes covalently modified forms of the relevant proteins, particularly phosphorylated cyclin D1 (at Thr286) and ubiquitinated cyclin D1. The invention provides methods for producing these modified forms both in vitro and in vivo.  

### Components for Polyubitquitination Assays  

The invention provides all necessary components for reconstituting the polyubiquitination of cyclin D1 in vitro, including: E1 ubiquitin-activating enzyme, E2 conjugating enzymes (particularly UbcH5C), ubiquitin, ATP, FBXW8-containing E3 ligase complexes, and phosphorylated cyclin D1 substrate.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Cyclin D1 Protein is Destabilized Specifically in S Phase in Cancer Cells  

Example 1 demonstrates that cyclin D1 is specifically destabilized during S phase in cancer cell lines (HCT 116, SW480, T98G) but remains stable in normal cell lines (NIH 3T3, WI-38, CCD841 CoN). Pulse-chase experiments show the half-life of cyclin D1 decreases from 27.5 minutes in G1 phase to 11.8 minutes in S phase in HCT 116 cells, while remaining stable in NIH 3T3 cells throughout the cell cycle.  

### Cyclin D1 Protein is Degraded During the S Phase Through the Ubiquitin-proteasome Pathway in Cancer Cells  

Example 2 shows that treatment with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 causes accumulation of cyclin D1 specifically in S phase in cancer cells. Immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrate polyubiquitinated forms of cyclin D1 that increase with MG132 treatment. Fractionation studies show cyclin D1 is predominantly ubiquitinated and degraded in the cytoplasm during S phase.  

### Expression of Cyclin D1 Protein in the Nucleus Decreases Through the G1-S Transition in Tumor Cells  

Example 3 reveals that cyclin D1 localizes predominantly to the nucleus during G1 phase but exits to the cytoplasm during S phase in cancer cells. Immunofluorescence studies show colocalization of FBXW8 (cytoplasmic) with cyclin D1 during S phase but not G1 phase, explaining the phase-specific degradation.  

### Phosphorylation of Cyclin D1 Protein is Mediated by the Same Mechanism as Cyclin D1 Gene Expression, Via the MAPK Signaling Pathway  

Example 4 demonstrates that MAPK (ERK2) phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286 through interaction with a D-domain (amino acids 179-193). In vitro kinase assays show ERK2 phosphorylates wild-type cyclin D1 but not T286A or ΔD mutants. Inhibition of MAPK signaling stabilizes cyclin D1 in cancer cells.  

### Example 5  

Example 5 shows that FBXW8 specifically associates with phosphorylated cyclin D1 and mediates its ubiquitination through both SCF (CUL1-containing) and SCF-like (CUL7-containing) complexes. Depletion of FBXW8, CUL1 or CUL7 stabilizes cyclin D1 and inhibits cancer cell proliferation. Expression of nuclear-localized T286A cyclin D1 rescues the proliferation defect caused by FBXW8 inhibition.
