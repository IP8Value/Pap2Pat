# DESCRIPTION  

## Materials and Methods  

### Antibodies, Inhibitors and Product Used  

The invention utilizes specific antibodies, inhibitors, and products to evaluate the role of CCR3 in prostate cancer (PCa) metastasis. Monoclonal antibodies directed against CCR3 (clone 444-11), CXCR1 (clone 42705), CXCR2 (clone 48311), and CXCR4 (clone 44716) were employed to block receptor activity. Pharmacological inhibitors included the CCR1/CCR3 inhibitor UCB35625, CCR2 inhibitor sc-202525, CXCR1/2 inhibitor SB225002, and CXCR4 inhibitor AMD3100. These reagents were used at concentrations previously validated for their efficacy in inhibiting receptor-mediated signaling.  

### Cell Culture  

Human prostate cancer cell lines C4-2B and PC-3 were cultured in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) and 1% streptomycin-penicillin. Cells were maintained in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37°C and were used within two months after thawing to ensure consistent experimental conditions.  

### Ex Vivo Differentiated Medullary Adipocyte Model  

Primary human bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) were isolated from patients undergoing hip replacement surgery. Adipocytes were purified by flotation and washed extensively to remove hematopoietic contaminants. Conditioned media (CM) were prepared by incubating BM-Ads in DMEM supplemented with 1% BSA overnight. This model recapitulates the secretory profile of BM-Ads and their interaction with prostate cancer cells.  

### Human Medullary Adipocyte Samples  

BM-Ad samples were obtained from male patients aged 51–73 years with a body mass index (BMI) of 20–25 kg/m². Additional samples were collected from obese (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²) and elderly (≥70 years) patients to assess the impact of metabolic and age-related changes on adipocyte function.  

### Human Visceral Adipose Tissue Samples for Analysis of the Expression of CCL7 by Q-PCR  

Visceral adipose tissue (VAT) samples were analyzed for CCL7 expression using quantitative PCR (Q-PCR). RNA was extracted, reverse-transcribed, and amplified using gene-specific primers. CCL7 expression levels were normalized to housekeeping genes and compared across different patient cohorts.  

### Human Periprostatic Adipose Tissue Samples  

Periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT) was collected from prostatectomy specimens. PPAT-conditioned media were prepared and used in migration assays to compare chemoattractant properties with BM-Ad secretions.  

### Human Yellow Marrow and Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue Samples  

Yellow marrow and subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) samples were processed similarly to BM-Ads. Conditioned media from these tissues were tested for their ability to induce prostate cancer cell migration.  

### Mouse Perigonadal Adipose Tissue Samples  

Perigonadal adipose tissue from mice was used to validate findings in a preclinical model. Tissue-conditioned media were prepared and tested in migration assays with murine prostate cancer cell lines.  

### Measurement of Genic Expression by Q-PCR  

Gene expression analysis was performed using Q-PCR with primers specific for CCR3, CCL7, and other chemokine receptors. RNA was isolated from cells or tissues, reverse-transcribed, and quantified using SYBR Green chemistry.  

## Results  

### The Ligand of CCR3, CCL7 is Involved in the Migration of Prostate Tumor Cells Against Conditioned Medium of Adipocytes  

Conditioned media from BM-Ads significantly enhanced the migration of PC-3 and C4-2B prostate cancer cells. This effect was abrogated by CCR3 blockade using neutralizing antibodies or pharmacological inhibitors. CCL7, the primary ligand of CCR3, was identified as the key chemokine responsible for this chemoattraction.  

### Validation of the Involvement of CCR3 in Migration in Response to Adipocyte Secretions in a Mouse Tumor Line Model (TRAMP-C1P3)  

The TRAMP-C1P3 murine prostate cancer line was used to confirm CCR3-dependent migration. BM-Ad-CM induced robust migration, which was inhibited by CCR3 blockade, reinforcing the role of this receptor in adipocyte-mediated tumor cell homing.  

### The Receptor CCR3 is Involved in Tumor Progression in Mouse Models In Vivo  

In vivo studies demonstrated that CCR3-expressing prostate cancer cells exhibited increased bone metastasis formation. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of CCR3 reduced metastatic burden, confirming its functional role in tumor dissemination.  

### Immunohistochemical Quantification of the Vitamin B12 Transport Protein (TCH), Cell Surface Receptor (TCH-R) and Ki-67 in Human Tumor Xenografts  

Immunohistochemical analysis of xenograft tumors revealed elevated CCR3 expression in metastatic lesions. Co-staining with proliferation markers (Ki-67) confirmed that CCR3-positive cells exhibited higher proliferative activity.  

### Sphingosine Kinase-1 Activity and Expression in Human Prostate Cancer Resection Specimens  

Sphingosine kinase-1 (SPHK1) activity was correlated with CCR3 expression in clinical specimens. High SPHK1 activity was associated with aggressive disease and bone metastasis, suggesting a synergistic role in tumor progression.  

### The Expression of the Receptor CCR3 Plays a Role in the Migration of Prostate Cancer Cells Induced by Medullary Adipocytes  

Meta-analysis of clinical datasets revealed that CCR3 expression was significantly upregulated in bone metastases compared to primary tumors. Immunohistochemical validation confirmed enrichment of CCR3-positive cells in metastatic bone lesions, supporting its role in directed migration.
