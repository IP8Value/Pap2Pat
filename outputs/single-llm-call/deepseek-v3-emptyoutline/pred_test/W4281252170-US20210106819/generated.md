# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of cancer diagnostics and therapeutics, specifically targeting phosphatidylserine (PS) as a biomarker for tumor imaging and treatment. More particularly, the invention encompasses methods and compositions for utilizing PS-targeting agents, including SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, monoclonal antibodies, and peptide-peptoid hybrids, in conjunction with electric field modulation to enhance the efficacy of cancer therapies. The invention further provides novel imaging modalities utilizing PS as a target for optical, magnetic resonance, and positron emission tomography/single-photon emission computed tomography (PET/SPECT) imaging.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with conventional treatments such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery often resulting in significant off-target effects and limited efficacy. Immunotherapy has emerged as a promising addition to cancer treatment modalities; however, the development of targeted therapies with minimal toxicity remains a critical unmet need. Biomarker-based strategies offer a solution by enabling selective targeting of cancer cells while sparing healthy tissues. Among potential biomarkers, phosphatidylserine (PS) has demonstrated unique utility due to its aberrant exposure on the surface of cancer cells, a phenomenon not observed in normal cells under physiological conditions.  

PS is a phospholipid typically localized to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane in healthy cells. In cancer cells, loss of membrane asymmetry leads to surface exposure of PS, which contributes to immune evasion, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. This surface PS exposure varies among cancer types and even within individual tumors, presenting an opportunity for diagnostic stratification and targeted intervention. Prior efforts to exploit PS as a therapeutic target have included monoclonal antibodies such as bavituximab and annexin-based imaging agents, though limitations such as short half-life and nonspecific binding have hindered clinical translation.  

There exists a need for improved PS-targeting strategies that enhance imaging sensitivity and therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects. The present invention addresses this need by integrating PS-targeting agents with advanced imaging techniques and electric field modulation to optimize tumor detection and treatment.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides novel methods and compositions for diagnosing and treating cancer by targeting phosphatidylserine (PS) on tumor cells. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. **PS-Targeting Agents**: Utilization of SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, monoclonal antibodies (e.g., bavituximab, PGN635), and peptide-peptoid hybrids (e.g., PPS1D1) to selectively bind surface-exposed PS on cancer cells for imaging and therapeutic applications.  
2. **Imaging Modalities**: Development of optical, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and PET/SPECT techniques employing PS-targeting agents to visualize tumors with high specificity.  
3. **Electric Field Modulation**: Application of non-contact direct current electric fields (DC-EF) to modulate surface PS levels, thereby sensitizing cancer cells to PS-targeting therapies.  
4. **Combination Therapies**: Integration of PS-targeting agents with conventional treatments such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy to enhance therapeutic outcomes.  

The invention further discloses specific formulations, dosages, and protocols for administering PS-targeting agents and electric field modulation in preclinical and clinical settings.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Phosphatidylserine  

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a phospholipid component of cell membranes, typically confined to the inner leaflet in healthy cells due to the activity of flippases and translocases. In cancer cells, dysregulation of membrane asymmetry leads to externalization of PS, which serves as a biomarker for malignancy and therapeutic targeting. The extent of PS exposure correlates with tumor aggressiveness and resistance to conventional therapies, making it a critical diagnostic and therapeutic target.  

### PS-Targeting Drugs  

The invention encompasses several classes of PS-targeting agents:  
- **SapC-DOPS Nanovesicles**: Comprising saposin C (SapC) embedded in dioleoylphosphatidylserine (DOPS) nanovesicles, these agents selectively bind PS on cancer cells under acidic conditions (pH ~6), a hallmark of the tumor microenvironment.  
- **Monoclonal Antibodies**: Bavituximab and PGN635 target PS-β2 glycoprotein 1 complexes, inducing immune-mediated tumor cell death.  
- **Peptide-Peptoid Hybrids**: PPS1D1, a dimeric peptide-peptoid hybrid, exhibits high affinity for PS and induces cytotoxicity in cancer cells.  

### Altering the Surface PS to Increase the Efficacy of PS Targeting Modalities  

The invention introduces electric field modulation as a novel method to enhance PS-targeting therapy. Application of non-contact DC-EF (75–150 V/cm) increases surface PS exposure on glioblastoma cells without affecting viability, thereby sensitizing tumors to PS-targeting agents. This approach avoids the thermal damage associated with traditional electroporation and enables combinatorial regimens with chemotherapy or immunotherapy.  

### Phosphatidylserine as a Tumor Imaging Target  

PS serves as a versatile target for tumor imaging across multiple modalities:  
- **Optical Imaging**: Fluorophore-labeled SapC-DOPS (e.g., CellVue Maroon) enables real-time visualization of tumors in preclinical models.  
- **MRI**: Gadolinium- or iron oxide-loaded SapC-DOPS enhances contrast in T1- and T2-weighted imaging, respectively.  
- **PET/SPECT**: Radiolabeled PS-targeting antibodies (e.g., 89Zr-PGN635) provide high-resolution tumor detection with low background signal.  

### Electric Field  

The invention employs low-amplitude, non-contact electric fields to modulate PS exposure. Unlike tumor-treating fields (TTFields) or irreversible electroporation, this method avoids cytotoxicity to normal cells while enhancing the binding of PS-targeting agents to tumors.  

### Optical Imaging  

Optical imaging using PS-targeting probes (e.g., IRDye 800CW-labeled PGN635) offers rapid, cost-effective tumor detection. Limitations such as tissue autofluorescence are addressed by incorporating dual-modality probes for simultaneous PET and optical imaging.  

### PET/SPECT Imaging  

Radiotracers such as 74As-bavituximab and 125I-SapC-DOPS enable quantitative assessment of tumor burden and treatment response. These agents exhibit prolonged circulation times and high tumor-to-background ratios, making them suitable for clinical translation.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Optical Imaging of Glioblastoma Using SapC-DOPS-CVM**: Orthotopic glioblastoma models were injected with CellVue Maroon-labeled SapC-DOPS and imaged using a rotational bed system. Tumor-specific fluorescence was detected at 10° angles, with signal intensity correlating with disease progression.  

### Example 2  

**MRI of Neuroblastoma with SapC-DOPS-IO**: Mice bearing neuroblastoma xenografts received ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide (USPIO)-loaded SapC-DOPS. T2*-weighted imaging at 7T revealed a 5-fold increase in iron concentration within tumors compared to controls.  

### Example 3  

**PET Imaging with 89Zr-PGN635**: In murine models of apoptosis-inducing tumors, 89Zr-PGN635 accumulation reached 30% ID/g tissue, with tumor-to-blood ratios of 13.  

### Example 4  

**DC-EF Modulation of PS in Glioblastoma**: U87ΔEGFR-Luc cells exposed to 150 V/cm DC-EF exhibited a 2.5-fold increase in surface PS, enhancing SapC-DOPS binding and cytotoxicity.  

### Example 5  

**Combination Therapy with Bavituximab and Electroporation**: Irreversible electroporation (IRE) increased PS exposure in lung adenocarcinoma, potentiating the effects of bavituximab and docetaxel in vivo.
