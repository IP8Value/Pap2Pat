# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of genetic diagnostics, particularly to methods for early detection of Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) in newborns. More specifically, the invention provides a multiplex quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assay for identifying hemi(homo)-zygotic deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 of the DMD gene. This assay enables the identification of newborns who may benefit from exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, and 53, thereby facilitating early intervention and improved therapeutic outcomes.  

## BACKGROUND  

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) is a severe X-linked recessive disorder characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness, primarily affecting males. The disease is caused by mutations in the DMD gene, which encodes the dystrophin protein essential for maintaining muscle fiber integrity. Approximately 60–70% of DMD cases result from large deletions of one or more exons in the DMD gene. The absence of functional dystrophin leads to muscle wasting, loss of ambulation, and premature death, typically by the third decade of life.  

Current therapeutic strategies for DMD include exon-skipping therapies, which aim to restore the reading frame of the DMD gene by skipping specific exons, thereby producing a truncated but partially functional dystrophin protein. Approved therapies such as eteplirsen (Exondys) and golodirsen (Vyondys 53) target exons 51 and 53, respectively, and are effective only in patients with specific deletion patterns. Early diagnosis is critical for maximizing the therapeutic benefits of these treatments, as initiating therapy before symptom onset can significantly delay disease progression.  

Traditional newborn screening for DMD relies on creatine kinase (CK) or its muscular isoform (CKMM) assays, which have limitations in sensitivity and specificity. These assays often yield false positives or negatives, necessitating confirmatory genetic testing. Moreover, CK-based screening does not distinguish between patients who are amenable to exon-skipping therapies and those who are not. There is thus a need for a more accurate and targeted screening method that can identify DMD patients eligible for exon-skipping therapies at birth.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing DMD screening methods by providing a multiplex qPCR assay designed to detect deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 of the DMD gene. These exons are strategically selected because their deletion patterns are indicative of patients who may benefit from exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, and 53. The assay is performed on dried blood spots (DBS), making it suitable for large-scale newborn screening programs.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **High Sensitivity and Specificity**: The assay correctly identifies all subjects with or without deletions of the target exons, achieving 100% sensitivity and specificity in validation studies.  
2. **Early Intervention**: By enabling early diagnosis, the assay allows for the timely initiation of exon-skipping therapies, which are most effective when administered pre-symptomatically.  
3. **Cost-Effectiveness**: The assay is economically viable, with a cost of less than 5 euros per sample, and can be integrated into existing newborn screening protocols.  
4. **Scalability**: The qPCR-based method is robust, high-throughput, and easily adaptable to population-wide screening.  

The invention also includes a two-step validation protocol for samples showing no amplification of any target exon, ensuring accurate results. Positive findings are confirmed using multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA), a commercially available assay, to precisely characterize the deletion and confirm eligibility for exon-skipping therapy.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention comprises a multiplex qPCR assay for detecting deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 of the DMD gene. The assay is performed on DNA extracted from dried blood spots (DBS), which are routinely collected for newborn screening. The following embodiments describe the technical details and implementation of the assay:  

1. **Primer and Probe Design**:  
   - Primers and probes are designed to amplify and detect each of the five target exons (44, 46, 50, 52, and 54) in a single multiplex reaction.  
   - The sequences of the primers and probes are optimized to minimize polymorphisms and ensure high specificity (see Table 3 in the research paper).  
   - Fluorescent labels (e.g., FAM, YakimaYellow, ROX, Atto 647N, Atto 700) are used to distinguish the amplification products of each exon.  

2. **DNA Extraction and qPCR Protocol**:  
   - DNA is extracted from a single 3.1-mm DBS using a standardized protocol.  
   - The qPCR reaction mix includes Takyon master mix, primers, probes, and 1 µL of extracted DNA in a total volume of 25 µL.  
   - Amplification is performed on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR System under the following conditions: 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds and 61°C for 75 seconds.  

3. **Data Interpretation**:  
   - The absence of amplification for a target exon indicates a deletion of that exon.  
   - A normalized fluorescence ratio (NFR) is calculated by dividing the endpoint fluorescence of each sample by the median endpoint fluorescence of the corresponding exon in the run. An NFR below 0.2 is considered indicative of a deletion.  

4. **Validation Protocol**:  
   - Samples showing no amplification for any of the five exons are re-tested in a 2-plex qPCR reaction combining each exon with the reference gene RPP30 to confirm the deletion.  
   - Positive results are confirmed on a fresh EDTA blood sample using MLPA to precisely map the deletion and determine eligibility for exon-skipping therapy.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—DMD Genotyping Assay  

A validation study was conducted using 120 samples, including 51 male DMD patients, 50 female carriers, and 19 healthy controls. The assay correctly identified all 34 DMD patients with deletions overlapping at least one of the target exons (exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54) by the absence of amplification for the corresponding exon. The remaining 17 DMD patients, who had deletions of non-target exons, showed normal amplification for all five exons. Female carriers and controls also exhibited normal amplification profiles. The assay demonstrated 100% sensitivity and specificity in this cohort.  

### Example 2—DMD Genotyping Assay  

The assay was further validated using samples from the LOVD-DMD database, which includes over 26,000 patient entries. Analysis revealed that 54% of patients with deletions overlapping at least one target exon (exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54) would benefit from exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, or 53. This confirms the clinical utility of the assay in identifying treatable DMD patients at birth.
