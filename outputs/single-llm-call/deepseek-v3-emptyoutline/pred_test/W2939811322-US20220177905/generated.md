## TECHNICAL FIELD  
The present invention relates generally to the field of genetic engineering and gene drive systems. More specifically, the invention provides novel compositions and methods for achieving super-Mendelian inheritance of allelic variants through two distinct allelic-drive mechanisms: copy-cutting and copy-grafting. These systems enable efficient propagation of subtle genetic modifications, including single nucleotide polymorphisms, throughout populations of organisms.  

## BACKGROUND  
Current gene-drive systems based on CRISPR-Cas9 technology are limited to copying relatively large DNA cargo sequences (approximately 1-10 kilobases). However, many desirable genetic traits result from allelic variants that alter only one or a few base pairs. Existing systems lack efficient mechanisms for achieving super-Mendelian inheritance of such subtle genetic variants. There exists an unmet need for genetic drive systems capable of propagating point mutations and other small genetic modifications through populations.  

Prior attempts to develop allelic-drive systems have faced several technical challenges. First, many naturally occurring allelic variants cannot be selectively targeted by guide RNAs due to insufficient sequence differences. Second, non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) repair mechanisms often generate drive-resistant alleles that reduce drive efficiency. Third, conventional gene drive systems often show reduced efficiency when attempting to drive multiple genetic elements simultaneously. The present invention overcomes these limitations through novel mechanisms of action and system configurations.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides two distinct but complementary allelic-drive systems: copy-cutting and copy-grafting. The copy-cutting system involves a Cas9-gRNA complex that selectively cuts one allelic variant, followed by homology-directed repair (HDR)-mediated replacement with a non-cleavable allele of the same gene provided in trans. The copy-grafting system involves copying a short genomic interval encompassing a favored allele in proximity to a gRNA cut site, where the favored allele is associated with neighboring sequences resistant to gRNA cleavage.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1) The ability to drive single nucleotide polymorphisms and other small genetic variants with high efficiency  
2) A novel co-drive phenomenon that maintains linkage between drive elements and desired alleles  
3) A lethal mosaicism mechanism that eliminates non-functional NHEJ-induced alleles  
4) Shadow-drive capability extending drive effects for an additional generation  
5) Flexibility to work in both cis and trans configurations  

The systems are particularly useful for applications including: assembling complex genotypes for research purposes, propagating favorable traits in agricultural species, and reversing pesticide resistance in pest populations. The invention represents a significant advance over existing gene drive technologies by enabling precise genetic modifications to spread through populations with unprecedented efficiency.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides novel genetic drive systems capable of achieving super-Mendelian inheritance of allelic variants through two distinct mechanisms: copy-cutting and copy-grafting. These systems overcome limitations of conventional gene drive technologies by enabling efficient propagation of subtle genetic modifications, including single nucleotide polymorphisms.  

The copy-cutting system comprises a genetic construct containing at least one guide RNA (gRNA) sequence designed to selectively target a sensitive allele while sparing a desired resistant allele. The system further includes a source of Cas9 endonuclease, which may be provided in cis or in trans. When the system is present in a heterozygous individual carrying both sensitive and resistant alleles, the gRNA directs Cas9-mediated cleavage of the sensitive allele. The cleaved allele is then repaired using the resistant allele as a template through homology-directed repair, resulting in conversion of the sensitive allele to the resistant form.  

The copy-grafting system operates through a distinct but complementary mechanism. This system involves a gRNA that cuts near, but not at, the site of a desired allelic variant. The desired allele is linked to sequences that render it resistant to gRNA-mediated cleavage. Following cutting of the sensitive allele, homology-directed repair copies a short genomic interval encompassing both the cleavage-resistant site and the desired allele from the donor chromosome to the receiver chromosome.  

Both systems exhibit several novel and advantageous features. First, they demonstrate a co-drive phenomenon wherein copying of the drive element and conversion of the target allele are strongly correlated. This correlation depends on chromosome pairing and is abolished when the receiver chromosome carries inversions affecting the targeted gene. Second, the systems exhibit lethal mosaicism, a mechanism that dominantly eliminates non-functional NHEJ-induced alleles through maternal effects. Third, the systems display shadow-drive capability, where perduring Cas9-gRNA complexes transmitted maternally can extend drive effects for an additional generation.  

The systems are highly flexible in their configuration. They function effectively whether the drive elements and desired alleles are arranged in cis or in trans. The systems are compatible with various sources of Cas9, including vasaCas9 and nanosCas9 transgenes. The invention also encompasses the use of Cas9 variants with broadened PAM specificities to increase the range of targetable alleles.  

### EXAMPLES  

Example 1 demonstrates a copy-cutting system targeting the Drosophila Notch locus. The system comprises a DsRed-marked CopyCat element inserted at the yellow locus, containing two gRNAs: one directing copying of the CopyCat element (gRNA-y) and another (gRNA-N+) targeting the wild-type Notch allele. The system drives conversion of the wild-type allele to a cleavage-resistant NAx16 allele with approximately 93.6% efficiency in males and 87.5% in females, exceeding the copying efficiency of the CopyCat element itself.  

Example 2 illustrates the lethal mosaicism mechanism. When the system generates NHEJ-induced loss-of-function Notch alleles, these alleles are dominantly eliminated in progeny inheriting both the drive element and a zygotic source of Cas9. This mechanism effectively prevents propagation of drive-resistant null alleles.  

Example 3 shows the copy-grafting system driving conversion of a cleavage-sensitive NAxE2 allele to a wild-type N+IS allele. The system achieves approximately 78.2% conversion efficiency, comparable to the copy-cutting system. This demonstrates the general applicability of the invention to drive various allelic configurations.  

Example 4 characterizes the shadow-drive phenomenon. When F2 females not inheriting the Cas9 transgene are crossed to N+IS males, their F3 progeny show substantial residual drive activity, including approximately 30% conversion of receiver chromosomes. This demonstrates the persistence of drive effects beyond the initial generation.  

Example 5 examines the co-drive phenomenon. Analysis reveals that when the CopyCat element copies to a receiver chromosome, the NAx16 allele also copies in approximately 92.2% of cases, nearly double the rate observed without CopyCat copying. This strong correlation maintains linkage between drive elements and desired alleles.  

The examples collectively demonstrate that the invention provides a robust and flexible platform for achieving super-Mendelian inheritance of allelic variants. The systems are particularly valuable for applications requiring precise genetic modifications or combinations of multiple favorable alleles. Potential applications include agricultural improvement, pest control, and biomedical research.  

The invention encompasses various modifications and alternative embodiments. For instance, the systems may incorporate multiple gRNAs to target several loci simultaneously or to provide redundant targeting of a single locus. The drive elements may be configured to include functional recoded portions of essential genes to protect against lethal mosaicism while still eliminating non-functional alleles. These and other modifications fall within the scope of the present invention.
