# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Alzheimer’s disease represents a significant global health challenge, characterized by the accumulation of amyloid plaques composed primarily of amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides in the brain. Among these peptides, the 42-amino acid Aβ42 variant is particularly pathogenic due to its propensity to form neurotoxic oligomers. Historically, research focused on insoluble fibrillar aggregates as the primary pathogenic agents; however, emerging evidence indicates that soluble, low-molecular-weight oligomers of Aβ42 are the principal neurotoxic species. These oligomers, including dimers, trimers, and tetramers, form during early stages of Aβ42 aggregation and are implicated in synaptic dysfunction and neuronal death.  

Current therapeutic strategies targeting Aβ42 aggregation have largely concentrated on inhibiting fibril formation, yet these approaches may inadvertently promote the accumulation of toxic oligomeric intermediates. A critical unmet need exists for methods that specifically inhibit the earliest stages of Aβ42 oligomerization, thereby preventing the formation of neurotoxic species. Existing models for studying Aβ42 oligomerization, such as in vitro assays or bacterial systems, lack the biological complexity necessary to accurately recapitulate the oligomerization process in eukaryotic cells.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a novel yeast-based model system that enables the study of Aβ42 oligomerization in a eukaryotic cellular environment. This system leverages the well-characterized yeast prion protein Sup35p, whose translational termination activity serves as a sensitive reporter for Aβ42-induced oligomerization. By fusing Aβ42 to the essential MRF domain of Sup35p, the invention creates a functional assay wherein oligomerization of the fusion protein impairs translational termination, yielding a measurable phenotypic output. This system facilitates high-throughput screening of compounds and genetic modifiers that selectively inhibit early Aβ42 oligomerization, offering a powerful tool for therapeutic development.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a yeast-based model system for studying the oligomerization of the amyloid-β peptide Aβ42, comprising a fusion protein wherein Aβ42 is linked to the MRF domain of the yeast translation termination factor Sup35p. The fusion protein, termed AβMRF, forms SDS-stable low-n oligomers that impair the translational termination activity of Sup35p, resulting in a detectable phenotypic change in yeast growth and color. This system enables the identification of agents that modulate Aβ42 oligomerization by monitoring the restoration of Sup35p function.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **AβMRF Fusion Protein**: A construct in which the Aβ42 peptide is fused to the MRF domain of Sup35p, wherein oligomerization of Aβ42 induces the formation of SDS-stable low-n oligomers (dimers, trimers, and tetramers) detectable by immunoblotting.  
2. **Functional Assay**: The use of Sup35p’s translational termination activity as a reporter, wherein oligomerization of AβMRF leads to readthrough of a premature stop codon in the *ade1-14* allele, conferring adenine prototrophy and a white colony phenotype.  
3. **Modulation by Aggregation-Disrupting Mutations**: Introduction of point mutations (e.g., F19,20T or F19,20T/I31P) in the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF inhibits oligomerization and restores translational termination activity, yielding a red colony phenotype.  
4. **Chemical and Genetic Screens**: The system is responsive to guanidine hydrochloride, which enhances AβMRF oligomerization, and to deletion of *HSP104*, which reduces oligomer levels, providing a platform for screening inhibitors of early Aβ42 aggregation.  

The invention further encompasses methods for using the AβMRF system to identify compounds or genetic perturbations that inhibit Aβ42 oligomerization, as well as applications in drug discovery for Alzheimer’s disease.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Model of Aβ42 Oligomerization  

The invention is based on a yeast model system in which the Aβ42 peptide is fused to the MRF domain of Sup35p, creating the AβMRF fusion protein. Sup35p is an essential translation termination factor in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, and its activity can be monitored using a nonsense-suppression assay. In this assay, the *ade1-14* allele contains a premature stop codon that is efficiently terminated by functional Sup35p, resulting in adenine auxotrophy (Ade−) and accumulation of a red pigment. Impairment of Sup35p activity, such as through prion aggregation ([PSI+]), leads to readthrough of the stop codon, conferring adenine prototrophy (Ade+) and a white colony phenotype.  

The AβMRF fusion protein mimics the oligomerization behavior of free Aβ42, forming SDS-stable low-n oligomers (dimers, trimers, and tetramers) that resist dissociation under mild SDS treatment. These oligomers are detectable by immunoblotting using antibodies against the Aβ42 peptide or the Sup35p RF domain. Oligomerization of AβMRF disrupts its translational termination activity, resulting in a white, Ade+ phenotype analogous to [PSI+] cells. In contrast, mutations in the Aβ42 portion (e.g., F19,20T or F19,20T/I31P) disrupt oligomerization, restoring MRF activity and yielding a red, Ade− phenotype.  

The system recapitulates key features of Aβ42 oligomerization in mammalian systems, including:  
- **SDS-Stable Oligomers**: AβMRF oligomers resemble those formed by Aβ42 in vitro and in human brain tissue, as evidenced by their migration patterns in SDS-PAGE and agarose gels.  
- **Epitope Accessibility**: Antibody recognition of AβMRF oligomers decreases with increasing oligomer size, suggesting that the Aβ42 epitopes become less accessible due to intermolecular interactions, similar to observations with natural Aβ42 oligomers.  
- **Dependence on Aggregation-Prone Regions**: Mutations in predicted aggregation-prone regions of Aβ42 (residues 15–21, 24–32, 35–37, and 40–42) disrupt oligomerization, validating the system’s relevance to Aβ42 biology.  

### Example 2  

**Guanidine Hydrochloride Enhances AβMRF Oligomerization**  
Guanidine hydrochloride (GuHCl), a known inhibitor of the yeast chaperone Hsp104, was tested for its effects on AβMRF oligomerization. Surprisingly, GuHCl treatment (6.3 mM) increased the abundance of AβMRF oligomers while depleting monomeric pools, as demonstrated by immunoblotting. This effect was specific to oligomerization-competent AβMRF and was not observed with aggregation-deficient mutants (Aβm1MRF or Aβm2MRF).  

Mechanistically, GuHCl’s effect on AβMRF oligomerization is independent of Hsp104, as evidenced by:  
1. **Hsp104-Independent Action**: GuHCl still enhanced oligomerization in *hsp104Δ* cells, ruling out Hsp104 as the primary target.  
2. **Protective Role of Hsp104**: Deletion of *HSP104* reduced total AβMRF levels by ~40% and shifted the oligomer-monomer equilibrium toward monomers, suggesting Hsp104 stabilizes AβMRF by preventing degradation.  

These findings imply that GuHCl acts on an unknown cellular factor that promotes AβMRF disaggregation or degradation, and that Hsp104 protects AβMRF from this factor. The system thus provides a tool to identify such factors and their inhibitors.  

### Example 3  

**Hsp104 Binds AβMRF and Modulates Oligomerization**  
Co-immunoprecipitation experiments revealed that Hsp104 physically interacts with AβMRF but not with MRF alone, indicating that the Aβ42 portion mediates this interaction. This binding is consistent with prior reports of Hsp104-Aβ42 interactions in vitro and suggests a role for Hsp104 in regulating Aβ42 oligomerization in vivo.  

The functional implications of Hsp104-AβMRF interaction include:  
- **Stabilization of AβMRF**: Hsp104 may shield AβMRF from degradation by cellular proteases or disaggregases.  
- **Modulation of Oligomer-Monomer Equilibrium**: Hsp104 deletion increases monomeric AβMRF, possibly by exposing the protein to disaggregating factors.  

This example highlights the utility of the AβMRF system for studying chaperone-mediated regulation of Aβ42 oligomerization.  

### Example 4  

**Aggregation-Disrupting Mutations Restore MRF Activity**  
To validate the system’s sensitivity to Aβ42 oligomerization, point mutations were introduced into the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF:  
1. **Aβm1MRF (F19,20T)**: Disrupts the first aggregation-prone region, reducing but not eliminating oligomerization.  
2. **Aβm2MRF (F19,20T/I31P)**: Disrupts two aggregation-prone regions, further inhibiting oligomerization.  

Immunoblot analysis confirmed that these mutations reduced SDS-stable oligomers, with Aβm2MRF primarily existing as monomers. Phenotypically, these mutations restored translational termination activity, as evidenced by:  
- **Ade− Growth**: Cells expressing Aβm1MRF or Aβm2MRF failed to grow on adenine-deficient medium.  
- **Red Colony Color**: Accumulation of red pigment on complex medium, indicative of functional MRF.  

This example demonstrates the system’s ability to discriminate between varying degrees of oligomerization and its utility for screening anti-aggregation compounds.  

### Example 5  

**Detection of AβMRF Oligomers by Immunoblotting**  
AβMRF oligomers were characterized using SDS-PAGE and agarose gel electrophoresis:  
- **Polyacrylamide Gels**: Resolved monomers (~77 kDa) and oligomers (dimers, trimers, tetramers).  
- **Agarose Gels**: Improved resolution of larger oligomers, confirming the absence of fibrillar aggregates.  

Key observations:  
- **SDS Stability**: Oligomers resisted 1–2% SDS at room temperature but dissociated upon boiling.  
- **Antibody Specificity**: Anti-Aβ antibodies (e.g., 6E10) detected oligomers less efficiently as size increased, suggesting epitope occlusion.  

This example establishes standardized methods for detecting Aβ42-like oligomers in the yeast system.  

### A Suppressible Allele  

The *ade1-14* nonsense allele serves as a sensitive reporter for AβMRF oligomerization. In this system:  
- **Functional MRF**: Efficient termination at the *ade1-14* stop codon results in Ade− (red) phenotype.  
- **Oligomerized AβMRF**: Impaired termination leads to Ade+ (white) phenotype.  

The allele’s utility was validated by:  
1. **Correlation with Oligomer Levels**: Phenotypic changes (white-to-red) mirrored reductions in oligomers (e.g., with Aβm1MRF or Aβm2MRF).  
2. **Response to Modulators**: Guanidine intensified the white phenotype, while *hsp104Δ* partially restored red color.  

This reporter system enables high-throughput screening by coupling oligomerization to a simple growth assay.  

### Example 7  

**Applications in Drug Discovery**  
The AβMRF system is designed for identifying inhibitors of early Aβ42 oligomerization. Screening strategies include:  
1. **Chemical Libraries**: Compounds that restore Ade−/red phenotype may disrupt oligomerization.  
2. **Genetic Screens**: Overexpression or deletion libraries can reveal modifiers of AβMRF oligomerization.  

Advantages of the system:  
- **Eukaryotic Context**: Recapitulates chaperone and proteostatic influences absent in vitro.  
- **Quantitative Output**: Phenotypic and immunoblot readouts provide orthogonal validation.  

This example outlines the invention’s potential for accelerating Alzheimer’s disease therapeutic development.
