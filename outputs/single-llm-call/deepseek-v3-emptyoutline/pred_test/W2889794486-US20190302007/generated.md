## BACKGROUND  

Ultraviolet-visible (UV-VIS) spectrophotometry has been a fundamental analytical technique in chemistry, biology, and materials science for decades. Conventional spectrophotometry measures optical extinction, which comprises both absorption and scattering components. However, in complex media such as biological tissues, nanoparticle suspensions, or turbid solutions, scattering dominates the extinction spectrum, making it difficult to extract the true absorption profile.  

Existing methods to separate absorption from scattering, such as integrating spheres or time-resolved spectroscopy, suffer from significant limitations. Integrating spheres require precise calibration and are sensitive to sample geometry, while time-resolved techniques necessitate expensive ultrafast lasers and complex detection systems. Furthermore, these methods are not easily integrated into standard spectrophotometers, limiting their practical utility.  

Plasmonic nanoparticles, particularly gold nanoparticles (GNPs), exhibit strong absorption and scattering due to localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR). Conventional UV-VIS spectrophotometry cannot accurately distinguish between these contributions, leading to misinterpretations of nanoparticle behavior in biological or complex media. This limitation hinders applications in drug delivery, photothermal therapy, and diagnostic imaging, where precise knowledge of absorption properties is critical.  

Thus, there remains an unmet need for a robust, non-contact method to measure true optical absorption spectra independently of scattering, particularly in turbid or nanoparticle-containing media.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a non-contact photoacoustic (PA) spectrophotometry method for accurately measuring optical absorption spectra in scattering media. The method employs a pulsed laser to generate photoacoustic signals in a sample, which are detected remotely using a fiber-optic interferometer. Unlike conventional spectrophotometry, the PA signal amplitude is directly proportional to the absorption coefficient (μₐ) and is minimally affected by scattering.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **Scattering Insensitivity**: The method isolates absorption from scattering, enabling accurate measurements even in highly turbid media.  
2. **Non-Contact Detection**: Ultrasound signals are detected remotely via an optical probe, eliminating the need for direct acoustic coupling.  
3. **Broad Applicability**: The technique is compatible with nanoparticles, biological tissues, and complex solutions, making it suitable for biomedical and materials science applications.  
4. **Integration with Conventional Spectrophotometry**: The system can be combined with UV-VIS spectrophotometers to provide complementary absorption and extinction data.  

The invention is particularly useful for characterizing plasmonic nanoparticles, such as gold nanorods (GNRs), in biological environments. By quantifying true absorption spectra, it enables optimized design of nanoparticle-based therapies and imaging techniques.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### System Overview  

The non-contact PA spectrophotometer comprises:  
1. **Pulsed Laser Source**: A tunable diode-pumped laser (700–900 nm) with nanosecond pulse duration and high repetition rate (up to 1 kHz).  
2. **Sample Chamber**: A cuvette with quartz windows, holding the sample in a thin (0.1 mm) diaphragm to ensure uniform excitation.  
3. **Remote Ultrasound Detection**: A fiber-optic Sagnac interferometer detects surface displacements caused by PA signals without physical contact.  
4. **Data Acquisition and Processing**: A high-speed digitizer and computer system record and analyze PA signals to reconstruct absorption spectra.  

### Measurement Procedure  

1. **Laser Excitation**: The tunable laser delivers pulses at discrete wavelengths across the spectral range. The beam is focused to a small spot (~0.5 mm) to minimize scattering effects.  
2. **Photoacoustic Signal Generation**: Absorbed light generates heat, inducing thermoelastic expansion and ultrasound waves.  
3. **Non-Contact Detection**: The fiber-optic interferometer measures surface vibrations on the quartz window, converting them into electrical signals.  
4. **Spectral Reconstruction**: PA signal amplitudes are plotted against wavelength to produce the absorption spectrum. Calibration with a known absorber (e.g., cupric sulfate) ensures quantitative accuracy.  

### Key Innovations  

1. **Narrow-Beam Excitation**: The small beam diameter (< transport mean free path) ensures that the PA signal is linearly dependent on absorption, independent of scattering.  
2. **Wavelength Agility**: Rapid laser tuning enables high-speed spectral acquisition (~10 minutes for 21 wavelengths).  
3. **Robust Calibration**: The system is calibrated using standards with known absorption coefficients, ensuring reproducibility.  

### Applications  

1. **Nanoparticle Characterization**: Measures true absorption spectra of GNPs, correcting for scattering artifacts in conventional UV-VIS data.  
2. **Biological Samples**: Quantifies absorption in cell suspensions or tissues, enabling label-free monitoring of nanoparticle uptake.  
3. **Dynamic Processes**: Tracks spectral changes in real time, useful for studying chemical reactions or nanoparticle aggregation.
