# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to synthetic peptide compositions and methods for treating neoplastic disorders through targeted membrane disruption. More specifically, the invention encompasses a family of engineered polypeptides designated SGP (Synthetic Glycoprotein) and its functional analogs SGP-L and SGP-E, which exhibit potent anti-tumor activity through selective plasma membrane destabilization. The invention further provides pharmaceutical formulations, methods of synthesis, and therapeutic protocols for administering these membrane-disrupting agents to mammalian subjects, particularly for the treatment of solid tumors including carcinomas, sarcomas, and melanomas. The technology demonstrates unexpected selectivity in vivo despite broad-spectrum cytotoxicity in vitro, attributable to extracellular matrix-mediated protection of non-target tissues.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Conventional cancer therapies including chemotherapy and radiation suffer from systemic toxicity and limited efficacy against advanced malignancies. While pore-forming peptides derived from natural sources such as magainin and cecropin have demonstrated in vitro cytotoxicity, their clinical translation has been hindered by poor pharmacokinetics and lack of tumor specificity. Previous attempts to engineer membrane-disrupting proteins have focused on modifying existing bacterial toxins or antimicrobial peptides, yielding compounds with insufficient therapeutic indices.  

A critical unmet need exists for anti-cancer agents capable of physically disrupting tumor cell membranes while sparing normal tissues. The extracellular matrix surrounding non-malignant cells presents a natural barrier that has not been previously exploited for therapeutic targeting. Furthermore, existing synthetic peptides lack the structural precision required for controlled membrane interaction, often causing non-specific lysis. The present invention overcomes these limitations through rational protein design incorporating: (1) optimized amphipathic helices for lipid bilayer insertion, (2) tunable hydrophobicity profiles, and (3) matrix-sensitive activity modulation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides a synthetic glycoprotein (SGP) composition comprising a 75-amino acid polypeptide with the sequence: Gly6-Ala10-Leu25-Asp3-Pro3-Tyr3-Lys18, having a molecular weight of 7554.8 Da as determined by mass spectrometry. The protein features three α-helical domains that undergo conformational changes upon contact with plasma membranes, inducing rapid non-apoptotic cell death within 60 seconds at concentrations above 10 μM.  

Key innovations include:  
1. A core membrane-interaction module containing alternating hydrophobic (Leu) and cationic (Lys) residues that generate toroidal pores in lipid bilayers  
2. Structural variants (SGP-L with all-leucine helices; SGP-E with glutamic acid substitutions) demonstrating attenuated activity for safety profiling  
3. Extracellular matrix-dependent activity modulation, wherein matrigel or fibronectin coatings completely inhibit membrane disruption  
4. Tumor-localized efficacy evidenced by xenograft regression without systemic toxicity  

Therapeutic methods comprise intratumoral injection of SGP at 100 μM to 1 mM concentrations in physiologically compatible buffers, with treatment cycles adapted for tumor type and staging. Pharmaceutical formulations may incorporate matrix metalloproteinase-cleavable linkers to enhance tumor penetration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides fundamentally novel protein therapeutics designed de novo for cancer treatment. The 75-residue SGP polypeptide was synthesized via Fmoc solid-phase chemistry on PEG resin, with trifluoroacetic acid cleavage yielding 85% pure product before HPLC purification. Analytical characterization confirmed correct amino acid composition through acid hydrolysis and mass spectrometry.  

The tertiary structure comprises three amphipathic α-helices stabilized by:  
- Hydrophobic core interactions between Leu25 residues  
- Ionic bridges among Lys18 side chains  
- Pro3-induced helix termination points  

This configuration enables:  
1. Rapid oligomerization upon contact with plasma membranes  
2. Formation of 3-5 nm diameter pores through lipid bilayer insertion  
3. Osmotic lysis via uncontrolled ion flux  

Unexpectedly, the presence of extracellular matrix components (matrigel, polymeric fibronectin) completely abrogates membrane disruption through:  
- Steric hindrance of helix insertion  
- Charge shielding of lysine residues  
- Conformational stabilization in solution phase  

This accounts for the observed tumor selectivity in vivo despite broad cytotoxicity in vitro.  

### EXAMPLE 1  
**Synthesis and Purification**  
SGP was synthesized on Fmoc-Leu-PEG resin using automated peptide synthesis (Miligen 9050). After TFA cleavage, crude product was purified by reverse-phase HPLC (ODS column, 20×250 mm) with 0.1% TFA/water-acetonitrile gradient. Final purity exceeded 98% with characteristic UV absorbance at 280 nm (ε=8000). Fast atom bombardment mass spectrometry confirmed molecular weight (observed 7555.1 vs calculated 7554.8).  

### EXAMPLE 2  
**In Vitro Cytotoxicity Profiling**  
KS1767 Kaposi's sarcoma cells were treated with SGP variants:  
- 10 μM: 100% viability loss in 60 sec (non-apoptotic)  
- 5-10 μM: Necrosis over 60 min  
- <5 μM: Apoptosis within 24 hr (caspase-3 activation)  
Control peptides (SGP-L, SGP-E) showed >10-fold reduced potency.  

### EXAMPLE 3  
**Breast Carcinoma Xenograft Model**  
MDA-MB-435 tumors (100-600 mm³) in nude mice received:  
- PBS control: 400% volume increase by week 8  
- 100 μM SGP: 80% reduction (p<0.01)  
- 1 mM SGP: 85% reduction (p<0.001)  
Histology confirmed apoptosis with intact stroma.  

### EXAMPLE 4  
**Prostate Cancer Efficacy**  
PC-3 xenografts showed:  
- 78% volume reduction after four weekly SGP injections  
- No detectable metastases at necropsy  
- Normal organ histology  

### EXAMPLE 5  
**Matrix Protection Mechanism**  
KS1767 cells coated with matrigel or sFN:  
- 1 mM SGP: 95% viability maintained  
- Ethanol/doxorubicin: <10% viability  
Demonstrates matrix-specific activity modulation.  

### EXAMPLE 6  
**Dose Optimization**  
Titration studies established:  
- Minimum effective dose: 50 μM  
- Maximum tolerated dose: 5 mM (local administration)  
- Therapeutic window: 100-1000 μM  

### EXAMPLE 7  
**Safety Profile**  
Intradermal injection (100 μM):  
- No erythema/necrosis over 14 days  
- Normal skin histology  
Systemic administration showed no hematologic toxicity.
