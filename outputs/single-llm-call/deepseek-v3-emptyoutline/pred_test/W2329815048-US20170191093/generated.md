# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Triacylglycerols (TAGs) serve as essential energy storage molecules and structural components in microbial cells, with significant industrial applications in biofuels, specialty chemicals, and nutritional supplements. Microbial lipid production, particularly in oleaginous yeasts such as *Yarrowia lipolytica*, offers a sustainable alternative to plant- and animal-derived lipids. However, achieving high lipid yields and productivity in microbial systems remains a challenge due to metabolic inefficiencies and competing biochemical pathways.  

Prior efforts to enhance lipid accumulation in *Y. lipolytica* have focused on overexpression of endogenous lipid biosynthesis genes, including diacylglycerol acyltransferases (DGATs), which catalyze the final step of TAG synthesis. While overexpression of native *DGA1* (DGAT2) and *DGA2* (DGAT1) has improved lipid production, the full potential of these enzymes remains unrealized due to suboptimal catalytic efficiency, transcriptional regulation, and post-translational modifications. Additionally, TAG degradation by intracellular lipases, such as *TGL3*, limits net lipid accumulation during fermentation.  

There exists a need for engineered microbial strains with enhanced lipid biosynthesis capacity, reduced TAG degradation, and improved overall lipid yield and titer for industrial-scale production.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides engineered microbial strains, particularly oleaginous yeasts such as *Yarrowia lipolytica*, with enhanced lipid production capabilities through genetic modifications targeting TAG biosynthesis and degradation pathways. The invention encompasses:  

1. **Overexpression of heterologous *DGA1* and *DGA2* genes** derived from high-lipid-producing organisms, including *Rhodosporidium toruloides*, *Lipomyces starkeyi*, and *Claviceps purpurea*, to increase TAG synthesis efficiency.  
2. **Deletion or suppression of *TGL3* lipase**, a key enzyme in TAG degradation, to prevent lipid mobilization during late fermentation stages.  
3. **Combinatorial genetic modifications**, including simultaneous overexpression of *DGA1* and *DGA2* alongside *TGL3* knockout, to maximize lipid accumulation.  

The engineered strains exhibit unprecedented lipid content (up to 77% of dry cell weight in batch fermentation and 85 g/L titer in fed-batch fermentation) and productivity (0.73 g/L/h), surpassing prior benchmarks for microbial lipid production.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Overview  

The invention leverages metabolic engineering strategies to optimize TAG biosynthesis and storage in oleaginous yeasts. Key modifications include:  
- Introduction of high-activity *DGA1* and *DGA2* variants from donor organisms with superior lipid accumulation phenotypes.  
- Disruption of *TGL3* to minimize TAG hydrolysis during nutrient-limited conditions.  
- Use of strong constitutive promoters (e.g., *GPD1*, *TEF1*) to drive transgene expression.  

### Definitions  

- **DGA1 (DGAT2)**: A diacylglycerol acyltransferase that catalyzes the acylation of diacylglycerol using acyl-CoA as an acyl donor, localizing to lipid droplets.  
- **DGA2 (DGAT1)**: An endoplasmic reticulum-localized acyltransferase involved in nascent lipid body formation.  
- **TGL3**: A triacylglycerol lipase implicated in TAG degradation.  
- **Oleaginous yeast**: A microorganism capable of accumulating lipids exceeding 20% of its dry cell weight.  

### Microbe Engineering  

The invention employs genetic tools for *Yarrowia lipolytica* transformation, including:  
- **Random integration** of expression cassettes using antibiotic resistance markers (e.g., *nat1*, *ble*, *hph*).  
- **Gene deletion** via homologous recombination facilitated by hydroxyurea treatment to synchronize cell cycles.  
- **Codon optimization** of heterologous genes for enhanced expression in *Y. lipolytica*.

## EXEMPLIFICATION  

### Example 1: Method to Increase the Activity of a DGA1 Protein (DGAT2 Gene)  

*Y. lipolytica* strain NS297 was engineered by integrating *R. toruloides DGA1* (NG66) under the *GPD1* promoter. Transformants exhibited a twofold increase in lipid content (Fig. 2), confirming the superiority of heterologous *DGA1*.  

### Example 2: Lipid Assay  

Lipid content was quantified via GC analysis of fatty acid methyl esters (FAMEs). Strains overexpressing *DGA1* and *DGA2* showed 77% lipid content in batch fermentation (Table 3).  

### Example 3: Analysis and Screening of *Y. lipolytica* Strains that Express *DGA1*  

Fluorescence screening of 96-well cultures identified top-performing *DGA1* transformants (Fig. 2). *R. toruloides DGA1* (NG66) and *L. starkeyi DGA1* (NG68) yielded the highest lipid accumulation.  

### Example 4: Method to Knockout Triacylglycerol Lipase Knockout Gene in *Y. lipolytica*  

*TGL3* was deleted in strain NS377 (*RtDGA1*, *Δtgl3*) using a split-marker *hph* cassette. NS377 showed 20% higher lipid content than NS281 (*RtDGA1*) at 140 h fermentation (Fig. 4c).  

### Example 5: Cells that Overexpress Both *DGA1* and *DGA2* and That Contain a *TGL3* Deletion Accumulate More TAGs  

Strain NS432 (*RtDGA1*, *CpDGA2*, *Δtgl3*) achieved 85 g/L lipid titer in fed-batch fermentation (Fig. 5b), demonstrating synergistic effects of combinatorial engineering.
