# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of virology and oncology, specifically to methods of inhibiting Human Papillomavirus (HPV)-driven oncogenesis through modulation of viral gene expression. More particularly, the invention discloses the use of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) as an effective therapeutic agent for suppressing HPV long control region (LCR)-mediated transcription of oncoproteins E6 and E7, thereby arresting proliferation of HPV-transformed cells. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing LIF and methods of treating HPV-associated pathologies including cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), cervical carcinoma, and other HPV-dependent malignancies.  

## BACKGROUND  

Human Papillomavirus (HPV) infection represents a significant global health burden, with high-risk HPV types such as HPV-16 and HPV-18 being established as necessary causative agents for cervical cancer, the second most common cancer in women worldwide. HPV has also been implicated in anal cancer, oropharyngeal cancer, and certain breast cancers. Despite advances in screening and prevention, no approved nonsurgical interventions exist for treating established HPV infections or pre-malignant cervical dysplasia. Current treatments for invasive cervical cancer rely on cytotoxic chemotherapy and radiation, which are associated with substantial toxicity and limited efficacy against recurrent or metastatic disease.  

The oncogenic potential of HPV is primarily mediated through the viral proteins E6 and E7, which are constitutively expressed in HPV-transformed cells under the control of the viral long control region (LCR). E6 promotes degradation of the tumor suppressor p53, while E7 inactivates retinoblastoma protein (pRb), collectively disrupting cell cycle regulation and enabling uncontrolled proliferation. The level of E6/E7 expression correlates with disease progression, making these oncoproteins attractive therapeutic targets. Previous attempts to inhibit E6/E7 through RNA interference, peptide aptamers, or E2 gene transfer have demonstrated proof-of-concept but face challenges in clinical translation.  

Cytokine-based therapies have shown variable effects on HPV transcription, with some cytokines like IL-6 suppressing LCR activity while paradoxically promoting cell proliferation. There remains an unmet need for agents that can effectively suppress HPV oncogene expression while inhibiting cancer cell growth with acceptable toxicity profiles.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing methods and compositions utilizing Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) to treat HPV-associated diseases. LIF has been discovered to potently inhibit HPV-16 LCR-driven transcription, leading to significant reductions in E6 and E7 mRNA levels in HPV-transformed cervical cancer cells. This transcriptional repression is accompanied by marked inhibition of cell proliferation, suggesting cell cycle arrest rather than apoptosis.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of LIF to suppress HPV LCR activity and downstream oncogene expression.  
2. Pharmaceutical formulations containing therapeutically effective amounts of LIF for treating HPV-associated conditions.  
3. Methods of treating cervical dysplasia and carcinoma by administering LIF to reduce E6/E7 expression.  
4. Combination therapies pairing LIF with conventional cytotoxic agents to enhance treatment efficacy.  

The invention is supported by experimental data demonstrating that LIF treatment reduces HPV LCR reporter activity by approximately 60% and decreases E7 mRNA expression in a dose-dependent manner. LIF-treated HPV-transformed cells show proliferation rates approximately half those of untreated controls. These effects occur through mechanisms involving STAT3 phosphorylation, indicating engagement of canonical LIF signaling pathways.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Classification of Papillomaviruses  

Papillomaviruses constitute a diverse family of small DNA viruses that infect epithelial cells across various species. Based on genomic sequence homology, HPV types are classified into genera (Alpha-, Beta-, Gamma-, etc.), with high-risk carcinogenic types predominantly found in the Alphapapillomavirus genus. HPV-16 and HPV-18, belonging to species Alpha-9 and Alpha-7 respectively, account for approximately 70% of cervical cancer cases worldwide. The viral genome comprises early (E) genes encoding regulatory proteins, late (L) genes encoding structural proteins, and the long control region containing transcriptional regulatory elements.  

### Papillomaviruses and Cancer  

HPV-associated carcinogenesis follows a well-characterized sequence from persistent infection to precancerous lesions and ultimately invasive carcinoma. The integration of viral DNA into host chromosomes frequently disrupts E2 regulatory sequences, leading to constitutive overexpression of E6 and E7 oncoproteins. These viral proteins subvert critical tumor suppressor pathways: E6 binds p53 targeting it for proteasomal degradation, while E7 inactivates pRb family members, disrupting cell cycle checkpoints. Additional oncogenic mechanisms include telomerase activation, genomic instability induction, and immune evasion.  

### Diagnosis and Treatment of HPV  

Current diagnostic approaches for HPV-related disease include cytological examination (Pap smear), HPV DNA testing, and histopathological evaluation of biopsy specimens. Treatment modalities vary by disease stage, ranging from ablative procedures for precancerous lesions to radical hysterectomy or chemoradiation for invasive cancer. Therapeutic vaccines targeting E6/E7 are under investigation but face challenges related to antigen presentation and immune tolerance. Small molecule inhibitors of HPV oncoproteins have shown limited clinical efficacy due to poor bioavailability and off-target effects.  

### Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and its Utility in Treating HPV-Transformed Cells by Inhibiting LCR-Driven Transcription  

LIF is a pleiotropic cytokine belonging to the IL-6 family that signals through the LIF receptor (LIFR) complexed with gp130, activating JAK/STAT, MAPK, and PI3K pathways. The invention demonstrates that LIF treatment of HPV-transformed cervical cancer cells (SiHa and CaSki) results in significant inhibition of HPV-16 LCR transcriptional activity, as measured by reporter assays and quantitative PCR of E6/E7 mRNA. This effect is dose-dependent, with maximal inhibition occurring at concentrations between 1-10 ng/mL.  

Mechanistic studies reveal that LIF induces transient phosphorylation of STAT3 at tyrosine 705 in HPV-transformed cells, consistent with canonical LIF signaling. Surprisingly, despite STAT3's general association with pro-survival signaling, LIF treatment leads to marked reduction in cell proliferation without significant apoptosis induction. Without being bound by theory, this suggests that LIF-mediated suppression of E6/E7 may restore normal cell cycle control despite concurrent STAT3 activation.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**LIF Inhibition of HPV-16 LCR Activity**  
SiHa cervical cancer cells stably transfected with an HPV-16 LCR-driven Gaussia luciferase reporter were treated with 10 ng/mL LIF or control buffer for 24 hours. Luciferase activity in culture supernatants was measured by chemiluminescence. LIF treatment reduced LCR-driven reporter expression by 58.3% ± 4.2% (mean ± SEM, n=6, p<0.01) compared to untreated controls. Dose-response experiments demonstrated significant inhibition beginning at 0.1 ng/mL, with maximal effect at 10 ng/mL.  

### Example 2  

**Reduction of E6/E7 mRNA by LIF**  
CaSki cells were treated with 1 or 10 ng/mL LIF for 72 hours. Quantitative RT-PCR using TaqMan probes specific for E6 and E7 transcripts showed dose-dependent reductions in oncogene expression. At 72 hours, E7 mRNA levels were decreased by 42.7% ± 5.1% (1 ng/mL) and 67.9% ± 6.3% (10 ng/mL) relative to untreated cells (p<0.05 for both). E6 mRNA showed similar reductions of 38.2% ± 4.8% and 63.1% ± 5.9% respectively.  

### Example 3  

**Anti-Proliferative Effects of LIF**  
CaSki cells were seeded in 96-well plates and treated with 10 ng/mL LIF in serum-free medium. After 40 hours, cell proliferation was assessed by MTT assay. LIF-treated wells showed optical density readings of 0.32 ± 0.04 AU compared to 0.61 ± 0.05 AU for untreated controls (p<0.01), representing a 47.5% reduction in proliferative activity. Microscopic examination revealed no significant increase in apoptotic morphology, suggesting growth arrest rather than cell death.
