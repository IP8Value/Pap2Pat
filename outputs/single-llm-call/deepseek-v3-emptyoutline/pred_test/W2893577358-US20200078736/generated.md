## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates generally to nanoporous membranes with controlled ion transport properties. More specifically, the invention pertains to layer-by-layer (LbL) deposited polyelectrolyte bilayers on nanoporous supports, wherein the ionic selectivity and conductivity are independently tunable through polyelectrolyte chemistry, bilayer thickness, and cross-linking methodologies. The membranes find particular utility in applications requiring selective ion transport such as electrodialysis, fuel cells, flow batteries, and water purification systems.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Nanoporous membranes have emerged as critical platforms for controlling ion transport in electrochemical systems. The high surface-area-to-volume ratio of nanopores allows surface charge manipulation to govern ionic flux. While various responsive chemistries have been employed to modify surface charge, conventional approaches suffer from limited tunability, poor stability, or complex fabrication requirements.  

Prior attempts to create ion-selective membranes have utilized track-etched polymers, anodic alumina, and other nanoporous substrates coated with polyelectrolytes. However, these systems exhibit insufficient control over the interplay between ionic selectivity and conductivity. Furthermore, existing polyelectrolyte coatings often lack mechanical and chemical stability under operational conditions. The industry lacks a robust, scalable method to produce membranes where ionic transport properties can be systematically tuned without compromising membrane integrity.  

Layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes presents a promising solution but faces challenges in achieving uniform pore coverage, controlled thickness, and stable cross-linking. Previous LbL systems using poly(styrene sulfonate) (PSS) and poly(allylamine hydrochloride) (PAH) demonstrate limited tunability due to fixed charge ratios. There remains an unmet need for polyelectrolyte membranes that enable independent control over ionic selectivity and conductivity through tailored polymer chemistry and cross-linking strategies.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention provides a nanoporous membrane comprising:  
1) A nanoporous polymeric support substrate;  
2) A polyelectrolyte multilayer coating deposited via layer-by-layer (LbL) assembly, comprising alternating layers of poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) and poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI);  
3) A cross-linked network formed within the polyelectrolyte layers through either:  
   a) Glutaraldehyde (GA) cross-linking of amine groups, or  
   b) Carbodiimide (EDC) cross-linking between amine and carboxylic acid groups.  

Key advantages include:  
- Independent tuning of ionic selectivity and conductivity through bilayer number and cross-linking chemistry  
- GA cross-linking selectively reduces cationic charge density, enhancing cation selectivity  
- EDC cross-linking maintains charge neutrality while improving mechanical stability  
- Bilayer thickness from 1 to 5 layers provides graded control over transport properties  
- Complete pore coverage achieved with ≥3 bilayers plus cross-linking  

The membranes demonstrate superior performance characteristics:  
- Cation transference numbers (t+) tunable from 0.65 to 0.85  
- Conductivities ranging from 0.1 to 1.0 mS/cm in 10-100 mM NaCl  
- Enhanced chemical stability against dissolution or surface diffusion  
- Scalable fabrication via dip-coating on large-area supports  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Example: LbL Deposition of Bilayer PAA and PEI Polyelectrolytes  
The invention is practiced through the following exemplary embodiment:  

**Membrane Preparation**  
Nanoporous polycarbonate (PC) membranes (50 nm pores, 90 mm diameter) are first cleaned by NaOH etching and UV-ozone treatment. The LbL assembly process comprises:  
1) Immersion in 0.1 wt% PEI solution (pH 10.4) for 5 minutes (first layer)  
2) Rinsing in deionized water  
3) Immersion in 0.2 wt% PAA solution (pH 3.2) for 5 minutes (second layer)  
4) Subsequent bilayers deposited via 1-minute dips in each polymer solution  

**Cross-Linking Methods**  
After bilayer deposition, membranes are cross-linked via:  
- GA cross-linking: 12-hour immersion in 25% glutaraldehyde solution  
- EDC cross-linking: 12-hour immersion in 100 mM EDC solution  

**Structural Characterization**  
FTIR spectroscopy confirms:  
- PAA carboxyl peaks at 1714 cm⁻¹  
- PEI amine peaks at 1550 cm⁻¹  
- GA cross-linking reduces amine peaks disproportionately  
- EDC cross-linking reduces both amine and carboxyl peaks equally  

SEM analysis shows:  
- ≥3 bilayers required for complete pore coverage  
- Cross-linking prevents polymer redissolution  
- 5-bilayer coatings achieve 1230 ± 193 nm thickness  

**Transport Properties**  
Electrochemical measurements demonstrate:  
- GA cross-linking increases cation selectivity (t+ up to 0.85)  
- Conductivity decreases initially then stabilizes with additional bilayers  
- EDC cross-linking maintains intermediate selectivity (t+ ≈ 0.75)  
- Non-cross-linked membranes show poorest performance  

The complete membrane fabrication and characterization process enables precise tuning of ion transport properties for specific applications while maintaining robust mechanical and chemical stability.
