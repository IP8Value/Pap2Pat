# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNOLOGICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology, specifically to noninvasive methods and devices for manipulating intraocular particulate matter using acoustic radiation forces. More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of standing acoustic waves to control the movement of pathological or therapeutic particles within the anterior chamber of the eye, thereby preventing complications such as obstruction of the visual axis, trabecular meshwork dysfunction, and secondary glaucoma.  

The invention utilizes acoustic transducers to generate controlled standing waves that direct particles toward nodal points, where they aggregate without causing thermal or mechanical damage to ocular tissues. This technology represents a significant advancement over existing treatments, which are limited to managing secondary complications rather than preventing particle-induced pathologies. The disclosed method provides a safe, noninvasive alternative to surgical interventions, with potential applications in clearing the visual axis, protecting the trabecular meshwork, and enhancing targeted drug delivery.  

## BACKGROUND  

Intraocular particulate matter, whether pathological (e.g., pigment dispersion, pseudoexfoliation material, hyphema) or therapeutic (e.g., triamcinolone acetonide), poses significant clinical challenges. Such particles can obstruct the visual axis, impair aqueous humor outflow by clogging the trabecular meshwork, and deposit on the corneal endothelium, leading to complications like glaucoma and corneal staining. Current clinical practice lacks preventive or active management strategies; interventions are typically delayed until secondary complications arise, often necessitating invasive surgical procedures.  

Existing technologies in acoustic ophthalmology are primarily limited to diagnostic imaging (e.g., ultrasound biomicroscopy) or tissue-destructive applications (e.g., high-intensity focused ultrasound for ciliary body ablation). While acoustic manipulation of particles has been explored in other fields—such as microfluidics for cell sorting or urinary stone repositioning—its application to intraocular particles has not been previously demonstrated. The eye’s delicate structures and the small size of intraocular particles (often <50 µm) present unique technical hurdles, including the need for precise control of acoustic forces without inducing tissue damage.  

Prior attempts to address intraocular particles have relied on pharmacological dissolution or surgical removal, both of which carry risks of inflammation, infection, or mechanical trauma. The invention fills this unmet need by providing a noninvasive, physics-based approach to particle control, leveraging the inherent acoustic contrast between particles and aqueous humor.  

## GENERAL DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides a system and method for acoustically manipulating intraocular particles comprising:  
1. **Acoustic Transducer**: A piezoelectric resonator (e.g., lead zirconate titanate) configured to generate standing waves at frequencies of 0.5–2 MHz, optimally 1.1 MHz, with an intensity of 20–80 mW/cm². The transducer is coated with a biocompatible layer (e.g., polydimethylsiloxane) to ensure safe contact with ocular tissues.  
2. **Particle Aggregation**: Particles with positive acoustic contrast factors (denser and less compressible than aqueous humor) are driven to nodal points of standing waves, forming stable aggregates. This includes pathological particles (e.g., pigment, fibrin) and therapeutic agents (e.g., corticosteroid suspensions).  
3. **Dynamic Control**: The system allows real-time adjustment of wave frequency (±100 kHz) and transducer positioning to relocate particle aggregates to predefined anatomical sites (e.g., peripheral angle to spare the trabecular meshwork).  
4. **Safety Mechanisms**: Integrated thermal monitoring and cooling systems prevent temperature rises exceeding 2.5°C, while high-frequency operation minimizes cavitation risks.  

Key advantages include:  
- **Noninvasiveness**: Eliminates the need for incisional surgery or intraocular instrumentation.  
- **Selectivity**: Targets only particulate matter, sparing surrounding tissues.  
- **Versatility**: Applicable to diverse particle types and sizes (0.5–50 µm).  
- **Compatibility**: Can be combined with existing diagnostic or therapeutic devices.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### Acoustic Transducer Assembly  
The preferred embodiment employs a cylindrical radial resonator (inner diameter 22 mm, outer diameter 26 mm) operating at 1.1 MHz. The resonator is driven by a signal generator (e.g., Siglent SDG 5162) amplified to 10–20 Vpp, producing acoustic pressures of 0.5–2 kPa. A soft silicone coating (70 µm thick) ensures biocompatibility. Alternative embodiments may use phased-array transducers for three-dimensional particle steering or miniaturized probes for posterior segment applications.  

### Particle Manipulation Mechanism  
When activated, the transducer establishes standing waves with nodal spacings of 0.75 ± 0.1 mm in the anterior chamber. Particles migrate to nodes at velocities proportional to their size and acoustic contrast:  
- **Triamcinolone acetonide (11.5 µm)**: 755 µm/s at 10 Vpp (71 pN force).  
- **Pigment particles (<2 µm)**: Slower aggregation due to reduced acoustic scattering.  

The system’s efficacy is demonstrated in ex vivo porcine eyes, where particles formed concentric rings within 240 seconds (Figure 4). Particle aggregates remained stable post-deactivation, with no reflux observed over 60 minutes.  

### Safety and Monitoring  
Thermal imaging confirmed temperature rises of <0.5°C (10 Vpp) and ≤2.5°C (20 Vpp), well below tissue-damaging thresholds. Optical coherence tomography and histology revealed no corneal endothelial or stromal injury. A failsafe algorithm automatically terminates operation if temperatures exceed 3°C or if cavitation signatures are detected.  

### Clinical Workflow  
1. **Diagnostic Phase**: Ultrasound biomicroscopy identifies particle distribution.  
2. **Treatment Phase**: Transducer placement and activation for 1–5 minutes to aggregate particles at safe zones (e.g., inferior angle).  
3. **Verification**: Post-treatment imaging confirms clearance of critical structures.  

### Alternative Applications  
- **Drug Delivery**: Localizing injected therapeutics (e.g., anti-VEGF suspensions) to target tissues.  
- **Cell Therapy**: Organizing corneal endothelial cells into monolayer grafts.  
- **Diagnostics**: Differentiating particle types by their acoustic migration patterns.  

The invention’s scope encompasses all variations of acoustic parameters, transducer geometries, and particle types that achieve noninvasive intraocular manipulation without departing from its core principles.
