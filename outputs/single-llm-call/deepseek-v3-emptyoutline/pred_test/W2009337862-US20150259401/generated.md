# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and antibody engineering. More specifically, the invention pertains to chimeric antibodies comprising human variable regions and ferret constant regions, wherein said antibodies incorporate specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region that enhance binding to ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and prolong serum half-life in ferrets. The invention further provides methods for producing such antibodies, compositions comprising the same, and their use in pharmacokinetic studies and therapeutic applications in ferret models.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Ferrets serve as valuable animal models for studying human diseases, particularly influenza and other respiratory infections, due to their susceptibility to human pathogens and similar disease manifestations. However, the use of human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in ferret models has been limited by two major challenges: rapid clearance from circulation and immunogenicity. Human mAbs typically exhibit short half-lives in ferrets, often necessitating frequent dosing regimens that complicate experimental protocols. Moreover, the foreign nature of human antibodies in ferrets frequently triggers anti-drug antibody (ADA) responses that can further accelerate clearance and potentially interfere with experimental outcomes.  

Previous attempts to address these limitations have involved creating chimeric antibodies by combining the variable regions of human antibodies with constant regions from the target animal species. While this approach has shown promise in reducing immunogenicity in other animal models, the unique characteristics of ferret immunology and Fc receptor biology present distinct challenges. Notably, the endogenous half-life of antibodies in ferrets appears intrinsically shorter than in other common laboratory animals, suggesting fundamental differences in FcRn-mediated antibody recycling mechanisms.  

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) plays a crucial role in determining antibody persistence in circulation by protecting IgG from lysosomal degradation through pH-dependent binding and recycling. In humans, specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region (such as M252Y, S254T, T256E, M428L, and N434S) have been shown to enhance FcRn binding and extend serum half-life. However, the applicability of these modifications to ferret antibodies remained uncertain due to potential differences between human and ferret FcRn interactions.  

There exists a significant unmet need for optimized antibody formats that combine reduced immunogenicity with extended half-life in ferret models, particularly for studies requiring prolonged antibody exposure or repeated dosing. The current invention addresses this need through the development of novel chimeric human-ferret antibodies with engineered Fc regions that demonstrate improved pharmacokinetic properties in ferrets.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides chimeric antibodies comprising human variable regions and ferret constant regions, wherein the ferret Fc region incorporates specific amino acid substitutions that enhance binding to ferret FcRn and prolong serum half-life. The antibodies of the invention demonstrate significantly extended persistence in ferret circulation compared to both unmodified human antibodies and wild-type chimeric human-ferret antibodies, while simultaneously exhibiting reduced immunogenicity.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Chimeric antibodies comprising:  
   - Human-derived variable regions  
   - Ferret-derived constant regions  
   - At least one amino acid substitution in the ferret Fc region selected from S252M, S252Y, S254T, T256E, M428L, and N434S (using EU numbering system applied to ferret sequences)  

2. Preferred embodiments comprising the S252Y substitution, either alone or in combination with S254T and T256E (YTE variant), which demonstrate particularly strong binding to ferret FcRn and extended half-life.  

3. Nucleic acids encoding the chimeric antibodies, expression vectors containing said nucleic acids, and host cells transformed with said vectors for antibody production.  

4. Methods for producing the chimeric antibodies comprising:  
   - Cloning ferret immunoglobulin constant region sequences  
   - Constructing expression vectors encoding human variable regions and ferret constant regions with Fc modifications  
   - Expressing and purifying the chimeric antibodies  

5. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the chimeric antibodies and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers.  

6. Methods of using the antibodies in ferret models for research, diagnostic, or therapeutic purposes, particularly in studies of infectious diseases such as influenza.  

The invention provides substantial advantages over existing approaches by combining reduced immunogenicity (through ferret constant regions) with extended half-life (through Fc engineering), enabling more reliable and convenient antibody studies in ferret models. The antibodies maintain full antigen-binding capability while exhibiting improved pharmacokinetic properties, making them particularly valuable for long-term or repeated-dose experiments.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### General  

The present invention provides engineered chimeric antibodies optimized for use in ferret models. These antibodies combine human variable regions (providing desired antigen specificity) with ferret constant regions (reducing immunogenicity) and incorporate specific Fc modifications that enhance binding to ferret FcRn, thereby extending serum half-life.  

The antibodies are particularly useful for preclinical studies in ferrets, offering improved pharmacokinetic properties and reduced immunogenicity compared to either human antibodies or unmodified chimeric antibodies. While the examples focus on antibodies specific for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F glycoprotein, the approach is broadly applicable to antibodies against any target of interest, as the variable regions can be exchanged while maintaining the advantageous constant region framework.  

### DEFINITIONS  

For purposes of interpreting this specification, the following definitions will apply:  

"Antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specific binding to a target antigen. This includes intact immunoglobulins as well as antigen-binding fragments (e.g., Fab, F(ab')2, scFv) that retain antigen-binding capability.  

"Chimeric antibody" refers to an antibody comprising variable regions from one species and constant regions from another species. In the context of this invention, chimeric antibodies specifically combine human variable regions with ferret constant regions.  

"FcRn" refers to the neonatal Fc receptor, a heterodimeric protein complex comprising an α chain and β2-microglobulin that mediates IgG protection and recycling.  

"Half-life extending substitutions" refers to amino acid modifications in the Fc region that enhance binding to FcRn at acidic pH (e.g., endosomal pH) and thereby prolong serum persistence. In the context of this invention, these substitutions are specifically adapted for ferret FcRn interactions.  

"EU numbering" refers to the standardized numbering system for antibody sequences based on the EU index as described in Kabat et al.  

"Humanized antibody" refers to an antibody from a non-human species whose protein sequences have been modified to increase their similarity to antibodies produced naturally in humans.  

### Amino Acid Substitutions  

The invention incorporates specific amino acid substitutions in the ferret Fc region that enhance binding to ferret FcRn. Key substitutions include:  

1. S252M: Substitution of serine with methionine at position 252 (EU numbering)  
2. S252Y: Substitution of serine with tyrosine at position 252  
3. YTE variant: Combination of S252Y, S254T, and T256E substitutions  
4. N434S: Substitution of asparagine with serine at position 434  

These positions correspond to regions of the Fc domain known to participate in FcRn interactions across species. The substitutions were selected based on their demonstrated effects in human antibodies and adapted for ferret FcRn binding through empirical testing.  

Of particular note is the discovery that position 252, which naturally contains serine in ferret antibodies (compared to methionine in humans), represents a key determinant of FcRn binding affinity in ferrets. Substitution to either methionine or tyrosine at this position significantly enhances FcRn binding, with tyrosine providing particularly strong improvement.  

### Antibodies of the Invention  

The antibodies of the invention comprise:  

1. Human-derived variable regions providing antigen specificity  
2. Ferret-derived constant regions comprising:  
   - Heavy chain constant regions (CH1, hinge, CH2, CH3)  
   - Light chain constant regions (CL)  
3. At least one half-life extending substitution in the Fc region  

The variable regions may be derived from any human antibody of interest, including but not limited to antibodies against viral antigens, bacterial antigens, tumor antigens, or other targets relevant to ferret models. The ferret constant regions are preferably derived from ferret IgG, most preferably matching the sequences disclosed herein.  

The antibodies may be of any isotype compatible with ferret biology, with IgG being preferred. The antibodies may be full-length immunoglobulins or may comprise antigen-binding fragments (e.g., Fab, F(ab')2) as needed for specific applications.

The preparation process ensures proper assembly and folding of the chimeric antibodies while maintaining antigen-binding capability and Fc functionality.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Cloning of Ferret Immunoglobulin Constant Regions**  

RNA was isolated from ferret kidney, lung, liver, and spleen tissues using Trizol reagent. Following reverse transcription with oligo(dT) primers, PCR amplification was performed using primers designed based on partial ferret sequences and homologous mink sequences. Products were cloned into mammalian expression vectors and sequenced.  

The complete ferret heavy chain constant region sequence showed 89% identity to mink, with most differences in the hinge region. The light chain constant region sequence matched known partial ferret sequences and showed 94% identity to mink. These sequences were used to construct expression vectors for chimeric antibody production.  

### Example 2  

**Preparation of Chimeric Antibody Variants**  

Variable regions from a human anti-RSV antibody were spliced upstream of ferret constant regions in expression vectors. Site-directed mutagenesis introduced Fc modifications (S252M, S252Y, YTE, N434S) into the ferret heavy chain. Antibodies were expressed in HEK293 cells and purified using tandem protein A/protein G chromatography.  

SDS-PAGE and size-exclusion chromatography confirmed proper assembly and homogeneity of all variants. Antigen-binding assays demonstrated retained specificity for RSV F glycoprotein, with IC50 values comparable to the original human antibody.  

### Example 3  

**Ferret FcRn Cloning and Expression**  

The ferret FcRn α chain was cloned using primers designed from multi-species alignments. The complete sequence showed 92% identity to dog FcRn. Soluble ferret FcRn (α chain + β2-microglobulin) was expressed in HEK293 cells and purified by nickel affinity chromatography. SDS-PAGE analysis showed proper assembly of the heterodimeric complex.  

### Example 4  

**FcRn Binding Assays**  

AlphaScreen competitive binding assays measured chimeric antibody binding to ferret FcRn at pH 6.0. The S252Y variant showed 24-fold stronger binding than wild-type (IC50 3.2 μg/ml vs 78 μg/ml). The YTE variant showed similar improvement, while S252M showed intermediate enhancement (IC50 13 μg/ml). These results demonstrated that Fc modifications significantly impact ferret FcRn interactions.  

### Example 5  

**Pharmacokinetics in Ferrets**  

Ferrets received single 2 mg/kg IV doses of antibody variants. Plasma concentrations were monitored for 21 days. The wild-type chimeric antibody showed a half-life of 65 hours, already improved over human antibody (31 hours). The S252M variant extended this to 127 hours, while S252Y and YTE variants reached ~200 hours (8-9 days). No abrupt concentration drops suggested absence of ADA responses.  

### Example 6  

**Assessment of Immunogenicity**  

Plasma samples from pharmacokinetic studies were analyzed for anti-antibody responses using bridging ELISA. No ADA were detected in any chimeric antibody-treated ferrets, in contrast to human antibody-treated animals where ADA were clearly observed. Residual antibody in late samples partially inhibited surrogate ADA detection, suggesting actual ADA levels may have been even lower than measured.
