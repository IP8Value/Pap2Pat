# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of genetic engineering and transcriptional regulation. More specifically, the invention pertains to engineered transcription activator-like (TAL) effector proteins capable of programmable DNA-binding and transcriptional activation in human cells. The invention further encompasses methods for constructing synthetic TAL proteins with customized DNA-binding specificities and enhanced transcriptional activation capabilities through modular assembly techniques.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Current technologies for targeted gene regulation in human cells primarily rely on zinc finger (ZF) proteins, which can be engineered to bind specific DNA sequences. However, ZF proteins present significant limitations, including complex design requirements and unpredictable DNA-binding specificities. Transcription activator-like (TAL) effectors, originally identified as virulence factors in Xanthomonas bacteria, offer a promising alternative due to their modular DNA-binding domains. Each TAL effector repeat unit recognizes a single DNA base pair through repeat-variable diresidues (RVDs), enabling precise targeting of desired sequences.  

While TAL effectors have been demonstrated to function in plant cells, their application in human cells has remained largely unexplored. Native TAL effectors contain a C-terminal activation domain (AD) that drives transcription in plant hosts, but this domain exhibits limited activity in heterologous systems. Additionally, the repetitive nature of TAL effector sequences presents challenges for genetic engineering and assembly of custom DNA-binding domains. There exists a need for improved TAL effector derivatives optimized for human cell applications, along with robust methods for constructing synthetic TAL proteins with programmable specificity.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides engineered TAL effector proteins capable of programmable DNA-binding and transcriptional activation in human cells. The invention encompasses TAL effector derivatives wherein the native activation domain is replaced or supplemented with heterologous activation domains, such as the herpes simplex virus VP16 activation domain, to enhance transcriptional activity in human cells.  

A key aspect of the invention is the development of a modular cloning strategy, termed "Golden TAL Technology," for rapid assembly of synthetic TAL proteins with customized repeat architectures. This technology utilizes type IIs restriction enzymes to enable precise, error-free assembly of TAL repeat arrays without reliance on PCR amplification. The method allows construction of TAL proteins with 1 to 24 designed repeats through combinatorial assembly of a minimal set of repeat modules.  

The invention further demonstrates application of engineered TAL proteins for targeted activation of both reporter genes and endogenous human genes, including PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1. The synthetic TAL proteins exhibit specific DNA-binding and transcriptional activation across multiple human cell lines, establishing their utility as programmable transcription factors for research and therapeutic applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides engineered TAL effector proteins optimized for function in human cells. Native TAL effectors, such as AvrBs3 and Hax3 from Xanthomonas bacteria, contain a central repeat domain that mediates sequence-specific DNA binding and a C-terminal activation domain that drives transcription in plant hosts. The inventors discovered that while native TAL effectors retain measurable activity in human cells, their transcriptional activation potential is significantly enhanced by replacement of the native activation domain with heterologous activation domains optimized for human systems.  

Preferred embodiments utilize the VP16 activation domain from herpes simplex virus, which when fused to TAL effector derivatives increases transcriptional activation 2-3 fold compared to native TAL proteins and 50-80 fold compared to negative controls. The VP16-TAL fusions demonstrate activity across multiple human cell lines, including HEK293T-Rex, Huh7, and HeLa cells, though activation levels vary depending on cell type. Alternative activation domains, such as the yeast GAL4 activation domain, may also be employed but show less potent activation in human cells.  

The DNA-binding specificity of TAL effector derivatives is determined by the composition and order of their repeat domains. Each 34-amino acid repeat unit recognizes a single DNA base pair through hypervariable residues at positions 12 and 13 (repeat-variable diresidues or RVDs). The invention encompasses TAL proteins containing between 1 and 24 designed repeats, with preferred embodiments utilizing at least 10.5 repeats for maximal DNA-binding activity. The last repeat may be truncated to 20 amino acids while retaining RVD functionality.  

A critical innovation of the invention is the Golden TAL Technology, a modular cloning system for rapid assembly of custom TAL repeat arrays. The technology employs type IIs restriction enzymes (BpiI and BsaI) to enable scarless assembly of repeat modules while maintaining proper amino acid sequences. Key features include:  

1. A library of 24 single-repeat plasmids encoding the four most common RVD specificities (NI for A, HD for C, NN for G, and NG for T)  
2. Assembly vectors allowing combination of 1-6 repeats into intermediate constructs  
3. Flanking modules for N-terminal tags (e.g., GFP), C-terminal domains (e.g., VP16), and vector backbones  
4. Golden Gate cloning principles enabling one-step assembly of complete TAL expression constructs  

The modular design allows flexible construction of TAL proteins targeting any desired DNA sequence without PCR amplification, reducing errors associated with repetitive sequences. The technology further permits easy expansion through addition of new modules for alternative tags, activation domains, or expression systems.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**TAL Effector Derivatives with Heterologous Activation Domains**  

TAL effector derivatives were constructed by replacing the native activation domains of AvrBs3 and Hax3 with the VP16 (68 amino acids) or GAL4 (113 amino acids) activation domains. All constructs included an N-terminal GFP tag for localization studies. The proteins were expressed in human HEK293T-Rex cells alongside luciferase reporter constructs containing either AvrBs3- or Hax3-specific target sequences upstream of a minimal CMV promoter.  

Native AvrBs3 and Hax3 showed specific activation of matching reporters (22-fold and 18-fold over background, respectively). Deletion of the native activation domain reduced activity to 15% of wild-type levels. Fusion with VP16 increased activation to 50-80 fold over background, while GAL4 fusions showed only modest improvement. All constructs localized to the nucleus, confirming proper nuclear import signal function.  

### Example 2  

**Cell-Type Specific Activity of TAL-VP16 Fusions**  

The cell-type specificity of TAL-VP16 activity was tested in HEK293T-Rex, Huh7, and HeLa cells using the reporter system described in Example 1. All three cell lines showed specific activation of reporters by matching TAL-VP16 fusions, though activation levels varied: HEK293T-Rex (50-80 fold), Huh7 (30-50 fold), and HeLa (20-30 fold). Western blot analysis confirmed comparable expression levels across cell lines, suggesting differences in activation reflect cell-specific factors rather than protein stability.  

### Example 3  

**Golden TAL Technology Assembly**  

A synthetic TAL protein targeting the human PUMA gene promoter was constructed using the Golden TAL Technology. The design incorporated 18 repeats with RVDs matching the PUMA promoter sequence. Assembly proceeded in two steps:  

1. Six intermediate assemblies were created by BpiI-mediated ligation of 3 repeats each  
2. The complete repeat array was assembled with N-terminal GFP and C-terminal VP16 modules using BsaI  

The entire process required three days and produced the desired construct with >90% efficiency as determined by colony PCR and sequencing. The synthetic TAL protein demonstrated specific binding to the PUMA target sequence in electrophoretic mobility shift assays.  

### Example 4  

**Activation of Endogenous Human Genes**  

Three synthetic TAL-VP16 proteins were designed to target promoters of the human PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1 genes. HEK293T-Rex cells were transfected with each construct, and target gene expression was measured by qRT-PCR 24 hours post-transfection.  

The TAL-VP16 fusions induced specific upregulation of their target genes: PUMA (1.5-fold), IFNα1 (3.1-fold), and IFNβ1 (3.5-fold). No activation was observed for non-target genes. Replacement of the minimal CMV promoter in reporter constructs with the native IFNβ1 promoter yielded similar activation levels (3.2-fold), suggesting endogenous chromatin environment limits activation efficiency.  

### Example 5  

**Comparison of Golden TAL Technology to Alternative Methods**  

The Golden TAL Technology was compared to PCR-based assembly methods for constructing TAL repeat arrays. Key advantages observed:  

1. Higher fidelity - Sequencing revealed 0.5% errors with Golden TAL vs 3-5% with PCR assembly  
2. Faster assembly - Complete constructs in 3 days vs 5-7 days for PCR methods  
3. Greater flexibility - Easy substitution of modules for different tags or domains  
4. Lower resource requirements - Only 24 repeat plasmids needed vs 48 for equivalent PCR library  

These advantages make the Golden TAL Technology particularly suitable for high-throughput construction of custom TAL proteins for research and therapeutic applications.
