# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Prostate cancer represents one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting men worldwide, with significant disparities observed across different ancestral populations. Men of African ancestry exhibit markedly higher incidence rates and poorer clinical outcomes compared to other ethnic groups. While substantial research efforts have characterized genomic alterations in prostate cancers from populations of European descent, comprehensive molecular analyses of tumors from African American patients remain critically lacking.  

Current diagnostic and prognostic approaches fail to account for population-specific molecular features, resulting in suboptimal risk stratification and therapeutic decision-making for African American patients. The well-characterized TMPRSS2-ERG gene fusion and PTEN deletions, which serve as established biomarkers in Caucasian populations, demonstrate significantly lower prevalence in African American prostate cancers. This diagnostic gap underscores the urgent need for ancestry-specific molecular markers that can accurately reflect disease biology across diverse patient populations.  

Existing genomic profiling technologies have primarily focused on alterations common in European-derived populations, leaving a substantial portion of African American prostate cancer biology unexplored. The lack of population-tailored biomarkers contributes to ongoing health disparities, as current clinical tools may fail to identify aggressive disease subtypes prevalent in African American men. There exists a pressing need for novel diagnostic methods capable of detecting ancestry-specific genomic alterations that drive prostate cancer pathogenesis in underserved populations.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for detecting population-specific genomic alterations in prostate cancer, with particular utility for African American patients. Through comprehensive whole genome analyses, the inventors have identified recurrent deletions within the LSAMP (Limbic System-Associated Membrane Protein) locus at chromosome 3q13.31 as a hallmark genomic alteration in African American prostate cancers. These LSAMP deletions demonstrate significant association with aggressive disease features and poor clinical outcomes, including biochemical recurrence and pathological stage progression.  

The invention encompasses diagnostic assays for detecting LSAMP locus alterations, including but not limited to fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) probes targeting the 3q13.31 genomic region, PCR-based detection methods for LSAMP deletion breakpoints, and next-generation sequencing approaches for comprehensive characterization of structural variations at this locus. The disclosed methods provide superior prognostic accuracy for African American prostate cancer patients compared to existing biomarkers developed primarily for Caucasian populations.  

Furthermore, the invention includes kits comprising specific nucleic acid probes and primers for LSAMP deletion detection, as well as computer-implemented methods for analyzing sequencing data to identify LSAMP-associated genomic alterations. The diagnostic platforms of the invention enable identification of high-risk prostate cancer subsets in African American patients, facilitating personalized treatment strategies and improved clinical management.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### DEFINITIONS  

As used throughout this specification and claims, the following terms shall have the meanings specified herein, unless context clearly indicates otherwise:  

"LSAMP locus" refers to the genomic region spanning chromosome 3q13.31, encompassing the LSAMP gene (NCBI Gene ID 4045) and adjacent genomic sequences including but not limited to ZBTB20, GAP43, and GSK3B genes.  

"African American prostate cancer" denotes prostate adenocarcinoma occurring in individuals of recent African ancestry, as determined by genetic ancestry markers or self-reported ethnicity.  

"Biochemical recurrence" refers to prostate-specific antigen (PSA) elevation following definitive local therapy for prostate cancer, specifically a PSA level ≥ 0.2 ng/mL after radical prostatectomy or PSA nadir + 2 ng/mL after radiation therapy.  

"Structural variation" encompasses genomic alterations including deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations, and other large-scale rearrangements affecting DNA segments greater than 50 base pairs in length.  

"Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) probe" means a nucleic acid sequence complementary to a specific genomic region, conjugated to a detectable fluorescent label, suitable for hybridization to chromosomal DNA in situ.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Detection of LSAMP Locus Deletions by Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization**  

A fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assay was developed for detection of LSAMP locus deletions in prostate cancer specimens. The assay employs two distinct probe sets:  

1. A locus-specific probe combination spanning approximately 500 kilobases of genomic sequence between ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes, labeled with CytoOrange fluorescent dye.  
2. A chromosome 3 centromeric control probe labeled with CytoGreen fluorescent dye.  

Prostate tissue sections were subjected to standard FISH processing protocols including deparaffinization, protease digestion, and target DNA denaturation. The probe mixture was applied to prepared slides and hybridized overnight at 37°C. Following post-hybridization washes, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI and visualized by fluorescence microscopy.  

Tumor samples were scored as LSAMP-deleted when >75% of evaluable tumor cells showed loss of one or both locus-specific signals while retaining two centromeric signals. Focal deletions were called when >25% of tumor cells showed signal loss or when >50% of cells in gland clusters exhibited deletion.  

Validation studies demonstrated 26% prevalence of LSAMP deletions in African American prostate cancers compared to 7% in Caucasian American cases (p=0.007). LSAMP deletion status significantly correlated with biochemical recurrence and pathological stage progression in African American patients.  

**Example 2: Next-Generation Sequencing Analysis of LSAMP Structural Variations**  

Whole genome sequencing was performed on matched tumor and normal samples from African American prostate cancer patients. Sequencing libraries were prepared using Illumina TruSeq DNA PCR-Free protocols and sequenced to ≥30× haploid coverage on Illumina platforms.  

Sequence reads were aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh37/hg19) using optimized alignment algorithms. Structural variations affecting the LSAMP locus were identified using a combination of breakpoint detection algorithms including BreakDancer and Genomatix Mapper.  

Analysis revealed three distinct patterns of LSAMP locus alteration in African American tumors:  
1. Large deletions spanning up to 23 megabases of the 3q13.31 region  
2. Focal 1 megabase deletions specifically targeting LSAMP  
3. Complex rearrangements creating novel fusion junctions involving LSAMP  

All three alteration patterns were validated by orthogonal methods including Sanger sequencing and RNA-seq analysis of fusion transcripts. Patients harboring these LSAMP alterations demonstrated significantly higher rates of disease recurrence following prostatectomy.  

**Example 3: Quantitative PCR Assay for LSAMP Copy Number Assessment**  

A quantitative PCR (qPCR) assay was developed to rapidly assess LSAMP copy number status in prostate cancer samples. The assay employs TaqMan probes targeting three conserved exonic regions of LSAMP (exons 2, 5, and 8) along with reference assays for chromosome 3 pericentromeric sequences.  

DNA samples were extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded prostate tissue sections using optimized protocols. qPCR reactions were performed in triplicate using 10 ng input DNA and commercially available master mix reagents. Copy number was calculated using the ΔΔCt method with normalization to reference loci.  

The qPCR assay demonstrated 92% concordance with FISH results for LSAMP deletion detection. This method provides a rapid, high-throughput alternative to FISH for clinical assessment of LSAMP copy number alterations in African American prostate cancer patients.  

**Example 4: Prognostic Stratification Using LSAMP Deletion Status**  

A retrospective analysis was conducted to evaluate the prognostic significance of LSAMP deletions in African American prostate cancer patients. A cohort of 126 patients with clinically localized disease was stratified by LSAMP status as determined by FISH analysis.  

Patients with LSAMP deletions demonstrated significantly shorter biochemical recurrence-free survival compared to those with intact LSAMP (median 24 months vs. not reached, p<0.001). Multivariate analysis confirmed LSAMP deletion as an independent predictor of recurrence after adjusting for Gleason score, pathological stage, and surgical margin status (HR 3.12, 95% CI 1.89-5.15).  

These findings establish LSAMP deletion as a potent prognostic biomarker for aggressive disease in African American prostate cancer patients, enabling improved risk stratification and personalized treatment approaches.  

**Example 5: Tissue Microarray Validation of LSAMP Deletion Frequency**  

A tissue microarray comprising 473 cores from 101 prostate cancer patients (42 African American, 59 Caucasian American) was analyzed by LSAMP FISH. The microarray included multiple tumor regions from each case along with matched benign tissue controls.  

LSAMP deletions were observed in 26% of African American cases compared to 7% of Caucasian American cases (p=0.007). Among African American patients, LSAMP deletion significantly associated with:  
- Higher pathological stage (pT3 vs pT2, p=0.03)  
- Positive surgical margins (p=0.04)  
- Shorter time to biochemical recurrence (p=0.01)  

These results confirm the population-specific prevalence and clinical significance of LSAMP deletions in African American prostate cancer, supporting their utility as ancestry-informed biomarkers.  

**Example 6: Development of a Clinical Decision Support Algorithm**  

A computational algorithm was developed to integrate LSAMP deletion status with standard clinicopathological parameters for risk assessment in African American prostate cancer patients. The algorithm incorporates:  
1. LSAMP FISH or qPCR results  
2. Gleason grade group  
3. Pathological stage  
4. Surgical margin status  
5. Preoperative PSA level  

Application of this algorithm to a validation cohort demonstrated improved discrimination of recurrence risk compared to existing nomograms (concordance index 0.78 vs 0.68, p=0.008). The integrated model enables identification of high-risk African American patients who may benefit from intensified adjuvant therapy or clinical trial participation.  

This example illustrates the clinical utility of LSAMP deletion detection when combined with standard prognostic factors for personalized management of African American prostate cancer patients.  

**Example 7: Targeted Next-Generation Sequencing Panel**  

A targeted sequencing panel was designed to comprehensively assess LSAMP locus alterations and related genomic markers in prostate cancer. The panel includes:  
- Complete exon coverage of LSAMP, ZBTB20, and adjacent genes  
- Intronic regions frequently involved in structural variations  
- Known breakpoint hotspots within 3q13.31  
- Control regions from stable genomic loci  

The panel demonstrated 100% sensitivity for detecting LSAMP deletions identified by whole genome sequencing while providing single-base resolution of breakpoint junctions. This approach enables detailed characterization of LSAMP alterations for both clinical and research applications.  

**Example 8: Liquid Biopsy Detection of LSAMP Alterations**  

A circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) assay was developed to non-invasively detect LSAMP deletions in plasma samples from prostate cancer patients. The assay employs:  
- Targeted capture of 3q13.31 genomic regions  
- Unique molecular identifiers for error correction  
- High-depth sequencing (>10,000×)  
- Computational algorithms for structural variation detection  

In preliminary studies, the assay detected LSAMP deletions in 78% of metastatic African American prostate cancer patients with known LSAMP alterations in matched tissue samples. This approach enables longitudinal monitoring of LSAMP status during disease progression and treatment.  

**Example 9: Therapeutic Implications of LSAMP Deletion Status**  

Preclinical studies were conducted to evaluate therapeutic vulnerabilities associated with LSAMP deletion in prostate cancer models. LSAMP-deficient cell lines demonstrated:  
- Increased sensitivity to DNA damaging agents (cisplatin, carboplatin)  
- Enhanced response to PARP inhibition (olaparib, talazoparib)  
- Reduced proliferation following WNT pathway inhibition  

These findings suggest LSAMP deletion may serve as a predictive biomarker for targeted therapy selection in African American prostate cancer patients, enabling precision medicine approaches tailored to molecular tumor characteristics.  

**Example 10: Population-Specific Diagnostic Algorithm**  

A diagnostic algorithm was developed to optimize prostate cancer biomarker testing based on patient ancestry:  

For African American patients:  
1. Primary testing for LSAMP deletions (FISH or qPCR)  
2. Secondary assessment of PTEN status  
3. Optional ERG fusion testing  

For Caucasian American patients:  
1. Primary testing for TMPRSS2-ERG fusions  
2. Secondary assessment of PTEN status  
3. Optional LSAMP deletion testing  

This ancestry-informed approach improves diagnostic yield and clinical relevance compared to uniform biomarker testing across populations, addressing critical gaps in precision oncology for diverse patient groups.
