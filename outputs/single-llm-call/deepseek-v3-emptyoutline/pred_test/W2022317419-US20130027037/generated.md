# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging has emerged as a valuable clinical tool for assessing myocardial wall motion abnormalities following exercise stress testing. Current techniques allow for real-time cine imaging immediately after treadmill exercise, enabling the capture of transient stress-induced wall motion abnormalities that rapidly fade following cessation of physical activity. However, the existing methodologies suffer from significant limitations in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and temporal/spatial resolution when implemented under post-exercise conditions.  

The physiological challenges posed by rapid heart rates and exaggerated breathing patterns following exercise create substantial obstacles for conventional real-time cine imaging approaches. Traditional segmented k-space acquisition methods become impractical due to patient inability to maintain breath-holding during recovery from strenuous exercise. While real-time imaging eliminates the need for ECG triggering and breath-holding requirements, this comes at the cost of reduced image quality through diminished SNR and compromised resolution parameters.  

Prior attempts to address these limitations have relied upon conventional parallel imaging reconstruction techniques such as TGRAPPA (Temporal Generalized Autocalibrating Partially Parallel Acquisitions). However, these methods fail to adequately compensate for the unique challenges presented by exercise stress cine imaging, particularly in maintaining sufficient SNR while controlling motion-induced artifacts. There exists an unmet clinical need for improved reconstruction algorithms capable of delivering diagnostic-quality images under the demanding conditions of post-exercise cardiac imaging.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a novel image reconstruction system and method specifically optimized for exercise stress cardiac cine imaging. The disclosed technique extends the SPIRIT (Iterative Self-consistent Parallel Imaging Reconstruction) framework through innovative incorporation of temporal sensitivity estimation and spatial regularization components, collectively referred to as TSPIRIT (Temporal SPIRIT).  

The system comprises specialized hardware configurations including an MR-compatible treadmill system integrated with a magnetic resonance imaging scanner equipped with a multi-channel receiver coil array. The method involves acquiring temporally interleaved k-space data frames during free-breathing post-exercise conditions, followed by a multi-stage reconstruction process that significantly improves upon conventional approaches.  

Key innovations include: dynamic temporal sensitivity estimation through Karhunen-Loeve transform filtering of auto-calibration signal (ACS) lines; frame-specific SPIRIT calibration; and a hybrid solver approach combining LSQR matrix inversion with non-linear conjugate gradient optimization incorporating spatial regularization constraints. This comprehensive reconstruction pipeline achieves substantial improvements in image quality, demonstrated through clinical testing to provide an average 38.2% increase in SNR without introducing additional ghosting artifacts compared to standard TGRAPPA reconstruction of identical raw data.  

The invention represents a significant advancement in cardiac stress imaging by enabling high-quality real-time cine acquisition during the critical post-exercise period when diagnostic information is most valuable but most challenging to capture. The technical solution addresses specific limitations of prior art through novel integration of temporal sensitivity modeling with spatially constrained iterative reconstruction, optimized for the unique requirements of exercise stress CMR applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS AND PRESENTLY PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

Figure 1 illustrates a comparative example of image quality improvement achieved by the TSPIRIT reconstruction method versus conventional TGRAPPA processing. The TSPIRIT-reconstructed image demonstrates markedly improved myocardial border definition and blood pool contrast, with reduced noise artifacts while maintaining anatomical fidelity. Visual assessment confirms preservation of fine structural details including trabeculations and papillary muscle architecture that are often obscured in traditional reconstructions.  

Figure 2 presents quantitative analysis of SNR enhancement and artifact performance across multiple test cases. The graphical representation shows consistent SNR improvement averaging 38.2% (±17.8% standard deviation) with statistical significance (P<1e-5), while maintaining equivalent ghosting artifact levels (P=0.960) compared to conventional methods. These metrics validate the technical advantages of the disclosed invention without introducing trade-offs in artifact performance.  

In preferred embodiments, the system implementation comprises a 1.5T magnetic resonance imaging scanner (such as the Siemens Avanto platform) equipped with a 32-channel phased-array cardiac receiver coil. The pulse sequence utilizes balanced steady-state free precession (bSSFP) acquisition with the following optimized parameters: repetition time (TR) 1.09ms, echo time (TE) 0.9ms, image matrix size 160×80, flip angle 58°, nominal resolution 2.44×2.44 mm², bandwidth 1420Hz/pixel, and acceleration rate of 4 using time-interleaved k-space sampling patterns.  

The reconstruction pipeline begins with generation of auto-calibration signal (ACS) lines through averaging of all temporally interleaved k-space frames. GRAPPA reconstruction is initially applied to estimate full k-space data for each temporal frame, serving as input for subsequent processing stages. The Karhunen-Loeve transform filtering is then applied to enhance temporal sensitivity estimation, followed by frame-specific SPIRIT calibration to generate reconstruction kernels.  

The core reconstruction algorithm employs a two-stage solver approach: first performing LSQR-based matrix inversion followed by iterative optimization using a non-linear conjugate gradient method incorporating spatial regularization constraints. The regularization terms are carefully tuned to balance noise reduction with preservation of anatomical details, particularly important for visualizing subtle wall motion abnormalities. Convergence criteria are established to ensure reconstruction quality while maintaining clinically feasible processing times.  

Alternative embodiments may incorporate variations in specific acquisition parameters, including adjustment of acceleration factors from 2-6 depending on desired temporal resolution requirements. The reconstruction framework remains adaptable to different field strengths (ranging from 1.0T to 3.0T) and various multi-channel coil configurations (typically 8-64 channels). The spatial regularization parameters may be automatically adjusted based on measured noise characteristics or manually tuned for specific diagnostic priorities.  

The invention has been validated through clinical testing in healthy volunteer populations, demonstrating consistent improvements in image quality metrics. Future implementations may incorporate machine learning components to further optimize reconstruction parameters or automate quality assessment. The system architecture allows for integration with existing clinical workflow systems while providing specialized processing for exercise stress applications.
