# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION
The present invention relates to novel pharmaceutical compositions and methods for preventing and treating non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastropathy. More particularly, the invention concerns tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid molecules that protect gastric mucosa by targeting mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) through scavenging of intramitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) and chelation of free iron. The invention further provides methods of synthesizing these hybrid molecules, pharmaceutical formulations containing them, and therapeutic methods for their use in preventing and healing NSAID-induced gastric mucosal damage.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are among the most commonly used medications worldwide for their analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory properties. However, their use is frequently associated with significant gastrointestinal toxicity, including gastropathy characterized by gastric mucosal injury, ulceration, and bleeding. The pathogenesis of NSAID-induced gastropathy involves both cyclooxygenase (COX)-dependent and COX-independent mechanisms. While proton pump inhibitors and histamine H2 receptor antagonists are commonly used to prevent NSAID-induced gastric damage, these agents have limitations including incomplete protection and various adverse effects.

Recent research has identified mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) as a key mechanism in NSAID-induced gastropathy. NSAIDs such as indomethacin interact with mitochondrial complex I of the electron transport chain, leading to electron leakage and generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). This oxidative stress is further exacerbated by the release of free iron from damaged iron-sulfur clusters, which catalyzes the formation of highly reactive hydroxyl radicals through Fenton chemistry. The resulting MOS causes mitochondrial dysfunction, activates apoptotic pathways, and ultimately leads to gastric mucosal injury.

Current approaches to prevent NSAID-induced gastropathy do not specifically target mitochondrial oxidative stress. There exists a need for novel therapeutic agents that can specifically address the mitochondrial pathology underlying NSAID-induced gastric damage while avoiding the limitations of current gastroprotective agents.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION
The primary objective of the present invention is to provide novel pharmaceutical compositions that effectively prevent and treat NSAID-induced gastropathy by targeting mitochondrial oxidative stress. A specific objective is to provide tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid molecules that combine mitochondrial targeting capability with antioxidant and iron-chelating properties.

Another objective is to provide methods for synthesizing these hybrid molecules through efficient chemical processes that allow for large-scale production. A further objective is to provide pharmaceutical formulations containing these hybrid molecules in forms suitable for oral, parenteral, or other routes of administration.

An additional objective is to provide therapeutic methods for using these compositions to prevent NSAID-induced gastric mucosal damage and to accelerate healing of existing damage. The invention also aims to provide compositions with superior efficacy and safety profiles compared to existing gastroprotective agents.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
The present invention provides novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid molecules, particularly SEGA (3a), that effectively prevent and treat NSAID-induced gastropathy through a unique mechanism targeting mitochondrial oxidative stress. These hybrid molecules combine the mitochondrial targeting capability of tryptamine derivatives with the potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties of gallic acid.

The invention encompasses the chemical synthesis of these hybrid molecules through conjugation reactions, particularly amide bond formation between tryptamine derivatives and gallic acid. The preferred embodiment, SEGA (3a), demonstrates superior antioxidant activity in vitro as measured by ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assays compared to other conjugates.

In vivo studies demonstrate that SEGA (3a) effectively prevents indomethacin-induced gastric mucosal injury in a dose-dependent manner, with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg body weight. The compound protects against mitochondrial oxidative stress by scavenging intramitochondrial superoxide anions and chelating free iron, thereby preventing mitochondrial dysfunction and subsequent apoptosis. SEGA (3a) also accelerates healing of existing gastric mucosal damage by restoring normal mucosal architecture.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical formulations containing these hybrid molecules, methods for their administration, and their use in combination with NSAIDs to prevent gastrointestinal toxicity while maintaining the therapeutic benefits of NSAID therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION
The present invention provides novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid molecules that effectively target mitochondrial oxidative stress in NSAID-induced gastropathy. The hybrid molecules are synthesized through conjugation reactions between tryptamine derivatives and gallic acid, preferably through amide bond formation to produce compounds with optimal pharmacological properties.

The preferred embodiment, designated SEGA (3a), is synthesized by conjugating 5-hydroxytryptamine (5HT) with gallic acid (GA) through an amide linkage. This specific conjugation preserves the mitochondrial targeting capability of 5HT while enhancing the bioavailability and pharmacological activity of GA. The amide linkage in SEGA (3a) proves superior to ester linkages in terms of antioxidant potency and gastroprotective efficacy.

The synthesis of SEGA (3a) involves the following steps: (1) activation of the carboxyl group of gallic acid using a carbodiimide coupling agent such as EDC (1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide) in the presence of 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt); (2) reaction with 5-hydroxytryptamine in the presence of a base such as triethylamine; (3) purification of the product by column chromatography; and (4) optional debenzylation if protected gallic acid derivatives are used in the synthesis.

The invention encompasses variations of this basic structure, including conjugates formed with different tryptamine derivatives (e.g., tryptamine, 5-methoxytryptamine) and different antioxidants (e.g., 4-hydroxycinnamic acid, indole-3-acetic acid). However, SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior activity in both in vitro and in vivo models of NSAID-induced gastropathy.

### Biological Effects
SEGA (3a) exhibits multiple biological effects that contribute to its gastroprotective activity. The compound demonstrates potent antioxidant activity in vitro, as measured by FRAP and DPPH radical scavenging assays. In the FRAP assay, SEGA (3a) shows significantly greater reducing ability (Fe(III) to Fe(II)) compared to other conjugates. In the DPPH assay, SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior radical scavenging activity with an IC50 significantly lower than other tested compounds.

The compound also exhibits strong iron-chelating properties, as demonstrated by its ability to prevent formation of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex in vitro. This property is crucial for preventing iron-catalyzed ROS generation in mitochondria. In cellular models, SEGA (3a) effectively scavenges intramitochondrial superoxide anions and chelates free iron, as shown by fluorescence microscopy using MitoSOX and Phen Green SK probes.

In vivo, SEGA (3a) prevents indomethacin-induced gastric mucosal injury in a dose-dependent manner, with complete protection observed at 50 mg/kg body weight. The compound preserves mitochondrial function by maintaining respiratory control ratio (RCR), mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), and dehydrogenase activity in gastric mucosal cells exposed to indomethacin.

SEGA (3a) inhibits the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis by preventing caspase-9 and caspase-3 activation, modulating Bcl-2 family protein expression, and reducing DNA fragmentation in gastric mucosal cells. The compound also accelerates healing of existing gastric mucosal damage by promoting restoration of normal mucosal architecture.

## EXAMPLES
The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.

### Example 1
**Synthesis of SEGA (3a):** To a solution of gallic acid (1 equivalent, 12 mmol) and 5-hydroxytryptamine hydrochloride (1.2 equivalents, 14.4 mmol) in N,N-dimethylformamide, 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (1 equivalent, 12 mmol) and triethylamine (6 equivalents, 72 mmol) were added. EDC hydrochloride (1.2 equivalents, 14.4 mmol) was added at 0°C, and the reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight. The reaction was monitored by thin layer chromatography (TLC). The mixture was quenched with ice-cold water and extracted with ethyl acetate. The organic phase was washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by silica gel column chromatography to yield SEGA (3a) as a white solid.

### Example 2
**In Vitro Antioxidant Activity (FRAP Assay):** FRAP reagent was prepared by mixing acetate buffer (200 mM, pH 3.6), TPTZ solution (10 mM in 40 mM HCl), and ferric chloride solution (20 mM). SEGA (3a) and comparator compounds were dissolved in methanol at concentrations ranging from 1-100 μM. Samples (25 μL) were mixed with FRAP solution (175 μL) in a 96-well microplate. Absorbance at 595 nm was monitored for 150 minutes. SEGA (3a) showed significantly greater Fe(III) reducing ability compared to other conjugates, with a FRAP value at 6 minutes of 0.85 ± 0.05 (mean ± SEM, n=6).

### Example 3
**In Vitro Radical Scavenging Activity (DPPH Assay):** SEGA (3a) and comparator compounds (10-100 μM) in methanol were mixed with DPPH solution (0.15 mM in 80% methanol). After 30 minutes at room temperature, absorbance at 517 nm was measured. SEGA (3a) showed concentration-dependent radical scavenging activity with an IC50 of 15.2 ± 1.3 μM (mean ± SEM, n=6), significantly lower than other tested conjugates.

### Example 4
**Iron Chelating Activity:** Fe(II) solution (10 μM in phosphate buffer, pH 7.4) was mixed with SEGA (3a) (500 nM to 100 μM) and TPTZ (20 μM). Absorbance of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex at 595 nm was measured immediately. SEGA (3a) completely prevented formation of the Fe(II)-TPTZ complex at concentrations ≥10 μM, demonstrating potent iron chelating activity comparable to desferrioxamine.

### Example 5
**In Vivo Gastroprotective Activity:** Sprague-Dawley rats (180-220 g) were fasted for 24 hours and pretreated with SEGA (3a) (1-50 mg/kg, intraperitoneal) 30 minutes before oral indomethacin (48 mg/kg). After 4 hours, gastric injury was scored. SEGA (3a) showed dose-dependent protection with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg. At 50 mg/kg, SEGA (3a) reduced the injury index from 52 ± 4 (indomethacin alone) to 2 ± 1 (mean ± SEM, n=6).

### Example 6
**Mitochondrial Protection:** Gastric mucosal mitochondria were isolated from control, indomethacin-treated, and SEGA (3a)-pretreated rats. SEGA (3a) pretreatment prevented indomethacin-induced: (1) decrease in respiratory control ratio (from 2.1 ± 0.2 to 4.8 ± 0.3, control=5.0 ± 0.4); (2) loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (590/530 nm fluorescence ratio from 1.2 ± 0.1 to 3.8 ± 0.3, control=4.0 ± 0.3); and (3) reduction in dehydrogenase activity (MTT reduction from 55 ± 5% to 95 ± 3% of control).

### Example 7
**Antiapoptotic Effects:** SEGA (3a) pretreatment prevented indomethacin-induced: (1) caspase-9 activation (from 210 ± 15% to 110 ± 10% of control); (2) caspase-3 activation (from 230 ± 20% to 120 ± 10% of control); (3) down-regulation of Bcl-2 (from 40 ± 5% to 90 ± 8% of control); and (4) up-regulation of Bax (from 300 ± 25% to 120 ± 10% of control). TUNEL assay showed SEGA (3a) reduced apoptotic cells from 45 ± 5% to 10 ± 2%.

### Example 8
**Healing of Gastric Mucosal Injury:** After inducing gastric injury with indomethacin, rats were treated with SEGA (3a) (50 mg/kg). SEGA (3a) accelerated healing compared to autohealing: at 20 hours post-injury, the injury index was 0 ± 0 for SEGA (3a) versus 20 ± 2 for autohealing (mean ± SEM, n=6). Histology showed complete mucosal restoration with SEGA (3a) treatment versus persistent damage in untreated controls.

### Example 9
**Mitochondrial Uptake:** SEGA (3a) was radiolabeled with 99mTc and administered to rats (5-8 μCi, intraperitoneal). After 4 hours, mitochondrial uptake was 0.05 ± 0.01% of administered dose per mg mitochondrial protein (mean ± SEM, n=6). The labeled compound showed excellent stability in physiological saline and serum over 24 hours at 37°C (retention of label >95%).

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION
The tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid molecules of the present invention offer several advantages over existing therapies for NSAID-induced gastropathy. First, these compounds specifically target mitochondrial oxidative stress, addressing the root cause of NSAID-induced gastric damage rather than just symptomatically reducing acid secretion. This targeted mechanism results in superior gastroprotective efficacy compared to conventional agents like H2 receptor antagonists.

Second, the hybrid structure combines the mitochondrial targeting capability of tryptamine derivatives with the potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties of gallic acid, creating a synergistic therapeutic effect. The amide linkage in SEGA (3a) provides optimal pharmacological properties, including enhanced stability and bioavailability compared to the parent compounds administered separately.

Third, these compounds demonstrate a favorable safety profile. The effective dose in humans (approximately 8 mg/kg based on animal-to-human dose conversion) is well below levels associated with toxicity. Unlike proton pump inhibitors, SEGA (3a) does not alter gastric pH or intestinal microbiota, avoiding complications such as bacterial infections and small intestinal damage associated with long-term PPI use.

Fourth, the compounds not only prevent gastric damage but also accelerate healing of existing damage, offering therapeutic benefits beyond simple prophylaxis. This dual action makes them particularly valuable for patients who have already developed NSAID-induced gastropathy.

Fifth, the synthetic methods for producing these hybrid molecules are scalable and cost-effective, using readily available starting materials and standard chemical processes. This facilitates large-scale production and commercial viability.

Finally, the compounds can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, parenteral, and possibly topical forms, providing flexibility in clinical use. Their stability in physiological conditions ensures adequate shelf life and in vivo persistence for therapeutic effect.
