# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology, specifically to methods and compositions for protecting ocular surface epithelial cells and their glycocalyx from damage caused by oxidative stress and other damaging agents. More particularly, the invention discloses the use of dynamin inhibitors such as Dynasore and Dyngo-4a to prevent damage to ocular surface cells and maintain barrier function, thereby preventing uptake of vital dyes used in clinical diagnostics. The invention further provides methods for treating ocular surface diseases characterized by epithelial damage, such as dry eye disease and solution-induced corneal staining.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The ocular surface is continuously exposed to environmental insults including ultraviolet light, microorganisms, allergens, and desiccating conditions that can cause epithelial damage. Such damage is characteristic of dry eye disease, which affects a significant portion of the population worldwide. Current clinical methods for assessing ocular surface damage rely on staining with vital dyes such as fluorescein and rose bengal, which penetrate compromised epithelial barriers. However, the precise mechanisms by which these dyes enter damaged cells remain poorly understood.  

Recent studies have suggested that vital dye uptake occurs in living cells that have sustained sublethal damage, rather than in dead or dying cells. This damage may stimulate reparative plasma membrane remodeling processes that allow dye penetration. While endocytosis has been proposed as one mechanism for dye uptake in monolayer cell cultures, this does not explain observations in stratified epithelial models that better represent the ocular surface. There remains an unmet need for compositions and methods that protect the ocular surface epithelium from damage while maintaining normal barrier function.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for protecting ocular surface epithelial cells and their glycocalyx from damage. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that dynamin inhibitors, particularly Dynasore and its more potent analogue Dyngo-4a, effectively prevent oxidative stress-induced damage to both cells and their surface glycocalyx.  

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for protecting ocular surface epithelial cells comprising administering an effective amount of a dynamin inhibitor to a subject in need thereof. The dynamin inhibitor is selected from the group consisting of Dynasore, Dyngo-4a, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts thereof.  

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for preventing vital dye uptake at the ocular surface comprising administering an effective amount of a dynamin inhibitor prior to exposure to damaging conditions. The method is particularly useful for preventing solution-induced corneal staining associated with contact lens multipurpose solutions.  

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a dynamin inhibitor in a formulation suitable for ocular administration. The composition may further include pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, carriers, or other active ingredients useful for treating ocular surface disorders.  

The invention further provides methods for treating ocular surface diseases characterized by epithelial damage, including dry eye disease, by administering dynamin inhibitors to protect epithelial cells and maintain barrier function. The methods are particularly effective when administered prophylactically before damage occurs, but may also provide benefit when administered chronically to protect newly regenerating epithelial cells.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on extensive experimental studies demonstrating that dynamin inhibitors protect ocular surface epithelial cells from oxidative stress-induced damage. As shown in the Examples below, Dynasore and Dyngo-4a prevent damage to both the plasma membrane and glycocalyx barriers that normally exclude vital dyes. This protective effect occurs through mechanisms distinct from their known inhibition of endocytosis.  

The dynamin inhibitors of the invention include Dynasore (3-hydroxy-naphthalene-2-carboxylic acid (3,4-dihydroxy-benzylidene)-hydrazide) and its more potent analogue Dyngo-4a, as well as pharmaceutically acceptable salts and derivatives thereof. These compounds are small molecule inhibitors of dynamin GTPases that were originally developed to study endocytosis but have now been found to have unexpected protective effects on ocular surface epithelial cells.  

For use in the methods of the invention, the dynamin inhibitors are formulated for ocular administration. Suitable formulations include solutions, suspensions, gels, ointments, inserts, or other dosage forms appropriate for topical ocular delivery. The formulations may contain additional components such as preservatives, viscosity enhancers, buffers, and other excipients known in the art of ophthalmic formulations.  

The effective amount of dynamin inhibitor will vary depending on factors such as the specific compound used, the severity of the condition being treated, and the frequency of administration. Generally, concentrations between 10 μM and 100 μM of Dynasore or between 5 μM and 30 μM of Dyngo-4a are effective for protective effects. The compounds may be administered one or more times daily as needed to maintain protection.  

The methods of the invention are particularly useful for preventing damage from oxidative stress, which is implicated in many ocular surface disorders. However, the protective effects extend to other forms of stress including those caused by contact lens solutions, environmental irritants, and inflammatory mediators. The methods may be used prophylactically before exposure to damaging conditions or therapeutically in established disease to protect newly regenerating epithelial cells.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Protective Effects of Dynasore on Oxidative Stress-Induced Damage**  

Human corneal limbal epithelial (HCLE) cells were cultured as monolayers or allowed to stratify and differentiate for 7 days in differentiation medium. Cells were subjected to oxidative stress using tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBHP) at concentrations of 3 mM or 10 mM. Some cultures were treated simultaneously with 40 μM Dynasore.  

Cell damage was assessed using multiple assays. The MTT and WST-1 metabolic assays showed that oxidative stress significantly increased absorbance values, indicating cellular damage. Dynasore treatment reduced this damage, particularly in the WST-1 assay which measures plasma membrane electron transport. The trypan blue exclusion assay demonstrated that Dynasore almost completely prevented stress-induced plasma membrane permeability changes.  

Glycocalyx damage was assessed by measuring shedding of LGALS3 (galectin-3) into the culture medium. Western blot analysis showed that oxidative stress increased LGALS3 release, indicating glycocalyx disruption. Dynasore treatment prevented this increase, demonstrating protection of the glycocalyx barrier.  

Vital dye uptake experiments showed that Dynasore prevented fluorescein and rose bengal staining when administered simultaneously with oxidative stress. However, Dynasore had no effect on dye uptake when administered after stress, indicating it acts by preventing damage rather than blocking dye uptake mechanisms. Similar results were obtained using Dyngo-4a at lower concentrations (15 μM).  

These results demonstrate that dynamin inhibitors protect both the cellular and glycocalyx barriers of ocular surface epithelial cells from oxidative stress-induced damage, thereby preventing subsequent vital dye uptake. The protective effects are most effective when administered before or during exposure to damaging conditions.
