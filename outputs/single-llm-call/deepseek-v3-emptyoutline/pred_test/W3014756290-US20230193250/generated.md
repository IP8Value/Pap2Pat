# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNOLOGY FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics, specifically methods and compositions for enhancing the efficacy of radiotherapy in malignant gliomas through modulation of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). More particularly, the invention provides novel lncRNA targets, pharmaceutical compositions targeting these lncRNAs, and methods for diagnosing and treating malignant gliomas using these targets in combination with radiation therapy. The invention further encompasses three-dimensional human brain organoid models for evaluating therapeutic efficacy and toxicity of lncRNA-targeting therapies.  

## BACKGROUND  

Malignant gliomas, including glioblastoma (GBM) in adults and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) in children, represent devastating central nervous system malignancies with extremely poor prognoses. Current standard treatments involving surgical resection followed by radiotherapy provide only modest survival benefits, with median survival times of approximately 14 months for GBM and 9-10 months for DIPG. The limited efficacy of radiotherapy stems from both intrinsic resistance of glioma cells and dose-limiting toxicity to surrounding normal brain tissue.  

The human genome encodes thousands of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) - transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins. Many lncRNAs exhibit highly cell type-specific expression patterns and functions, making them attractive candidates for targeted cancer therapies. However, the vast majority of lncRNAs remain functionally uncharacterized, and systematic approaches to identify lncRNAs that could sensitize cancer cells to radiotherapy have been lacking.  

CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) technology has enabled genome-scale functional screens in mammalian cells, allowing systematic investigation of both coding and non-coding genes. While these methods have identified genes essential for various cellular phenotypes, their application to discover targets that enhance the efficacy of ionizing radiation - a critical adjunctive therapy for many malignancies - has not been previously explored at scale.  

There exists a critical unmet need for therapeutics that can selectively enhance the radiosensitivity of malignant glioma cells while sparing normal brain tissue. Such agents would allow for improved tumor control at lower radiation doses, reducing treatment-related toxicity and potentially improving patient outcomes.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for identifying and targeting long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) that sensitize malignant glioma cells to radiotherapy. Through a genome-scale CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screen of 5,689 lncRNA loci in glioma cells treated with clinically relevant fractionated radiation, the inventors identified 467 lncRNAs that modify glioma cell growth in the presence of radiation. Among these, 33 lncRNAs demonstrated specific radiation-sensitizing properties, with nine showing expression in both adult and pediatric malignant gliomas.  

A top candidate, designated lncRNA Glioma Radiation Sensitizer-1 (lncGRS-1; also known as CTC-338M12.4), located on chromosome 5q35.3, was found to be essential for glioma cell proliferation and to potentiate the effects of radiation therapy. Knockdown of lncGRS-1 using either CRISPRi or antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) significantly inhibited the growth of both adult GBM and pediatric DIPG cells while showing no toxicity to normal human astrocytes or neurons.  

The invention further provides a novel three-dimensional mature brain organoid (MBO) model comprising induced astrocytes and neurons derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). This model recapitulates key features of the mature human brain microenvironment and supports invasive growth of human glioma cells. Using this platform, the inventors demonstrated that ASO-mediated knockdown of lncGRS-1 selectively reduces glioma tumor growth and enhances radiation sensitivity without affecting normal brain organoid viability.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Identification of lncGRS-1 and related lncRNAs as novel therapeutic targets for malignant glioma  
2. Methods for diagnosing and treating malignant gliomas by modulating lncGRS-1 activity  
3. Pharmaceutical compositions targeting lncGRS-1, including ASOs and other nucleic acid-based therapeutics  
4. Three-dimensional mature brain organoid models for evaluating glioma therapeutics  
5. Methods for screening additional lncRNA radiation sensitizers using the disclosed CRISPRi platform  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"Malignant glioma" refers to primary tumors of the central nervous system exhibiting glial differentiation, including but not limited to glioblastoma (GBM), diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG), anaplastic astrocytoma, and other high-grade gliomas.  

"Radiotherapy sensitizer" refers to an agent that enhances the cytotoxic effects of ionizing radiation on target cells when administered in combination with radiation therapy.  

"CRISPR interference (CRISPRi)" refers to a gene repression system comprising a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein (dCas9) fused to a transcriptional repressor domain (e.g., KRAB), which when targeted to a gene's transcriptional start site by a guide RNA, reduces transcription of the target gene.  

"Mature brain organoid (MBO)" refers to a three-dimensional tissue construct comprising induced astrocytes and/or neurons derived from human pluripotent stem cells that recapitulate features of postnatal human brain tissue.  

"Antisense oligonucleotide (ASO)" refers to a single-stranded deoxyribonucleotide analog capable of binding to a target RNA transcript through Watson-Crick base pairing, typically modified to enhance stability and binding affinity.  

### Method for Identification of a Radiotherapy Sensitizer  

The invention provides a method for identifying lncRNAs that sensitize malignant glioma cells to radiotherapy, comprising:  

(a) Providing a population of malignant glioma cells engineered to express a CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) system comprising dCas9-KRAB;  

(b) Introducing into the cells a library of guide RNAs targeting transcriptional start sites of a plurality of lncRNAs;  

(c) Exposing the cells to fractionated ionizing radiation comprising multiple doses of approximately 2 Gy delivered over time;  

(d) Culturing the cells for a period sufficient to allow proliferation differences to emerge between cells with different guide RNAs;  

(e) Isolating genomic DNA from the cells and quantifying guide RNA abundances by sequencing;  

(f) Identifying guide RNAs that are depleted or enriched following radiation exposure compared to control conditions;  

(g) Selecting lncRNAs targeted by depleted guide RNAs as potential radiation sensitizers.  

In preferred embodiments, the fractionated radiation comprises a total dose of approximately 8 Gy delivered in 2 Gy fractions every other day. The screening method may further comprise validating candidate lncRNAs in patient-derived glioma cultures and normal human astrocytes to confirm glioma-specific radiation sensitizing effects.  

### Methods of Diagnosis and Treatment  

The invention provides methods for diagnosing and treating malignant gliomas based on lncGRS-1 expression and targeting. Diagnostic methods comprise detecting lncGRS-1 expression levels in a biological sample from a subject, wherein elevated lncGRS-1 expression compared to normal brain tissue indicates the presence of malignant glioma and identifies the subject as likely to benefit from lncGRS-1-targeted therapy.  

Therapeutic methods comprise administering to a subject with malignant glioma a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that reduces lncGRS-1 expression or activity in combination with a therapeutically effective dose of ionizing radiation. The agent may be selected from:  

(a) An antisense oligonucleotide complementary to lncGRS-1 RNA;  
(b) A small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting lncGRS-1;  
(c) A CRISPRi system targeting the lncGRS-1 transcriptional start site;  
(d) A small molecule inhibitor of lncGRS-1 transcription or function.  

In preferred embodiments, the antisense oligonucleotide comprises locked nucleic acid (LNA) modifications and is administered directly into the cerebrospinal space. The radiation therapy is preferably fractionated radiation delivered in daily doses of approximately 2 Gy to a total dose between 50-60 Gy for glioblastoma or 54 Gy for DIPG.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising nucleic acid agents targeting lncGRS-1 formulated for therapeutic use. Preferred compositions comprise:  

(a) An antisense oligonucleotide of 15-25 nucleotides in length with at least 90% sequence complementarity to lncGRS-1 RNA;  
(b) A pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for central nervous system delivery;  
(c) Optionally, one or more additional chemotherapeutic or radiosensitizing agents.  

The antisense oligonucleotide may contain chemical modifications including phosphorothioate backbone linkages, 2'-O-methyl modifications, and locked nucleic acid (LNA) nucleotides to enhance stability and binding affinity. The pharmaceutical composition may be formulated for intrathecal, intraventricular, or convection-enhanced delivery to the brain.  

### Three-Dimensional Human Brain Organoid of Malignant Glioma  

The invention provides a three-dimensional mature brain organoid (MBO) model for studying malignant glioma and evaluating therapeutics, comprising:  

(a) Induced astrocytes derived from human pluripotent stem cells;  
(b) Optionally, induced neurons derived from human pluripotent stem cells;  
(c) Human malignant glioma cells growing invasively within the organoid matrix.  

The MBO recapitulates key features of the mature human brain microenvironment and demonstrates differential sensitivity to radiation and lncRNA-targeting therapies between tumor and normal brain cells. The model is particularly valuable for evaluating therapies targeting primate-specific lncRNAs like lncGRS-1 that lack rodent orthologs.  

Methods for generating the MBO comprise:  

(a) Differentiating human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) into mature astrocytes through prolonged culture in neural induction media;  
(b) Optionally, differentiating iPSCs into mature cortical neurons through inducible expression of neurogenin-2;  
(c) Combining the induced astrocytes and/or neurons in defined ratios in three-dimensional culture;  
(d) Seeding the organoid with patient-derived malignant glioma cells;  
(e) Maintaining the co-culture in serum-free neural media.  

### Systems  

The invention provides integrated systems for identifying and validating lncRNA radiation sensitizers, comprising:  

(a) A CRISPRi screening platform with guide RNA libraries targeting lncRNAs;  
(b) An in vitro radiation delivery system capable of fractionated dosing;  
(c) Patient-derived glioma cell cultures for hit validation;  
(d) Mature brain organoids for efficacy and toxicity testing;  
(e) Bioinformatics pipelines for analyzing screening data and prioritizing clinical candidates.  

The system enables rapid translation from lncRNA target discovery to preclinical validation, with particular utility for targets lacking rodent orthologs. The integrated platform supports evaluation of therapeutic index by simultaneously assessing tumor kill and normal tissue toxicity in human-derived models.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**CRISPRi Radiation Modifier Screen Identifies lncGRS-1 as a Top Radiosensitizer**  

A genome-scale CRISPRi screen was performed in U87 glioblastoma cells stably expressing dCas9-KRAB using a library of 56,890 guide RNAs targeting 5,689 lncRNA transcriptional start sites along with 1,202 non-targeting controls. Cells were exposed to fractionated radiation (8 Gy total in 2 Gy fractions every other day) over 12 days of culture. Guide RNA abundances were quantified by next-generation sequencing at baseline and endpoint.  

Analysis revealed 467 lncRNA targets whose knockdown modified glioma cell growth in the presence of radiation. Comparison with growth screens in non-irradiated cells identified 33 lncRNAs that specifically sensitized cells to radiation. Nine of these were expressed in both adult and pediatric glioma samples and were designated lncGRS-1 through lncGRS-9.  

lncGRS-1 (CTC-338M12.4) emerged as a top candidate based on its high sensitizer score (ratio of radiation screen score to growth screen score) and glioma-specific expression pattern. Nanopore long-read sequencing confirmed lncGRS-1 as a spliced, polyadenylated nuclear RNA of 687-1013 nucleotides located on chromosome 5q35.3.  

Validation studies demonstrated that CRISPRi-mediated lncGRS-1 knockdown reduced U87 cell proliferation by 48% without radiation and by an additional 37% relative to radiation alone. The combination of lncGRS-1 knockdown and radiation resulted in a 95% decrease in proliferation, significantly greater than the predicted 83% decrease from additive effects (p=0.0052), demonstrating synergistic interaction.  

Similar effects were observed in patient-derived GBM and DIPG cultures, with lncGRS-1 knockdown inhibiting proliferation by 80% in multiple primary cell lines. Notably, lncGRS-1 knockdown did not affect viability or proliferation of normal human astrocytes or other non-glioma cell types, demonstrating tumor-specific essentiality.
