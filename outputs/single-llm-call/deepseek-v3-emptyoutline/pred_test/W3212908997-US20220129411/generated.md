# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Quantum computation represents one of the most promising frontiers in computational technology, offering potential exponential speedups for certain classes of problems compared to classical computers. A fundamental challenge in realizing practical quantum computation lies in the efficient implementation of quantum circuits that perform desired unitary operations. Among these operations, the synthesis and optimization of Clifford group circuits present particular importance due to their wide-ranging applications in quantum error correction, randomized benchmarking, quantum state distillation, and quantum simulation algorithms.

Current approaches to quantum circuit synthesis face significant limitations. While asymptotically optimal implementations exist for n-qubit Clifford circuits using Θ(n²/log(n)) gates, these methods often produce suboptimal results for practical circuit sizes. Existing techniques for exactly optimal Clifford circuit synthesis become prohibitively expensive beyond six qubits, creating a substantial gap between theoretical possibilities and practical implementations. Furthermore, conventional optimization methods fail to fully exploit the unique algebraic properties of Clifford circuits, resulting in inefficient gate counts that increase error rates and computational overhead.

The limitations of current quantum circuit synthesis methods become particularly apparent in physical implementations where entangling gates (such as CNOT operations) carry significantly higher costs than single-qubit gates in terms of both time and error rates. Existing synthesis techniques often produce circuits with excessive entangling gate counts, directly impacting the feasibility and fidelity of quantum computations. This problem becomes increasingly acute as quantum systems scale to larger numbers of qubits, where optimal gate sequences become essential for maintaining computational integrity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel systems and methods for the synthesis and optimization of Clifford quantum circuits that overcome the limitations of existing approaches. The disclosed technology introduces two primary innovations: a Clifford-specific extension of template matching techniques and a novel symbolic peephole optimization algorithm. These methods synergistically combine to produce quantum circuits with dramatically reduced gate counts, particularly focusing on minimizing costly entangling operations.

The template matching extension employs a three-stage approach that partitions quantum circuits into distinct "compute," "swap," and "Pauli" stages. This partitioning enables specialized optimization of each circuit component according to its unique characteristics. The compute stage undergoes template-based simplification using a library of identity-implementing gate sequences. The swap stage benefits from gate merging techniques that effectively reduce swap operations to single entangling gate equivalents. The Pauli stage leverages the algebraic properties of Clifford circuits to push Pauli gates to circuit boundaries where they can be eliminated or simplified.

The symbolic peephole optimization represents a significant advancement over conventional peephole methods by introducing symbolic Pauli gates (SPGs) that maintain entanglement information while enabling local optimization. This technique considers subcircuits spanning small qubit subsets while preserving their entanglement with the remaining circuit through symbolic variables. Dynamic programming techniques then optimize these subcircuits while accounting for the symbolic constraints, producing globally optimal solutions for local circuit segments.

The invention further includes a bidirectional greedy compiler that recursively synthesizes circuits by simultaneously applying transformations to both input and output sides of the unitary operation. This approach significantly outperforms unidirectional compilation methods by minimizing the combined gate count of disentangling operations. The compiler employs a weight-restricted search over Pauli operators to maintain computational tractability while achieving near-optimal results.

Experimental results demonstrate the exceptional performance of the disclosed methods. For six-qubit Clifford circuits with known optimal CNOT counts, the techniques recover optimal implementations for 97.9% of test cases. In larger-scale applications involving Hamiltonian evolution circuits with up to 64 qubits, the methods achieve an average 64.7% reduction in CNOT gate counts compared to canonical forms. These advancements enable practical implementation of quantum algorithms that were previously infeasible due to gate count limitations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The detailed description begins with the mathematical foundations of Clifford circuits and their representation. An n-qubit Clifford circuit consists of quantum gates selected from the set comprising Hadamard (H), Phase (S), and controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, along with Pauli X, Y, and Z gates. These circuits generate the Clifford group Cₙ, a finite subgroup of unitary transformations characterized by their property of mapping Pauli operators to other Pauli operators under conjugation. This algebraic structure enables specialized optimization techniques that form the core of the present invention.

The template matching extension operates through a three-phase process. First, the input circuit undergoes partitioning into compute, swap, and Pauli stages by systematically pushing swap and Pauli gates toward the circuit output using conjugation rules. These rules specify how each gate type transforms when commuted through others, allowing their progressive migration to the circuit periphery. The compute stage, now containing only H, S, CNOT, and CZ gates, undergoes template-based optimization using a predefined library of identity-implementing gate sequences. Each template application identifies matching subsequences in the target circuit and replaces them with more efficient equivalents.

The swap stage optimization exploits the observation that swap gates can be implemented at the effective cost of one entangling gate when properly aligned with existing CNOT or CZ operations. The method scans the circuit for such alignment opportunities and performs gate merging transformations that reduce overall entangling gate counts. The Pauli stage benefits from algebraic simplifications that combine or eliminate redundant Pauli operations at circuit boundaries.

The symbolic peephole optimization introduces several technical innovations. For a selected subset of qubits A, the method represents their entanglement with remaining qubits B through symbolic variables vᵢ ∈ {0,1}. These variables control symbolic Pauli gates (SPGs) that replace actual entangling operations during local optimization. The technique constructs a symbolic Clifford circuit U_A(v) that preserves the mathematical relationship between subsets A and B while enabling independent optimization of A's subcircuit.

The optimization of U_A(v) employs dynamic programming to minimize a cost function that accounts for both CNOT gates and SPGs. The algorithm respects temporal ordering constraints among SPGs and preserves global phase relationships through careful tracking of phase factors. By restricting the optimization to small qubit subsets (typically 2-3 qubits) and leveraging precomputed lookup tables of optimal circuits, the method achieves efficient runtime performance while producing high-quality results.

The bidirectional greedy compiler implements a recursive synthesis strategy that simultaneously reduces circuit complexity from both input and output sides. At each recursion step, the algorithm selects pairs of anti-commuting Pauli operators (P,P') that minimize the combined cost of left and right disentangling operations. The search space for these operator pairs is restricted to weight-2 Pauli operators to maintain computational feasibility. The compiler constructs the final circuit by composing these progressively simpler intermediate unitaries, resulting in implementations with substantially reduced gate counts compared to unidirectional approaches.

The invention further includes specialized techniques for handling practical implementation considerations. Circuit resynthesis with randomized variations improves optimization results through parallel exploration of alternative compilation paths. Adaptive time limits balance solution quality against computational resources, particularly important for larger circuits. The methods also accommodate various physical connectivity constraints through straightforward modifications to the optimization criteria.

Experimental validation demonstrates the invention's effectiveness across multiple dimensions. For six-qubit Clifford unitaries with known optimal CNOT counts between 5-15, the methods recover optimal implementations for 97.9% of test cases, with remaining instances deviating by at most one CNOT gate. In larger-scale applications modeling Hamiltonian evolution on graphs with up to 64 qubits, the techniques achieve average CNOT count reductions of 64.7% compared to canonical forms. These improvements directly translate to enhanced feasibility and fidelity of quantum computations in practical settings.

The disclosed methods represent significant advances over prior art by combining deep theoretical insights with practical engineering considerations. The template matching extensions specifically adapted for Clifford circuits outperform generic optimization techniques by exploiting algebraic properties unique to the Clifford group. The symbolic peephole optimization overcomes fundamental limitations of conventional peephole methods through its innovative use of symbolic variables to maintain entanglement information. Together with the bidirectional compilation strategy, these techniques enable efficient quantum circuit implementations that push the boundaries of feasible quantum computation.
