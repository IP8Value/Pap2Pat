# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of antiviral therapeutics and more specifically to compositions and methods for inhibiting avian influenza virus (AIV) replication using RNA interference (RNAi) technology delivered via a novel transkingdom RNAi (tkRNAi) platform. Avian influenza outbreaks in poultry populations have caused significant economic losses worldwide due to mass culling of infected birds and trade restrictions. Current vaccination strategies for poultry face numerous limitations including the requirement for subtype-specific vaccines, delayed onset of immunity, and impractical administration methods for large flocks. Conventional vaccines must be administered via injection, making mass immunization logistically challenging. Furthermore, vaccinated birds develop antibodies that interfere with disease surveillance testing, creating trade barriers. 

Existing RNAi-based antiviral approaches have demonstrated efficacy against influenza viruses in laboratory settings but face substantial delivery challenges that limit clinical application. Synthetic siRNA carriers exhibit low delivery efficiency and require high doses that can be toxic. Viral vectors for shRNA delivery raise safety concerns including hepatotoxicity and tumorigenesis. There remains an unmet need for an effective, scalable, and safe antiviral approach that can provide rapid protection against diverse AIV strains without the limitations of current vaccination methods. The present invention addresses these needs through development of a novel tkRNAi delivery system that enables targeted delivery of antiviral shRNAs to avian mucosal epithelial cells using engineered nonpathogenic bacteria.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting avian influenza virus replication in avian subjects using a transkingdom RNA interference (tkRNAi) delivery platform. The invention utilizes nonpathogenic Escherichia coli bacteria engineered to deliver short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting conserved regions of the AIV nucleoprotein (NP) and polymerase acidic (PA) genes. The bacterial vectors are modified to express invasin protein for epithelial cell targeting and listeriolysin O for endosomal escape. 

Key aspects of the invention include: (1) shRNA constructs targeting highly conserved sequences within the AIV NP and PA genes that demonstrate efficacy across multiple AIV subtypes; (2) a diaminopimelic acid (Dap) auxotrophic E. coli delivery vector containing genes for invasin (inv) and listeriolysin O (hlyA) to enable mucosal epithelial cell targeting and cytoplasmic delivery of shRNAs; (3) methods for inhibiting AIV replication in avian epithelial cells through administration of the anti-AIV vectors; and (4) prophylactic treatment regimens for poultry populations using intranasal, oral, or aerosolized delivery of the anti-AIV vectors.

The invention provides significant advantages over existing AIV control methods including: rapid onset of antiviral activity, broad protection against multiple AIV subtypes, non-invasive administration suitable for mass treatment, inability to integrate into host genome, and absence of antibody production that interferes with disease surveillance. The tkRNAi platform has demonstrated safety in clinical applications and enables cost-effective large-scale production for stockpiling against outbreaks.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Design of siRNAs and Use of Viral Specific siRNAs to Inhibit Avian Influenza in an Avian Tissue Model

The invention utilizes short interfering RNA (siRNA) sequences targeting highly conserved regions of the avian influenza virus nucleoprotein (NP) and polymerase acidic (PA) genes. The NP-targeting siRNA comprises the sense strand sequence 5'-GGA TCT TAT TTC TTC GGA G-3' (SEQ ID NO:1) corresponding to nucleotides 1453-1471 of the A/chicken/Texas/473-2/2010(H6N2) NP gene (GenBank KM244053) and nucleotides 1486-1504 of the A/turkey/Colorado/235497/2003(H8N4) NP gene (GenBank GU051910). The PA-targeting siRNA comprises the sense strand sequence 5'-GCA ATT GAG GAG TGC CTG A-3' (SEQ ID NO:2) corresponding to nucleotides 2065-2083 of the H6N2 PA gene (GenBank KM244051) and nucleotides 2077-2095 of the H8N4 PA gene (GenBank GU051912).

These siRNA sequences were selected based on several criteria: (1) 100% conservation across multiple AIV subtypes including H5, H7, and H9 highly pathogenic strains; (2) absence of significant sequence complementarity to known chicken genes as verified by BLAST analysis; (3) demonstrated antiviral efficacy in previous studies using conventional transfection methods; and (4) targeting of essential viral genes required for replication. The siRNA sequences were incorporated into short hairpin RNA (shRNA) constructs expressed from the pmbv43 plasmid vector.

The shRNA expression cassettes were designed with the following structure: BamHI restriction site-sense sequence-hairpin loop (5'-TTC AAG AGA-3')-antisense sequence-TTTTTTTTTT-SalI restriction site. This structure allows for efficient processing of the shRNA into functional siRNA by the cellular RNAi machinery. The NP and PA shRNA constructs were cloned into the pmbv43 plasmid which additionally contains genes encoding invasin (inv) and listeriolysin O (hlyA) for targeted delivery to mucosal epithelial cells.

### Inhibiting Avian Influenza Replication in a Chicken Cell Model Using a Unique RNAi Delivery Technology

The invention employs a transkingdom RNA interference (tkRNAi) delivery system comprising nonpathogenic, diaminopimelic acid (Dap) auxotrophic Escherichia coli bacteria engineered to deliver antiviral shRNAs to avian epithelial cells. The delivery platform utilizes three key components: (1) the pmbv43 plasmid encoding shRNAs under constitutive expression; (2) the invasin (inv) gene product that mediates bacterial uptake through interaction with β(1) integrin receptors on epithelial cells; and (3) the listeriolysin O (hlyA) gene product that facilitates endosomal escape of shRNAs into the cytoplasm.

The anti-AIV vectors were evaluated in chicken hepatocellular carcinoma epithelial (LMH) cells as an avian tissue model. LMH cells express β(1) integrin receptors as confirmed by RT-PCR analysis, enabling efficient vector uptake. Optimal vector concentration was determined to be 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL, which provided effective delivery without cytotoxicity as assessed by crystal violet staining and microscopic examination. Intracellular uptake of the vectors was verified using fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry of LMH cells treated with RFP-labeled anti-AIV vectors, demonstrating 74-76% invasion efficiency.

For antiviral testing, LMH cells were treated with anti-AIV vectors targeting NP (anti-AIV/NP), PA (anti-AIV/PA), both genes (anti-AIV/cocktail), or a scrambled control (anti-AIV/scramble) at 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL. Twenty-four hours post-vector treatment, cells were infected with AIV strains H6N2 or H8N4 at MOI 0.01. Viral titers in culture supernatants at 48 hours post-infection were quantified by TCID50 assay on MDCK cells. The anti-AIV/cocktail vector produced the most potent antiviral effect, reducing H8N4 titers by 4.0 log10 (10,000-fold) and H6N2 titers by 1.9 log10 (79.4-fold) compared to untreated controls. Individual anti-AIV/NP and anti-AIV/PA vectors also showed significant antiviral activity, while the scramble control demonstrated more modest reductions (1.3-2.1 log10), likely due to nonspecific immune stimulation by bacterial components.

### Prophylactic Treatment of Chicken Populations with Anti-AIV Vectors

The invention provides methods for prophylactic treatment of poultry populations against avian influenza virus infection. The anti-AIV vectors can be administered to birds via multiple routes including intranasal, oral, or aerosol delivery. For mass administration to flocks, aerosolization represents the preferred embodiment due to its suitability for large-scale application and targeted delivery to respiratory mucosa where AIV infection initiates.

The treatment regimen involves administration of the anti-AIV vectors at a dose range of 10^5-10^8 CFU per bird, with optimal dosing determined to be approximately 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL based on in vitro efficacy and safety data. The vectors can be administered as a single dose prior to potential AIV exposure or as multiple doses during outbreak situations. The anti-AIV/cocktail vector targeting both NP and PA genes provides the most comprehensive protection and represents the preferred embodiment for field use.

The prophylactic method offers several advantages over conventional vaccination: (1) rapid onset of antiviral activity within 24 hours; (2) broad protection against diverse AIV subtypes due to targeting of conserved viral sequences; (3) no interference with AIV surveillance testing since the method does not induce antibody production; (4) suitability for mass administration via non-invasive routes; and (5) inability of the vectors to integrate into host genome or cause insertional mutagenesis.

For large-scale production, the anti-AIV vectors can be cultured in Brain Heart Infusion broth containing kanamycin (25 μg/mL) and diaminopimelic acid (50 μg/mL). The bacterial cultures are grown to OD600 = 1.0, concentrated, and formulated in phosphate-buffered saline with 20% glycerol for long-term storage at -80°C. Prior to administration, vectors are thawed and resuspended in appropriate delivery vehicles such as sterile PBS for intranasal administration or nebulization solutions for aerosol delivery.

## GLOSSARY OF CLAIM TERMS

Anti-AIV vector: A nonpathogenic, diaminopimelic acid (Dap) auxotrophic Escherichia coli bacterium engineered to deliver short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting avian influenza virus genes, comprising: (a) a plasmid encoding shRNAs under constitutive expression; (b) the invasin (inv) gene for epithelial cell targeting; and (c) the listeriolysin O (hlyA) gene for endosomal escape.

Transkingdom RNA interference (tkRNAi): An RNAi delivery platform utilizing engineered bacteria to produce and deliver shRNAs to target cells, comprising bacterial uptake via invasin-β(1) integrin interaction, endosomal escape mediated by listeriolysin O, and cytoplasmic release of shRNAs for processing into functional siRNAs.

shRNA: Short hairpin RNA comprising a sense strand, loop sequence, and antisense strand that is processed into siRNA by the cellular RNAi machinery, designed to target specific mRNA sequences for degradation.

Invasin (inv): A bacterial surface protein encoded by the inv gene that mediates binding to β(1) integrin receptors on eukaryotic cells, facilitating receptor-mediated endocytosis of the bacterial vector.

Listeriolysin O (hlyA): A pore-forming toxin encoded by the hlyA gene that facilitates rupture of endosomal membranes following bacterial uptake, enabling release of shRNAs into the host cell cytoplasm.

Avian influenza virus (AIV): Influenza A viruses that primarily infect birds, including multiple subtypes (e.g., H5N1, H7N9, H9N2) that can cause significant poultry losses and pose zoonotic transmission risks.

Nucleoprotein (NP): An essential structural protein of influenza viruses that encapsidates viral RNA and is required for viral transcription and replication, encoded by a highly conserved gene sequence.

Polymerase acidic (PA): A component of the influenza virus RNA-dependent RNA polymerase complex that is essential for viral replication, encoded by a conserved viral gene.
