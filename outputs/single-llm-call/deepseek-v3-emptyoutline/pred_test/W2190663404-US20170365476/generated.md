# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Silicon-based optoelectronic devices traditionally suffer from limited absorption of sub-bandgap photon energies due to silicon's intrinsic bandgap properties. While various methods exist to enhance silicon's optical absorption through hyperdoping (supersaturation with dopants) and surface texturing (light-trapping structures), these approaches have faced significant limitations. Conventional hyperdoping techniques often produce materials with poor crystallinity or unstable dopant configurations that degrade under thermal processing. Similarly, surface texturing methods frequently create microstructures that interfere with electrical properties or fail to maintain structural integrity during subsequent processing steps.  

Prior attempts to combine hyperdoping with surface texturing have resulted in materials where either the optical properties degrade during crystallization or the electrical properties become compromised. Thermal annealing processes, while effective for improving crystallinity and electrical rectification, typically deactivate the sub-bandgap optical absorption properties that make hyperdoped materials valuable for infrared applications. There exists a critical need for processing methods that can simultaneously achieve high crystallinity, stable sub-bandgap optical absorption, and proper electrical functionality in hyperdoped silicon materials.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a novel method for producing hyperdoped black silicon with superior optoelectronic properties through a combination of nanosecond laser annealing and thermal processing. The method involves first creating hyperdoped black silicon through femtosecond laser irradiation in the presence of dopant species, followed by precise nanosecond laser annealing that simultaneously:  

1. Crystallizes the surface layer while maintaining light-trapping surface morphology  
2. Preserves or reactivates sub-bandgap optical absorption properties  
3. Maintains electrical rectification properties at homojunctions  

The invention further describes sequential processing techniques where thermal annealing and nanosecond laser annealing are combined to produce materials with optimized combinations of crystallinity, optical absorption, and electrical properties. The nanosecond laser annealing parameters (including fluence, wavelength, and pulse duration) are carefully controlled to achieve surface melting depths that optimize dopant configuration while preserving the beneficial microstructure.  

This processing approach overcomes fundamental limitations in existing hyperdoped silicon technologies by providing independent control over crystallinity, dopant activation, and surface morphology. The resulting materials exhibit unprecedented combinations of sub-bandgap optical absorption (>80% absorptance), high crystallinity (comparable to single-crystal silicon), and electrical rectification (rectification ratios >50), making them ideal for infrared photodetectors and intermediate band photovoltaic devices.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides a comprehensive method for fabricating hyperdoped black silicon with optimized optoelectronic properties through controlled laser and thermal processing. The detailed process flow comprises several key steps:  

The initial hyperdoped black silicon is fabricated by irradiating a crystalline silicon substrate with femtosecond laser pulses in the presence of dopant species. Suitable dopants include sulfur, selenium, tellurium, or other chalcogens delivered via gas phase (e.g., SF6) or thin film precursors. The femtosecond laser parameters (typically 100-500 fs pulse duration, 800 nm wavelength, 1-10 kHz repetition rate, and 1-10 kJ/m² fluence) are selected to simultaneously induce hyperdoping through non-equilibrium incorporation of dopants and create light-trapping surface structures through self-organized surface texturing.  

Following initial fabrication, the hyperdoped black silicon undergoes nanosecond laser annealing using carefully controlled parameters. The nanosecond laser system (operating at 193-355 nm wavelength, 4-25 ns pulse duration) delivers pulses with fluences between 0.5-2.5 J/cm², sufficient to melt and recrystallize surface layers while preserving the underlying microstructure. The nanosecond laser annealing achieves several critical transformations:  

First, it converts amorphous and pressure-induced crystalline phases in the surface layer to conventional crystalline silicon (Si-I) while maintaining the hyperdoped state. This is evidenced by Raman spectroscopy showing elimination of amorphous silicon signatures (480 cm⁻¹ peak) and pressure-induced phase peaks while retaining the crystalline silicon peak at 520 cm⁻¹.  

Second, the process preserves the light-trapping surface morphology by limiting melting depths to approximately 100 nm, leaving micron-scale surface structures intact. This is confirmed by scanning electron microscopy showing preservation of conical surface features while demonstrating nanometer-scale smoothing of surface roughness.  

Third, the nanosecond laser annealing maintains or reactivates sub-bandgap optical absorption properties, with absorptance measurements showing >80% absorption across 1.2-4.0 μm wavelengths. This occurs through non-equilibrium dopant incorporation during rapid resolidification, placing dopant atoms in optically active lattice sites.  

For applications requiring improved electrical properties, the invention incorporates optional thermal annealing steps (typically 500-700°C in forming gas) either before or after nanosecond laser annealing. When performed before electrode deposition, thermal annealing improves homojunction rectification but deactivates sub-bandgap absorption. Subsequent nanosecond laser annealing then reactivates the optical properties while maintaining crystallinity.  

The invention further describes electrode integration methods using rapid thermal annealing (200-500°C for 30-60 seconds) to form ohmic contacts, followed by additional nanosecond laser annealing to reactivate any optical absorption losses from contact formation. Metal electrode stacks (e.g., Ti/Ni/Ag) are deposited with thicknesses optimized to withstand subsequent laser processing while maintaining electrical functionality.  

### EXEMPLIFICATION  

Several specific embodiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the invention:  

In a first embodiment, hyperdoped black silicon fabricated with 8 kJ/m² femtosecond laser pulses (producing 10 μm tall conical structures) was treated with 2.2 J/cm² nanosecond laser pulses at 355 nm wavelength. This treatment:  
- Reduced amorphous silicon content from >50% to <5% as measured by Raman spectroscopy  
- Maintained conical surface structures with <10% change in geometrical dimensions  
- Preserved sub-bandgap absorptance at >90% while improving above-bandgap absorptance to >95%  
- Produced single-crystal regions as confirmed by selected area electron diffraction  

A second embodiment demonstrated reactivation of deactivated material. Hyperdoped black silicon that had been thermally annealed (700°C for 30 minutes), reducing its sub-bandgap absorptance from 80% to 20%, was treated with 1.1 J/cm² nanosecond laser pulses. This treatment:  
- Restored sub-bandgap absorptance to 70-80%  
- Maintained high crystallinity (amorphous content <5%)  
- Produced a characteristic sub-bandgap absorption peak at 2.0±0.8 μm wavelength  

A third embodiment showed integration with electrical contacts. After electrode deposition and rapid thermal annealing (500°C for 30 seconds), which reduced sub-bandgap absorptance to 60%, additional nanosecond laser annealing (1.1 J/cm²) restored absorptance to 70-80% while maintaining:  
- Rectification ratios >50 at ±10V bias  
- Turn-on voltages of 0.5-1.5V  
- Ohmic contact behavior with contact resistances <10 Ω·cm²  

## CONCLUSION  

The present invention provides a significant advance in hyperdoped silicon technology by enabling independent control of crystallinity, optical absorption, and electrical properties through innovative nanosecond laser annealing techniques. The method overcomes fundamental limitations in existing approaches by:  

1. Enabling high crystallinity while maintaining light-trapping surface structures  
2. Preserving or reactivating sub-bandgap optical absorption properties after thermal processing  
3. Maintaining electrical rectification properties critical for device functionality  

The sequential combination of thermal and nanosecond laser annealing allows optimization of material properties for specific applications, whether prioritizing optical performance, electrical characteristics, or balanced properties. The techniques are compatible with standard semiconductor processing and can be implemented with conventional laser systems.  

The resulting hyperdoped black silicon materials exhibit unprecedented combinations of properties that make them ideal for infrared photodetectors, intermediate band photovoltaics, and other optoelectronic applications requiring broadband absorption. The invention further provides a general framework for processing non-equilibrium materials where competing property requirements have previously limited practical applications.
