# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of neurodegenerative diseases, particularly Alzheimer's disease (AD). More specifically, the invention concerns methods and compositions for reducing amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptide production through modulation of transient receptor potential canonical 6 (TRPC6) protein interactions with amyloid precursor protein (APP). The invention provides novel therapeutic strategies targeting the specific interaction between TRPC6 and APP to selectively inhibit γ-secretase cleavage of APP while avoiding interference with other γ-secretase substrates.  

## BACKGROUND  

Alzheimer's disease is characterized by the accumulation of amyloid-beta peptides, particularly Aβ40 and Aβ42, which form neurotoxic plaques in the brain. These peptides are generated through sequential proteolytic cleavage of amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β-secretase and γ-secretase. Current therapeutic approaches targeting γ-secretase have faced significant challenges due to the enzyme's broad substrate specificity, particularly its essential role in Notch signaling. Non-selective γ-secretase inhibitors cause severe side effects by interfering with Notch-dependent processes.  

There remains an unmet need for therapeutic strategies that can specifically reduce Aβ production without affecting other γ-secretase substrates. The present invention addresses this need by identifying TRPC6 as a novel endogenous regulator of γ-secretase activity that specifically targets APP cleavage. TRPC6 is shown to interact directly with APP to prevent its cleavage by γ-secretase, while having no effect on γ-secretase processing of other substrates such as Notch. This discovery enables the development of targeted therapies that mimic TRPC6's selective inhibitory effect on Aβ production.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides methods and compositions for reducing amyloid-beta peptide production through modulation of TRPC6-APP interactions. Key aspects include:  

1. The discovery that TRPC6 specifically interacts with APP to inhibit its cleavage by γ-secretase, while having no effect on γ-secretase processing of other substrates such as Notch.  

2. The identification that this inhibitory effect is mediated through the second transmembrane (TM2) domain of TRPC6 (amino acids 487-508), independent of TRPC6's channel activity.  

3. The development of cell-permeable peptides (e.g., TAT-TM2) containing the TRPC6 TM2 domain that effectively reduce Aβ production in vitro and in vivo.  

4. Methods for treating or preventing Alzheimer's disease by administering compounds that enhance TRPC6-APP interactions or mimic the inhibitory effects of TRPC6's TM2 domain on γ-secretase cleavage of APP.  

5. Pharmaceutical compositions containing TRPC6-derived peptides or small molecules that specifically target the TRPC6-APP interaction interface.  

The invention represents a significant advance over existing approaches by providing a mechanism to selectively reduce Aβ production without broadly inhibiting γ-secretase activity or interfering with essential Notch signaling pathways.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that TRPC6 protein specifically interacts with amyloid precursor protein (APP) to inhibit its cleavage by γ-secretase, thereby reducing production of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides. This effect is mediated through the second transmembrane (TM2) domain of TRPC6 and occurs independently of TRPC6's ion channel activity.  

TRPC6 was found to co-immunoprecipitate with APP but not with other γ-secretase components or substrates such as presenilin 1 (PS1) or Notch. This selective interaction prevents APP from engaging with the γ-secretase complex, specifically reducing Aβ production without affecting γ-secretase processing of other substrates. The inhibitory effect was demonstrated in multiple experimental systems including primary neurons, HEK293 cells expressing APP, and transgenic mouse models of Alzheimer's disease.  

The TM2 domain of TRPC6 (amino acids 487-508) was identified as essential for both the interaction with APP and the inhibition of Aβ production. Mutations in this domain abolished TRPC6's ability to reduce Aβ levels. A cell-permeable peptide (TAT-TM2) containing this domain was shown to effectively reduce Aβ production in cell cultures and in APP/PS1 transgenic mice when administered systemically.  

The invention provides several therapeutic approaches:  
1) Enhancing endogenous TRPC6 expression or activity in neurons  
2) Administering TRPC6-derived peptides containing the TM2 domain  
3) Developing small molecule mimetics of the TRPC6 TM2 domain  
4) Identifying compounds that stabilize the TRPC6-APP interaction  

These approaches specifically target the pathogenic production of Aβ while avoiding the side effects associated with broad γ-secretase inhibition. The invention also provides methods for screening compounds that modulate TRPC6-APP interactions and their effects on Aβ production.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**TRPC6 Reduces Aβ Levels Independent of Channel Activity**  

In primary cultured rat cortical neurons, downregulation of TRPC6 (but not other calcium channels including TRPC5, Cav1.2, Cav3.1 or Cav3.3) significantly increased both Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels as measured by ELISA. Treatment with TRPC channel activators (OAG) or blockers (SKF96365) did not affect Aβ levels, indicating the effect is independent of TRPC6's channel activity.  

In HEK293 cells stably expressing APP (HEK293APP), TRPC6 expression markedly reduced levels of Aβ40, Aβ42, Aβ38 and Aβ1-x in the culture medium. TRPC5 expression had no effect. The dominant-negative form of TRPC6 (DN-TRPC6), which lacks channel activity, still reduced Aβ levels, further confirming the channel-independent mechanism.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

**TRPC6 Specifically Inhibits γ-Secretase Cleavage of APP**  

TRPC6 expression did not affect:  
- APP levels or processing by α- or β-secretase  
- Expression or activity of ADAM10 or BACE1  
- Aβ degradation or clearance mechanisms  
- γ-secretase component levels (PS1, PS2, nicastrin, PEN2)  
- γ-secretase activity toward fluorogenic substrates  

However, TRPC6 significantly reduced:  
- Aβ production from C99 (the immediate γ-secretase substrate)  
- Generation of APP intracellular domain (AICD)  
Without affecting γ-secretase cleavage of:  
- Notch (NICD production)  
- E-cadherin or N-cadherin  

### EXAMPLE 3  

**TRPC6 Selectively Interacts with APP**  

Co-immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrated:  
- TRPC6 forms complexes with APP in brain lysates and cultured cells  
- TRPC6 does not interact with PS1, Notch or APLP2  
- The interaction occurs with wild-type APP and familial AD mutants (I716F, V717F)  
- TRPC6 prevents APP (C99) from interacting with PS1  

Immunofluorescence and fractionation studies showed co-localization of TRPC6 and APP in cellular organelles.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

**The TM2 Domain Mediates TRPC6's Effects on Aβ**  

Systematic domain analysis revealed:  
- The 437-508 aa region of TRPC6 is required for Aβ reduction  
- The second transmembrane (TM2) domain (487-508 aa) is essential  
- TM2 mutations abolish TRPC6's ability to:  
  - Reduce Aβ levels  
  - Interact with APP  

A cell-permeable TAT-TM2 peptide (containing 487-508 aa):  
- Reduced Aβ levels in HEK293APP cells and primary neurons  
- Inhibited Aβ production in an in vitro γ-secretase assay  
- Did not affect Notch cleavage  
- Bound directly to APP  

### EXAMPLE 5  

**TAT-TM2 Reduces Aβ In Vivo**  

Intraperitoneal injection of TAT-TM2 in APP/PS1 mice:  
- Significantly reduced brain Aβ levels within 3 hours  
- Did not produce observable toxicity  

The C-terminal 11 aa of TM2 were critical for activity, with point mutations abolishing the effect.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

**TRPC6 Reduces Aβ and Improves Cognition in AD Mice**  

APP/PS1/TRPC6 transgenic mice showed:  
- Reduced amyloid plaque load compared to APP/PS1 mice  
- Lower levels of soluble and insoluble Aβ  
- Prevention of spine density loss in hippocampal neurons  
- Improved performance in Morris water maze tests  

These effects occurred without changes in γ-secretase component levels or activity toward other substrates.
