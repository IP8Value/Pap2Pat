# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

The global demand for polymers continues to rise, with plastics accounting for over 7% of annual fossil fuel consumption. Despite increasing environmental concerns, renewable polymers constitute less than 5% of the market due to perceived high costs and inferior performance compared to petroleum-based counterparts. Existing renewable polymer solutions often require complex synthesis routes, extreme conditions, or specialized catalysts, limiting their scalability and commercial viability.  

Terpenes, such as α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone, represent promising renewable feedstocks due to their natural abundance and structural versatility. However, conventional polymerization methods for terpenes face significant challenges, including low monomer incorporation, crosslinking issues, and the need for costly or environmentally harmful reagents. Prior attempts to functionalize terpenes into polymerizable monomers have relied on multi-step syntheses, toxic reagents, or non-scalable conditions, hindering widespread adoption.  

There remains an unmet need for a scalable, sustainable, and efficient method to convert terpenes into high-performance polymers suitable for industrial applications, including coatings, adhesives, and structural materials.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses novel terpene-derived acrylate and methacrylate monomers and methods for their synthesis and polymerization. The monomers are prepared via a two-step hydroboration-oxidation/esterification process or a one-pot catalytic allylic oxidation, enabling high yields and scalability. These monomers undergo facile free-radical polymerization to form polymers with tunable properties, including high glass transition temperatures (Tg) up to 142°C, comparable to petroleum-based polycarbonates.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Utilization of abundant, renewable terpene feedstocks.  
- Green synthetic routes avoiding toxic reagents (e.g., replacing acryloyl chloride with acrylic acid and T3P® catalyst).  
- High monomer conversion and controllable molecular weights using chain transfer agents (e.g., dodecanethiol).  
- Tailorable polymer properties (Tg, solubility, crosslinking) for diverse applications.  
- Demonstrated utility in high-performance coatings, including powder coatings with excellent gloss, hardness, and chemical resistance.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers derived from α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene, and carvone. Monomers are synthesized via:  
1. **Hydroboration-Oxidation**: Terpenes are converted to alcohols using hydroboration (e.g., BH3·THF) followed by oxidative workup (H2O2/NaOH).  
2. **Esterification**: The resulting terpene alcohols are esterified with acrylic or methacrylic acid using propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®) as a green coupling agent, yielding acrylate/methacrylate monomers.  

Alternatively, a **one-pot catalytic method** is employed for β-pinene, using Pd(OAc)2/benzoquinone to directly couple methacrylic acid at allylic positions (50°C, air atmosphere). This route achieves 82% yield with minimal byproducts.  

### Terpinolene and Gamma-Terpinene as Chain Transfer Agents  

The invention further discloses the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as renewable chain transfer agents (CTAs) to control polymer molecular weight during free-radical polymerization. These terpenes act as efficient CTAs, replacing conventional toxic thiols (e.g., dodecanethiol). For example, polymerization of α-pinene methacrylate with 0.5–5% terpinolene yields polymers with Mn = 4,800–23,600 g/mol and Tg = 85–142°C, demonstrating precise molecular weight control.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Synthesis of α-Pinene Methacrylate (1e)**:  
1. α-Pinene (50 g) was hydroborated with BH3·THF (0.5 equiv, 0°C, 2 h), followed by oxidation with H2O2/NaOH to yield α-pinene alcohol (1a, 92% yield).  
2. 1a was esterified with methacrylic acid (1.2 equiv) using T3P® (50% in EtOAc, 1.1 equiv) in CH2Cl2 (25°C, 12 h). The crude product was purified by column chromatography to afford 1e (85% yield).  

**Polymerization**: 1e (10 g), AIBN (1 mol%), and terpinolene (1 wt%) in toluene (50% w/v) were heated at 65°C for 24 h. The resulting polymer (1e-P) had Mn = 18,300 g/mol and Tg = 138°C (DSC).  

### Example 2  
**One-Pot Catalytic Synthesis of β-Pinene Methacrylate (5/6)**:  
β-Pinene (20 g), methacrylic acid (2 equiv), Pd(OAc)2 (2 mol%), and benzoquinone (2 equiv) in DMSO were stirred at 50°C under air for 8 h. The product mixture (5:6 = 10:1) was isolated in 82% yield by flash chromatography.  

**Crosslinked Coating Formulation**:  
A powder coating was prepared by blending 5/6 (95 wt%), trimethylolpropane tris(3-mercaptopropionate) (5 wt%), and flow additives. The mixture was extruded, ground, and electrostatically sprayed onto aluminum panels. UV curing (120°C, 2 min) yielded a glossy, crosslinked film with pencil hardness = 2B and König hardness = 185 s.  

## CONCLUSIONS  

The invention enables the scalable production of high-performance renewable polymers from terpenes. Key innovations include:  
- Green monomer synthesis routes with high atom economy.  
- Tunable polymer properties (e.g., Tg, crosslinking) for coatings, adhesives, and thermoplastics.  
- Demonstrated industrial applicability, such as powder coatings with superior hardness and gloss.  

This technology represents a significant advance toward sustainable polymers without compromising performance or cost.
