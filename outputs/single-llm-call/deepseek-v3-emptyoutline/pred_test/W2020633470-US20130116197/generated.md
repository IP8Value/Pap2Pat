# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) that bind vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) with high affinity and specificity. More particularly, the invention provides anti-VEGF-A DARPins capable of inhibiting angiogenesis and vascular leakage through intravitreal or topical administration for the treatment of ocular disorders such as wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD), diabetic retinopathy, and corneal neovascularization. The invention further encompasses methods of producing said DARPins, pharmaceutical compositions comprising them, and their therapeutic applications in ophthalmology.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

VEGF-A is a critical regulator of angiogenesis and vascular permeability, playing a central role in pathological conditions such as cancer and ocular diseases. In ophthalmology, excessive VEGF-A activity contributes to retinal and choroidal neovascularization, as seen in wet AMD and diabetic macular edema. Current anti-VEGF therapies, including monoclonal antibodies (e.g., bevacizumab) and antibody fragments (e.g., ranibizumab), require frequent intravitreal injections due to their limited ocular retention and potency. These repeated injections pose risks such as endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, and increased healthcare costs.  

While topical administration of anti-VEGF agents could mitigate these risks, conventional biologics such as antibodies face challenges including poor ocular penetration and instability. There remains an unmet need for highly potent, stable, and penetrative VEGF inhibitors that can be administered less frequently via intravitreal injection or, ideally, as eye drops.  

DARPins represent a promising alternative due to their small size, high stability, and strong binding affinity. Derived from natural ankyrin repeat proteins, DARPins can be engineered to bind targets with picomolar affinity while exhibiting superior solubility and thermal stability compared to antibodies. However, prior to this invention, no DARPins had been developed that demonstrated sufficient potency and ocular bioavailability to serve as next-generation VEGF inhibitors for ophthalmic applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides anti-VEGF-A DARPins isolated from naïve DARPin libraries using ribosome display, without requiring additional affinity maturation steps. These DARPins exhibit single-digit picomolar binding affinity for VEGF-A, surpassing the potency of currently approved anti-VEGF agents such as ranibizumab and bevacizumab.  

Key advantages of the disclosed DARPins include:  
- High affinity (KD ≤ 25 pM) for VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, enabling prolonged therapeutic effects with reduced dosing frequency.  
- Robust stability, allowing formulation as high-concentration solutions for intravitreal or topical administration.  
- Effective inhibition of VEGF-induced vascular leakage and angiogenesis in vivo, as demonstrated in rabbit and rat models.  
- Favorable ocular penetration, with detectable retinal distribution following intravitreal injection and efficacy via topical application.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these DARPins, methods of treating VEGF-mediated ocular disorders, and processes for large-scale production of the DARPins in bacterial expression systems.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The anti-VEGF-A DARPins of the invention were generated using ribosome display from naïve N2C and N3C DARPin libraries. Selection rounds included three standard rounds followed by three off-rate selection rounds to enrich high-affinity binders. The final pool was screened via crude extract ELISA, yielding multiple DARPins with strong VEGF-A binding.  

**Structural and Functional Characteristics**  
The DARPins are single-domain proteins of approximately 18-21 kDa, lacking post-translational modifications or fusion partners. Their sequences comprise conserved ankyrin repeat motifs flanked by N- and C-terminal capping repeats. Purified DARPins exhibit >90% homogeneity by SDS-PAGE and size-exclusion chromatography, with melting temperatures exceeding 70°C, indicative of high thermal stability.  

In vitro assays confirmed their potency:  
- **Quantikine ELISA**: DARPins suppressed VEGF-A binding by >50% at 25 pM VEGF-A, with an IC50 of ≤10 pM for the lead candidate (DARPin #4).  
- **VEGFR-2 Phosphorylation Assay**: DARPins (1.4 nM) fully inhibited VEGF-A-induced receptor phosphorylation, matching or exceeding ranibizumab’s efficacy.  
- **HUVEC Sprouting Assay**: DARPins (1 nM) reduced endothelial sprout formation by >80%, comparable to ranibizumab.  

**Ocular Pharmacokinetics and Efficacy**  
- **Intravitreal Administration**: Alexa-555-labeled DARPins injected into mouse vitreous showed rapid retinal penetration, with fluorescence detectable in all retinal layers for ≥7 days.  
- **Vascular Leakage Inhibition**: In rabbits, intravitreal DARPin #4 (50 μg) reduced VEGF-A-induced fluorescein leakage by >90% (p = 0.025 vs. PBS control).  
- **Topical Administration**: In a rabbit corneal suture model, DARPin eye drops (20 mg/mL, 8× daily) reduced neovascularization by 43% in vessel density and 31% in vessel length.  
- **Choroidal Neovascularization (CNV)**: Topical DARPin #6 (10 mg/mL) inhibited laser-induced CNV in rats to a similar extent as intravitreal triamcinolone (p = 0.025 vs. PBS).  

**Formulation and Safety**  
The DARPins are formulated in PBS (pH 7.4) at concentrations up to 200 mg/mL. No inflammation or toxicity was observed in any animal model, even with high-dose intravitreal or topical administration.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Generation and Purification of Anti-VEGF-A DARPins  

DARPin libraries N2C and N3C were subjected to ribosome display as follows:  
1. **Selection Rounds**: VEGF-A165-coated Maxisorp plates were incubated with ribosome-displayed DARPin libraries for 1 h at 4°C. Unbound ribosomes were removed by washing.  
2. **Off-Rate Selection**: Biotinylated VEGF-A (1 nM to 10 pM) with 1 μM free VEGF-A competitor was used to isolate slow-dissociating binders.  
3. **Cloning and Screening**: Selected DARPins were cloned into pMPAG6, expressed in E. coli XL1-Blue, and screened via crude extract ELISA. Positive clones were sequenced and scaled up in LB medium (200 mg/L yield).  

Purification involved Ni-NTA affinity chromatography, dialysis into PBS, and endotoxin removal (EndoTrap). Purity was confirmed by mass spectrometry and SEC-HPLC.  

### Example 2: In Vitro Characterization of DARPin #4  

DARPin #4 was tested in the following assays:  
- **Quantikine ELISA**: Serial dilutions (0.1–100 pM) were incubated with 20 pM VEGF-A. IC50 was 10 pM (95% CI: 8–12 pM).  
- **Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR)**: KD was 5.2 pM (kon = 2.1 × 10^6 M−1s−1, koff = 1.1 × 10−5 s−1).  
- **Cross-Reactivity**: Bound human, rabbit, and mouse VEGF-A121/165 but not VEGF-C (≤5% signal vs. VEGF-A in ELISA).  

### Example 3: Intravitreal Efficacy in Rabbits  

Pigmented rabbits (n = 5/group) received:  
- **Day 1**: Intravitreal injection of DARPin #4 (50 μg in 100 μL PBS) or PBS.  
- **Day 4**: Intravitreal VEGF-A165 (500 ng).  
- **Day 6**: Fluorescein angiography.  
**Results**: DARPin-treated eyes showed 92% less leakage vs. PBS (ratio: 0.8 ± 0.1 vs. 6.9 ± 3.1; p = 0.025).  

### Example 4: Topical Efficacy in Corneal Neovascularization  

Rabbit eyes (n = 4) with corneal sutures were treated:  
- **Regimen**: DARPin #4 (20 mg/mL) or PBS, 8× daily for 20 days.  
- **Outcome**: Vessel density reduced by 43% (p < 0.01), maximum length by 31% (p < 0.05).  

### Example 5: CNV Inhibition in Rats  

Brown Norway rats (n = 6/group) underwent laser-induced CNV and received:  
- **Topical DARPin #6**: 10 mg/mL, 4× daily.  
- **Intravitreal Triamcinolone**: 120 μg single dose.  
**Results**: Day 15 CNV lesion size was reduced by 62% (DARPin) and 68% (triamcinolone) vs. PBS (p < 0.05).
