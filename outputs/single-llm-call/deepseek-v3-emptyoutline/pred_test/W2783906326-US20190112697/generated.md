# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to a silicon (Si)-gradient steel sheet with improved magnetic properties, particularly suitable for use as a core material in high-frequency electrical applications such as transformers and reactors. More specifically, the invention pertains to a steel sheet exhibiting a controlled Si concentration gradient across its thickness, optimized crystal grain structure, and internal stress distribution to minimize eddy current losses while maintaining favorable mechanical and magnetic characteristics.  

## BACKGROUND  

Conventional electrical steel sheets containing high silicon content (e.g., 6.5% Si) are known for their high resistivity and low magnetostriction, which reduce eddy current losses under alternating current (AC) magnetization. However, increasing the Si content beyond 4% significantly reduces ductility, making conventional rolling processes impractical for producing thin steel sheets. While chemical vapor deposition (CVD) siliconizing has enabled industrial production of 6.5% Si steel sheets, these materials still exhibit limitations in high-frequency applications due to their homogeneous composition.  

Prior attempts to develop Si-gradient steel sheets via CVD siliconizing have demonstrated reduced iron losses at high frequencies compared to homogeneous 6.5% Si steel. However, fundamental research on such gradient structures remains limited, and their magnetic properties are not fully understood. In particular, the relationship between Si concentration distribution, internal stress, crystal grain morphology, and magnetic performance has not been systematically optimized for industrial applications.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a Si-gradient steel sheet with a precisely controlled composition and microstructure to achieve superior high-frequency magnetic performance. The steel sheet comprises:  

1. A surface part with an average Si concentration of 2.5% to 6.5%, exhibiting tensile stress parallel to the surface.  
2. An inner part with an average Si concentration of 2.0% or less, exhibiting compressive stress parallel to the surface.  
3. A boundary part between the surface and inner regions with a Si concentration gap of 0.4% or more.  
4. Crystal grains in the surface part having an average aspect ratio (parallel-to-surface dimension vs. perpendicular-to-surface dimension) of 0.7 to 4.0.  
5. A surface part thickness constituting 10% to 40% of the total steel sheet thickness.  

The invention further specifies optimal ranges for carbon (C) content (0.020% or less) and silicon (Si) content (0.15% to 2.0% in the base material), as well as a preferred steel sheet thickness range of 0.03 to 0.5 mm. These structural and compositional features synergistically reduce eddy current losses while maintaining mechanical integrity and magnetic permeability.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Average Aspect Ratio of Crystal Grains Included in Surface Part: Ratio of Dimension of Crystal Grains in Parallel-to-Surface Direction to Dimension of Crystal Grains in Perpendicular-to-Surface Direction (Depth Direction) is 0.7 or More and 4.0 or Less  

The crystal grains in the surface part of the steel sheet are characterized by an average aspect ratio between 0.7 and 4.0, where the aspect ratio is defined as the ratio of the grain dimension parallel to the sheet surface to the grain dimension perpendicular to the surface (depth direction). This controlled grain morphology is achieved through specific annealing conditions during manufacturing and contributes significantly to the magnetic properties. When the aspect ratio is less than 0.7, the magnetic flux path becomes discontinuous, increasing hysteresis losses. Conversely, when the aspect ratio exceeds 4.0, excessive grain elongation may lead to mechanical instability during processing. The optimal range of 0.7 to 4.0 ensures continuous magnetic domain alignment while maintaining structural integrity.  

### Thickness of Surface Part: 10% to 40% of Thickness of Steel Sheet (Preferable Condition)  

The surface part, defined as the region from the outermost surface to the point where the Si concentration begins to decrease significantly, constitutes 10% to 40% of the total steel sheet thickness. This range is critical for balancing magnetic performance and mechanical properties. A surface part thickness below 10% provides insufficient high-Si material to effectively reduce eddy current losses, while exceeding 40% leads to excessive brittleness and manufacturing difficulties. Within this range, the surface part optimally concentrates magnetic flux while the inner part provides necessary mechanical support.  

### Average Si Concentration in Surface Part: 2.5% to 6.5% (Preferable Condition)  

The surface part exhibits an average Si concentration between 2.5% and 6.5%, with the concentration gradually decreasing towards the inner part. This gradient is achieved through controlled diffusion during the siliconizing process. Below 2.5% Si, the resistivity increase is insufficient for significant eddy current loss reduction. Above 6.5% Si, the material becomes excessively brittle. The preferred range provides optimal resistivity for high-frequency applications while maintaining workability.  

### Average Si Concentration in Inner Part: 2.0% or Less (Preferable Condition)  

The inner part of the steel sheet maintains an average Si concentration of 2.0% or less to preserve ductility and formability. This lower Si content region serves as a mechanical support structure while contributing to the overall stress distribution. The transition from high-Si surface to low-Si interior creates beneficial internal stresses that influence magnetic domain orientation.  

### Si Concentration Gap in Boundary Part: 0.4% or More (Preferable Condition)  

A distinct boundary exists between the surface and inner parts, characterized by a Si concentration gap of 0.4% or more over a short distance. This sharp transition creates controlled internal stresses that influence magnetic domain alignment. The stress gradient helps concentrate magnetic flux in the high-permeability surface layer while minimizing losses in the inner part.  

### Surface Part: Tensile Stress of 50 to 200 MPa in Direction Parallel to Surface, Inner Part: Compressive Stress of 50 to 200 MPa in Direction Parallel to Surface (Preferable Condition)  

The surface part exhibits tensile stress of 50 to 200 MPa parallel to the sheet surface, while the inner part shows compressive stress of 50 to 200 MPa in the same direction. This stress distribution arises from lattice constant differences between high-Si and low-Si regions and is essential for optimal magnetic performance. Below 50 MPa, the stress-induced magnetic anisotropy is insufficient to significantly affect domain alignment. Above 200 MPa, excessive stress may cause mechanical defects or undesirable magnetic hardening.  

### Thickness of Steel Sheet: 0.03 to 0.5 mm (Preferable Condition)  

The overall steel sheet thickness ranges from 0.03 to 0.5 mm, suitable for high-frequency applications. Thinner sheets below 0.03 mm are difficult to manufacture consistently, while thicknesses exceeding 0.5 mm show increased eddy current losses at high frequencies.  

### C: 0.020% or Less  

The carbon content is limited to 0.020% or less to prevent formation of carbides that could impede magnetic domain movement and increase hysteresis losses. Excess carbon also interferes with silicon diffusion during processing.  

### Si: 0.15% to 2.0%  

The base steel composition contains 0.15% to 2.0% silicon before siliconizing. This range ensures adequate initial resistivity while maintaining sufficient ductility for rolling and processing.  

## Example 1  

A steel sheet with initial thickness of 0.15 mm and base composition of 0.01% C, 1.5% Si was processed through CVD siliconizing to create a Si-gradient structure. The resulting sheet exhibited:  
- Surface part thickness: 30% of total thickness (0.045 mm)  
- Average Si in surface part: 5.0%  
- Average Si in inner part: 1.0%  
- Si concentration gap at boundary: 1.2%  
- Surface tensile stress: 150 MPa  
- Inner compressive stress: 120 MPa  
- Crystal grain aspect ratio in surface: 2.5  

Magnetic testing showed 30% lower eddy current losses at 20 kHz compared to homogeneous 6.5% Si steel of equivalent thickness.  

## Example 2  

A 0.10 mm thick sheet with base composition of 0.008% C, 0.8% Si was processed to achieve:  
- Surface part thickness: 20% of total thickness (0.02 mm)  
- Average Si in surface part: 4.0%  
- Average Si in inner part: 0.5%  
- Si concentration gap at boundary: 0.8%  
- Surface tensile stress: 100 MPa  
- Inner compressive stress: 80 MPa  
- Crystal grain aspect ratio in surface: 1.8  

This sample demonstrated superior high-frequency performance with 40% lower iron losses at 50 kHz compared to conventional 6.5% Si steel.  

## Example 3  

A thicker variant of 0.35 mm was produced from base material containing 0.015% C, 1.8% Si, resulting in:  
- Surface part thickness: 15% of total thickness (0.0525 mm)  
- Average Si in surface part: 3.0%  
- Average Si in inner part: 1.2%  
- Si concentration gap at boundary: 0.5%  
- Surface tensile stress: 70 MPa  
- Inner compressive stress: 60 MPa  
- Crystal grain aspect ratio in surface: 3.2  

While thicker, this configuration still showed 25% improvement in high-frequency losses compared to homogeneous 3% Si steel of same thickness, demonstrating the versatility of the gradient design across different thicknesses.  

The examples illustrate how the invention's specific combination of compositional gradients, stress states, and microstructural features synergistically improve high-frequency magnetic performance while maintaining manufacturability.
