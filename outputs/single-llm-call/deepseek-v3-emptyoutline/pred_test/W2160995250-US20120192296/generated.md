# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and immunology, specifically to monoclonal antibodies that act as agonists of the Ectodysplasin A Receptor (EDAR). More particularly, the invention provides anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies capable of activating EDAR signaling pathways, methods for their production, and their therapeutic use in treating ectodermal dysplasia conditions such as X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED). The antibodies demonstrate cross-species reactivity and can effectively reverse pathological phenotypes in animal models of EDAR-related disorders.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Ectodermal dysplasias represent a heterogeneous group of genetic disorders characterized by abnormal development of ectodermal structures including hair, teeth, sweat glands, and other skin appendages. X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED), caused by mutations in the gene encoding Ectodysplasin A (EDA), is the most common form. EDA is a tumor necrosis factor (TNF) family ligand that signals through its receptor EDAR, a member of the TNF receptor superfamily.  

Current treatment options for XLHED are limited to symptomatic management. While recombinant EDA protein replacement therapy has shown promise in animal models, it suffers from limitations including short half-life and potential immunogenicity. There remains an unmet need for stable, long-acting EDAR agonists that can effectively activate the EDAR signaling pathway to restore normal ectodermal development.  

Prior attempts to develop EDAR agonists have focused primarily on recombinant EDA proteins. However, these approaches have not addressed the need for stable, long-lasting agonists that can effectively cross the placental barrier for prenatal treatment and provide sustained activation of the EDAR pathway. The development of monoclonal antibodies targeting EDAR presents a novel therapeutic strategy that overcomes these limitations.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to and activate EDAR. These agonist anti-EDAR antibodies are capable of:  

1) Binding to EDAR with high affinity, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance measurements showing dissociation constants (KD) ranging from 0.5 to 40 nM.  

2) Activating EDAR signaling pathways both in vitro and in vivo, as evidenced by their ability to induce apoptosis in EDAR-Fas reporter cell lines and rescue ectodermal phenotypes in EDA-deficient animal models.  

3) Demonstrating cross-species reactivity, binding to EDAR from human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken species.  

4) Recognizing distinct epitopes within the extracellular domain of EDAR, including the cysteine-rich domains CRD1, CRD2, and CRD3.  

5) Exhibiting long-lasting biological activity in vivo, with demonstrated half-lives exceeding 10 days in animal models.  

The antibodies of the invention are particularly useful for treating ectodermal dysplasia conditions, as demonstrated by their ability to restore normal development of hair, teeth, sweat glands, and other ectodermal structures in EDA-deficient animal models. The therapeutic effects persist long-term, with phenotypic corrections maintained for over two years in treated animals.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides monoclonal antibodies that function as agonists of the Ectodysplasin A Receptor (EDAR). These antibodies were generated by immunizing EDAR-deficient mice with recombinant human or mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins. Following immunization, hybridomas were screened for both EDAR binding and agonist activity using two complementary assays: an in vitro EDAR-Fas reporter cell assay and an in vivo tail hair rescue assay in EDA-deficient Tabby mice.  

The antibodies of the invention exhibit several unique and advantageous properties. First, they demonstrate high-affinity binding to EDAR, with dissociation constants ranging from 0.5 to 40 nM as measured by surface plasmon resonance. The binding kinetics vary among different antibodies, with association rate constants (ka) ranging from 1.0 × 10^5 to 1.0 × 10^6 M^-1s^-1 and dissociation rate constants (kd) ranging from 1.0 × 10^-3 to 1.0 × 10^-4 s^-1.  

Second, the antibodies recognize distinct epitopes within the extracellular domain of EDAR. Epitope mapping reveals that some antibodies bind specifically to cysteine-rich domain 1 (CRD1), while others require both CRD1 and CRD2 for binding. One antibody recognizes a conformational epitope requiring all three cysteine-rich domains (CRD1-3). Notably, all antibodies require the presence of CRD1 for binding, suggesting this domain may be essential for proper EDAR folding.  

Third, the antibodies demonstrate remarkable cross-species reactivity, binding to EDAR from human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken species. This broad reactivity suggests the recognized epitopes are evolutionarily conserved and increases the likelihood of therapeutic efficacy across different mammalian species.  

The agonist activity of the antibodies has been demonstrated in multiple functional assays. In an EDAR-Fas reporter cell system, certain antibodies induce apoptosis with EC50 values comparable to recombinant Fc-EDA1. In EDA-deficient Tabby mice, the antibodies rescue tail hair formation with EC50 values ranging from 0.1 to 0.7 mg/kg when administered to newborns. The therapeutic effects extend beyond hair development, with treated animals showing restoration of sweat gland function, normal tooth morphology, and proper development of tracheal glands.  

Mechanistically, the antibodies function as divalent agonists. F(ab')2 fragments retain full agonist activity while Fab fragments are inactive, demonstrating that antibody bivalency is both necessary and sufficient for EDAR activation. This distinguishes the anti-EDAR antibodies from other TNF receptor agonists which often require higher-order oligomerization for activity.  

The antibodies exhibit favorable pharmacokinetic properties, with measured half-lives exceeding 10 days in mice. This long half-life enables sustained EDAR activation and makes the antibodies particularly suitable for treating developmental disorders where prolonged receptor stimulation is required.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Generation and Characterization of Anti-EDAR Monoclonal Antibodies**  

Anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing EDAR-deficient OVE1B mice with either human or mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins. Following immunization, lymph node cells were fused with myeloma cells and hybridomas were selected in hypoxanthine/aminopterin/thymidine medium.  

Hybridoma supernatants were screened for EDAR binding by ELISA using hEDAR-Fc coated plates. Positive clones were further tested for agonist activity using two assays: (1) induction of apoptosis in EDAR-Fas-expressing Jurkat cells, and (2) rescue of tail hair formation in newborn EDA-deficient Tabby mice.  

Selected hybridomas were subcloned twice by limiting dilution and adapted to serum-free culture conditions. Antibodies were purified from conditioned medium by protein G affinity chromatography. The monoclonal nature of the antibodies was confirmed by native gel electrophoresis showing sharp, single bands.  

Sequence analysis of the antibody variable regions revealed usage of diverse VH and VL genes, though certain gene combinations appeared preferentially in multiple hybridomas. Despite sequence diversity, all antibodies demonstrated specific binding to EDAR and agonist activity in vivo.  

### Example 2  

**Epitope Mapping of Anti-EDAR Antibodies**  

The epitopes recognized by anti-EDAR antibodies were characterized using a panel of EDAR-Fc constructs containing different combinations of extracellular domains. Constructs included:  

1) Full extracellular domain (CRD1-3 plus stalk)  
2) CRD1 only  
3) CRD1+CRD2  
4) CRD2+CRD3  

ELISA analysis revealed three distinct binding patterns:  

1) Antibodies binding to CRD1 alone (e.g., antibodies 1, 3, 8)  
2) Antibodies requiring both CRD1 and CRD2 (e.g., antibodies 2, 4, 10)  
3) One antibody (antibody 9) that required all three CRDs for binding  

Western blot analysis under non-reducing conditions showed that all antibodies recognized denatured EDAR, but only those binding to CRD1 alone maintained reactivity under reducing conditions. This suggests that antibodies recognizing CRD2 or CRD3 require intact disulfide bonds for epitope recognition.  

### Example 3  

**Cross-Species Reactivity of Anti-EDAR Antibodies**  

The ability of anti-EDAR antibodies to bind EDAR from different species was tested by flow cytometry using 293T cells expressing glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored EDAR from human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken.  

Most antibodies (13 of 15 tested) showed strong reactivity with EDAR from all five species. One antibody (antibody 5) showed weak binding to chicken EDAR, while another (antibody 15) specifically recognized only human and dog EDAR.  

The broad cross-reactivity correlates with the high sequence conservation of EDAR's extracellular domain, which shows only 4 amino acid differences between human and dog, 10 between human and mouse, and 13 between human and chicken.  

### Example 4  

**Binding Kinetics and Affinity Measurements**  

The binding characteristics of anti-EDAR antibodies were quantified by surface plasmon resonance using monomeric Fab fragments. Human EDAR-Fc was captured on anti-human IgG Fc-derivatized CM5 chips, and Fab solutions at varying concentrations were applied.  

Affinities (KD) ranged from 0.5 to 40 nM, with association rate constants (ka) between 1.0 × 10^5 and 1.0 × 10^6 M^-1s^-1 and dissociation rate constants (kd) between 1.0 × 10^-3 and 1.0 × 10^-4 s^-1. Two antibodies (7 and 14) showed biphasic dissociation kinetics, suggesting complex binding behavior.  

Antibodies with the lowest dissociation constants tended to show the strongest activity in the EDAR-Fas reporter assay, though this correlation did not hold for in vivo activity where most antibodies showed similar potency regardless of affinity.  

### Example 5  

**In Vitro Activity in EDAR-Fas Reporter Assay**  

The agonist activity of anti-EDAR antibodies was quantified using Jurkat cells stably expressing fusion proteins comprising the extracellular domain of human or mouse EDAR linked to the intracellular domain of Fas. Antibody binding induces apoptosis through Fas signaling.  

Only a subset of antibodies (1, 3, 8, 10, and 12) showed activity against mEDAR-Fas cells, with EC50 values 1-2 orders of magnitude higher than Fc-EDA1. Fewer antibodies (1, 3, 10, and 12) were active against hEDAR-Fas cells.  

Interestingly, antibodies active in this assay tended to have lower dissociation constants, suggesting that sustained receptor engagement may be particularly important for signaling through the chimeric EDAR-Fas pathway.  

### Example 6  

**In Vivo Activity in Newborn Tabby Mice**  

The therapeutic potential of anti-EDAR antibodies was assessed in newborn EDA-deficient Tabby mice. Antibodies were administered intraperitoneally within 24 hours of birth at doses ranging from 0.01 to 10 mg/kg.  

All antibodies tested rescued tail hair formation in a dose-dependent manner, with EC50 values between 0.1 and 0.7 mg/kg for most antibodies. One antibody (antibody 11) showed reduced potency (EC50 ~3 mg/kg). The activity profile was similar to recombinant Fc-EDA1 (EC50 ~0.05 mg/kg).  

Notably, monomeric IgG (purified by size exclusion chromatography) showed activity equivalent to unfractionated antibody preparations, demonstrating that antibody aggregates are not required for in vivo efficacy.  

### Example 7  

**Divalent Requirement for Agonist Activity**  

The structural requirements for anti-EDAR antibody activity were investigated using antibody fragments. F(ab')2 fragments retained full agonist activity in newborn Tabby mice, while Fab fragments were completely inactive.  

When F(ab')2 was administered at day 3 post-birth (the latest time point for inducing ventral tail hair), its activity could be inhibited by co-administration of excess Fab fragments. This competitive inhibition confirms that the lack of Fab activity is due to monovalency rather than rapid clearance.  

These results demonstrate that divalency is both necessary and sufficient for anti-EDAR antibody agonist activity in vivo.  

### Example 8  

**Prenatal Treatment and Long-Term Effects**  

To assess the ability of anti-EDAR antibodies to correct tooth development, pregnant Tabby mice were treated intravenously at gestation days 13 and 20 with 400 μg of antibody 3.  

Offspring showed complete phenotypic reversion including:  
1) Normal tail hair development  
2) Functional sweat glands  
3) Hair behind the ears  
4) Mucus-secreting tracheal glands  
5) Normalized eye appearance  
6) Completely rescued molar tooth morphology  

The therapeutic effects persisted long-term, with one treated mouse maintaining normal phenotype for over two years. This demonstrates that transient EDAR activation during critical developmental windows can produce permanent correction of ectodermal dysplasia phenotypes.  

### Example 9  

**Treatment of EDA-deficient Dogs**  

The cross-species therapeutic potential of anti-EDAR antibodies was evaluated in EDA-deficient dogs. Antibody 3 was administered intravenously to three dogs at either 2 days (10 mg/kg) or 14 days (7 mg/kg) of age.  

Treated dogs showed:  
1) Corrected dentition, including in the dog treated at 14 days  
2) Improved lacrimation in two of three dogs  
3) Development of tracheal, bronchial, and esophageal glands  
4) Absence of pneumonia or dry eye complications  

Notably, dogs treated earlier (day 2) showed more complete gland development than the dog treated at day 14, suggesting there are critical windows for EDAR signaling in different ectodermal structures.  

The results demonstrate that anti-EDAR antibodies can effectively treat XLHED across species, with therapeutic effects on multiple ectodermal structures including teeth, which are particularly resistant to correction with other therapies.
