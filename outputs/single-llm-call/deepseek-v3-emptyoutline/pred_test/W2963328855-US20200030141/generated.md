## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to ophthalmic surgical devices and methods. More specifically, the invention pertains to a laser-based system and technique for treating conjunctivochalasis, a condition characterized by redundant conjunctival tissue that disrupts tear distribution and causes ocular discomfort. The disclosed system utilizes near-infrared (NIR) laser energy at a wavelength of approximately 1460 nm to induce controlled thermal shrinkage of conjunctival tissue while minimizing collateral damage to surrounding ocular structures.  

## BACKGROUND  

Conjunctivochalasis is a common age-related ocular condition where the conjunctiva—the thin, transparent membrane covering the sclera—becomes loose and forms redundant folds. These folds often prolapse onto the lower eyelid margin, disrupting the normal tear film distribution and leading to chronic eye irritation, blurred vision, and dry eye symptoms that are refractory to conventional treatments such as artificial tears or anti-inflammatory medications. In severe cases, the mechanical friction between the conjunctival folds and eyelid during blinking causes persistent trauma to both tissues.  

Current surgical treatments for conjunctivochalasis involve removing or reducing the redundant conjunctival tissue through various techniques including thermocautery, electrocautery, and the paste-pinch-cut method. However, these approaches present significant drawbacks. Thermocautery and electrocautery employ temperatures exceeding 100°C, creating full-thickness burns that are painful, require extended healing periods (often up to one month), and carry risks of excessive inflammation, scarring, and pyogenic granuloma formation. Moreover, inadvertent patient movement during these procedures may result in unintended damage to adjacent corneal or conjunctival tissues. While the paste-pinch-cut technique causes less thermal injury, it necessitates an operating room setting, increases procedural costs, and still involves several weeks of postoperative inflammation.  

Laser conjunctivoplasty has emerged as a potential alternative due to its ability to precisely control energy delivery. Prior systems using argon lasers (532 nm wavelength) are limited by strong hemoglobin absorption, which can induce vessel rupture and hemorrhage. Given that conjunctival tissue comprises approximately 70% water, a laser wavelength preferentially absorbed by water would provide a safer and more effective means of achieving thermal shrinkage. The near-infrared spectrum contains two strong water absorption peaks at 1460 nm and 1940 nm, with the former being more practical due to the commercial availability of high-efficiency diode lasers at this wavelength.  

Thus, there remains an unmet need for a conjunctivochalasis treatment system that combines the precision of laser energy delivery with optimized thermal parameters to achieve consistent tissue shrinkage while minimizing pain, inflammation, and procedural complexity. The present invention addresses these needs through a novel NIR laser system with specific pulse parameters and a specialized handheld probe design.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF DISCLOSED EMBODIMENTS  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system and method for laser thermal conjunctivoplasty (LTC) using a 1460 nm diode laser. The system comprises three principal components: (1) a programmable pulsed laser diode module, (2) a handheld probe with integrated focusing optics and aiming beam, and (3) angled forceps for tissue stabilization during the procedure.  

The laser diode module generates NIR light at approximately 1460 nm with a maximum continuous-wave output of 12 W. A custom control circuit enables precise adjustment of pulse parameters including duty cycle (10-30%), repetition rate (1 Hz), and treatment duration (typically 4 seconds). The system incorporates a 650 nm aiming beam to facilitate proper targeting before NIR energy delivery. A foot pedal serves as a safety interrupter, immediately terminating laser output if released during the procedure.  

The handheld probe features a cylindrical lens that transforms the optical output from a 200 μm core diameter optical fiber into a line-focused beam measuring 10 mm × 1 mm at the focal plane. This linear profile ensures uniform energy distribution across the target conjunctival fold while maintaining a compact form factor. The probe interfaces with specially designed angled forceps that serve dual purposes: (a) stabilizing the redundant conjunctival tissue during laser exposure and (b) maintaining a consistent 5.8 mm working distance between the probe tip and tissue surface. The forceps' angled platform positions the conjunctival fold perpendicular to the incident laser beam, optimizing energy delivery.  

Critical to the invention's therapeutic efficacy is the optimization of laser parameters to achieve sufficient collagen shrinkage (typically 45%) while avoiding tissue boiling or excessive thermal damage. Experimental data demonstrate that a peak power of 3 W combined with a 20% duty cycle (200 ms pulse duration) raises tissue temperature to approximately 88°C—sufficient to induce collagen denaturation while remaining below the 100°C threshold for rapid vaporization. This parameter set delivers four pulses over a 4-second treatment interval, allowing adequate thermal relaxation between pulses to confine energy deposition within the 0.5 mm thickness of the grasped conjunctival fold.  

Real-time monitoring modalities enhance procedural safety and precision. An infrared thermal camera provides continuous temperature feedback during treatment, while optical coherence tomography (OCT) enables visualization of the dynamic shrinkage process. Particle image velocimetry analysis of OCT data confirms centripetal tissue contraction consistent with effective thermal remodeling.  

Post-treatment evaluation shows the invention achieves several advantages over prior techniques:  
- Reduced collateral thermal damage due to precise wavelength selection (1460 nm) and controlled pulse parameters  
- Minimal inflammation and faster healing compared to cautery-based methods  
- Office-based procedure eliminating need for operating room resources  
- Real-time visual confirmation of therapeutic effect through tissue shrinkage observation  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: System Configuration and Operation**  
The LTC system was implemented using a 12 W, 1460 nm diode laser module (M1F2S22-1470.10-12C-SS5.x) coupled to a 200 μm core diameter optical fiber. A LabVIEW-based interface allowed adjustment of pulse width (100-300 ms), repetition rate (1 Hz), and treatment duration (4 seconds). The handheld probe incorporated a 5.8 mm focal length cylindrical lens to produce a 10 mm × 1 mm line focus at the conjunctival surface. Angled forceps maintained the target tissue at the optimal working distance.  

During treatment, the surgeon first identified redundant conjunctival folds using slit-lamp examination. The 650 nm aiming beam helped position the probe over the target area. Upon foot pedal activation, four 200 ms pulses of 1460 nm light (3 W peak power) were delivered over 4 seconds, with real-time temperature monitoring ensuring the tissue remained between 62-100°C. Immediate shrinkage of 36-45% was observed, with OCT confirming uniform collagen contraction.  

**Example 2: Parameter Optimization**  
Ex vivo experiments using porcine eyes demonstrated the relationship between laser parameters and therapeutic outcomes. At constant 20% duty cycle, peak powers of 2.33 W, 3 W, and 3.66 W produced shrinkage of 21%, 36%, and 45% respectively. Temperatures remained below 100°C at 3 W (88°C) but exceeded this threshold at higher powers. Similarly, at fixed 3 W power, duty cycles of 10%, 20%, and 30% yielded shrinkage of 8%, 36%, and 45%, with 30% duty cycle causing undesirable tissue damage (>110°C). These results established 3 W peak power with 20% duty cycle as the optimal parameter set.  

**Example 3: Comparative Efficacy**  
The invention's performance was compared to conventional thermocautery in a porcine eye model. While both methods achieved comparable shrinkage (~40%), histological analysis revealed significant differences:  
- LTC samples showed intact epithelium and limited stromal damage confined to the treatment zone  
- Thermocautery samples exhibited full-thickness epithelial necrosis and widespread stromal coagulation  
- OCT angiography demonstrated preserved microvasculature in LTC-treated tissue versus occluded vessels after thermocautery  

These findings confirm the invention's superior safety profile while maintaining therapeutic effectiveness.  

**Example 4: Clinical Workflow**  
A typical treatment session involves:  
1. Topical anesthetic application  
2. Identification of redundant conjunctival folds using slit-lamp biomicroscopy  
3. Marking target area with surgical dye  
4. Positioning probe using aiming beam guidance  
5. Delivering four laser pulses (3 W, 200 ms duration, 1 Hz rate) while monitoring tissue response  
6. Immediate assessment of shrinkage adequacy  
7. Repeat treatment to adjacent areas as needed  

Postoperative care consists of antibiotic ointment application for 3-5 days, with patients reporting minimal discomfort and returning to normal activities within 24 hours.  

The examples demonstrate the invention's ability to provide a precise, controlled, and office-based solution for conjunctivochalasis that addresses the limitations of existing surgical approaches. Through optimized wavelength selection, parameter tuning, and specialized delivery optics, the system achieves effective tissue remodeling with minimal adverse effects.
