# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of virology and antiviral therapeutics. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting dengue virus replication through modulation of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and interferon-mediated antiviral responses. The invention further encompasses diagnostic methods for detecting dengue infection through measurement of specific chemokine biomarkers.  

## BACKGROUND  

Dengue virus infection represents a significant global health burden, with an estimated 50-100 million cases of dengue fever and 250,000-500,000 cases of severe dengue hemorrhagic fever/dengue shock syndrome occurring annually. Despite this substantial disease burden, current understanding of dengue pathogenesis remains incomplete, and no specific antiviral therapies exist for clinical use. The four serotypes of dengue virus (DENV1-4) present significant challenges for vaccine development and therapeutic intervention.  

Current approaches to dengue treatment remain largely supportive, focusing on fluid replacement and symptom management. The complex immune responses during dengue infection, including antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) and T cell "original antigenic sin," complicate therapeutic development. While interferon treatment has shown some antiviral effects against dengue in vitro, its clinical utility remains limited due to systemic side effects and incomplete efficacy.  

The ubiquitin-proteasome system has emerged as a potential target for antiviral therapy across multiple viral pathogens. However, the specific role of this pathway in dengue virus replication has not been previously characterized. Similarly, while certain interferon-stimulated genes have demonstrated antiviral activity against other viruses, their specific effects against dengue virus remain largely unexplored.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for the treatment and diagnosis of dengue virus infection. Through comprehensive gene expression analysis in multiple cell systems and clinical samples, the inventors have identified specific components of the host response that are critical for dengue virus replication and pathogenesis.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of proteasome inhibitors, including but not limited to MG-132 and ALLN, for the specific inhibition of dengue virus replication  
2. The identification of viperin as an interferon-induced antiviral protein with specific activity against dengue virus  
3. The characterization of chemokines IP-10 (CXCL10) and I-TAC (CXCL11) as biomarkers for dengue infection  
4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising effective amounts of proteasome inhibitors for the treatment of dengue infection  
5. Methods for detecting dengue infection through measurement of IP-10 and I-TAC levels in biological samples  

The invention further encompasses variants and functional equivalents of the identified compounds, methods for screening additional antiviral compounds targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, and combination therapies incorporating these approaches with existing antiviral strategies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Dengue  

Dengue virus is an enveloped, positive-stranded RNA virus belonging to the Flaviviridae family. The virus exists as four distinct serotypes (DENV1-4) that circulate in endemic areas. Primary infection with one serotype typically provides lifelong immunity to that serotype but does not protect against infection with other serotypes. The virus is transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes.  

Following transmission, initial viral replication occurs in dendritic cells and macrophages, followed by systemic spread to various tissues including liver, spleen, and lymph nodes. The clinical manifestations range from mild dengue fever (DF) to severe dengue hemorrhagic fever/dengue shock syndrome (DHF/DSS), characterized by plasma leakage, thrombocytopenia, and potentially fatal circulatory collapse.  

### Assays for Dengue  

The invention provides various assays for detecting and quantifying dengue virus infection. These include:  
- Plaque assays for measuring infectious virus particles  
- Flow cytometry methods for detecting viral proteins in infected cells  
- Real-time RT-PCR techniques for viral RNA quantification  
- Microarray and quantitative PCR approaches for analyzing host gene expression responses  
- ELISA-based detection of specific chemokines (IP-10 and I-TAC) in patient samples  

These assays may be used for diagnostic purposes, monitoring disease progression, or evaluating therapeutic efficacy. The invention particularly emphasizes the use of IP-10 and I-TAC levels as early biomarkers of dengue infection, with elevated levels detectable within 1-3 days of fever onset.  

### Ubiquitin-Proteasome Pathway  

The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway represents a central component of the invention's therapeutic approach. Comprehensive gene expression analysis revealed significant upregulation of multiple components of this pathway during dengue infection, including:  
- HERC5 and HERC6 (E3 ubiquitin ligases)  
- UBE2L6 (ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme)  
- USP15 and USP18 (ubiquitin-specific proteases)  

Functional studies demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of the proteasome using MG-132 or ALLN significantly reduces dengue virus replication in cell culture models. These inhibitors achieve greater than 50% reduction in viral titers at non-cytotoxic concentrations, with over 90% inhibition achievable at higher doses.  

The invention encompasses the use of these and other proteasome inhibitors to disrupt critical steps in the dengue virus life cycle, including viral assembly, maturation, and release. The specific molecular mechanisms may involve interference with viral protein processing, cellular protein degradation required for viral replication, or intracellular trafficking of viral components.

Secondary screens may evaluate cytotoxicity, specificity for dengue virus versus other flaviviruses, and potential for combination therapies.

The invention encompasses not only the specific proteasome inhibitors ALLN (N-Acetyl-Leu-Leu-Nle-CHO) and MG-132 (Z-Leu-Leu-Leu-al), but also structural analogs, derivatives, and functional equivalents thereof. These include:

Functional equivalents are defined as compounds that achieve at least 50% inhibition of dengue virus replication at concentrations that maintain >80% cell viability in standard cytotoxicity assays. The invention further includes combinations of these inhibitors with other antiviral agents or immune modulators.

### Examples  

**Example 1: Inhibition of Dengue Replication by Proteasome Inhibitors**  
HepG2 cells were infected with dengue virus serotype 2 (TSV01 strain) at MOI=1. At 24 hours post-infection, cells were treated with MG-132 or ALLN at concentrations ranging from 0.1-10 μM. Viral titers in culture supernatants were measured by plaque assay at 72 hours post-infection. Both inhibitors demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition of viral replication, with 50% inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of 1.2 μM for MG-132 and 2.5 μM for ALLN. Cytotoxicity assays confirmed these concentrations maintained >85% cell viability.  

**Example 2: Viperin Overexpression Reduces Dengue Replication**  
A549 cells stably overexpressing viperin were infected with dengue virus (MOI=1). Compared to wild-type controls, viperin-expressing cells showed a 65% reduction in viral titers at 48 hours post-infection (p<0.001). Pre-treatment with interferon-β (500 U/ml) enhanced this effect, resulting in 85% reduction in viral replication.  

**Example 3: IP-10 and I-TAC as Diagnostic Markers**  
Serum samples from 10 dengue patients and 10 non-dengue fever controls were analyzed by ELISA. IP-10 levels were significantly elevated in dengue patients during early infection (mean 1250 pg/ml vs 150 pg/ml in controls, p<0.001). I-TAC showed similar elevation (mean 480 pg/ml vs 60 pg/ml, p<0.001). Levels declined during convalescence, demonstrating utility as acute-phase markers.  

**Example 4: Screening for Novel Proteasome Inhibitors**  
A library of 5,000 compounds was screened for inhibition of dengue replication in HepG2 cells. Primary hits (≥50% inhibition at 10 μM) were counterscreened for proteasome inhibition using a fluorogenic substrate assay. Confirmed hits included novel peptide analogs and non-peptide inhibitors with IC50 values ranging from 0.5-5 μM.  

**Example 5: Formulation of Proteasome Inhibitors**  
Pharmaceutical compositions containing MG-132 (5 mg/ml) were prepared in 10% DMSO, 40% PEG-300, and 50% saline for intravenous administration. Stability testing confirmed >90% drug integrity after 7 days at 4°C. In vivo pharmacokinetic studies in rats demonstrated therapeutic plasma levels for 6-8 hours post-dose.  

The examples demonstrate the utility of the invention for both therapeutic and diagnostic applications in dengue virus infection. The specific embodiments described may be modified or adapted while maintaining the essential characteristics of the invention.
