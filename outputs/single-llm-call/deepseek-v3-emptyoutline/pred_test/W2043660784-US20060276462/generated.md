# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for enhancing cognitive performance, particularly under conditions of sleep deprivation. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of ampakines, specifically the compound CX717 or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, to improve performance in memory and attention-based tasks by modulating α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptor activity. The invention further encompasses methods for administering such compositions to non-human primates and humans to counteract deficits induced by sleep deprivation, as demonstrated by behavioral and neuroimaging data.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation  

Sleep deprivation disrupts neural activity, particularly in cortical and subcortical regions involved in cognition. Electroencephalographic (EEG) studies reveal increased delta (0.5–5 Hz) and beta (18–25 Hz) band power during sleep deprivation, indicative of microsleep episodes and impaired vigilance. These electrophysiological changes correlate with diminished performance in tasks requiring sustained attention and working memory.  

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation  

Functional imaging studies identify the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DPFC), medial temporal lobe (MTL), dorsal striatum (DStr), and thalamus as key regions affected by sleep deprivation. Metabolic activity in these areas, measured via positron emission tomography (PET), shows marked reductions in glucose utilization during sleep deprivation, paralleling behavioral deficits. The DPFC and MTL, critical for working memory, are particularly vulnerable.  

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance  

Sleep deprivation induces physiological stress, exacerbating cognitive decline. Stress hormones such as cortisol impair synaptic plasticity, further compromising glutamatergic transmission mediated by AMPA receptors. Compensatory activation of alternative neural circuits during sleep deprivation often fails to restore baseline performance, highlighting the need for targeted pharmacological intervention.  

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory  

Sleep deprivation severely impairs delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) task performance, a paradigm assessing short-term memory and attention. Deficits manifest as reduced accuracy and prolonged response latencies, particularly at longer delays (21–30 s) and with higher cognitive load (5–6 images). These effects are reversible with AMPA receptor modulation, suggesting a mechanistic link between sleep deprivation and glutamatergic dysfunction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein:  
- **Ampakine**: A positive allosteric modulator of AMPA receptors, exemplified by CX717 (1-(quinoxalin-6-ylcarbonyl)piperidine).  
- **Sleep deprivation**: A state induced by ≥30 hours of continuous wakefulness, characterized by EEG alterations and cognitive deficits.  
- **DMS task**: A delayed-match-to-sample paradigm assessing memory and attention via image-matching accuracy and latency.  
- **CMRglc**: Cerebral metabolic rate for glucose, measured by [¹⁸F]-FDG PET to quantify regional brain activity.  

### II. Non-Human Primate Model for Testing Effects of Sleep Deprivation on a Delayed-Match-to-Sample Task (DMTS)  

The invention employs a validated non-human primate (rhesus macaque) model to evaluate CX717’s efficacy. Key features include:  
1. **Task Design**: Monkeys perform a DMS task with variable delays (1–30 s) and image loads (2–6 non-match stimuli).  
2. **Sleep Deprivation Protocol**: Animals are kept awake for 30–36 hours via environmental stimulation (e.g., videos, intermittent interaction).  
3. **Outcome Measures**:  
   - **Behavioral**: Percent correct trials and response latencies.  
   - **Neurophysiological**: EEG power spectra (delta, beta bands).  
   - **Metabolic**: PET-based CMRglc in DPFC, MTL, DStr, and thalamus.  

### Salts of Compounds  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutically acceptable salts of CX717, including but not limited to:  
- Hydrochloride, sulfate, or phosphate salts.  
- Salts formed with cyclodextrins (e.g., hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin) to enhance solubility.  

### Pharmaceutical Formulations  

Formulations comprise:  
1. **Active Ingredient**: CX717 (0.3–1.5 mg/kg) or equivalent doses of salts.  
2. **Vehicle**: 10% w/v hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin in 0.45% saline for intravenous administration.  
3. **Alternative Routes**: Oral tablets, intranasal sprays, or transdermal patches for human use.  

### Dosage  

- **Non-Human Primates**: 0.3–1.5 mg/kg IV, optimally 0.8 mg/kg for maximal performance enhancement.  
- **Humans**: Projected equivalent doses (scaled by body surface area) range from 0.1–0.5 mg/kg.  
- **Timing**: Administered 10 minutes pre-task to coincide with peak plasma concentrations.
