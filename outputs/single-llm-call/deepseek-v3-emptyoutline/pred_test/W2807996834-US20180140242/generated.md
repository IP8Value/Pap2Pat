# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of mental health monitoring and relapse prediction in schizophrenia patients. More specifically, the invention pertains to a method and system for early detection of impending relapse in schizophrenia patients by monitoring a subset of symptoms from the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) that exhibit the most significant changes prior to relapse. The invention further relates to the use of mobile communication devices and wearable technology to facilitate frequent symptom monitoring through periodic self-reports and continuous passive sensing, enabling timely intervention to prevent full relapse.  

## BACKGROUND  

Schizophrenia is a chronic and severe mental disorder characterized by frequent relapse of psychotic symptoms. Repeated relapses often lead to treatment resistance, cognitive and functional impairment, decreased quality of life, and increased economic burden. The high suicide rate among schizophrenia patients further underscores the critical need for effective relapse prevention strategies. Current standard care for schizophrenia relies on brief assessments during infrequent outpatient visits, which are insufficient for capturing early warning signs of relapse. Comprehensive symptom assessment tools such as the PANSS, while thorough, are time-consuming and impractical for frequent administration in clinical practice.  

Recent advancements in mobile technology have opened new avenues for remote mental health monitoring. Smartphones and wearable devices can collect both passive sensing data and periodic self-reports on symptoms. However, the cognitive burden associated with frequent and lengthy self-reports makes it impractical to monitor all schizophrenia symptoms regularly. There remains an unmet need to identify the most predictive symptoms of impending relapse and determine the optimal frequency for monitoring these symptoms to enable early intervention.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the aforementioned challenges by identifying a subset of PANSS items that exhibit the most significant increases prior to relapse in schizophrenia patients. Through analysis of data from three large-scale clinical trials, the invention demonstrates that seven specific PANSS items—P1 (delusions), P2 (conceptual disorganization), P3 (hallucinations), P4 (excitement), P6 (suspiciousness), G2 (anxiety), and G4 (tension)—show the greatest changes in the days leading up to relapse. These symptoms begin to increase approximately 7–10 days before relapse, reaching an average one-point increase 0.3–1.2 days prior to relapse.  

The invention provides a method for monitoring these key symptoms through technology-enabled remote assessment solutions, combining periodic self-reports with continuous passive sensing data. By focusing on this subset of highly predictive symptoms, the invention reduces patient burden while maintaining high accuracy in relapse prediction. The invention further specifies that symptom monitoring should occur at least weekly to detect early warning signs effectively.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on a comprehensive analysis of data from three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials involving 907 schizophrenia patients, of whom 267 experienced a relapse during the study period. The analysis identified seven PANSS items that exhibited the most significant increases prior to relapse, with mean changes ranging from 1.12 to 1.53 points at the time of relapse. These symptoms were consistent across patients with different relapse triggers and treatment regimens, indicating their broad applicability.  

The invention utilizes mixed-effect modeling to characterize the trajectories of symptom changes before relapse. The results demonstrate that the seven key PANSS items begin to increase approximately 7–10 days before relapse, with the rate of increase accelerating as relapse approaches. This finding contrasts with previous retrospective studies that suggested symptom changes occur 2–4 weeks before relapse, highlighting the importance of frequent monitoring enabled by the present invention.  

The invention further specifies that the monitoring system should prioritize these seven PANSS items in periodic self-reports, as they provide the highest predictive value for relapse. The system may also incorporate passive sensing data, such as sleep patterns, mobility, and social interactions, to enhance prediction accuracy. By combining these data streams, the invention provides a comprehensive solution for early relapse detection in schizophrenia patients.  

### PANSS  

The Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) is a well-established tool for assessing symptom severity in schizophrenia patients. It comprises 30 items grouped into three subscales: positive symptoms (7 items), negative symptoms (7 items), and general psychopathology (16 items). Each item is scored on a scale of 1–7, with higher scores indicating greater symptom severity. The invention leverages the PANSS framework but focuses on the subset of items most predictive of relapse to optimize monitoring efficiency.  

## EXAMPLES  

### PANSS: Methods  

The methods employed in the invention involve pooling data from three clinical trials (NCT00086320, NCT00111189, and NCT01529515) to analyze symptom trajectories before relapse. Each trial included a screening phase, an open-label stabilization phase, and a double-blind phase where patients were randomized to receive either active treatment or placebo. PANSS assessments were conducted at regular intervals, with frequency ranging from weekly to every four weeks depending on the trial phase.  

Relapse was defined by specific criteria, including psychiatric hospitalization, suicidal/homicidal ideation, aggressive behavior, or significant increases in PANSS total score or pre-specified PANSS items. The invention's analysis focused on patients who met these relapse criteria, examining changes in individual PANSS items from baseline to relapse.  

### PANSS: Definition of Relapse  

In the context of the invention, relapse is defined as meeting any of the following criteria:  
1. Psychiatric hospitalization due to symptom decompensation.  
2. Deliberate self-injury, aggressive behavior, or clinically significant suicidal/homicidal ideation.  
3. A 25% increase in PANSS total score for two consecutive assessments within seven days (for patients with baseline scores >40) or a 10-point increase (for patients with baseline scores ≤40).  
4. An increase in pre-specified PANSS items (P1, P2, P3, P6, P7, G8) to ≥5 (for patients with baseline scores ≤3) or ≥6 (for patients with baseline scores of 4) for two consecutive assessments within seven days.  

### PANSS: Statistical Analysis  

The statistical analysis employed in the invention involves linear and non-linear mixed-effect models to characterize symptom trajectories before relapse. Patient observations were aligned by days relative to relapse rather than scheduled visits to account for variable relapse timing. The non-linear mixed-effect model used an exponential function to describe symptom increases, with parameters indicating the speed of increase (a), the time of one-point increase relative to relapse (b), and the pre-relapse symptom level (c).  

The analysis revealed that the seven key PANSS items had negative b parameters, indicating they reached a one-point increase before relapse. The time of one-point increase ranged from 0.33 to 1.17 days before relapse for these items, confirming their utility as early warning signs.  

### PANSS: Demographics and Characteristics of Patients Experienced a Relapse During Double-Blind Phase  

Among the 907 patients analyzed, 267 experienced a relapse during the double-blind phase. The relapsed patients had similar demographics to non-relapsed patients but exhibited higher baseline PANSS total scores (54.9 vs. 52.5, p=0.002). Relapse reasons varied, with 63 patients relapsing due to psychiatric events, 65 due to increases in pre-specified PANSS items, and 139 due to increases in PANSS total score without meeting item-specific criteria.  

### Example 5  

An exemplary implementation of the invention involves a mobile application that prompts schizophrenia patients to complete brief self-reports on the seven key PANSS items daily. The application analyzes these reports alongside passive sensing data (e.g., sleep duration, step count, social interactions) to detect early signs of relapse. When symptom increases suggestive of impending relapse are identified, the system alerts healthcare providers, enabling timely intervention such as medication adjustment or therapy sessions.  

This example demonstrates the invention's practical application in real-world settings, providing a scalable solution for improving schizophrenia management and reducing relapse rates. The combination of targeted symptom monitoring and passive sensing data offers a robust approach to relapse prediction while minimizing patient burden.
