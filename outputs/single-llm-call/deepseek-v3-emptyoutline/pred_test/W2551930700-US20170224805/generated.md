# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel vaccine delivery systems and methods for inducing systemic and mucosal immune responses against infectious diseases, particularly influenza. Traditional vaccine administration methods, such as intramuscular or subcutaneous injections, primarily elicit systemic immune responses but often fail to induce robust mucosal immunity, which is critical for protection against pathogens that infect mucosal surfaces.  

Current oral vaccine formulations face significant challenges, including degradation in the gastrointestinal tract, inefficient absorption, and inconsistent immune responses. There remains an unmet need for an effective oral vaccine delivery system that can reliably target specific regions of the intestine to maximize immune induction while overcoming the limitations of conventional approaches.  

The invention addresses these challenges by providing a targeted oral vaccine delivery system that utilizes recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vectors encapsulated in radio-controlled capsules (RCCs) for precise release in the small intestine, particularly the ileum or jejunum. This approach enhances antigen presentation, promotes mucosal and systemic immune responses, and improves vaccine efficacy compared to existing methods.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel oral vaccine delivery system comprising immunogenic biological agents, such as recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vectors expressing influenza hemagglutinin (HA), formulated for targeted intestinal delivery. The system utilizes radio-controlled capsules (RCCs) that release the vaccine payload in specific regions of the small intestine (ileum or jejunum) upon external activation, ensuring optimal immune induction.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Immunogenic Biological Agents**: Recombinant adenovirus vectors engineered to express influenza antigens, such as HA, which stimulate robust B-cell and antibody responses.  
2. **Targeted Delivery Systems**: RCCs designed to protect the vaccine payload during gastrointestinal transit and release it in the ileum or jejunum, where gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) is abundant.  
3. **Induction of Mucosal and Systemic Immunity**: The vaccine elicits both IgA and IgG antibody-secreting cells (ASCs), serum neutralizing antibodies, and mucosal immune responses, providing comprehensive protection against influenza.  

The invention also encompasses methods of administering the vaccine, monitoring immune responses, and optimizing delivery parameters to enhance efficacy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### I. Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

- **Recombinant Adenovirus (rAd)**: A genetically modified adenovirus vector engineered to express heterologous antigens, such as influenza hemagglutinin (HA), for vaccination purposes.  
- **Radio-Controlled Capsule (RCC)**: A swallowable device equipped with a radio receiver and release mechanism that dispenses its payload in response to an external signal, enabling targeted delivery to specific intestinal regions.  
- **Gut-Associated Lymphoid Tissue (GALT)**: Immune inductive sites in the intestine, including Peyer’s patches and mesenteric lymph nodes, where antigen presentation and B-cell activation occur.  
- **Mucosal Homing Receptors**: Cell surface markers, such as α4β7 integrin and CCR9, that direct immune cells to mucosal tissues following antigen exposure.  

### II. Immunogenic Biological Agents  

The immunogenic biological agents of the invention comprise recombinant adenovirus vectors expressing influenza antigens, particularly hemagglutinin (HA). The rAd vectors are produced under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions and formulated in liquid suspensions for encapsulation.  

Key features of the rAd vectors include:  
- A particle-to-infectious unit (IU) ratio of approximately 27.8, ensuring high immunogenicity.  
- Stability in gastrointestinal fluids when protected by the RCC.  
- Efficient transduction of intestinal epithelial cells and antigen-presenting cells in GALT.  

The vaccine dose may range from 1×10^9 to 1×10^11 IU per administration, with optimal immune responses observed at 1×10^11 IU.  

### III. Targeted Delivery Systems  

The invention employs radio-controlled capsules (RCCs) for precise intestinal delivery. The RCCs are size 000 capsules equipped with:  
- A radio receiver to trigger payload release upon external activation.  
- A mechanical or osmotic release mechanism to eject the liquid vaccine formulation.  
- A radioactive tracer (e.g., technetium-99m) for real-time tracking via gamma scintigraphy.  

The RCCs are administered orally after an overnight fast to ensure consistent gastrointestinal transit. Release is confirmed by scintigraphy, with optimal delivery sites being the jejunum or ileum.  

### IV. Immune Responses and Vaccines  

The vaccine induces robust immune responses, including:  
1. **Antibody-Secreting Cells (ASCs)**: Significant increases in HA-specific IgA and IgG ASCs are observed in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) post-vaccination, particularly after ileum-targeted delivery.  
2. **Serum Antibodies**: Hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) and microneutralizing (MN) antibody titers rise significantly, with seroconversion rates of up to 67% in ileum-targeted groups.  
3. **Mucosal Immunity**: Fecal and nasal IgA responses are detected, indicating mucosal homing of activated B cells. Flow cytometry reveals increases in α4β7+ and CCR9+ B cells, which migrate to intestinal and respiratory mucosa.  

### V. Examples  

**Example 1: Clinical Study Design**  
A Phase I open-label study was conducted in healthy male volunteers aged 18–49. Participants received a single dose of 1×10^11 IU rAd-HA vaccine delivered via RCC to the jejunum or ileum. Immune responses were assessed at days 0, 7, and 28.  

**Example 2: Immune Response Data**  
- Ileum-targeted release resulted in higher IgA and IgG ASCs (median 54 and 200 per 1×10^6 PBMCs, respectively) compared to jejunum release.  
- HAI seroconversion rates were 67% (ileum) and 44% (jejunum).  
- Flow cytometry confirmed induction of α4β7+CCR9+ B cells, indicative of mucosal homing.  

**Example 3: Formulation Optimization**  
The vaccine was dialyzed and concentrated in sucrose buffer for stability. Alternative formulations, including enteric-coated tablets, were also evaluated for scalability.
