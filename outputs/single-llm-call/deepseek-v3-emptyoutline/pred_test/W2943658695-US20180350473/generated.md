# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to nuclear reactor safeguards and monitoring systems. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and systems for monitoring nuclear reactor fuel cycle activities using stand-off neutron detection at distances of up to approximately 100 meters from a reactor core. The invention enables verification of fissile isotope inventory changes within reactor cores through detection and analysis of neutron emissions that have escaped reactor shielding. The system provides an economical, non-invasive means for nuclear safeguards verification that is particularly suitable for monitoring small modular reactors (SMRs) and research reactors where conventional safeguards measures may be challenging to implement.  

## BACKGROUND  

Nuclear safeguards measures are critical for verifying that nuclear materials are not diverted from peaceful uses. Current safeguards approaches at power reactors typically employ tamper-indicating seals, surveillance cameras, and periodic physical inventory verifications. However, these traditional methods present significant challenges when applied to non-traditional reactor types such as small modular reactors and research reactors. These challenges arise from factors including sealed core designs that preclude direct fuel sampling, continuous refueling operations, wide variations in reactor designs, and the use of highly enriched uranium fuels in many research reactors.  

Existing safeguards technologies are often inadequate for addressing these challenges due to limitations in timeliness, cost-effectiveness, and non-invasiveness. While neutron detection has been used previously for reactor power monitoring, current systems lack the capability to correlate neutron detection signals with isotopic inventory changes at meaningful stand-off distances from reactor cores. There exists a need for improved safeguards technologies that can provide real-time verification of reactor operations while overcoming the limitations of conventional approaches, particularly for monitoring sealed-core and continuously refueled reactors.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a system and method for nuclear reactor safeguards monitoring through stand-off neutron detection. The invention utilizes one or more large-area neutron detectors positioned at strategic locations outside of reactor shielding, typically at distances ranging from approximately 10 meters to 100 meters from the reactor core. The system monitors neutron emissions that escape the reactor shielding and correlates these detections with reactor thermal power measurements to verify changes in fissile isotope inventory within the core.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. The recognition that the neutron flux per unit reactor power is proportional to a weighted sum of the fissile isotope inventory in the reactor core, enabling verification of isotopic composition changes through neutron detection.  

2. The deployment of large-area neutron detectors (each with approximately 0.18 m² detection area and weighing approximately 10 kg) at multiple strategic locations around a reactor facility to provide coordinated detection signals.  

3. The correlation between detected neutron count rates and reactor thermal power to establish baseline operational parameters and identify anomalies indicative of potential fuel diversion or misuse.  

4. The capability to detect changes in fissile isotope inventory on the order of 1 kg of uranium-235 or plutonium-239 within practical timeframes suitable for meeting international safeguards timeliness goals.  

The system provides particular advantages for monitoring sealed-core reactors, continuously refueled reactors, and research reactors where conventional safeguards measures may be difficult to implement. The stand-off nature of the detection method allows for non-invasive monitoring without requiring direct access to reactor cores or fuel elements.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system and method for nuclear reactor safeguards monitoring through stand-off neutron detection. The detailed operation and implementation of the invention are described below with reference to specific embodiments and operational parameters.  

**Neutron Detection Principle**  

The fundamental principle underlying the invention is that the neutron flux within a reactor core, and consequently the neutron emissions escaping the core, are directly related to the fissile isotope inventory through the relationship:  

P_tot = V⟨φ⟩Σ_i⟨Σ_f,i⟩E_f,i = V⟨φ⟩Σ_iN_i⟨σ_f,i⟩E_f,i  

where P_tot is the reactor thermal power, V is the reactor core volume, ⟨φ⟩ is the average neutron flux, N_i is the number density of the ith fissile isotope, ⟨σ_f,i⟩ is the average microscopic fission cross-section, and E_f,i is the energy released per fission for the ith isotope.  

This relationship demonstrates that changes in fissile isotope inventory (N_i) will produce corresponding changes in the ratio of neutron flux to reactor power (⟨φ⟩/P_tot). By monitoring neutron emissions at stand-off distances and correlating these measurements with reactor power, the system can detect and quantify changes in core isotopic composition.  

**Detector System Configuration**  

The invention employs one or more large-area neutron detectors positioned at strategic locations around a reactor facility. Each detector preferably has an active detection area of approximately 0.18 m² (e.g., 18 cm × 100 cm) and weighs approximately 10 kg, making the system practical for deployment at multiple locations. The detectors may utilize various neutron detection technologies, including but not limited to:  

1. Boron-lined detectors incorporating boron-10 enriched coatings  
2. Helium-3 proportional counters  
3. Other thermal neutron detection technologies known in the art  

The detectors are preferably equipped with appropriate moderation materials (such as high-density polyethylene) to optimize detection efficiency for the energy spectrum of neutrons escaping the reactor shielding.  

**Data Acquisition and Analysis**  

The system incorporates sophisticated data acquisition and analysis capabilities:  

1. Time-stamped list mode data recording to enable detailed temporal analysis of neutron detection events  
2. Real-time correlation with reactor power measurements  
3. Statistical analysis algorithms to identify deviations from established operational baselines  
4. Multi-detector coordination to discriminate against local environmental interferences  

The data analysis focuses particularly on establishing and monitoring the ratio of neutron count rate to reactor power, as this ratio provides the most sensitive indicator of changes in core isotopic composition.  

**Operational Implementation**  

In practical implementation, the system operates as follows:  

1. One or more neutron detectors are positioned at selected locations around the reactor facility, taking into account factors such as:  
   - Distance from the reactor core  
   - Intervening shielding materials  
   - Potential for monitoring specific operational areas (e.g., fuel transfer points)  

2. The detectors establish baseline neutron count rates during normal reactor operations across various power levels.  

3. The system continuously monitors the relationship between neutron count rates and reactor power, comparing current measurements against established baselines.  

4. Significant deviations from expected relationships trigger alerts for further investigation, potentially indicating changes in core isotopic composition due to fuel diversion or other unauthorized activities.  

**Performance Characteristics**  

The system has demonstrated capability to:  

1. Detect changes in fissile isotope inventory equivalent to approximately 1 kg of uranium-235 or plutonium-239 within practical monitoring timeframes.  

2. Operate effectively at stand-off distances of up to approximately 100 meters from reactor cores.  

3. Maintain sensitivity despite intervening shielding materials typically found in reactor facilities.  

4. Distinguish between normal operational variations and potential safeguards concerns through coordinated multi-detector analysis.  

**Applications**  

The invention finds particular utility in the following applications:  

1. Monitoring of sealed-core small modular reactors where direct fuel verification is impractical.  

2. Safeguards verification at research reactors using highly enriched uranium fuels.  

3. Continuous monitoring of power reactors with online refueling capabilities.  

4. Detection of undeclared nuclear activities through monitoring of neutron signatures.  

The system provides nuclear safeguards authorities with an economical, non-invasive means to verify reactor operations and detect potential diversion scenarios while overcoming many limitations of conventional safeguards approaches.
