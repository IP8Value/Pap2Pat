# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  
The present invention relates generally to the field of carbon nanomaterials and more specifically to methods for producing graphene from petroleum coke. The invention provides an electrochemical exfoliation process for converting petroleum coke, particularly needle coke, into high-quality graphene nanosheets. This technology enables the sustainable repurposing of refinery by-products into high-value nanomaterials suitable for applications in energy storage, electronics, composites, and other advanced material applications.  

## BACKGROUND  
Petroleum coke, a by-product of oil refining processes, has traditionally been used as a fuel source or as an anode material in steel and aluminum production. However, these applications result in significant greenhouse gas emissions. Meanwhile, graphene has emerged as a highly valuable nanomaterial with applications across numerous industries due to its exceptional electrical, thermal, and mechanical properties. Current methods for graphene production primarily rely on natural graphite as a feedstock, which presents limitations due to finite global reserves and inconsistent quality.  

There exists an unmet need for alternative graphene production methods that utilize more sustainable and readily available feedstocks while maintaining high product quality. Previous attempts to produce graphene from petroleum coke have focused on graphene oxide production or mechanical exfoliation methods, which either yield inferior materials or require extensive post-processing. The electrochemical exfoliation of coke has been attempted, but prior methods produced either small graphene quantum dots or materials requiring additional processing steps.  

## SUMMARY  
The present invention provides a method for producing high-quality graphene nanosheets from petroleum coke through an optimized electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. The method comprises: (1) pretreating needle coke with nitric acid to enhance hydrophilicity; (2) separating particle sizes via centrifugation to isolate optimal precursor material; (3) electrochemical exfoliation in a compressed chamber using ammonium sulfate electrolyte at controlled voltage; and (4) post-reaction centrifugation to isolate graphene nanosheets from unreacted material and impurities.  

The resulting coke-derived graphene exhibits characteristic graphene morphology as confirmed by electron microscopy, with crystalline structure verified by electron diffraction and Raman spectroscopy. The material demonstrates suitable electrical conductivity for applications in energy storage devices, which can be further enhanced through thermal annealing. This invention provides a pathway for converting low-value petroleum by-products into high-value nanomaterials while reducing environmental impact compared to traditional coke utilization methods.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  
The invention provides a comprehensive process for transforming petroleum coke, particularly needle coke fines, into high-quality graphene nanosheets. The process begins with selection and preparation of the coke feedstock. Needle coke is preferred due to its high crystallinity and layered structure, which facilitates effective exfoliation. The selected coke undergoes washing with dichloromethane followed by deionized water to remove surface impurities.  

A critical pretreatment step involves exposing the coke to 6M nitric acid at elevated temperature (120°C) for 4 hours in an autoclave. This treatment enhances the material's hydrophilicity, improving subsequent electrolyte penetration during electrochemical exfoliation. The acid-treated material is then thoroughly washed and dried.  

Prior to electrochemical processing, the pretreated coke undergoes size separation via centrifugation (4000 rpm for 10 minutes) to remove smaller particles less suitable for exfoliation. The retained material is compacted into a dialysis membrane serving as the working electrode in the ECE system. The electrochemical cell employs a platinum working electrode and copper mesh counter electrode submerged in 0.1M ammonium sulfate electrolyte.  

A distinctive feature of the invention is the application of 12V potential for 2 hours while maintaining mechanical compression on the coke material. This combination of electrical and mechanical forces enables continuous exfoliation while maintaining electrical contact throughout the reaction, overcoming limitations of conventional electrochemical methods where exfoliation disrupts electrical connectivity.  

Following electrochemical processing, the product mixture contains unreacted coke, non-graphitic byproducts, and exfoliated graphene. The invention employs a two-step centrifugation protocol to isolate the graphene product. First, low-speed centrifugation (2000 rpm for 10 minutes) removes larger unreacted particles. The supernatant then undergoes high-speed centrifugation (5000 rpm for 20 minutes) to separate graphene nanosheets (sediment) from smaller impurities (supernatant).  

Characterization of the final product confirms graphene formation through multiple analytical techniques. Field emission scanning electron microscopy reveals sheet-like morphology with lateral dimensions of 1-3 μm. High-resolution transmission electron microscopy shows hexagonal crystalline lattice structure characteristic of graphene. Electron diffraction patterns demonstrate long-range order, and Raman spectroscopy exhibits distinct G and 2D bands confirming graphene formation.  

The coke-derived graphene exhibits electrical conductivity suitable for various applications, which can be enhanced through thermal annealing. Annealing at temperatures between 500-1100°C under inert atmosphere improves conductivity from approximately 57 S/m to over 470 S/m, making the material suitable for energy storage applications.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
Needle coke fines (CK-1) were washed with dichloromethane followed by deionized water, then treated with 6M nitric acid at 120°C for 4 hours. The acid-treated material was washed neutral and dried at 100°C overnight. The pretreated coke was suspended in water (5 mg/mL) and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 minutes to remove small particles. The retained material was compacted into a dialysis membrane and subjected to electrochemical exfoliation at 12V for 2 hours in 0.1M ammonium sulfate electrolyte. The reaction product was centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 10 minutes to remove unreacted material, followed by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 20 minutes to isolate graphene nanosheets. The final product exhibited sheet-like morphology with lateral dimensions of 1-3 μm and electrical conductivity of 56.9 S/m.  

### Example 2  
The graphene nanosheets produced in Example 1 were annealed at 500°C for 12 hours under argon atmosphere. This treatment increased the electrical conductivity to 250 S/m. Extended annealing at 500°C for 24 hours further improved conductivity to 258 S/m. Raman spectroscopy confirmed maintenance of graphene structure after annealing, with increased intensity of the G band relative to the D band, indicating improved crystallinity.  

### Example 3  
Graphene nanosheets from Example 1 were annealed at higher temperatures to optimize electrical properties. Annealing at 900°C for 12 hours increased conductivity to 345 S/m, while annealing at 1100°C for 12 hours achieved 474 S/m. Thermogravimetric analysis showed these higher temperature treatments removed residual impurities without significant oxidation of the graphene structure. The annealed materials demonstrated suitability for incorporation into lithium-ion battery anodes, with conductivity values meeting industry requirements for such applications.
