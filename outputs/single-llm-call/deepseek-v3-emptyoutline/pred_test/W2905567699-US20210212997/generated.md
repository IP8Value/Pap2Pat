# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of immunology and cancer therapeutics. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for modulating macrophage polarization through targeting integrin CD11b/CD18 (αMβ2) to promote anti-tumor immune responses. The invention further discloses small molecule agonists of CD11b capable of repolarizing tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) from an immunosuppressive phenotype to a pro-inflammatory phenotype, thereby inhibiting tumor progression.  

### Background Information  

Macrophages play dual roles in tumor progression by either stimulating cytotoxic T cell responses to suppress tumors or promoting immune suppression and angiogenesis to facilitate tumor growth. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) predominantly exhibit an immunosuppressive phenotype, characterized by expression of cytokines such as IL-10, TGF-β, and PDGF-BB, which contribute to tumor immune evasion and neovascularization. While prior studies have identified signaling pathways involving CSF1R, PI3Kγ, and BTK as regulators of macrophage polarization, the role of integrin-mediated adhesion in controlling macrophage function remains incompletely understood.  

Integrin CD11b/CD18 (αMβ2) is a myeloid cell adhesion receptor known to mediate leukocyte trafficking during inflammation. However, its role in tumor immunity has been unclear. Previous work demonstrated that integrin α4β1 promotes myeloid cell recruitment to tumors and subsequent immune suppression, whereas CD11b was not required for myeloid cell trafficking into tumors. Surprisingly, the present invention reveals that CD11b activation regulates macrophage polarization by promoting pro-inflammatory gene expression through a microRNA Let7a-dependent mechanism, thereby restraining immunosuppressive macrophage polarization.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for modulating macrophage polarization and enhancing anti-tumor immunity through targeting integrin CD11b. The invention demonstrates that CD11b activation promotes pro-inflammatory macrophage transcription by upregulating microRNA Let7a, which suppresses IL-6 and c-Myc expression, key drivers of immune suppression. Conversely, loss of CD11b function enhances immunosuppressive macrophage polarization, increases PDGF-BB-dependent vascular normalization, and accelerates tumor growth.  

The invention further discloses the use of a small molecule CD11b agonist, Leukadherin-1 (LA1), which repolarizes TAMs to a pro-inflammatory phenotype, recruits CD8+ T cells, and inhibits tumor progression in murine and human cancer models. LA1 treatment synergizes with chemotherapy and radiation, demonstrating its potential as a therapeutic agent for cancer immunotherapy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that integrin CD11b regulates macrophage polarization through a Let7a-dependent mechanism, thereby controlling tumor immune responses and vascularization. Genetic deletion of CD11b (Itgam−/−) resulted in macrophages with enhanced immunosuppressive gene expression (e.g., Arg1, Tgfb, Il10) and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine production (e.g., Ifng, Nos2, Tnfa). This shift was mediated by decreased Let7a expression, leading to elevated IL-6 and c-Myc levels, which drive immune suppression.  

CD11b activation via ICAM-1 ligation or pharmacological agonism with LA1 restored Let7a expression, suppressed IL-6/STAT3 signaling, and promoted NFκB-dependent pro-inflammatory transcription. In tumor models, CD11b deficiency increased pericyte-covered, "normalized" blood vessels due to elevated PDGF-BB expression, whereas CD11b activation reduced vascular maturation and enhanced tumor perfusion.  

The small molecule CD11b agonist LA1 demonstrated potent anti-tumor effects by repolarizing TAMs, increasing MHC-II+ macrophages, decreasing CD206+ immunosuppressive macrophages, and recruiting CD8+ T cells. LA1 synergized with chemotherapy (e.g., gemcitabine) and radiation, highlighting its utility in combination therapies. Importantly, LA1's effects were CD11b-dependent, as they were abolished in Itgam−/− mice.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**CD11b Regulates Macrophage Polarization via Let7a and c-Myc**  

Bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) from WT and Itgam−/− mice were analyzed for cytokine expression under basal, IL-4 (anti-inflammatory), or IFNγ/LPS (pro-inflammatory) conditions. Itgam−/− macrophages exhibited elevated immunosuppressive markers (Arg1, Tgfb, Il10) and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines (Ifng, Nos2, Tnfa). Transient siRNA knockdown of CD11b replicated this phenotype, confirming CD11b's role in polarization.  

Mechanistically, CD11b activation on ICAM-1-coated surfaces increased Let7a expression, which suppressed IL-6 and c-Myc. Loss of CD11b reduced Let7a, leading to IL-6/STAT3 activation and c-Myc stabilization, driving immune suppression. Pharmacological inhibition of c-Myc restored Let7a expression and reversed immunosuppressive polarization in Itgam−/− macrophages.  

**In Vivo Validation:**  
- Tumors in Itgam−/− mice grew faster than in WT mice, with increased Tregs and reduced CD8+ T cells.  
- Constitutively active CD11b (I332G knockin) mice exhibited slower tumor growth, confirming CD11b's anti-tumor role.  
- Targeted delivery of anti-Let7a nanoparticles to myeloid cells mimicked Itgam−/− phenotypes, accelerating tumor growth and vascular normalization.  

**Therapeutic Application of LA1:**  
- LA1-treated macrophages adoptively transferred with tumor cells abolished tumor growth.  
- Systemic LA1 administration inhibited orthotopic breast (CL66) and lung (LLC) tumors, synergizing with taxol and radiation.  
- LA1's effects were absent in Itgam−/− mice, proving CD11b specificity.  

**Conclusion:** The invention establishes CD11b as a master regulator of macrophage polarization through Let7a/c-Myc/IL-6 signaling and provides LA1 as a novel therapeutic agent for repolarizing TAMs and enhancing anti-tumor immunity.
