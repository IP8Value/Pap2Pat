## FIELD  
The present invention relates to the field of microbiology and infectious diseases, particularly to novel compositions and methods involving Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) urease modifications for therapeutic applications. More specifically, the invention discloses modified urease enzymes with altered surface properties that affect bacterial persistence in host organisms while maintaining ureolytic activity. The invention further encompasses methods of using these modified urease enzymes for developing treatments against H. pylori infections and associated gastric disorders.

## BACKGROUND  
Helicobacter pylori chronically infects the gastric mucosa of approximately half the human population worldwide and is a major causative agent of peptic ulcer disease and gastric cancer. The urease enzyme produced by H. pylori plays a critical role in bacterial colonization by neutralizing gastric acidity through urea hydrolysis. However, prior research has suggested that urease possesses additional functions beyond acid neutralization that contribute to bacterial persistence in the host. Conventional approaches to combat H. pylori infections have focused primarily on inhibiting urease's enzymatic activity, but these methods have shown limited success in eradicating chronic infections. There remains an unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies that target alternative functions of urease essential for bacterial persistence. The present invention addresses this need by identifying specific surface regions of the urease enzyme that are critical for host-pathogen interactions independent of ureolytic activity, thereby providing new targets for therapeutic intervention.

## SUMMARY  
The invention provides modified H. pylori urease enzymes containing specific surface alterations that impair bacterial persistence in host organisms while maintaining ureolytic activity. Through structure-guided mutagenesis, the inventors identified discrete surface-exposed regions of the urease complex, particularly designated as site 3 and site 8, where modifications significantly affect bacterial colonization and persistence without abolishing enzymatic function. The modified urease enzymes of the invention comprise insertions of epitope tags at these specific surface locations, separated by flexible amino acid linkers to minimize structural disruption. Notably, modifications at site 8 resulted in H. pylori strains that initially colonized mice similarly to wild-type bacteria but showed dramatically reduced persistence during chronic infection. This discovery reveals that the surface properties of urease, distinct from its enzymatic activity, play a crucial role in maintaining chronic H. pylori infections. The invention further provides methods for using these modified urease enzymes to develop novel therapeutics, vaccines, and diagnostic tools against H. pylori infections by targeting these newly identified persistence mechanisms.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

### Isolation of Clinical Strains  
The preferred embodiments of the invention utilize H. pylori strain X47 as the parental strain for generating modified urease variants. This streptomycin-resistant strain was selected for its well-characterized colonization properties in animal models. Clinical isolates were maintained on Brain Heart Infusion (BHI) agar plates supplemented with 5% horse blood under microaerobic conditions containing 5% CO2. Antibiotic selection was performed using erythromycin (10 μg/mL) or streptomycin (10 μg/mL) when appropriate. For selection of functional urease-producing strains, bacteria were cultured on BHI agar plates supplemented with 7% horse serum, phenol red (100 mg/L), and urea (600 mg/L), with the medium acidified to pH 5 using 1M HCl to create selective pressure for urease activity.

### Example 2  
In this embodiment, the invention provides a modified urease enzyme containing an insertion at site 1 of the urease complex. The modification was created by inserting DNA encoding epitope tags (specifically HA and FLAG tags) flanked by flexible linkers at a surface-exposed loop region identified through structural analysis. The insertion was introduced into the chromosomal ureA or ureB genes using splicing by overlap extension PCR and homologous recombination. While this modification resulted in urease with reduced acid stability compared to wild-type (p<0.05 by Student's T-test), the corresponding H. pylori strain surprisingly maintained normal colonization ability in short-term mouse infections. This unexpected result demonstrates that certain surface modifications can be tolerated without affecting initial colonization, suggesting that different surface regions mediate distinct aspects of host-pathogen interaction.

### Example 3  
This embodiment describes a modified urease enzyme containing an insertion at site 3, another surface-exposed region identified through structural analysis. Similar to Example 2, this modification involved the insertion of epitope tags separated by flexible linkers. However, unlike the site 1 modification, the site 3 alteration completely abolished the ability of H. pylori to colonize mice, despite the modified urease retaining enzymatic activity in vitro. This striking result provides compelling evidence that the surface region surrounding site 3 plays an essential role in initial colonization, independent of urease's acid-neutralizing function. The site 3 region is located near the rotation axis connecting alpha/beta trimers in the urease complex, suggesting that modifications at this location may affect higher-order interactions important for host interactions.

### Example 4  
The invention further provides a modified urease enzyme containing an insertion at site 4, another surface-exposed region selected based on structural analysis. This modification, created using the same epitope tag insertion strategy as previous examples, resulted in urease with acid stability comparable to wild-type enzyme. The corresponding H. pylori strain colonized mice efficiently in both short-term and long-term infections, similar to wild-type bacteria. This result indicates that not all surface-exposed regions of urease are equally important for host interactions, and that specific, discrete surface areas (such as sites 3 and 8) mediate critical host-pathogen interactions.

### Example 5  
This embodiment describes a modified urease enzyme containing an insertion at site 8, which produced the most significant and unexpected results. The site 8 modification, created through the same epitope tag insertion strategy, resulted in urease with acid stability similar to wild-type enzyme. While the corresponding H. pylori strain initially colonized mice as efficiently as wild-type bacteria, it showed dramatically reduced persistence during chronic infection (10 months). Bacterial titers in mouse stomachs and anti-H. pylori IgG levels in serum were significantly lower compared to wild-type infections. Notably, the humoral response against the urease B subunit (UreB) was weaker than against wild-type urease, suggesting that the site 8 modification affects immune recognition. The site 8 region is located in a conserved beta-meander turn on the urease surface, and its importance for persistence reveals a previously unrecognized function of urease in maintaining chronic infection.

### Example 6  
The invention further provides methods for using the modified urease enzymes, particularly the site 8 variant, as tools for studying H. pylori persistence mechanisms. In this embodiment, the site 8-modified urease is used to identify host factors interacting with this critical surface region. Comparative analysis of immune responses elicited by wild-type versus site 8-modified H. pylori reveals differences in cytokine profiles and antibody specificities that correlate with persistence. These methods provide valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms by which urease surface properties contribute to chronic infection, facilitating the development of targeted therapies.

### Example 7  
This embodiment describes pharmaceutical compositions containing the modified urease enzymes or fragments thereof for use as vaccines or therapeutic agents. The compositions may include the site 8-modified urease as an immunogen to elicit protective immune responses without inducing factors that promote chronicity. Alternatively, the compositions may contain peptides or mimetics derived from the site 3 or site 8 regions as competitive inhibitors of urease-host interactions. The compositions may be formulated with appropriate adjuvants, carriers, or delivery systems optimized for mucosal immunization against H. pylori.

### Example 8  
The final embodiment provides methods for diagnosing H. pylori infections and predicting disease outcomes based on urease surface properties. These methods utilize antibodies or other detection reagents specific for the site 3 or site 8 regions to assess the persistence potential of clinical H. pylori isolates. The methods may involve detecting polymorphisms in these surface regions that correlate with virulence or treatment resistance, enabling personalized therapeutic approaches. Diagnostic kits incorporating these reagents provide tools for clinicians to stratify patients based on infection prognosis and optimize treatment strategies accordingly.
