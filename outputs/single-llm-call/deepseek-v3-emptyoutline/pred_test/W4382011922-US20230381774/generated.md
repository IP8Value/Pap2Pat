# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of cellular reprogramming and regenerative medicine. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficiency of direct cell reprogramming through modulation of cellular mechanical properties and epigenetic states. The invention discloses novel approaches involving transient nuclear deformation, reduction of intracellular tension, manipulation of cell adhesion, and three-dimensional culture systems to promote open chromatin structures that facilitate cell fate conversion. These methods find particular utility in the generation of induced neurons from somatic cells such as fibroblasts, with applications in regenerative therapies, disease modeling, and drug discovery.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Current approaches to cell reprogramming face significant limitations in efficiency and reliability. While transcription factors and small molecules have been employed to induce cell fate changes, the conversion rates remain suboptimal for therapeutic applications. The mechanical microenvironment of cells, including cytoskeletal tension and cell-matrix interactions, has been increasingly recognized as an important regulator of cell behavior, yet its role in reprogramming processes remains poorly understood. Existing methods fail to adequately address the epigenetic barriers that hinder efficient cell fate conversion, particularly the maintenance of heterochromatin structures that restrict access to lineage-specific genes. There exists an unmet need for improved reprogramming techniques that can overcome these limitations by integrating mechanical and epigenetic modulation strategies. The present invention addresses these deficiencies by providing methods that transiently modify cellular mechanical properties to induce favorable epigenetic states conducive to efficient reprogramming.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides novel methods for enhancing cellular reprogramming efficiency through mechanical modulation of epigenetic states. Key aspects include: 1) transient induction of nuclear deformation to prime chromatin accessibility; 2) reduction of intracellular tension through cytoskeletal modulation to promote open chromatin structures; 3) manipulation of cell adhesion properties to facilitate epigenetic remodeling; and 4) three-dimensional culture systems that optimize mechanical signaling for cell fate conversion. The methods are particularly effective when applied during early stages of reprogramming, where they promote global increases in histone acetylation and H3K4 methylation while reducing heterochromatin marks. This mechanical-epigenetic coupling enables more efficient activation of target lineage genes while suppressing donor cell identity markers. The invention further provides specific compositions and culture conditions for implementing these methods, including small molecule inhibitors, biomaterial substrates, and three-dimensional culture systems optimized for mechano-epigenetic priming. These approaches significantly improve the yield and quality of reprogrammed cells compared to existing methods.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention describes comprehensive methods for enhancing cellular reprogramming through mechanical modulation of epigenetic states. The fundamental principle involves creating transient mechanical perturbations that induce favorable epigenetic remodeling, thereby lowering the barrier for cell fate conversion. The methods are broadly applicable to various reprogramming contexts but are particularly effective for direct conversion of fibroblasts to induced neurons (iNs).

Central to the invention is the discovery that controlled reduction of intracellular tension during early reprogramming stages promotes global chromatin opening through specific mechanisms. Treatment with cytoskeletal-modulating agents such as blebbistatin (a myosin II inhibitor) or Y-27632 (a ROCK inhibitor) during the first 3-5 days of reprogramming induces several beneficial effects: increased histone acetyltransferase (HAT) activity, decreased histone deacetylase (HDAC) activity, elevated H3K4-specific histone methyltransferase (HMT) activity, and reduced histone demethylase (HDM) activity. These enzymatic changes produce a chromatin state characterized by increased AcH3, H3K4me3, and H3K4me1 marks with concurrent decreases in H3K27me3 and H3K9me3 heterochromatin marks.

The invention further discloses that modulation of cell adhesion through focal adhesion kinase (FAK) inhibition produces similar epigenetic benefits. Treatment with FAK inhibitors such as PF573228 reduces phosphorylation of FAK at Tyr-397, decreases cell and nuclear stiffness, and promotes chromatin accessibility at neuronal gene loci. This approach maintains the advantages of cytoskeletal modulation while providing additional benefits through extracellular matrix signaling pathways.

Biomaterial engineering constitutes another key aspect of the invention. Specific substrate topographies and compositions are disclosed that optimally reduce intracellular tension while maintaining essential cell functions. These include polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) membranes with microgroove patterns and binary colloidal crystals (BCCs) with controlled particle sizes. These substrates reduce stress fiber formation and FAK phosphorylation while promoting the desired epigenetic changes.

The methods are particularly effective when combined with transcription factor-mediated reprogramming approaches. For neuronal conversion, optimal results are achieved using combinations of Brn2, Ascl1, and Myt1l (BAM factors), with Ascl1 playing a particularly important role in initiating the mechanical phenotype changes that facilitate subsequent epigenetic remodeling. The mechanical interventions are most beneficial when applied during early reprogramming stages (days 1-5), suggesting they primarily act to overcome initial epigenetic barriers rather than influence later maturation processes.

The invention also provides detailed protocols for assessing reprogramming efficiency and characterizing the resulting cells. Key quality control measures include immunostaining for neuronal markers (TUBB3, NeuN, MAP2, synapsin), electrophysiological analysis of membrane properties, and single-cell RNA sequencing to evaluate transcriptional profiles. These methods ensure that the mechanical interventions enhance reprogramming without compromising the functional properties of the resulting cells.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Transient Nuclear Deformation Primes Epigenetic State and Promotes Cell Reprogramming

This example demonstrates that controlled nuclear deformation during early reprogramming stages induces favorable epigenetic changes that enhance conversion efficiency. Fibroblasts were transduced with BAM factors and subjected to transient mechanical compression using microfluidic constriction devices. Quantitative deformability cytometry revealed that cells exhibited increased stiffness at day 1 post-induction, followed by softening at day 3. This mechanical perturbation induced partial disassembly of nuclear lamina without complete detachment of lamina-associated chromatin domains. The transient deformation led to global increases in active chromatin marks (AcH3, H3K4me3, H3K4me1) and decreases in heterochromatin marks (H3K27me3, H3K9me3). ATAC-seq analysis showed increased accessibility at promoter regions of neuronal genes including Ascl1, Myt1l, and Tubb3. These epigenetic changes correlated with a 4.5-fold increase in reprogramming efficiency compared to non-deformed controls, with the resulting iN cells exhibiting normal electrophysiological properties.

### Example 2: Reduction of Intracellular Tension and Cell Adhesion Promotes Open Chromatin Structure and Enhances Cell Reprogramming

This example illustrates how modulation of cytoskeletal tension and cell adhesion enhances reprogramming through epigenetic mechanisms. Treatment with blebbistatin (10 μM) during days 1-5 of BAM-induced reprogramming reduced cell and nuclear stiffness while decreasing focal adhesion assembly. These mechanical changes were accompanied by increased HAT and H3K4 HMT activity, decreased HDAC and HDM activity, and reduced DNA methyltransferase activity. Chromatin immunoprecipitation revealed increased AcH3, H3K4me3 and H3K4me1 at neuronal gene promoters. Similarly, FAK inhibition with PF573228 (5 μM) produced comparable epigenetic benefits through distinct signaling pathways. Both treatments significantly increased reprogramming efficiency while maintaining normal neuronal functionality, as confirmed by patch-clamp electrophysiology. The mechanical interventions were particularly effective when applied during early reprogramming stages, with treatment during days 1-3 being sufficient to produce maximal benefits.

### Example 3: 3D Spheroid Culture to Increase Cell Reprogramming Efficiency

This example describes three-dimensional culture systems that optimize mechanical signaling for enhanced reprogramming. Fibroblasts transduced with BAM factors were cultured as spheroids in ultra-low attachment plates or within defined hydrogel matrices. Compared to traditional 2D cultures, 3D conditions reduced intracellular tension and promoted more uniform chromatin opening across the cell population. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed more consistent upregulation of neuronal genes in 3D cultures, with particular enhancement of axonogenesis and synaptogenesis pathways. The 3D systems also facilitated better nutrient exchange and paracrine signaling, contributing to an overall 3.2-fold increase in reprogramming efficiency. The resulting iN spheroids exhibited enhanced maturation and functional properties, including more complex neurite outgrowth and higher spontaneous electrical activity.

### Example 4: Mechano-Epigenetic Mapping for Guided Gene Activation and Silencing

This example discloses methods for creating comprehensive mechano-epigenetic maps to guide targeted gene regulation during reprogramming. By integrating ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and RNA-seq data from cells subjected to various mechanical perturbations, we identified specific chromatin accessibility patterns associated with successful reprogramming. These maps revealed that mechanical modulation preferentially opens chromatin at loci controlled by pioneer factors such as Ascl1, while maintaining or increasing repression at fibroblast-specific genes. Using this information, we developed optimized mechanical intervention protocols that combine transient cytoskeletal inhibition (days 1-3) with subsequent FAK inhibition (days 3-5) to sequentially activate neuronal genes and silence fibroblast genes. This staged approach produced a 5.8-fold improvement in reprogramming efficiency compared to standard methods, with particularly strong enhancement of glutamatergic neuron specification.

### Example 5: Matrix Stiffness Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming

This example demonstrates how substrate stiffness influences reprogramming efficiency through mechano-epigenetic mechanisms. Fibroblasts were cultured on polyacrylamide hydrogels with defined elastic moduli ranging from 0.5 kPa to 50 kPa during BAM-induced reprogramming. Intermediate stiffness (1-5 kPa) substrates produced optimal reprogramming outcomes, correlating with balanced cytoskeletal tension that promoted open chromatin states without excessive mechanical stress. These conditions induced global increases in active histone marks while specifically increasing accessibility at neuronal gene loci. Extremely soft (<1 kPa) or stiff (>10 kPa) substrates produced suboptimal epigenetic states and reduced conversion efficiency. The stiffness effects were mediated through differential FAK phosphorylation and cytoskeletal organization, with intermediate stiffness promoting transient actin cage formation that facilitated nuclear remodeling. These findings provide guidelines for designing biomaterials that optimize mechanical signaling for reprogramming applications.

### Example 6: Viscoelasticity of Matrix Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming

This example extends the mechanical modulation approach to include viscoelastic matrix properties. Fibroblasts were cultured on hyaluronic acid-based hydrogels with controlled viscoelastic relaxation times during reprogramming. Matrices with intermediate viscoelasticity (relaxation time ~30 min) produced the most favorable epigenetic changes, including increased H3K4 methylation and reduced DNA methylation. These materials allowed sufficient initial cell adhesion to enable mechanotransduction while subsequently relaxing to reduce sustained intracellular tension. The viscoelastic group showed 4.1-fold higher reprogramming efficiency compared to purely elastic controls, with particularly strong enhancement of GABAergic neuron specification. Time-lapse imaging revealed that the viscoelastic materials promoted dynamic cytoskeletal rearrangements that facilitated periodic nuclear deformation and chromatin remodeling. These findings highlight the importance of considering time-dependent mechanical properties in designing reprogramming substrates.
