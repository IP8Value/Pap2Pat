# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to microfluidic systems and more specifically to integrated flow rate sensors for microfluidic applications. The invention particularly concerns a novel flow rate sensing device comprising a thin silicon nitride membrane integrated with a microwave resonator, capable of detecting both liquid and gas flow rates with high sensitivity. The disclosed technology enables real-time monitoring of flow rates in microfluidic channels through two distinct sensing mechanisms: quasi-static membrane deformation and periodic membrane pulsation. The invention finds particular utility in organ-on-a-chip systems, digital microfluidics, biomedical reactors, and other applications requiring precise flow rate control at microscale dimensions. The sensor architecture provides significant advantages over conventional thermal flow sensors and Coriolis flowmeters by eliminating dependence on fluid heat capacity while maintaining robust performance across varying fluid compositions.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION (PRIOR ART)  

Existing microfluidic flow sensing technologies suffer from several limitations that impede their widespread adoption in precision applications. Thermal flow sensors, while commonly used, exhibit degraded performance when fluid composition changes due to their inherent dependence on the thermal properties of the measured fluid. These sensors require precise knowledge of the fluid's heat capacity and face accuracy challenges when dealing with complex, multi-component fluids where unaccounted heat transfer pathways may exist.  

Coriolis flowmeters represent another class of flow sensing devices that operate by detecting forces perpendicular to the flow direction in an oscillating channel. While these devices offer wider dynamic ranges compared to thermal sensors, they suffer from increased complexity, higher manufacturing costs, and limitations imposed by small fluidic channel diameters. Additionally, the heat induced in the fluid by these active flowmeters can adversely affect sensitive biological samples.  

Passive flow sensing approaches have been explored as alternatives, including capacitive sensors that measure drag forces in microfluidic channels. Cantilever-based sensors with variable-sized holes have demonstrated sensitivity to flow rate changes through correlation with cantilever deflection. However, these designs require the fluid to pass through perforated mechanical structures, which may introduce flow resistance and potential clogging issues. Other electrical flow sensors operating at radio frequencies have been reported, including non-contact designs that measure deflection of polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) layers. While these achieve reasonable sensitivity, the elastomeric nature of PDMS results in long recovery timescales on the order of several minutes, limiting their utility in applications requiring rapid successive measurements.  

The limitations of existing technologies highlight the need for a robust, sensitive flow sensor that combines fast response times with independence from fluid composition. An ideal solution would provide accurate measurements across both liquid and gas phases while maintaining structural integrity under varying flow conditions. The present invention addresses these needs through a novel integration of thin silicon nitride membranes with microwave resonator technology.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a microfluidic flow rate sensor comprising a thin silicon nitride membrane integrated with a microwave resonator for precise flow rate measurement. The sensor operates through two complementary mechanisms: detection of quasi-static membrane deformation and measurement of periodic membrane pulsation frequency.  

In the primary sensing mode, fluid flow induces deformation of a 220 nm thick silicon nitride membrane that forms part of a microfluidic channel. This deformation alters the capacitance of an integrated coplanar waveguide (CPW) microwave resonator by changing the distance between signal and ground electrodes patterned directly on the membrane. The resulting shift in resonance frequency provides a sensitive measure of the applied flow rate. The ceramic nature of the silicon nitride membrane ensures rapid mechanical recovery and durability under varying flow conditions.  

A secondary sensing mechanism emerges under steady flow conditions, where the membrane exhibits periodic pulsations with frequencies that correlate with the flow rate. These pulsations are electronically detectable through phase response changes in the microwave resonator, providing an alternative measurement modality particularly suited for higher flow rates.  

The invention includes several key innovations: (1) use of silicon nitride membranes for their superior mechanical properties compared to elastomeric materials, enabling measurements of both liquid and gas flows through the same architecture; (2) integration of microwave resonators as sensitive transducers of membrane deformation; (3) dual-mode operation combining quasi-static deformation and dynamic pulsation measurements; and (4) a fabrication process that aligns microfluidic channels precisely with membrane-integrated microwave resonators.  

The sensor demonstrates a sensitivity of 0.5 μL/min for liquid flows and maintains linear response across pressure ranges up to 4 bar for gas flows. The thin membrane geometry enables fast response times while the microwave readout provides high signal-to-noise ratio. Multiple device configurations with varying membrane dimensions allow optimization for different flow rate ranges, from sub-μL/min to over 100 μL/min.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The flow rate sensor of the present invention comprises several key components integrated into a unified microfluidic architecture. The central element is a thin silicon nitride membrane, typically 220 nm thick, that forms part of a microfluidic channel wall. The membrane dimensions may vary between approximately 600-1500 μm in length and 300-500 μm in width, with specific geometries selected based on the desired flow rate measurement range.  

Fabrication begins with a wafer substrate consisting of a 500 μm silicon base layer, 2 μm silicon dioxide intermediate layer, and 220 nm silicon nitride top layer. Photolithography defines an etch window on the wafer backside, followed by inductively coupled plasma etching and potassium hydroxide wet etching to form the freestanding membrane. This process creates a robust, ceramic membrane capable of withstanding pressures up to 4 bar while maintaining sufficient flexibility for sensitive flow detection.  

A coplanar waveguide (CPW) microwave resonator is patterned on the membrane surface through photolithographic deposition of 100 nm gold electrodes. The CPW structure features a 50 Ω impedance design matching the electronic measurement system, with a 400 μm gap between signal and ground electrodes that narrows to 20 μm over the membrane region. This geometry creates a localized sensing area where membrane deformation most significantly affects the resonator's capacitance. Changes in electrode spacing due to flow-induced membrane deflection alter the resonator's capacitance, thereby shifting its resonance frequency.  

The microfluidic channel is fabricated separately using SU-8 photoresist molds and polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) casting, typically forming channels 300 μm wide and 150 μm deep. Plasma cleaning enables permanent bonding of the PDMS channel to the silicon nitride membrane chip, with precise alignment ensuring the membrane forms part of the channel wall directly beneath the CPW resonator's active sensing region.  

Electronic measurement employs a phase-locked loop system to track resonance frequency shifts. A custom heterodyne circuit extends the measurement range beyond the limitations of conventional lock-in amplifiers. Phase-sensitive detection locks the resonator phase to 0 degrees, with deviations generating error signals that update the signal generator frequency. This approach provides real-time tracking of resonance shifts with high precision.  

For liquid flow measurements, the sensor demonstrates two distinct operating regimes. At lower flow rates, the membrane exhibits quasi-static deformations proportional to the applied flow, detectable as continuous resonance frequency shifts. At higher flow rates, the membrane enters a pulsation mode where periodic deformations occur at frequencies correlated with the flow rate. The transition between these regimes depends on membrane dimensions and channel geometry, with larger membranes favoring quasi-static response and smaller membranes more readily exhibiting pulsations.  

Gas flow measurements show similar behavior, with linear frequency response to applied pressure in the range of 20 mbar to 1000 mbar. The sensor's ceramic membrane structure enables seamless transition between liquid and gas flow measurements without modification, a significant advantage over conventional thermal sensors.  

Calibration curves relate resonance frequency shifts to absolute flow rates, with different membrane geometries optimized for specific measurement ranges. Smaller membranes (e.g., 0.8 mm × 0.35 mm) provide enhanced sensitivity for low flow rates (0-50 μL/min), while larger membranes (3 mm × 1 mm) accommodate higher flow rates up to 120 μL/min. The pulsation frequency measurement mode extends the dynamic range further, with observed pulse intervals ranging from 14 seconds at 5 μL/min to approximately 0.5 seconds at 120 μL/min.  

The invention's dual sensing mechanisms provide complementary advantages: the quasi-static mode offers continuous measurement capability ideal for low flow rates, while the pulsation mode enables high dynamic range measurements at elevated flow rates. Both modalities benefit from the silicon nitride membrane's fast mechanical response and the microwave resonator's high sensitivity, resulting in a robust flow sensor suitable for diverse microfluidic applications.
