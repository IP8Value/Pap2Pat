# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for modulating skeletal muscle function and metabolism. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP) and its analogs to enhance glucose uptake, mitochondrial activity, and contractile kinetics in skeletal muscle cells. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides and methods of administering such compositions to treat conditions associated with impaired muscle function, such as metabolic disorders, muscle fatigue, and degenerative muscle diseases.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Skeletal muscle is essential for voluntary movement and plays a critical role in glucose metabolism, accounting for approximately 40% of glucose-associated energy requirements and 80% of insulin-stimulated glucose uptake. Impaired muscle function is a hallmark of metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes, where reduced glucose uptake and mitochondrial dysfunction contribute to muscle weakness and fatigue. Current therapeutic strategies, such as insulin sensitizers and glucose-lowering agents, often fail to address the underlying metabolic deficiencies in skeletal muscle.  

The teneurin family of proteins and their associated peptides, particularly TCAP, have been implicated in neuronal glucose metabolism and cytoskeletal regulation. TCAP, derived from the extracellular domain of teneurins, interacts with latrophilin receptors (LPHNs) to modulate intracellular calcium flux and energy metabolism. However, the role of TCAP in skeletal muscle physiology has remained unexplored until now.  

There exists a need for novel therapeutic agents that directly enhance skeletal muscle glucose uptake, mitochondrial function, and contractile efficiency without relying on insulin-dependent pathways. The present invention addresses this need by demonstrating that TCAP-1 activates a unique signaling cascade in skeletal muscle, leading to improved energy availability and muscle performance.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for enhancing skeletal muscle function through the administration of TCAP peptides. The invention is based on the discovery that TCAP-1 increases glucose uptake, mitochondrial activity, and calcium dynamics in skeletal muscle cells via interactions with latrophilin receptors (LPHN-1 and LPHN-3).  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, to stimulate glucose transporter (GLUT4) translocation and glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells.  
2. The modulation of intracellular calcium flux through the phospholipase C (PLC)-inositol triphosphate (IP3) pathway, leading to enhanced mitochondrial respiration and ATP production.  
3. The improvement of skeletal muscle contractile kinetics, including fatigue resistance and relaxation rates, through TCAP-mediated metabolic regulation.  
4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides formulated for systemic or localized delivery to skeletal muscle tissue.  
5. Methods of treating metabolic disorders, muscle fatigue, and degenerative muscle conditions by administering TCAP peptides.  

The invention further encompasses analogs and derivatives of TCAP-1 that retain its biological activity, including modified peptides with enhanced stability, bioavailability, or receptor specificity.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms have the following meanings:  

- **TCAP (Teneurin C-terminal Associated Peptide):** A peptide derived from the C-terminal extracellular region of teneurin proteins, particularly TCAP-1, which corresponds to the distal tip of teneurin-1. The peptide may be synthetically produced or naturally cleaved from teneurin precursors.  
- **LPHN (Latrophilin):** A family of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), including LPHN-1 and LPHN-3, which interact with TCAP to mediate intracellular signaling.  
- **GLUT4:** Insulin-sensitive glucose transporter protein predominantly expressed in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.  
- **IP3-DAG Pathway:** A signaling cascade involving phospholipase C (PLC)-mediated generation of inositol triphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG), leading to calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.  
- **Mitochondrial Membrane Potential:** The electrochemical gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which drives ATP synthesis.  

### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells  

The invention demonstrates that TCAP-1 enhances skeletal muscle energy metabolism through multiple mechanisms:  

1. **Glucose Uptake and Utilization:** TCAP-1 stimulates GLUT4 translocation to the sarcolemma, increasing glucose import independent of insulin. This is evidenced by elevated ³H-2-deoxyglucose uptake in TCAP-1-treated C2C12 myotubes.  
2. **Calcium Signaling:** TCAP-1 activates LPHN-1 and LPHN-3, triggering PLC-mediated IP3 production and calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. This calcium flux enhances mitochondrial respiration and ATP synthesis.  
3. **Mitochondrial Activation:** TCAP-1 increases mitochondrial calcium uptake, depolarizing the mitochondrial membrane and stimulating the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. This is reflected in elevated succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) activity and NADH production.  
4. **Contractile Efficiency:** By improving calcium handling and ATP availability, TCAP-1 enhances muscle contraction kinetics, including fatigue resistance and relaxation rates, as demonstrated in vivo in rodent models.  

These effects make TCAP-1 a promising therapeutic agent for conditions characterized by skeletal muscle dysfunction, such as type 2 diabetes, muscular dystrophy, and age-related sarcopenia.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Modes of Administration  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP-1 or its analogs formulated for systemic or localized delivery. Suitable formulations include:  

1. **Injectable Solutions:** TCAP-1 dissolved in saline or buffered solutions for subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intravenous administration.  
2. **Sustained-Release Formulations:** Polymeric microspheres or liposomes encapsulating TCAP-1 to prolong peptide activity.  
3. **Topical Preparations:** Creams or gels containing TCAP-1 for transdermal delivery to targeted muscle groups.  
4. **Oral Formulations:** Enteric-coated tablets or capsules incorporating TCAP-1 analogs resistant to gastrointestinal degradation.  

Dosage regimens may vary depending on the condition being treated, with typical doses ranging from 1–100 nmol/kg body weight. Administration frequency may range from daily to weekly, based on the desired therapeutic outcome.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis  

TCAP-1 (pE-Gly-Leu-Pro-Gly-Ser-Leu-Leu-Ser-Gln-Arg-Leu-Gln-Gly-Leu-Arg-NH₂) and scrambled TCAP-1 (pE-Leu-Gly-Ser-Pro-Leu-Gln-Gly-Leu-Ser-Leu-Arg-Gln-Leu-Arg-Gly-NH₂) were synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis at >95% purity. Peptides were acetylated at the N-terminus and amidated at the C-terminus to enhance stability. Purification was performed using reverse-phase HPLC, and identity was confirmed by mass spectrometry.  

### Example 2—In-Vivo Experiments  

Male Sprague-Dawley rats (250–350 g) were administered TCAP-1 (10–25 nmol/kg) or vehicle via subcutaneous injection. Muscle function was assessed using electrical stimulation of the tibialis anterior (TA) muscle. TCAP-1-treated animals exhibited improved contraction velocity (p < 0.05) and fatigue resistance compared to controls.  

### Example 3—Effect of TCAP on Blood Glucose Levels  

Wistar rats treated with TCAP-1 (10 nmol/kg) showed a significant reduction in serum glucose levels (p < 0.01) 3 days post-administration, as measured by enzymatic assays. Functional PET imaging confirmed increased ¹⁸F-2-deoxyglucose uptake in hind-limb muscle tissue.  

### Example 4—Glucose, Insulin, and Glucagon Changes in Blood  

TCAP-1 administration in rats led to decreased serum insulin levels (p < 0.05) without altering glucagon concentrations, suggesting an insulin-independent mechanism of glucose uptake.  

### Example 5—Liver and Muscle Glycogen Content in Extracted Liver of TCAP-1 Treated Wistar Rats  

Glycogen content in TA muscle increased by 30% (p < 0.01) in TCAP-1-treated rats, while liver glycogen remained unchanged, indicating skeletal muscle-specific metabolic effects.  

### Example 6—Haematology Assays  

Complete blood counts and serum biochemistry revealed no adverse effects of TCAP-1 treatment, supporting its safety profile.  

### Example 7—Localization of TCAP in Muscle Tissue  

Immunohistochemistry demonstrated TCAP-1 co-localization with β-dystroglycan at the sarcolemma, confirming its interaction with muscle cell membranes.  

### Example 8—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to β-Dystroglycan in Skeletal Cells  

Confocal microscopy revealed TCAP-1 binding at discrete nodes along the sarcolemma, overlapping with β-dystroglycan staining.  

### Example 9—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to Rapsyn in Skeletal Cells  

TCAP-1 was observed near rapsyn clusters, suggesting a role in neuromuscular junction regulation.  

The invention is further supported by in vitro data showing TCAP-1-induced calcium flux, GLUT4 translocation, and mitochondrial activation in C2C12 myotubes. These findings collectively establish TCAP-1 as a novel therapeutic agent for enhancing skeletal muscle function and metabolism.
