# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Biofouling presents a significant challenge in industrial water treatment systems, particularly in membrane-based technologies such as reverse osmosis, microfiltration, and ultrafiltration. The accumulation of microorganisms on membrane surfaces leads to the formation of biofilms, which reduce membrane permeability, increase energy consumption, and necessitate frequent cleaning or replacement. Traditional methods for mitigating biofouling, including chemical biocides (e.g., chlorine, ozone) and physical cleaning, suffer from drawbacks such as the production of harmful byproducts, incomplete removal of microbial colonies, and the potential for bacterial regrowth.  

Membrane surface modification has emerged as a promising strategy to combat biofouling by altering surface properties to resist microbial adhesion. Hydrophilic and negatively charged surfaces have been shown to reduce bacterial attachment due to electrostatic repulsion and the formation of a hydration layer that prevents microbial adhesion. However, existing modifications often rely on synthetic additives, which may introduce toxicity or instability.  

Arabic gum (AG), a natural polysaccharide derived from Acacia species, has demonstrated potential as an additive in membrane fabrication due to its hydrophilic and negatively charged functional groups. Prior studies have explored AG's role in enhancing membrane porosity and hydrophilicity but have been limited to specific bacterial strains or lacked comprehensive characterization of its anti-biofouling mechanisms.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a novel polysulfone (PS) membrane modified with Arabic gum (AG) for enhanced resistance to biofouling. The membrane comprises a PS polymer matrix blended with AG at loadings ranging from 0.1 wt.% to 7 wt.%, with optimal performance observed at 3 wt.% and 7 wt.% AG. The incorporation of AG significantly improves membrane properties, including:  

1. **Enhanced Hydrophilicity**: AG's hydrophilic polysaccharide subunits reduce the water contact angle, promoting wettability and forming a hydration layer that inhibits bacterial adhesion.  
2. **Increased Negative Surface Charge**: The carboxylic groups in AG contribute to a highly negative zeta potential, repelling negatively charged bacterial cells.  
3. **Improved Porosity and Flux**: AG accelerates solvent-nonsolvent demixing during membrane fabrication, resulting in a porous structure with interconnected channels that enhance water permeability.  

The membrane exhibits broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against both Gram-positive (e.g., *Staphylococcus aureus*) and Gram-negative (e.g., *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) bacteria. Unlike bactericidal agents, the membrane operates via anti-adhesive mechanisms, preventing biofilm formation without damaging bacterial cells. This makes it suitable for long-term use in water treatment applications without promoting resistance.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Membrane Fabrication  

The PS/AG membranes are fabricated using a phase inversion technique. A casting solution is prepared by dissolving PS (16 wt.%) in *N,N*-dimethylacetamide (DMA) (84 wt.%), followed by the addition of AG at varying concentrations (0.1–7 wt.%). The solution is sonicated and stirred to ensure homogeneous dispersion of AG, degassed to remove air bubbles, and cast onto a glass plate using a knife gap height of 200 µm. The cast film is immersed in a deionized water coagulation bath, where solvent-nonsolvent exchange induces membrane precipitation. The resulting membrane is washed and stored in deionized water.  

### Structural and Functional Characterization  

1. **Morphology**: Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) reveals that AG incorporation increases surface porosity and macrovoid formation in the membrane cross-section. At 3 wt.% AG, the membrane exhibits optimal pore connectivity and uniformity.  
2. **Porosity and Pore Size**: Gravimetric analysis shows a 24% increase in porosity for membranes with 3 wt.% AG compared to neat PS. Pore size, calculated using the Guerout-Elford-Ferry equation, peaks at 39 nm for 3 wt.% AG.  
3. **Hydrophilicity**: Water contact angle measurements demonstrate a 45% reduction in hydrophobicity at 7 wt.% AG, attributed to the orientation of hydrophilic AG residues at the membrane surface.  
4. **Surface Charge**: Zeta potential measurements confirm a 105% increase in negative charge at pH 7 for 7 wt.% AG membranes, due to the dissociation of AG's carboxylic groups.  

### Anti-Biofouling Performance  

The membrane's efficacy is evaluated using bacterial adhesion assays with *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, and *P. aeruginosa*. Key findings include:  

- **Gram-Negative Bacteria**: Membranes with 3 wt.% and 7 wt.% AG reduce bacterial colonization by >90% compared to neat PS. FESEM images show intact bacterial cells, confirming anti-adhesive (non-bactericidal) action.  
- **Gram-Positive Bacteria**: *S. aureus* exhibits partial resistance, likely due to localized AG agglomeration or membrane heterogeneity.  
- **Long-Term Stability**: Membranes incubated overnight with *E. coli* maintain anti-adhesive properties, with no significant increase in bacterial attachment.  

### Mechanisms of Action  

The membrane's anti-biofouling performance arises from synergistic effects:  

1. **Hydration Layer Formation**: Hydrophilicity promotes a bound water layer that physically blocks bacterial adhesion.  
2. **Electrostatic Repulsion**: Negative surface charge repels similarly charged bacterial cells.  
3. **Morphological Advantages**: Porous structures limit biofilm maturation by reducing surface area available for colonization.  

### Applications  

The PS/AG membrane is suitable for:  

- Drinking water purification  
- Wastewater treatment  
- Seawater desalination  
- Industrial cooling systems  

Its natural composition and non-toxic mode of action make it environmentally preferable to conventional biocidal membranes.
