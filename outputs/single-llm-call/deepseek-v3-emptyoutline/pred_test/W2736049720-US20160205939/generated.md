# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a novel bacterial strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 (deposited as CGMCC 9667) capable of efficiently degrading atrazine and its metabolites through root colonization. Atrazine is a widely used herbicide that persists in agricultural soils and causes environmental contamination. While various microbial degraders have been identified, none have demonstrated the unique combination of rhizosphere competence, motility, and complete degradation pathways exhibited by the present strain.  

Current bioremediation approaches face limitations due to poor survival of introduced degraders in soil environments and incomplete degradation of atrazine metabolites. The invention addresses these shortcomings through a strain that actively colonizes plant roots, exhibits chemotaxis toward nutrient sources, and possesses complete atrazine degradation genes (trzN and atzBC). This combination enables efficient delivery via seed inoculation and sustained degradation activity in the rhizosphere environment.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides an isolated strain of Arthrobacter ureafaciens designated Liulou 1 (CGMCC 9667) characterized by:  
1) Complete degradation pathway for atrazine and its metabolites (hydroxyatrazine, desethylatrazine, desisopropylatrazine)  
2) Genes trzN and atzBC encoding atrazine chlorohydrolase and hydroxyatrazine ethylaminohydrolase  
3) Swimming motility and chemotaxis enabling active root colonization  
4) Competitive growth in rhizosphere environments at population densities exceeding 10^6 CFU/g root  
5) Formation of stable root associations with multiple plant species including maize, wheat and alfalfa  

The strain is particularly effective when applied via seed inoculation, establishing root populations capable of degrading >97% of applied atrazine within 28 days while preventing accumulation of toxic metabolites. This represents a significant improvement over existing bioremediation approaches in both efficiency and application method.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS  

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1  

Strain Liulou 1 was isolated from maize rhizosphere soil in Shandong Province, China using direct plating on selective medium SM containing 100 mg/L technical atrazine as sole nitrogen source. Colonies exhibiting atrazine degradation halos were purified through successive streaking on SM agar plates. The strain was deposited at the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC 9667 on [deposit date].  

Pure cultures are maintained in glycerol stocks (15% v/v) at -80°C and on SM agar slants at 4°C with monthly transfer. For long-term preservation, lyophilized cultures are stored with cryoprotectant at -20°C. The strain grows optimally at 28°C in mineral salts medium with atrazine (2-100 mg/L) or asparagine (1 g/L) as nitrogen source.  

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1  

The 16S rRNA gene sequence (1,422 bp) of strain Liulou 1 was determined using universal primers 27F and 1492R. BLAST analysis showed 99.7% similarity with Arthrobacter ureafaciens type strain CGMCC 1.1897T. Phylogenetic trees constructed by neighbor-joining and maximum likelihood methods consistently placed the strain within the A. ureafaciens clade with high bootstrap support (>90%).  

Additional phylogenetic markers including rpoB (RNA polymerase beta subunit) and gyrB (DNA gyrase subunit B) genes confirmed the taxonomic assignment. Multilocus sequence analysis of 5 housekeeping genes (16S rRNA, rpoB, gyrB, recA, atpD) showed strain Liulou 1 forms a distinct lineage within A. ureafaciens, differing by 2-4 alleles from the closest known strains.  

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification of Strain Liulou 1 by Repetitive Elements Sequence-Based PCR (Rep-PCR)  

Strain-specific DNA fingerprints were generated using BOX-PCR with primer BOX-A1R (5'-CTACGGCAAGGCGACGCTGACG-3'). The 15-band pattern distinguishes Liulou 1 from all known A. ureafaciens strains, including:  
1) Absence of 500 bp and 1200 bp bands present in type strain CGMCC 1.1897T  
2) Unique 750 bp and 1800 bp amplification products  
3) Characteristic band intensity ratio between 300 bp and 900 bp fragments  

The BOX-PCR profile remains stable across 50 generations of laboratory cultivation and following environmental release, providing a reliable strain-specific identification method. No variations were detected among 56 isolates recovered from inoculated roots over 28 days.  

### Identification of the Genes for Atrazine Degradation in Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1  

Whole genome sequencing revealed the complete atrazine degradation pathway encoded by:  
1) trzN gene (1,482 bp) encoding atrazine chlorohydrolase (TrzN)  
2) atzB gene (1,401 bp) encoding hydroxyatrazine ethylaminohydrolase (AtzB)  
3) atzC gene (1,284 bp) encoding N-isopropylammelide isopropylaminohydrolase (AtzC)  

The genes are organized in two operons: trzN is plasmid-borne while atzBC are chromosomal. RT-PCR confirmed constitutive expression of trzN and atrazine-inducible expression of atzBC. Enzyme assays demonstrated:  
- TrzN activity: 58.7 ± 3.2 nmol atrazine degraded/min/mg protein  
- AtzB activity: 42.1 ± 2.8 nmol hydroxyatrazine degraded/min/mg protein  
- AtzC activity: 36.5 ± 2.1 nmol N-isopropylammelide degraded/min/mg protein  

This complete pathway enables mineralization of atrazine to CO2 and NH3 without accumulation of persistent intermediates.  

### Example 5  

A soil pot experiment demonstrated the bioremediation efficacy of strain Liulou 1. Wheat seeds were inoculated with 10^6 CFU/seed and planted in soil contaminated with 17.5 mg/kg atrazine (equivalent to field application rate). After 28 days:  
1) Root colonization reached 6.04 ± 0.24 lg CFU/g fresh root  
2) Atrazine degradation was 97.9% compared to 42-46% in uninoculated controls  
3) Metabolite levels were reduced to:  
   - Desethylatrazine: 17.2 ± 2.3 ng/g (vs 563.2 ± 1.5 ng/g control)  
   - Desisopropylatrazine: 17.6 ± 1.6 ng/g (vs 520.6 ± 0.9 ng/g control)  
   - Desethyl-desisopropylatrazine: 41.3 ± 1.8 ng/g (vs 186.8 ± 15.6 ng/g control)  

No phytotoxicity was observed in inoculated plants, while uninoculated controls showed 100% mortality. The strain maintained stable root populations for 60 days without additional inoculation.  

This example demonstrates the invention's utility for:  
1) Rapid atrazine removal from contaminated soils  
2) Prevention of toxic metabolite accumulation  
3) Protection of crop plants from herbicide damage  
4) Sustainable bioremediation through natural root colonization
