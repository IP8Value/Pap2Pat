## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to virology and immunology, and more specifically to methods and compositions for treating or preventing Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection. The invention particularly concerns the identification of Liver/Lymph Node-Specific Intercellular Adhesion Molecule-3 Grabbing Non-integrin (L-SIGN) as a novel receptor for SARS-CoV-2 and its role in Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19)-associated coagulopathy. The invention further provides therapeutic strategies targeting the interaction between SARS-CoV-2 and L-SIGN to prevent or treat COVID-19 complications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), has resulted in a global pandemic with significant morbidity and mortality. While the majority of infected individuals remain asymptomatic, symptomatic patients exhibit a wide spectrum of clinical manifestations ranging from viral pneumonia and acute respiratory distress syndrome to multiorgan dysfunction. A particularly severe complication observed in critically ill COVID-19 patients is coagulopathy and thrombosis, which are associated with poor prognosis and increased mortality.  

Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) has been identified as the primary receptor mediating SARS-CoV-2 entry into host cells. However, ACE2 expression is notably absent in vascular endothelial cells, despite evidence of endothelial injury and activation in severe COVID-19 cases. This discrepancy has left unresolved the mechanisms by which SARS-CoV-2 contributes to endothelial dysfunction and the associated coagulopathy observed in severe COVID-19.  

Liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs) are known to express high levels of L-SIGN, a C-type lectin receptor that mediates pathogen recognition through Ca²⁺-dependent binding to glycans. LSECs are particularly significant as they serve as major sources of procoagulant proteins, including von Willebrand factor (vWF) and coagulation factor VIII (FVIII). The potential role of L-SIGN in SARS-CoV-2 infection and its contribution to COVID-19-associated coagulopathy has remained unexplored prior to the present invention.  

There exists an unmet need in the art for understanding alternative mechanisms of SARS-CoV-2 infection beyond ACE2-mediated entry, particularly those that may contribute to the coagulopathy observed in severe COVID-19 cases. Furthermore, there is a critical need for novel therapeutic strategies targeting these alternative infection pathways to prevent or treat COVID-19 complications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides the seminal discovery that L-SIGN serves as a functional receptor for SARS-CoV-2, mediating viral entry into liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs). This discovery explains previously unexplained observations of endothelial dysfunction in COVID-19 patients despite the absence of ACE2 expression in vascular endothelial cells.  

The invention demonstrates that L-SIGN binds to the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in a high-mannose-type N-glycan- and Ca²⁺-dependent manner. This interaction occurs specifically through recognition of N-glycosylation sites on the spike protein, particularly at Asn343. The invention further shows that authentic SARS-CoV-2 virus can infect L-SIGN-expressing LSECs, leading to endothelial activation characterized by increased expression of procoagulant factors vWF and FVIII.  

Based on these discoveries, the invention provides several therapeutic approaches for preventing or treating COVID-19-associated coagulopathy, including:  
1) Inhibition of L-SIGN binding to SARS-CoV-2 spike protein using anti-L-SIGN antibodies  
2) Blockade of mannose-dependent interactions using mannan or other high-mannose glycan inhibitors  
3) Competitive inhibition using soluble L-SIGN/Fc recombinant proteins  
4) Targeting of specific N-glycosylation sites on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein  

The invention further provides diagnostic methods for identifying patients at risk for severe COVID-19 complications based on L-SIGN expression levels and associated markers of endothelial activation. Additionally, the invention provides animal models for studying COVID-19-associated coagulopathy by engineering L-SIGN expression in appropriate cell types.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the groundbreaking discovery that L-SIGN serves as a functional receptor for SARS-CoV-2, mediating viral entry into specific endothelial cell populations and contributing to COVID-19-associated coagulopathy. This detailed description provides specific embodiments of the invention, including experimental evidence supporting the discovery and various applications thereof.  

**L-SIGN as a SARS-CoV-2 Receptor**  
The invention demonstrates through multiple experimental approaches that L-SIGN binds to the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and mediates viral entry. Lentivirus-based SARS-CoV-2 pseudo-typed virus specifically infected cells expressing L-SIGN, with infection efficiency correlating with L-SIGN expression levels. This infection was distinct from ACE2-mediated entry, as demonstrated by differential inhibition profiles.  

The binding interaction between L-SIGN and the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein was shown to be dependent on high-mannose-type N-glycans present on the spike protein, particularly at Asn343. Glycan structure analysis revealed that high-mannose-type N-glycans were frequent on the spike protein's receptor-binding domain (RBD). Mutation of Asn343 to glutamine (SpikeN343Q/Fc) abolished L-SIGN binding while maintaining ACE2 binding, demonstrating the specificity of this interaction.  

The Ca²⁺-dependence of this interaction was confirmed through inhibition studies using the Ca²⁺ chelator EGTA. Furthermore, the essential role of the EPN motif in L-SIGN's extracellular domain was demonstrated through mutagenesis studies, where substitution of this motif abolished spike protein binding and pseudo-viral infection.  

**Tissue-Specific Expression of L-SIGN**  
Immunohistochemical analysis of human tissues revealed that L-SIGN is highly expressed on LSECs in the liver and on LECs in lymph nodes, but is absent from other vascular endothelial cells. This specific expression pattern explains the observed tropism of SARS-CoV-2 for these cell types and their potential contribution to COVID-19 pathogenesis.  

Notably, ACE2 was not detected in these L-SIGN-positive cells, confirming that L-SIGN mediates an alternative entry pathway independent of ACE2. Flow cytometric analysis of various endothelial cell lines confirmed that L-SIGN expression is restricted to specific endothelial cell populations and is not induced by inflammatory cytokines in other endothelial cell types.  

**Authentic SARS-CoV-2 Infection via L-SIGN**  
The invention provides critical evidence that authentic SARS-CoV-2 virus can infect L-SIGN-expressing LSECs. Immunostaining of liver autopsy samples from COVID-19 patients revealed SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein within LSECs, accompanied by increased L-SIGN expression compared to uninfected controls.  

In vitro infection experiments demonstrated that authentic SARS-CoV-2 infection of L-SIGN-transduced LSECs was dose-dependently inhibited by mannan, confirming the mannose-dependent nature of this entry mechanism. Unlike ACE2-mediated infection, L-SIGN-mediated infection did not result in productive viral replication, suggesting distinct intracellular trafficking pathways.  

**Role in COVID-19-Associated Coagulopathy**  
The invention provides mechanistic insight into how L-SIGN-mediated SARS-CoV-2 infection contributes to coagulopathy. LSECs from COVID-19 patient livers showed significantly increased expression of vWF and a trend toward increased FVIII expression compared to controls. These procoagulant factors are known to be major products of LSECs and their upregulation is consistent with the hypercoagulable state observed in severe COVID-19.  

The invention further demonstrates that LSECs from COVID-19 patients exhibited reduced expression of Lyve-1, a marker of endothelial cell activation. Single-cell RNA-sequencing analysis confirmed co-expression of L-SIGN (CLEC4M) with genes encoding key coagulation factors (vWF, F8, PLAT) in LSECs, providing additional support for their role in COVID-19-associated coagulopathy.  

**Therapeutic Applications**  
Based on these discoveries, the invention provides several therapeutic strategies:  

1) **Antibody-mediated inhibition**: Anti-L-SIGN monoclonal antibodies were shown to dose-dependently inhibit pseudo-typed SARS-CoV-2 infection of L-SIGN-expressing cells without affecting control virus infection.  

2) **Mannose-dependent inhibition**: Mannan, a β-mannose polymer that blocks high-mannose-type N-glycan interactions, effectively inhibited both pseudo-typed and authentic SARS-CoV-2 infection of L-SIGN-expressing cells.  

3) **Soluble receptor inhibition**: Recombinant L-SIGN/Fc protein competitively inhibited viral infection by binding to spike protein glycans, preventing viral attachment to cell-surface L-SIGN.  

4) **Glycosylation-targeted approaches**: The identification of specific N-glycosylation sites (particularly Asn343) critical for L-SIGN binding provides targets for glycan-modifying therapies.  

**Diagnostic Applications**  
The invention provides diagnostic methods for assessing COVID-19 severity risk based on:  
1) L-SIGN expression levels in relevant tissues  
2) Circulating markers of LSEC activation (vWF, FVIII)  
3) Genetic polymorphisms affecting L-SIGN expression or function  

**Animal Models**  
The invention provides engineered animal models for studying COVID-19-associated coagulopathy by introducing human L-SIGN expression in appropriate cell types, particularly LSECs and LECs. Such models are particularly valuable given that current animal models of SARS-CoV-2 infection fail to recapitulate the coagulopathy observed in human COVID-19 patients.
