# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present disclosure relates to recombinant immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) protease polypeptides, derivatives thereof, and methods of producing such polypeptides. More particularly, the disclosure pertains to isolated IgA1 protease domains that exhibit proteolytic activity against human IgA1, pharmaceutical compositions comprising such proteases, and methods of using these proteases for the treatment of IgA deposition disorders such as IgA nephropathy (IgAN). The invention further encompasses optimized recombinant expression systems for producing soluble and active IgA1 protease domains in bacterial host cells, particularly Escherichia coli, at significantly higher yields compared to prior methods.  

## BACKGROUND  

Immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) proteases are bacterial enzymes that specifically cleave the hinge region of human IgA1 and certain great ape IgA1 molecules. These proteases exhibit strict substrate specificity, as they do not cleave human IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from other species such as mice, rats, or monkeys. IgA1 proteases are classified into at least three structural families based on their catalytic mechanisms: serine-type, metallo-type, and cysteine-type proteases.  

Serine-type IgA1 proteases, produced by pathogens such as Haemophilus influenzae and Neisseria species, are initially synthesized as precursor proteins comprising four domains: (i) an N-terminal signal peptide, (ii) a protease domain, (iii) an α-protein domain, and (iv) a β-core domain. The mature protease is released through autoproteolytic cleavage events. Metallo-type IgA1 proteases, produced by Streptococcus species, contain metal ion-binding sites critical for their activity. The cysteine-type IgA1 proteases from Prevotella species represent a third structural class.  

IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is characterized by pathological deposition of IgA1 antibodies in kidney glomeruli, leading to renal dysfunction and potential kidney failure. Current therapeutic approaches have explored the use of exogenous proteases to cleave deposited IgA1; however, non-specific proteases such as chymopapain and subtilisin lack specificity for IgA1 and may cause undesirable proteolysis of other proteins. While IgA1-specific proteases from H. influenzae have shown promise in animal models, their therapeutic application has been limited by extremely low natural production yields in pathogenic bacterial hosts and technical challenges in recombinant expression.  

Previous attempts to express recombinant IgA1 proteases in E. coli have resulted in insoluble inclusion bodies, inactive enzymes, or extremely low yields (typically <2 mg/L). These limitations stem from attempts to express the full-length precursor protein, which requires complex processing to generate the active protease domain. There remains an unmet need for efficient production methods yielding sufficient quantities of soluble, active IgA1 protease for therapeutic applications.  

## SUMMARY  

The present disclosure provides solutions to the aforementioned limitations through novel recombinant IgA1 protease constructs and optimized expression methods. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Isolated IgA1 protease polypeptides consisting essentially of the protease domain from bacterial IgA1 proteases (particularly from H. influenzae), lacking the α-protein and β-core domains present in native precursors.  

2. Recombinant expression constructs encoding these minimal protease domains, optionally including signal peptides for periplasmic localization.  

3. Optimized bacterial expression systems, particularly in E. coli strain C41(DE3), yielding soluble and active IgA1 protease at unprecedented levels (approximately 100 mg/L total soluble protein, with 20-40 mg/L after purification).  

4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinant IgA1 protease domains for treatment of IgA deposition disorders.  

5. Methods of treating IgA nephropathy by administering therapeutic doses of the recombinant IgA1 proteases to cleave pathogenic IgA1 deposits in the kidneys.  

The disclosed methods overcome previous limitations by expressing only the essential protease domain without the ancillary domains required in native bacterial systems. This approach eliminates the need for complex autoprocessing while maintaining full proteolytic activity. The recombinant proteases exhibit specific cleavage of human IgA1 at levels comparable or superior to native enzymes (approximately 46 ng/μL/min per ng of protease). The high-yield production enables practical therapeutic applications that were previously impossible due to supply limitations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

As used throughout this disclosure, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"IgA1 protease polypeptide" refers to a polypeptide comprising at least the proteolytically active domain of a bacterial IgA1 protease, capable of specifically cleaving human IgA1 in the hinge region.  

"Derivative" in reference to IgA1 protease polypeptides includes variants having at least 80% sequence identity to a native IgA1 protease domain while retaining proteolytic activity, as well as chemically modified forms (e.g., PEGylated variants) with enhanced pharmaceutical properties.  

"IgA deposition disorder" encompasses pathological conditions characterized by abnormal accumulation of IgA1 molecules in tissues, including but not limited to IgA nephropathy, Henoch-Schönlein purpura, and IgA-associated dermatoses.  

"Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier" includes any non-toxic solvent, dispersion medium, coating, antibacterial or antifungal agent, isotonic agent, absorption delaying agent, or other compatible substance for pharmaceutical administration.  

### IgA Protease Polypeptides  

The IgA1 protease polypeptides of the invention comprise the proteolytically active domain from bacterial IgA1 proteases, particularly the serine-type proteases from H. influenzae (GenBank: X59800). The polypeptides typically consist of approximately 100 kDa proteins corresponding to residues 1-1002 of the H. influenzae IgA1 protease precursor (signal peptide removed), or functional fragments thereof retaining proteolytic activity.  

Key structural features include:  
- The catalytic triad characteristic of serine proteases (Ser-His-Asp)  
- Substrate-binding pockets conferring specificity for human IgA1 hinge regions  
- Absence of the C-terminal α-protein and β-core domains present in native precursors  

The polypeptides may include N-terminal signal peptides (e.g., the native H. influenzae signal sequence or heterologous signal sequences) for periplasmic localization in expression hosts. Alternatively, cytoplasmic expression constructs lacking signal peptides are also effective.  

### Derivatives of IgA Protease Polypeptides  

The invention encompasses derivatives of native IgA1 protease domains that maintain therapeutic efficacy while potentially improving pharmaceutical properties. Such derivatives include:  

1. Sequence variants with conservative amino acid substitutions (e.g., ≤20% sequence variation) that preserve the catalytic activity and IgA1 specificity.  

2. Fusion proteins combining the protease domain with heterologous domains such as Fc regions, albumin-binding domains, or purification tags (e.g., His-tags).  

3. Covalently modified forms, including PEGylated derivatives with extended serum half-life or glycosylated variants with enhanced solubility.  

4. Multimeric forms (e.g., dimeric or tetrameric complexes) with increased avidity for IgA1 substrates.  

Activity of derivatives can be confirmed using the Western blot or Experion-based activity assays described herein, requiring at least 50% of the activity of native protease domains.  

### IgA Deposition Disorders  

The recombinant IgA1 proteases are particularly suited for treating IgA nephropathy (Berger's disease), where glomerular IgA1 deposits lead to inflammation and renal damage. Other applicable disorders include:  

- Henoch-Schönlein purpura (IgA vasculitis)  
- IgA-associated dermatoses (e.g., dermatitis herpetiformis)  
- IgA-related hepatic disorders  
- IgA-mediated pulmonary conditions  

The proteases may be administered systemically (e.g., intravenous infusion) or locally (e.g., renal artery infusion) at doses sufficient to cleave pathogenic IgA1 deposits without significant off-target effects. Typical therapeutic regimens involve periodic administration (e.g., weekly to monthly) to maintain reduced IgA1 deposition.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions of IgA Proteases and Methods of Using IgA Proteases  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. Exemplary formulations include:  

- Lyophilized preparations for reconstitution in sterile saline  
- Aqueous solutions containing stabilizing agents (e.g., glycerol, sucrose)  
- Controlled-release formulations (e.g., microspheres, liposomes)  

Administration methods include:  
1. Intravenous infusion for systemic delivery  
2. Targeted delivery to kidneys via renal artery catheterization  
3. Subcutaneous injection for prolonged release  

Dosages typically range from 0.1-10 mg/kg body weight, adjusted based on disease severity and therapeutic response. Treatment efficacy is monitored through renal function tests and reduction in proteinuria.  

### Representative Embodiments of the Disclosure  

1. An isolated polypeptide consisting of residues 1-1002 of H. influenzae IgA1 protease (mature protease domain) with a C-terminal His-tag, having the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO:1.  

2. A recombinant expression vector comprising a nucleic acid encoding the polypeptide of embodiment 1 operably linked to a T7 promoter, particularly plasmid pET-IGAN.  

3. A method of producing soluble IgA1 protease comprising:  
   a) Transforming E. coli C41(DE3) with the vector of embodiment 2  
   b) Culturing at 20°C in LB medium containing carbenicillin  
   c) Inducing expression with 0.4 mM IPTG for 24 hours  
   d) Recovering soluble IgA1 protease from cell lysates  

4. A pharmaceutical composition comprising the polypeptide of embodiment 1 at 5-50 mg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline, pH 7.4.  

5. A method of treating IgA nephropathy comprising administering the composition of embodiment 4 intravenously at 1 mg/kg weekly for 12 weeks.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Construction of IgA1 Protease Expression Vectors**  

DNA fragments encoding the H. influenzae IgA1 protease domain (residues 1-1002) were amplified by PCR from plasmid pFG26 using primers incorporating NdeI and BamHI restriction sites. Four constructs were generated:  

1. pET-S-IGAN: Encodes protease domain with native signal peptide in pET21a  
2. pET-IGAN: Encodes protease domain without signal peptide in pET21a  
3. pCold-S-IGAN: Encodes protease domain with signal peptide in pColdIV  
4. pCold-IGAN: Encodes protease domain without signal peptide in pColdIV  

All constructs included C-terminal 6xHis tags for purification. Sequences were verified by restriction digest and DNA sequencing.  

### Example 2  

**Optimization of Soluble IgA1 Protease Expression**  

The four expression constructs were transformed into various E. coli strains (BL21(DE3), ArcticExpress(DE3), C41(DE3), etc.). Cultures were grown at 37°C to OD600=0.6, then induced with 0.2-1 mM IPTG at 12-30°C for 3-24 hours.  

ELISA quantification revealed:  
- pET-IGAN in C41(DE3) yielded highest soluble expression (~100 mg/L)  
- Optimal conditions: 0.4 mM IPTG, 20°C, 24 hours  
- Cytoplasmic expression (no signal peptide) outperformed periplasmic targeting  

SDS-PAGE and Western blot confirmed expression of ~100 kDa proteins reactive with anti-His antibodies. Activity assays showed specific cleavage of human IgA1.  

### Example 3  

**Purification and Characterization of Recombinant IgA1 Protease**  

C41(DE3)/pET-IGAN cultures were lysed by high-pressure homogenization. The soluble fraction was sequentially purified by:  

1. Nickel affinity chromatography (IMAC Chelating Sepharose)  
2. Anion exchange chromatography (Q Sepharose FF)  
3. Size exclusion chromatography (S300 Sephacryl HR)  

Final purity exceeded 95% by SDS-PAGE (Figure 6). Yield was 20-40 mg purified protease per liter culture.  

Activity was quantified using an Experion automated electrophoresis system:  
- C41-derived protease: 46 ng/μL/min per ng enzyme  
- Native H. influenzae protease control: 27 ng/μL/min per ng enzyme  

The recombinant protease showed superior activity, likely due to higher purity and lack of degradation.  

### Example 4  

**Therapeutic Efficacy in IgA Nephropathy Model**  

Purified recombinant IgA1 protease was administered intravenously to mice with passive IgA nephropathy (3 mg/kg twice weekly for 4 weeks). Results showed:  

- Significant reduction in glomerular IgA deposition (p<0.01 vs. controls)  
- Improved proteinuria scores  
- No detectable adverse effects  

Histological examination revealed decreased mesangial matrix expansion and inflammatory infiltrates in treated animals. These results support therapeutic potential for human IgA nephropathy.
