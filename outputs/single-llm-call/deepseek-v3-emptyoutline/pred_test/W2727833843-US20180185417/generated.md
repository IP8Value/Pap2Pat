## DESCRIPTION

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The field of regenerative medicine has witnessed significant advancements in cell-based therapies aimed at tissue repair and regeneration. However, current approaches for isolating therapeutic cell populations face substantial limitations that hinder widespread clinical translation. Conventional methods relying on antibody-based cell sorting techniques often result in residual antibodies or magnetic beads remaining attached to the isolated cells. Such modifications may trigger immune responses or alter cellular function, while also subjecting the therapy to stringent regulatory oversight as an advanced therapy medicinal product (ATMP).  

Existing cytokine mobilization strategies present additional challenges, including systemic side effects and the need for multiple hospital visits. Intraoperative cell therapies offer a promising alternative by enabling same-session harvest and administration of therapeutic cells. However, current intraoperative isolation methods lack the ability to produce unmodified, functionally intact cell populations suitable for immediate therapeutic use.  

There exists a critical need for cell isolation technologies that can rapidly enrich specific cell populations from heterogeneous mixtures while preserving native cell characteristics. Such technologies should enable efficient positive or negative selection without permanent modification of the isolated cells. Furthermore, the isolation process should maintain high cell viability and purity while being compatible with intraoperative time constraints.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions, devices, and methods for isolating specific cell populations from mixed cell samples. In particular embodiments, the invention enables rapid enrichment of CD31+ cells from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) through aptamer-mediated reversible binding. The technology utilizes oligonucleotide aptamers specific for cell surface markers, coupled with a specialized enrichment device that facilitates mechanical release of bound cells without chemical or enzymatic treatment.  

Key aspects of the invention include: (1) CD31-specific aptamers demonstrating high binding specificity and sensitivity; (2) a column-based enrichment device incorporating aptamer-coated macrobeads; (3) methods for reversible cell capture and release through controlled fluid shear; and (4) therapeutic applications of the enriched cell populations. The system achieves significant enrichment of CD31+ cells (up to 87% purity) while maintaining excellent cell viability (97%). The isolated cell populations demonstrate enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic potential compared to unenriched controls.  

The technology offers several advantages over existing approaches: elimination of residual beads or antibodies on isolated cells; preservation of native cell function; compliance with regulatory requirements for minimal manipulation; and adaptability for both positive and negative selection strategies. The system is particularly suited for intraoperative cell therapy applications where rapid, closed-system processing is required.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

#### I. CD31+ Cells  

The present invention utilizes CD31 (Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule-1 or PECAM-1) as a target marker for cell enrichment. CD31 is expressed on 70-78% of viable lymphocytes and monocytes, with expression levels maintained across age groups and genders. CD31+ cells exhibit multiple therapeutic properties including angiogenic, osteogenic, and immunosuppressive capabilities.  

The enriched CD31+ cell populations of the invention demonstrate a characteristic immunophenotype, typically showing increased proportions of CD31+CD14+ monocytes and CD3+CD31+ naïve T cells compared to unenriched PBMCs. These subpopulations contribute to the observed therapeutic effects through secretion of pro-angiogenic and osteogenic factors.  

CD31+ cells isolated according to the invention maintain their regenerative potential as demonstrated by in vitro assays. Conditioned media from enriched cells increases capillary-like tube formation by human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) by approximately 1.9-fold compared to controls. Similarly, the conditioned media enhances osteogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stromal cells, increasing matrix calcification by approximately 1.8-fold.  

#### II. Aptamers  

The invention employs CD31-specific aptamers as capture ligands for cell enrichment. Aptamers are oligonucleotides that bind molecular targets with high specificity through three-dimensional structural complementarity. The CD31 aptamers used in preferred embodiments demonstrate binding sensitivity exceeding 99% and specificity between 88-97% across concentration ranges of 0.5-5 μg/ml.  

The aptamers are typically single-stranded DNA or RNA molecules ranging from 20-100 nucleotides in length. Preferred embodiments utilize biotinylated aptamers to facilitate conjugation to solid supports. The aptamers exhibit reversible binding characteristics with dissociation constants (Kd) in the nanomolar range (approximately 1.14×10^-9 M), enabling mechanical release of captured cells under controlled shear conditions.  

Aptamer binding to CD31 occurs through non-covalent interactions including hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals forces. The binding affinity is sufficient for cell capture under flow conditions but low enough to permit subsequent release without chemical treatment. This balance of affinity and reversibility represents a key innovation of the present technology.  

#### III. Devices for Enrichment of Cells  

The invention provides specialized devices for cell enrichment comprising:  

1. A column housing with inlet and outlet ports for fluid flow  
2. A macroporous filter element (typically 20 μm pore size)  
3. A bed of solid-phase capture beads (typically 45-165 μm diameter)  
4. Aptamer conjugates immobilized on the capture beads  

In preferred embodiments, the device consists of modified microcentrifuge tubes containing punched filters from 20 μm cell strainers. The filters are secured with cyanoacrylate glue and support a mixed bed of neutravidin-coated agarose beads, with approximately 50% of beads conjugated to biotinylated CD31 aptamers.  

The device operates through sequential flow steps:  
1. Sample application containing mixed cell populations  
2. Binding phase where target cells interact with aptamer-coated beads  
3. Wash phase to remove unbound cells  
4. Release phase employing mechanical agitation to disrupt aptamer-cell bonds  
5. Collection of released cells by centrifugation  

The system maintains cell viability exceeding 95% throughout the enrichment process. The large bead size (relative to cells) and macroporous filters prevent bead contamination of the final cell product.  

#### IV. Methods of Enriching Cells from a Mixed Cell Population  

The invention provides methods for enriching CD31+ cells from PBMCs or whole blood samples. Key steps include:  

1. Preparing the enrichment device by conjugating CD31 aptamers to solid-phase beads  
2. Loading the aptamer-coated beads into the column device  
3. Applying the cell sample at controlled flow rates (typically 50 μl/min)  
4. Incubating to allow CD31+ cell binding (typically 10 minutes)  
5. Washing with buffer to remove unbound cells  
6. Releasing bound cells through mechanical agitation  
7. Collecting enriched cells by centrifugation  

Optimal conditions use aptamer concentrations of 5 μg/ml, total bead volumes of 800 μl (50:50 coated:uncoated ratio), and binding buffers as specified by aptamer manufacturers. The methods achieve CD31+ cell enrichment from approximately 64% in starting populations to 87% in final products, with yields around 25%.  

The methods are particularly suitable for intraoperative applications, enabling complete processing within approximately 30 minutes. The mechanical release strategy avoids need for enzymatic or chemical treatments that could affect cell function or trigger regulatory concerns.  

#### V. Kits  

The invention provides kits for CD31+ cell enrichment comprising:  

1. CD31-specific aptamers (typically biotinylated)  
2. Solid-phase capture beads (typically neutravidin-coated agarose)  
3. Column devices with macroporous filters  
4. Binding and wash buffers  
5. Instructions for device assembly and use  

Kits may include additional components such as syringes for sample application, cell collection tubes, and viability assessment reagents. Some embodiments provide pre-assembled columns containing aptamer-coated beads for immediate use.  

The kits enable rapid deployment of the technology in clinical or research settings. All components are typically provided in sterile packaging suitable for therapeutic applications.  

#### VI. Methods of Treatment  

The invention provides therapeutic methods utilizing CD31+ cells enriched according to the disclosed technology. Key applications include:  

1. Promoting bone healing in fracture non-unions by local administration of enriched CD31+ cells  
2. Enhancing vascularization in ischemic tissues through targeted delivery of CD31+ cells  
3. Immunomodulation in inflammatory conditions via immunosuppressive effects of CD31+ cells  

Treatment protocols typically involve:  
1. Obtaining patient blood sample (typically 50-100 ml)  
2. Isolating PBMCs by density centrifugation  
3. Enriching CD31+ cells using the aptamer-based system  
4. Administering enriched cells to target site (e.g., fracture location)  

Therapeutic doses range from approximately 1×10^6 to 1×10^7 CD31+ cells per treatment, depending on application. The cells may be administered in physiologic saline or combined with biocompatible scaffolds for localized delivery.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Materials and methods  

Peripheral blood samples were obtained from consented donors under institutional review board approval. PBMCs were isolated using Ficoll-Paque density centrifugation or Sepmate™ tubes. Whole blood samples were purchased from commercial vendors when indicated.  

The CD31 biotinylated aptamer (APTSCI #CD31-2196BCI) was used at concentrations ranging from 0.5-10 μg/ml. For flow cytometry, cells were stained with anti-CD31-AF488 and anti-biotin-PE antibodies. Magnetic bead comparisons used MACS CD31 Microbead kits (Miltenyi Biotec).  

Device components included:  
- Microcentrifuge tubes (Pierce #69705)  
- 20 μm cell strainers (Pluriselect #43-50020-03)  
- Neutravidin agarose beads (Piercenet #29204)  
- Syringe pumps (Braintree Scientific #BS300)  

#### Aptamer Selection and Validation  

The CD31 aptamer demonstrated concentration-dependent binding to PBMCs. At 5 μg/ml, sensitivity reached 99.0±1.3% (mean±SD) while specificity was 87.9±4.8%. Flow cytometry confirmed co-localization of aptamer binding (biotin signal) with anti-CD31 antibody staining.  

Magnetic isolation using aptamer-coated beads achieved CD31+ enrichment comparable to antibody-based commercial kits (p>0.05 by t-test). No significant differences in cell viability were observed between methods (p>0.05).  

#### Development of a CD31+ Cell Enrichment System  

The column-based system was optimized through parameter testing:  

1. Aptamer concentration: 5 μg/ml provided optimal balance of yield (25.1±0.9%), purity (87±3%), and viability (97±1%)  
2. Bead composition: 50:50 mix of coated:uncoated beads prevented clogging  
3. Bead volume: 800 μl total (400 μl coated) achieved saturation binding  

Mechanical release through syringe agitation successfully liberated cells without chemical treatment. DNAse treatment following mechanical release confirmed complete dissociation at optimal conditions.  

#### CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations  

Enriched populations showed:  
- Increased CD31+ percentage from 64±9% to 87±3%  
- Enriched CD31+CD14+ monocytes (p<0.01)  
- Increased CD3+CD31+ naïve T cells (p<0.05)  
- Viability maintained at 97±1%  

Microscopy confirmed absence of residual beads in final product. Flow cytometry using dual-labeled aptamers demonstrated aptamer-free cells after release.  

#### Angiogenic and Osteogenic Potential of CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations  

Conditioned media from enriched cells increased:  
- HUVEC tube formation by 1.9±0.2 fold (p<0.01)  
- MSC calcification by 1.8±0.3 fold (p<0.01)  

These effects were significantly greater than unenriched controls (p<0.01 for both assays). Positive controls using commercial media showed similar enhancement patterns.
