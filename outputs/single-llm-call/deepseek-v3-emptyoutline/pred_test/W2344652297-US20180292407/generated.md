# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of monoclonal antibody production, specifically to a novel method for generating antigen-specific monoclonal antibodies from fixed and intracellularly stained single cells. More particularly, the invention provides an improved technique for isolating rare antigen-specific plasma cells (ASPCs) that produce antibodies against specific post-translational modifications, such as phosphorylation sites, with high sensitivity and specificity. The method combines cell fixation, intracellular staining with fluorescently labeled antigens, fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), and optimized reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) to enable efficient amplification of immunoglobulin variable region genes from single cells.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Conventional methods for generating monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) rely on hybridoma technology or phage display, which often fail to isolate rare ASPCs producing highly specific antibodies against post-translationally modified proteins. Existing techniques face limitations in sensitivity, specificity, and RNA preservation, particularly when targeting intracellular immunoglobulins in plasma cells. Fixation methods such as paraformaldehyde (PFA) cross-link RNA and proteins, impairing subsequent gene amplification. Prior attempts to reverse cross-linking have been limited to short RNA targets, making full-length immunoglobulin gene amplification challenging. Additionally, conventional FACS-based approaches for ASPC isolation suffer from low signal-to-noise ratios when detecting rare cells, resulting in poor yields of antigen-specific antibodies. There remains a need for a robust method that combines high-sensitivity cell sorting with efficient RNA recovery to enable the production of phosphorylation site-specific mAbs.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a method termed Fixed Antigen-Antibody Amplification (FIXAA), which enables the isolation of ASPCs with high specificity and subsequent amplification of full-length immunoglobulin variable genes from fixed single cells. The method involves optimized fixation conditions, intracellular staining with fluorescently labeled antigens, stringent RNase inactivation, and protease treatment to reverse RNA-protein cross-linking. FIXAA achieves a lower detection limit of less than 0.01% for rare ASPCs and enables the production of mAbs against specific post-translational modifications, such as phosphorylated peptides, with high success rates.  

### Solution to Problem  

The invention solves the problem of isolating rare ASPCs and amplifying full-length immunoglobulin genes from fixed cells by:  
1. Using short-duration PFA fixation to preserve antigen-binding activity while minimizing RNA degradation.  
2. Incorporating a protease treatment step to reverse RNA-protein cross-linking, enabling efficient RT-PCR amplification.  
3. Employing a subtraction staining strategy to eliminate cross-reactive cells, ensuring high specificity for target antigens.  
4. Optimizing FACS gating parameters to distinguish rare ASPCs from background noise.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENT  

### (1) Fixing Step  

Cells are fixed with ice-cold 2% PFA in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 8 minutes to preserve intracellular immunoglobulins while inactivating endogenous RNases. The fixation time is critical; prolonged fixation leads to excessive RNA-protein cross-linking, impairing subsequent gene amplification. After fixation, cells are washed and resuspended in staining buffer.  

### (2) Cell Membrane Lysis Step  

Fixed cells are permeabilized with PBS containing 0.1% Triton X-100 to allow access of fluorescently labeled antigens and antibodies to intracellular immunoglobulins. RNase inhibitors are included in the permeabilization buffer to prevent RNA degradation.  

### (3) Staining Step  

Cells are stained intracellularly with:  
- Fluorescently labeled antigen (e.g., DyLight 488-conjugated peptide).  
- Secondary antibodies against species-specific IgG (e.g., anti-guinea pig IgG-DyLight 650).  
- A nucleic acid stain (e.g., DAPI) for viability assessment.  
For phosphorylation-specific mAbs, a subtraction step is included using a fluorescently labeled unmodified peptide (e.g., DyLight 550-conjugated) to eliminate cross-reactive cells.  

### (4) Cell Isolation Step  

Stained cells are analyzed by FACS, with gating parameters optimized to distinguish ASPCs (high antigen and anti-IgG signal, low background staining). Single cells are sorted into 96-well plates containing lysis buffer and oligo(dT)25 magnetic beads for mRNA capture.  

### (6) Antigen Specific Monoclonal Antibody Preparing Step  

Sorted single cells undergo protease K treatment (50°C, 1 hour) to reverse RNA-protein cross-linking. Full-length VH and VL genes are amplified by 5’ RACE PCR, cloned into expression vectors, and transfected into mammalian cells (e.g., Expi293) for recombinant antibody production.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

OKT10 hybridomas were fixed with PFA for 8 minutes, stained intracellularly with DyLight 488-CD38 and anti-mouse IgG, and sorted by FACS. Protease K treatment enabled full-length VH gene amplification with an 87% success rate from a 1:100 hybridoma-Jurkat cell mixture.  

### Example 2  

Guinea pigs were immunized with a RORγt peptide. Lymph node cells were fixed, stained with RORγt-488, RORγ-550 (subtraction control), and anti-IgG, and sorted. Cloned mAbs showed 95% specificity for RORγt over RORγ.  

### Example 3  

Lymph node cells from guinea pigs immunized with a triphospho-p53 peptide were fixed and stained with TP-p53-488, UM-p53-550, and anti-IgG. FACS isolated phospho-specific PCs (0.01% of lymphocytes). 5’ RACE PCR achieved 82% paired VH/VL amplification, and 96% of expressed mAbs were phospho-specific.  

### Comparative Example 1 (Method in NPL 4)  

Live cell staining with ER-tracker and antigens (ERIAA method) yielded a 34% success rate for V gene amplification and only 16% phospho-specific mAbs, demonstrating FIXAA’s superiority.  

### Example 3 (Continued)  

Clone #23, isolated from Example 3, specifically detected p53 phosphorylated at T18 (pT18) by immunofluorescence, Western blot, and peptide competition. The KD for pT18 was 0.2 nM.  

### Example 4  

A mAb against T68-phosphorylated CHK2 (#34) was produced with a KD of 0.08 nM, confirming the method’s reproducibility for phospho-specific mAbs.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The invention is industrially applicable for:  
1. High-throughput production of phosphorylation site-specific mAbs for research and diagnostics.  
2. Generating therapeutic antibodies targeting post-translationally modified proteins in diseases like cancer and autoimmune disorders.  
3. Isotyping rare ASPCs from immunized animals for vaccine development.
