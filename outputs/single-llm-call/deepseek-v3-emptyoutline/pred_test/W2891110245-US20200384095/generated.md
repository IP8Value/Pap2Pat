# **DESCRIPTION**  

## **FIELD OF INVENTION**  

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccinology, specifically to a novel bivalent vaccine composition derived from Outer Membrane Vesicles (OMVs) of *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A. The invention provides a method of preparation, characterization, and use of said OMVs as an immunogenic composition capable of inducing broad-spectrum protective immunity against enteric fever caused by typhoidal salmonellae. The vaccine formulation is designed to activate both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, thereby offering long-term protection against systemic and mucosal infections.  

## **BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION**  

Enteric fever, caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi (*S. Typhi*) and Paratyphi A (*S. Paratyphi A*), remains a significant global health burden, particularly in developing nations. Current vaccines against *S. Typhi* include the live-attenuated oral vaccine (Ty21a), the Vi polysaccharide vaccine, and a tetanus toxoid-conjugated Vi vaccine. However, none of these vaccines provide adequate protection against *S. Paratyphi A*, which is increasingly prevalent in endemic regions.  

Outer Membrane Vesicles (OMVs) are naturally secreted by Gram-negative bacteria and have emerged as promising vaccine candidates due to their inherent immunogenicity. OMVs contain pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), including lipopolysaccharides (LPS), outer membrane proteins (OMPs), and other immunostimulatory components that activate innate and adaptive immunity without requiring additional adjuvants. The only licensed OMV-based vaccine, MeNZB, targets *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup B and has demonstrated high efficacy.  

Despite these advances, no OMV-based vaccine exists for typhoidal salmonellae. Prior attempts at developing *Salmonella* vaccines have focused on attenuated strains or subunit antigens, which often fail to induce robust mucosal and systemic immunity. There remains an unmet need for a bivalent vaccine capable of conferring cross-protection against both *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*.  

## **OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION**  

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a bivalent OMV-based vaccine composition derived from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* that induces long-term protective immunity against enteric fever.  

Further objectives include:  
1. Developing a method for isolating and purifying OMVs from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* cultures.  
2. Characterizing the immunogenic components of said OMVs, including LPS, OMPs, and other vesicle-associated proteins.  
3. Demonstrating the ability of the bivalent OMV formulation to elicit both humoral (IgG, IgA, IgM) and cell-mediated (Th1, Th17) immune responses.  
4. Establishing the protective efficacy of the vaccine in an animal model against lethal challenges with *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*.  
5. Providing evidence of passive immunity through adoptive transfer studies.  

## **DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION**  

The invention discloses a bivalent OMV vaccine formulation comprising equal parts of OMVs isolated from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*. The OMVs are prepared under optimized culture conditions, purified via ultracentrifugation, and characterized for protein and LPS content. The vaccine is administered orally or parenterally and induces broad-spectrum immune responses.  

### **Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions**  

The OMVs are derived from *S. Typhi* strain C-6953 and *S. Paratyphi A* strain C-6915, obtained from the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases (NICED) culture bank. Strains are cultured in Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB) at 37°C under shaking conditions (100 rpm) until reaching exponential growth phase. Cultures are centrifuged at 8,000 rpm for 40 minutes at 4°C to remove bacterial cells, followed by filtration through a 0.22 µm membrane.  

### **Preparation of OMVs**  

OMVs are purified from filtered supernatants via ultracentrifugation (140,000 × g, 4°C, 4 hours) using a Sorvall T-865 rotor. The pellet is resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4), and protein concentration is quantified using the Lowry assay. LPS content is determined via the Dubois method, confirming the presence of immunogenic O-antigens.  

### **Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria**  

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is employed to visualize OMVs and OMV-secreting bacteria. Samples are negatively stained with 2% uranyl acetate and examined under a Tecnai 12 Bio Twin microscope. OMVs exhibit a size range of 130–300 nm, with *S. Typhi* OMVs being larger than those from *S. Paratyphi A*.  

### **Animals**  

Female BALB/c mice (7 weeks old) are used for immunization studies. Mice are housed under controlled conditions (25°C, 75% humidity) and immunized orally with 25 µg of bivalent OMVs on days 0, 14, and 28.  

### **Collection of Serum and Stool**  

Blood and stool samples are collected at intervals (0, 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90 days post-immunization). Serum is isolated via centrifugation, and stool homogenates are prepared in PBS with protease inhibitors.  

### **SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot**  

OMV proteins are separated on 12% SDS-PAGE gels and visualized via Coomassie or silver staining. Immunoblots are performed using anti-OMV sera to identify immunodominant antigens (20–90 kDa).  

### **Dot Blot Assay**  

LPS from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* is immobilized on nitrocellulose membranes and probed with immune sera to confirm antibody specificity.  

### **ELISA**  

Serum IgG, IgA, IgM, and subclasses (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3) are quantified via ELISA. Microtiter wells are coated with OMVs (5 µg/well), incubated with sera, and developed using HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies.  

### **Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells**  

Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) are cultured with OMVs, and cytokine secretion (IL-6, IL-12, IL-17, IFN-γ) is measured via ELISA. Co-culture with splenic CD4+ T cells demonstrates Th1/Th17 polarization.  

## **The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:**  

1. **Broad-Spectrum Protection:** The bivalent OMV formulation induces immunity against both *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*, addressing a critical gap in current vaccines.  
2. **Self-Adjuvanting Properties:** OMVs naturally contain LPS and OMPs that activate innate and adaptive immunity without exogenous adjuvants.  
3. **Mucosal and Systemic Immunity:** Oral immunization elicits robust IgA and IgG responses, preventing intestinal invasion and systemic dissemination.  
4. **Long-Term Immunity:** Antibody titers remain detectable for 90 days post-immunization, indicating durable protection.  
5. **Passive Immunity Transfer:** Adoptive transfer of immune sera or splenocytes confers protection in naïve mice, validating humoral and cellular mechanisms.  
6. **Inhibition of Bacterial Motility:** Anti-OMV antibodies agglutinate bacteria and inhibit mucin penetration, reducing colonization.  
7. **Scalable Production:** The OMV purification process is cost-effective and adaptable to large-scale manufacturing.
