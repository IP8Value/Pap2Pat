## 1 MATERIALS AND METHODS  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for inducing protective immune responses against Plasmodium falciparum malaria through vaccination. The vaccine compositions comprise recombinant viral vectors encoding one or more P. falciparum liver-stage antigens selected from PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, PfUIS3, PfLSA3, and PfFalstatin. These antigens are delivered using a heterologous prime-boost regimen comprising a chimpanzee adenovirus (ChAd63) prime followed by a modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) boost.  

The recombinant viral vectors were constructed by inserting codon-optimized P. falciparum antigen sequences into the E1 locus of ChAd63 and the thymidine kinase locus of MVA. Each antigen sequence was preceded by a Kozak sequence and the tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) leader sequence was added to enhance antigen secretion, except for membrane-anchored proteins PfLSA3, PfLSAP1 and PfUIS3. Repetitive regions were deleted from PfLSA1 and PfLSA3 to optimize immunogenicity.  

For immunization, mice received an intramuscular injection of 1×10^8 infectious units of ChAd63 followed by 1×10^7 plaque forming units of MVA after an eight-week interval. Immunogenicity was assessed through intracellular cytokine staining, IFNγ ELISpot assays, and luminescence immunoprecipitation system (LIPS) for antibody detection. Protective efficacy was evaluated by challenging vaccinated mice with 1000 chimeric P. berghei sporozoites expressing the cognate P. falciparum antigen eight days after the MVA boost. Parasitemia was monitored by blood smear microscopy from day 4 post-challenge.  

## 2 ASSESSING IMMUNOGENICITY AND EFFICACY OF EIGHT CANDIDATE P. FALCIPARUM VACCINES IN MICE  

A comprehensive evaluation was conducted to assess the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of eight candidate P. falciparum liver-stage antigens delivered via ChAd63-MVA prime-boost vaccination in murine models. The antigens tested included PfLSA1, PfLSA3, PfCelTOS, PfUIS3, PfFalstatin, PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfETRAMP5. These antigens were selected based on their high expression during liver-stage development, recognition by T cells during natural infection or irradiated sporozoite immunization, and evidence of protection from rodent malaria orthologs.  

Immunogenicity studies in BALB/c mice revealed that all antigens except PfLSAP1 induced detectable CD8+ IFNγ+ T cell responses in blood one week post-MVA boost, with the highest frequencies observed for PfLSA1 (6.3% of CD8+ cells), PfLSA3 (4.8%), and PfUIS3 (4.2%). Splenic IFNγ ELISpot responses confirmed strong cellular immunogenicity for PfUIS3 (1200 SFU/million splenocytes) and PfLSA1 (868 SFU/million). Humoral responses were detected against all antigens except PfLSAP1, with the highest antibody levels induced by PfFalstatin and PfLSAP2.  

Parallel studies in C57BL/6 mice showed generally higher but more variable CD8+ T cell responses, with PfUIS3 (13.7% of CD8+ cells) and PfLSA3 (12.8%) being the most immunogenic. Notably, no cellular responses were detected against PfLSA1 or PfLSAP1 in this strain, highlighting MHC-restricted immunodominance effects. These findings demonstrate that the ChAd63-MVA platform can induce robust cellular immunity against multiple liver-stage antigens, with the magnitude and consistency of responses varying by antigen and mouse strain.  

## 3 ASSESSMENT OF PfUIS3, PfLSA1 AND PfLSAP2 AS CANDIDATES FOR A LIVER-STAGE MALARIA VACCINE  

Among the eight candidate antigens evaluated, PfLSA1, PfLSAP2 and PfUIS3 emerged as particularly promising targets for liver-stage malaria vaccination based on their immunogenicity profiles and protective efficacy. PfLSA1 vaccination induced sterile protection in 87.5% of BALB/c mice challenged with chimeric P. berghei sporozoites expressing PfLSA1, significantly outperforming control vaccines expressing PfCSP (37.5% protection) or PfTRAP (0% protection). Similarly, PfLSAP2 vaccination protected 87.5% of BALB/c mice against homologous challenge.  

PfUIS3 demonstrated more modest but statistically significant protection, with 12.5% sterile protection and a median 1.42-day delay in parasitemia onset in BALB/c mice. Mechanistic studies revealed that protection mediated by PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 was highly dependent on CD8+ T cells, as demonstrated by depletion experiments. CD8+ T cell depletion reduced PfLSAP2-mediated protection from 75% to 12.5% (p=0.03) and PfLSA1-mediated protection from 57.1% to 12.5%. Both vaccines induced antigen-specific CD8+ effector memory T cells in the liver at frequencies comparable to those in the spleen, suggesting the establishment of liver-resident memory populations capable of rapid response to infection.  

### 4. PROTECTIVE EFFICACY OF THE CANDIDATES VACCINES IN CD-1 OUTBRED MICE  

To evaluate vaccine efficacy in a more genetically diverse model that better approximates human populations, studies were conducted in outbred CD-1 mice. Remarkably, both PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 maintained high levels of protection in this model, with 87.5% and 70% sterile protection respectively, significantly exceeding the protection conferred by PfCSP (33.3%) or PfTRAP (30%).  

PfFalstatin also showed significant efficacy in CD-1 mice, inducing a median 0.97-day delay in parasitemia onset (p<0.0001). In contrast, PfUIS3 failed to confer significant protection in outbred mice despite its partial efficacy in BALB/c mice, highlighting potential limitations of inbred mouse models for predicting human immunogenicity. The maintenance of high-level protection for PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 across mouse strains suggests these antigens may overcome MHC restriction and immunodominance effects that limit other candidates.  

## 5. SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel malaria vaccine compositions comprising recombinant viral vectors encoding one or more of the liver-stage antigens PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, PfUIS3, PfLSA3, and PfFalstatin. These vaccines are administered using a heterologous prime-boost regimen with ChAd63 followed by MVA. The vaccines induce robust CD8+ T cell responses and provide unprecedented levels of sterile protection against liver-stage malaria in both inbred and outbred murine models.  

PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 emerge as particularly promising candidates, demonstrating >70% sterile protection across mouse strains, dependence on CD8+ T cells for protection, and induction of liver-resident memory populations. These findings support the clinical development of these antigens as components of a next-generation malaria vaccine targeting the liver stage of infection.  

## 5. SPECT-1  

The vaccine compositions of the invention may be formulated as pharmaceutical compositions comprising:  
1) A priming dose of 1×10^8 to 1×10^10 infectious units of ChAd63 encoding a P. falciparum liver-stage antigen selected from PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, PfUIS3, PfLSA3, or PfFalstatin; and  
2) A boosting dose of 1×10^7 to 1×10^9 plaque forming units of MVA encoding the same antigen, administered 4-12 weeks after the priming dose.  

The compositions may further comprise pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, stabilizers, or adjuvants. The vaccines may be administered via intramuscular, intradermal, or subcutaneous routes. Multi-antigen vaccines combining two or more of the protective antigens may be formulated to broaden immune coverage.  

## 6. RESULTS  

The experimental results demonstrate that:  
1) ChAd63-MVA vaccines expressing PfLSA1 or PfLSAP2 induce sterile protection in 70-87.5% of mice across genetic backgrounds;  
2) Protection is mediated primarily by CD8+ T cells and associated with liver-resident memory populations;  
3) The magnitude of CD8+ T cell responses correlates with protective efficacy;  
4) The vaccines outperform current clinical candidates PfCSP and PfTRAP in head-to-head comparisons;  
5) The protective efficacy is maintained in outbred mice, suggesting potential applicability to genetically diverse human populations.  

These results establish PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as leading candidates for inclusion in next-generation malaria vaccines and provide a platform for evaluating additional liver-stage antigens using the chimeric parasite challenge model.
