# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD AND BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cellular biology and regenerative medicine, specifically to methods for redifferentiating expanded human pancreatic β-cells. More particularly, the invention provides novel techniques involving HES1 inhibition to restore functional insulin-producing capacity in cultured islet cells that have undergone dedifferentiation during in vitro expansion.  

Diabetes mellitus remains a global health challenge, with an estimated 463 million adults affected worldwide as of 2019. While insulin therapy has been the cornerstone of treatment for type 1 diabetes, cell replacement therapy using pancreatic islet transplantation offers the potential for physiological glucose regulation. However, this approach faces significant limitations due to the scarcity of donor islets and the need for multiple donors per recipient.  

Previous attempts to expand human β-cells in vitro have been hampered by the phenomenon of dedifferentiation, where cultured β-cells lose their specialized functions including insulin production. During this process, the cells undergo morphological and molecular changes, including increased expression of mesenchymal markers and substantial nuclear enlargement. While various redifferentiation protocols have been proposed, their efficiency remains limited, typically restoring insulin expression in only a fraction of the expanded cell population.  

The NOTCH signaling pathway plays a crucial role in cell fate determination and maintenance of progenitor cell populations. HES1, a primary effector of NOTCH signaling, has been implicated in maintaining the proliferative capacity of various cell types while suppressing differentiation. In pancreatic development, NOTCH signaling is known to regulate the balance between endocrine progenitor maintenance and differentiation. However, the specific role of HES1 in adult human β-cell redifferentiation had not been elucidated prior to the present invention.  

There exists an unmet need in the art for improved methods to redifferentiate expanded human β-cells that address the limitations of current approaches. The present invention fulfills this need by providing novel techniques involving HES1 inhibition that significantly enhance the efficiency of β-cell redifferentiation, both as a standalone treatment and in combination with other redifferentiation factors.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods for redifferentiating expanded human pancreatic β-cells through inhibition of HES1 expression or activity. The invention is based on the surprising discovery that targeted downregulation of HES1 in cultured human islet cells induces redifferentiation of β-cell-derived (BCD) cells, as evidenced by restoration of insulin expression, reduction in nuclear size, and formation of islet-like cell clusters.  

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for redifferentiating expanded human β-cells comprising: (a) culturing human islet cells under conditions that promote expansion and dedifferentiation; (b) introducing into said cells an effective amount of HES1 inhibitory agent selected from the group consisting of HES1-specific short hairpin RNA (shRNA), small interfering RNA (siRNA), antisense oligonucleotides, and small molecule inhibitors; and (c) maintaining the cells under conditions suitable for redifferentiation.  

The HES1 inhibition induces multiple markers of β-cell redifferentiation, including: (i) significant upregulation of insulin (INS) gene expression, typically in the range of 4-7 fold compared to control cells; (ii) increased expression of β-cell transcription factors including PDX1 (4-5 fold), NEUROD1 (4-5 fold), MAFA, and NKX6.1; (iii) restoration of components of the glucose sensing apparatus including glucokinase (GCK), sulfonylurea receptor 1 (SUR1), and potassium inwardly-rectifying channel subfamily J member 11 (KIR6.2); (iv) reduction in nuclear size from approximately 319 ± 185 μm² to 201 ± 151 μm²; and (v) formation of three-dimensional cell clusters resembling native islets.  

In another embodiment, the invention provides a combined redifferentiation method wherein HES1 inhibition is coupled with serum-free medium (SFM) culture. This combination produces additive effects on β-cell redifferentiation, with INS transcript levels increasing approximately 23-fold compared to expanded controls. The method may further incorporate additional redifferentiation factors including high glucose (25 mM), nicotinamide (10 mM), exendin-4 (8 nM), and activin A (8 nM) in what is termed "redifferentiation medium" (RM).  

Remarkably, the combined HES1 inhibition/RM treatment increases the percentage of C-peptide-positive cells to approximately 20% of the total cell population, representing redifferentiation of about 50% of the BCD cells present in the culture. This represents a 2-3 fold improvement over RM treatment alone. Furthermore, cells redifferentiated through this method demonstrate advanced differentiation characteristics, including mutually exclusive expression of islet hormones (C-peptide, glucagon, somatostatin) similar to mature native islets, unlike the co-expression patterns typically seen in immature progenitor cells.  

The invention further encompasses the use of lineage tracing to confirm the β-cell origin of redifferentiated cells. Through specific labeling of β-cell-derived (BCD) cells using insulin promoter-driven Cre recombinase and a loxP-flanked eGFP reporter system, the invention demonstrates that approximately 80% of insulin-positive cells following redifferentiation originate from bona fide β-cells rather than other islet cell types.  

In vivo studies demonstrate that cells redifferentiated through HES1 inhibition acquire functional capacity to normalize blood glucose levels when transplanted into hyperglycemic mice. Notably, while HES1 inhibition in vitro induces a modest 4-5 fold increase in INS transcripts, transplantation leads to an additional 90-fold increase, suggesting the cells retain capacity for further maturation in vivo.  

The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising redifferentiated β-cells prepared according to the disclosed methods, and their use in cell replacement therapy for diabetes. These compositions may further include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, extracellular matrix components, and immunomodulatory agents to enhance engraftment and function.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The following detailed description illustrates specific embodiments of the invention but should not be construed as limiting its scope, which is defined by the appended claims.  

### Cell Culture and Expansion  

Human islet cells are obtained from cadaveric donors through standard isolation procedures, typically 2-3 days after isolation. The islets are dissociated into single cells using enzymatic digestion and cultured in CMRL 1066 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), antibiotics (100 U/ml penicillin, 100 μg/ml streptomycin, 100 μg/ml gentamycin, 5 μg/ml amphotericin B), and maintained at 5.6 mM glucose. The cultures are maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere, with medium changes twice weekly and splitting at a 1:2 ratio approximately once per week.  

Under these conditions, the β-cells undergo dedifferentiation over 5-7 weeks (5-7 passages), characterized by loss of insulin expression, increased proliferation (approximately 15% BrdU incorporation), and significant nuclear enlargement (average 319 ± 185 μm²). These dedifferentiated β-cell-derived (BCD) cells maintain potential for redifferentiation, which can be enhanced through the methods disclosed herein.  

### HES1 Inhibition  

HES1 inhibition is achieved through RNA interference using lentiviral delivery of HES1-specific shRNA. The shRNA sequence (GenBank HC 916590) is cloned into the pLKO.1 lentiviral vector containing a puromycin resistance gene. Lentivirus is produced in 293T cells using standard techniques.  

For infection, cultured islet cells at passages 5-7 are exposed to viral particles at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 2.5:1 in growth medium containing 8 μg/ml Polybrene overnight. The medium is then replaced with regular culture medium. Infection efficiency typically exceeds 90%, as determined by parallel infections with GFP-expressing control viruses.  

Alternative methods of HES1 inhibition include:  
1. siRNA oligonucleotides targeting HES1 mRNA  
2. Antisense oligonucleotides complementary to HES1 transcripts  
3. Small molecule inhibitors of NOTCH signaling that indirectly reduce HES1 expression  
4. CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing to disrupt the HES1 locus  

Following HES1 inhibition, protein levels decrease by approximately 40% within 9 days, accompanied by a 50% increase in p57 (CDKN1C) protein levels. This leads to growth arrest, with BrdU incorporation dropping from 15.8% to 0.7%.  

### Redifferentiation Conditions  

Following HES1 inhibition, cells may be maintained in several alternative media formulations to promote redifferentiation:  

1. **Standard Growth Medium**: CMRL 1066 with 5.6 mM glucose and 10% FBS. Even in serum-containing medium, HES1 inhibition alone induces redifferentiation markers.  

2. **Serum-Free Medium (SFM)**: CMRL 1066 with 5.6 mM glucose, 1% BSA, insulin/transferrin/selenium (ITS), and antibiotics. This enhances the redifferentiation effects of HES1 inhibition.  

3. **Redifferentiation Medium (RM)**: SFM supplemented with:  
   - 25 mM D-glucose  
   - 10 mM nicotinamide  
   - 8 nM exendin-4  
   - 8 nM activin A  

For optimal cluster formation, cells are seeded in ultra-low attachment plates at 3.2 × 10⁴ cells/cm². The medium is replaced every 2 days, and cells are typically analyzed after 4-11 days in culture.  

### Lineage Tracing of β-Cell-Derived Cells  

To specifically track β-cell-derived (BCD) cells during redifferentiation, a lineage tracing system employs two lentiviral vectors:  
1. RIP-Cre/ER: Expresses Cre recombinase-estrogen receptor fusion protein under control of the insulin promoter  
2. pTrip-loxP-NEO-STOP-loxP-eGFP: Reporter cassette where CMV promoter drives eGFP following Cre-mediated excision of a floxed STOP sequence  

Cells are infected with both vectors during initial culture, and 4-hydroxytamoxifen (1 μM) is added to activate Cre-ER. This permanently labels β-cells with eGFP. During redifferentiation experiments, the absence of tamoxifen prevents new labeling, ensuring eGFP⁺ cells represent original β-cells.  

### Characterization of Redifferentiated Cells  

Redifferentiated cells exhibit multiple markers of functional β-cells:  

**Gene Expression (qPCR):**  
- INS: 6.7 ± 4.3 fold increase  
- PDX1: 4.5 ± 1.4 fold increase  
- NEUROD1: 4.7 ± 3.3 fold increase  
- MAFA, NKX6.1, GCK, SUR1, KIR6.2: Significant upregulation  
- IAPP: Increased expression  

**Protein Expression (Immunofluorescence):**  
- C-peptide: 15-20% of cells positive in combined treatments  
- PDX1: Nuclear localization in insulin⁺ cells  
- NKX2.2: Co-expression with insulin in redifferentiated cells  

**Morphological Changes:**  
- Nuclear size reduction from 319 ± 185 μm² to 201 ± 151 μm²  
- Formation of three-dimensional cell clusters  
- Increased cell-cell contacts  

**Functional Assessment:**  
- Glucose-responsive insulin secretion in vitro  
- Normalization of blood glucose in transplanted diabetic mice  
- Human C-peptide detectable in mouse serum (0.4 ± 0.2 ng/ml)  

### In Vivo Transplantation  

Redifferentiated cells are tested in NOD-SCID mice rendered hyperglycemic by streptozotocin (180 mg/kg). Cells (2 × 10⁶) are transplanted under the kidney capsule 4 days after HES1 shRNA infection.  

Grafts analyzed after 56 days show:  
- 433 ± 98 fold increase in human INS mRNA vs. cultured controls  
- Co-expression of insulin, C-peptide, and IAPP  
- Histological organization resembling native islets  

### Examples  

#### Example 1: HES1 Inhibition Alone Induces β-Cell Redifferentiation  

Human islet cells expanded for 5-7 passages were infected with HES1 shRNA or control shRNA lentiviruses. After 9 days:  
- HES1 protein decreased by 40%  
- p57 protein increased by 50%  
- INS mRNA increased 6.7 ± 4.3 fold  
- Nuclear size decreased from 319 ± 185 μm² to 201 ± 151 μm²  
- BrdU incorporation dropped from 15.8% to 0.7%  

#### Example 2: Combined HES1 Inhibition and RM Treatment  

Sorted eGFP⁺ BCD cells were treated with:  
1. Nontarget shRNA + growth medium  
2. HES1 shRNA + growth medium  
3. Nontarget shRNA + RM  
4. HES1 shRNA + RM  

Results after 4 days:  
- INS mRNA:  
  - Group 1: Baseline  
  - Group 2: 4.8 ± 2.0 fold increase  
  - Group 3: 23.5 ± 10.5 fold increase  
  - Group 4: 144.2 ± 29.7 fold increase  
- C-peptide⁺ cells:  
  - Group 3: 6.8% of eGFP⁺ cells  
  - Group 4: 15% of eGFP⁺ cells  

#### Example 3: In Vivo Function of Redifferentiated Cells  

HES1 shRNA-treated cells transplanted into diabetic mice:  
- 3 of 6 mice showed normalized blood glucose  
- Human C-peptide detected in serum (0.4 ± 0.2 ng/ml)  
- Grafts contained insulin⁺, C-peptide⁺, IAPP⁺ cells  
- INS mRNA in grafts: 433 ± 98 fold over cultured controls  

The invention thus provides novel, efficient methods for redifferentiating expanded human β-cells through HES1 inhibition, offering significant improvements over existing approaches for generating insulin-producing cells for diabetes therapy.
