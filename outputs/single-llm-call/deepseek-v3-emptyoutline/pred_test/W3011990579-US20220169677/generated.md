# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE  

The present disclosure relates to novel peptide compounds that function as inhibitors of Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) receptor activity. More specifically, the disclosure encompasses cysteine-modified peptide derivatives exhibiting enhanced binding affinity to NRP-1, pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds, and methods of using them for therapeutic applications, particularly in inhibiting pathological angiogenesis associated with cancer and other diseases.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) is a transmembrane receptor that plays a critical role in angiogenesis, axon guidance, and tumor progression. It acts as a co-receptor for vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and other growth factors, amplifying their signaling through receptor tyrosine kinases. Overexpression of NRP-1 in cancer cells correlates with poor prognosis, making it a promising therapeutic target.  

Existing peptidic inhibitors of NRP-1, such as the A7R heptapeptide (ATWLPPR) and tuftsin (TKPR), exhibit moderate binding affinity (IC50 = 60–100 μM). These peptides typically contain a C-terminal Arg/Lys motif (CendR rule) essential for receptor interaction. However, their therapeutic utility is limited by suboptimal potency and susceptibility to enzymatic degradation.  

There remains an unmet need for improved NRP-1 inhibitors with higher binding affinity, metabolic stability, and therapeutic efficacy.  

## SUMMARY  

The present disclosure provides novel peptide compounds of Formula (I):  

**Cys-X1-X2-X3-Arg**  

wherein:  
- **Cys** is cysteine;  
- **X1**, **X2**, and **X3** are independently selected from natural or non-natural amino acids;  
- **Arg** is arginine.  

In certain embodiments, the peptides are branched, such as **Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg**, further modified by N-terminal cysteine extension.  

Key advantages of the disclosed compounds include:  
1. **Enhanced binding affinity**: Cysteine modification reduces IC50 values to nanomolar ranges (e.g., 60–80 nM), a >50-fold improvement over prior art.  
2. **Plasma stability**: The compounds exhibit extended half-lives (>24 h) in human plasma due to structural resistance to proteolysis.  
3. **Dual-mechanism action**: The thiol group of cysteine enables additional interactions (hydrogen bonding, S-π interactions) with NRP-1, complementing the CendR motif.  

The disclosure further covers:  
- Methods of synthesizing the peptides using Fmoc-based solid-phase chemistry.  
- Pharmaceutical formulations for intravenous or oral administration.  
- Therapeutic uses for treating cancers, diabetic retinopathy, and other angiogenesis-dependent disorders.  

### and R5 is H,  

In specific embodiments, R5 represents a hydrogen atom in the cysteine thiol group (-SH), which may participate in disulfide bonding or receptor interactions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE  

### Compounds of the Disclosure  

The peptides are characterized by the general structure **Cys-X1-X2-X3-Arg**, where:  
- **Cys** is critical for high-affinity binding (CysR rule).  
- **X1–X3** may include hydrophobic (e.g., Pro, Leu), polar (e.g., Asp, Lys), or non-natural amino acids (e.g., Dhp, Dab).  

Exemplary compounds include:  
1. **Linear peptides**:  
   - Cys-Leu-Pro-Pro-Arg (derived from A7R).  
   - Cys-Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg (tuftsin analogue).  
   - Cys-Asp-Lys-Pro-Arg (VEGF-A165 fragment analogue).  

2. **Branched peptides**:  
   - Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg.  
   - Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg.  

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)  

The peptides are synthesized via **solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS)** using Fmoc chemistry:  
1. **Resin loading**: Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.39 mmol/g) serves as the solid support.  
2. **Coupling steps**: Amino acids (2 eq) are coupled using HATU/DIPEA in DMF. Alloc deprotection uses Pd(PPh3)4/PhSiH3.  
3. **Cleavage**: Peptides are cleaved with TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (88:5:5:2) and purified via RP-HPLC.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The compounds are formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients for:  
- **Injectable solutions**: 0.1–10 mg/mL in saline, with stabilizers (e.g., heparin).  
- **Oral tablets**: Enteric coatings to enhance bioavailability.  
- **Nanoparticle carriers**: Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) for targeted delivery.  

### Therapeutic Uses  

The peptides are indicated for:  
1. **Cancer therapy**: Inhibition of tumor angiogenesis in breast, lung, or prostate cancer.  
2. **Ocular diseases**: Treatment of age-related macular degeneration (AMD) or diabetic retinopathy.  
3. **Anti-metastatic agents**: Blockade of NRP-1-mediated cell migration.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthesis of Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg  

1. **Step 1**: Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.5 g) was swelled in DCM.  
2. **Step 2**: Sequential coupling of Dhp, Dab, Lys(Alloc), and Cys using HATU/DIPEA.  
3. **Step 3**: Alloc deprotection with Pd(PPh3)4/PhSiH3, followed by hArg coupling.  
4. **Step 4**: Cleavage with TFA cocktail, precipitation in cold ether, and HPLC purification (Phenomenex Jupiter column).  
5. **Yield**: 85%; purity >95% (HRMS confirmed).  

### Example 2: NRP-1 Binding Assay  

1. **Method**: Recombinant NRP-1 (200 ng/well) was coated on a 96-well plate.  
2. **Incubation**: Peptides (0.005–50 μM) and biotinylated VEGF-A165 (400 ng/mL) were added.  
3. **Detection**: Chemiluminescence measured after streptavidin-HRP treatment.  
4. **Results**: IC50 = 60 nM for Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-Dhp-Arg vs. 4.3 μM for the parent peptide.  

### Example 3: Plasma Stability Study  

1. **Incubation**: Peptides (1 μmol/mL) in human plasma at 37°C.  
2. **Analysis**: RP-HPLC at 0, 24, 48, and 96 h.  
3. **Results**: 50% remaining after 24 h; 40% after 96 h for Cys-hArg branched analogue.
