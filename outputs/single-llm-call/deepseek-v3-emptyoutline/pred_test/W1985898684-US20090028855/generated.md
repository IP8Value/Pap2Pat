# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel inhibitors of the protein kinase Akt (also known as Protein Kinase B or PKB), and more particularly to small molecule compounds that selectively inhibit Akt activity by binding to the pleckstrin homology (PH) domain of Akt. Aberrant activation of the Akt pathway occurs in approximately 50% of human malignancies, making it a critical target for cancer therapy. Current approaches to inhibit Akt suffer from limitations including lack of specificity, adverse side effects, and poor efficacy against constitutively active Akt mutants. There exists an unmet need for potent and selective Akt inhibitors that can effectively target both wild-type and mutant forms of Akt while minimizing off-target effects on other kinases. The present invention addresses these needs through the discovery of API-1 (Akt/PKB Inhibitor-1), a small molecule that specifically binds to the PH domain of Akt and inhibits its membrane translocation and subsequent activation.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel small molecule inhibitor of Akt, designated API-1, which demonstrates selective inhibition of Akt activity through binding to the PH domain. API-1 effectively inhibits all three isoforms of Akt (Akt1, Akt2, and Akt3) and shows potent activity against both wild-type Akt and constitutively active mutants including myristoylated Akt and the naturally occurring E17K mutant. Unlike prior art inhibitors, API-1 does not significantly affect upstream regulators of Akt (PI3K, PDK1, mTOR complexes) or other AGC family kinases (PKA, PKC, SGK). The compound demonstrates selective cytotoxicity against cancer cells with hyperactivated Akt while showing minimal effects on cells with normal Akt activity. In vivo studies show that API-1 significantly inhibits tumor growth in xenograft models of cancers with elevated Akt activity while having little effect on tumors with normal Akt levels.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### MATERIALS AND METHODS  

The invention encompasses the compound API-1 (pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidines, NSC 177223) and methods for its use in inhibiting Akt activity. API-1 was identified through screening of 2300 compounds from the NCI/DTP Open Chemical Repository using AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells. Compounds that selectively inhibited growth of AKT2-transformed cells but not control LXSN-transfected NIH3T3 cells were identified as potential Akt inhibitors.  

For in vitro studies, recombinant Akt proteins (Akt1, Akt2, Akt3) were expressed in HEK293 cells and purified. Kinase assays were performed using Akt/SGK substrate peptide in the presence of varying concentrations of API-1. For binding studies, API-1 was immobilized on NHS-activated Sepharose beads through its amino group and incubated with recombinant Akt or GST fusion proteins containing different Akt domains (PH domain, kinase domain, C-terminal domain).  

Cell-based assays were performed using various cancer cell lines with different Akt activation status (OVCAR3, OVCAR8, MCF7, PANC1 with high Akt activity; OVCAR5, MDA-MB-435s with low Akt activity). Cells were treated with API-1 and analyzed for Akt phosphorylation (Thr308, Ser473), downstream target phosphorylation (GSK3β, mTOR, p70 S6K, Bad, FOXO3a), cell proliferation, and apoptosis (annexin V staining, PARP cleavage).  

In vivo efficacy was evaluated in nude mouse xenograft models where tumors with hyperactivated Akt (OVCAR3, PANC1) or normal Akt levels (OVCAR5, COLO357) were implanted subcutaneously. Mice were treated with API-1 (10 mg/kg/day) or vehicle control when tumors reached approximately 100 mm³, and tumor growth was monitored.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Identification and Characterization of API-1 as an Akt Inhibitor**  

API-1 was identified as a selective inhibitor of AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells from a screen of 2300 compounds. In vitro kinase assays demonstrated that API-1 inhibited EGF-induced kinase activity of all three Akt isoforms (Akt1, Akt2, Akt3) when tested at 5 μM concentration. Treatment of OVCAR3 cells (high basal Akt activity) with API-1 resulted in dose-dependent reduction of Akt phosphorylation at Ser473 with an IC50 of approximately 0.8 μM, while total Akt levels remained unchanged.  

Unlike ATP-competitive inhibitors, API-1 showed minimal direct inhibition of recombinant Akt kinase activity in vitro, suggesting a distinct mechanism of action. Binding assays revealed that API-1 specifically interacts with the PH domain of Akt, as demonstrated by pull-down experiments using GST-PH domain fusion proteins. This interaction was maintained across a range of salt concentrations (50-150 mM NaCl), indicating a stable binding interaction.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

**Mechanism of Action: Inhibition of Akt Membrane Translocation**  

The invention demonstrates that API-1 inhibits Akt activation by preventing its translocation to the plasma membrane. Immunofluorescence studies in HeLa cells transfected with Myc-Akt showed that IGF1-induced membrane localization of Akt was completely blocked by pretreatment with API-1 (10 μM, 30 min). Similar results were observed with GFP-tagged full-length Akt and GFP-tagged Akt PH domain alone, confirming that API-1 acts through the PH domain.  

This mechanism explains API-1's ability to inhibit both growth factor-stimulated Akt activation and constitutively active mutants that depend on PH domain function for membrane localization. Importantly, API-1 did not affect the activity or phosphorylation of upstream regulators including PI3K, PDK1, or mTOR complexes, demonstrating its specificity for Akt.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

**Selectivity Profile of API-1**  

API-1 shows remarkable selectivity for Akt over related kinases. In vitro kinase assays demonstrated no significant inhibition of PKA, PKC, or SGK activities at concentrations up to 10 μM, while positive control inhibitors showed expected inhibition. Similarly, API-1 had no detectable effect on phosphorylation or activity of other signaling molecules including ERK, JNK, p38, and STAT3 in OVCAR3 cells treated for up to 24 hours.  

This selectivity profile represents a significant improvement over prior art Akt inhibitors, many of which show substantial off-target activity against other AGC family kinases or upstream regulators of Akt. The unique binding mode of API-1 to the PH domain accounts for this exceptional selectivity.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

**Inhibition of Constitutively Active Akt Mutants**  

A key advantage of API-1 is its ability to inhibit various forms of constitutively active Akt, including:  
1) Myristoylated Akt1 and Akt2 (membrane-targeted through lipid modification)  
2) Akt1-E40K (PH domain mutant)  
3) Naturally occurring cancer-associated mutant AKT1-E17K  

In vitro kinase assays showed that API-1 (5 μM) inhibited kinase activity of all these mutants by >80%. For AKT1-E17K, API-1 reduced both kinase activity and phosphorylation at Thr308/Ser473 in a dose-dependent manner (IC50 ~1 μM). This is particularly significant as AKT1-E17K is resistant to many allosteric Akt inhibitors currently in development.  

### EXAMPLE 5  

**Cellular Effects in Cancer Models**  

API-1 demonstrated selective cytotoxicity against cancer cells with hyperactivated Akt:  
- In OVCAR3 (ovarian) and MDA-MB-468 (breast) cancer cells, API-1 (10 μM) induced 50-70% growth inhibition and 4-9 fold increase in apoptosis  
- Similar effects were seen in PANC1 (pancreatic) and MCF7 (breast) cancer cells  
- Cells with normal Akt activity (OVCAR5, MDA-MB-435s) showed only 10-30% growth inhibition and minimal apoptosis at the same concentration  

Mechanistically, API-1 treatment led to dephosphorylation of Akt downstream targets including GSK3β, mTOR, p70 S6K, Bad, and FOXO3a, consistent with specific Akt pathway inhibition. Comparison with another Akt inhibitor (API-2/triciribine) showed that API-1 was significantly more potent in inhibiting both wild-type and mutant Akt activity.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

**In Vivo Antitumor Activity**  

In nude mouse xenograft models:  
- API-1 (10 mg/kg/day, i.p.) inhibited growth of OVCAR3 tumors by 70% and PANC1 tumors by 50% compared to vehicle controls  
- Tumors with normal Akt activity (OVCAR5, COLO357) showed minimal response to API-1 treatment  
- Tumor samples from API-1-treated mice showed ~70% reduction in phospho-Akt levels without changes in total Akt  
- API-1 treatment caused no significant changes in body weight, blood glucose, or other signs of toxicity  

These results demonstrate that API-1 selectively inhibits tumor growth in vivo when Akt is hyperactivated, validating its potential as a targeted therapy for Akt-dependent cancers. The lack of significant toxicity at effective doses suggests a favorable therapeutic window for clinical development.

The examples demonstrate that API-1 represents a novel class of Akt inhibitors with unique properties and significant advantages over existing inhibitors.
