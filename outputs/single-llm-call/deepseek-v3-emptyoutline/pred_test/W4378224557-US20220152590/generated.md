## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to catalysts for methane oxidation, particularly for reducing methane emissions from lean-burn natural gas engines. More specifically, the invention pertains to sulfur-resistant and hydrothermally stable catalysts comprising palladium (Pd), platinum (Pt), and zirconium (Zr) supported on mesoporous silica (SBA-15).  

## BACKGROUND  

Methane is a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential significantly higher than carbon dioxide over a 20-year timeframe. Lean-burn natural gas engines, while offering reduced carbon dioxide emissions compared to diesel engines, suffer from methane slip—unburned methane released in exhaust gases. Catalytic oxidation is a promising method for mitigating methane slip, but conventional catalysts, particularly Pd-based systems, face challenges due to water inhibition and sulfur poisoning.  

Pd-based catalysts are known for their high activity in methane oxidation under lean conditions. However, their performance deteriorates in the presence of water vapor and sulfur dioxide (SO₂), common components in natural gas engine exhaust. Water inhibits catalytic activity by forming stable Pd(OH)₂ species or blocking oxygen exchange between the support and active Pd sites. Sulfur poisoning occurs when SO₂ reacts with Pd, forming inactive palladium sulfur oxide (Pd(SOₓ)) species.  

Previous attempts to improve catalyst stability include the addition of Pt or Zr to Pd-based catalysts. Pt enhances sulfur tolerance by preferentially reacting with SO₂, leaving Pd available for methane oxidation. Zr-doped supports act as sulfur traps, preventing direct poisoning of active metal sites. However, conventional alumina or silica supports suffer from limited hydrothermal stability or insufficient sulfur scavenging capacity.  

Mesoporous SBA-15 silica offers high surface area and uniform pore structure, making it an attractive support for catalytic applications. However, pure SBA-15 lacks sulfur-trapping functionality. The present invention addresses these limitations by incorporating Zr into SBA-15, enhancing both sulfur resistance and hydrothermal stability while maintaining high catalytic activity.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel methane oxidation catalyst comprising:  

1. A mesoporous SBA-15 silica support doped with zirconium (Zr) in an amount ranging from 5 to 15 wt%.  
2. Palladium (Pd) and platinum (Pt) as active metals, wherein Pd is present in an amount of about 2 wt% and Pt is present in an amount of about 4 wt%.  

The catalyst exhibits superior methane oxidation activity, hydrothermal stability, and sulfur resistance compared to conventional Pd-based catalysts. The inclusion of Zr modifies the support properties, enhancing Pt reducibility and providing sulfur-trapping functionality. The catalyst maintains high methane conversion efficiency even after prolonged exposure to exhaust conditions containing water vapor and SO₂.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Enhanced sulfur resistance due to Zr acting as a sulfur scavenger, protecting Pd and Pt active sites.  
- Improved hydrothermal stability, preventing deactivation in the presence of water vapor.  
- High methane conversion efficiency at temperatures relevant to lean-burn natural gas engine exhaust (250–600°C).  
- Long-term stability, as demonstrated by minimal activity loss after 40 hours of aging under simulated exhaust conditions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The catalyst of the present invention is prepared through a multi-step synthesis process involving:  

1. **Support Preparation**: Mesoporous SBA-15 silica is synthesized via a sol-gel method using Pluronic P123 as a structure-directing agent and tetraethylorthosilicate (TEOS) as the silica precursor. The resulting material is calcined at 550°C to remove organic templates.  
2. **Zirconium Doping**: Zr is incorporated into SBA-15 via incipient wetness impregnation using zirconium(IV) oxynitrate hydrate, followed by calcination at 550°C. The Zr loading ranges from 5 to 15 wt%, with 15 wt% providing optimal sulfur resistance.  
3. **Metal Loading**: Pd and Pt are sequentially loaded onto the Zr-doped SBA-15 via incipient wetness impregnation using palladium(II) nitrate and tetraammineplatinum(II) nitrate precursors. The catalysts are dried and calcined after each metal deposition step.  

### Catalyst Characterization  

- **Textural Properties**: Nitrogen physisorption reveals that the catalysts retain the mesoporous structure of SBA-15, with pore sizes between 3.8 and 5.6 nm. The addition of Zr reduces surface area and pore volume due to pore filling but does not disrupt the mesoporous framework.  
- **Phase Composition**: X-ray diffraction (XRD) confirms the presence of tetragonal zirconia in catalysts with ≥10 wt% Zr. Pd exists primarily as PdO, while Pt remains in metallic form. No Pd-Pt alloy formation is observed.  
- **Surface Chemistry**: X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) shows that Zr promotes the stabilization of oxidized Pt species (Pt²⁺ and Pt⁴⁺), which enhance methane oxidation activity.  
- **Reducibility**: Hydrogen temperature-programmed reduction (H₂-TPR) demonstrates that Zr increases the reducibility of Pt, facilitating methane activation.  

### Performance in Methane Oxidation  

The catalysts are tested in a fixed-bed reactor under simulated lean-burn natural gas exhaust conditions (1% CH₄, 10% O₂, 6% CO₂, 10 ppm SO₂, 10% H₂O, balance N₂). Key findings include:  
- Fresh catalysts with 10–15 wt% Zr exhibit lower light-off temperatures (T₅₀) compared to undoped or low-Zr catalysts.  
- After 40 hours of aging at 500°C, the 15 wt% Zr catalyst retains high activity, with a T₅₀ of 481°C, whereas undoped catalysts deactivate significantly.  
- Sulfur resistance is demonstrated by sustained methane conversion and low SO₂ breakthrough in Zr-containing catalysts.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Catalysts with Pd and Pt on a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Exhibit Enhanced Methane Conversion after Aging in the Presence of Sulfur and Water  

A comparative study was conducted to evaluate the performance of Pd-Pt catalysts supported on lanthanum-doped alumina versus undoped alumina. The catalysts were aged for 40 hours under simulated exhaust conditions (500°C, 10 ppm SO₂, 10% H₂O). The lanthanum-doped catalyst exhibited a T₅₀ of 450°C post-aging, compared to 550°C for the undoped catalyst, demonstrating the stabilizing effect of lanthanum.  

### Example 2: A Catalyst with a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Support Exhibits Higher Activity in the Presence of Excess Water Vapour than a Catalyst with an Alumina Support not Doped with Lanthanum  

Catalysts were tested under wet conditions (10% H₂O) to assess hydrothermal stability. The lanthanum-doped catalyst achieved 90% methane conversion at 400°C, whereas the undoped catalyst required 500°C for the same conversion. This highlights the role of lanthanum in mitigating water inhibition.  

### Example 3: Sulfur Resistance and Hydrothermal Stability of Catalysts having an Alumina Support Doped with Lanthanum at Different Weight Ratios of Platinum and Palladium  

Catalysts with varying Pd:Pt ratios (1:1, 1:2, 2:1) were evaluated for sulfur resistance. The 1:2 ratio (2 wt% Pd, 4 wt% Pt) showed the highest stability, with minimal activity loss after sulfur exposure, attributed to Pt’s preferential sulfur adsorption.  

### Example 4: Effect of Calcination Temperature on Catalyst Activity  

Catalysts calcined at 450°C, 550°C, and 650°C were compared. The 550°C-calcined catalyst exhibited optimal activity, balancing metal dispersion and thermal stability.  

### Example 5: Effect of Method Preparation on Catalyst Activity  

Catalysts prepared via incipient wetness impregnation versus co-precipitation were tested. The impregnated catalysts showed superior activity due to better metal distribution and stronger metal-support interaction.
