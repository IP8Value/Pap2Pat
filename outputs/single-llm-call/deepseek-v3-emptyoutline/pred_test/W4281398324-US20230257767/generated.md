# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, specifically to recombinant protein production in mammalian cell cultures. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel promoter derived from the Chinese hamster heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 5 (hspa5) gene, which enhances monoclonal antibody (mAb) production in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell expression systems. The promoter demonstrates sustained high activity throughout fed-batch culture, particularly in the latter stages, thereby improving overall antibody yield.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are critical biopharmaceuticals due to their high specificity and therapeutic efficacy. Over 120 mAb-based drugs have been approved since the first, Muromonab-CD3, was launched in 1986. CHO cells are the predominant host cells for mAb production due to their robust growth, efficient post-translational modifications, and compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards.  

Various strategies have been employed to enhance mAb production in CHO cells, including genomic optimization (e.g., vector integration site and copy number), transcriptional modulation (e.g., promoter and enhancer engineering), translational efficiency improvements (e.g., chaperone co-expression), and culture condition optimization (e.g., medium composition and pH control). Promoters such as human cytomegalovirus (hCMV), simian virus 40 early (SV40E), Rous sarcoma virus (RSV), and endogenous promoters like Chinese hamster elongation factor-1α (CHEF1α) have been used. However, many of these promoters exhibit declining activity in the late stages of fed-batch culture, limiting overall productivity.  

The hspa5 gene, encoding the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) chaperone BiP/GRP78, is known to be upregulated during ER stress as part of the unfolded protein response (UPR). While Hspa5 protein overexpression has been explored to mitigate ER stress in high-producing clones, the use of the hspa5 promoter (Hspa5p) to drive recombinant protein expression has not been previously demonstrated.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

Existing promoters for recombinant protein expression in CHO cells often exhibit declining activity in the late stages of fed-batch culture, leading to suboptimal antibody yields. There is a need for a promoter that maintains high activity throughout the entire culture period, particularly during the latter phases when cell density and productivity are critical.  

### Solution to Problem  

The invention provides a novel promoter derived from the upstream region of the Chinese hamster hspa5 gene (Hspa5p), which demonstrates sustained high activity in CHO cells, especially during the late stages of fed-batch culture. The promoter includes a conserved 0.6 kb region upstream of the hspa5 transcription start site, containing core promoter elements (TATA-box, CpG island, CCAAT-box, and GC-box) and ER stress-responsive elements (ERSE). This region drives high-level expression of transgenes, including mAbs, with improved yield compared to conventional promoters like human elongation factor-1α (hEF1α).  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

1. **Sustained High Activity**: Hspa5p maintains strong transcriptional activity throughout fed-batch culture, unlike conventional promoters that decline in late stages.  
2. **Improved Antibody Yield**: Cells expressing mAbs under Hspa5p show higher specific production rates (SPR) and titers, particularly in the latter culture phases.  
3. **Broad Applicability**: The promoter is effective for various IgG subclasses (IgG1, IgG2, IgG4Pro) and is functional across different CHO cell lines and culture media.  
4. **Endogenous ER Stress Synergy**: Hspa5p activity is synergistically enhanced by ER stress induced by recombinant protein production, creating a positive feedback loop for sustained expression.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The Hspa5p of the invention comprises the 0.6 kb region upstream of the hspa5 transcription start site (SEQ ID NO: 1), which includes conserved core promoter elements and ERSE. The promoter can be cloned into expression vectors to drive transgene expression, such as mAb heavy and light chains.  

**Vector Construction**:  
- The Hspa5p sequence is inserted into a plasmid upstream of the luciferase reporter gene (e.g., pGL4.10) or mAb genes (e.g., IgG heavy and light chains).  
- Stable CHO cell pools are generated by transfection and selection with Geneticin.  

**Culture Conditions**:  
- Fed-batch cultures are performed in custom media (e.g., G13 or CD DA1) in Erlenmeyer flasks or small-scale bioreactors.  
- Cultures are maintained at 37°C, 5% CO2, and controlled dissolved oxygen (50% air saturation) and pH (7.00 ± 0.05).  

**Productivity Assessment**:  
- Luciferase activity or mAb titers are measured over 14 days.  
- Transcript levels of transgenes and endogenous hspa5 are quantified by qPCR.  
- mAb quality (e.g., aggregation) is analyzed by size-exclusion chromatography.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Identification of Hspa5p**  
Transcriptome analysis of CHO-K1 cells cultured in fed-batch mode revealed that hspa5 mRNA levels increased in late culture stages, unlike other highly expressed genes (e.g., gapdh, actβ). A 3.0 kb upstream region of hspa5 was cloned into pGL4.10, and transient luciferase assays confirmed its high activity.  

**Example 2: Promoter Optimization**  
Truncation studies identified a 0.6 kb minimal Hspa5p (SEQ ID NO: 1) with the highest activity. Heterologous promoters from human, mouse, and rat hspa5 genes showed comparable activity, indicating cross-species conservation of functional elements.  

**Example 3: mAb Production**  
CHO cells expressing IgG1, IgG2, or IgG4Pro under Hspa5p exhibited:  
- 1.5- to 2-fold higher SPR in late culture (day 10–14) vs. hEF1αp controls.  
- 20–30% higher final titers (day 14).  
- No differences in cell growth, metabolism, or product quality.  

**Example 4: ER Stress Correlation**  
Clones with higher mAb productivity showed upregulated UPR genes (PERK, IRE1, ATF6), confirming Hspa5p activation by ER stress. However, exogenous ER stress inducers (e.g., DTT) did not further enhance productivity, indicating endogenous stress suffices.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The Hspa5p of the invention is industrially applicable for large-scale mAb production in CHO cells, particularly in fed-batch or perfusion cultures where sustained late-stage expression is critical. It is compatible with existing CHO expression systems and can be integrated into commercial manufacturing processes without requiring additional culture additives or modifications. The promoter’s broad applicability across IgG subclasses and robustness under diverse culture conditions make it a versatile tool for biopharmaceutical production.
