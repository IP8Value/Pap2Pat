# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) contrast agents, particularly to manganese-intercalated graphitic and graphene-based nanostructures exhibiting enhanced proton relaxivity for improved MRI diagnostic applications. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel compositions comprising manganese ions confined within graphene sheets, graphene nanoplatelets, or graphene nanoribbons, which demonstrate superior relaxivity properties compared to conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes. The invention further encompasses methods of synthesizing such compositions and their use as high-efficiency T₁ MRI contrast agents.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a cornerstone of modern diagnostic radiology, providing non-invasive anatomical and functional insights into tissues and organs. Contrast agents (CAs) are routinely administered to enhance image quality by altering the relaxation times of water protons in tissues. Current clinical MRI CAs predominantly comprise gadolinium (Gd³⁺) or manganese (Mn²⁺) chelate complexes, which operate by shortening longitudinal (T₁) or transverse (T₂) relaxation times. However, the relaxivity of these agents remains suboptimal, with theoretical models suggesting potential improvements of up to 100-fold.  

Gadolinium-based CAs, while effective, pose risks such as nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF) in patients with renal impairment, prompting regulatory restrictions. Manganese, an essential trace element, has re-emerged as a safer alternative due to its biological compatibility. However, conventional Mn²⁺ chelates exhibit limited relaxivity, necessitating higher doses for clinical efficacy. Recent advances in nanotechnology have explored carbon nanostructures (e.g., carbon nanotubes, fullerenes) as carriers for paramagnetic ions. For instance, gadolinium-encapsulated single-walled carbon nanotubes (gadonanotubes) exhibit relaxivities orders of magnitude higher than small-molecule chelates. Despite these advances, the potential of Mn²⁺-intercalated graphene nanostructures remains unexplored.  

A critical gap exists in understanding how geometric confinement of paramagnetic ions within graphene sheets influences relaxivity. Prior studies on gadonanotubes suggest that nanoporous confinement alters relaxation mechanisms, but whether this effect extends to other graphitic architectures is unknown. Moreover, existing contrast agents suffer from inconsistent performance across magnetic field strengths, limiting their versatility in clinical MRI systems operating at varying field strengths (0.01–3 Tesla).  

Thus, there is an unmet need for Mn²⁺-based contrast agents that combine high relaxivity, biocompatibility, and field-strength adaptability. The present invention addresses these limitations by leveraging the unique properties of graphene-confined manganese ions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides manganese-intercalated graphitic and graphene nanostructures as high-performance MRI contrast agents. Key aspects include:  

1. **Composition**: The contrast agents comprise manganese ions (Mn²⁺) confined within graphene-based nanostructures, including but not limited to graphene nanoplatelets, graphene nanoribbons, and oxidized/reduced graphene derivatives. The confinement is achieved via intercalation during synthesis, wherein Mn²⁺ ions are embedded between graphene sheets, as confirmed by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR).  

2. **Enhanced Relaxivity**: The nanostructures exhibit longitudinal relaxivity (r₁) values 8–10 times higher and transverse relaxivity (r₂) values 19–60 times higher than conventional Mn²⁺ or Gd³⁺ chelates at 0.47 Tesla. Remarkably, graphene nanoribbons demonstrate relaxivities up to 724 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ at ultra-low fields (0.01 MHz), surpassing all known clinical agents.  

3. **Unique NMRD Profiles**: Nuclear magnetic resonance dispersion (NMRD) studies reveal unconventional field-dependent relaxivity profiles, distinct from small-molecule or macromolecular chelates. For example, graphene nanoribbons show a steep, linear increase in relaxivity at fields below 10 MHz, a feature attributed to confinement-induced modifications in inner-sphere water dynamics.  

4. **Synthesis Method**: The nanostructures are synthesized via oxidative exfoliation of graphite or multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) using potassium permanganate (KMnO₄), followed by reduction or functionalization. Trace Mn²⁺ ions become intercalated during this process, as evidenced by SQUID magnetometry and EPR spectroscopy.  

5. **Applications**: The compositions are suitable as T₁-dominant contrast agents for clinical MRI, particularly in neurological and cardiovascular imaging. Their biocompatibility and high relaxivity enable lower doses, reducing toxicity risks.  

The invention thus establishes manganese-intercalated graphene nanostructures as a paradigm-shifting class of MRI contrast agents, combining unprecedented relaxivity with scalable synthesis and clinical adaptability.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### 1. Composition and Structure  

The invention encompasses manganese-intercalated graphene nanostructures with the following structural characteristics:  

- **Graphene Nanoplatelets**: These comprise 1–10 layers of graphene sheets with Mn²⁺ ions intercalated between layers. Oxidation during synthesis introduces functional groups (e.g., carboxyl, epoxy) that stabilize Mn²⁺ confinement. Reduced derivatives exhibit superparamagnetism at room temperature, as confirmed by SQUID hysteresis curves.  

- **Graphene Nanoribbons**: Derived from oxidative unzipping of MWCNTs, these structures feature Mn²⁺ ions confined within ribbon-like graphene domains. EPR spectra reveal hyperfine coupling constants (Aₘₙ ≈ 95 Gauss), indicative of octahedral Mn²⁺ coordination.  

- **Mn²⁺ Confinement**: ICP-OES data confirm Mn²⁺ concentrations of 0.1–1.0 wt%, with no free Mn²⁺ detected in solution. The confinement is further evidenced by large EPR linewidths (ΔH₁/₂ > 30 Gauss), reflecting strong dipolar interactions between Mn²⁺ ions.  

### 2. Synthesis Methodology  

#### 2.1 Graphene Nanoplatelets  
1. **Oxidation**: Micro-graphite is oxidized via Hummer’s method using KMnO₄/H₂SO₄, introducing Mn²⁺ intercalation.  
2. **Exfoliation**: The oxidized product is sonicated in water to yield graphene nanoplatelets.  
3. **Reduction**: Hydrazine hydrate treatment at 100°C produces reduced nanoplatelets with enhanced superparamagnetism.  

#### 2.2 Graphene Nanoribbons  
1. **Oxidative Unzipping**: MWCNTs are treated with KMnO₄/H₂SO₄ at 55–70°C, cleaving tubes into nanoribbons.  
2. **Purification**: Washing with water/ethanol removes residual KMnO₄, leaving intercalated Mn²⁺.  

### 3. Mechanism of Enhanced Relaxivity  

The relaxivity enhancement arises from:  
- **Inner-Sphere Water Coordination**: Fits to Solomon-Bloembergen-Morgan (SBM) equations indicate 8 inner-sphere water molecules (q = 8), a 4-fold increase over conventional chelates.  
- **Slow Water Exchange**: Water residence times (τₘ ≈ 100 ns) are prolonged due to graphene confinement, amplifying proton relaxation.  
- **Nanoconfinement Effects**: Graphene sheets restrict Mn²⁺ mobility, increasing rotational correlation times (τᵣ ≈ 1–100 ns) and dipolar interactions.  

### 4. Comparative Advantages  

- **Safety**: Mn²⁺ is biologically essential, unlike nephrotoxic Gd³⁺.  
- **Field Adaptability**: Unprecedented relaxivity at low fields (<1 Tesla) addresses a critical need for portable MRI systems.  
- **Scalability**: Synthesis uses inexpensive graphite/MWCNT precursors and standard chemical methods.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Synthesis of Oxidized Graphene Nanoplatelets  
1. Micro-graphite (200 mg) was oxidized in KMnO₄/H₂SO₄ for 2 h.  
2. The product was sonicated in water (200 mL) for 1 h, then centrifuged to isolate nanoplatelets.  
3. ICP-OES confirmed 0.5 wt% Mn²⁺ intercalation.  

#### Example 2: Relaxivity Measurement  
1. Graphene nanoribbons (1 mg) were dispersed in 1% Pluronic F127 and sonicated.  
2. Supernatant relaxivity at 0.47 Tesla: r₁ = 65 mM⁻¹s⁻¹, r₂ = 1200 mM⁻¹s⁻¹.  

#### Example 3: NMRD Profiling  
1. Field-cycling relaxometry (0.01–80 MHz) revealed r₁ = 724 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ at 0.01 MHz for nanoribbons.  

#### Example 4: In Vitro Safety  
1. Cell viability assays (HeLa cells) showed >90% survival at 0.1 mg/mL, comparable to Gd-DTPA.
