# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  
The present invention relates to medical devices and surgical methods for hemodialysis access. Specifically, the invention pertains to an optimized arteriovenous graft design with a predetermined graft-to-vein anastomosis angle that minimizes pathological flow fields in the venous outflow tract. The invention is particularly directed to brachio-axillary arteriovenous grafts used for chronic hemodialysis in patients with end-stage renal disease.  

## BACKGROUND  
Hemodialysis is a life-sustaining procedure for patients with end-stage renal disease, requiring reliable vascular access through arteriovenous grafts or fistulas. Conventional arteriovenous grafts, typically made of polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), are anastomosed to an artery at one end and a vein at the other end, with the graft body tunneled subcutaneously to facilitate repeated cannulation. Despite advances in graft materials, a significant proportion of arteriovenous grafts fail within the first year due to thrombosis, often caused by pathological flow conditions at the graft-to-vein anastomosis.  

Current surgical techniques for graft implantation allow surgeons to select the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle empirically based on anatomical constraints. This variability in anastomotic angles contributes to inconsistent hemodynamic performance, with certain angles promoting pathological shear stresses that lead to intimal hyperplasia, stenosis, and eventual graft failure. Prior attempts to improve graft patency have focused on pharmacological interventions or mechanical modifications such as venous cuffs, but these approaches have not significantly reduced failure rates.  

There remains an unmet need for an arteriovenous graft design that optimizes hemodynamic flow conditions at the venous anastomosis to minimize thrombotic complications and improve long-term patency rates.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  
The present invention provides an arteriovenous graft with a predetermined graft-to-vein anastomosis angle that has been optimized through computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analysis to minimize pathological shear stress conditions in the adjacent vein. The invention is based on the discovery that anastomosis angles between approximately 20° and 40°, and particularly about 30°, create optimal flow conditions that avoid both excessively high shear rates (which can cause endothelial injury) and excessively low shear rates (which promote thrombosis).  

The optimized arteriovenous graft comprises a tubular conduit with a tapered diameter, transitioning from a smaller arterial end (typically about 4 mm) to a larger venous end (typically about 7 mm). The venous end features a beveled hood configured to create the predetermined optimal anastomosis angle when surgically connected to a vein. The graft may be manufactured with markings or indicators to guide proper orientation during implantation.  

The invention also encompasses surgical methods for implanting the optimized arteriovenous graft, including techniques for achieving the predetermined anastomosis angle and verifying proper hemodynamic flow conditions intraoperatively.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  
The present invention provides an arteriovenous graft design that has been optimized through extensive computational modeling to promote physiological flow conditions at the venous anastomosis. Computational fluid dynamics simulations were performed using anatomically accurate models of brachio-axillary arteriovenous grafts with varying anastomosis angles. The simulations accounted for pulsatile blood flow, non-Newtonian fluid properties of blood, and realistic boundary conditions based on clinical measurements.  

The computational models demonstrated that the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle significantly affects wall shear stress distribution in the adjacent vein. Anastomosis angles greater than approximately 40° were found to create regions of excessively high shear stress (>1000 s⁻¹), which can damage the venous endothelium and promote platelet aggregation. Conversely, angles less than approximately 20° created regions of excessively low shear stress (<50 s⁻¹), which promote fibrin deposition and thrombosis.  

The optimal anastomosis angle was determined to be approximately 30°, as this configuration minimized both high and low shear stress regions while maintaining physiological flow patterns. At this angle, the majority of the venous wall experiences shear stresses between 50 s⁻¹ and 1000 s⁻¹, which are known to maintain endothelial health and prevent thrombotic complications.  

The optimized arteriovenous graft of the invention comprises several key features:  

1. A tapered tubular structure with an inner diameter transitioning from about 4 mm at the arterial end to about 7 mm at the venous end, suitable for brachio-axillary placement.  

2. A beveled venous end configured to create a 30° anastomosis angle when surgically connected to a vein, with the exact angle being adjustable within a range of 25° to 35° to accommodate anatomical variations.  

3. Optional orientation markers on the graft exterior to guide proper surgical positioning and ensure correct anastomotic angle.  

4. The graft may be constructed from conventional materials such as expanded PTFE, with or without surface modifications to enhance biocompatibility.  

The invention also includes surgical implantation techniques that ensure proper achievement of the optimized anastomosis angle. These techniques may involve specialized clamps or alignment tools that maintain the predetermined angle during suturing. Intraoperative flow measurements or imaging may be used to verify optimal hemodynamic conditions before completing the procedure.  

### EXAMPLES  
Example 1: Computational Optimization of Anastomosis Angle  
A series of computational fluid dynamics simulations were performed to evaluate different graft-to-vein anastomosis angles ranging from 13° to 90°. Each model incorporated realistic blood properties, pulsatile flow conditions, and anatomical dimensions typical of brachio-axillary grafts. The simulations tracked wall shear stress distributions over complete cardiac cycles and calculated metrics for pathological flow conditions.  

The results demonstrated that a 30° anastomosis angle provided the optimal balance between minimizing both high shear stress regions (which decreased with smaller angles) and low shear stress regions (which increased with angles below 30°). This angle resulted in less than 5% of the venous wall area experiencing pathological shear stresses during the cardiac cycle.  

Example 2: Prototype Graft Design  
A prototype arteriovenous graft was manufactured with a 30° bevel at the venous end. The graft featured a tapered design with a 4 mm arterial end and 7 mm venous end, constructed from expanded PTFE. Orientation markers were placed along the graft length to assist with proper rotational alignment during implantation.  

Example 3: Surgical Implantation Technique  
The optimized graft was implanted in a surgical model using a specialized alignment tool that maintained the 30° anastomosis angle during suturing. Intraoperative ultrasound confirmed physiological flow patterns with minimal flow disturbances at the venous anastomosis. Post-implantation computational modeling of the implanted configuration verified maintenance of optimal shear stress conditions.  

The examples demonstrate that the invention provides a significant improvement over conventional arteriovenous grafts by systematically optimizing the hemodynamic performance through controlled anastomosis geometry. This approach addresses a major cause of graft failure while maintaining compatibility with existing surgical techniques and materials.
