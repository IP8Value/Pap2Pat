# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of veterinary vaccines and more specifically to plant-produced virus-like particle (VLP) vaccines against porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2). PCV-2 is a significant pathogen in swine populations worldwide, causing substantial economic losses to the global pork industry through various associated disease syndromes including postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome, and reproductive failure.  

Current vaccination strategies against PCV-2 rely primarily on whole-killed virus vaccines or subunit vaccines based on the capsid protein (CP) encoded by open reading frame 2 (ORF2) of the viral genome. While these vaccines have demonstrated efficacy, they present certain limitations including production challenges, safety concerns, and variable immunogenicity. The production of conventional vaccines typically requires complex and expensive cell culture systems with stringent biosafety requirements.  

Virus-like particles have emerged as promising vaccine candidates as they mimic the native virion structure without containing viral genetic material, thereby combining the immunogenicity advantages of whole virus vaccines with the safety profile of subunit vaccines. However, current VLP production systems using bacterial, yeast, or insect cell cultures remain costly and technically demanding. There exists a need for more economical, scalable, and safe production platforms for PCV-2 vaccines that can meet global demand while maintaining high immunogenicity standards.  

Plant-based expression systems offer several advantages over traditional production platforms, including lower production costs, reduced risk of contamination with animal pathogens, and scalability. The present invention addresses the limitations of current PCV-2 vaccine production methods by providing a novel plant-based system for producing immunogenic PCV-2 VLPs.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for producing immunogenic PCV-2 virus-like particles (VLPs) in plant expression systems. In one embodiment, the invention comprises a recombinant nucleic acid construct containing a plant-optimized PCV-2 capsid protein (CP) coding sequence operably linked to regulatory elements for expression in plant cells.  

The invention further provides methods for transient expression of PCV-2 CP in plants, particularly through Agrobacterium-mediated infiltration of Nicotiana benthamiana leaves. The expressed CP self-assembles into VLPs that are morphologically similar to native PCV-2 virions. The invention includes efficient purification methods for these plant-produced VLPs using density gradient centrifugation techniques.  

In another aspect, the invention provides vaccine compositions comprising the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs and methods for inducing protective immune responses against PCV-2 in swine. The plant-produced VLPs demonstrate strong immunogenicity in animal models, comparable to commercial PCV-2 vaccines, while offering production advantages in terms of safety, scalability, and cost-effectiveness.  

The plant-based production system of the present invention represents a significant advancement in PCV-2 vaccine technology by providing a safe, efficient, and economically viable alternative to conventional production methods. The invention has particular utility in veterinary medicine for the prevention and control of PCV-2-associated diseases in swine populations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel plant-based production system for PCV-2 virus-like particles (VLPs) and related vaccine compositions. The detailed description that follows sets forth preferred embodiments of the invention, but it should be understood that these embodiments represent examples of the invention's application and do not limit its scope.  

The foundation of the invention lies in the discovery that the PCV-2 capsid protein (CP) can be efficiently expressed in plant cells and self-assemble into immunogenic VLPs. The CP is encoded by ORF2 of the PCV-2 genome and represents the major antigenic determinant for protective immune responses. The inventors developed a plant-optimized version of the PCV-2 ORF2 gene that has been modified for enhanced expression in plant systems while maintaining the authentic antigenic structure of the viral protein.  

For expression in plants, the optimized PCV-2 CP coding sequence is incorporated into a recombinant expression vector, preferably the pEAQ-HT vector system. This vector contains regulatory elements suitable for high-level transient expression in plant cells, including a cauliflower mosaic virus (CaMV) 35S promoter and terminator sequences. The vector is transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strains, particularly LBA4404 or EHA105, for subsequent plant infiltration.  

The transient expression system employs vacuum infiltration of Nicotiana benthamiana plants with recombinant Agrobacterium cultures. Optimal protein expression occurs when leaves are harvested approximately 4 days post-infiltration (dpi) using Agrobacterium cultures at an optical density (OD600) of 1.0. This expression system yields approximately 6.5 mg of purified PCV-2 CP per kilogram of fresh leaf weight, representing a significant improvement over some conventional expression systems.  

Following expression, the plant material is homogenized and clarified by centrifugation. The PCV-2 CP is then purified using density gradient centrifugation techniques. A particularly effective purification method involves sucrose gradient centrifugation, where the VLPs are concentrated in the pellet fraction. Further purification can be achieved using cesium chloride (CsCl) density gradient centrifugation, with the VLPs typically banding at a density of approximately 1.564 g/cm³.  

Transmission electron microscopy analysis confirms that the plant-produced PCV-2 CP self-assembles into VLPs with diameters ranging between 12-24 nm, closely resembling native PCV-2 virions. The VLPs demonstrate excellent stability and maintain their structural integrity through purification processes.  

Immunogenicity studies in mice demonstrate that the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs induce strong antibody responses comparable to commercial PCV-2 vaccines. The immune response is specific to PCV-2 CP and shows significant elevation compared to control groups, indicating the vaccine potential of the plant-produced VLPs.  

The invention also encompasses vaccine formulations containing the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs. These formulations may include appropriate adjuvants, such as mineral oil-based adjuvants (e.g., MONTANIDE™ ISA 50V2), and may be administered via various routes including intramuscular or subcutaneous injection. The vaccine compositions are particularly suited for immunization of swine against PCV-2 infection and associated diseases.  

### Example 1  

A specific embodiment of the invention involves the production of PCV-2 VLPs in Nicotiana benthamiana plants. A plant-optimized PCV-2 ORF2 gene (GenBank accession number KJ128269) was synthesized with modifications including codon optimization for plant expression, increased GC content, and removal of unwanted restriction sites. The optimized gene was cloned into the pEAQ-HT expression vector between AgeI and XhoI restriction sites.  

The recombinant vector was transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404. Bacterial cultures were grown to OD600 1.0 in induction medium containing acetosyringone. Six-to-eight-week-old N. benthamiana plants were vacuum infiltrated with the bacterial suspension and maintained under controlled growth conditions.  

Leaves were harvested at 4 days post-infiltration and homogenized in extraction buffer. The crude extract was clarified by centrifugation at 8,000 g for 20 minutes. The supernatant was subjected to sucrose density gradient centrifugation (45% and 65% sucrose layers) at 120,000 g for 4 hours. The pellet fraction containing PCV-2 VLPs was resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).  

Further purification was achieved by CsCl density gradient centrifugation (1.285-1.658 g/cm³ range) at 180,000 g for 16 hours. The VLP-containing fraction was identified by immunoblotting and had a buoyant density of 1.564 g/cm³. Transmission electron microscopy confirmed the presence of VLPs with diameters of 16-24 nm.  

Protein quantification estimated a yield of 6.5 mg PCV-2 CP per kg of fresh leaf weight. Immunization studies in mice demonstrated that the plant-produced VLPs induced PCV-2-specific antibody responses comparable to a commercial PCV-2 vaccine (Ingelvac CircoFLEX®).  

### Discussion  

The plant-based production system of the present invention offers several advantages over conventional PCV-2 vaccine production methods. The transient expression system provides rapid protein production without the need for stable transformation or complex bioreactor systems. The use of plants eliminates concerns about contamination with animal pathogens that may arise with mammalian or insect cell culture systems.  

The purification process is simplified compared to traditional methods, as the VLPs can be efficiently concentrated and partially purified through a single sucrose gradient centrifugation step. This represents a significant cost advantage over affinity chromatography-based purification methods commonly used with other expression systems.  

The immunogenicity data demonstrate that the plant-produced VLPs maintain the conformational epitopes necessary for inducing protective immune responses. The immune response elicited in mice suggests that the plant-produced VLPs would be effective in swine, the natural host for PCV-2.  

The scalability of the plant-based production system makes it particularly suitable for meeting global demand for PCV-2 vaccines. The entire production process from plant infiltration to purified VLPs can be completed within one week, offering potential advantages in rapid response to emerging PCV-2 variants or outbreaks.  

The invention also has potential applications beyond vaccines, as the plant-produced PCV-2 VLPs may be useful as diagnostic reagents or as platforms for displaying heterologous antigens. The principles demonstrated with PCV-2 may be applicable to other circoviruses or small non-enveloped viruses of veterinary importance.
