# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Autoimmune diseases represent a significant and growing clinical challenge, affecting an estimated 15-24 million patients in the United States alone. These conditions, which include relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS), often lead to irreversible tissue damage before diagnosis occurs, creating a critical need for improved methods of early detection and monitoring. Current diagnostic approaches, such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), provide limited information about underlying immunopathology and cannot reliably predict disease relapse or monitor therapeutic response.  

The current standard of care for autoimmune conditions like RRMS involves continuous, long-term immunosuppressive therapy, which carries substantial side effects and demonstrates variable efficacy among patients. Furthermore, existing diagnostic methods fail to provide real-time information about immune system activity within affected tissues, making it impossible to implement targeted, preemptive treatment strategies that could prevent disease relapse before tissue damage occurs.  

There exists an urgent need for a reliable, minimally invasive system capable of monitoring immune dysregulation within target tissues to identify relapse onset and predict therapeutic response. Such a system would enable strategic administration of treatment pulses rather than lifelong immunosuppression, fundamentally transforming the management of autoimmune diseases.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel system for monitoring immune activity and predicting autoimmune disease progression through implantable immunological niches (INs). The invention comprises microporous polymeric scaffolds that, when implanted subcutaneously, form vascularized tissues that dynamically reflect the immune status of affected organs. These engineered INs serve as accessible biopsy sites that capture immunological changes occurring within target tissues, such as the central nervous system in multiple sclerosis, before clinical symptoms manifest.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

The development of implantable microporous scaffolds that induce formation of vascularized immunological niches capable of extravasating immune cells from circulation. These INs maintain populations of both innate and adaptive immune cells that mirror the phenotypic alterations occurring in diseased tissues.  

A method for detecting impending autoimmune disease relapse through analysis of gene expression signatures within the INs. The invention identifies specific gene expression patterns that correlate with disease onset, remission, and relapse, enabling preemptive therapeutic intervention before tissue damage occurs.  

A system for monitoring therapeutic response by analyzing changes in the IN gene expression profile following treatment administration. This allows for rapid assessment of treatment efficacy and facilitates personalized therapeutic strategies.  

The invention further provides methods for fabricating the implantable scaffolds, implanting them subcutaneously, and analyzing the immunological niches through gene expression profiling and cellular characterization. The system represents a significant advancement over current diagnostic methods by providing real-time, tissue-specific immunological data that can guide clinical decision-making.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to implantable immunological niches (INs) and their use in monitoring autoimmune disease activity. The INs comprise microporous polymeric scaffolds that, following subcutaneous implantation, become vascularized and infiltrated by host immune cells. These engineered tissues serve as accessible proxies for monitoring immune activity within affected organs, enabling early detection of disease relapse and assessment of therapeutic response.  

The scaffolds are fabricated from biocompatible, biodegradable polymers such as poly(ε-caprolactone) (PCL) using a salt porogen technique. This process creates interconnected pores with diameters ranging from 100-500 μm, allowing for cellular infiltration and vascularization. The scaffolds may be formed into various shapes and sizes suitable for subcutaneous implantation, typically ranging from 2-10 mm in diameter and 1-3 mm in thickness.  

Following implantation, the scaffolds become populated with immune cells including CD4+ T-cells, CD8+ T-cells, dendritic cells, macrophages, and other leukocytes. The cellular composition and gene expression profile of these INs dynamically reflects systemic immune status, particularly changes associated with autoimmune disease activity. Notably, the INs demonstrate gene expression patterns that correlate with disease states before clinical symptoms appear, providing a critical window for preemptive treatment.  

The invention includes methods for analyzing IN biopsies to detect disease activity. Gene expression profiling of IN tissue reveals specific signatures comprising 21 or more differentially expressed genes that distinguish healthy from diseased states. These signatures include genes such as CD163, Il1b, Il1f9, S100a9, and TREM1, which show consistent expression patterns across different disease models and stages. Computational analysis methods, including singular value decomposition (SVD) and machine learning algorithms like bootstrap aggregated decision tree ensembles (Bagged Trees), process the gene expression data to generate quantitative scores that predict disease state.  

The system enables monitoring of disease dynamics through serial IN biopsies. During disease remission, IN gene expression patterns normalize toward healthy baselines, while relapse is marked by recurrence of disease-associated signatures. This allows clinicians to track disease progression and modify treatment strategies accordingly.  

Therapeutic monitoring represents another key application of the invention. Administration of effective treatments normalizes IN gene expression profiles, while ineffective therapies fail to alter disease-associated signatures. This enables rapid assessment of treatment response and facilitates personalized therapeutic regimens. The system has demonstrated particular utility in monitoring responses to both standard immunosuppressive therapies and novel tolerogenic nanoparticle treatments.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

In a murine model of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), microporous PCL scaffolds were implanted subcutaneously in SJL mice two weeks prior to adoptive transfer of autoreactive T-cells. Gene expression analysis of INs harvested during presymptomatic (day 7) and symptomatic (day 9) phases revealed 130 differentially expressed genes compared to controls. Computational analysis identified a 21-gene signature capable of distinguishing diseased from healthy mice with high accuracy. Notably, this signature detected disease activity before clinical symptoms appeared, demonstrating the system's predictive capability.  

### Example 2  

The invention was further validated in an active immunization model of EAE. Comparison of INs from immunized diseased mice versus controls revealed 222 differentially expressed genes. A 25-gene signature was identified, with four genes (CD163, Il1b, Il1f9, and S100a9) overlapping with the signature from the adoptive transfer model. This demonstrates the robustness of the approach across different disease induction methods.  

### Example 3  

Flow cytometric analysis of IN cellular populations showed that while total cell numbers were similar between healthy and diseased mice, specific T-cell subsets differed significantly. INs from EAE mice exhibited elevated levels of Th1 cells compared to controls, mirroring changes observed in spinal cord tissue but not in lymphoid organs. This confirms that INs reflect tissue-specific immune changes rather than systemic alterations.  

### Example 4  

Longitudinal monitoring demonstrated the system's ability to track disease dynamics. INs biopsied during disease remission showed gene expression patterns similar to healthy controls, while samples taken during relapse recurrence of disease-associated signatures. Computational scoring of these patterns accurately distinguished remission from relapse states, enabling clinical monitoring of disease activity.  

### Example 5  

The invention's predictive capability was leveraged for preemptive treatment. Administration of dexamethasone or tolerogenic nanoparticles following detection of disease-associated signatures in presymptomatic mice (day 7) prevented clinical disease onset. This demonstrates the system's potential to guide timely therapeutic intervention before tissue damage occurs.  

### Example 6  

Therapeutic monitoring was evaluated by analyzing INs from mice treated with antigen-specific nanoparticles. Effective treatment (PLP-loaded nanoparticles in EAE mice) normalized IN gene expression scores to control levels, while ineffective treatment (OVA-loaded nanoparticles) failed to alter disease-associated signatures. This establishes the system's utility in assessing treatment response.  

### Example 7  

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated the diagnostic efficacy of IN-based monitoring. Area under the curve (AUC) values ranged from 0.98-1.00 for distinguishing healthy from diseased states and 0.81-0.90 for differentiating remission from relapse, confirming the system's high predictive accuracy.  

### Example 8  

Histological examination of explanted INs revealed well-vascularized tissues containing mixed immune cell populations. The structural integrity and cellular composition remained stable over time, supporting the feasibility of repeated biopsies for longitudinal monitoring.  

### Example 9  

The invention was further validated through comparison with blood-based monitoring. While blood gene expression analysis failed to reliably distinguish diseased from healthy mice, IN-based assessment provided clear discrimination. This highlights the superior tissue-specific information provided by the IN system compared to peripheral blood analysis.
