# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Protein glycosylation represents a critical posttranslational modification wherein glycans (carbohydrate chains) attach to proteins, influencing biological processes such as cell signaling, immune responses, and cancer pathogenesis. Among these glycans, N-linked glycans—connected to asparagine residues—are abundant in membrane and secreted proteins, modified by glycosyltransferases in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus. Alterations in glycosylation patterns have been identified in various cancers, including ovarian, prostate, and breast cancer, suggesting their utility as biomarkers.  

Current cancer biomarkers, such as CA-125 for ovarian cancer, exhibit limited sensitivity, particularly in early-stage detection. Given that early diagnosis significantly improves survival rates, there is an urgent need for more reliable diagnostic methods. Mass spectrometry (MS) has emerged as a powerful tool for glycomic analysis, enabling the profiling of serum glycans without chemical derivatization. However, existing techniques lack the sensitivity and specificity required for early-stage cancer screening.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a method for analyzing serum N-glycans using MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry (MS) to identify a multibiomarker panel capable of distinguishing ovarian cancer patients from healthy controls with high accuracy.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for diagnosing ovarian cancer by analyzing N-glycans extracted from human serum. The method comprises:  
1. Isolating N-glycans from serum samples using peptide-N-glycosidase F (PNGase F) and solid-phase extraction (SPE) with graphitized carbon cartridges (GCC).  
2. Eluting glycans in sequential fractions (e.g., 10%, 20%, and 40% acetonitrile/H2O) to enhance detection of minor glycan species.  
3. Profiling the glycans using MALDI-TOF MS to generate mass spectra.  
4. Identifying biomarker peaks by comparing normalized absolute peak intensities (NAPI) between cancer patients and healthy controls.  
5. Selecting a multibiomarker panel based on statistical significance (P < 10⁻¹²) and classification efficiency (AUC > 0.72).  
6. Applying a scoring system integrating weighted biomarker intensities to classify samples.  

The method demonstrates superior sensitivity (84%) and specificity (83%) compared to conventional CA-125 testing, particularly in early-stage and recurrent ovarian cancer.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Glycan Analysis from Human Serum  

The invention involves extracting N-glycans from serum samples through denaturation and enzymatic release using PNGase F. Proteins are precipitated with ethanol, and glycans are purified via SPE with GCC. Sequential elution with 10%, 20%, and 40% acetonitrile/H2O fractions isolates glycans of varying polarity, enhancing detection of low-abundance species.  

MALDI-TOF MS analysis is performed in positive-ion reflectron mode (m/z range: 1000–3000). Glycan solutions are mixed with 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid (DHB) matrix and analyzed using internal calibrants for mass accuracy (±0.2 m/z). Peak intensities are normalized to total ion abundance (NAPI) to minimize inter-sample variability.  

### Example 2  

A training set of 40 ovarian cancer patients and 40 healthy controls was analyzed. N-glycans were isolated, and MALDI-TOF MS spectra were acquired in quadruplicate. ANOVA identified 24 potential biomarkers (P < 10⁻⁹), of which 15 were selected based on AUC thresholds (10% fraction: AUC > 0.72; 20% fraction: AUC > 0.75). The multibiomarker panel achieved an AUC of 0.89 in the training set.  

### Example 3  

A blind test set (23 controls, 37 patients) validated the panel’s predictive power. Using a scoring system with cut-offs derived from the training set, the method classified samples with 84% sensitivity and 83% specificity, outperforming CA-125 (78% sensitivity).  

### Example 4  

The method was applied to recurrent ovarian cancer cases (n=8), correctly classifying all patients. In contrast, CA-125 detected only 60% of recurrences, highlighting the panel’s utility for post-treatment monitoring.  

### Example 5  

Histological subtype analysis revealed enhanced sensitivity for clear-cell ovarian cancer (20% improvement over CA-125). The panel’s performance was consistent across serous and mucinous subtypes, though larger cohorts are needed for definitive conclusions.  

The invention thus provides a robust, MS-based diagnostic tool for ovarian cancer, enabling early detection and improved patient outcomes.
