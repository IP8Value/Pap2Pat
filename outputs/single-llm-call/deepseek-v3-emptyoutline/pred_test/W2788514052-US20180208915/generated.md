# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to mutant enzymes derived from endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases), particularly Endo-S from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, with improved transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity. The invention further encompasses methods for remodeling glycans on antibodies using these mutant enzymes, including novel one-pot transglycosylation reactions that eliminate the need for chemically synthesized glycan donors. The disclosed enzymes and methods are particularly useful for large-scale production of antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms, which are critical for therapeutic applications, antibody-drug conjugates, and glycoengineering research.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Antibody glycosylation plays a crucial role in modulating immune effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). The glycan structure attached to the Fc region of IgG significantly influences these biological activities. Current methods for producing antibodies with defined glycoforms rely on genetic engineering of expression systems or in vitro chemoenzymatic remodeling. While genetic engineering allows some control over glycosylation, it lacks precision and often results in heterogeneous glycoforms.  

In vitro chemoenzymatic remodeling using ENGases, such as Endo-S, enables precise glycan modification by transferring pre-synthesized oligosaccharide oxazolines to deglycosylated antibodies. However, this approach is limited by the residual hydrolysis activity of ENGases, which degrades the reaction product, and the requirement for large quantities of chemically synthesized oxazoline donors. Previous attempts to engineer ENGases, such as the Endo-S D233Q mutant, improved transglycosylation efficiency but still suffered from significant hydrolysis and high donor-to-acceptor ratios.  

There remains a need for ENGase mutants with enhanced transglycosylation activity, minimal hydrolysis, and compatibility with simplified reaction schemes that avoid the laborious synthesis of oxazoline donors. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing novel Endo-S mutants and methods for efficient glycan remodeling.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The primary technical problem addressed by the invention is the inefficiency of existing ENGase-based glycan remodeling methods, which are hindered by:  
1. High hydrolysis activity of ENGases, leading to degradation of the transglycosylation product.  
2. Requirement for large excesses of chemically synthesized oxazoline donors, increasing cost and complexity.  
3. Lack of robust one-pot systems for transferring glycans from natural substrates (e.g., sialoglycopeptides) to antibodies without intermediate oxazoline synthesis.  

### Solution to Problem  

The invention provides the following solutions:  
1. **Novel Endo-S Mutants**: Engineered variants of Endo-S with mutations such as D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A exhibit significantly improved transglycosylation efficiency (>90%) and reduced hydrolysis activity at low donor-to-acceptor ratios (≤5 molar eq).  
2. **One-Pot Transglycosylation**: A method combining Endo-S mutants with auxiliary ENGases (e.g., Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H) enables direct transfer of glycans from sialoglycopeptides (SGPs) to antibodies, eliminating the need for oxazoline synthesis. This system achieves >95% transglycosylation efficiency.  
3. **Scalable Production**: The mutants and methods are compatible with large-scale antibody glycoengineering, as demonstrated by the successful remodeling of trastuzumab at 10 mg scale.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The invention offers several advantages over prior art:  
1. **High Efficiency**: The Endo-S mutants achieve >90% transglycosylation at low donor-to-acceptor ratios, reducing material costs.  
2. **Minimal Hydrolysis**: Selected mutants (e.g., D233Q/Y402F/D405A) exhibit negligible hydrolysis activity, preserving the product.  
3. **Simplified Workflow**: The one-pot system bypasses oxazoline synthesis, streamlining glycan remodeling.  
4. **Broad Applicability**: The methods are compatible with various ENGases (Endo-M, Endo-CC) and glycan donors (SGPs, SG-Asn), enabling flexibility in glycoengineering.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>  

The mutant enzymes of the invention are derived from Endo-S (SEQ ID NO: 1) and include combinations of mutations such as D233Q, Q303L, E350Q, D405A, Y402F, and D279S. Preferred embodiments include:  
- **D233Q/Q303L**: Exhibits 96% transglycosylation efficiency with minimal hydrolysis.  
- **D233Q/E350Q**: Achieves rapid transglycosylation (78% at 2 h) and high final yield.  
- **D233Q/D405A**: Combines fast kinetics with low hydrolysis.  
- **D233Q/Y402F/D405A**: Shows undetectable hydrolysis activity up to 48 h.  

The mutants are produced by site-directed mutagenesis of the Endo-S gene, followed by expression in *E. coli* and purification via GST affinity chromatography.  

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>  

The genes encoding the mutant enzymes are cloned into expression vectors (e.g., pGEX-4T-3) and transformed into host cells such as *E. coli* BL21(DE3). Expression is induced with IPTG, and the enzymes are purified using glutathione sepharose and gel filtration. The purified enzymes are stable in PBS or Tris-HCl buffers (pH 6.0–7.4) and retain activity for >48 h at 4°C.  

### <Sugar Remodeling>  

The invention provides methods for remodeling antibody glycans using the mutant enzymes:  
1. **Deglycosylation**: Wild-type or mutant Endo-S removes the native glycan from IgG (e.g., trastuzumab) at 37°C in phosphate buffer (pH 6.0).  
2. **Transglycosylation**: The deglycosylated antibody is incubated with a glycan donor (e.g., SG-oxazoline or SGP) and a mutant Endo-S in Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at 28–37°C.  
3. **One-Pot Reaction**: For SGP donors, auxiliary ENGases (Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H) are added to generate the active intermediate in situ.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Hydrolysis and Transglycosylation Activity of Endo-S Mutants**  
Deglycosylated trastuzumab (800 μg) was incubated with SG-oxazoline (5 eq) and Endo-S mutants (16 μg) in Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at 30°C. Aliquots were analyzed by capillary electrophoresis. Results:  
- **D233Q/Q303L**: 96% transglycosylation at 24 h, <5% hydrolysis.  
- **D233Q/D405A**: 78% transglycosylation at 2 h, <10% hydrolysis at 48 h.  

### Example 2  

**One-Pot Transglycosylation with Endo-M N175Q**  
Deglycosylated trastuzumab (1 mg) and SGP (300 eq) were incubated with Endo-S D233Q (20 μg) and Endo-M N175Q (5 mU) at 28°C. The reaction reached 95% efficiency at 8 h, yielding homogeneous SG-trastuzumab.  

### Example 3  

**One-Pot Transglycosylation with Endo-CC N180H**  
The reaction was performed as in Example 2 but with Endo-CC N180H (33 mU) at 28°C. Efficiency reached 57% at 8 h, but hydrolysis was observed at 37°C.  

### Example 4  

**Medium-Scale Production of SG-Trastuzumab**  
Deglycosylated trastuzumab (10 mg) and SGP (300 eq) were reacted with Endo-S D233Q (200 μg) and Endo-M N175Q (0.05 U) at 28°C for 4.5 h. The product was purified by protein A and hydroxyapatite chromatography, yielding 8.0 mg of homogeneous SG-trastuzumab (97% efficiency by LC-MS).
