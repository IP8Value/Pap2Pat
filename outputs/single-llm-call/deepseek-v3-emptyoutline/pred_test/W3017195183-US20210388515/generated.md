# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to electrochemical energy conversion and storage systems, and more particularly to protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs) with improved triple conducting oxide electrodes. The invention specifically discloses a perovskite-structured triple conducting oxide material PrNi0.5Co0.5O3-δ (PNC) that simultaneously conducts protons (H+), oxygen ions (O2-), and electrons (e-), enabling superior performance in both fuel cell and electrolysis modes at reduced operating temperatures (400-600°C). The invention further encompasses methods for synthesizing the PNC material, cell configurations incorporating this electrode material, and systems utilizing such cells for reversible energy conversion between electricity and hydrogen fuel.  

## BACKGROUND  

The increasing integration of renewable energy sources into power grids has created significant challenges due to the intermittent nature of solar and wind generation. Efficient energy storage technologies are urgently needed to address the mismatch between energy supply and demand. Protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs) represent a promising solution as they can operate reversibly - producing hydrogen through water electrolysis during periods of excess renewable generation and generating electricity from the stored hydrogen when needed.  

Current PCEC technology faces several limitations that hinder widespread adoption. Conventional mixed ionic-electronic conducting (MIEC) electrodes restrict the electrochemical reactions to triple phase boundaries (TPBs) where the electrolyte, electrode, and gas phases meet. This limitation results in sluggish electrode kinetics, especially at intermediate temperatures (400-600°C). Additionally, existing electrode materials suffer from performance degradation under high steam concentrations and during thermal cycling. While the concept of triple conducting oxides (TCOs) that conduct protons, oxygen ions, and electrons could extend the active reaction zones throughout the electrode bulk, very few viable TCO materials have been developed due to the difficulty in achieving sufficient proton conduction while maintaining adequate electronic conductivity.  

Previous attempts to develop TCO materials have focused on doping rare-earth or transition metals into barium cerate/zirconate proton conductors. Examples include BaCe0.5Bi0.5O3-δ, BaZr0.5Pr0.3Y0.2O3-δ, and BaZr0.6Co0.4O3-δ. However, these materials exhibit insufficient total conductivity for practical applications. More recently, layered perovskite materials such as PrBa0.5Sr0.5Co2O5+δ have shown promise, but their proton conductivity remains unverified. There remains an unmet need for high-performance TCO electrode materials that enable efficient, durable PCEC operation at reduced temperatures.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a solution to the aforementioned challenges through the development of a novel triple conducting oxide electrode material - PrNi0.5Co0.5O3-δ (PNC). This perovskite-structured material demonstrates simultaneous proton, oxygen ion, and electron conduction, enabling electrochemical reactions to occur throughout the entire electrode volume rather than being confined to TPBs.  

Key advantages of the PNC electrode material include:  

1. Enhanced proton conductivity through reduced proton migration energy barriers, as confirmed by density functional theory (DFT) calculations and experimental measurements.  

2. Excellent chemical stability under high steam concentrations (up to 30% H2O) and during thermal cycling between 400-600°C.  

3. Superior electrochemical performance in both fuel cell and electrolysis modes, with demonstrated peak power densities of 528 mW/cm² at 600°C and electrolysis current densities of 1.31 A/cm² at 1.4V and 600°C.  

4. Capability for self-sustainable reversible operation, where hydrogen produced during electrolysis can be immediately used to generate electricity without external hydrogen supply.  

The invention further encompasses optimized microstructures for the PNC electrode, including a self-architectured mesh-like configuration with hollow fibers that enhance gas diffusion and reaction kinetics. These structural improvements enable even higher performance, achieving electrolysis current densities of 1.72 A/cm² at 1.4V and 600°C.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution for advanced protonic ceramic electrochemical cells through the development of a triple conducting oxide electrode material PrNi0.5Co0.5O3-δ (PNC). The material composition, synthesis methods, structural characteristics, electrochemical properties, and cell configurations are described in detail below.  

### Material Composition and Synthesis  

The PNC material is synthesized through a modified glycine-citrate combustion method using stoichiometric amounts of praseodymium nitrate (Pr(NO3)3·6H2O), nickel nitrate (Ni(NO3)3·6H2O), and cobalt nitrate (Co(NO3)3·6H2O) as precursors. The precursor solution is prepared by dissolving these compounds in a citrate-glycine aqueous solution. Under heating and stirring conditions, the solution forms a viscous gel that undergoes combustion to produce an ash precursor. This precursor is then calcined at 1000°C in air for 5 hours to form the perovskite crystal structure.  

The resulting PNC material crystallizes in an orthorhombic perovskite structure (space group Pbnm) with lattice parameters a = 5.405 Å, b = 5.380 Å, and c = 7.617 Å. The material maintains excellent phase stability after prolonged exposure (200 hours) to wet air (50% H2O) at 600°C, demonstrating its suitability for PCEC applications.  

### Proton Conduction Mechanism  

The proton conduction in PNC arises from a unique combination of factors:  

1. **Oxygen Vacancy Formation**: The partial substitution of Co with Ni in the B-site of the perovskite structure significantly reduces the oxygen vacancy formation energy. DFT calculations show this energy decreases from 3.40 eV to 1.61 eV at O1 sites and from 3.49 eV to 1.72 eV at O2 sites compared to undoped PrCoO3.  

2. **Hydration Behavior**: The oxygen vacancies facilitate water incorporation into the lattice to form proton defects (OH•). Thermogravimetric analysis demonstrates a 0.055% weight increase in PNC due to hydration at 500°C - significantly higher than conventional MIEC electrodes like La0.6Sr0.4Co0.2Fe0.8O3-δ (0.031%).  

3. **Proton Migration**: DFT calculations reveal reduced proton migration energy barriers in PNC compared to undoped materials. The minimum energy path for proton hopping between oxygen sites shows significantly lower activation energies, particularly for intra-octahedral hopping pathways.  

Experimental validation of proton conduction is provided through hydrogen permeation measurements. A dense PNC membrane demonstrates hydrogen fluxes of 4 × 10-8 mol cm-2 s-1 in dry 3% H2 and 1.5 × 10-7 mol cm-2 s-1 in wet 3% H2 at 500°C - exceeding the performance of conventional proton-conducting ceramics.  

### Electrochemical Performance  

When incorporated as an oxygen electrode in PCECs with BaCe0.4Zr0.4Y0.1Yb0.1O3 (BCZYYb4411) electrolyte, the PNC material demonstrates exceptional performance:  

**Fuel Cell Mode**:  
- Open circuit voltage: 1.06 V at 600°C (close to theoretical Nernst potential)  
- Peak power densities:  
  - 528 mW/cm² at 600°C  
  - 354 mW/cm² at 550°C  
  - 230 mW/cm² at 500°C  

**Electrolysis Mode**:  
- Current densities at 1.4V:  
  - 1.31 A/cm² at 600°C  
  - 0.82 A/cm² at 550°C  
  - 0.62 A/cm² at 500°C  
  - 0.27 A/cm² at 450°C  
  - 0.15 A/cm² at 400°C  

The cells demonstrate excellent stability during long-term operation (480 hours at 500°C) and thermal cycling between 400-600°C. Faradaic efficiencies range from 79.3% at 600°C to 86.2% at 500°C.  

### Microstructural Optimization  

The electrochemical performance can be further enhanced through microstructural engineering of the PNC electrode. A self-architectured mesh-like structure composed of hollow PNC fibers (3 μm diameter) with nanoparticle surfaces (20-50 nm) provides:  
- High porosity for improved gas diffusion  
- Extended triple phase boundaries throughout the electrode volume  
- Strong interfacial bonding with the electrolyte  

Cells incorporating this optimized structure achieve:  
- 15.7% higher peak power density (611 mW/cm² at 600°C)  
- 31% higher electrolysis current density (1.72 A/cm² at 1.4V and 600°C)  
- Reduced polarization resistance (0.055 Ω cm² at 500°C)  

### Self-Sustainable Reversible Operation  

A key innovation enabled by the PNC electrode is the demonstration of self-sustainable reversible operation. The PCEC can:  
1. Produce hydrogen through electrolysis (e.g., 0.6 A/cm² for 3 minutes)  
2. Immediately switch to fuel cell mode using the generated hydrogen (0.2 A/cm² for 2 minutes)  
3. Repeat this cycle without external hydrogen supply  

This capability represents a significant advancement for energy storage applications, with calculated hydrogen production rates of 44.7 mL cm-2 h-1 at 500°C and electricity generation yields of 70 mWh cm-2 per cycle.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Material Synthesis and Characterization**  
PNC powder was synthesized via glycine-citrate combustion method. Stoichiometric amounts of Pr(NO3)3·6H2O, Ni(NO3)3·6H2O, and Co(NO3)3·6H2O were dissolved in aqueous citrate-glycine solution. The resulting gel was combusted and calcined at 1000°C for 5 hours. XRD analysis confirmed single-phase orthorhombic perovskite structure (Pbnm). TEM showed homogeneous element distribution and high crystallinity.  

**Example 2: Electrochemical Cell Fabrication**  
NiO-BCZYYb4411 electrode supports were fabricated by tape casting and sintered at 1450°C. BCZYYb4411 electrolyte layers were laminated and co-sintered. PNC electrode ink was screen-printed and fired at 950°C to form full cells with 3.1 cm² active area. For enhanced structures, PNC nanofiber meshes were prepared by template method and bonded to the electrolyte.  

**Example 3: Performance Testing**  
Cells were tested in both fuel cell and electrolysis modes between 400-600°C. In fuel cell mode with pure H2 fuel, peak power densities reached 528 mW/cm² at 600°C. In electrolysis mode with 10% H2/90% Ar fuel and humidified air oxidant, current densities reached 1.31 A/cm² at 1.4V and 600°C. Cells demonstrated stable operation for 480 hours at 500°C.  

**Example 4: Reversible Operation Demonstration**  
A cell was cycled between electrolysis (0.6 A/cm², 3 min) and fuel cell (0.2 A/cm², 2 min) modes without external hydrogen supply. Stable voltages were maintained over multiple cycles, demonstrating self-sustainable operation. Hydrogen production rates reached 44.7 mL cm-2 h-1 at 500°C.
