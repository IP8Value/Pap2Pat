# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy, particularly to methods and compositions for enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against cancer cells, specifically acute myeloid leukemia (AML). More specifically, the invention pertains to the blockade of the T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT) and its ligands poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112, Nectin-2) to augment immune responses against AML cells. The invention further encompasses the combination of such blockade with bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) antibody constructs, such as AMG 330, to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a transformative approach in oncology, with immune checkpoint inhibitors and bispecific antibodies demonstrating significant clinical efficacy. Despite these advances, many patients exhibit suboptimal responses due to immune evasion mechanisms employed by tumor cells. One such mechanism involves the suppression of T-cell activation through inhibitory immune checkpoint pathways, such as those mediated by cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1). While antibodies targeting CTLA-4 (e.g., ipilimumab) and PD-1 (e.g., pembrolizumab, nivolumab) have shown promise in various malignancies, resistance and incomplete responses remain significant challenges.  

The TIGIT pathway represents a novel immune checkpoint that suppresses T-cell and natural killer (NK) cell activity upon interaction with its ligands, PVR and PVRL2. TIGIT is expressed on activated and memory T cells, regulatory T cells, and NK cells, and its engagement with PVR or PVRL2 transmits inhibitory signals through immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs). PVR and PVRL2 are overexpressed in several cancers, including AML, and their interaction with TIGIT contributes to immune evasion.  

Bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®) are another class of immunotherapeutic agents that redirect T cells to tumor cells by simultaneously binding CD3 on T cells and a tumor-associated antigen (e.g., CD33 in AML). AMG 330, a CD33/CD3 BiTE®, has shown preclinical activity in AML but may benefit from combinatorial strategies to overcome immune suppression.  

There remains an unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies that enhance T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity in AML, particularly by targeting additional immune checkpoints such as the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis. The present invention addresses this need by providing methods and compositions for blocking this axis, either alone or in combination with BiTE® antibody constructs, to improve AML treatment outcomes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity against AML cells by blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 immune checkpoint axis. The invention is based on the discovery that AML cells highly express PVR and PVRL2, and their interaction with TIGIT on immune cells suppresses anti-leukemic immune responses. Blockade of this axis, either by targeting PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT, significantly augments T-cell-mediated lysis of AML cells and synergizes with BiTE® antibody constructs such as AMG 330.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Blockade of the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 Axis**: Antibodies or other inhibitory molecules targeting PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT disrupt this inhibitory pathway, leading to enhanced T-cell activation and cytotoxicity against AML cells.  
2. **Combination with BiTE® Antibody Constructs**: The anti-leukemic effects of CD33/CD3 BiTE® constructs (e.g., AMG 330) are potentiated by concurrent blockade of the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis, resulting in superior tumor cell killing.  
3. **Diagnostic and Prognostic Utility**: High expression of PVR and PVRL2 in AML patients correlates with poor prognosis, suggesting their utility as biomarkers for identifying patients likely to benefit from TIGIT pathway blockade.  
4. **Therapeutic Compositions**: Pharmaceutical formulations comprising PVR/PVRL2/TIGIT inhibitors alone or in combination with BiTE® antibody constructs for treating AML.  

The invention further encompasses CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of PVR and PVRL2 in AML cells, which recapitulates the effects of antibody blockade and provides a genetic strategy for enhancing immune responses. Preclinical data demonstrate that PVR/PVRL2 knockout AML cells are more susceptible to T-cell-mediated killing and confer a survival advantage in vivo.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention is further illustrated by the following detailed description, which provides experimental evidence supporting the therapeutic potential of targeting the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis in AML.  

### Expression of PVR and PVRL2 in AML  

Flow cytometric analysis revealed high expression of PVR and PVRL2 on AML cell lines (n=9) and primary CD33+ AML blasts from untreated patients (n=17). Notably, leukemia-initiating cells (CD34+/CD38−) also expressed these ligands, suggesting their role in immune evasion. TIGIT was detected on 13.6 ± 3.7% of naïve CD3+ T cells from healthy donors, supporting its involvement in immune regulation.  

### Enhanced Cytotoxicity via Blockade of PVR/PVRL2 or TIGIT  

In vitro cytotoxicity assays demonstrated that blocking PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT significantly increased T-cell-mediated lysis of AML cell lines (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1, MV4-11). For example:  
- Blocking PVR and PVRL2 with antibodies increased specific lysis of TF-1 cells by 2.5-fold (p ≤ 0.001).  
- Anti-TIGIT antibodies similarly enhanced killing of MV4-11 cells (p ≤ 0.05).  
- Granzyme B secretion, a marker of T-cell activation, was elevated in supernatants following blockade.  

### Synergy with AMG 330  

The combination of PVR/PVRL2/TIGIT blockade with AMG 330 further augmented cytotoxicity:  
- TF-1 cell lysis increased by 3-fold when AMG 330 was combined with anti-PVR (p ≤ 0.001).  
- MV4-11 PVR/PVRL2 knockout cells showed enhanced susceptibility to AMG 330-mediated killing (p ≤ 0.001).  

### In Vivo Efficacy  

NSG mice transplanted with PVR/PVRL2 knockout MV4-11 cells and reconstituted with human T cells exhibited prolonged survival compared to wildtype controls:  
- Median survival: 37 days (knockout) vs. 27 days (wildtype) in the placebo group.  
- AMG 330 treatment further extended survival (33 days vs. not reached at 50 days).  

### Clinical Relevance  

High PVR/PVRL2 expression in AML patients (n=139) correlated with poor prognosis:  
- High PVR was an independent negative prognostic marker for event-free survival (HR 1.68, p=0.009).  
- PVRL2 high expressors had shorter relapse-free survival (HR 1.30, p=0.017).  

## Examples  

### Example 1: Antibody Blockade of PVR/PVRL2 Enhances AML Cell Lysis  

AML cell lines (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1) were co-cultured with healthy donor PBMCs in the presence of anti-PVR, anti-PVRL2, or both antibodies. Specific lysis was measured by flow cytometry after 24 hours. Results showed a significant increase in cytotoxicity with blockade (p ≤ 0.05).  

### Example 2: CRISPR/Cas9 Knockout of PVR/PVRL2  

MV4-11 cells were genetically modified to knockout PVR and PVRL2 using CRISPR/Cas9. These cells exhibited heightened susceptibility to T-cell-mediated killing and Granzyme B release compared to wildtype cells (p ≤ 0.001).  

### Example 3: Combination Therapy with AMG 330  

Primary AML blasts from patients were treated with AMG 330 ± anti-PVR/PVRL2 antibodies. Five of nine CD33+ samples showed enhanced lysis with combination therapy (p ≤ 0.05).  

### Example 4: In Vivo Survival Benefit  

NSG mice transplanted with PVR/PVRL2 knockout AML cells and treated with AMG 330 survived significantly longer than controls (log-rank p < 0.001).
