# DESCRIPTION  

## PRIOR ART  

The production of liquid fuels from various sources such as coal, biomass, and gas via the Fischer-Tropsch process has seen renewed interest in recent years. Diesel fuels produced through this process exhibit superior properties compared to conventional crude oil-derived diesel, including very high cetane numbers typically above 70, and virtual absence of sulfur, nitrogen, and aromatic compounds, resulting in reduced pollutant emissions.  

Traditional methods for upgrading Fischer-Tropsch products involve catalytic hydrocracking of the heaviest hydrocarbon fractions to produce middle distillates or selective hydroisomerization to produce lubricant base oils. These processes typically employ bifunctional catalysts containing both hydrogenation/dehydrogenation and acidic functions. However, existing catalysts often suffer from limitations such as excessive cracking, poor isomerization selectivity, and sensitivity to oxygenates present in Fischer-Tropsch feedstocks.  

Previous attempts to improve hydrocracking catalysts have focused on balancing the hydrogenation and acidic functions, but achieving ideal hydrocracking conditions where primary cracking dominates remains challenging. Similarly, conventional hydroisomerization catalysts often fail to maximize isomer yields while minimizing cracking, particularly for long-chain paraffins.  

## Subject-Matter of the Invention  

The present invention relates to novel bifunctional catalysts and processes for the hydrocracking and hydroisomerization of Fischer-Tropsch waxes. The catalysts are specifically designed to achieve ideal hydrocracking conditions for maximum middle distillate yields or selective hydroisomerization for lubricant base oil production.  

The invention provides a hydrocracking catalyst composition comprising:  
- A hydrogenation/dehydrogenation function selected from noble metals or reduced base metals with modifiers to suppress hydrogenolysis  
- An acidic function comprising mesoporous materials with controlled Brønsted acidity  

For hydroisomerization, the invention provides catalysts comprising:  
- Noble metal hydrogenation components  
- Shape-selective zeolites with specific pore mouth configurations optimized for monobranching  

The invention further includes processes for utilizing these catalysts under conditions that maximize desired product yields while minimizing unwanted side reactions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### The Hydrocracking and Hydroisomerisation Catalyst  

The bifunctional catalysts of the invention comprise two essential components: a hydrogenation/dehydrogenation (HD/DHD) function and an acidic function. For hydrocracking applications, the HD/DHD function preferably comprises platinum or palladium, though nickel or cobalt with copper or tin modifiers may also be employed to suppress excessive hydrogenolysis. The acidic function for hydrocracking preferably consists of amorphous silica-alumina or modified zirconia with controlled acidity to minimize secondary cracking.  

For hydroisomerization applications, the HD/DHD function preferably consists of platinum or palladium to ensure rapid hydrogenation/dehydrogenation equilibrium. The acidic function comprises shape-selective zeolites with one-dimensional pore systems, particularly those belonging to the ZSM-48 family, which demonstrate exceptional selectivity for monobranching through pore mouth and key-lock catalysis mechanisms.  

The intimacy between the HD/DHD and acidic functions is carefully controlled to meet Weisz's intimacy criterion, ensuring olefin transport does not become rate-limiting. This is achieved through precise preparation methods that distribute the metal components uniformly throughout the acidic support.  

### The Embodiments According to the Invention  

In one embodiment, the hydrocracking catalyst comprises 0.1-1.0 wt% platinum supported on a silica-alumina matrix with a silica content of 20-50 wt%. The catalyst demonstrates ideal hydrocracking behavior under mild conditions (300-350°C, 3-5 MPa), producing middle distillate yields up to 80% with minimal secondary cracking.  

In another embodiment, the hydroisomerization catalyst comprises 0.3 wt% platinum supported on ZBM-30 zeolite synthesized using triethylenetetramine as a structure-directing agent. This catalyst shows exceptional selectivity for monobranched isomers, achieving yields up to 85% for C18 paraffins at 230°C and 0.45 MPa.  

The invention further includes processes for pretreating Fischer-Tropsch feedstocks to remove oxygenates that could affect catalyst balance, as well as methods for regenerating spent catalysts.  

## The Products Obtained  

### Example 1  

A Fischer-Tropsch wax with carbon numbers predominantly above C20 was hydrocracked using a catalyst comprising 0.5 wt% Pt on silica-alumina (30% SiO2) at 330°C and 4 MPa. The product distribution showed 78% middle distillates (C10-C20), 18% naphtha (C5-C9), and 4% LPG (C3-C4), demonstrating near-ideal primary cracking behavior. The middle distillate fraction had a cetane number above 70 and excellent cold flow properties due to significant branching.  

### Example 2  

n-Octadecane was hydroisomerized over a Pt/ZBM-30 catalyst at 230°C and 0.45 MPa. At 85% conversion, the product contained 65% monobranched isomers, 20% dibranched isomers, and less than 5% cracked products. The isomerized product had a pour point below -30°C, making it suitable as lubricant base stock.  

### Example 3  

A mixed Fischer-Tropsch wax was processed using sequential hydrotreatment and hydrocracking with the invented catalysts. The hydrotreatment step removed oxygenates and olefins at 280°C, followed by hydrocracking at 340°C. The final product distribution showed 82% middle distillates with excellent cold flow properties and negligible sulfur content.  

The invention thus provides superior catalysts and processes for converting Fischer-Tropsch waxes into high-value products with exceptional efficiency and selectivity. The unique combination of optimized HD/DHD functions with tailored acidic components enables unprecedented control over product distributions and properties.
