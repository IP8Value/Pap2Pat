# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Pompe disease is a lysosomal storage disorder caused by deficiency of acid α-glucosidase (GAA), leading to pathological accumulation of glycogen in cardiac and skeletal muscle tissues. Current enzyme replacement therapies rely on recombinant human GAA (rhGAA) containing mannose 6-phosphate (M6P) residues for uptake via the cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor (CI-MPR). However, the efficiency of this uptake mechanism is limited by variable glycosylation patterns and suboptimal receptor binding affinity. There exists an unmet need for improved lysosomal targeting strategies that enhance cellular uptake and glycogen clearance while maintaining enzymatic activity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a chimeric enzyme comprising human GAA fused to a glycosylation-independent lysosomal targeting (GILT) tag derived from insulin-like growth factor II (IGF-II). The GILT tag confers high-affinity binding to the IGF-II binding site of CI-MPR, enabling efficient lysosomal delivery independent of M6P modifications. The chimeric enzyme, designated BMN 701, exhibits a 26-fold improvement in cellular uptake compared to untagged rhGAA and demonstrates superior glycogen clearance in vivo. The invention further encompasses expression vectors, host cells, pharmaceutical compositions, and methods of treating Pompe disease using the GILT-tagged GAA.  

## DEFINITIONS  

As used herein:  
- **Acid α-glucosidase (GAA)** refers to the human enzyme (UniProt ID P10253) or functional variants thereof retaining hydrolytic activity toward glycogen.  
- **GILT tag** denotes a polypeptide comprising residues 8–67 of mature IGF-II (UniProt ID P01344) or derivatives thereof retaining CI-MPR binding affinity.  
- **Furin-resistant GILT tag** refers to a GILT tag engineered to resist proteolytic cleavage by furin-like proteases.  
- **CI-MPR** (cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor) refers to the IGF2R receptor (UniProt ID P11717) or fragments thereof binding IGF-II.  
- **Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT)** denotes administration of exogenous GAA to compensate for endogenous deficiency.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Lysosomal Enzymes  
Lysosomal enzymes such as GAA require efficient delivery to lysosomes to exert their therapeutic effects. Conventional ERT relies on M6P-mediated uptake, which is limited by heterogeneity in glycosylation and competition with endogenous lysosomal enzymes for CI-MPR binding. The present invention circumvents these limitations by exploiting the high-affinity interaction between IGF-II and CI-MPR.  

### Pompe Disease  
Pompe disease results from mutations in the GAA gene, leading to lysosomal glycogen accumulation in muscle tissues. Clinical manifestations include cardiomyopathy, respiratory failure, and skeletal muscle weakness. The invention addresses the need for enhanced ERT by improving lysosomal delivery of GAA.  

### Enzyme Replacement Therapy  
BMN 701 comprises GAA (residues 70–952) fused to the GILT tag via a three-amino acid spacer (Gly-Ser-Gly). The chimeric enzyme retains native GAA activity (specific activity: 140,000 U/mg) while achieving a K<sub>uptake</sub> of 5.4 nM in L6 myoblasts, a 26-fold improvement over rhGAA (K<sub>uptake</sub>: 141 nM).  

### Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting  
The GILT tag enables lysosomal targeting independent of M6P modifications, as demonstrated by the absence of M6P competition in uptake assays (FIG. 2). This property ensures consistent pharmacokinetics regardless of glycosylation patterns.  

### Furin-Resistant GILT Tag  
The GILT tag is engineered to resist cleavage by furin-like proteases through substitution of protease-sensitive residues (e.g., Arg<sup>37</sup> → Ala). This modification enhances plasma stability without compromising CI-MPR binding.  

### Binding Affinity for the Insulin Receptor  
Surface plasmon resonance (FIG. 8) confirms high-affinity binding of BMN 701 to CI-MPR (K<sub>d</sub>: 16 nM), comparable to IGF-II (K<sub>d</sub>: 2.5 nM). The GILT tag does not cross-react with the insulin receptor, minimizing off-target effects.  

### Mutagenesis  
Site-directed mutagenesis of the GILT tag (e.g., I1572T in CI-MPR) modulates binding affinity. Variants with K<sub>d</sub> values <50 nM are preferred for therapeutic use.  

### Spacer  
A flexible spacer (e.g., Gly-Ser-Gly) between the GILT tag and GAA ensures proper folding of both domains. Spacer lengths of 3–10 amino acids are optimal.  

### Cells  
BMN 701 is produced in CHO cells (e.g., sCHO-S-GAA 701-R35) or HEK293 cells. Stable cell lines achieve titers of 250 mg/L at 1000-L scale.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Construction of GILT-tagged GAA:** Plasmid p701 was generated by ligating a DNA cassette encoding the GILT tag (residues 8–67 of IGF-II) and GAA (residues 70–952) into vector pCEP4. Transient transfection of HEK293 cells yielded BMN 701 with specific activity of 140,000 U/mg.  

### Example 2  
**Uptake Assays:** L6 myoblasts incubated with BMN 701 (5 nM) showed 25-fold higher uptake than rhGAA (141 nM). Uptake was inhibited by IGF-II (2.4 mM) but not M6P (5 mM), confirming GILT-dependent targeting (FIG. 2).  

### Example 3  
**In Vivo Glycogen Clearance:** Pompe mice treated with BMN 701 (5 mg/kg) exhibited significantly lower glycogen levels in heart and skeletal muscle than mice treated with rhGAA (20 mg/kg) (FIG. 9).  

### Example 4  
**Surface Plasmon Resonance:** BMN 701 bound CI-MPR domains 10–13 with K<sub>d</sub> of 16 nM, comparable to IGF-II (K<sub>d</sub>: 2.5 nM) (Table 1).  

### Example 5  
**Immunofluorescence:** C2C12 myoblasts internalized BMN 701, which co-localized with lysosomal marker LAMP1 (FIG. 6).  

## EQUIVALENTS  
The invention encompasses functional variants of BMN 701 with ≥80% sequence identity to the GILT tag or GAA, provided lysosomal targeting and enzymatic activity are retained. Alternative expression systems (e.g., Pichia pastoris) and fusion partners (e.g., IGF-II analogs) are also included.
