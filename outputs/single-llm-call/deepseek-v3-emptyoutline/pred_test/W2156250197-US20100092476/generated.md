## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to the field of cancer diagnostics, specifically to methods and compositions for the early detection of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) through proteomic analysis. The invention encompasses the identification of protein biomarkers in plasma or serum that are indicative of early-stage pancreatic cancer, as well as panels of such biomarkers for use in diagnostic assays.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Pancreatic cancer, particularly pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), is a highly lethal malignancy with a five-year survival rate of less than 5%. A major challenge in managing PDAC is the lack of reliable diagnostic tools for early detection, as symptoms typically manifest only at advanced stages when the disease is no longer surgically resectable. Current diagnostic methods, such as imaging and the measurement of carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9), lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity for early-stage detection or differentiation from benign conditions like chronic pancreatitis.  

Proteomic approaches have been explored for biomarker discovery, but the vast dynamic range of protein abundance in plasma and the presence of tumor-derived proteins at low concentrations present significant technical hurdles. Genetically engineered mouse (GEM) models of human cancer, which faithfully recapitulate the molecular and clinical features of PDAC, offer a controlled system for identifying tumor-associated protein changes that may be relevant to human disease. However, prior attempts using such models have not yielded clinically validated biomarkers.  

There remains an urgent need for noninvasive, blood-based diagnostic methods capable of detecting PDAC at early, treatable stages. The present invention addresses this need by leveraging proteomic analysis of GEM models to identify a panel of protein biomarkers with demonstrated relevance to human pancreatic cancer.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides methods and compositions for the early detection of pancreatic cancer by analyzing plasma or serum samples for the presence of specific protein biomarkers. The biomarkers were identified through a comprehensive proteomic analysis of plasma from a genetically engineered mouse model of PDAC, followed by validation in human samples.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Biomarker Panels**: A panel of proteins, including but not limited to ALCAM, TIMP1, ICAM1, LCN2, TNFRSF1A, REG1A, REG3, WFDC2, and IGFBP4, which are elevated in plasma or serum during early stages of pancreatic cancer.  
2. **Diagnostic Assays**: Methods for detecting these biomarkers using techniques such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), mass spectrometry, or immunohistochemistry.  
3. **Early Detection**: The panel's ability to discriminate pancreatic cancer cases from controls in blood samples collected up to 13 months prior to clinical diagnosis.  
4. **Improved Specificity**: The combination of biomarkers outperforms CA19-9 in distinguishing pancreatic cancer from chronic pancreatitis.  

The invention also encompasses kits comprising reagents for detecting the biomarkers and algorithms for interpreting assay results to provide a diagnostic score.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Materials and Methods  
The invention employs proteomic techniques to identify and validate biomarkers. Plasma samples were collected from GEM mice at early (PanIN) and advanced (PDAC) stages of tumor development, as well as from matched controls. Samples were immunodepleted of high-abundance proteins (e.g., albumin, IgG) and subjected to isotopic labeling (D0/D3 acrylamide) for quantitative analysis. Proteins were fractionated using anion-exchange and reversed-phase chromatography, followed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS).  

Protein identification and quantification were performed using computational tools (e.g., X!Tandem, PeptideProphet) with a false discovery rate (FDR) threshold of <1%. Candidate biomarkers were selected based on:  
- Elevated levels in tumor-bearing mice (ratio ≥1.5, p < 0.05).  
- Absence of annotation as acute-phase reactants or coagulation factors.  
- Evidence of orthologous human genes and tissue-specific expression in pancreatic cancer.  

Validation in human samples involved ELISA measurements of candidate proteins in sera from PDAC patients, healthy controls, and individuals with chronic pancreatitis.  

### Example 2  
**Biomarker Panel Performance in Human Samples**  
A panel of nine biomarkers (ALCAM, TIMP1, ICAM1, LCN2, TNFRSF1A, REG1A, REG3, WFDC2, IGFBP4) was tested in 30 PDAC patients, 20 healthy controls, and 15 chronic pancreatitis patients. The panel achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.96 for discriminating PDAC from healthy controls, outperforming CA19-9 (AUC = 0.79). Notably, TIMP1 and ICAM1 showed superior specificity in distinguishing PDAC from pancreatitis.  

### Example 3  
**Early Detection in Prediagnostic Samples**  
A subset of five biomarkers (LCN2, REG1A, REG3, TIMP1, IGFBP4) was evaluated in blinded prediagnostic sera from the CARET cohort. The panel discriminated pancreatic cancer cases from controls (AUC = 0.817, p = 0.005), with further improvement when combined with CA19-9 (AUC = 0.911).  

### Example 4  
**Immunohistochemical Validation**  
Immunohistochemistry confirmed overexpression of ALCAM, PTPRG, TIMP1, and TNC in mouse and human pancreatic tumor tissues, supporting the tissue origin of circulating biomarkers.
