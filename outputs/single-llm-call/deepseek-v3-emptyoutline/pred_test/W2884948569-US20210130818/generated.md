# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in genome editing applications. Genome editing technologies, particularly those utilizing CRISPR-Cas9 systems, have revolutionized biological research and therapeutic development by enabling precise modifications to genomic DNA. However, a significant limitation of these technologies is the inherent inefficiency of HDR relative to non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), the predominant DNA repair pathway in mammalian cells.  

NHEJ is an error-prone repair mechanism that often introduces insertions or deletions (indels) at the site of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs), leading to unpredictable genetic alterations. In contrast, HDR enables precise genome editing by utilizing a donor template to guide repair, but its efficiency is substantially lower than NHEJ in most mammalian cell types. Current approaches to enhance HDR, such as chemical inhibitors of NHEJ or cell cycle synchronization, suffer from limitations including toxicity, lack of specificity, and impracticality for in vivo applications.  

There exists a critical need for improved methods to enhance HDR efficiency that are genetically encoded, tunable, and compatible with diverse cell types and in vivo applications. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel system that simultaneously modulates key regulators of HDR and NHEJ pathways using CRISPR-based transcriptional activation and interference (CRISPRa/i) in conjunction with active Cas9-mediated genome editing.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in genome editing applications. The invention utilizes a dual CRISPR-based approach comprising:  

1) A catalytically active Cas9 nuclease complexed with single guide RNAs (sgRNAs) to induce targeted DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) at desired genomic loci, and  

2) Catalytically dead guide RNAs (dgRNAs) complexed with the same active Cas9 to simultaneously:  
   a) Activate expression of HDR-promoting factors (e.g., CDK1, CtIP) through CRISPR activation (CRISPRa), and/or  
   b) Repress expression of NHEJ-promoting factors (e.g., KU70, KU80, LIG4) through CRISPR interference (CRISPRi).  

The system employs modified dgRNA scaffolds containing RNA aptamer sequences (e.g., MS2 or Com loops) that recruit effector domains (e.g., MCP-P65-HSF1 for activation or COM-KRAB for repression) to targeted genomic loci. This approach enables coordinated enhancement of HDR efficiency while maintaining the ability to perform precise genome editing, all mediated by a single Cas9 protein.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Genetic encoding allowing stable integration and viral delivery  
- Compatibility with inducible expression systems for temporal control  
- Simultaneous genome editing and DNA repair pathway modulation  
- Enhanced HDR efficiency (4-8 fold improvement) without chemical treatments  
- Adaptability to diverse cell types and in vivo applications  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions, kits, and methods for using these compositions in research and therapeutic applications requiring precise genome editing.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the following meanings:  

"CRISPR-Cas9 system" refers to the clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats-associated protein 9 system, including all variants and derivatives thereof capable of targeted DNA binding and/or cleavage.  

"dgRNA" or "dead guide RNA" refers to a guide RNA comprising a truncated targeting sequence (14-15 nucleotides) that maintains target binding capacity but does not direct Cas9-mediated DNA cleavage.  

"CRISPRa" or "CRISPR activation" refers to the use of CRISPR systems to increase transcription of targeted genes, typically through recruitment of transcriptional activation domains.  

"CRISPRi" or "CRISPR interference" refers to the use of CRISPR systems to decrease transcription of targeted genes, typically through recruitment of transcriptional repression domains.  

"HDR" or "homology-directed repair" refers to the DNA repair pathway that uses homologous sequences as templates for accurate repair of DNA double-strand breaks.  

"NHEJ" or "non-homologous end joining" refers to the predominant DNA repair pathway in mammalian cells that directly ligates broken DNA ends without requiring a template, often resulting in small insertions or deletions.  

### Description  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for enhancing HDR efficiency during genome editing by simultaneously:  
1) Creating targeted DNA double-strand breaks using active Cas9 complexed with standard sgRNAs, and  
2) Modulating the expression of key DNA repair pathway components using the same active Cas9 complexed with dgRNAs designed to either activate HDR-promoting genes or repress NHEJ-promoting genes.  

The system employs two classes of modified dgRNAs:  
1) Activation dgRNAs containing MS2 RNA aptamer sequences in their scaffold that recruit MCP-P65-HSF1 (MPH) transcriptional activation complexes to targeted promoters, and  
2) Repression dgRNAs containing Com RNA aptamer sequences in their scaffold that recruit COM-KRAB (CK) transcriptional repression complexes to targeted promoters.  

By co-expressing these dgRNAs with standard sgRNAs in cells expressing active Cas9, the invention enables simultaneous genome editing and modulation of DNA repair pathway balance. This coordinated approach significantly enhances HDR efficiency compared to conventional genome editing methods.

### Methods  

The invention provides methods for enhancing HDR efficiency in genome editing applications, comprising:  

1) Introducing into a cell:  
   - An active Cas9 nuclease  
   - At least one sgRNA targeting a desired genomic locus  
   - At least one dgRNA targeting a promoter of:  
     * An HDR-promoting gene (for activation), or  
     * An NHEJ-promoting gene (for repression)  
   - An HDR donor template  

2) Allowing simultaneous:  
   - Cas9/sgRNA-mediated DNA cleavage at the target locus  
   - dgRNA-mediated modulation of DNA repair gene expression  

3) Culturing the cells under conditions permitting DNA repair  

Preferred embodiments include:  
- Using dgRNAs targeting CDK1 and/or CtIP for activation  
- Using dgRNAs targeting KU70, KU80, and/or LIG4 for repression  
- Combinations of activation and repression dgRNAs (e.g., CDK1 activation + KU80 repression)  
- Inducible expression systems for temporal control  
- Viral delivery methods for stable integration  

The methods achieve 4-8 fold enhancement of HDR efficiency compared to standard genome editing approaches, as demonstrated in multiple human cell lines (HEK293, HEK293T, HeLa) at various genomic loci (AAVS1, ACTB).

## Experimental Examples  

### Example 1  

**Enhancement of HDR Efficiency Using dgRNA-Mediated DNA Repair Pathway Programming**  

A traffic light reporter (TLR) system was established in HEK293 cells stably expressing Cas9 (HEK293-Cas9-TLR) to quantitatively measure HDR and NHEJ outcomes. The TLR contained a nonfunctional EGFP variant (bf-Venus) and a frameshifted mCherry reporter (fs-mCherry). DSBs induced by Cas9/sgRNA could be repaired by:  
1) NHEJ, restoring mCherry expression in ~1/3 of events, or  
2) HDR using an EGFP donor template, restoring Venus expression while leaving mCherry nonfunctional.  

Cells were transfected with:  
- dgRNA-MS2:MPH constructs targeting HDR genes (CDK1, CtIP)  
- dgRNA-Com:CK constructs targeting NHEJ genes (LIG4, KU70, KU80)  
- EGFP HDR donor template  
- sgRNA targeting the TLR locus  

Flow cytometry analysis revealed:  
- Baseline HDR efficiency: 2.42% EGFP+ cells  
- dgCDK1 activation: 7.58% EGFP+ (3.1-fold increase)  
- dgKU80 repression: 6.64% EGFP+ (2.7-fold increase)  
- Combined dgCDK1+dgKU80: 15.3% EGFP+ (6.3-fold increase)  

Sequencing confirmed precise HDR-mediated repair in EGFP+ cells and NHEJ-mediated indels in mCherry+ cells.
