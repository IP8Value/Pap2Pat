# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) represent a significant unmet medical need, characterized by progressive neuronal degeneration leading to cognitive, motor, and psychological impairments. These conditions share common pathological features including excessive production of nitric oxide (NO) by neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS), which contributes to neurotoxicity and disease progression.  

Current therapeutic strategies for modulating NO production have been limited by poor selectivity among the three nitric oxide synthase isoforms: neuronal (nNOS), endothelial (eNOS), and inducible (iNOS). While nNOS inhibition offers neuroprotective benefits, non-selective inhibition disrupts essential physiological functions mediated by eNOS (vascular homeostasis) and iNOS (immune response). Prior art compounds targeting nNOS have demonstrated insufficient isoform selectivity, unfavorable pharmacokinetic properties, or suboptimal blood-brain barrier penetration.  

There exists a critical need for novel nNOS inhibitors that exhibit: (1) high potency and selectivity for nNOS over eNOS and iNOS isoforms; (2) favorable pharmacokinetic properties including blood-brain barrier permeability; and (3) minimal off-target activity in the central nervous system. The present invention addresses these limitations through a novel class of 7-phenyl-2-aminoquinoline derivatives that demonstrate exceptional nNOS selectivity and drug-like properties.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to novel 7-phenyl-2-aminoquinoline derivatives that function as potent and selective inhibitors of neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS). These compounds demonstrate therapeutic potential for treating neurodegenerative disorders while avoiding undesirable effects on vascular (eNOS-mediated) and immune (iNOS-mediated) functions.  

### Chemical Structures  

The core structure of the inventive compounds comprises a 7-phenyl-2-aminoquinoline scaffold with strategic modifications that confer optimal nNOS inhibition and selectivity. In preferred embodiments, the compounds have the general structure shown in Formula I, where:  

- The 2-aminoquinoline moiety serves as an arginine mimic, forming critical hydrogen bonds with active site residues Trp587/Trp592 and Glu592/Glu602 (rnNOS/hnNOS numbering)  
- The central phenyl ring occupies a hydrophobic pocket between heme propionates A and D  
- The aminoalkyl tail interacts with nNOS-specific aspartate residues (Asp597/Asp602 in rnNOS/hnNOS)  
- 4-position substituents engage isoform-specific residues (Met336/Met341 and Leu337/His342 in rnNOS/hnNOS)  

### Structure-Activity Relationships  

Extensive structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies revealed that:  

1. Meta-substitution of the aminoalkyl tail (e.g., compound 12) provides superior nNOS potency and selectivity compared to para- or ortho-substituted analogs  
2. Constraining the amino group into rigid ring systems (e.g., isoindoline 16) reduces flexibility but maintains inhibitory activity  
3. 4-position modifications (e.g., ethoxy in 24, cyclopropylmethoxy in 29) enhance potency through interactions with Met336/Met341 and Leu337/His342  
4. Heterocyclic substituents (e.g., thiazole in 35) can improve human nNOS affinity while maintaining selectivity  

### Synthetic Methods  

The compounds may be prepared through the following general synthetic routes:  

1. Miyaura borylation of 7-bromoquinoline intermediates  
2. Suzuki coupling with appropriately substituted aryl halides  
3. Stepwise deprotection of aminoquinoline intermediates  
4. Final purification as hydrochloride salts  

Specific synthetic examples include:  

- Preparation of lead compound 12 via coupling of 7-boronotrifluoride-quinoline with meta-Boc-protected phenethylamine bromide, followed by deprotection  
- Synthesis of optimized analog 29 through introduction of a cyclopropylmethoxy group at the 4-position  
- Construction of heterocyclic derivatives (e.g., 35) via ether formation with thiazole-containing benzyl halides  

### Biological Activity  

Representative compounds demonstrate exceptional pharmacological properties:  

1. Potency:  
   - Compound 12: Ki = 55 nM (rnNOS), 60 nM (hnNOS)  
   - Compound 29: Ki = 39 nM (rnNOS), most potent in series  

2. Selectivity:  
   - Compound 12: 877-fold hn/heNOS selectivity  
   - Compound 32: 312-fold hn/heNOS selectivity  

3. CNS Penetration:  
   - PAMPA-BBB Pe values > 4.0 × 10^-6 cm/s for all tested compounds  
   - Compound 12 shows highest permeability (Pe = 15.5 × 10^-6 cm/s)  

4. Metabolic Stability:  
   - Compound 12 demonstrates >60 minute half-life in human liver microsomes  
   - Minimal degradation in buffer control samples
