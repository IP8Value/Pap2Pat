## FIELD  
The present invention relates generally to the field of genetic engineering and protein biosynthesis. More specifically, the invention pertains to engineered orthogonal translation systems (OTS) for incorporation of non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) into proteins, coupled with post-translational proofreading mechanisms to enhance specificity and purity of nsAA incorporation.  

## BACKGROUND  
The incorporation of non-standard amino acids (nsAAs) into proteins has broad applications in biotechnology, including the development of novel therapeutics, biocontainment strategies, and industrial protein production. Current orthogonal translation systems (OTS) often suffer from promiscuity, leading to misincorporation of standard amino acids in place of the intended nsAAs. This misincorporation reduces the purity and functionality of the resulting proteins and can compromise biocontainment strategies that rely on nsAA-dependent essential genes.  

Existing methods to improve OTS specificity primarily focus on engineering the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (aaRS) component to enhance discrimination between nsAAs and standard amino acids. However, these approaches are limited by the inherent challenges of achieving absolute specificity at the translational level. There remains a need for systems that can further discriminate between correctly and incorrectly incorporated amino acids post-translationally, particularly at the N-terminus of proteins where misincorporation can be most detrimental.  

## SUMMARY  
The present invention provides compositions and methods for enhancing the specificity of non-standard amino acid incorporation into proteins through post-translational proofreading. The system combines engineered orthogonal translation systems (OTS) with modified components of the N-end rule pathway to selectively degrade proteins containing misincorporated standard amino acids while preserving those with correctly incorporated nsAAs.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Engineered E. coli strains containing orthogonal translation systems and modified N-end rule pathway components  
2. Methods for screening and selecting OTS variants with enhanced specificity for nsAAs  
3. Compositions comprising engineered ClpS variants with altered binding specificity for N-terminal amino acids  
4. Applications in biocontainment, protein therapeutics, and industrial protein production  

The invention demonstrates particular utility with bipyridinylalanine (BipA) and related nsAAs, showing improved containment efficacy and reduced escape frequencies in biocontained strains. The proofreading system can distinguish between structurally similar nsAAs and standard amino acids, enabling higher purity protein production and more reliable synthetic auxotrophy-based biocontainment.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### II. Removable Protecting Groups  
The invention encompasses the use of removable protecting groups on non-standard amino acids to facilitate their incorporation and subsequent modification. In particular embodiments, the tert-butyl group serves as a protecting group that can be removed after protein synthesis to expose reactive moieties for further chemical modification. The protecting group strategy enables incorporation of nsAAs that might otherwise interfere with protein folding or function, while maintaining the ability to introduce desired chemical functionalities post-translationally.  

### III. Detectable Moiety  
The system incorporates detectable moieties, particularly fluorescent proteins such as superfolder green fluorescent protein (sfGFP), to monitor nsAA incorporation efficiency and proofreading activity. The reporter constructs feature cleavable ubiquitin domains followed by test codons and sfGFP with C-terminal tags. This configuration allows quantitative assessment of protein stability based on the identity of the N-terminal amino acid exposed after ubiquitin cleavage, with fluorescence serving as a proxy for protein abundance.  

### IV. Genetic Modifications  
The invention includes genetic modifications to host organisms, particularly E. coli strain C321.ΔA, which has been genomically recoded to eliminate UAG codons and associated release factors. Additional modifications include integration of reporter constructs and expression cassettes for proofreading components. The genetic modifications enable precise control over translation and degradation systems while minimizing background interference from endogenous pathways.  

### V. Adapter Protein Protease Systems  
Central to the invention are engineered adapter protein-protease systems derived from the N-end rule pathway. The system utilizes UBP1 ubiquitin cleavase to expose the N-terminal amino acid, coupled with ClpS variants that selectively recognize destabilizing residues. The ClpS adapter delivers target proteins to the ClpAP protease complex for degradation. Engineered ClpS variants, particularly ClpS V65I, exhibit altered specificity that enables discrimination between standard amino acids and structurally similar nsAAs.  

### VI. Cells  
The invention provides engineered bacterial cells, particularly E. coli strains, containing the orthogonal translation and proofreading systems. These cells demonstrate improved growth characteristics and reduced escape frequencies when used in biocontainment applications. The cells maintain robust protein production capabilities while achieving unprecedented specificity in nsAA incorporation through the coupled translation and proofreading systems.  

### VII. Standard Amino Acid  
The invention addresses the challenge of discriminating between standard amino acids and non-standard analogs during protein synthesis. Particular focus is given to tyrosine, phenylalanine, leucine, and tryptophan, which are most likely to be misincorporated by promiscuous OTSs. The proofreading system effectively distinguishes these standard amino acids from their non-standard counterparts when present at protein N-termini.  

### VIII. Non-Standard Amino Acid  
The system is optimized for incorporation of various non-standard amino acids, particularly bipyridinylalanine (BipA) and related phenylalanine derivatives. The invention demonstrates successful incorporation of p-acetyl-phenylalanine, p-azido-phenylalanine, p-bromophenylalanine, p-iodo-phenylalanine, and 5-hydroxytryptophan, among others. The proofreading system shows particular efficacy in discriminating between these nsAAs and their standard amino acid analogs.  

## Exemplary Degradation Materials and Methods  
The invention provides materials and methods for implementing the proofreading system. Key components include expression plasmids for orthogonal translation systems (pEVOL derivatives), proofreading components (pZE21-based vectors), and genomically integrated reporter constructs. The methods describe culturing conditions, induction protocols, and screening procedures for identifying improved OTS variants. Particular emphasis is placed on fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) strategies for high-throughput screening of OTS libraries under varying stringency conditions.  

## Example II  

### A Method of Making a Protein Having a Non-Standard Amino Acid Incorporated at its N-Terminus in an Engineered E. coli Having Orthogonal Translation Systems by Engineering Post-Translational Proofreading to Discriminate Non-Standard Amino Acids  
This example describes a specific method for producing proteins with nsAAs incorporated at their N-termini using the engineered proofreading system. The method involves:  
1. Transforming an engineered E. coli strain with plasmids encoding an orthogonal translation system and proofreading components  
2. Culturing the cells in media containing the desired nsAA  
3. Inducing expression of the target protein, orthogonal synthetase, and proofreading components  
4. Allowing post-translational proofreading to selectively degrade proteins with misincorporated standard amino acids  
5. Isculating and purifying the remaining proteins containing correctly incorporated nsAAs  

The method demonstrates particular utility with the BipA OTS and ClpS V65I proofreading system, yielding proteins with significantly higher purity of nsAA incorporation compared to conventional methods.  

### Example III  
This example details the engineering and characterization of ClpS variants with altered specificity for N-terminal amino acids. Through targeted mutagenesis of the ClpS binding pocket, several variants were identified that differentially recognize standard amino acids versus nsAAs. The ClpS V65I variant showed particularly desirable properties, stabilizing proteins containing all tested phenyl-based nsAAs while effectively degrading those with standard amino acids at their N-termini.  

### Example IV  
This example describes the application of the proofreading system to evolve more selective orthogonal translation systems. Using the coupled translation-proofreading system and FACS-based screening, novel BipA OTS variants were isolated that showed significantly reduced promiscuity. Variant 10 demonstrated exceptional selectivity for BipA over standard amino acids and other nsAAs, with in vitro aminoacylation assays confirming the improved specificity observed in vivo.  

### Example V  
This example demonstrates the enhanced biocontainment achieved using the more selective OTS variants in synthetic auxotrophy systems. Strains containing essential genes redesigned to depend on BipA showed escape frequencies more than two orders of magnitude lower when using variant 10 OTS compared to the parental OTS. The improved containment efficacy was accompanied by better growth characteristics, suggesting reduced metabolic burden from misincorporation events.  

### Example VI  
This example illustrates the broad applicability of the proofreading system to different classes of nsAAs. The system successfully distinguished 5-hydroxytryptophan from standard tryptophan, demonstrating that the approach is not limited to phenylalanine derivatives. The ability to tune proofreading specificity through engineered ClpS variants suggests the system can be adapted to diverse nsAAs with appropriate modification of the recognition components.
