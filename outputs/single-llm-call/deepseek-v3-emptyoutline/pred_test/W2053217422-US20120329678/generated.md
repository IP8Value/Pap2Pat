# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The field of genomic sequencing has undergone significant advancements with the development of next-generation sequencing technologies. However, a persistent challenge in de novo genome assembly remains the accurate reconstruction of complex genomic regions containing repetitive sequences. Traditional methods for generating mate-pair libraries, which provide crucial long-range genomic information, suffer from several limitations including low efficiency, limited insert size capabilities, and high rates of chimeric reads. Existing commercial kits for mate-pair library preparation typically produce libraries with insert sizes not exceeding 5 kb, while clone-based methods using fosmid or BAC vectors, though capable of larger inserts, suffer from limited library complexity due to their in vivo nature. Furthermore, current ligation-based approaches often lack identifiable junction sites between paired ends, making it difficult to distinguish true mate pairs from chimeric artifacts, particularly when sequencing read lengths increase. These technical shortcomings substantially hinder genome assembly quality, especially for complex genomes with abundant repetitive elements. There exists a pressing need for an improved method of generating high-quality mate-pair libraries that can produce larger insert sizes with higher efficiency while maintaining clearly identifiable junction sites between paired ends.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel in vitro method for constructing high-quality mate-pair libraries, termed CLIP-PE (Cre-LoxP Inverse PCR Paired-End), that overcomes the limitations of existing technologies. The method utilizes the Cre-LoxP recombination system combined with inverse PCR to generate mate-pair libraries with insert sizes ranging from 5 kb to over 22 kb. Key advantages of the CLIP-PE method include: (1) the ability to produce larger insert sizes compared to commercial kits; (2) higher library complexity compared to clone-based methods; (3) incorporation of a clearly identifiable junction site between paired ends to prevent chimeric reads; and (4) compatibility with Illumina sequencing platforms. The method involves circularizing genomic DNA fragments using Cre recombinase-mediated intramolecular recombination at LoxP sites, followed by secondary fragmentation, self-ligation, and selective amplification of fragments containing paired ends via inverse PCR. The resulting libraries demonstrate superior performance in genome assembly, as evidenced by higher percentages of correctly distanced mate pairs, lower chimeric rates, and significant improvements in scaffold N50 sizes when used in combination with standard short-insert libraries.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Definitions  

As used herein, "mate-pair library" refers to a collection of DNA fragments where sequences from both ends of large DNA inserts are brought together in a single sequencing construct. "Insert size" denotes the original length of genomic DNA between the two sequenced ends. "Chimeric reads" are sequencing artifacts where parts of two unrelated genomic regions appear joined in a single read. "Non-redundant pairs" are mate pairs that map unambiguously to unique genomic locations at the expected distance and orientation. "Cre-LoxP system" refers to the site-specific recombination system derived from bacteriophage P1, where Cre recombinase catalyzes recombination between two LoxP sites. "Inverse PCR" describes a technique for amplifying unknown flanking sequences by using primers oriented away from each other on circular DNA templates.

### I. Introduction  

The CLIP-PE method represents a significant advancement in mate-pair library construction by combining the efficiency of Cre-LoxP recombination with the specificity of inverse PCR amplification. This approach addresses three major limitations of current technologies: size constraints, junction ambiguity, and library complexity. The method is particularly valuable for de novo genome assembly, structural variation detection, and finishing complex genomes. The following sections describe the key components and steps of the CLIP-PE method in detail.

### II. Generation of Mate-Pair Libraries  

The CLIP-PE library preparation process comprises several optimized steps: First, genomic DNA is randomly sheared to the desired insert size (5 kb, 12 kb, or 22 kb) using mechanical fragmentation. The DNA fragments undergo end repair to generate blunt ends, followed by ligation of specialized adapters containing both LoxP sites and Illumina P1/P2 primer sequences. Size selection is performed to isolate fragments of the target size range, which is particularly critical for larger insert libraries where pulse-field gel electrophoresis may be employed. The adapter-ligated fragments then undergo Cre recombinase-mediated circularization, bringing the LoxP sites together to form circular DNA molecules. Linear DNA is removed using plasmid-safe DNase treatment to enrich for successfully circularized molecules. The circular DNA is then subjected to secondary fragmentation, which can be accomplished either by random shearing or by restriction enzyme digestion. For optimal results, 4-base cutters that do not cleave within the P1-LoxP-P2 adapter sequence are preferred, with enzymes generating 4-base overhangs (e.g., NlaIII) showing superior efficiency compared to those generating 2-base overhangs or blunt ends. The fragmented DNA undergoes self-ligation, after which inverse PCR using Illumina P1 and P2 primers selectively amplifies only those molecules containing the original paired ends joined by the LoxP junction. The final PCR products are size-selected to obtain libraries suitable for Illumina sequencing.

### III. Software and Data Analysis  

The CLIP-PE method incorporates specific bioinformatic processing to maximize data quality. Sequencing reads are trimmed at the restriction enzyme recognition site when enzyme digestion was used for secondary fragmentation, ensuring clear identification of the junction between paired ends and preventing chimeric reads. For libraries prepared with random shearing, alternative junction identification methods may be employed. Read alignment utilizes standard tools such as BWA, with special consideration given to the expected insert size distribution. Library quality metrics include the percentage of non-redundant pairs, chimeric rate (pairs mapping to different chromosomes or incorrect orientations), and clone coverage uniformity across the genome. For genome assembly applications, the CLIP-PE libraries are typically combined with standard short-insert libraries, with assembly software parameters adjusted to account for the different insert size distributions and expected orientation of mate pairs.

### IV. Kits  

The invention further provides kits for practicing the CLIP-PE method, comprising: (1) integrated LoxP-P1 and LoxP-P2 adapter oligonucleotides; (2) Cre recombinase enzyme; (3) selection of 4-base cutter restriction enzymes lacking recognition sites in the adapter sequence; (4) plasmid-safe DNase for linear DNA removal; (5) optimized buffers for recombination and ligation reactions; and (6) control DNA samples for quality assessment. The kits may include components for multiple insert size ranges (5 kb, 12 kb, 22 kb) with appropriate size selection protocols for each. Optional components may include streptavidin beads for biotinylated adapter capture and specialized enzymes for efficient end repair and fill-in reactions.

## EXAMPLES  

### CLIP-PE Method can Consistently Generate High Quality Mate Pair Libraries  

Comparative studies between CLIP-PE and commercial Illumina jumping libraries demonstrated the superior performance of the CLIP-PE method. In parallel preparations of 5 kb libraries from Haloterrigena turkmenica DNA, CLIP-PE libraries yielded 20.6% non-redundant pairs at the expected insert size, compared to only 8.7% for the Illumina jumping method. Notably, while the Illumina libraries contained a high proportion (91%) of small fragment artifacts (<600 bp), CLIP-PE libraries showed only 11% such artifacts. Clone coverage analysis revealed 4,746× coverage for CLIP-PE versus 18× for the jumping library, with corresponding reductions in uncovered gaps (7 vs. 767) and average gap sizes (8±13 bp vs. 41±133 bp). The chimeric rate for CLIP-PE (2.3%) was substantially lower than the jumping method (9.2%). Similar advantages were observed with 12 kb and 22 kb CLIP-PE libraries prepared from Saccharomyces cerevisiae DNA, demonstrating the method's scalability. Three independent 12 kb libraries showed highly reproducible results, with averages of 59% non-redundant pairs at the expected insert size and low chimeric rates (5-7% interchromosomal, <0.05% wrong orientation).

### Ligation Efficiency Affects the Productivity and Quality of CLIP-PE Libraries  

The choice of secondary fragmentation method significantly impacts CLIP-PE library quality. Comparative analysis of three 22 kb S. cerevisiae libraries prepared with different fragmentation approaches revealed that restriction digestion with 4-base overhang enzymes (NlaIII) yielded the highest efficiency (11.1% non-redundant pairs), followed by 2-base overhang enzymes (HpyCH4IV, 4.0%), with random shearing (blunt ends) showing the lowest efficiency (2.5%). This efficiency gradient correlates with known ligation biochemistry, where 4-base overhangs permit the most efficient self-ligation. All libraries maintained low chimeric rates (∼1.5-1.7%) and nearly complete clone coverage, demonstrating that the basic CLIP-PE architecture remains robust across fragmentation methods. The data further indicate that while random shearing provides the most unbiased fragmentation, enzyme digestion offers substantial efficiency advantages that may outweigh potential minor coverage biases in many applications.

### Discussion  

The CLIP-PE method's advantages stem from its unique combination of Cre-LoxP recombination and inverse PCR amplification. The Cre-LoxP system efficiently circularizes large DNA fragments (up to at least 22 kb in demonstrated examples), while the integrated adapter design ensures specific amplification of properly recombined molecules through a single critical ligation step. This contrasts with commercial methods requiring multiple ligation steps with correct end combinations. The identifiable junction site, whether from restriction enzyme recognition or alternative marking strategies, enables longer read lengths without chimeric read risks, a significant advantage over Illumina's jumping method. Genome assembly tests combining CLIP-PE libraries with standard short-insert data showed dramatic improvements, particularly in scaffold N50 sizes (7-fold increases from ∼100 kb to ∼750 kb). The method's versatility allows adaptation to various insert sizes and sequencing platforms, with potential extensions to in vivo systems like fosmid cloning for even larger inserts. Future optimizations may focus on enhancing recombination efficiency for very large fragments and expanding the repertoire of compatible restriction enzymes to ensure uniform genomic coverage across diverse organisms.

## Methods  

### Illumina Library Preparation  

Standard Illumina shotgun libraries were prepared using 1 μg genomic DNA with the commercial Illumina Paired-End kit following manufacturer protocols, without PCR amplification. Jumping libraries used 5 μg DNA with the Illumina Mate-Pair Library Prep Kit V2 according to recommended procedures. All libraries were quantified using fluorometric methods and quality assessed by capillary electrophoresis before sequencing.

### CLIP-PE Library Preparation  

The CLIP-PE protocol involves: (1) shearing 5-30 μg genomic DNA to target sizes (5, 12, or 22 kb) using calibrated hydrodynamic forces; (2) end repair using T4 DNA polymerase, Klenow fragment, and T4 polynucleotide kinase; (3) adapter ligation with integrated LoxP-P1/P2 oligonucleotides; (4) size selection by gel electrophoresis (standard for ≤12 kb, pulse-field for 22 kb); (5) fill-in reaction with Bst DNA polymerase; (6) Cre recombinase-mediated circularization (1-4 reactions of 300 ng DNA with 2-10U Cre at 37°C); (7) linear DNA removal by plasmid-safe DNase; (8) secondary fragmentation by restriction enzyme (e.g., NlaIII) or random shearing; (9) self-ligation of fragments; (10) optional additional linear DNA removal; (11) inverse PCR amplification using Phusion polymerase and Illumina primers; and (12) final size selection (300-600 bp) by gel extraction. Critical parameters include DNA input amounts, Cre reaction optimization, and restriction enzyme selection based on genome characteristics.

### Illumina Sequencing  

All sequencing was performed on Illumina Genome Analyzer IIx instruments using manufacturer-recommended protocols. Standard paired-end libraries were sequenced with 2×100 cycles and trimmed to 76 bp based on quality scores. CLIP-PE and jumping libraries used 2×76 cycle runs. For jumping libraries, reads were trimmed to 35 bp to avoid junction read-through, while CLIP-PE reads were trimmed at restriction sites when applicable, maintaining average lengths of 70 bp.

### Post-Sequencing Analysis  

Read processing included quality trimming and, for CLIP-PE libraries, junction site identification. Alignment used BWA with parameters adjusted for expected insert sizes. Quality metrics included non-redundant pair percentage, chimeric rates, and coverage uniformity. For genome assemblies, CLIP-PE data were combined with standard short-insert libraries using Velvet with appropriate k-mer lengths and pair-joining thresholds. Assembly quality was assessed by contiguity metrics (N50, scaffold numbers) and accuracy measures (misassemblies, relocations, inversions) relative to reference sequences.

### Data Simulations and Genome Assembly  

Simulated mate-pair reads were generated from reference genomes using wgsim with 76 bp length and 1% error rate. Assemblies compared CLIP-PE data against simulated libraries of equivalent insert sizes, using Velvet with auto-coverage settings and minimum contig length of 200 bp. Assembly accuracy was evaluated using dnadiff for structural comparisons, with particular attention to relocation, translocation, and inversion frequencies between assemblies and references.
