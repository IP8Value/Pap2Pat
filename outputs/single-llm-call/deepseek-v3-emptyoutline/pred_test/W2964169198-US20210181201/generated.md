# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of extracellular vesicle (EV) research and biomedical applications. More specifically, it pertains to recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) as biological reference materials for calibration, normalization, and quality control in EV isolation, quantification, and characterization methods. The invention further encompasses methods for producing, detecting, and utilizing rEV in diagnostic, therapeutic, and research applications.  

## BACKGROUND  

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are membrane-bound nanoparticles released by cells that play critical roles in intercellular communication and disease pathogenesis. Their involvement in cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, and autoimmune diseases has spurred significant interest in EVs as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic agents. However, EV research faces substantial challenges due to:  

1. **Methodological Variability**: The lack of standardized protocols for EV isolation leads to inconsistent results across laboratories.  
2. **Absence of Reference Materials**: Current calibration standards (e.g., polystyrene beads, silica beads, liposomes) lack EV-like biochemical and physical properties, resulting in inaccurate measurements.  
3. **Inability to Track EVs**: Biological reference materials derived from biofluids or cell cultures cannot be distinguished from sample EVs, limiting their utility for normalization and quality control.  

Existing synthetic reference materials fail to replicate key EV characteristics such as surface markers, lipid composition, and refractive index (~1.39). Biological reference materials, while closer in composition, lack specificity for tracking and differentiation from sample EVs. Thus, there is an unmet need for a biological reference material that:  

- Mimics the physical and biochemical properties of EVs.  
- Is trackable and distinguishable from sample EVs.  
- Behaves similarly to sample EVs under experimental conditions.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) derived from the HIV-1 gag polyprotein, which hijacks the endogenous EV biogenesis pathway to produce EV-like particles. These rEVs exhibit EV-like morphology, size, density, and molecular composition while being trackable via exogenous fluorescent or genetic markers.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) as a biological reference material for EV research and applications. rEVs are produced by transfecting cells (e.g., HEK293T) with a gag-EGFP fusion construct, which directs the formation of EV-like particles via the ESCRT pathway. Key features of rEV include:  

1. **EV-like Physical Properties**:  
   - Size distribution: 50–180 nm (mean ~108.6 nm).  
   - Buoyant density: 1.086–1.119 g/mL (matching endogenous EVs).  
   - Refractive index: ~1.37 (similar to sample EVs).  
   - Zeta potential: −32.3 mV (comparable to EVs from biofluids).  
   - Cup-shaped morphology (confirmed by TEM).  

2. **EV-like Biochemical Composition**:  
   - Presence of EV-associated proteins (ALIX, TSG101, CD9, CD63, CD81).  
   - Lipid profile enriched in phosphatidylserine, sphingomyelin, and cholesterol.  
   - Encapsulation of exogenous EGFP mRNA for trackability.  

3. **Production and Isolation**:  
   - Transient transfection of HEK293T cells with gag-EGFP plasmid.  
   - Isolation via OptiPrep density gradient (ODG) centrifugation.  
   - Yield: ~5 × 10^11 rEV per harvest from 3 × 10^9 cells.  

4. **Stability and Storage**:  
   - Stable at −80°C for ≥12 months.  
   - Lyophilizable in PBS with 5% trehalose without loss of integrity.  

5. **Detection Methods**:  
   - Fluorescence-based: fNTA, high-resolution flow cytometry.  
   - Protein-based: p24 ELISA, Western blot (gag-EGFP).  
   - RNA-based: RT-qPCR (EGFP mRNA).  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Recombinant Extracellular Vesicles (rEV) Share Physical and Biochemical Characteristics with Endogenous Sample Extracellular Vesicles (Sample EV)  

rEVs were compared to EVs isolated from urine, plasma, and conditioned media of breast cancer cells (MCF7, 4T1) and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF). Key findings:  

- **Size and Morphology**: NTA and TEM confirmed equivalent size distributions (108.6 ± 9.5 nm) and cup-shaped morphology.  
- **Density**: ODG centrifugation showed identical buoyant densities (1.086–1.119 g/mL).  
- **Protein Composition**: Mass spectrometry identified 792 shared proteins (89% overlap), including ESCRT components and tetraspanins.  
- **Lipid Profile**: Lipidomics revealed comparable phospholipid and cholesterol content (Spearman r = 0.921).  

#### Example 2: Identification of rEV Detection Methods  

rEVs were quantified using multiple platforms:  

- **Fluorescence NTA (fNTA)**: Linear correlation for rEV concentrations (5 × 10^8–1 × 10^10).  
- **High-Resolution Flow Cytometry**: Equivalent sensitivity to fNTA.  
- **p24 ELISA**: Linear detection range (1 × 10^6–1 × 10^7 rEV).  
- **RT-qPCR**: Semi-logarithmic quantification of EGFP mRNA (1 × 10^7–1 × 10^9 rEV).  

#### Example 3: Identification of Optimized Storage Conditions of rEV  

- **Freezing**: No loss of integrity after 12 months at −80°C.  
- **Lyophilization**: Maintained size, fluorescence, and density when stored with 5% trehalose.  

#### Example 4: The Use of rEV to Estimate the Efficiency of EV Isolation Methods  

rEVs were spiked into plasma, urine, and culture medium to evaluate recovery efficiencies:  

- **Size Exclusion Chromatography (SEC)**: ~100% recovery.  
- **ODG Centrifugation**: 30% recovery.  
- **Differential Ultracentrifugation (dUC)**: 5–10% recovery.  

#### Example 5: rEV Allows for an Accurate Comparison of EV Numbers in Plasma from Cancer Patients Versus Healthy Individuals  

Normalization with rEV revealed a 2.5-fold increase in EV concentration in breast cancer patients (1.46 × 10^11 vs. 5.83 × 10^10 EVs/mL, p < 0.001). Absolute counts without normalization showed no significant difference.  

#### Example 6: Implementing the PSI Sequence to Load EGFP mRNA in rEV  

The gag polyprotein’s RNA-binding domains enabled encapsulation of EGFP mRNA, allowing RT-qPCR-based quantification. Custom RNA loading (e.g., therapeutic miRNAs) is feasible by modifying the gag construct.
