# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to catalytic compositions and methods for converting ethanol into hydrocarbon blend-stocks. More particularly, the invention concerns a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst comprising indium and vanadium exchanged onto ZSM-5 framework (InV-ZSM-5), which demonstrates superior performance in converting ethanol to liquid hydrocarbons with reduced ethylene production and enhanced catalyst durability compared to monometallic analogs. The invention further encompasses methods of synthesizing said catalyst, characterization techniques verifying its unique bimetallic structure, and processes for converting ethanol feedstocks into hydrocarbon mixtures suitable for fuel applications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The conversion of bio-derived ethanol to hydrocarbon fuels presents a sustainable pathway for producing renewable transportation fuels. Conventional zeolite catalysts such as H-ZSM-5 predominantly yield ethylene through dehydration pathways, necessitating subsequent oligomerization steps. While vanadium-exchanged ZSM-5 (V-ZSM-5) reduces Brønsted acidity and consequently ethylene formation, such monometallic catalysts exhibit limited selectivity toward higher hydrocarbons (C3+) and suffer from rapid deactivation due to coking.  

Prior attempts to modify zeolite catalysts for ethanol conversion have focused on either suppressing dehydration pathways or promoting oligomerization/aromatization through separate catalytic stages. These approaches incur process inefficiencies and fail to address fundamental limitations in catalyst durability. Moreover, conventional characterization methods prove inadequate for verifying the structure of bimetallic zeolites, leading to uncertainties in structure-activity relationships. There remains an unmet need for a unified catalyst system that simultaneously achieves high C3+ selectivity, minimal ethylene byproduction, and extended operational lifetime under industrial process conditions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst comprising indium and vanadium co-exchanged onto ZSM-5 (InV-ZSM-5), wherein the catalyst exhibits:  
- A vanadium content between 0.5-1.0 wt% and indium content between 1.0-2.0 wt% as measured by elemental analysis;  
- Unique UV-Vis absorption characteristics showing absence of the 402 nm band characteristic of V-ZSM-5;  
- Distinct Raman spectral features at 484 cm⁻¹ and 919 cm⁻¹ not attributable to V₂O₅, In₂O₃, or mechanical mixtures;  
- C3+ hydrocarbon selectivity exceeding 50% at 250-350°C with concurrent suppression of ethylene formation to <20%;  
- Operational durability allowing continuous operation for ≥12 hours at weight hourly space velocities (WHSV) of 4 h⁻¹ before requiring regeneration.  

The catalyst is prepared through sequential ion exchange of ZSM-5 with vanadium(III) chloride followed by indium(III) nitrate, with intermediate calcination steps at 500°C. Characterization confirms the formation of non-mechanical bimetallic sites through combined UV-Vis, Raman, and elemental analysis.  

The invention further provides methods for converting ethanol to hydrocarbons by contacting ethanol feedstock with InV-ZSM-5 at 250-450°C, yielding a product stream comprising:  
- 19% C3-C4 olefins;  
- 35% C3-C4 paraffins;  
- 33% C5+ liquids having research octane number (RON) >105;  
- <20% ethylene across all temperatures.  

The process tolerates aqueous ethanol feeds containing up to 60% water and maintains stable operation through periodic oxidative regeneration at 500°C.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Catalyst Composition and Synthesis  

The InV-ZSM-5 catalyst is synthesized through sequential ion exchange of NH₄-ZSM-5 (SiO₂/Al₂O₃ = 23) with vanadium and indium precursors. In the first stage, NH₄-ZSM-5 undergoes refluxing in 0.05M V(III)Cl₃ aqueous solution at 80°C for 16 hours, followed by filtration, drying at 105°C, and calcination at 500°C for 4 hours to produce V-ZSM-5. The vanadium-exchanged intermediate is then subjected to secondary exchange in 0.015M In(NO₃)₃ solution under identical reflux conditions, with subsequent calcination under identical parameters to yield the final InV-ZSM-5 catalyst.  

Elemental analysis verifies the bimetallic composition, typically showing 0.6±0.1 wt% vanadium and 1.6±0.2 wt% indium. The absence of phase segregation is confirmed by UV-Vis spectroscopy, where the characteristic 402 nm band of V-ZSM-5 disappears upon indium incorporation, while the 300 nm absorption persists due to overlapping contributions from In³⁺ and V⁵⁺ species. Raman spectroscopy further distinguishes the bimetallic catalyst through unique vibrational modes at 484 cm⁻¹ and 919 cm⁻¹, which do not correspond to any known oxides or hydroxides of the individual metals.  

### Catalytic Performance  

Ethanol conversion experiments demonstrate 100% feedstock conversion across 250-450°C with no oxygenate byproducts. Key performance advantages include:  

1. **Selectivity Modulation**: At 350°C, InV-ZSM-5 produces 60% C3+ hydrocarbons versus 45% for V-ZSM-5 and 35% for H-ZSM-5, while suppressing ethylene to 15% compared to 25% for V-ZSM-5. The C5+ liquid fraction exhibits an average molecular weight of 97.6 g/mol and RON of 105.7, suitable for direct gasoline blending.  

2. **Durability Enhancement**: At WHSV=4 h⁻¹, InV-ZSM-5 maintains >80% initial C3+ yield for 12 hours versus 6 hours for V-ZSM-5. Full activity restoration is achieved through oxidative regeneration at 500°C, whereas 450°C treatment only recovers 94% activity due to incomplete coke removal.  

3. **Feedstock Flexibility**: The catalyst processes ethanol-water mixtures (10-100% ethanol) without selectivity changes, enabling use of partially purified fermentation broths. Impurities including acetaldehyde (<0.1%) do not affect catalyst performance over 100-hour tests.  

### Reaction Mechanism  

Deuterium labeling studies using C₂H₅OD reveal deuterium incorporation in all hydrocarbon products except ethylene, indicating parallel reaction pathways:  

1. **Initiation**: Ethanol either dehydrates to ethylene or forms surface ethoxy species, with both pathways contributing to initial hydrocarbon pool formation.  

2. **Propagation**: Hydrocarbon pool mechanisms dominate, evidenced by deuterium scrambling into C3+ products through reversible addition/elimination steps across olefinic and aromatic intermediates.  

3. **Termination**: Progressive coking occurs via hydrocarbon pool condensation, reversible through high-temperature oxidation.  

In-situ DRIFTS studies corroborate this mechanism, showing transient alkene formation (1690 cm⁻¹ band) within 0.5 minutes of ethanol exposure, followed by rapid aromatic accumulation (1567 cm⁻¹ band). The temporal evolution of C-H stretches (2987→2911 cm⁻¹) further supports gradual transformation of linear alkenes into alkylated aromatics.  

### Example 1  

**Preparation of InV-ZSM-5**:  
1. Dissolve 2.5g VCl₃ in 320mL DI water to prepare 0.05M solution.  
2. Add 12.17g NH₄-ZSM-5 (Zeolyst CBV2314), reflux at 80°C for 16h.  
3. Filter, dry at 105°C, calcine at 500°C (1°C/min ramp) for 4h to obtain V-ZSM-5.  
4. Prepare 0.015M In(NO₃)₃ solution, add V-ZSM-5, repeat reflux/drying/calcination steps.  

**Characterization**:  
- Elemental analysis: 0.6 wt% V, 1.6 wt% In.  
- UV-Vis: Absence of 402 nm band confirms non-mechanical mixing.  
- Raman: Peaks at 484 cm⁻¹ and 919 cm⁻¹ verify bimetallic interaction.  

### Example 2  

**Ethanol Conversion Process**:  
1. Load 0.2g InV-ZSM-5 (250-500μm) into quartz reactor (8mm ID).  
2. Pretreat at 450°C under 50 sccm He for 0.5h.  
3. Feed pure ethanol at WHSV=4 h⁻¹, 350°C.  
4. Analyze products by GC/MS:  
   - C2 selectivity: 15%  
   - C3+ selectivity: 60% (19% olefins, 35% paraffins, 33% C5+ aromatics)  
5. Regenerate after 12h at 500°C in air (2°C/min ramp, 1h hold).
