## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to methods and compositions for antigen retrieval in biological samples, particularly those fixed with formaldehyde or formaldehyde-containing fixatives. Formaldehyde fixation is widely used in histology and immunohistochemistry to preserve tissue morphology and stabilize cellular components. However, this process introduces methylene bridges that cross-link proteins, masking epitopes and reducing antibody binding efficiency during immunostaining. Conventional antigen retrieval techniques rely on heat-induced epitope retrieval (HIER) using buffers such as citrate or EDTA, but these methods are often insufficient for fully reversing formaldehyde-induced modifications.  

Existing approaches fail to address the transfer of formaldehyde-derived labels from heavily cross-linked proteins to target antigens during retrieval, further complicating epitope exposure. There remains a critical need for improved antigen retrieval agents that effectively reverse formaldehyde-induced modifications without introducing artifacts or compromising tissue integrity. The present invention provides novel formaldehyde scavengers that function as superior antigen retrieval agents, enabling enhanced epitope exposure while preventing unwanted label transfer.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention discloses compositions and methods for improved antigen retrieval in formaldehyde-fixed biological samples. The invention utilizes formaldehyde scavengers, including but not limited to ascorbic acid, 2-imidazolidinone, and tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (tris), which are buffered to an acidic pH (3–4) and applied under controlled heating conditions. These scavengers not only reverse formaldehyde-induced cross-links but also prevent the transfer of formaldehyde-derived labels from heavily modified proteins to target antigens.  

The invention further encompasses optimized protocols for antigen retrieval, including heating parameters (e.g., 80–125°C for 45–60 minutes) and buffer formulations that maximize epitope exposure while preserving tissue morphology. The disclosed methods are compatible with various downstream applications, including immunofluorescence, immunohistochemistry, and mass spectrometry. Additionally, the invention provides model systems for validating the efficacy of formaldehyde scavengers using synthetic peptides such as human angiotensin I and ACTH (18–39).  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Compounds  
The invention employs formaldehyde scavengers selected from the group consisting of ascorbic acid, 2-imidazolidinone, and tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (tris). These compounds are dissolved in aqueous solutions at concentrations ranging from 1% to 10% (w/v) and adjusted to a pH of 3–4 using suitable buffers. The acidic pH enhances the scavengers' ability to disrupt methylene bridges while minimizing tissue damage.  

For example, ascorbic acid is prepared as a 5% (w/v) solution in water, buffered to pH 3–4 with citrate or phosphate buffer. Similarly, 2-imidazolidinone is dissolved at 5% (w/v) in water and adjusted to the same pH range. Tris is used at comparable concentrations and pH levels. These scavengers are applied to paraffin-embedded tissue sections following deparaffinization and rehydration.  

### EXAMPLE 2  
In this example, paraffin-embedded brain sections from formaldehyde-fixed mice were subjected to antigen retrieval using ascorbic acid. Sections were heated to 80°C, raised to 95°C, and held for 45 minutes before cooling to 80°C over 60 minutes. Immunofluorescence staining with anti-Collagen IV (1:500) and anti-Claudin 5 (1:200) antibodies revealed robust epitope exposure and minimal background, demonstrating the efficacy of ascorbic acid as an antigen retrieval agent.  

### EXAMPLE 3  
This example illustrates the use of 2-imidazolidinone for antigen retrieval. Formaldehyde-fixed brain sections were treated with a 5% (w/v) solution of 2-imidazolidinone (pH 3–4) under the same heating conditions as in Example 2. Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed the reversal of formaldehyde-induced modifications in model peptides (human angiotensin I and ACTH 18–39) without label transfer artifacts.  

### EXAMPLE 4  
Here, tris was evaluated as a formaldehyde scavenger. Brain sections fixed with formaldehyde-free Accustain were processed with a 5% (w/v) tris solution (pH 3–4) and heated as described above. Immunofluorescence and mass spectrometry data showed comparable performance to ascorbic acid and 2-imidazolidinone, with no detectable label transfer.  

### EXAMPLE 5  
This example demonstrates the prevention of formaldehyde label transfer from heavily cross-linked proteins to target antigens. Formaldehyde-treated equine myoglobin was incubated with ACTH (18–39) in the presence or absence of ascorbic acid. Mass spectrometry revealed that ascorbic acid completely blocked the transfer of formaldehyde-derived labels to ACTH, whereas control samples showed significant label transfer.  

### EXAMPLE 6  
A comparative study was conducted to evaluate the efficiency of the disclosed scavengers versus conventional citrate buffer. Formaldehyde-fixed brain sections were processed with either citrate (pH 6.0) or ascorbic acid (pH 3–4). Immunofluorescence intensity measurements confirmed superior epitope exposure with ascorbic acid, with a 2.5-fold increase in signal compared to citrate.  

### EXAMPLE 7  
This example outlines the application of the invention in MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. Formaldehyde-treated peptides (human angiotensin I and ACTH 18–39) were subjected to antigen retrieval with 2-imidazolidinone. Spectra acquired at 0, 20, and 45 minutes demonstrated progressive reversal of formaldehyde modifications, with complete recovery of native peptide masses by 45 minutes.  

### EXAMPLE 8  
The final example describes a high-throughput protocol for antigen retrieval in clinical specimens. Formaldehyde-fixed paraffin-embedded tissues from multiple organs (brain, liver, kidney) were processed using the disclosed scavengers in a 96-well format. Automated imaging and analysis confirmed consistent epitope retrieval across all samples, validating the scalability of the method.
