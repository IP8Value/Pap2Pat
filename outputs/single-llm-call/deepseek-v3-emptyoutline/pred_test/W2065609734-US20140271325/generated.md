# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The field of powder metallurgy faces persistent challenges in achieving full densification of refractory metals and their alloys while maintaining nanoscale or ultrafine grain structures. Conventional sintering methods for tungsten and similar high-melting-point materials typically require extremely high temperatures, often approaching 0.7-0.8 of the absolute melting temperature, which inevitably leads to substantial grain growth. Existing approaches to accelerate sintering—including liquid phase sintering, activated sintering, and field-assisted sintering techniques—present significant limitations. Liquid phase sintering introduces low-melting-point phases that may degrade high-temperature performance, while activated sintering typically relies on thin interfacial films that are difficult to control. Pressure-assisted methods constrain component geometry and scale, limiting industrial applicability.  

A critical unmet need exists for sintering techniques that simultaneously enable: (1) rapid densification at reduced temperatures, (2) preservation of nanoscale microstructures, and (3) pressureless operation suitable for complex component geometries. The present invention addresses these needs through a novel approach termed "nanophase separation sintering," which leverages synergistic effects between nanocrystalline powder structures and metastable alloy compositions to create self-generating rapid diffusion pathways during thermal processing.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention discloses a materials processing method for accelerated pressureless sintering of nanostructured alloys, comprising:  

Providing a mechanically alloyed powder composition comprising a base metal matrix (preferably tungsten or chromium) containing 5-25 atomic percent of a supersaturated alloying element (preferably chromium or nickel), where the powder particles exhibit microscale dimensions (1-10 μm) with internal nanoscale grain structures (5-50 nm). The supersaturated solid solution is metastable at room temperature but poised for phase separation upon heating.  

Forming a green compact from said powder through conventional pressing operations (typically 200-500 MPa), then subjecting the compact to controlled thermal treatment in a reducing atmosphere. During heating to temperatures between 800-1500°C, the alloy undergoes in situ phase separation where the solute element (e.g., Cr in W) precipitates preferentially at particle surfaces and interparticle necks, forming continuous high-diffusivity pathways.  

The precipitated phase (e.g., Cr-rich regions in W-Cr) serves as a rapid transport medium for the base metal atoms (e.g., W), enabling densification rates orders of magnitude faster than conventional solid-state sintering. Kinetic analysis confirms the process is governed by diffusion of the base metal through the precipitated phase (activation energy 373 kJ/mol for W-Cr), distinct from conventional sintering mechanisms. The method achieves >98% theoretical density at temperatures 300-500°C below standard practice while maintaining grain sizes below 200 nm.  

The invention further encompasses optimized multicomponent alloys (e.g., W-Ti-Cr) where additional elements (Ti) stabilize the nanostructure during sintering, and demonstrates generalizability across alloy systems (e.g., Cr-Ni). Compared to prior art, the disclosed method uniquely combines: (1) pressureless operation, (2) low-temperature densification, (3) nanograin retention, and (4) applicability to bulk components (>15 mm dimensions).  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description illustrates the invention's implementation through exemplary embodiments, though the scope is not limited to these examples. All compositions are given in atomic percent unless otherwise specified.  

**Powder Preparation:**  
High-purity elemental powders (W, Cr, Ni, Ti) with initial particle sizes of 1-10 μm are mechanically alloyed via high-energy ball milling using WC media. A ball-to-powder ratio of 5:1 with 1 wt% stearic acid process control agent achieves complete solute dissolution (e.g., 15% Cr in W) while maintaining powder particle sizes >1 μm. Milling durations of 15-30 hours produce nanocrystalline internal structures with grain sizes of 10-20 nm, verified by XRD and TEM. The resulting metastable solid solutions exhibit single-phase body-centered cubic structures despite equilibrium immiscibility (e.g., W-Cr). Contamination from milling media is limited to <3 wt% WC.  

**Compaction and Sintering:**  
The alloyed powders are uniaxially pressed at 360 MPa into green compacts with 60-70% theoretical density. Sintering proceeds in flowing Ar-3% H2 atmosphere using constant heating rates of 5-20°C/min. Critical to the invention is the thermal profile where phase separation initiates at 800-950°C, coinciding with accelerated densification onset. In the W-15Cr system, precipitation of Cr-rich phases at particle surfaces and necks is confirmed by STEM-EDS (Fig. 1d), creating continuous networks of high-diffusivity paths.  

**Mechanistic Aspects:**  
The nanophase separation sintering mechanism involves three concurrent processes: (1) decomposition of the supersaturated solid solution upon heating, (2) preferential precipitation of the solute-rich phase at high-diffusivity sites (particle surfaces, grain boundaries, and necks), and (3) rapid mass transport through the precipitated phase. For W-15Cr, the measured activation energy (373 kJ/mol) matches literature values for W diffusion in Cr (386 kJ/mol), confirming this unique transport pathway. The nanocrystalline starting structure provides abundant short-circuit diffusion paths enabling rapid solute redistribution to neck regions.  

**Microstructural Control:**  
Final sintered densities exceed 98% with grain sizes of 50-200 nm, representing a 10-100x refinement compared to conventional tungsten sintering. In optimized W-Ti-Cr alloys (e.g., W-35Ti-10Cr), Ti additions further stabilize nanostructures, yielding 100 nm grains at full density. The method's generality is demonstrated in Cr-Ni systems where Ni precipitation similarly accelerates densification (activation energy 258 kJ/mol, matching Cr diffusion in Ni).  

**Comparative Advantages:**  
Control experiments establish that neither nanocrystallinity alone (nc-W) nor supersaturation alone (coarse-grained W(Cr)) produces significant densification. Only the combination of both features enables the disclosed accelerated sintering. Compared to liquid phase or activated sintering, the invention avoids: (1) melting (Cr melts at 1863°C vs. sintering at 950-1500°C), (2) thin grain boundary films (<1 nm), and (3) external pressure requirements.  

### Non-Limiting Working Examples  

**Example 1 - W-15Cr Alloy:**  
Tungsten and chromium powders (85:15 atomic ratio) were milled for 20 hours to create a supersaturated nanocrystalline solid solution (13 nm grains). Compacts were heated at 10°C/min to 1500°C, achieving 98% density with 120 nm final grain size. STEM-EDS confirmed Cr-rich necks (Fig. 1d) with W diffusion through these regions controlling kinetics (Q=373 kJ/mol).  

**Example 2 - W-35Ti-10Cr Alloy:**  
Addition of 35% Ti to W-10Cr enhanced nanostability during sintering. Pressureless heating to 1350°C yielded full-density compacts with 100 nm grains (Fig. 4e), representing unprecedented refinement for bulk tungsten.  

**Example 3 - Cr-15Ni Alloy:**  
Chromium-nickel powders (85:15) milled for 15 hours demonstrated the method's generality. Sintering at 1200°C produced dense compacts via Ni-rich neck formation, with kinetics governed by Cr diffusion in Ni (Q=258 kJ/mol).  

**Example 4 - Bulk Component Fabrication:**  
A 15mm diameter × 11mm height W-15Cr specimen achieved full density in a conventional furnace, proving scalability beyond laboratory scales.  

The above examples demonstrate the invention's versatility across compositions and scales while achieving the combined benefits of low-temperature densification, nanograin retention, and pressureless operation unobtainable by prior methods.
