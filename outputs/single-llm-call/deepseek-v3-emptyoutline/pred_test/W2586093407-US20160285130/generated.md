## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to novel electrolyte compositions for proton-conducting batteries, particularly nickel/metal hydride (Ni/MH) batteries, comprising mixtures of ionic liquids (ILs) and non-aqueous acids. These electrolyte compositions exhibit wide electrochemical stability windows, high proton conductivity, and improved energy density compared to conventional aqueous electrolytes. The invention provides specific combinations of ILs and acids, such as acetic acid or phosphoric acid, which enhance proton transport through both vehicle and Grotthuss mechanisms. The electrolytes are particularly useful in batteries employing metal hydride anodes and nickel hydroxide cathodes, enabling stable charge/discharge cycles with reduced overpotentials.  

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE  

### x is B or Al and  
The invention encompasses electrolyte compositions where the ionic liquid comprises cations and anions selected to optimize proton conductivity and electrochemical stability. The cations may include imidazolium-based structures such as 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium ([EMIM]+) or 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium ([BMIM]+), while the anions may include trifluoromethanesulfonate ([TfO]-), bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide ([TFSI]-), or acetate ([Ac]-). These combinations provide a balance between viscosity reduction and proton transport efficiency.  

### X is B or Al and  
In certain embodiments, the ionic liquid is selected from the group consisting of 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium trifluoromethanesulfonate ([EMIM][TfO]), 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium trifluoromethanesulfonate ([BMIM][TfO]), 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide ([EMIM][TFSI]), 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide ([BMIM][TFSI]), 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate ([EMIM][Ac]), 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate ([BMIM][Ac]), 1-ethylimidazolium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide ([EIM][TFSI]), and diethylmethylammonium trifluoromethanesulfonate ([DEMA][TfO]). These ILs are particularly effective when mixed with non-aqueous acids to form proton-conductive electrolytes.

The non-aqueous acids used in the electrolyte compositions include, but are not limited to, acetic acid (CH3COOH) and phosphoric acid (H3PO4). These acids function as proton donors, facilitating proton transport through the electrolyte. The concentration of acid in the IL can range from 0.1 M to 6 M, with optimal performance observed at around 2 M for acetic acid in [EMIM][Ac].  

### X is B or Al and  
The electrolyte compositions exhibit ionic conductivities in the range of 10^-2 to 10^-1 S•cm^-1, which are comparable to those of conventional carbonate-based electrolytes used in lithium-ion batteries. The addition of acetic acid to ILs such as [EMIM][Ac] reduces ion association and viscosity, leading to enhanced proton conductivity. For example, [DEMA][TfO] with 6 M acetic acid achieves a conductivity of 14.3 mS•cm^-1, compared to 9.0 mS•cm^-1 for the pure IL.  

### X is B or Al and  
The invention also covers methods of preparing the electrolyte compositions, which involve mixing the ionic liquid with the non-aqueous acid under anhydrous conditions. Prior to use, the ILs are typically dried at 115°C in a vacuum oven for 48 hours to reduce water content to below 1000 ppm. The resulting electrolytes are stable under battery operating conditions and exhibit negligible vapor pressure, non-flammability, and low toxicity.

The electrochemical performance of the electrolytes is characterized by cyclic voltammetry and charge/discharge testing in Ni/MH battery cells. The electrolytes enable stable charge/discharge cycles with voltage plateaus at approximately 1.6 V (charge) and 1.2 V (discharge) for AB5-type anodes. The extended electrochemical stability window of the IL-based electrolytes (up to 2-3 times wider than aqueous electrolytes) allows the use of high-potential electrode materials, thereby increasing battery energy density.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Acetic Acid  
Example 1: A mixture of 1 M acetic acid in [EMIM][TfO] was prepared by dissolving anhydrous acetic acid in the dried ionic liquid. The conductivity of the resulting electrolyte was measured to be 8.2 mS•cm^-1 at 25°C. This electrolyte was tested in a Ni/MH half-cell with an AB5 anode and exhibited a discharge capacity of 15.3 mAh•g^-1 at the 20th cycle.  

### Electrolyte Composition Comprising Ionic Liquid and Solvent  
Example 2: A mixture of 1 M acetic acid in [BMIM][TFSI] was prepared and tested under identical conditions. The electrolyte showed a conductivity of 6.5 mS•cm^-1 and a discharge capacity of 12.7 mAh•g^-1 at the 20th cycle. The lower performance compared to [EMIM][TfO] was attributed to the higher viscosity of [BMIM][TFSI].  

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Protic Acid  
Example 3: A mixture of 1 M phosphoric acid in [EMIM][Ac] was prepared by heating the IL to 50°C to dissolve the solid acid. The electrolyte exhibited a conductivity of 5.8 mS•cm^-1 at 25°C but showed poor cycling stability due to the instability of the Ni(OH)2 cathode in the acidic environment.  

### Electrolyte Composition Containing 2 M Acetic Acid in EMIM Ac  
Example 4: A 2 M acetic acid in [EMIM][Ac] electrolyte was prepared and tested in a Ni/MH full cell. The electrolyte achieved a conductivity of 10.1 mS•cm^-1 and enabled a discharge capacity of 161.9 mAh•g^-1 for an AB5 anode at a current density of 4 mA•g^-1. The charge and discharge plateaus were stable at 1.6 V and 1.2 V, respectively, over 100 cycles.  

### Example 5  
Example 5: A comparative study was conducted using 30 wt% KOH aqueous electrolyte in the same Ni/MH cell configuration. While the aqueous electrolyte showed higher conductivity (σ ≈ 500 mS•cm^-1) and capacity (320 mAh•g^-1), the IL-based electrolyte demonstrated a wider electrochemical stability window and reduced risk of hydrogen evolution, making it suitable for high-energy-density applications.
