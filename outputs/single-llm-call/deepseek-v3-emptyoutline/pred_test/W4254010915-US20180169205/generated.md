# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of immunology and vaccine development. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel vaccine scaffolds capable of eliciting T-cell independent (TI) immune responses through engineered polymeric flagellin (FliC) structures. The invention provides hybrid flagellin proteins wherein the native D3 domain is replaced with antigenic determinants from pathogens, creating polymeric vaccine candidates that stimulate robust antibody production even in T-cell deficient conditions.  

### Related Art  

Current vaccine strategies predominantly rely on T-cell dependent (TD) immune responses, wherein T helper cells facilitate B cell activation, isotype switching, and plasma cell development. However, this approach fails in immunocompromised individuals with T-cell deficiencies such as AIDS, chemotherapy patients, or those with congenital immunodeficiencies. While certain "innate-like" B cells (marginal zone B cells and B1 B cells) can mount TI responses through B cell receptor (BCR) crosslinking by polymeric antigens, existing TI vaccine platforms have shown limited efficacy.  

Prior attempts to develop TI vaccines have focused on bacterial capsular components, but these suffer from poor immunogenicity and limited antigen display capacity. Flagellin from Salmonella typhimurium has been explored as an adjuvant due to its Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) binding properties, but previous studies suggested it required T-cell help for humoral responses. Conventional approaches to multivalent vaccine design through linear protein linkage have proven inadequate for many diseases, particularly for pathogens like Dengue virus where balanced immunity against multiple serotypes is crucial.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel vaccine scaffold comprising engineered hybrid flagellin (hFliC) proteins capable of stimulating both TD and TI immune responses. The invention involves replacing the solvent-exposed D3 domain of native FliC with conformationally intact antigenic domains from pathogens, while preserving the protein's ability to polymerize into immunogenic filaments. These engineered filaments display repetitive antigenic epitopes spaced approximately 5 nm apart - an optimal configuration for BCR crosslinking and TI activation.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1) The polymeric structure provides sufficient length (>500 nm) and epitope density (10-20 BCR crosslinks) for potent TI responses  
2) The scaffold maintains TLR5 adjuvant activity through preserved D1 domains  
3) The modular design allows insertion of diverse antigenic domains while preserving structural integrity  
4) Demonstrated efficacy in T-cell restricted animal models  

Particular embodiments feature insertion of Dengue virus envelope (E) protein domains, creating filaments that induce robust IgM and IgG3 responses characteristic of TI activation. The platform shows particular promise for immunocompromised populations and as a universal Dengue vaccine strategy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides engineered flagellin proteins wherein the D3 domain (residues 185-285) is replaced with heterologous antigenic domains through flexible glycine-serine linkers. In preferred embodiments, the antigenic domain comprises the full extracellular portion of Dengue virus serotype 2 envelope protein (DENV2 E), though the platform accommodates antigens from other pathogens.  

The hybrid proteins maintain critical structural features:  
- Preserved α-helical domains (D0-D2) necessary for polymerization  
- Intact TLR5/NLRC4 binding sites in D1 for adjuvant activity  
- Proper folding of inserted antigenic domains as confirmed by circular dichroism  

Polymerization occurs under physiological salt conditions (1.5M ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate), forming filaments of >500 nm length with 35 nm diameter. Electron microscopy confirms surface exposure of inserted antigens, while immunogold labeling demonstrates preserved antigenicity. The repetitive antigen display (spaced ~5 nm) enables effective BCR crosslinking as shown by:  
- Confocal microscopy demonstrating receptor clustering on 4G2 hybridoma cells  
- Strong IgM/IgG3 responses in T-cell restricted (6.5-TCR) mouse models  
- Dose-dependent antibody titers following intraperitoneal administration  

### Example 1  

**Construction and Characterization of hFliC-DENV2 E**  

The hFliC protein was engineered by:  
1) PCR amplification of FliC fragments (residues 1-184 and 285-494) with overlapping restriction sites  
2) Insertion of DENV2 E domain (flanked by GGGSGGGS linkers) via KpnI/EcoRI sites  
3) Baculovirus expression in SF9 cells and purification via His-tag affinity chromatography  

Structural analysis revealed:  
- Circular dichroism spectra showing hybrid α-helical/β-sheet characteristics  
- 85 kDa molecular weight by SDS-PAGE/Western blot  
- Polymerization into filaments with increased diameter (35 nm vs 20 nm for wild-type)  

Functional validation included:  
- Binding to DENV2-specific 4G2 monoclonal antibody in ELISA  
- Immunogold labeling confirming surface exposure of E domains  
- BCR crosslinking demonstrated by receptor clustering assays  

### Example 2  

**Immunogenicity in Animal Models**  

Wild-type (C57BL/6J) and T-cell restricted (6.5-TCR) mice received either:  
- Monomeric hFliC  
- Polymeric hFliC filaments  
Administered via intraperitoneal (IP) or intranasal (IN) routes with boosters at weeks 1-3  

Key findings:  
1) Wild-type mice showed:  
- Strong IgA responses to IN administration  
- Robust IgG/IgM to IP administration (particularly IgG1/IgG2b)  
- 5-fold higher titers with polymeric vs monomeric forms  

2) 6.5-TCR mice demonstrated:  
- Significant IgM production (comparable to wild-type)  
- Selective IgG3 response (characteristic of TI activation)  
- No IgA production (confirming T-cell independence)  

The IP route proved particularly effective, suggesting preferential targeting of peritoneal B1 cells. Dose-response studies indicated optimal immunization at 20 μg protein per dose.  

## CONCLUSIONS  

The present invention establishes flagellin as a versatile scaffold for TI vaccine development. By engineering hybrid proteins that polymerize into immunogenic filaments, we demonstrate:  

1) Successful integration of conformationally intact pathogen antigens while preserving flagellin's adjuvant properties  
2) Robust antibody responses in both TD and TI immunological pathways  
3) Particular efficacy against Dengue virus through engineered E protein display  

This platform addresses critical unmet needs in vaccinology, including:  
- Protection for immunocompromised populations  
- Development of universal vaccines for highly variable pathogens  
- Enhanced immunogenicity through combined BCR crosslinking and TLR activation  

The modular design allows adaptation to diverse pathogens by substituting alternative antigenic domains while maintaining the core scaffold architecture. This represents a significant advance over existing TI vaccine technologies with broad applications in infectious disease prevention.
