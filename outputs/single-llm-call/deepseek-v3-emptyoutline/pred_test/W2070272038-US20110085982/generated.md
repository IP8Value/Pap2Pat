## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to the field of cancer biology and specifically to a novel cell line model of triple-negative breast cancer. More particularly, the invention provides the DKAT cell line, derived from an aggressive, treatment-resistant human triple-negative breast cancer, which exhibits unique epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity both in vitro and in vivo. The invention further encompasses methods of using the DKAT cell line for studying cancer metastasis, screening potential therapeutic compounds, and developing personalized treatment strategies for triple-negative breast cancer patients.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Triple-negative breast cancers, characterized by their lack of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification, represent a particularly aggressive subtype of breast cancer with limited treatment options and poor prognosis. Current understanding of the molecular mechanisms driving the metastatic potential of these cancers remains incomplete, in part due to the lack of appropriate experimental models that accurately recapitulate the biological behavior of these tumors in patients.  

The epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process, wherein epithelial cells lose their characteristic markers and acquire mesenchymal properties, has been implicated in cancer metastasis. However, existing breast cancer cell lines fail to adequately model the reversible nature of this process as it may occur in human tumors. Furthermore, current models do not fully capture the phenotypic heterogeneity observed in clinical specimens of aggressive triple-negative breast cancers.  

There exists a critical need in the field for improved experimental models that can faithfully reproduce the biological characteristics of treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancers, particularly those demonstrating epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity. Such models would enable more accurate study of metastatic mechanisms and provide improved platforms for drug discovery and development.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing the DKAT cell line, a novel model system derived from an aggressive, treatment-resistant human triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line exhibits unique properties of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity, including the ability to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to environmental cues.  

Key features of the invention include:  
1. A biologically stable cell line capable of long-term culture while maintaining its characteristic phenotypic plasticity  
2. A model system that demonstrates reversible EMT/MET transitions in vitro in response to defined culture conditions  
3. A xenograft model that recapitulates the heterogeneous phenotypes observed in the original human tumor  
4. A platform for studying the molecular mechanisms underlying epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity in aggressive breast cancers  
5. A tool for screening potential therapeutic compounds that may target plastic cancer cell populations  

The DKAT cell line provides significant advantages over existing models, including its derivation from a clinically aggressive tumor, its stable maintenance of phenotypic plasticity through multiple passages, and its ability to form tumors in immunocompromised mice at very low cell numbers. These characteristics make the DKAT cell line particularly valuable for studying metastatic mechanisms and developing new treatment strategies for triple-negative breast cancer.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The DKAT cell line was isolated from the malignant pleural effusion of a patient with rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The cell line has been maintained continuously in culture for over 70 passages while retaining its characteristic properties of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity.  

Karyotypic analysis reveals that DKAT cells have a modal chromosome number of 56, with two predominant clones identified by spectral karyotyping. The cell line carries a missense mutation in TP53 at codon 273 (CGT > CAT), a mutation known to be deleterious to p53 function and commonly found in aggressive breast cancers. Sequencing of PIK3CA exons 9 and 20 revealed no mutations in these regions.  

At baseline, DKAT cells grown in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media (MEGM) express characteristic epithelial markers including E-cadherin, cytokeratin 5, and cytokeratin 18, with minimal expression of the mesenchymal marker vimentin. Flow cytometry analysis demonstrates that a high percentage of DKAT cells exhibit the CD44+/CD24-/low phenotype associated with tumor-initiating cells and cells that have undergone EMT.  

A defining feature of the DKAT cell line is its ability to undergo reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transitions in response to changes in culture conditions. When switched from MEGM to Stromal Cell Growth Media (SCGM), DKAT cells rapidly upregulate vimentin expression (within 1 hour) and undergo morphological changes to a spindle-shaped appearance (within 2 hours). By 14 days in SCGM, cells demonstrate significant downregulation of E-cadherin and upregulation of mesenchymal markers including N-cadherin. This transition is accompanied by increased migratory capacity in scratch assays.  

Remarkably, this process is reversible. DKAT cells maintained in SCGM for multiple passages can be returned to an epithelial phenotype by switching back to MEGM culture conditions. Within five passages, these cells demonstrate significant reduction in vimentin expression and re-expression of epithelial markers.  

The Zeb1 transcription factor has been identified as a key regulator of this plasticity. Zeb1 expression increases during the EMT transition in DKAT cells, and forced overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial-state DKAT cells is sufficient to induce mesenchymal characteristics. Conversely, knockdown of Zeb1 prevents the increased migratory capacity associated with the mesenchymal state.  

In vivo, DKAT cells demonstrate significant tumorigenicity, forming tumors in immunocompromised mice at injection doses as low as 10 cells. The resulting xenografts recapitulate the phenotypic heterogeneity observed in the original human tumor, with different regions exhibiting varying degrees of epithelial and mesenchymal characteristics depending on the local microenvironment.  

Single DKAT cells demonstrate multi-lineage potential in tumorsphere assays, forming structures that contain both epithelial (E-cadherin+/vimentin-) and mesenchymal (E-cadherin-/vimentin+) cell populations. This provides further evidence of the inherent plasticity of these cells and their similarity to cancer stem cell populations.  

## DEFINITIONS  

For purposes of interpreting this specification, the following definitions will apply:  

"DKAT cell line" refers to the triple-negative breast cancer cell line deposited under ATCC Accession No. [INSERT NUMBER] and any derivatives thereof that maintain the characteristic epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity described herein.  

"Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)" refers to the biological process wherein epithelial cells lose their characteristic polarity and cell-cell adhesion properties and acquire a migratory, mesenchymal phenotype, characterized by changes in morphology, marker expression, and functional properties.  

"Mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET)" refers to the reverse process wherein mesenchymal cells regain epithelial characteristics.  

"Phenotypic plasticity" refers to the ability of cells to reversibly transition between different phenotypic states in response to environmental cues.  

"Triple-negative breast cancer" refers to breast cancers that lack expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification as determined by standard clinical assays.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Establishment and Characterization of the DKAT Cell Line**  

The DKAT cell line was established from the malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old woman with aggressive triple-negative breast cancer. Cells were cultured in Mammary Epithelial Growth Medium (MEGM) and expanded for characterization.  

Karyotypic analysis revealed a modal chromosome number of 56, with two predominant clones identified. Sequencing detected a TP53 mutation at codon 273 (CGT > CAT) but no mutations in PIK3CA exons 9 or 20. Baseline characterization showed expression of epithelial markers (E-cadherin, cytokeratins 5, 17, and 18) and phosphorylation of Akt at Serine 473. Flow cytometry demonstrated a CD44+/CD24-/low phenotype in the majority of cells.  

### Example 2  
**Induction of EMT in DKAT Cells**  

DKAT cells cultured in MEGM were switched to Stromal Cell Growth Medium (SCGM). Within 1 hour, vimentin expression increased 100-fold by western blot. By 2 hours, cells exhibited spindle-shaped morphology and relocalization of E-cadherin from membrane to cytoplasm. After 14 days in SCGM, E-cadherin expression was significantly decreased while N-cadherin expression increased. Scratch assays demonstrated enhanced migratory capacity in SCGM-cultured cells compared to MEGM controls.  

### Example 3  
**Reversal of EMT (MET) in DKAT Cells**  

DKAT cells maintained in SCGM for 10 passages were switched back to MEGM. After 5 passages, cells showed significant reduction in vimentin and N-cadherin expression with concomitant increase in E-cadherin and cytokeratins. Immunofluorescence confirmed re-establishment of epithelial morphology and marker expression patterns.  

### Example 4  
**Role of Zeb1 in DKAT Plasticity**  

Western blot analysis demonstrated increased Zeb1 expression during EMT. Constitutive overexpression of Zeb1 in epithelial-state DKAT cells induced mesenchymal characteristics. Conversely, Zeb1 knockdown using shRNA prevented the increased migration normally observed during EMT. These experiments establish Zeb1 as a key regulator of plasticity in the DKAT model.  

### Example 5  
**Tumorigenicity of DKAT Cells**  

Limiting dilution experiments demonstrated that as few as 10 DKAT cells could form tumors in immunocompromised mice. Tumors appeared within 2-4 weeks and reached endpoint size (15mm diameter) by 13 weeks in most animals. The high tumorigenicity suggests the presence of tumor-initiating cell populations within the DKAT line.  

### Example 6  
**Phenotypic Heterogeneity in DKAT Xenografts**  

Histological analysis of DKAT xenografts revealed three distinct growth patterns: 1) infiltrating growth without dense fibrous matrix, 2) infiltrating growth with dense fibrous matrix, and 3) intraductal and Pagetoid spread. Immunohistochemistry showed variable expression of epithelial and mesenchymal markers in different regions of the tumors, recapitulating the heterogeneity observed in the original human tumor.  

### Example 7  
**Matrigel Effects on Xenograft Phenotype**  

Addition of Matrigel to DKAT cell injections altered tumor morphology and marker expression. Tumors formed nests and cords of cells staining for both cytokeratin 5 and smooth muscle actin, suggesting myoepithelial differentiation. Peripheral invasive cells maintained cytokeratin 5 expression while central tumor regions showed larger nuclei and more abundant cytoplasm.  

### Example 8  
**Single-Cell Derivation of Clonal Lines**  

Clonal cell lines derived from single DKAT cells maintained in MEGM demonstrated the same capacity for EMT induction as the bulk population when switched to SCGM. Similarly, clonal lines derived from SCGM-maintained DKAT cells could generate both epithelial and mesenchymal populations when returned to MEGM, confirming the plasticity is an inherent property of individual DKAT cells rather than selection of pre-existing subpopulations.  

### Example 9  
**Tumorsphere Formation and Multi-lineage Potential**  

Single DKAT cells formed tumorspheres with 3-10% efficiency in non-adherent culture conditions. These tumorspheres contained distinct populations of cells expressing either epithelial (E-cadherin+/vimentin-) or mesenchymal (E-cadherin-/vimentin+) markers, demonstrating that individual DKAT cells can generate heterogeneous progeny with different phenotypic characteristics.
