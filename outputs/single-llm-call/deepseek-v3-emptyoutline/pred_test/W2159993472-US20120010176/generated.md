# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic treatments for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of small-molecule compounds, particularly nabumetone, for the upregulation of utrophin expression in muscle tissues as a means to compensate for the absence of dystrophin in DMD patients. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, methods of administration, and therapeutic regimens for the treatment of DMD through utrophin modulation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe X-linked genetic disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene, leading to progressive muscle degeneration, respiratory failure, and premature death. Dystrophin is essential for maintaining muscle integrity by linking the cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix. In its absence, muscle fibers undergo repeated cycles of degeneration and regeneration, ultimately resulting in fibrosis and functional impairment.  

Current therapeutic strategies for DMD include corticosteroids, gene therapy, exon-skipping oligonucleotides, and stop codon readthrough agents. However, these approaches are limited by mutation specificity, immune responses, and delivery challenges. A promising alternative involves upregulating utrophin, an autosomal paralog of dystrophin that can functionally compensate for dystrophin deficiency. Utrophin shares structural and functional similarities with dystrophin and is naturally expressed in fetal muscle, neuromuscular junctions, and other tissues. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that utrophin overexpression ameliorates the dystrophic phenotype in animal models.  

Despite these advances, existing utrophin-upregulating therapies, such as protein-based or viral vector-mediated approaches, face obstacles in clinical translation due to immunogenicity, manufacturing complexity, and regulatory hurdles. Small-molecule drugs offer a more tractable solution, given their established safety profiles, oral bioavailability, and ease of large-scale production.  

There remains an unmet need for clinically viable small-molecule therapeutics that effectively upregulate utrophin expression in muscle tissues to treat DMD. The present invention addresses this need by identifying and validating nabumetone and related compounds as novel utrophin modulators.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for treating Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a small-molecule compound that upregulates utrophin expression. In particular, the invention discloses the use of nabumetone, a COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor, as a potent inducer of utrophin promoter activity, mRNA, and protein levels in muscle cells.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Identification of Utrophin-Upregulating Compounds**: A high-throughput screening assay was developed to evaluate compounds for their ability to activate the utrophin A promoter. Nabumetone emerged as a lead candidate, demonstrating dose-dependent upregulation of utrophin expression in vitro.  
2. **Mechanism of Action**: Nabumetone enhances endogenous utrophin transcription, leading to increased mRNA and protein levels, thereby compensating for dystrophin deficiency in DMD.  
3. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: Formulations of nabumetone and related compounds suitable for oral, intravenous, or intramuscular administration.  
4. **Therapeutic Applications**: Methods for treating DMD patients by administering utrophin-upregulating compounds to improve muscle function and delay disease progression.  

The invention further encompasses derivatives, prodrugs, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts of nabumetone, as well as combination therapies with anti-inflammatory agents or other DMD therapeutics.  

## DEFINITIONS  

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms are defined as follows:  

- **Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)**: A genetic disorder characterized by progressive muscle degeneration due to mutations in the dystrophin gene.  
- **Utrophin**: An autosomal paralog of dystrophin capable of functionally compensating for dystrophin deficiency when upregulated.  
- **Nabumetone**: A nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) with COX-1/COX-2 inhibitory activity, identified herein as an utrophin upregulator.  
- **Therapeutically effective amount**: A dosage sufficient to induce utrophin expression and ameliorate DMD symptoms without causing significant adverse effects.  
- **Pharmaceutically acceptable salt**: A salt form of nabumetone or related compounds that retains biological activity and is suitable for therapeutic use.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that nabumetone and structurally related compounds activate the utrophin A promoter, leading to increased utrophin mRNA and protein expression in muscle cells. This effect was identified through a high-throughput screening assay utilizing a luciferase reporter system under the control of the utrophin promoter.  

### **Screening and Validation**  
A stable C2C12 muscle cell line was engineered to express luciferase under the regulation of the human utrophin A promoter. This cell line was used to screen the Prestwick Chemical Library, yielding nabumetone as a validated hit. Subsequent dose-response studies confirmed nabumetone’s ability to upregulate utrophin promoter activity by up to 3.5-fold.  

Independent validation experiments demonstrated that nabumetone treatment increased endogenous utrophin A mRNA levels by 1.8-fold and protein levels by 1.2-fold in C2C12 myoblasts. These findings support the therapeutic potential of nabumetone in DMD by partially restoring the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC) through utrophin compensation.  

### **Mechanistic Insights**  
Nabumetone’s upregulation of utrophin is believed to occur via transcriptional activation, though post-transcriptional mechanisms may also contribute. The compound’s anti-inflammatory properties, mediated by COX inhibition, may provide additional benefits in DMD by reducing muscle inflammation.  

### **Pharmaceutical Compositions and Methods of Administration**  

Nabumetone and related compounds may be formulated for various routes of administration, including:  
- **Oral formulations**: Tablets, capsules, or suspensions.  
- **Parenteral formulations**: Injectable solutions for intramuscular or intravenous delivery.  
- **Controlled-release formulations**: Sustained-release matrices to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations.  

Dosage regimens may vary based on patient age, disease severity, and pharmacokinetic profiles. A typical dose range for nabumetone in DMD treatment may be between 25 µM and 200 µM equivalents in plasma, adjusted for bioavailability.  

## EXAMPLES  

### **Example 1: High-Throughput Screening of Utrophin Promoter Activators**  
The Prestwick Chemical Library (1,120 compounds) was screened using the C2C12utrn reporter cell line. Nabumetone was identified as a hit, showing a 1.8-fold increase in luciferase activity at 5 µM.  

### **Example 2: Dose-Response Validation**  
Nabumetone exhibited a sigmoidal dose-response curve with an EC50 of ~10 µM. Maximum utrophin promoter activation (3.5-fold) was achieved at 25 µM.  

### **Example 3: Endogenous Utrophin Upregulation**  
C2C12 cells treated with 25 µM nabumetone showed a 1.8-fold increase in utrophin A mRNA and a 1.2-fold increase in protein levels, confirming transcriptional activation.  

### **Example 4: In Vivo Efficacy (Proposed Study)**  
Future preclinical studies will evaluate nabumetone in mdx mice to assess utrophin upregulation, muscle function improvement, and long-term safety.
