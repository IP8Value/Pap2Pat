# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to devices and methods for real-time intraoperative detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes. More particularly, the invention provides a metabolism-based diagnostic tool that utilizes pH sensing to rapidly and accurately identify cancerous lymph nodes during surgical procedures, particularly in breast cancer patients. The system employs a novel combination of a syringe-based fluid delivery mechanism with integrated pH-sensitive indicators to detect metabolic changes characteristic of metastatic cancer cells.  

## BACKGROUND  

Current methods for detecting lymph node metastasis during surgery rely primarily on frozen section pathology, which suffers from several critical limitations. The frozen section technique requires significant time, specialized equipment, and highly trained pathologists, often resulting in delays that impact surgical decision-making. Furthermore, due to time constraints, only selected portions of lymph nodes are typically examined, increasing the risk of false negatives when small metastatic foci are missed.  

Alternative approaches proposed in the art include fluorescent labeling techniques using anti-EGFR or anti-HER2 markers combined with radioactive tracers, microwave-based detection systems requiring 15-30 minutes for analysis, and tissue impedance imaging methods with scan times of 5-10 minutes. While these methods represent improvements over traditional pathology, they remain complex, time-consuming, and often require specialized preoperative preparations.  

A fundamental biological insight underlying the present invention is the discovery that cancer cells metastasizing to lymph nodes undergo a metabolic shift from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO). This metabolic reprogramming, mediated through YAP signaling pathways, leads to characteristic acidification of the lymph node microenvironment through biochemical reactions that generate hydrogen ions. The resulting pH differential between healthy and metastatic lymph nodes provides a reliable biomarker that the current invention exploits for rapid, intraoperative diagnosis.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD) system comprising a specialized syringe apparatus integrating pH-sensitive indicators for real-time detection of cancerous lymph nodes. The system includes:  

1) A micro-syringe (preferably gauge 18 with an inner needle diameter of approximately 838 μm) containing a sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solution;  
2) A syringe needle embedded with a pH-sensitive indicator (preferably a litmus paper such as ColorpHast® pH Test Strips);  
3) A standardized protocol for lymph node sampling involving PBS injection, fluid sampling, and pH measurement; and  
4) Diagnostic criteria establishing pH thresholds for metastatic detection (preferably pH <6.0 indicating malignancy).  

The MMLD system operates by injecting PBS into excised lymph nodes, allowing the solution to equilibrate with the nodal microenvironment, and then measuring pH through colorimetric changes in the embedded indicator. This process requires less than 30 seconds per lymph node, providing surgeons with near-instantaneous diagnostic information. Clinical validation studies demonstrate 90% selectivity and 92% sensitivity in detecting metastatic involvement, outperforming conventional frozen section pathology (83% on both metrics).  

Key advantages over existing methods include:  
- Real-time results enabling immediate surgical decision-making  
- Elimination of specialized pathology equipment requirements  
- Reduced false negatives through whole-node assessment  
- Prevention of unnecessary lymph node dissections  
- Minimization of postoperative complications like lymphedema  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The MMLD system represents a significant advancement in intraoperative cancer diagnostics by leveraging fundamental cancer metabolism principles into a practical clinical tool. The detailed operational protocol comprises three principal phases:  

**Preparation Phase:**  
The MMLD apparatus is pre-sterilized according to ISO 22441 plasma sterilization standards. Each diagnostic unit consists of a syringe pre-loaded with 100 μL sterile PBS and a needle incorporating a pH-sensitive litmus indicator. Three independent units are typically prepared for each lymph node assessment to ensure diagnostic reliability through redundant measurements.  

**Sampling Phase:**  
Following lymph node excision, the surgeon inserts the MMLD needle into the nodal tissue and injects the PBS solution. The injected fluid circulates through the lymph node's interstitial spaces, equilibrating with the local biochemical environment. After a brief incubation period (approximately 15-20 seconds), the needle withdraws a fluid sample that carries the characteristic pH signature of the lymph node microenvironment.  

**Diagnostic Phase:**  
The pH-sensitive indicator immediately responds to the sampled fluid, producing a color change proportional to the hydrogen ion concentration. In metastatic lymph nodes where fatty acid oxidation predominates, the pH typically falls below 6.0, while healthy nodes maintain pH values between 7.0-10.0. The surgeon interprets the color change against standardized reference charts, with acidic readings indicating malignancy.  

The biochemical basis for this pH differential stems from cancer cell metabolism in lymph nodes. Metastatic cells upregulate fatty acid oxidation, producing acetyl-CoA molecules that enter the Krebs cycle. Subsequent oxidative phosphorylation generates CO₂, which reacts with water to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃). This weak acid dissociates into bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) and hydrogen ions (H⁺), creating the acidic microenvironment detected by the MMLD system.  

Clinical validation involved testing on 65 lymph nodes from 25 breast cancer patients, comparing MMLD results against gold standard permanent H&E pathology. The system demonstrated exceptional diagnostic accuracy, with only one false positive and one false negative among all samples. This performance surpasses conventional frozen section pathology in both sensitivity (92% vs 83%) and selectivity (90% vs 83%).  

### Example 1: Real-Time Diagnosis of Cancer-Involved Lymph Nodes  

A 47-year-old female patient presented with invasive ductal carcinoma of the right breast. During sentinel lymph node biopsy, three lymph nodes were excised and immediately analyzed using the MMLD system.  

**Node 1:**  
- PBS injection volume: 100 μL  
- Incubation time: 20 seconds  
- pH indicator result: Yellow (pH 5.8)  
- Interpretation: Positive for metastasis  
- Subsequent permanent pathology: Confirmed metastatic involvement (25% tumor replacement)  

**Node 2:**  
- PBS injection volume: 100 μL  
- Incubation time: 20 seconds  
- pH indicator result: Green (pH 7.2)  
- Interpretation: Negative for metastasis  
- Subsequent permanent pathology: Benign lymphoid tissue  

**Node 3:**  
- PBS injection volume: 100 μL  
- Incubation time: 20 seconds  
- pH indicator result: Yellow-green (pH 6.4)  
- Interpretation: Borderline result, recommended further analysis  
- Subsequent permanent pathology: Microscopic metastatic focus (<1mm)  

This case demonstrates the MMLD system's ability to:  
1) Correctly identify frankly metastatic nodes (Node 1)  
2) Reliably exclude non-metastatic nodes (Node 2)  
3) Flag borderline cases requiring additional scrutiny (Node 3)  

The entire diagnostic process for all three nodes required less than 2 minutes, enabling immediate surgical decision-making regarding axillary lymph node dissection. Notably, Node 2's negative result prevented unnecessary additional lymph node removal, potentially sparing the patient from lymphedema complications.  

The MMLD system's clinical utility extends beyond breast cancer applications, with potential adaptation for melanoma, head and neck cancers, and other malignancies where lymph node assessment guides therapeutic decisions. Future embodiments may incorporate digital colorimetric analysis for enhanced objectivity and quantitative pH measurement.
