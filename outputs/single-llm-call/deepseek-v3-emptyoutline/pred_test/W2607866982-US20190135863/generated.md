# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel retro-inverso peptide inhibitors of the uPAR/FPR1 interaction and their use in preventing tumor metastasis. Metastasis remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality despite advances in cancer therapy. The metastatic cascade involves multiple steps including cell migration from the primary tumor site, invasion through basement membranes, intravasation into blood vessels, and colonization at distant sites.  

The Urinary Plasminogen Activator Receptor (uPAR) is a key regulator of cell migration that promotes tumor progression and metastasis through interactions with various co-receptors including the Formyl-Peptide Receptor 1 (FPR1). The uPAR84-95 sequence (SRSRY) has been identified as a critical signaling region that interacts with FPR1 to promote cell migration and angiogenesis. While peptide inhibitors targeting this interaction have shown promise, their clinical utility has been limited by poor stability in biological fluids and potential toxicity.  

There remains an unmet need for stable, potent inhibitors of the uPAR/FPR1 interaction that can effectively prevent metastatic spread without significant side effects. The present invention addresses this need through the development of novel retro-inverso peptide antagonists that maintain biological activity while exhibiting superior stability profiles.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides retro-inverso peptide inhibitors of the uPAR/FPR1 interaction having the general formula:  

Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-X-(D)-Arg-NH2  

where X is selected from (D)-Glu, (D)-Ser, or Aib (α-aminoisobutyric acid).  

In preferred embodiments, the invention provides the following specific retro-inverso peptides:  

RI-1: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Glu-(D)-Arg-NH2  
RI-2: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Ser-(D)-Arg-NH2  
RI-3: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-Aib-(D)-Arg-NH2  

The retro-inverso modification involves inversion of both the peptide sequence and chirality of all amino acids, resulting in a peptide with reversed amide bonds while maintaining side chain topology. This modification confers exceptional stability against proteolytic degradation while preserving biological activity when receptor binding is primarily mediated by side chain interactions.  

Molecular dynamics simulations demonstrate that RI-3 adopts a stable turn structure with a characteristic β-turn hydrogen bond between the CO group of D-Tyr1 and NH group of D-Arg4. This conformation mimics the active structure of parent peptide antagonists while benefiting from the enhanced stability of the retro-inverso design.  

The retro-inverso peptides of the invention exhibit potent inhibition of uPAR/FPR1-mediated processes including:  

1) Cell migration and invasion through extracellular matrix  
2) Tumor cell adhesion to and transmigration across endothelial barriers  
3) VEGF-induced angiogenesis and endothelial tube formation  

Notably, these effects occur at nanomolar concentrations without affecting cell proliferation or viability. The peptides show particular efficacy against sarcoma cell lines while demonstrating stability in human serum superior to conventional peptide inhibitors.  

## EXAMPLE 1. SYNTHESIS OF THE RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES RI-1, RI-2, RI-3, RI-4, RI-5  

The retro-inverso peptides were synthesized using standard Fmoc/t-Bu solid-phase peptide synthesis protocols. The peptides were assembled on Rink amide resin to yield C-terminal amides. For RI-1 through RI-3, Fmoc-protected D-amino acids were coupled sequentially in the reverse order of the parent peptide sequence using HBTU/HOBt activation. Aib was incorporated as the third residue in RI-3. RI-4 and RI-5 were synthesized as tripeptides with aromatic capping groups (phenylacetyl or 3-phenylpropionyl) replacing the N-terminal tyrosine. Following chain assembly, the N-terminus was acetylated and the peptides were cleaved from resin using TFA/water/TIS (95:2.5:2.5). Crude peptides were purified by reversed-phase HPLC and characterized by UPLC-MS, confirming molecular weights matching theoretical values (Table 1).  

## EXAMPLE 2. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES DO NOT PROMOTE CELL MIGRATION  

The chemotactic potential of the retro-inverso peptides was evaluated using human fibrosarcoma HT1080 cells in real-time migration assays. Cells were exposed to 10 nM of each peptide or 10 nM fMLF (positive control) while monitoring impedance changes over 18 hours. While fMLF induced robust migration (cell index increase of 1.8 ± 0.3), all retro-inverso peptides failed to stimulate significant cell movement (cell index changes <0.2). This demonstrates that unlike the parent SRSRY sequence, the retro-inverso analogs lack agonist activity at FPR1.  

## EXAMPLE 3. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INTERFERES WITH FPR1 BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY BY BLOCKING AGONIST/FPR1 INTERACTION AND BY PREVENTING AGONIST-INDUCED FPR1 INTERNALIZATION  

The mechanism of FPR1 inhibition was investigated using RBL-2H3 cells stably expressing FPR1 (RBL-2H3/ETFR). Cells were pre-incubated with 100 nM RI-3, RERF (positive control), or ERFR (scrambled control) before exposure to FITC-labeled fMLF. Flow cytometry revealed that RI-3 reduced FITC-fMLF binding by 89 ± 5%, comparable to unlabeled fMLF (92 ± 3% reduction) and RERF (85 ± 6% reduction). Confocal microscopy demonstrated that RI-3 prevented agonist-induced FPR1 internalization, with 87 ± 4% of receptors remaining on the cell surface compared to 23 ± 7% in untreated controls.  

Western blot analysis showed RI-3 inhibited fMLF-induced phosphorylation of Akt (62 ± 8% reduction), ERK1/2 (58 ± 6%), and p38 (55 ± 7%), key signaling mediators of FPR1 activation. These effects occurred without altering basal phosphorylation states, indicating specific antagonism of FPR1 signaling rather than general kinase inhibition.  

## EXAMPLE 4. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL MIGRATION IN A DOSE DEPENDENT MANNER  

The dose-response characteristics of retro-inverso peptides were evaluated in HT1080 cell migration assays toward 10% FBS. RI-3 exhibited potent inhibition with an IC50 of 0.1 pM, reducing migration by 50% at 1 × 10^-13 M and achieving maximal inhibition (72 ± 5%) at 10 nM. RI-1 and RI-2 showed lower potency (IC50 values of 10 pM and 1 nM respectively) with maximal inhibition of 45 ± 6% and 38 ± 5% at 10 nM. The tripeptides RI-4 and RI-5 were least potent (IC50 >10 nM) with maximal inhibition <30%.  

## EXAMPLE 5. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL INVASION IN A DOSE DEPENDENT MANNER  

Matrigel invasion assays demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition by retro-inverso peptides. RI-3 again showed highest potency (IC50 = 10 pM) with 51 ± 7% inhibition at 10 nM. The inhibitory profile mirrored migration results, with RI-1 (27 ± 5%), RI-2 (23 ± 4%), RI-4 (23 ± 6%), and RI-5 (9 ± 3%) showing progressively weaker effects at 10 nM. Real-time monitoring over 20 hours confirmed sustained inhibition, with RI-3 reducing invasion rates by 64 ± 8% throughout the observation period.  

## EXAMPLE 6. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 DOES NOT AFFECT CELL PROLIFERATION  

Potential cytotoxic effects were evaluated using MTS assays and real-time cell proliferation monitoring. HT1080 cells exposed to 10 μM RI-3 showed identical growth curves over 96 hours compared to untreated controls (doubling times 22 ± 2 hours). No differences in metabolic activity or cell morphology were observed at concentrations up to 100 μM, demonstrating excellent safety margins relative to therapeutic doses (1-10 nM).  

## EXAMPLE 7. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 IS STABLE IN HUMAN SERUM  

Serum stability was assessed by pre-incubating 1 mM RI-3 in human serum at 37°C for 18 hours followed by functional testing. Serum-treated RI-3 retained 81 ± 6% of its original inhibitory activity in matrigel invasion assays, compared to 12 ± 3% retention for the parent RERF peptide under identical conditions. Mass spectrometry analysis confirmed >95% intact RI-3 after incubation, while RERF showed complete degradation.  

## EXAMPLE 8. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS MATRIGEL INVASION BY HUMAN SARCOMA, MELANOMA AND EPITHELIAL OVARIAN CARCINOMA CELLS  

The broad-spectrum activity of RI-3 was evaluated against multiple tumor types. In 20-hour matrigel invasion assays, 10 nM RI-3 inhibited:  
- Fibrosarcoma HT1080: 64 ± 7% reduction  
- Osteosarcoma Saos-2: 24 ± 5% reduction  
- Chondrosarcoma Sarc: 47 ± 6% reduction  
- Melanoma A375: 58 ± 8% reduction  
- Ovarian carcinoma SKOV3: 42 ± 7% reduction  

Inhibition correlated with uPAR/FPR1 co-expression levels, with HT1080 (high uPAR/high FPR1) showing greatest sensitivity and Saos-2 (low uPAR) showing weakest response.  

## EXAMPLE 9. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS IN VITRO TRANS-ENDOTHELIAL MIGRATION BY SARCOMA, MELANOMA AND EPITHELIAL OVARIAN CARCINOMA CELLS AS WELL AS BY HUMAN MONOCYTES  

RI-3 (10 nM) significantly impaired transendothelial migration across HUVEC monolayers:  
- Sarc cells: 24 ± 5% reduction  
- HT1080: 31 ± 6% reduction  
- A375: 28 ± 4% reduction  
- SKOV3: 19 ± 3% reduction  
- Human monocytes: 45 ± 7% reduction  

Time-lapse imaging revealed RI-3 primarily affected the adhesion phase, reducing tumor cell attachment to endothelium by 72 ± 8% within the first 5 minutes.  

## EXAMPLE 10. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INHIBITS BOTH FBS AND VEGF-TRIGGERED ENDOTHELIAL TUBE FORMATION  

In matrigel tube formation assays, RI-3 (10 nM) inhibited:  
- FBS-induced angiogenesis: 61 ± 7% reduction  
- VEGF165-induced angiogenesis: 75 ± 8% reduction  

This anti-angiogenic effect occurred without endothelial cytotoxicity, as confirmed by unchanged MTS readings and lack of caspase-3 activation.  

## EXAMPLE 11. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 EXERTS ANTI-METASTATIC EFFECT REDUCING IN VIVO TUMOR GROWTH AND VASCULAR INFILTRATION BY SARCOMA CELLS  

In nude mouse xenograft models, daily intraperitoneal RI-3 (6 mg/kg) for 10 days:  
- Reduced Sarc tumor volume by 44 ± 12% (185 ± 82 mm3 vs 330 ± 163 mm3 controls)  
- Decreased microvessel density by 62 ± 8% (CD31+ vessels/field)  
- Lowered circulating tumor cells from 10.04 ± 3.7 to 2.6 ± 2.7 CTCs/mL  
- Prevented lung metastasis in 100% of treated animals vs 60% of controls  

Notably, RI-3 treatment caused no weight loss or other observable toxicity, and tumors from treated mice showed no evidence of necrosis or increased apoptosis, consistent with a primarily anti-metastatic rather than cytotoxic mechanism.
