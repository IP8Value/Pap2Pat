# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to lignocellulosic composites (LCs) and methods for their production. More particularly, the invention pertains to environmentally friendly composite materials comprising a partially dissolved cellulose matrix reinforced with lignocellulosic materials, wherein the composites are formed using cold aqueous alkaline solvents. The invention further relates to processes for optimizing the mechanical properties of such composites through controlled reaction parameters including temperature, matrix concentration, solvent volume, and alkaline formulation.  

## BACKGROUND  

Lignocellulosic biomass, composed primarily of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, has long been recognized as a potential source for sustainable materials. However, the inherent resistance of lignocellulosic structures to breakdown has limited their widespread utilization. Traditional methods for processing lignocellulosic materials often involve harsh chemical treatments or energy-intensive mechanical processes, which can degrade material properties and generate environmentally harmful byproducts.  

Prior attempts to create all-cellulose composites (ACCs) have focused on complete dissolution of cellulose in cold alkaline solvents, followed by regeneration to form homogeneous materials. While these methods produce composites with desirable properties, they require high cellulose purity and extensive processing, making them costly and impractical for large-scale applications. Additionally, ACCs lack the structural diversity and reinforcement potential offered by incorporating other lignocellulosic components.  

There remains a need for cost-effective, environmentally benign composite materials that leverage the structural advantages of lignocellulosic reinforcements while maintaining favorable mechanical properties. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel composite material and an efficient method for its production.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a lignocellulosic composite comprising:  
- A partially dissolved cellulose matrix constituting 15% to 35% of the composite by weight; and  
- Lignocellulosic reinforcement materials constituting 65% to 85% of the composite by weight, selected from the group consisting of osage orange wood particles, agave fibers, and mixtures thereof.  

The composite is produced by:  
1. Partially dissolving cellulose material in a cold aqueous alkaline solvent to form a viscous gel matrix;  
2. Mixing the gel matrix with lignocellulosic reinforcement materials;  
3. Forming the mixture into a desired shape; and  
4. Removing the alkaline solvent through washing and drying.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Utilization of abundant, low-cost biomass materials without requiring chemical pretreatment;  
- Elimination of synthetic adhesives or resins, resulting in fully biodegradable composites;  
- Tunable mechanical properties through control of processing parameters; and  
- Lightweight yet strong material characteristics comparable to conventional composites.  

The invention further provides optimized processing conditions, including preferred reaction temperatures (-10°C to -15°C), matrix concentrations (25-35% cellulose), and solvent formulations (4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea), which yield composites with superior mechanical properties.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention involves the production of novel lignocellulosic composites through controlled partial dissolution of cellulose in cold alkaline solvents, followed by combination with lignocellulosic reinforcement materials. The detailed process and material characteristics are described below.  

### Raw Materials  

The cellulose matrix is preferably derived from cotton due to its high cellulose content (approximately 96%) and availability. Alternative cellulose sources include other plant fibers with high cellulose purity.  

The lignocellulosic reinforcement materials are selected for their structural properties and compatibility with the cellulose matrix. Preferred materials include:  
- Osage orange wood (OOW) particles, providing small particle size for effective matrix binding;  
- Agave fibers (AF), offering long fiber reinforcement; and  
- Mixtures of OOW and AF, combining the advantages of both materials.  

These materials are used without chemical pretreatment, significantly reducing processing costs and environmental impact.  

### Alkaline Solvent System  

The cold aqueous alkaline solvent system comprises:  
- An alkaline component selected from LiOH·H₂O, NaOH, or KOH;  
- A urea component; and  
- Water.  

The preferred formulation contains 4.6% LiOH·H₂O and 15% urea in water, cooled to temperatures between -10°C and -15°C. This formulation has been found to optimally balance cellulose dissolution with preservation of matrix binding properties.  

### Composite Formation Process  

1. **Matrix Preparation**: Cellulose material (e.g., cotton) is immersed in the precooled alkaline solvent and stirred to achieve partial dissolution, forming a viscous, adhesive gel. Complete dissolution is avoided to maintain matrix integrity.  

2. **Reinforcement Incorporation**: Lignocellulosic reinforcement materials are added to the gel matrix and thoroughly mixed to ensure uniform distribution.  

3. **Molding**: The composite mixture is transferred to molds of desired shape and compressed to remove excess solvent and air pockets.  

4. **Solvent Removal**: The molded composite undergoes repeated water exchanges to remove alkaline solvent, followed by drying under controlled temperature and pressure conditions.  

5. **Densification**: Optional hot pressing may be applied to enhance mechanical properties and dimensional stability.  

### Process Optimization  

The mechanical properties of the resulting composites can be optimized by controlling key process parameters:  

- **Temperature**: Optimal reaction temperatures range from -12.5°C to -15°C, ensuring sufficient cellulose dissolution while maintaining matrix viscosity.  
- **Matrix Concentration**: Composites containing 25-35% cellulose matrix exhibit the best balance of mechanical properties and material costs.  
- **Solvent Volume**: Approximately 60 ml of solvent per 10 g of composite materials provides ideal dissolution without excessive dilution.  
- **Solvent Formulation**: LiOH·H₂O/urea systems outperform NaOH or KOH-based solvents in producing composites with superior mechanical properties.  

### Material Properties  

The resulting lignocellulosic composites exhibit:  
- High strength-to-weight ratios;  
- Complete biodegradability;  
- Thermal stability up to approximately 200°C; and  
- Mechanical properties comparable to conventional plastic composites.  

The composites are particularly suitable for applications requiring sustainable, lightweight materials with moderate mechanical demands, such as packaging, automotive components, and construction materials.  

### Examples  

#### Example 1: Temperature Optimization  

LCs were prepared using 25% cotton matrix and 75% OOW reinforcement with 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent at various temperatures (-5°C to -15°C). Composites produced at -12.5°C exhibited ultimate tensile strength (σu), elastic modulus (E), flexural strength (σfm), and bending modulus (Eb) values that were 108%, 151%, 183%, and 203% higher, respectively, than those prepared at -5°C.  

#### Example 2: Matrix Concentration  

LCs containing 15-35% cotton matrix with OOW/AF reinforcement were prepared. Composites with 35% cotton matrix showed σu, E, σfm, and Eb values 135%, 343%, 153%, and 109% higher, respectively, than those with 15% cotton matrix.  

#### Example 3: Solvent Volume  

Using 25% cotton and 75% OOW/AF, LCs prepared with 60 ml solvent/panel demonstrated σu, E, σfm, and Eb values 58%, 45%, 55%, and 58% higher, respectively, than those prepared with 105 ml/panel.  

### Exemplary Embodiments  

1. A lignocellulosic composite comprising:  
   - 25% by weight partially dissolved cotton cellulose matrix; and  
   - 75% by weight reinforcement comprising equal parts OOW particles and AF;  
   wherein the composite is prepared using 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent at -12.5°C.  

2. The composite of embodiment 1, having:  
   - Ultimate tensile strength ≥ 20 MPa;  
   - Elastic modulus ≥ 1.5 GPa;  
   - Flexural strength ≥ 30 MPa; and  
   - Bending modulus ≥ 2.0 GPa.  

3. A method for producing a lignocellulosic composite comprising:  
   a) Preparing a cold alkaline solvent containing 4.6% LiOH·H₂O and 15% urea;  
   b) Cooling the solvent to -12.5°C;  
   c) Partially dissolving cotton cellulose in the solvent to form a gel matrix;  
   d) Mixing the gel with OOW and AF reinforcement materials;  
   e) Molding the mixture;  
   f) Removing solvent through water exchange; and  
   g) Drying the composite under vacuum at 60°C.  

The invention thus provides novel, sustainable composite materials with tunable properties and an efficient, environmentally benign production method.
