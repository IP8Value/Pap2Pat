# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of electrochemical sensing, specifically to a novel ion-selective electrode (ISE) incorporating a metal-organic supercontainer (MOSC) as an ionophore for the size-selective potentiometric detection of molecular ions. The invention particularly discloses a mixed-matrix membrane (MMM) composition comprising the MOSC molecule designated as 1-Co, which exhibits high selectivity for large molecular cations such as methylene blue (MB+) and tetrabutylammonium (TBA+). The invention further encompasses methods for fabricating conventional and solid-contact ISEs (SC-ISEs) using the MMM, as well as applications in environmental monitoring, wastewater analysis, and biomedical sensing.  

## BACKGROUND  

Ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) are widely used in analytical chemistry for the detection of ionic species in solution. Traditional ISEs rely on ionophores that selectively bind target ions, generating a measurable potential difference. However, conventional ionophores, such as valinomycin or crown ethers, are limited in their ability to discriminate large molecular ions due to insufficient cavity sizes or lack of tailored binding domains.  

Calixarenes and related macrocyclic compounds have been explored as ionophores, but their recognition capabilities are often restricted to small metal ions (e.g., K+, Ag+, NH4+) due to structural constraints. Additionally, leaching of ionophores or sensing molecules from the membrane matrix can compromise sensor stability and longevity.  

There remains an unmet need for ionophores capable of selectively binding large molecular ions while maintaining stability in the membrane phase. The present invention addresses this gap by utilizing a MOSC molecule (1-Co) with precisely defined endo- and exo-cavities that enable size-selective recognition of bulky cations such as MB+ and TBA+. The invention further provides a robust MMM formulation that minimizes leaching and enhances sensor performance.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The invention provides a mixed-matrix membrane (MMM) composition for ion-selective electrodes, comprising:  
1. A polymeric matrix (e.g., polyvinyl chloride (PVC));  
2. A plasticizer (e.g., bis(2-ethylhexyl)sebacate (DOS));  
3. A lipophilic salt (e.g., potassium tetrakis(4-chlorophenyl) borate (KTpClPB));  
4. The MOSC ionophore 1-Co, which features an edge-directed octahedral geometry with one endo-cavity (Ø ~1.7 nm) and six exo-cavities (Ø ~0.74 nm); and  
5. A cationic dye (e.g., methylene blue (MB+)) for charge equilibration.  

The MMM exhibits near-Nernstian responses to MB+ (56.8 ± 2.5 mV/p[MB+]) and TBA+ (60.0 ± 1.5 mV/p[TBA+]) with detection limits of ~300 nM and ~1 μM, respectively. The MOSC’s cavities are dimensionally matched to MB+ (length: 1.6 nm, width: 0.7 nm), enabling selective binding via cation-π interactions. Smaller ions (K+, Ag+, NH4+) elicit minimal responses due to poor cavity fit.  

The invention further discloses methods for fabricating:  
- Conventional ISEs by dip-coating pipette tips with the MMM;  
- Solid-contact ISEs (SC-ISEs) by drop-casting the MMM onto pyrolyzed SU8-derived carbon electrodes.  

Applications include environmental monitoring (e.g., detecting MB+ in wastewater) and integration into microelectronic sensors. The MMM’s stability is demonstrated by leaching tests, showing no MB+ loss when 1-Co is present.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention is described in detail below with reference to specific embodiments and experimental data.  

### EXAMPLE 1: PROOF-OF-CONCEPT STUDY WITH MB+ AND TBA+  

The MOSC ionophore 1-Co was synthesized as previously reported and incorporated into a PVC-based MMM with DOS, KTpClPB, and MB+. Impedance spectroscopy revealed a 50% reduction in charge-transfer resistance for the 1-Co MMM compared to a control MMM without 1-Co, indicating enhanced ion transfer at the membrane-analyte interface.  

Potentiometric measurements using a conventional ISE showed near-Nernstian responses to MB+ (56.8 mV/decade) and TBA+ (60.0 mV/decade), with detection limits of 300 nM and 1 μM, respectively. The response times were <1 s for concentrations >30 μM and 50–100 s for lower concentrations. The selectivity arises from the dimensional match between 1-Co’s cavities and the target ions: MB+ fits the endo-cavity lengthwise (1.6 nm vs. 1.7 nm) and the exo-cavity widthwise (0.7 nm vs. 0.74 nm), while TBA+ (Ø ~0.8 nm) binds preferentially to the exo-cavities. In contrast, smaller ions (K+, Ag+, NH4+) elicited negligible responses (<10 mV/decade).  

### EXAMPLE 2: LEECHING STUDY  

To assess membrane stability, two MMMs—with and without 1-Co—were immersed in deionized water for one week. The 1-Co MMM retained its blue color, indicating no MB+ leaching, while the control MMM released MB+ into the solution (visible by solution coloration). This confirms that 1-Co immobilizes MB+ within the membrane, enhancing sensor longevity.  

### EXAMPLE 3: SIMULATED WASTE WATER SAMPLE  

A simulated wastewater sample was prepared using Fyris River water spiked with MB+. The 1-Co ISE maintained a near-Nernstian response, albeit with a shifted detection limit due to partial MB+ reduction by microorganisms in the river water. UV-vis spectroscopy confirmed lower MB+ concentrations in river water versus DI water, validating the ISE’s utility in complex matrices.  

### EXAMPLE 4: MOLECULAR ION SENSING  

The 1-Co SC-ISE was fabricated by drop-casting the MMM onto pyrolyzed SU8-carbon. The SC-ISE exhibited identical performance to the conventional ISE, demonstrating compatibility with miniaturized, solid-state sensors. Control experiments confirmed that the potentiometric response originated from 1-Co’s ionophoric properties, as plain carbon electrodes or MMMs lacking 1-Co showed no response to MB+.
