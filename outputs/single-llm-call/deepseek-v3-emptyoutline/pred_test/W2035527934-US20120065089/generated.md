# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  
The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and compositions for detecting intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) and distinguishing it from other hepatic conditions. More particularly, the invention provides a novel diagnostic system utilizing Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) and monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 for the specific detection of ICC-associated glycoproteins in biological samples, particularly bile specimens. The invention encompasses diagnostic assays, kits, and methods for early and accurate diagnosis of ICC using glycan profiling and immunoassay techniques.

## BACKGROUND ART  

### Prior Art Documents  
Current diagnostic methods for cholangiocarcinoma (CC) suffer from poor sensitivity and specificity. Conventional markers such as CA19-9 demonstrate limited diagnostic accuracy, with reported sensitivity of approximately 62% and specificity of 51%. Biliary cytology, while routinely used, shows only about 55% sensitivity. Previous attempts to develop glycoprotein-based markers for CC have focused on serum analysis, which lacks the direct proximity to tumor sites that bile specimens provide. Prior immunohistochemical methods using cytokeratin families and epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) can distinguish ICC from hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) but require complex multi-marker approaches.  

Existing diagnostic systems fail to adequately address the critical need for early and accurate ICC detection, particularly in distinguishing ICC from HCC in combined HCC-ICC cases. There remains an unmet need for a simple, highly sensitive diagnostic method that can reliably detect ICC at early stages using readily accessible biological samples.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  
The primary object of the present invention is to provide a highly sensitive and specific diagnostic method for ICC that overcomes the limitations of current diagnostic approaches. The invention specifically aims to:  
1) Provide a reliable method for distinguishing ICC from normal bile duct epithelium (BDE) and HCC;  
2) Enable early detection of ICC through analysis of bile specimens;  
3) Develop a diagnostic system with superior sensitivity and specificity compared to existing methods;  
4) Provide a simple diagnostic tool that can be implemented in clinical settings without requiring complex instrumentation.

### Means to Solve the Object  
The invention achieves these objects through a novel diagnostic system based on the following discoveries:  
1) Identification of WFA as a highly specific lectin for detecting ICC-associated glycans through comprehensive glycan profiling of tissue sections;  
2) Demonstration that sialylated MUC1 carries WFA-reactive glycans specific to ICC;  
3) Development of a sandwich ELISA system combining WFA for capture and monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 for detection of ICC-specific glycoproteins in bile specimens.  

The diagnostic method comprises:  
a) Obtaining a biological sample from a subject, preferably bile;  
b) Contacting the sample with WFA to bind ICC-specific glycoproteins;  
c) Detecting bound glycoproteins using MY.1E12 antibody;  
d) Quantifying the signal to determine presence or likelihood of ICC.

### Effect of the Invention  
The invention provides significant advantages over existing diagnostic methods:  
1) Superior sensitivity (90%) and specificity (76%) compared to biliary cytology (55% sensitivity) and CA19-9 testing (62% sensitivity);  
2) Ability to distinguish ICC from HCC with 100% accuracy in tested specimens;  
3) Early detection capability through analysis of bile specimens containing higher concentrations of tumor markers than serum;  
4) Simple implementation as a standardized ELISA test suitable for clinical laboratories;  
5) Potential for adaptation to other biological samples including serum through further development.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  
The invention may be practiced through various embodiments, with the following representing preferred implementations:  

The diagnostic system utilizes a sandwich ELISA format where WFA is immobilized on a solid support to capture ICC-specific glycoproteins from bile samples. Following capture, bound glycoproteins are detected using monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which recognizes sialylated MUC1 carrying WFA-reactive glycans. Signal generation employs standard ELISA detection systems such as horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies with colorimetric substrates.  

For tissue-based diagnosis, the invention provides immunohistochemical staining methods using biotinylated WFA to visualize ICC-specific glycans in tissue sections. This enables histological distinction of ICC from HCC and normal BDE.  

The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits containing:  
1) WFA immobilized on microtiter plates or other solid supports;  
2) Detection antibody MY.1E12;  
3) Necessary buffers and reagents for sample processing;  
4) Detection reagents including enzyme conjugates and substrates;  
5) Control samples for assay standardization.  

## EXAMPLE 1  
### Lectin Microarray Analysis of ICC Tissue Sections  
Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded ICC tissue sections were deparaffinized and cancerous lesions along with normal BDE were microdissected. Total protein extracts were labeled with Cy3 fluorescent dye and analyzed using a lectin microarray containing 45 different lectins. Comparative analysis revealed WFA as the most significant lectin differentiating ICC from normal BDE, showing a tumor-to-normal ratio of 4.6 (P<0.0001). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis demonstrated WFA's superior diagnostic performance with 87.4% sensitivity, 92.1% specificity, and area under the curve (AUC) of 0.93.

## EXAMPLE 2  
### Histochemical Validation Using WFA Staining  
Biotinylated WFA was used to stain 90 ICC sections, 25 normal BDE sections, 25 HCC sections, and 10 HCC-ICC sections. Strong WFA staining was observed in 92.2% of ICC cases and 80% of ICC elements in HCC-ICC, while normal BDE showed only 32% weak staining. Notably, HCC sections showed no WFA reactivity (0%), demonstrating perfect discrimination between ICC and HCC.

## EXAMPLE 3  
### Colocalization Studies with MY.1E12  
Fluorescence double-staining of ICC tissue sections with WFA and MY.1E12 showed complete colocalization at the apical surface of cancerous epithelial cells, confirming that sialylated MUC1 carries WFA-reactive glycans in ICC. This finding provided the basis for developing the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich detection system.

## EXAMPLE 4  
### WFA Affinity Purification from Bile Samples  
Bile samples from CC patients and controls were subjected to WFA affinity purification followed by Western blot analysis with MY.1E12. While sialylated MUC1 was present in crude bile from both groups, WFA-enriched fractions showed strong MY.1E12 reactivity only in CC samples, confirming the specificity of WFA-reactive sialylated MUC1 for CC.

## EXAMPLE 5  
### Development of WFA-MY.1E12 Sandwich ELISA  
A sandwich ELISA was developed with immobilized WFA capturing glycoproteins from bile samples, followed by detection with MY.1E12. Testing 30 CC and 38 benign disease samples showed 90% sensitivity and 76% specificity at optimal cutoff (AUC=0.86), significantly outperforming conventional MY.1E12-MY.1E12 ELISA (56.7% sensitivity, AUC=0.75).

## EXAMPLE 6  
### Comparison with Biliary Cytology  
The WFA-MY.1E12 ELISA was compared with biliary cytology in 18 cases. While cytology showed only 55.6% sensitivity, the ELISA correctly identified 90% of CC cases at the established cutoff, demonstrating superior diagnostic performance for early ICC detection.

## EXAMPLE 7  
### Discrimination of HCC-ICC Cases  
The WFA-based staining method was applied to 10 HCC-ICC cases, correctly identifying all ICC components (100% accuracy) while showing no reactivity with HCC elements. This perfect discrimination has significant clinical implications for treatment selection.

## EXAMPLE 8  
### Assay Optimization and Standardization  
Further optimization established optimal assay conditions:  
- WFA coating concentration: 1 μg/well  
- Sample incubation: 2 hours at room temperature  
- MY.1E12 detection antibody: 50 ng/well  
- Signal development: HRP-TMB system  
- Cutoff value: S/N ratio of 6.64  
Inter- and intra-assay variability was <10%, demonstrating robust reproducibility for clinical implementation.
