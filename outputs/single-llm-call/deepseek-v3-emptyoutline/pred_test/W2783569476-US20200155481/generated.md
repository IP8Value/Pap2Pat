# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for preventing malaria infections. More specifically, the invention pertains to long-acting injectable nanoformulations of atovaquone for sustained chemoprophylaxis against Plasmodium species, particularly Plasmodium falciparum and Plasmodium berghei. The nanoformulations provide prolonged release of atovaquone from an intramuscular depot, achieving therapeutic plasma concentrations capable of preventing liver-stage parasite development for extended durations following a single administration.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Malaria remains a significant global health burden, with Plasmodium falciparum being the most lethal species responsible for hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. Current malaria prevention strategies rely heavily on chemoprophylaxis using orally administered drugs such as mefloquine, doxycycline, and atovaquone-proguanil combinations. However, these regimens suffer from poor patient adherence due to the requirement for frequent dosing, often leading to suboptimal protection.  

Atovaquone, a hydroxynaphthoquinone compound, exhibits potent activity against both liver-stage (causal prophylaxis) and blood-stage (suppressive prophylaxis) malaria parasites by selectively inhibiting mitochondrial electron transport through binding to cytochrome b. While oral atovaquone formulations have demonstrated efficacy, their utility is limited by pharmacokinetic properties requiring daily dosing and vulnerability to non-adherence.  

Recent advances in long-acting injectable (LAI) nanomedicines have shown promise for sustained drug delivery, particularly in antiretroviral therapy. However, the application of this technology to antimalarial prophylaxis has not been previously explored. The development of a long-acting atovaquone formulation would address critical unmet needs in malaria prevention by combining the established efficacy of atovaquone with the adherence advantages of extended-release delivery systems.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel solid drug nanoparticle (SDN) formulations of atovaquone suitable for intramuscular administration and capable of sustaining therapeutic plasma concentrations for malaria prophylaxis over extended periods. The formulations comprise atovaquone nanoparticles stabilized with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, including polymers and surfactants, prepared through emulsion-templated freeze-drying (ETFD) methods.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1) High drug loading (up to 80% by weight) minimizing injection volume  
2) Sustained release properties providing plasma concentrations above the prophylactic threshold (200 ng/mL) for at least four weeks  
3) Causal prophylactic activity targeting liver-stage parasites  
4) Favorable safety profile using FDA-approved excipients  
5) Dose-dependent duration of protection  

The formulations demonstrate complete protection against Plasmodium berghei sporozoite challenge in murine models for up to four weeks post-administration, with pharmacokinetic data suggesting potentially longer durations in humans. The invention represents a significant advancement in malaria prevention by addressing the critical limitation of adherence associated with current oral regimens.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising atovaquone nanoparticles and methods for their use in malaria prophylaxis. The formulations are characterized by their unique physical properties, sustained release kinetics, and exceptional efficacy in preclinical models.  

The nanoparticle formulations are prepared using emulsion-templated freeze-drying (ETFD) techniques that produce solid drug nanoparticles stabilized by carefully selected excipient combinations. The process involves creating an oil-in-water emulsion containing atovaquone in the oil phase and stabilizing excipients in the aqueous phase, followed by rapid freezing and lyophilization to yield a porous monolith that can be readily dispersed in aqueous media.  

Critical formulation parameters include:  
- Drug loading between 20-80% by weight  
- Polymer stabilizers selected from PVA, HPMC, PVP, F127, F68, or Kollicoat  
- Surfactants selected from TPGS, Tween 20, Tween 80, NDC, AOT, or Solutol  
- Particle size <1000 nm upon aqueous dispersion  
- Polydispersity index <0.4  

The preferred formulations (ATQSDN7 and ATQSDN8) demonstrate optimal release kinetics, maintaining plasma concentrations above the 200 ng/mL threshold for at least 28 days in murine models. Pharmacokinetic analysis reveals a plasma half-life of approximately 105 hours following intramuscular administration, significantly longer than the 9-hour half-life observed with oral dosing.  

The mechanism of action involves sustained delivery of atovaquone to maintain concentrations sufficient to inhibit parasite cytochrome b in hepatocytes, thereby preventing liver-stage development following sporozoite inoculation. Notably, the formulations show predominantly causal rather than suppressive activity, with efficacy against sporozoite but not blood-stage challenges at equivalent plasma concentrations.  

### Examples  

#### Example 1: Preparation of Nanoparticle Formulations  

Candidate formulations were prepared by emulsifying an 80 mg/mL chloroform solution of atovaquone with aqueous solutions of polymer and surfactant stabilizers in a 1:4 oil:water ratio. The emulsion was formed using high-intensity ultrasound (Covaris S2x) for 30 seconds, immediately frozen cryogenically, and lyophilized for 42 hours. The resulting porous monoliths contained 80% atovaquone, 13% polymer, and 7% surfactant by weight.  

Forty-two formulations were prepared using combinations of seven polymers (PVA, HPMC, PVP, F127, F68, Kollicoat, PEG 1k) and six surfactants (TPGS, Tween 20, Tween 80, NDC, AOT, Solutol). Physical characterization by dynamic light scattering identified eleven candidates meeting selection criteria (complete aqueous dispersion, Z-average diameter <1000 nm, polydispersity index <0.4).  

#### Example 2: In Vitro Release Kinetics  

The eleven candidate formulations were evaluated using a rapid equilibrium dialysis model simulating intramuscular depot conditions. Formulations were dispersed in simulated interstitial fluid (35 mg/mL BSA, 2 mg/mL γ-globulin) at 1 mg/mL atovaquone concentration and placed in 8 kDa MWCO dialysis devices.  

ATQSDN7 (containing HPMC and Tween 80) demonstrated intermediate release kinetics with approximately 30% cumulative release over 6 hours, while ATQSDN8 (containing PVP and NDC) showed slightly faster release. Both formulations were selected for in vivo evaluation based on favorable release profiles and physical stability.  

#### Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy Studies  

C57BL/6 mice received single intramuscular injections of ATQSDN7 at doses of 36, 100, or 200 mg/kg and were challenged with 5000 P. berghei sporozoites at various intervals post-dosing. Complete protection was observed for:  
- 200 mg/kg dose: up to 28 days post-administration  
- 100 mg/kg dose: up to 21 days post-administration  
- 36 mg/kg dose: up to 14 days post-administration  

All protected animals remained parasite-free through 42 days of follow-up monitoring. Plasma atovaquone concentrations at time of challenge correlated strongly with efficacy, with concentrations >200 ng/mL associated with 100% protection.  

### Materials and Methods  

#### Nanoparticle Preparation  

Materials: Atovaquone (WIS Pharmtech), chloroform (Fisher Scientific), polymers and surfactants as listed (Sigma-Aldrich, BASF, Fluka). [3H]-atovaquone was obtained from RC Tritec for radiolabeled studies.  

Emulsion Formation: Stock solutions (80 mg/mL atovaquone in chloroform; 22.5 mg/mL polymer and surfactant in water) were combined in 100:63.7:31.5:304.8 ratio (atovaquone:polymer:surfactant:water). Emulsification was performed using a Covaris S2x (duty cycle 20, intensity 10, 500 cycles/burst) for 30 sec followed by immediate freezing.  

Lyophilization: Frozen emulsions were lyophilized (Virtis benchtop K) for 42 hours to yield dry porous monoliths.  

#### In Vivo Studies  

Animal Model: 6-week-old male C57BL/6 mice (Jackson Laboratory) maintained under IACUC-approved protocols.  

Dosing: Nanoparticles reconstituted in sterile water and administered intramuscularly (biceps femoris, 20 μL per limb) using 27-gauge needles.  

Challenge: P. berghei ANKA sporozoites (5000/mouse) or infected erythrocytes (1.5×105/mouse) administered intravenously at specified intervals.  

Monitoring: Blood smears examined weekly for 42 days post-challenge; protection defined as absence of detectable parasitemia (<0.05% sensitivity).  

#### Analytical Methods  

Particle Characterization: Z-average diameter and polydispersity by dynamic light scattering (Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS).  

Plasma Analysis: Atovaquone quantitation by UPLC-MS/MS (API 4000, SCIEX) with deuterated internal standard, validated range 5-5000 ng/mL.  

Statistical Analysis: Pharmacokinetic parameters determined by non-compartmental analysis (Phoenix WinNonlin); efficacy comparisons by Fisher's exact test (Stata 14.0).
