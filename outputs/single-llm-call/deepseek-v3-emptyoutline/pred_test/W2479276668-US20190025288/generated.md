# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  
The present invention relates to the field of oligonucleotide therapeutics and methods for predicting hepatotoxicity of single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs). More specifically, the invention provides an in vitro screening assay utilizing primary mammalian hepatocyte cells to assess the hepatotoxic potential of SSOs prior to in vivo testing. The assay enables early identification of oligonucleotide sequences with reduced hepatotoxicity risk, thereby improving the safety profile of SSO-based therapeutics.  

## BACKGROUND  
Single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) represent a promising class of therapeutic agents capable of modulating gene expression through Watson-Crick hybridization to target RNA. Despite decades of development, clinical success has been limited due to challenges including insufficient stability, delivery issues, and drug-induced toxicities such as hepatotoxicity and pro-inflammatory effects. These toxicities occur in a concentration-dependent manner but cannot be reliably predicted by current in vitro models.  

Traditional preclinical toxicity assessment relies heavily on rodent in vivo studies, which are resource-intensive, ethically challenging, and often fail to predict human hepatotoxicity. While certain sequence motifs have been associated with hepatotoxicity risk, no in vitro assay has successfully recapitulated the in vivo hepatotoxic profile of SSOs. There remains an unmet need for predictive in vitro models that can identify hepatotoxic SSOs early in development and reduce reliance on animal testing.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides a novel in vitro assay system for predicting SSO-induced hepatotoxicity using primary mammalian hepatocytes. The assay successfully recapitulates in vivo hepatotoxicity profiles by: (1) utilizing species-relevant primary hepatocytes capable of gymnotic (transfection reagent-free) SSO uptake; (2) measuring biomarkers with in vivo comparators including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), adenosine triphosphate (ATP), glutathione (GSH), caspase-3/7 activity, interleukin-1α (IL-1α), macrophage inflammatory protein-1α (MIP-1α), and microRNA-122 (miR-122); and (3) employing culture conditions that maintain hepatocellular function.  

The assay demonstrates excellent correlation between in vitro hepatotoxicity measurements and known in vivo hepatotoxicity profiles across a panel of SSOs. It enables identification of hepatotoxic SSOs based on characteristic patterns including increased LDH release, reduced ATP and GSH levels, elevated caspase-3/7 activity, and increased secretion of IL-1α, MIP-1α and miR-122. The invention further provides methods for assessing hepatotoxicity in both rodent and human primary hepatocytes, facilitating species translation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Prediction of In Vivo Toxicity  
The invention provides methods for predicting in vivo hepatotoxicity of SSOs by culturing primary mammalian hepatocytes with test oligonucleotides under gymnotic conditions and measuring hepatocellular damage markers. The assay demonstrates that toxic SSOs induce characteristic biomarker patterns including: increased LDH release (indicating cell membrane damage), reduced intracellular ATP (indicating metabolic dysfunction), decreased GSH (indicating oxidative stress), elevated caspase-3/7 activity (indicating apoptosis), and increased secretion of IL-1α, MIP-1α and miR-122 (indicating inflammatory response and hepatocellular damage). These patterns correlate strongly with known in vivo hepatotoxicity.  

### Primary Mammalian Hepatocyte Cells  
The assay utilizes freshly isolated or cryopreserved primary hepatocytes from relevant species, preferably mouse or human. Primary hepatocytes maintain differentiated functions including albumin secretion, drug metabolism, and gymnotic SSO uptake pathways similar to in vivo hepatocytes. The invention demonstrates that immortalized cell lines (e.g., HepG2) fail to recapitulate SSO-induced hepatotoxicity, highlighting the critical importance of using primary hepatocytes.  

### Administering the Oligonucleotide—Gymnotic Delivery (Gymnosis)  
SSOs are delivered to hepatocytes without transfection reagents or carrier molecules (gymnotic delivery) to mimic physiological uptake pathways. Oligonucleotides are dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and added directly to serum-free culture medium at concentrations ranging from 1-30 μM. This delivery method ensures that observed toxicities result from intrinsic SSO properties rather than delivery vehicle effects.  

### Control Data/Reference Values  
The assay includes appropriate controls comprising: (1) vehicle-treated hepatocytes (negative control); (2) hepatocytes treated with SSOs of known in vivo safety profile (reference controls); and (3) hepatocytes treated with SSOs of known hepatotoxicity (positive controls). Reference values for biomarker levels in control treatments establish baseline ranges for interpreting test SSO effects.  

### (Determination of) Hepatotoxicity  
Hepatotoxicity is determined by comparing biomarker levels in test SSO-treated hepatocytes to control values. Significant increases in LDH (≥2-fold), caspase-3/7 activity (≥3-fold), IL-1α (≥2-fold), MIP-1α (≥2-fold), and miR-122 (≥4-fold), coupled with significant decreases in ATP (≤50% of control) and GSH (≤70% of control) indicate hepatotoxic potential. The invention provides specific threshold values for each biomarker that differentiate toxic from non-toxic SSOs.  

### Parent Oligonucleotide  
The methods can be applied to evaluate parent oligonucleotide sequences comprising antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), gapmers, or other SSO designs. The parent oligonucleotide serves as a reference for comparing toxicity of modified variants.  

### Library of Variants  
The assay enables screening of SSO variant libraries to identify sequences with reduced hepatotoxicity while maintaining target engagement. Variants may include modifications to sugar chemistry, backbone linkages, sequence composition, or stereochemistry.  

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotide Embodiments:  
The invention encompasses evaluation of stereodefined oligonucleotide embodiments where specific phosphorothioate linkages are in Rp or Sp configuration. Stereodefined SSOs may exhibit differential hepatotoxicity profiles that can be characterized using the disclosed assay.  

### Primary Hepatocytes Assay Example:  
An exemplary assay involves plating primary mouse hepatocytes at 0.25×10^5 cells/well in collagen-coated 96-well plates. After 3-4 hours attachment, cells are treated with SSOs (1-30 μM) in serum-free medium for 3 days. Supernatants are collected for LDH, cytokine, and miR-122 measurements, while cells are lysed for ATP, GSH, and caspase-3/7 assays.  

## Definitions  

### Oligonucleotide  
As used herein, "oligonucleotide" refers to a single-stranded polymer of linked nucleosides, typically comprising 8-50 nucleoside units, including modified nucleosides and non-natural linkages.  

### Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs)  
ASOs are oligonucleotides designed to hybridize to complementary target RNA through Watson-Crick base pairing, typically to modulate RNA processing, stability, or translation.  

### Modified Internucleoside Linkages  
Modified linkages include phosphorothioate, phosphorodithioate, methylphosphonate, and other non-phosphodiester linkages between nucleosides that may influence oligonucleotide properties.  

### Modulation of Expression  
Refers to alteration of target gene expression levels, including reduction (knockdown) or enhancement, through oligonucleotide-mediated mechanisms such as RNase H recruitment, splicing modulation, or translation inhibition.  

### Modified Oligonucleotides  
Oligonucleotides containing chemical modifications to the sugar (e.g., 2'-O-methyl, 2'-fluoro, LNA), base, or backbone components that confer improved properties such as nuclease resistance, binding affinity, or pharmacokinetics.  

### RNase H Activity and Recruitment  
The ability of oligonucleotides to recruit RNase H enzyme to cleave target RNA in an oligonucleotide:RNA heteroduplex, a mechanism employed by gapmer ASOs.  

### Efficacy  
The desired therapeutic effect of an oligonucleotide, typically measured as reduction in target RNA or protein levels.  

### Conjugate Moieties  
Chemical groups (e.g., GalNAc, cholesterol) attached to oligonucleotides to enhance delivery, targeting, or pharmacokinetic properties.  

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotides  
Oligonucleotides where the stereochemical configuration (Rp or Sp) of phosphorothioate linkages is controlled at specific positions along the sequence.  

## Immunotoxicity Biomarker Assay:  
The invention includes measurement of immunotoxicity biomarkers (e.g., IL-1α, MIP-1α) in hepatocyte-non-parenchymal cell (NPC) co-cultures. NPCs are isolated from liver perfusate and co-cultured with hepatocytes at a 1:1 ratio. SSO-induced cytokine secretion profiles in co-cultures provide additional immunotoxicity risk assessment.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
Primary mouse hepatocytes were treated with seven myd88-targeting SSOs of known in vivo hepatotoxicity profile. After 3 days, toxic SSOs (36,37,43,47) showed significant increases in LDH release (2.5-4.5 fold) and decreases in ATP (40-70% reduction) compared to safe SSOs (32,33,35). Caspase-3/7 activity increased 3-8 fold with toxic SSOs by 48 hours.  

### Example 2  
Hepatocyte-NPC co-cultures treated with toxic SSOs showed elevated MIP-1α (5-fold at 24h) and IL-1α (3-fold at 48h), while safe SSOs showed no cytokine induction. miR-122 increased 4-10 fold in supernatants from toxic SSO-treated cultures.  

### Example 3  
Human primary hepatocytes treated with clinical-stage hepatotoxic SSOs (survivin and Bcl2 target) showed dose-dependent increases in LDH (2-3 fold) and decreases in ATP (50-60% reduction), recapitulating known clinical hepatotoxicity.  

### Example 4  
A library of 34 SSOs was screened in mouse hepatocytes. The assay correctly predicted in vivo hepatotoxicity for 32/34 SSOs based on ATP and LDH measurements, demonstrating 94% concordance with in vivo data.  

### Example 5  
Stereodefined SSO variants were evaluated. Rp-rich configurations showed reduced caspase-3/7 activation (2-fold vs 5-fold) compared to Sp-rich counterparts at 30 μM, indicating stereochemistry influences hepatotoxicity.  

### Example 6  
SSO uptake and target engagement was confirmed by qPCR showing 50-90% myd88 mRNA reduction for all SSOs, demonstrating that toxicity differences were not due to uptake variability.  

### Example 7  
Time-course analysis revealed MIP-1α secretion preceded other toxicity markers (24h), followed by IL-1α (48h), then LDH/ATP changes (72h), providing kinetic insights into toxicity progression.  

### Example 8  
GSH levels decreased significantly (30-50%) with toxic SSOs by 72h, indicating oxidative stress contributes to hepatotoxicity. Safe SSOs maintained normal GSH levels.  

### Example 9  
The assay differentiated hepatotoxicity risk among chemically modified SSOs, with 2'-MOE gapmers showing lower toxicity than LNA gapmers at equivalent doses, enabling safer design choices.
