## BACKGROUND  

### 1. Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of neurobiology and medicine. More specifically, the invention pertains to monoclonal antibodies that specifically inhibit connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting gap junction function, and their use in treating spinal cord injuries (SCI). The invention further encompasses methods of using said antibodies to reduce secondary damage following SCI, preserve neuronal tissue, improve motor function recovery, and attenuate glial scar formation.  

### 2. Description of Related Art  

Spinal cord injury represents a significant medical challenge, with approximately 13,000 hospital admissions annually in the United States and between 250,000-500,000 cases worldwide. Following the initial mechanical trauma (primary injury), secondary damage occurs through processes including edema, ischemia, metabolic dysfunction, and spreading depolarizations that expand the area of spinal cord damage. This secondary injury creates a cytotoxic environment characterized by elevated extracellular glutamate, lactate, potassium ions, nitric oxide, arachidonate, reactive oxygen species, and ammonia - all factors known to contribute to increased lesion size.  

While most SCI patients retain some spinal cord function (incomplete injuries), current therapeutic strategies to promote axonal regeneration remain limited. Approaches that mitigate secondary damage in incomplete SCIs could preserve greater neurological function. Secondary injury also leads to glial scar formation and neuronal apoptosis, which impair axon regeneration and contribute to complications like neuropathic pain and spasticity.  

Connexin 43 is abundantly expressed in spinal cord astrocytes, forming both gap junctions (which connect adjacent cells) and hemichannels (which open to extracellular space). Previous studies have shown Cx43 upregulation after SCI in rodents, with astrocyte-specific Cx43 deficiency reducing acute inflammation and traumatic lesions. While antisense oligodeoxynucleotide suppression of Cx43 improved functional recovery, these approaches did not distinguish between Cx43's roles in gap junctions versus hemichannels. The specific contribution of Cx43 hemichannels to post-SCI damage remained unclear until the development of the present invention.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a monoclonal antibody designated MHC1 that specifically binds to and inhibits the opening of Cx43 hemichannels without affecting gap junction function. This antibody represents a novel therapeutic approach for treating spinal cord injuries by targeting a key mechanism of secondary damage.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
- A monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to an extracellular domain of Cx43  
- The antibody's selective inhibition of Cx43 hemichannels while preserving gap junction communication  
- Pharmaceutical compositions containing the antibody  
- Methods of treating spinal cord injury by administering the antibody, particularly within 30 minutes post-injury  
- Use of the antibody to preserve white and gray matter, reduce lesion size, attenuate gliosis, and improve motor function recovery  
- Diagnostic kits containing the antibody for assessing Cx43 hemichannel activity  

The antibody demonstrates high binding affinity for Cx43 (EC50 = 7.63 × 10^-2 nM by ELISA; KD = 421 nM by bio-layer interferometry) and effectively inhibits hemichannel opening induced by low extracellular calcium/magnesium, mechanical stress, and inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β. When administered acutely after SCI, the antibody:  
- Reduces Evans blue dye uptake (marker of hemichannel activity) at injury sites  
- Improves hindlimb motor function as measured by Basso Mouse Scale scores  
- Preserves neuronal markers (MAP2) in perilesional areas  
- Attenuates reactive astrogliosis (GFAP expression)  
- Decreases overall lesion size  
- Increases sparing of white and gray matter  

Notably, these therapeutic effects are most pronounced when the antibody is administered within a critical window following injury (optimally within 30 minutes), corresponding to the period of blood-spinal cord barrier permeability and peak hemichannel activation.  

## DESCRIPTION  

### I. ANTIBODIES  

The invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to connexin 43 (Cx43) and inhibit hemichannel function without affecting gap junction communication. The preferred embodiment is the MHC1 antibody, a mouse-human chimeric monoclonal antibody generated through immunization with a Cx43 extracellular domain peptide.  

The MHC1 antibody comprises:  
- Mouse-derived variable regions that confer specificity for Cx43  
- Human constant regions (IgG1) that reduce immunogenicity for therapeutic use  

Key functional characteristics of MHC1 include:  
1. Specific binding to Cx43-expressing cells (e.g., HeLa-Cx43) with no binding to Cx43-negative parental HeLa cells  
2. High affinity binding to Cx43 extracellular domain peptides (EC50 = 7.63 × 10^-2 nM by ELISA)  
3. Selective inhibition of Cx43 hemichannels induced by:  
   - Low extracellular Ca2+/Mg2+ conditions  
   - Mechanical stress  
   - Inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β)  
4. No detectable effect on:  
   - Cx43 gap junction function (verified by scrape-loading dye transfer assays)  
   - Pannexin channel activity  

The antibody is produced by:  
1. Immunizing mice with Cx43 extracellular domain peptides  
2. Generating hybridomas from splenocytes  
3. Cloning variable region genes from selected hybridomas  
4. Constructing chimeric antibodies with human constant regions  
5. Expressing in HEK293F cells and purifying via protein A chromatography  

The invention encompasses:  
- The MHC1 antibody sequence  
- Antibodies binding the same epitope as MHC1  
- Humanized versions of MHC1  
- Antibody fragments (Fab, scFv) retaining Cx43 hemichannel inhibition  
- Pharmaceutical formulations containing these antibodies  

### II. TREATMENT OF DISEASES  

The MHC1 antibody and related compositions are useful for treating spinal cord injury, particularly during the acute phase following trauma. The therapeutic mechanism involves:  

1. Targeting Pathologically Open Hemichannels:  
   - SCI induces Cx43 hemichannel opening via mechanical stress and inflammatory mediators  
   - Open hemichannels release ATP, glutamate and other cytotoxic factors  
   - MHC1 binds extracellular Cx43 domains to stabilize hemichannels in closed state  

2. Preserving Spinal Cord Tissue:  
   - Reduces secondary expansion of lesion size  
   - Increases sparing of white and gray matter  
   - Protects neurons from apoptotic death  

3. Improving Functional Outcomes:  
   - Attenuates reactive astrogliosis and glial scar formation  
   - Enhances recovery of motor function (BMS scores)  
   - May reduce neuropathic pain complications  

Optimal treatment involves:  
- Intravenous or intraperitoneal administration  
- Dosing between 10-50 mg/kg (preferably 25 mg/kg)  
- Administration within 30 minutes post-injury  
- Single dose sufficient for therapeutic effect  

The invention also encompasses prophylactic use in high-risk situations (e.g., pre-surgical administration for spinal procedures) and combination therapies with other neuroprotective agents.

### IV. EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example 1: Antibody Characterization**  
MHC1 specificity was confirmed by:  
1. Immunocytochemistry showing selective binding to Cx43-expressing HeLa cells  
2. ELISA demonstrating nM affinity for Cx43 peptides  
3. Hemichannel inhibition assays showing:  
   - 66.7 nM MHC1 reduced EtBr uptake by 82% in low Ca2+/Mg2+ conditions  
   - No effect on pannexin-1 channels  
4. Gap junction assays confirming no inhibition of dye coupling  

**Example 2: In Vivo Efficacy in SCI Models**  
Two partial SCI mouse models showed:  
1. Model 1 (Broad Impact):  
   - MHC1 treatment within 30 minutes improved BMS scores from day 2 onward  
   - 56-day endpoint showed 3.5-fold greater functional recovery vs controls  

2. Model 2 (Focused Impact):  
   - Significant BMS improvement from day 26-56  
   - 47% reduction in lesion size  
   - 2.8-fold greater MAP2 preservation  

**Example 3: Tissue Preservation**  
ECR staining revealed:  
- 39% greater white matter sparing in MHC1-treated animals  
- 28% reduction in gray matter loss  
- 52% decrease in reactive astrogliosis (GFAP+ area)  

**Example 4: Therapeutic Window**  
Intracerebroventricular delivery at 30 days post-SCI:  
- Showed antibody delivery to lesion site  
- Reduced gliosis but no functional improvement  
- Confirms critical acute phase treatment window  

These examples demonstrate MHC1's specificity, efficacy, and mechanism of action as a novel therapeutic for acute spinal cord injury.
