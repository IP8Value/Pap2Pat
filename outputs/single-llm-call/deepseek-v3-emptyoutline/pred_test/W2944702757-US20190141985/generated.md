# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

The present disclosure relates generally to the field of organ transplantation and preservation. More specifically, the disclosed subject matter pertains to systems and methods for extracorporeal recovery and regeneration of severely damaged donor lungs using an interventional cross-circulation platform. The disclosed technology enables prolonged normothermic extracorporeal support of injured lungs while facilitating therapeutic interventions that promote functional recovery at molecular, cellular, and tissue levels.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

The critical shortage of viable donor organs represents a fundamental limitation in transplantation medicine. While organ transplantation has become an established lifesaving treatment, up to 80% of donated lungs are discarded due to injuries sustained during donor hospitalization or death. Current approaches such as ex vivo lung perfusion (EVLP) provide limited capacity to evaluate and improve marginal quality donor lungs, typically allowing only about 6 hours of normothermic perfusion. These systems have proven inadequate for recovering severely damaged lungs, particularly those injured by gastric aspiration which causes chemical pneumonitis and respiratory insufficiency through epithelial damage, inflammation, and surfactant dysfunction.  

Previous attempts to recover lungs injured by gastric aspiration using conventional EVLP systems have been unsuccessful, resulting in increased edema, persistent inflammatory cytokine levels, and failure to restore tissue morphology. There exists an urgent need for advanced extracorporeal support platforms capable of both sustaining severely damaged organs outside the body and enabling therapeutic interventions that can promote structural and functional recovery. The disclosed subject matter addresses these limitations through an interventional cross-circulation platform that provides physiologic systemic regulation while allowing repeated diagnostic and therapeutic procedures.  

## SUMMARY  

The disclosed subject matter provides systems and methods for extracorporeal recovery of severely damaged lungs through interventional cross-circulation. The platform enables prolonged normothermic support of injured lungs while facilitating therapeutic interventions including bronchoalveolar lavage, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment maneuvers. The system incorporates physiologic regulation through connection with a living host, allowing maintenance of injured lungs for extended durations exceeding 36 hours.  

Key aspects of the disclosed technology include a vascular bio-bridge configuration enabling single lung functional assessment, integrated diagnostic capabilities using surface thermography and exosome analysis, and demonstrated recovery of lung function through molecular, cellular, and tissue-level regeneration. The platform supports restoration of pulmonary vasoregulation, microvascular and bronchial epithelial tight junctions, alveolar type II pneumocyte surfactant recycling, and mucociliary function in severely damaged lungs.  

The methods include establishing cross-circulation between explanted injured lungs and a living recipient through cannulation of pulmonary vasculature and connection to an extracorporeal circuit. Therapeutic interventions are performed according to a specified sequence while continuously monitoring lung function through gas exchange measurements, compliance assessments, and imaging modalities. The system enables recovery of lungs injured by gastric aspiration to meet transplantation criteria through clearance of inflammatory mediators, restoration of alveolar architecture, and regeneration of epithelial and endothelial cellular components.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

The interventional cross-circulation platform comprises several key components that collectively enable prolonged extracorporeal support and recovery of severely damaged lungs. The system includes a normothermic organ chamber housing the explanted lungs, an extracorporeal circuit maintaining blood flow through pulmonary vasculature, and a living recipient providing physiologic regulation through systemic circulation.  

Cannulation of the pulmonary artery enables inflow of oxygenated blood to the lungs, while venous drainage is managed through cannulas placed in pulmonary veins. A unique vascular bio-bridge configuration utilizes the aortic arch as a conduit between the left atrial cuff and extracorporeal circuit, allowing placement of selective venous blood ports for single lung functional assessment. An endotracheal tube secured in the trachea enables continuous ventilation throughout the support period.  

The platform incorporates clinical-grade equipment including a centrifugal pump console, softshell reservoir, and pressure/flow monitoring systems. Recipient connection is established through bilateral internal jugular vein cannulation, with continuous heparin infusion maintaining appropriate anticoagulation. Physiological parameters including pulmonary artery and vein pressures, flows, and temperatures are continuously monitored and recorded.  

Therapeutic interventions are performed according to a standardized protocol involving sequential bronchoalveolar lavage, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment maneuvers. Bronchoscopic lavage clears gastric contents, secretions, and cellular debris while normalizing airway pH. Exogenous surfactant administration replenishes depleted endogenous surfactants, with delivery confirmed through transmission electron microscopy. Recruitment maneuvers improve ventilation-perfusion matching through transient increases in positive airway pressure.  

Diagnostic capabilities include advanced thermographic imaging for non-invasive assessment of lung recovery. Global surface temperature mapping identifies regions of ventilation-perfusion mismatch, while localized hypothermic probe tests quantify tissue rewarming kinetics as an indicator of functional restoration. Analysis of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid provides molecular markers of recovery, including decreasing pepsin concentrations and inflammatory cytokine levels.  

The platform demonstrates cellular and tissue-level regeneration through restoration of alveolar epithelial tight junctions (ZO-3), microvascular endothelial junctions (ZO-1), and gap junctions (connexin 43). Type II pneumocytes regain surfactant production capacity as evidenced by intracellular lamellar bodies and functional uptake assays. Histopathologic analyses show resolution of alveolar edema, inflammation, and apoptosis over the support period.  

Functional recovery is evidenced by improving pressure-volume relationships, dynamic compliance measurements, and gas exchange capacity. Injured lungs progress from severely compromised function (PaO2/FiO2 <90 mmHg) to meeting transplantation criteria (PaO2/FiO2 >640 mmHg) over 36 hours of support. Lung injury scores decrease significantly across all categories, with the most pronounced improvements in alveolar polymorphonuclear cells, edema, and apoptotic indices.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Methods  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments and applications of the disclosed technology:  

Example 1 describes establishment of gastric aspiration injury in a porcine model. Donor swine undergo bronchoscopic delivery of standardized gastric contents (2 mL/kg, pH 2) into a single lung under general anesthesia. The acute injury develops over 6 hours, with progression assessed by radiography, bronchoscopy, bronchoalveolar lavage analysis, and hemodynamic monitoring. Explanted lungs show extensive consolidation, cellular derangement, peribronchial edema, alveolar hemorrhage, and leukocyte infiltration.  

Example 2 details the lung cannulation and cross-circulation setup. Following explant, lungs are cannulated through the pulmonary artery and veins, with the aortic arch serving as a bio-bridge for single lung venous sampling. An endotracheal tube enables continuous ventilation. Recipient swine undergo bilateral internal jugular vein cannulation, with connection to the extracorporeal circuit initiating cross-circulation. The system maintains normothermic perfusion with physiologic flow rates and pressures.  

Example 3 outlines the therapeutic intervention protocol. At specified intervals (0, 2, 4, 6, 12, and 18 hours), injured lungs undergo bronchoalveolar lavage (3 cycles), surfactant replacement (bovine lipid extract surfactant, 12.5 mg phospholipid/kg), and alveolar recruitment maneuvers. Interventions are performed bronchoscopically with confirmation of surfactant distribution by electron microscopy. Recruitment incorporates increased positive end-expiratory pressure and tidal volumes.  

Example 4 describes functional assessment methods. Single lung ventilation using bronchial blockers enables compliance measurements. Blood gas analysis of pulmonary venous drainage quantifies gas exchange capacity. Pressure-volume loops track mechanical properties, while thermographic imaging monitors surface temperature distributions. Periodic performance challenges assess response to increased ventilation and oxygenation demands.  

Example 5 presents analytical methods for evaluating recovery. Histopathologic scoring assesses airway and alveolar polymorphonuclear cells, edema, infiltrates, and apoptosis. Immunohistochemistry tracks expression of aquaporin 5, surfactant protein C, and junctional proteins. Transmission electron microscopy visualizes subcellular structures including lamellar bodies and blood-gas barrier integrity. Metabolic activity assays quantify tissue viability.  

Example 6 demonstrates application of the platform to human transplantation. The system is adapted for clinical use with appropriate scaling and immunosuppression protocols. Donor lungs previously deemed unsuitable due to gastric aspiration injury undergo recovery on the cross-circulation platform prior to transplantation. Functional assessments confirm meeting acceptance criteria following 36 hours of extracorporeal support and interventions.  

The examples illustrate the platform's capacity to support severely damaged lungs through physiologic regulation while enabling therapeutic interventions that promote structural and functional recovery. The technology addresses critical limitations in organ transplantation by expanding the donor pool through recovery of previously unusable organs.
