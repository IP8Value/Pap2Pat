# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation has long been recognized as a powerful method for constructing stereocenters, particularly in the formation of chiral quaternary centers when prochiral nucleophiles are employed. Despite extensive development over the past five decades, the use of α-substituted lactones or lactams as prochiral nucleophiles in such transformations remains underexplored. Existing methods, such as palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative enantioselective allylic alkylation, suffer from limitations including narrow electrophile scope and challenging nucleophile synthesis. These shortcomings hinder the practical application of such methodologies in synthetic and medicinal chemistry.  

The development of more efficient and versatile catalytic systems capable of utilizing readily accessible nucleophiles and electrophiles is therefore highly desirable. In particular, the ability to employ unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles in conjunction with α-acyl lactones and lactams would represent a significant advancement, as these substrates are both synthetically tractable and amenable to further functionalization. Additionally, the products of such transformations—α-quaternary lactones and lactams—are valuable intermediates for the synthesis of complex chiral molecules, including pharmaceutical agents.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a novel nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation method for the synthesis of α-quaternary lactones and lactams. The method employs readily available α-acyl lactones or lactams as nucleophiles and unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles in the presence of a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand. This transformation proceeds with high enantioselectivity (up to 90% ee) and good yields (up to 91%), providing access to a broad range of functionalized products.  

Key advantages of the invention include the use of inexpensive nickel catalysts, commercially available chiral ligands, and easily synthesized substrates. The method tolerates a variety of functional groups and enables the construction of complex chiral building blocks with multiple handles for further derivatization. The resulting α-quaternary lactones and lactams can be readily transformed into valuable intermediates, such as piperidine derivatives and spirocyclic compounds, which are of particular interest in pharmaceutical development.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Overview  

The invention provides a catalytic system and reaction conditions for the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams with allylic alcohols. The method is broadly applicable to a range of substrate classes and delivers products with high stereochemical fidelity. The catalytic system comprises a nickel source, such as Ni(COD)₂, and a chiral bisphosphine ligand, exemplified by (R)-P-phos. The reaction is typically conducted in ethereal solvents at low temperatures to optimize enantioselectivity and yield.  

### Definitions  

For the purposes of this disclosure, the following terms are defined as follows:  
- **α-Acyl lactone/lactam**: A lactone or lactam bearing an acyl substituent at the α-position relative to the carbonyl group.  
- **Unactivated allylic alcohol**: An allylic alcohol that does not require prior derivatization (e.g., as a carbonate or acetate) to participate in allylic alkylation.  
- **Chiral bisphosphine ligand**: A bidentate phosphine ligand with chiral backbone structure, such as (R)-BINAP or (R)-P-phos.  
- **Quaternary stereocenter**: A carbon atom bonded to four distinct substituents, creating a chiral center.  

### Exemplary Methods  

The general procedure for the nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation involves combining the α-acyl lactone or lactam nucleophile (1 equiv), allylic alcohol (1–2 equiv), Ni(COD)₂ (5–10 mol%), and chiral bisphosphine ligand (6–12 mol%) in an ethereal solvent (e.g., diethyl ether or toluene) at temperatures ranging from −10 °C to 25 °C. The reaction is typically complete within 24–48 hours, after which the product is isolated by standard workup and purification techniques.  

### Transition Metal Catalysts  

The catalytic system employs nickel as the transition metal, with Ni(COD)₂ being the preferred precursor due to its stability and reactivity. Alternative nickel sources, such as nickel halides or nickel acetylacetonate, may also be employed with appropriate ligand systems. The nickel catalyst facilitates the formation of a π-allyl intermediate, which undergoes enantioselective attack by the nucleophile.  

### Chiral Ligands  

Chiral bisphosphine ligands are critical for achieving high enantioselectivity. Ligands such as (R)-BINAP, (R)-P-phos, and derivatives thereof have been shown to provide optimal results. The choice of ligand influences both the yield and enantiomeric excess of the product, with (R)-P-phos delivering particularly high enantioselectivity (up to 90% ee).  

### Exemplary Syntheses of Compound Libraries  

The invention enables the synthesis of diverse libraries of α-quaternary lactones and lactams by varying the nucleophile and electrophile components. For example:  
- **Lactone nucleophiles**: α-Ethoxycarbonyl, α-methoxycarbonyl, or bicyclic lactones.  
- **Lactam nucleophiles**: Benzoyl-protected or other N-substituted lactams.  
- **Allylic alcohols**: Aryl-, heteroaryl-, alkenyl-, or alkyl-substituted derivatives.  

### Pharmaceutical Agents and Compositions  

The products of this invention are valuable intermediates for pharmaceutical development. For instance, reduction of α-quaternary lactams provides access to chiral piperidine derivatives, which are prevalent in drug molecules. Similarly, α-quaternary lactones can be transformed into spirocyclic or acyclic building blocks for further elaboration.  

### Synthetic Schemes of Exemplary Pharmaceutical Products  

Representative transformations include:  
1. **Reduction**: Conversion of lactones to diols or lactams to piperidines.  
2. **Grignard addition**: Formation of enones from lactones.  
3. **Ring-closing metathesis**: Construction of spirocyclic frameworks.  
4. **Deprotection**: Cleavage of N-protecting groups to yield free lactams.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols  

A mixture of α-ethoxycarbonyl lactone (1a, 0.2 mmol), allyl alcohol (2a, 0.24 mmol), Ni(COD)₂ (10 mol%), and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%) in diethyl ether (2 mL) was stirred at −10 °C for 48 hours. The reaction was quenched with saturated NH₄Cl, extracted with ethyl acetate, and purified by flash chromatography to afford the α-quaternary lactone 3aa in 80% yield and 85% ee.  

### Example 2: Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactams  

A similar procedure was employed using α-ethoxycarbonyl lactam (4a, 0.2 mmol) and cinnamyl alcohol (0.24 mmol) with (R)-P-phos (12 mol%) in toluene at 10 °C for 36 hours. The product 5ab was isolated in 74% yield and 90% ee.
