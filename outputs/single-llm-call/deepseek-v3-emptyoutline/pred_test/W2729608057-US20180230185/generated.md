# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to novel cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that inhibit protein-protein interactions (PPI) between epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs), particularly heterodimerization of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) with other EGFR family members including HER3 and EGFR. More specifically, the invention provides symmetric cyclic peptidomimetics containing L-Pro-D-Pro sequences that bind to domain IV of HER2 extracellular domain (ECD) and inhibit HER2:HER3 and EGFR:HER2 heterodimerization. The compounds exhibit high specificity for HER2-overexpressing cancer cell lines with IC50 values in the low nanomolar range, demonstrate stability against enzymatic degradation, and show efficacy in inhibiting tumor growth in xenograft models. The invention further relates to pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds and methods for treating cancers characterized by HER2 overexpression or EGFR mutations, including breast, lung, and ovarian cancers.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

The human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family of receptor tyrosine kinases, consisting of HER1 (EGFR), HER2, HER3, and HER4, plays critical roles in cell growth and differentiation. Dysregulation of these receptors through overexpression or aberrant dimerization is implicated in various cancers, particularly breast, lung, and ovarian cancers. Among the possible receptor dimers, EGFR-HER2 and HER2-HER3 heterodimers are particularly significant in cancer progression and are associated with poor patient prognosis.  

Current therapeutic approaches targeting HER2 include monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab, which bind to different domains of the HER2 extracellular region. While these antibodies have shown clinical benefit, they suffer from limitations including immunogenicity, poor tissue penetration, and high production costs. Small molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as lapatinib target the intracellular kinase domain but often lead to resistance mutations like T790M in EGFR. There remains an unmet need for stable, potent inhibitors that directly target the extracellular dimerization interfaces of HER2-containing heterodimers.  

Peptidomimetics offer advantages over both antibodies and small molecules by combining target specificity with improved stability and tissue penetration. Previous linear peptidomimetics targeting HER2 dimerization showed promising activity but were susceptible to enzymatic degradation. Cyclization represents a proven strategy to enhance peptide stability, but designing cyclic peptidomimetics that maintain high affinity binding to HER2 while inhibiting multiple dimer pairs presents significant challenges. The chirality of both the scaffold and pharmacophore groups critically influences binding affinity and biological activity, requiring careful structural optimization.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that overcome limitations of existing HER2-targeted therapies. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. A symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic scaffold containing an L-Pro-D-Pro sequence that forms a β-turn/β-hairpin structure, exposing critical pharmacophore groups for HER2 binding while conferring stability against enzymatic degradation.  

2. Specific compounds, particularly Compound 18, that bind with high affinity (Kd = 0.125 μM) to domain IV of HER2 extracellular domain, competitively inhibiting both HER2:HER3 and EGFR:HER2 heterodimerization.  

3. Compounds demonstrating nanomolar IC50 values (18 nM in Calu-3 cells) against HER2-overexpressing cancer cell lines with >200-fold selectivity over normal cells.  

4. A chiral switch mechanism where biological activity depends on both the L-Pro-D-Pro scaffold conformation and the stereochemistry of β-amino acid residues, enabling optimization of binding affinity.  

5. Demonstrated in vivo efficacy in xenograft models, with Compound 18 significantly delaying tumor growth at 4 mg/kg doses and showing favorable toxicity profiles.  

6. Synergistic effects with existing TKIs like erlotinib, particularly against T790M mutation-bearing cells, providing potential combination therapy strategies to overcome resistance.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds, methods for their synthesis, and therapeutic methods for treating HER2-associated cancers by administering effective amounts of the compounds to patients in need thereof.  

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

### Definitions/General Discussion  

The following terms shall have the meanings set forth below throughout this specification:  

"Peptidomimetic" refers to a compound containing both peptide and non-peptide structural elements that mimics the biological activity of a natural peptide but has improved stability and pharmacological properties.  

"β-amino acid" refers to any amino acid where the amino group is bonded to the β-carbon rather than the α-carbon relative to the carboxyl group.  

"HER2 extracellular domain (ECD)" refers to the portion of HER2 receptor protruding from the cell membrane, comprising four domains (I-IV) that mediate ligand binding and receptor dimerization.  

"Domain IV" specifically refers to the membrane-proximal domain of HER2 ECD that contains critical residues for receptor dimerization.  

"Chiral switch" refers to the phenomenon where changing the stereochemistry of key residues (e.g., β-amino acids or Pro-Pro sequence) results in significant changes in biological activity.  

The general structure of inventive compounds features:  
- A cyclic backbone constrained by an L-Pro-D-Pro sequence forming a β-turn  
- Symmetric arrangement of pharmacophore groups (Arg, β-naphthyl-Ala, Phe, Asp)  
- Exposure of critical side chains for HER2 binding while protecting the peptide backbone from proteases  

Key structural features conferring activity include:  
1. The L-Pro-D-Pro sequence inducing a right-handed turn that properly orients pharmacophores  
2. β-naphthyl groups providing critical hydrophobic interactions with HER2 domain IV  
3. Arginine residues forming electrostatic interactions with HER2 surface residues  
4. Cyclization preventing N-/C-terminal degradation while maintaining conformational rigidity  

### Example 1. Compound 18: Synthesis and Characterization  

Compound 18 was synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry on 2-chlorotrityl chloride resin. The linear precursor was assembled using the following sequence:  

H-Arg-Anapa(R)-Phe-Asp-Arg-Anapa(R)-Phe-Asp-Pro-L-Pro-CTC resin  

After complete assembly, the peptide was cleaved from resin using TFE/DCM (1:1) and cyclized in solution using PyAOP/DIEA in THF/DMF (4:1). Final deprotection was achieved using TFA/water/TIPS (95:2.5:2.5).  

The product was purified by HPLC and characterized by:  
- Mass spectrometry: Observed m/z 1425.699 (calculated 1425.5893)  
- Circular dichroism: Characteristic β-turn spectrum  
- NMR: Confirmed cyclic structure and side chain orientations  

Key analytical data:  
1. HPLC purity >95% (C18 column, 10-90% acetonitrile gradient)  
2. NMR (DMSO-d6): Distinct amide proton dispersion confirming rigid structure  
3. TOCSY/NOESY: Sequential assignments confirming designed conformation  

Stability studies in mouse serum showed no degradation after 48 hours, demonstrating the protective effect of cyclization.  

### Example 2. Inhibition Testing  

Compound 18 was tested against multiple cancer cell lines:  

1. HER2-overexpressing lines:  
   - SKBR-3 (breast): IC50 = 194 nM  
   - Calu-3 (lung): IC50 = 18 nM  
   - BT-474 (breast): IC50 = 210 nM  

2. Controls:  
   - MCF-7 (HER2-low breast): IC50 >50 μM  
   - MCF-10A (normal breast): IC50 = 40 μM  

The >200-fold selectivity for HER2+ cells demonstrates exceptional target specificity. Competitive binding assays using FITC-labeled analogs showed dose-dependent displacement by Compound 18, confirming HER2 ECD binding.  

### Example 3. Competitive Binding Assays  

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) quantified binding to:  
1. Full HER2 ECD: Kd = 0.125 μM (kon = 4.76×10^7 M^-1s^-1, koff = 5.97 s^-1)  
2. Isolated domain IV: Similar affinity, confirming domain IV as primary binding site  

No significant binding was observed to:  
- EGFR, HER3, or HER4 ECDs  
- Control compounds lacking key pharmacophores  

These results demonstrate the specificity of Compound 18 for HER2 domain IV.  

### Example 4. Testing Compound 18 Analogs for PPI Inhibition  

Structural variants tested included:  

1. Pro-Pro stereoisomers:  
   - L-Pro-D-Pro (Compound 18): Active  
   - D-Pro-L-Pro (Compound 18-4): Reduced activity  

2. β-amino acid (Anapa) stereoisomers:  
   - (R)-Anapa (Compound 18): Active  
   - (S)-Anapa analogs: Inactive  

Key findings:  
- Only L-Pro-D-Pro with (R)-Anapa showed optimal activity  
- Docking studies revealed improper orientation of pharmacophores in inactive analogs  
- Confirmed chiral switch mechanism controlling HER2 binding

Proximity ligation assays quantified inhibition of:  
1. HER2:HER3 dimers: >80% inhibition at 1 μM  
2. EGFR:HER2 dimers: >75% inhibition at 1 μM  

PathHunter® assays confirmed:  
1. Dose-dependent inhibition of NRG-1-induced HER2:HER3 dimerization  
2. EC50 = 0.3 μM for dimerization inhibition  
3. Specificity versus control compounds  

These results demonstrate Compound 18's unique ability to inhibit multiple pathogenic HER2 heterodimers.
