# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to recombinant poxvirus-based vaccine vectors, particularly the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), for the prevention and treatment of viral infections. More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel vaccine platform utilizing a modified vaccinia virus (VACV) Copenhagen strain engineered with targeted gene deletions to ensure safety and efficacy. The invention further encompasses multi-pathogen vaccines, including a single-construct vaccine encoding immunogens from both Zika virus (ZIKV) and chikungunya virus (CHIKV), methods of production in mammalian cell lines, and applications in inducing protective immunity against these viruses as well as smallpox.  

## BACKGROUND  

Poxvirus-based vaccine vectors have historically been favored for their ability to accommodate large recombinant immunogens, lack of genomic integration, and potential for needle-free delivery. Traditional vectors, such as Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA) and other vaccinia strains, rely on production in avian cell lines like chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs), which present manufacturing challenges. While effective in boosting immune responses, these vectors often require heterologous prime-boost regimens to achieve robust immunity in naïve individuals.  

Recent outbreaks of arboviruses, including ZIKV and CHIKV, have underscored the need for single-construct, multi-pathogen vaccines. These viruses co-circulate in overlapping geographic regions, share mosquito vectors, and can co-infect hosts, making combined vaccines logistically advantageous. However, existing poxvirus vectors face limitations in scalability, safety, and immunogenicity, particularly when used as stand-alone vaccines.  

The SCV platform addresses these limitations through targeted deletions of essential viral genes (e.g., D13L, encoding the assembly protein D13) and production in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells engineered to complement these deletions. This approach enhances safety by preventing productive replication in vaccine recipients while enabling high-yield manufacturing in industrially validated cell lines.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a recombinant SCV vaccine platform characterized by:  
1. **Genetic modifications**: Deletion of the D13L gene in the VACV Copenhagen strain, rendering the virus incapable of producing infectious progeny in non-complementing cells.  
2. **Production system**: Propagation in CHO cells stably expressing D13 and the host range protein CP77, enabling scalable, serum-free manufacturing.  
3. **Multi-pathogen vaccines**: A single-vector vaccine (e.g., SCV-ZIKA/CHIK) encoding structural proteins from ZIKV and CHIKV, capable of inducing neutralizing antibodies and protective immunity against both viruses after one or two doses.  
4. **Broad applicability**: Demonstrated immunogenicity in non-human primates (NHPs), including protection against ZIKV challenge and cross-neutralizing responses against multiple CHIKV genotypes.  

The platform further exhibits potential as a smallpox vaccine, eliciting anti-VACV antibody responses comparable to licensed vaccines.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Construction of Vaccines  

The SCV backbone is derived from the VACV Copenhagen strain, with a targeted deletion of D13L to abolish infectious virion assembly. Recombinant vaccines are constructed by inserting heterologous gene cassettes (e.g., ZIKV prME and CHIKV structural polyproteins) into the viral genome under the control of poxvirus promoters. The vectors are propagated in CHO-D13/CP77 cells, which provide trans-complementation of D13 and CP77 to support viral replication. Purification involves sucrose cushion centrifugation to yield high-titer, sterile preparations.  

### Example 2  

**Immunogenicity in NHPs**: Rhesus macaques vaccinated with SCV-ZIKA/CHIK (10<sup>7</sup> or 10<sup>8</sup> plaque-forming units [pfu]) developed neutralizing antibodies against ZIKV (strain PRVABC59) and four CHIKV genotypes (IOL, WA, and Asian). A two-dose regimen (10<sup>8</sup> pfu) significantly boosted ZIKV-neutralizing titers (5.5-fold increase, *p* = 0.009). Cross-neutralizing responses to CHIKV Asian isolates, initially lower than those against IOL/WA isolates, increased 44-fold by day 70 (*p* = 0.005), demonstrating epitope spreading.  

### Example 3  

**Protection Against ZIKV Challenge**: NHPs vaccinated with SCV-ZIKA/CHIK showed reduced viremia post-challenge with ZIKV PRVABC59. Animals receiving two doses (10<sup>8</sup> pfu) had undetectable serum ZIKV RNA (<860 copies/mL), while controls exhibited high viral loads. Neutralizing antibody titers inversely correlated with viral load (*p* < 0.001), supporting humoral immunity as a key protective mechanism.  

### Conclusion  

The SCV platform combines safety (non-replicating in vivo), scalable production (CHO cells), and potent immunogenicity, enabling single-construct vaccines against co-circulating pathogens. The data support its use for ZIKV and CHIKV prevention, with potential extension to other viruses (e.g., dengue, smallpox).  

### Example 4  

**Anti-VACV Responses**: SCV vaccination induced VACV-neutralizing antibodies (mean PRNT<sub>50</sub> titer: 79 ± 24 after two 10<sup>8</sup> pfu doses), comparable to licensed smallpox vaccines (MVA, ACAM2000). ELISA titers (800 ± 163) further confirmed robust IgG responses, suggesting utility as a smallpox vaccine.  

### Example 5  

**Safety Profile**: No fever or weight loss was observed in vaccinated NHPs. One animal developed a transient rash, deemed unrelated to vaccination. The absence of adverse events aligns with historical safety data for MVA in humans.  

### Example 6  

**ADE Risk Mitigation**: SCV-ZIKA/CHIK did not enhance dengue virus disease in IFNAR1<sup>−/−</sup> mice, addressing concerns about antibody-dependent enhancement. Fusion loop or prM cleavage site modifications can be incorporated to further reduce theoretical risks.  

### Example 7  

**Dose Optimization**: A single 10<sup>7</sup> pfu dose sufficed for CHIKV immunity, while two 10<sup>8</sup> pfu doses optimized ZIKV protection. This flexibility supports tailored regimens for endemic regions or outbreak responses.
