## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel small molecule compounds that activate quorum sensing (QS) in Vibrio species by targeting the central QS regulator LuxO. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting LuxO ATPase activity to prematurely induce the high cell density QS program, thereby repressing virulence factor production in pathogenic Vibrios including Vibrio cholerae, Vibrio parahaemolyticus, Vibrio vulnificus, and Vibrio alginolyticus.  

## BACKGROUND  

Quorum sensing is a bacterial cell-cell communication process that enables populations of bacteria to synchronously alter gene expression in response to changes in population density. Many pathogenic bacteria employ QS to control virulence factor production, biofilm formation, and other collective behaviors. Traditional antibiotic therapies are increasingly ineffective due to widespread resistance, creating an urgent need for alternative anti-infective strategies that target virulence rather than bacterial growth.  

In Vibrio species, the QS circuit centers around LuxO, an NtrC-family response regulator that controls expression of small regulatory RNAs (Qrrs) which in turn regulate the master QS transcription factors AphA and HapR. At low cell density, phosphorylated LuxO activates Qrr expression, leading to repression of HapR and activation of virulence genes. At high cell density, autoinducer binding switches receptor kinases to phosphatases, dephosphorylating and inactivating LuxO, thereby allowing HapR production which represses virulence.  

While previous efforts have focused on developing QS inhibitors for pathogens where QS activates virulence, Vibrio cholerae and related pathogens exhibit the opposite pattern - QS represses virulence at high cell density. Therefore, molecules that prematurely activate QS could serve as anti-virulence agents against cholera and other Vibrio infections. However, no small molecule inhibitors of LuxO or other NtrC-family response regulators have been reported previously.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel small molecule compounds that activate Vibrio QS by specifically inhibiting the ATPase activity of LuxO. The compounds belong to a class of 5-thio-6-azauracil derivatives that function as uncompetitive inhibitors of LuxO ATP hydrolysis. Structural optimization has yielded compounds with EC50 values in the low micromolar range for QS activation in Vibrio cholerae.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. The compounds target a conserved mechanism (LuxO ATPase activity) across multiple Vibrio species, enabling broad-spectrum activity against both HSL and non-HSL based QS systems.  
2. The inhibitors reduce virulence factor production and cytotoxicity in pathogenic Vibrios without affecting bacterial growth.  
3. The compounds exhibit favorable drug-like properties including water solubility, stability, and low cytotoxicity to mammalian cells.  

The invention further provides methods of using these LuxO inhibitors to control Vibrio infections, pharmaceutical compositions containing the inhibitors, and methods for identifying additional LuxO inhibitors through structure-activity relationship studies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Materials and Methods  

The small molecule compounds of the invention were identified through a whole-cell high-throughput screen using Vibrio cholerae reporter strains genetically locked in the low cell density state. Screening was performed on a library of 90,000 compounds at the Rockefeller University High-Throughput Screening Resource Center.  

Bacterial strains used include Vibrio cholerae C6706 derivatives, Vibrio harveyi BB120 derivatives, and Vibrio parahaemolyticus strains provided by Dr. Linda McCarter. Standard culture conditions were employed using LB or LM media at 30-37°C with appropriate antibiotics.  

Protein purification was performed using E. coli BL21 Gold (DE3) transformed with pET28B vectors expressing His6-tagged LuxO variants. Proteins were purified by nickel affinity chromatography and stored at -80°C.  

ATPase activity was measured using a coupled enzyme assay monitoring NADH oxidation at 340 nm. DNA binding was assessed through electrophoretic mobility shift and fluorescence anisotropy assays.  

### Chemical Reactions.  

The active compounds feature a 5-thio-6-azauracil core structure with variable hydrocarbon side chains. Structure-activity relationship studies revealed that increasing steric bulk through incorporation of a tert-butyl carbinol side chain enhanced potency 3-fold (compound 12 vs compound 11). The synthetic route involves:  
1. Formation of the 5-thio-6-azauracil core through cyclization of appropriately substituted thiourea precursors  
2. Introduction of diverse alkyl side chains through nucleophilic substitution or coupling reactions  
3. Purification by column chromatography and characterization by NMR and mass spectrometry  

## Results  

### Investigation of the Targets of the QS Activating Compounds  

Two classes of QS-activating compounds were identified: Class 1 compounds function as CqsS receptor agonists, while Class 2 compounds (the subject of this invention) act downstream of the receptors. Genetic and biochemical evidence demonstrates that Class 2 compounds target LuxO rather than other downstream components (σ54, Hfq, or Qrrs). Key findings include:  
1. Class 2 compounds reduce transcription from the qrr4 promoter  
2. They do not affect transcription of other σ54-dependent genes  
3. They inhibit LuxO ATPase activity without affecting DNA binding  

### Structure-Activity-Relationship of Class 2 Compounds  

Systematic modification of the 5-thio-6-azauracil scaffold revealed that:  
1. The thio-azauracil core is essential for activity  
2. Branching in the alkyl side chain enhances potency  
3. A tert-butyl carbinol side chain provides optimal activity (compound 12)  
4. EC50 values correlate with ATPase inhibition potency  

### Class 2 Compounds Inhibit the LuxO ATPase Activity  

Detailed kinetic analysis showed that:  
1. Compounds 11 and 12 inhibit LuxO ATPase uncompetitively  
2. They decrease both Km and Vmax of the ATPase reaction  
3. Inhibition occurs through binding to the LuxO-ATP complex  
4. IC50 values match EC50 values for QS activation  

### A Genetic Screen to Identify LuxO Mutants Resistant to Compound 12  

Random mutagenesis and selection revealed four LuxO mutations conferring resistance:  
1. I211F, L215F in the GAFTGA domain helix  
2. L242F adjacent to the Walker B motif  
3. V294L facing the catalytic arginine  
These mutations map to regions bordering the ATP binding pocket.  

### Broad Spectrum Activation of Vibrio QS  

The LuxO inhibitors activate QS across multiple Vibrio species:  
1. Induce bioluminescence in Vibrio harveyi luxO D47E  
2. Restore opaque colony morphology in Vibrio parahaemolyticus luxO*  
This demonstrates conservation of LuxO function and inhibitor mechanism.  

### New Chemical Tools for Controlling Virulence in Vibrios  

The inhibitors reduce virulence phenotypes in pathogenic Vibrios:  
1. Decrease TcpA production in Vibrio cholerae  
2. Reduce VopD secretion in Vibrio parahaemolyticus  
3. Impair cytotoxicity toward HeLa cells  
These effects occur without inhibiting bacterial growth.  

## Discussion Of Results  

The LuxO inhibitors described herein represent the first reported small molecules targeting an NtrC-family response regulator ATPase. Their uncompetitive inhibition mechanism suggests binding to the LuxO-ATP complex induces conformational changes that block hydrolysis.  

The inhibitors' broad activity across Vibrio species stems from LuxO conservation, enabling control of both HSL and non-HSL QS systems. Their ability to reduce virulence factor production and cytotoxicity supports their potential as anti-infectives.  

Notably, the inhibitors show specificity for LuxO over the closely related E. coli NtrC, suggesting the identified resistance mutations define a LuxO-specific binding pocket. This specificity, combined with favorable drug-like properties, makes these compounds valuable both as chemical probes and as leads for anti-virulence drug development.  

Future directions include:  
1. Further optimization of the thio-azauracil scaffold  
2. Development of analogs targeting other NtrC-family proteins  
3. Evaluation in animal models of Vibrio infection  
4. Exploration of AAA+ ATPase inhibition more broadly  

The invention thus provides novel tools for studying QS and combating Vibrio infections through a unique anti-virulence mechanism.
