# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to modified bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) exhibiting enhanced resistance to inhibition by noggin, a natural extracellular antagonist of BMP signaling. More specifically, the invention pertains to engineered BMP variants, particularly BMP-6 and BMP-7 derivatives, wherein specific amino acid substitutions confer resistance to noggin-mediated suppression while retaining osteogenic activity. The modified BMPs are useful in therapeutic applications requiring bone regeneration, including spinal fusion, fracture repair, and treatment of skeletal defects.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) are members of the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) superfamily and play critical roles in osteogenesis, chondrogenesis, and tissue repair. Clinically, recombinant human BMP-2 and BMP-7 have been utilized to promote bone healing in spinal fusion and non-union fractures. However, their efficacy is limited by high dosing requirements, which may be attributed to negative feedback mechanisms, including induction of endogenous BMP antagonists such as noggin.  

Noggin binds BMPs with high affinity, preventing their interaction with cell surface receptors (e.g., ALK2, ALK6, BMPR-II) and thereby inhibiting downstream signaling. While BMP-6 and BMP-7 share significant structural homology, BMP-6 demonstrates superior osteogenic potency in vitro and in vivo, partly due to its reduced susceptibility to noggin inhibition. Prior attempts to enhance BMP activity have focused on optimizing delivery systems or increasing protein stability, but modifying intrinsic BMP-noggin interactions remains underexplored.  

Thus, there exists a need for BMP variants with improved resistance to noggin inhibition, enabling lower therapeutic doses and enhanced clinical outcomes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides modified BMP polypeptides, particularly BMP-6 and BMP-7 variants, wherein specific amino acid substitutions reduce binding affinity to noggin while preserving receptor activation capacity. Key embodiments include:  

1. **BMP-7 Variants**: Substitution of glutamic acid at position 60 (Glu60) with lysine (E60K) in the mature BMP-7 domain, which significantly reduces noggin sensitivity. Additional mutations (e.g., R48Q, Y65N) may further enhance resistance.  
2. **BMP-6/7 Chimeras**: Hybrid proteins incorporating BMP-6 domains (e.g., residues 45–80) into BMP-7, such as the 80-1 chimera, which exhibits noggin resistance comparable to native BMP-6.  
3. **BMP-2/4 Variants**: Analogous substitutions (e.g., P36K in BMP-2) to transfer noggin resistance to other BMP family members.  

The modified BMPs are produced via recombinant expression in host cells (e.g., HEK293T, CHO) and purified using chromatographic methods. Therapeutic compositions comprising these variants are effective at lower doses than wild-type BMPs in bone regeneration applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Bone Morphogenetic Proteins  
BMPs are dimeric secreted proteins with a conserved structure comprising a prodomain and a mature domain. The mature domain contains critical binding interfaces for receptors (type I: ALK2, ALK6; type II: BMPR-II, ActR-II) and antagonists (e.g., noggin). BMP-6 and BMP-7 share 73% amino acid identity but differ in noggin sensitivity due to structural variations in finger 1 and finger 2 regions.  

### Interactions Between BMPs and Their Antagonists  
Noggin inhibits BMP signaling by binding the mature domain, occluding receptor interaction sites. Surface plasmon resonance (BIAcore) assays reveal that BMP-6 binds noggin with slower dissociation kinetics (K<sub>d</sub> = 6 × 10<sup>−5</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>) compared to BMP-7 (K<sub>d</sub> = 1 × 10<sup>−3</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>), yet cellular assays show BMP-6 is functionally more resistant. This discrepancy suggests that BMP-6’s rigidity (stabilized by Lys60–Asn65 hydrogen bonds) reduces noggin’s inhibitory efficacy in the presence of receptors.  

### Generating Modified BMPs  
Site-directed mutagenesis (e.g., QuikChange) or PCR-based chimera assembly is used to introduce resistance-conferring mutations:  
- **BMP-7 E60K**: Substitution at Glu60 (BMP-7 numbering) with lysine, mimicking BMP-6’s residue.  
- **Chimeras**: Replacement of BMP-7 segments (e.g., residues 45–80) with BMP-6 sequences.  
- **BMP-2 P36K**: Pro36→Lys mutation in BMP-2, analogous to BMP-6 Lys60.  

### Preparing Nucleic Acids Encoding Modified BMPs  
DNA constructs encoding modified BMPs are synthesized using codon-optimized sequences for mammalian expression. For example, the BMP-7 E60K mutant is generated by amplifying the BMP-7 open reading frame (GenBank NM_001719) with mutagenic primers, followed by subcloning into an expression vector (e.g., pcDNA3.1).  

### Expression Vectors  
Plasmids are engineered with CMV promoters, secretion signals (e.g., BMP-7 prodomain), and epitope tags (e.g., His<sub>6</sub>) for purification. Vectors may also include selectable markers (e.g., neomycin resistance).  

### Transfection/Transformation  
HEK293T or CHO cells are transfected using lipid-based reagents (e.g., FuGENE 6) or electroporation. Stable cell lines are selected with antibiotics (e.g., Geneticin).  

### Hosts for Expression of Modified BMPs  
Suitable hosts include mammalian cells (HEK293T, CHO), bacteria (E. coli), or yeast (Pichia pastoris). Mammalian systems are preferred for proper post-translational modifications (e.g., glycosylation).  

### Expression Methods  
Transfected cells are cultured in serum-free media (e.g., DMEM + 0.5% FBS) to secrete BMPs into conditioned media. Large-scale production uses roller bottles or bioreactors.  

### Purification  
BMPs are isolated via:  
1. **Ammonium sulfate precipitation** (50% saturation).  
2. **Immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC)**: Ni<sup>2+</sup>-NTA resin captures His-tagged proteins.  
3. **HPLC**: Reverse-phase or size-exclusion chromatography removes impurities.  
Purity is verified by SDS-PAGE and Western blot (anti-BMP-7 antibodies).  

## EXAMPLES  

### BMP Induction of Alkaline Phosphatase Activity  
ROS 17/2.8 osteosarcoma cells were treated with wild-type BMP-6, BMP-7, or BMP-7 E60K (0–100 ng/mL). BMP-6 and BMP-7 E60K induced alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity at lower doses (EC<sub>50</sub> = 14 ng/mL and 18 ng/mL, respectively) compared to BMP-7 (EC<sub>50</sub> = 28 ng/mL). Noggin (500 ng/mL) inhibited BMP-7 activity by 80% but reduced BMP-6 and BMP-7 E60K activity by only 20%.  

### Noggin Inhibition of a Panel of Exemplary BMPs and Related Proteins  
A549-BRE reporter cells were stimulated with BMP-2, BMP-4, BMP-6, or BMP-7 (25 ng/mL) ± noggin (0–5000 ng/mL). IC<sub>50</sub> values for noggin inhibition were:  
- BMP-4: 50 ng/mL  
- BMP-2: 200 ng/mL  
- BMP-7: 800 ng/mL  
- BMP-6: >5000 ng/mL  

### BMP-6 Induction of Downstream Genes in Primary Human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells is Less Susceptible to Noggin Inhibition  
Quantitative PCR analysis showed noggin (100 ng/mL) suppressed BMP-7-induced expression of osteogenic markers (Id-1, Dlx-5) by 70–90%, but BMP-6-induced expression was unaffected.  

### BMP-7 Variant Proteins with Marked Resistance to Noggin Inhibition  
The BMP-7 E60K variant was purified from HEK293T-conditioned media and tested in A549-BRE assays. At 100 ng/mL noggin, wild-type BMP-7 activity was reduced by 75%, whereas BMP-7 E60K retained 85% activity.  

### Example 5  
In a rabbit ulnar critical-size defect model, 100 μg of BMP-6 or BMP-7 E60K induced complete bone bridging by 6 weeks, while wild-type BMP-7 required 500 μg for comparable healing. Micro-CT analysis confirmed 2-fold greater bone volume with BMP-6/BMP-7 E60K.
