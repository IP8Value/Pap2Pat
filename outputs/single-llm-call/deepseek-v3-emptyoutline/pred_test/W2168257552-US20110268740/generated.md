# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and virology, specifically to monoclonal antibodies targeting the matrix protein 2 (M2) of influenza A virus. More particularly, the invention provides isolated human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high affinity to the extracellular domain of M2 (M2e), demonstrate cross-reactivity across influenza A strains, and exhibit both prophylactic and therapeutic efficacy against influenza infection. The antibodies are capable of mediating antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) against influenza-infected cells, thereby providing protection against viral infection. The invention further encompasses methods of producing these antibodies, pharmaceutical compositions containing them, and their use in the prevention and treatment of influenza A infection in human subjects.  

## RELATED ART  

Influenza A virus remains a significant global health burden, causing millions of severe illnesses and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. Current vaccination strategies primarily target the viral surface proteins hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), which are subject to frequent antigenic drift and occasional antigenic shift, necessitating constant vaccine updates. Passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies against HA has shown efficacy but suffers from similar limitations due to viral variability.  

The matrix protein 2 (M2) of influenza A has been identified as a promising alternative target due to its remarkable conservation across human influenza A isolates. M2 is a tetrameric ion channel involved in viral uncoating and maturation, with its 23-amino acid extracellular domain (M2e) showing minimal variation over time. While M2 is abundantly expressed on infected cells, it is present in low quantities on virions, making it a suboptimal target for traditional neutralizing antibodies. Instead, M2-specific antibodies confer protection primarily through ADCC-mediated elimination of infected cells.  

Previous efforts to develop M2e-targeting monoclonal antibodies have included murine-derived and humanized antibodies, as well as fully human antibodies from transchromosomic mice. However, natural human monoclonal antibodies against M2e have not been reported prior to this invention. Natural human antibodies offer distinct advantages, including minimal immunogenicity, reduced off-target effects, and high affinity resulting from in vivo affinity maturation. Existing methods for isolating human antibodies, such as phage display or EBV immortalization of B cells, have limitations in efficiency and preservation of natural antibody pairs.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides isolated human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the extracellular domain of influenza A matrix protein 2 (M2e) with high affinity. These antibodies are characterized by their ability to recognize M2e from diverse influenza A strains, including recent H5N1 isolates, and to bind native M2 protein expressed on infected cells. The antibodies demonstrate potent prophylactic and therapeutic activity in murine models of influenza infection, protecting against lethal challenge even when administered after viral exposure.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Human monoclonal antibodies comprising heavy and light chain variable regions isolated from natural human B cells, wherein the antibodies bind M2e with a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 4-5 nM.  
2. Antibody variants including single-chain Fv (scFv) constructs fused to Fc regions and full-length human IgG1 formats, both exhibiting high expression yields in mammalian cells.  
3. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients.  
4. Methods for preventing or treating influenza A infection in a subject by administering a therapeutically effective amount of the antibodies.  
5. Processes for producing the antibodies through mammalian cell display technology, enabling efficient isolation of natural antibody pairs.  

The invention overcomes limitations of prior approaches by providing natural human antibodies with optimal safety profiles and high efficacy against conserved influenza targets. The antibodies are particularly suited for passive immunization in high-risk populations, including the elderly, immunocompromised individuals, and those requiring rapid protection post-exposure.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the isolation and characterization of natural human monoclonal antibodies specific for the M2e domain of influenza A virus. These antibodies were obtained using a mammalian cell display technology that preserves native heavy and light chain pairings, yielding antibodies with authentic human sequences and optimal binding characteristics.  

The antibodies exhibit several distinctive features:  
- High affinity binding to M2e peptides with Kd values in the low nanomolar range (4-5 nM)  
- Cross-reactivity with M2e sequences from different influenza strains, including H1N1 and H5N1 subtypes  
- Recognition of native M2 protein expressed on cell surfaces  
- Efficient recruitment of effector functions mediating ADCC  
- Protection against lethal influenza challenge in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings  

The antibodies were isolated from peripheral blood B cells of donors with high M2e titers, using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to select antigen-specific B cells. Variable region genes were amplified and expressed as scFv libraries displayed on mammalian cells via Sindbis virus vectors. Subsequent sorting and screening yielded multiple unique M2e-specific antibodies, including clones designated D005, E040, and F052.  

Antibody formats include:  
1. scFv-Fc fusion proteins (e.g., scFv-mouse Fcγ2c for murine studies)  
2. Full-length human IgG1 antibodies (e.g., IgG1κ-D005 for human applications)  

Both formats demonstrate high-level expression in mammalian cells (20-70 mg/L) and maintain functional activity after purification. The antibodies' protective efficacy has been established in a well-characterized mouse model of influenza A infection, showing complete protection against lethal challenge with single antibody doses as low as 20 μg.  

The invention further provides methods for using the antibodies in clinical applications:  
- Prophylaxis for individuals at high risk of influenza complications  
- Post-exposure prevention in recently exposed individuals  
- Treatment of early-stage influenza infection  
- Combination therapies with other antiviral agents  

Pharmaceutical compositions may include the antibodies formulated for intravenous, intramuscular, or pulmonary administration, with dosage regimens tailored to the clinical context. The antibodies' cross-reactivity with conserved M2e epitopes suggests potential utility against emerging pandemic strains.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Isolation of M2e-Specific Human Monoclonal Antibodies**  

M2e-specific human monoclonal antibodies were isolated using a mammalian cell display platform. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from a donor with high M2e antibody titers were stained with M2e consensus peptide conjugated to Qβ virus-like particles and sorted by FACS to obtain 334 antigen-specific B cells.  

Variable region genes from these cells were amplified by RT-PCR and assembled into single-chain Fv (scFv) libraries comprising approximately 2 million clones, ensuring coverage of natural heavy and light chain pairings. The libraries were cloned into Sindbis virus display vectors and used to infect BHK cells at low multiplicity to achieve single antibody expression per cell.  

Infected cells displaying M2e-binding scFvs were isolated through three rounds of FACS sorting using M2e-RNase or M2e-Qβ conjugates as bait. From 255 initially sorted cells, 201 showed viral infection upon expansion, and 130 demonstrated M2e binding in secondary screens. Highly reactive clones were selected for further characterization, including clones D005, E040, and F052.  

The scFv sequences were fused to mouse Fcγ2c domains for initial functional studies. All three antibodies showed high expression levels (20-40 mg/L) when produced in HEK293T cells using EBV-based episomal vectors, followed by protein A affinity purification. This example demonstrates the efficient isolation of natural human antibodies against a conserved viral target using mammalian cell display technology.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

**In Vitro Characterization of M2e-Specific Antibodies**  

The binding properties of antibodies D005, E040, and F052 were characterized through comprehensive in vitro assays. Affinity measurements using an ELISA-based solution equilibrium method determined dissociation constants (Kd) of 4-5 nM for M2e consensus peptide binding, indicating high-affinity interactions.  

Cross-reactivity was assessed by ELISA using M2e peptides from different influenza strains. All three antibodies showed similar binding to both the consensus M2e sequence and a peptide derived from an H5N1 strain (A/VN/1203/2004), with EC50 values of 5-10 ng/ml (45-90 pM). This demonstrates the antibodies' ability to recognize divergent M2e variants.  

Native antigen recognition was confirmed using L929 cells stably expressing full-length M2 from influenza A/PR/8/34 (H1N1). Concentration-dependent staining was observed with all three antibodies, proving their capacity to bind cell-surface M2 in its natural conformation. No significant differences in binding characteristics were detected among the three clones in these assays.  

These results establish that the isolated human antibodies exhibit high-affinity, cross-reactive binding to M2e in both peptide and native protein formats, fulfilling key requirements for therapeutic application against diverse influenza strains.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

**Prophylactic Efficacy in Influenza Infection Model**  

The protective activity of antibodies D005, E040, and F052 was evaluated in a murine model of lethal influenza A infection. Groups of mice received 500 μg of scFv-Fcγ2c antibody intraperitoneally two days before intranasal challenge with 4×LD50 of mouse-adapted influenza A/PR/8/34.  

Control mice treated with nonspecific mouse IgG developed severe symptoms, including dramatic weight loss (>20%) and hypothermia (<30°C body temperature), necessitating euthanasia by days 7-8 post-infection. In contrast, all animals receiving M2e-specific antibodies survived the challenge with minimal morbidity:  
- Transient weight loss (<10%) that resolved by day 10  
- Normal body temperature maintained throughout infection  
- No signs of respiratory distress or other clinical symptoms  

These results demonstrate complete protection against lethal influenza challenge through passive immunization with the human M2e-specific antibodies. The antibodies' efficacy is attributed to their ability to mediate ADCC against infected cells expressing M2, as evidenced by previous mechanistic studies of M2e-targeting antibodies.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

**Antibody Dose-Response Relationship**  

The minimum effective dose of antibody D005 was determined in the murine influenza model. Mice received decreasing amounts of scFv-D005-Fcγ2c (200 μg to 6 μg) or control IgG two days before lethal viral challenge.  

Dose-dependent protection was observed:  
- 200 μg and 60 μg doses provided 100% survival  
- 20 μg dose also conferred complete protection  
- 6 μg dose resulted in 50% survival  

All surviving animals showed complete recovery with transient weight loss, while control mice succumbed to infection within 12 days. These results establish that single doses as low as 20 μg (approximately 1 mg/kg) of M2e-specific antibody can provide complete protection against lethal influenza challenge, demonstrating remarkable potency.  

### EXAMPLE 5  

**Therapeutic Efficacy of Antibody D005**  

The potential for post-exposure treatment was investigated by administering antibody D005 at various timepoints after viral challenge. Mice infected with lethal influenza received single 200 μg doses of scFv-D005-Fcγ2c at 1, 2, or 3 days post-infection.  

Therapeutic outcomes were compared to:  
- Positive control: D005 given 2 days pre-infection (100% survival)  
- Negative control: nonspecific IgG (0% survival)  

Results showed:  
- Day 1 treatment: 100% survival  
- Day 2 treatment: 83% survival  
- Day 3 treatment: delayed mortality compared to controls  

Notably, a single antibody dose administered up to 48 hours after infection provided significant protection, contrasting with previous reports requiring multiple early treatments. This demonstrates the therapeutic potential of natural human M2e-specific antibodies for early-stage influenza intervention.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

**Fully Human IgG1 Format of Antibody D005**  

For clinical translation, antibody D005 was produced as a full-length human IgG1κ (IgG1-D005) using an EBV-based expression system. The antibody expressed at high levels (~70 mg/L) in HEK293T cells and was purified by protein A chromatography.  

Comparative testing in the influenza model showed equivalent protection between:  
- scFv-D005-Fcγ2c (mouse Fc)  
- IgG1-D005 (human Fc)  

Both formats provided:  
- 100% survival against lethal challenge  
- Prevention of weight loss and hypothermia  
- Equivalent pharmacokinetics in murine serum  

These results confirm that the human IgG1 format maintains full protective activity while being suitable for human administration. The human Fc portion effectively engages murine effector mechanisms, predicting similar functionality in humans.  

### EXAMPLE 7  

**Antibody Sequence Analysis**  

The variable region sequences of antibodies D005, E040, and F052 were determined through RT-PCR and sequencing of isolated clones. All antibodies originated from the same donor but represented distinct clonal lineages, as evidenced by:  
- Unique heavy and light chain V-J rearrangements  
- Diverse complementarity-determining region (CDR) sequences  
- Different patterns of somatic hypermutation  

Despite sequence differences, all three antibodies shared:  
- High affinity for M2e  
- Cross-reactive binding patterns  
- Similar protective efficacy  

This demonstrates that multiple natural human antibody solutions exist for targeting the conserved M2e epitope, providing opportunities for antibody cocktails or backup candidates for clinical development.  

### EXAMPLE 8  

**Production Methods for Clinical-Scale Manufacturing**  

The invention encompasses scalable production methods for the antibodies. IgG1-D005 was successfully produced using:  
1. Stable HEK293 cell pools maintained under puromycin selection  
2. Serum-free culture in roller bottles or multilayer flasks  
3. Extended production periods (up to 3 weeks) with periodic harvests  
4. Protein A affinity purification achieving >95% purity  
5. Yields exceeding 70 mg/L without process optimization  

This example demonstrates the feasibility of manufacturing the antibodies at scales appropriate for clinical development and commercial production, using standard mammalian cell culture techniques.

These formulations enable flexible dosing strategies tailored to different clinical scenarios, from hospital-based IV therapy to outpatient IM administration or respiratory delivery.
