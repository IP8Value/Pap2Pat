# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of gene therapy and genome editing, particularly to methods and compositions for treating Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) through CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing of the dystrophin gene in muscle satellite cells. The invention encompasses recombinant adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors encoding CRISPR/Cas9 components, pharmaceutical compositions, administration methods, and kits for delivering these therapeutic agents to muscle tissue.  

## INTRODUCTION  

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe, progressive genetic disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene, leading to muscle degeneration and premature death. Current treatments are limited to symptomatic management, as no cure exists. Gene editing technologies, particularly CRISPR/Cas9, offer a promising therapeutic approach by correcting the underlying genetic defect. A critical challenge in DMD gene therapy is achieving sustained dystrophin expression, as muscle degeneration requires continuous regeneration from satellite cells, the muscle stem cell population.  

The invention provides a novel strategy for durable therapeutic effects by targeting satellite cells with AAV-delivered CRISPR/Cas9 systems to correct dystrophin mutations. The invention identifies optimal AAV serotypes (AAV8 and AAV9) and muscle-specific promoters (MHCK7, CK8e, SPc5-12) for efficient satellite cell transduction and gene editing. The methods enable long-term dystrophin restoration through serial muscle regeneration from corrected satellite cells, overcoming the transient effects of episomal AAV vectors.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides compositions and methods for treating DMD by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated editing of the dystrophin gene in muscle satellite cells. Key aspects include:  

1. **AAV Vectors**: Recombinant AAV8 or AAV9 vectors encoding Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 (SaCas9) and guide RNAs (gRNAs) targeting dystrophin exon 23, packaged for intramuscular or systemic delivery.  
2. **Promoters**: Muscle-specific promoters (e.g., MHCK7) drive Cas9 expression to minimize off-target effects while maintaining satellite cell activity.  
3. **Gene Editing**: Dual-AAV systems induce exon 23 excision, restoring the dystrophin reading frame in mdx mice.  
4. **Therapeutic Efficacy**: Edited satellite cells contribute to sustained dystrophin expression after serial muscle injury and transplantation, demonstrating long-term functional correction.  
5. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: Formulations for local or systemic administration, including dosage optimization and combination therapies.  
6. **Kits**: Pre-packaged AAV-CRISPR components for clinical or research use.  

The invention addresses the unmet need for durable DMD treatments by enabling permanent genetic correction in self-renewing satellite cells.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### 1. Definitions  

- **AAV**: Adeno-associated virus, a non-pathogenic viral vector for gene delivery.  
- **CRISPR/Cas9**: A genome-editing system comprising Cas9 nuclease and guide RNAs targeting specific DNA sequences.  
- **Satellite Cells**: Muscle stem cells responsible for muscle regeneration.  
- **Dystrophin**: The protein product of the DMD gene, deficient in DMD patients.  
- **mdx Mouse**: A murine model of DMD with a dystrophin exon 23 mutation.  
- **Serotype**: AAV variants with distinct tissue tropism (e.g., AAV8, AAV9).  

### 2. Dystrophin Gene  

The dystrophin gene (DMD) spans 2.2 Mb and encodes a 427 kDa protein critical for muscle membrane stability. Mutations, particularly exon deletions, disrupt the reading frame, leading to non-functional dystrophin. The invention targets exon 23 in the mdx mouse model, where its excision restores the reading frame and functional protein expression.  

### 3. CRISPR/Cas9-Based Gene Editing System  

The system comprises:  
- **SaCas9**: A compact Cas9 variant under the control of muscle-specific promoters (e.g., MHCK7).  
- **gRNAs**: Two guide RNAs flanking exon 23 to induce double-strand breaks and excision.  
- **Delivery**: Dual-AAV vectors (one for SaCas9, one for gRNAs) to accommodate size constraints.  

Editing outcomes include exon deletions, small indels, and inversions, with exon excision being therapeutically relevant.  

### 4. Genetic Constructs  

- **AAV-CMV-Cre**: For reporter studies, with Cre recombinase under the CMV promoter.  
- **AAV-MHCK7-SaCas9**: SaCas9 driven by the MHCK7 promoter for muscle-specific expression.  
- **AAV-U6-gRNAs**: gRNAs under human U6 promoters for constitutive expression.  

### 5. Pharmaceutical Compositions  

Formulations include:  
- AAV8 or AAV9 vectors at 1E12–5E12 vg/dose.  
- Buffers (e.g., PBS) for intramuscular or intravenous administration.  
- Adjuvants to enhance transduction (e.g., bFGF).  

### 6. Administration  

- **Intramuscular**: Direct injection into skeletal muscle (e.g., tibialis anterior).  
- **Systemic**: Intravenous delivery for whole-body transduction.  
- **Dosing**: Single or multiple doses, with monitoring of dystrophin expression.  

### 7. Kits  

Pre-packaged kits include:  
- AAV-SaCas9 and AAV-gRNAs.  
- Protocols for administration and analysis.  
- Controls (e.g., PBS, non-targeting gRNAs).  

### 8. Methods  

1. **Satellite Cell Transduction**: AAV8/9 delivers CRISPR components to Pax7+ satellite cells.  
2. **Gene Editing**: Exon 23 is excised, restoring dystrophin expression.  
3. **Regeneration**: Edited satellite cells repopulate muscle after injury.  
4. **Validation**: PCR, sequencing, and immunofluorescence confirm editing and dystrophin restoration.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: AAV Serotype Screening  

Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice received intramuscular injections of AAV1, 2, 5, 6.2, 8, or 9-CMV-Cre (4.72E11 vg). Flow cytometry showed AAV8 and AAV9 transduced ~60% of satellite cells, superior to other serotypes (Figure 1C). Systemic delivery (2E12 vg) confirmed AAV8/9 tropism across muscle groups (Figure 1D).  

### Example 2: Exon 23 Editing in Satellite Cells  

Pax7nGFP;mdx mice received AAV9-CMV-SaCas9 and AAV9-gRNAs intramuscularly (1E12 vg/vector). Nested PCR of sorted satellite cells detected exon 23 deletions (Figure 2B). Systemic delivery (5E12 vg/vector) also showed editing in hindlimb and diaphragm satellite cells (Figure 2C).  

### Example 3: Muscle-Specific Promoters  

AAV9-Cre with CMV, CK8e, SPc5-12, or MHCK7 promoters was injected into TA muscles. MHCK7 showed 15.6% recombination efficiency, comparable to CMV (33%) (Figure 3A). AAV9-CRISPR with MHCK7-SaCas9 yielded the highest dystrophin+ fibers (73%) (Figure 3B).  

### Example 4: Serial Injury Model  

mdx mice treated with AAV9-CMV-CRISPR underwent BaCl2-induced muscle injury every 2 weeks. Dystrophin+ fibers persisted after 3 injuries despite SaCas9 clearance (Figures 4B–D), confirming satellite cell contribution.  

### Example 5: Satellite Cell Transplantation  

GFP+ satellite cells from AAV9-CMV-CRISPR-treated donors were transplanted into irradiated mdx hosts. Host muscles showed 5.95 dystrophin+ fibers/mm2, versus 1.46 in controls (Figures 5B–C).  

### Example 6: Unbiased Sequencing  

Tn5 tagmentation quantified editing outcomes in satellite cells: exon deletions (0.05%), indels (1.5%), and inversions (0.01%) (Figure 2E). mRNA analysis showed 0.5%–1% exon 23 exclusion (Figure 2F).
