# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of cellular signaling and molecular biology, specifically to the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and Src kinase, and the development of novel peptide inhibitors targeting this interaction. The invention has broad applicability in therapeutic interventions, particularly in conditions involving aberrant Src kinase activity, such as cardiac hypertrophy, cancer, and other proliferative disorders. The disclosed compositions and methods are industrially applicable in pharmaceutical development, biotechnology, and medical research.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Src kinase is a well-known regulator of cellular signaling pathways, influencing processes such as proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Dysregulation of Src activity is implicated in numerous pathological conditions, including cancer and cardiovascular diseases. The Na+/K+-ATPase, traditionally recognized for its role in maintaining cellular ion gradients, has recently been identified as a functional partner of Src, forming a signaling complex that modulates cellular responses to extracellular stimuli, such as ouabain.  

Previous studies have demonstrated that the Na+/K+-ATPase interacts with Src through its cytoplasmic domain (CD3), inhibiting Src activity. However, the specific substructures within the Na+/K+-ATPase responsible for this interaction and inhibition remained unclear. Additionally, existing Src inhibitors, such as PP2, lack specificity and often disrupt essential cellular functions, limiting their therapeutic utility. There is a need for targeted inhibitors that selectively modulate the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex without affecting other Src-dependent pathways.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these needs by identifying the ND1 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase as the primary Src-interacting and inhibitory region. Based on this discovery, the invention provides novel peptide inhibitors, termed NaKtides, derived from the ND1 domain. These peptides exhibit high specificity and potency in inhibiting Src, particularly the pool associated with the Na+/K+-ATPase.  

The invention further encompasses cell-permeable variants of NaKtide, such as HIV-Tat-NaKtide (pNaKtide), which effectively target the plasma membrane-localized Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex. These inhibitors act as ouabain antagonists, blocking ouabain-induced Src activation and downstream signaling events, such as ERK1/2 activation and cardiac hypertrophy. The specificity of NaKtides for the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated Src pool is demonstrated by their minimal effects on basal Src activity in cells lacking this complex, contrasting with broader-spectrum inhibitors like PP2.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex  

The interaction between Src and the Na+/K+-ATPase was investigated using GST pull-down assays with purified proteins. The CD3 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit, known to interact with Src, was dissected into subdomains to identify the specific binding region. The N domain (ND) of CD3, which is exposed in the cytosol, was found to bind Src effectively. Further mapping revealed that the N-terminal portion of ND (ND1) was sufficient for Src interaction, while the remaining portion (ND1R) showed negligible binding.  

Co-immunoprecipitation and FRET analyses confirmed that ND1 interacts with Src in live cells, forming a complex localized to the plasma membrane. This interaction is critical for the regulation of Src activity, as ND1 functions as a potent inhibitor, reducing Src phosphorylation (pY418) and downstream ERK1/2 activation.  

### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay  

To assess the functional relevance of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex, RNA interference was employed to knockdown Na+/K+-ATPase expression in LLC-PK1 cells, generating TCN23-19 cells. These cells exhibited elevated basal Src and ERK1/2 activity, confirming the inhibitory role of the Na+/K+-ATPase on Src. Treatment with pNaKtide preferentially inhibited the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated Src pool in LLC-PK1 cells, while showing more pronounced effects in TCN23-19 cells, where the regulatory interaction was disrupted.  

In Src-family kinase-deficient SYF cells, pNaKtide had no effect on ERK1/2 activity, whereas in Src-rescued SYF cells (SYF + Src), it significantly reduced ERK1/2 phosphorylation. These results underscore the specificity of NaKtides for Src-dependent signaling pathways linked to the Na+/K+-ATPase.  

### Example III  

The inhibitory potency of ND1-derived peptides was evaluated in vitro. Four 20-mer peptides spanning the ND1 domain were synthesized and tested for Src inhibition. Peptide 3 (NaKtide) exhibited the highest potency, with an IC50 of ~70 nM, while peptide 4 showed partial inhibition. Peptides 1 and 2 had no effect. NaKtide also inhibited Lyn, another Src-family kinase, but with lower potency (IC50 ~2.5 µM), and showed no activity against PKC, demonstrating its selectivity for tyrosine kinases.  

### Example IV  

To enhance cellular delivery, NaKtide was conjugated to cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs), including HIV-Tat (pNaKtide) and penetratin (AP-NaKtide). pNaKtide exhibited superior plasma membrane localization and potency, with an IC50 of ~4 nM in vitro. Confocal imaging confirmed efficient cellular uptake of FITC-labeled pNaKtide, which co-localized with the plasma membrane. In contrast, AP-NaKtide accumulated in intracellular vesicles, highlighting the influence of CPP choice on subcellular distribution.  

### Example V  

The therapeutic potential of pNaKtide as an ouabain antagonist was evaluated. Ouabain binding to the Na+/K+-ATPase activates Src and downstream ERK1/2 signaling. Pretreatment with pNaKtide blocked ouabain-induced ERK1/2 activation in LLC-PK1 cells and primary cardiac myocytes. FRET analysis revealed that pNaKtide disrupted the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex in a dose-dependent manner, with an IC50 of ~1 µM in cells.  

### Example VI  

The specificity of pNaKtide was compared to PP2, a broad-spectrum Src inhibitor. While both compounds inhibited Src in vitro, pNaKtide had minimal effects on basal Src activity in LLC-PK1 and cardiac myocytes, unlike PP2. Moreover, pNaKtide did not inhibit IGF-1-induced ERK1/2 activation, whereas PP2 did, further demonstrating its selectivity for the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src pathway.  

### Example VII  

The ability of pNaKtide to prevent ouabain-induced cardiac hypertrophy was tested in neonatal rat cardiomyocytes. Ouabain treatment increased cell volume, a hallmark of hypertrophy, which was dose-dependently inhibited by pNaKtide. Complete inhibition was achieved at 2 µM, supporting its potential as a therapeutic agent for cardiac remodeling.
