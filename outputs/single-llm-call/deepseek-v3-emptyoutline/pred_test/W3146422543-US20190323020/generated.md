## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of metabolic engineering and industrial biotechnology. More specifically, the invention provides recombinant fungal strains, methods, and compositions for producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) and related compounds through engineered metabolic pathways. The invention further relates to methods of improving 3HP production yields by modifying competing metabolic pathways in fungal hosts.  

## BACKGROUND  

The production of chemicals and fuels from renewable biological feedstocks represents a critical pathway toward establishing a sustainable bioeconomy. Currently, most industrial chemicals are derived from petroleum-based feedstocks, which are non-renewable and associated with significant environmental impacts. Among the valuable chemicals that could be produced biologically is 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP), a platform chemical that serves as a precursor for acrylic acid, biodegradable plastics, and carbon fibers.  

While various microorganisms have been engineered to produce 3HP through different metabolic pathways, existing production systems face limitations in yield, pH tolerance, and feedstock utilization. Filamentous fungi, particularly Aspergillus species, are known for their ability to produce organic acids at low pH and their capacity to utilize diverse carbon sources. However, prior to this invention, 3HP production had not been successfully demonstrated in filamentous fungi, nor had the metabolic challenges associated with 3HP degradation in these organisms been addressed.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides engineered fungal strains, particularly Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains, capable of producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) through a recombinant beta-alanine pathway. The invention further discloses methods for improving 3HP production yields by disrupting competing metabolic pathways, particularly the malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase pathway that leads to 3HP degradation.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Recombinant ΔcadA fungi engineered to express heterologous enzymes for 3HP production, including aspartate 1-decarboxylase (pand), beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase (bapat), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (hpdh).  
2. Methods of producing 3HP through cultivation of the engineered fungal strains under conditions that favor growth-associated production.  
3. Methods of increasing 3HP yields by disrupting the malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene (Apald6) to prevent 3HP degradation.  
4. Compositions and kits containing the engineered fungal strains for industrial 3HP production.  

The invention demonstrates that filamentous fungi can be effectively engineered for 3HP production at low pH, with yields significantly improved through targeted genetic modifications informed by multi-omics analysis.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Overview  

The present invention provides engineered fungal strains and methods for the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) through metabolic pathway engineering. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that filamentous fungi, particularly Aspergillus pseudoterreus, can be effectively engineered to produce 3HP through a recombinant beta-alanine pathway, and that production yields can be dramatically improved by disrupting competing metabolic pathways identified through multi-omics analysis.  

The engineered strains of the invention incorporate several key modifications: (1) disruption of the native cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cad1) gene to eliminate competing itaconic acid production; (2) introduction of a heterologous beta-alanine pathway for 3HP biosynthesis; and (3) targeted deletion of malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6) to prevent 3HP degradation. These modifications, combined with optimized cultivation conditions, enable efficient production of 3HP at low pH, where the product exists primarily in the free acid form advantageous for downstream processing.  

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi  

The invention provides recombinant fungal strains, particularly Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains, engineered for improved production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid. A key feature of these strains is the disruption of the cad1 gene encoding cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase, which eliminates production of itaconic acid and redirects metabolic flux toward 3HP production.  

The ΔcadA strains are further engineered to express a heterologous beta-alanine pathway comprising three key enzymes: (1) aspartate 1-decarboxylase (e.g., from Tribolium castaneum, TcPAND); (2) beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase (e.g., from Bacillus cereus, BcBAPAT); and (3) 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (e.g., from Escherichia coli, EcHPDH). These enzymes are expressed under the control of strong fungal promoters, such as the gpdA and enoA promoters, to ensure high-level expression.  

In preferred embodiments, the engineered strains contain multiple copies of the beta-alanine pathway genes integrated at different genomic locations. For example, one copy may be integrated at the cad1 locus, with additional copies integrated elsewhere in the genome. This multicopy integration has been shown to significantly increase 3HP production yields compared to single-copy strains.  

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid  

While the primary focus of the invention is 3HP production, the engineered strains also produce aconitic acid as a byproduct during certain cultivation conditions. The methods of the invention include strategies for modulating aconitic acid production through control of cultivation parameters.  

During bioreactor cultivation, it was observed that 3HP production occurs primarily during the growth phase, while production of organic acids including aconitic acid begins when biomass production slows. By maintaining conditions that favor growth-associated production (e.g., through pH control, aeration, and nutrient feeding), it is possible to extend the period of 3HP production while minimizing aconitic acid accumulation.  

The invention further provides methods for separating 3HP from aconitic acid and other organic acids in the fermentation broth, taking advantage of their different physicochemical properties and the low pH conditions maintained during fermentation.  

### Methods of Producing 3-HP  

The invention provides optimized methods for producing 3HP using the engineered fungal strains. Key aspects of these methods include:  

1. Cultivation Conditions: The engineered strains are cultivated in minimal medium containing glucose as the primary carbon source, with the pH maintained at approximately 2.8 through addition of base (e.g., KOH). This low pH favors production of 3HP in its free acid form and inhibits competing metabolic pathways.  

2. Scale-up: The methods are applicable across scales from shake flasks to industrial bioreactors. In bioreactor cultivations, aeration is maintained at approximately 0.4 vessel volumes per minute, with stirring rates adjusted based on culture density and pH.  

3. Process Monitoring: The methods incorporate real-time monitoring of key parameters including dissolved oxygen, CO2 evolution, and extracellular metabolite concentrations to optimize production conditions.  

4. Harvesting: 3HP is harvested during the growth phase, typically between 40-70 hours of cultivation, before significant degradation occurs. In strains where the Apald6 gene has been deleted, the harvesting window can be extended as 3HP degradation is minimized.  

The methods have been shown to produce 3HP at titers up to 0.88 g/L in small-scale bioreactors, with significantly higher yields possible through further optimization of cultivation conditions and strain engineering.  

### Compositions and Kits  

The invention further provides compositions and kits for practicing the methods of 3HP production. These include:  

1. Engineered fungal strain compositions, comprising Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains with the Δcad1 genotype and heterologous beta-alanine pathway genes, optionally further comprising the ΔApald6 genotype.  

2. Cultivation kits containing pre-measured media components for growing the engineered strains under optimized conditions for 3HP production.  

3. Bioreactor systems pre-configured for 3HP production, incorporating pH control, aeration, and monitoring systems tailored for the engineered strains.  

4. Purification kits containing materials and protocols for separating 3HP from fermentation broths, particularly at low pH where the free acid form predominates.  

These compositions and kits facilitate the adoption of the invention for both research-scale and industrial-scale 3HP production.  

### Example 1  

**Construction of 3HP-producing Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains**  

The cad1 gene of Aspergillus pseudoterreus ATCC® 32359TM was disrupted by integration of a synthetic beta-alanine pathway construct. The construct was assembled by Gibson assembly and contained the following elements: (1) the gpdA promoter from Aspergillus niger; (2) the codon-optimized TcPAND gene; (3) the A. niger elf3 transcriptional terminator; (4) the codon-optimized BcBAPAT gene; and (5) the A. niger eno1 promoter.  

The assembled construct was integrated at the cad1 locus via protoplast transformation, generating strain ABF_004528_2 (cad:3HP+). A second strain, ABF_004528_6 (cad:3HP+, 3HP+), was generated with an additional copy of the beta-alanine pathway integrated elsewhere in the genome.  

Both strains were cultivated in minimal medium (100 g/L glucose, pH 3.4) at 30°C with shaking. After 8 days, strain ABF_004528_2 produced 0.15 g/L 3HP, while strain ABF_004528_6 produced 0.28 g/L, demonstrating the benefit of multicopy integration.  

### Example 2  

**Multi-omics analysis of 3HP-producing strains**  

The 3HP-producing strains from Example 1 were subjected to multi-omics analysis during an 8-day production period. Global proteomics detected 4,674 proteins, while targeted proteomics quantified 79 central metabolic proteins. Extracellular and intracellular metabolomics were performed concurrently.  

Key findings included:  
1. The multicopy strain showed 1.66-fold higher expression of the heterologous pathway enzymes.  
2. Intracellular beta-alanine levels increased 148-249 fold in engineered strains.  
3. A novel metabolite, 2,4-diaminobutanoate, accumulated in engineered strains (41-131 fold increase).  
4. Malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6) was significantly upregulated in engineered strains.  

This analysis revealed metabolic bottlenecks and competing pathways that informed further strain engineering.  

### Example 3  

**Scale-up of 3HP production in bioreactors**  

Strain ABF_004528_6 was cultivated in a 30 L bioreactor with pH controlled at 2.8. 3HP production began during exponential growth, reaching a maximum titer of 0.69 g/L at 67 hours, followed by decline to 0.17 g/L by 120 hours.  

Analysis revealed that 3HP degradation coincided with onset of organic acid production. Follow-up experiments showed that 3HP is stable in acidic medium without cells, but is degraded biotically, suggesting active metabolism by the fungus.  

Flux balance analysis using a modified A. niger metabolic model predicted that 3HP could be degraded via malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6), prompting construction of a ΔApald6 strain.  

### Example 4  

**Improved 3HP production in ΔApald6 strain**  

The Apald6 gene was deleted in strain ABF_004528_6 using Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, creating strain ABF_008339.  

In shake flasks, the ΔApald6 strain showed 3.4-fold higher 3HP yield than the parent strain. In 0.5 L bioreactors, the parent strain produced 0.27 g/L 3HP by day 7, while the ΔApald6 strain produced 0.88 g/L (3.3-fold improvement).  

The ΔApald6 strain showed reduced production of organic acid byproducts and extended 3HP production, demonstrating the effectiveness of targeting competing metabolic pathways identified through multi-omics analysis.
