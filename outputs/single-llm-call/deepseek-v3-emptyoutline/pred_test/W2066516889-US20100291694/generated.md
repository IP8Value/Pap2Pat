# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The complement system is a critical component of innate immunity, playing a pivotal role in host defense against pathogens, clearance of immune complexes, and modulation of adaptive immune responses. However, dysregulation of complement activation has been implicated in numerous pathological conditions, including ischemia-reperfusion injury, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis, cardiopulmonary bypass, asthma, autoimmune diseases, and age-related macular degeneration.  

Current therapeutic strategies targeting the complement system face significant challenges, including systemic effects that may compromise host defense mechanisms. Existing inhibitors often target circulating complement components indiscriminately, leading to broad suppression of complement activity rather than selective inhibition of pathological activation pathways. There remains an unmet need for targeted complement inhibitors that specifically modulate the alternative pathway while preserving other complement functions essential for immune surveillance.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel antibodies that specifically bind to activated forms of complement component C3, particularly C3b, iC3b, and C3c, while showing minimal or no binding to native C3. These antibodies demonstrate selective inhibition of the alternative complement pathway without affecting classical pathway activation.  

In particular embodiments, the invention provides an antibody designated S77 that binds to an epitope formed by conformational changes occurring during C3 activation. The antibody recognizes surfaces on both the MG6 and MG7 domains of C3b that become accessible only after C3 cleavage. This selective binding allows the antibody to interfere with formation of both C3 and C5 convertases of the alternative pathway while maintaining classical pathway function.  

The antibodies of the invention exhibit several advantageous properties: (1) specific recognition of activated C3 fragments rather than native C3; (2) inhibition of alternative pathway convertase formation and activity; (3) preservation of classical pathway complement function; and (4) high affinity binding to human C3b with minimal cross-reactivity to non-human species. These properties make the antibodies particularly suitable for therapeutic applications where selective complement inhibition is desired.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

### Definitions  

As used in this specification, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"C3b" refers to the proteolytically activated form of complement component C3 that results from cleavage by C3 convertase, comprising the α' and β chains of C3.  

"iC3b" refers to the further processed form of C3b resulting from factor I-mediated cleavage in the presence of cofactors such as factor H or CR1.  

"C3c" refers to the major fragment produced from iC3b through additional proteolytic processing.  

"Alternative pathway" refers to the complement activation pathway that is initiated spontaneously through hydrolysis of C3 and is amplified through the C3bBb convertase.  

"Classical pathway" refers to the complement activation pathway initiated by antibody-antigen complexes or other pattern recognition molecules leading to formation of the C4b2a convertase.  

### MODES OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention provides antibodies that specifically bind to activated forms of C3, particularly C3b, iC3b, and C3c, while showing minimal binding to native C3. These antibodies are generated through a subtractive phage display approach that selects for binders to C3b while counter-selecting against binders to native C3.  

The preferred embodiment involves an antibody designated S77 that was affinity-matured from a parental clone (YW144.2.45) through combinatorial mutagenesis of complementarity-determining regions (CDRs). The mature S77 antibody contains seven amino acid changes in the heavy chain CDRs and two changes in the light chain CDR3 compared to the parental antibody, resulting in approximately 10-fold higher affinity.  

The antibodies of the invention are particularly useful for inhibiting alternative pathway complement activation while preserving classical pathway function. This is achieved through several mechanisms: (1) blocking factor B binding to C3b, preventing formation of the C3 convertase (C3bBb); (2) inhibiting C5 binding to C3b multimers, preventing formation of the C5 convertase; and (3) interfering with factor H and CR1 binding to C3b, reducing cofactor activity for factor I-mediated C3b processing.  

### Example 1  

**Generation and Characterization of C3b-Specific Antibodies**  

C3b-specific antibodies were generated using a subtractive phage display approach. Human synthetic phage antibody libraries displaying bivalent Fab fragments were panned against C3b-coated plates while pre-absorbing with soluble C3 to remove clones binding to native C3. After multiple rounds of selection with increasing stringency, phage antibodies specifically binding C3b but not C3 were identified.  

The selected Fab sequences were grafted onto a human IgG1 framework and expressed in mammalian cells. Initial characterization identified clone YW144.2.45 with a binding affinity (KD) of 160 nM for C3b as measured by surface plasmon resonance. This clone showed minimal binding to native C3.  

### CRIg Variants  

For comparison purposes, the binding properties of CRIg (complement receptor of the immunoglobulin family) were examined. While both S77 and CRIg bind C3b, iC3b, and C3c, they recognize distinct epitopes on C3b. CRIg primarily binds residues on the β-chain of C3b, while S77 binds predominantly to the α-chain. Despite these differences, both molecules bind to the same general surface of C3b opposite the TED domain, suggesting this region is critical for convertase function.  

### Example 3  

**Affinity Maturation of S77 Antibody**  

The parental YW144.2.45 antibody was subjected to affinity maturation through combinatorial mutagenesis of CDR regions. Three different CDR combinatorial libraries were created by randomizing selected amino acids in CDRs 1, 2, and 3 of both heavy and light chains.  

After six rounds of high-stringency selection, phage antibodies with improved affinity were reformatted to full-length IgGs. Clone YW144.2.45.S77 (S77 IgG) showed the highest affinity with a KD of 1.2-2.2 nM for various C3b fragments as measured by surface plasmon resonance. S77 IgG contained seven amino acid changes in heavy chain CDRs and two changes in light chain CDR3 compared to the parental antibody.  

### Example 4  

**Structural Basis for C3b Specificity**  

The crystal structure of S77 Fab in complex with C3b was determined at 3.1 Å resolution. The structure revealed that S77 binds to an epitope formed by both MG6 and MG7 domains of C3b, with the bulk of interactions mediated by heavy chain CDRs.  

Key interactions include:  
1) Phe-898 of C3b inserting into a hydrophobic pocket formed by CDR-H3 and CDR-L2 of the Fab  
2) His-897 of C3b forming hydrogen bonds with CDR-H1 and CDR-H3  
3) Total buried surface area of approximately 1930 Å²  

Comparison with C3 structure showed that a ~15° rotation of MG6 relative to MG7 during C3 activation creates the S77 binding epitope. In native C3, steric clashes prevent S77 binding, explaining its specificity for activated forms.  

### Example 5  

**Functional Characterization of S77**  

S77 demonstrated potent inhibition of alternative pathway complement activation through multiple mechanisms:  

1) Inhibition of factor H and CR1 binding to C3b (IC50 ~10 nM)  
2) Blockade of factor B binding to C3b, preventing C3 convertase formation  
3) Inhibition of C5 binding to C3b multimers, preventing C5 convertase activity  
4) No effect on pre-formed C3 convertase stability or properdin binding  

Importantly, S77 showed selective inhibition of alternative pathway in human serum (IC50 ~20 nM) while having no effect on classical pathway activation at concentrations up to 1 μM. The antibody also showed species specificity, binding human but not rhesus C3b due to a His897Phe substitution in the latter.  

These properties make S77 an ideal candidate for therapeutic applications requiring selective alternative pathway inhibition while preserving classical pathway function and host defense mechanisms.
