# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of photovoltaic devices, specifically to perovskite solar cells with enhanced stability and efficiency. More particularly, the invention discloses a novel hole transport layer (HTL) structure comprising sequentially deposited n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers, which significantly improves device performance and environmental stability. The invention further provides methods for optimizing doping concentrations and deposition conditions to achieve energy level alignment between the perovskite absorber and hole transport materials, thereby minimizing charge carrier injection barriers.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Perovskite solar cells have emerged as a promising photovoltaic technology due to their high power conversion efficiencies and low-cost solution processability. However, their commercialization has been hindered by stability issues, particularly degradation caused by environmental factors such as moisture and oxygen. Conventional hole transport layers, typically deposited via spin-coating, often contain pinholes that allow permeation of deleterious atmospheric components into the perovskite active layer.  

Previous attempts to address these stability challenges have focused on compositional engineering of the perovskite material or encapsulation techniques. While these approaches have shown some success, they fail to address the fundamental issue of pinhole formation in the hole transport layer. Additionally, conventional doped spiro-OMeTAD layers often exhibit suboptimal energy level alignment with the perovskite valence band, leading to inefficient hole extraction and reduced open-circuit voltages.  

Vacuum deposition of organic layers is known to produce denser, pinhole-free films compared to solution processing. However, the application of vacuum-deposited, multi-layer doped structures in perovskite solar cells has not been previously demonstrated, particularly with precise control over doping concentrations to achieve optimal energy level alignment. The present invention overcomes these limitations through a novel n-i-p structured hole transport layer that combines the benefits of vacuum deposition with precisely controlled doping engineering.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a perovskite solar cell comprising a novel hole transport layer structure that significantly improves both device efficiency and long-term stability. The key innovation lies in the vacuum-deposited triple-layer structure consisting of: (1) an n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer adjacent to the perovskite absorber, (2) an intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD intermediate layer, and (3) a p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD outer layer.  

The n-type doping is achieved using dimethyl carbonate (DMC) as a dopant, with the doping concentration precisely controlled through vapor pressure adjustment during vacuum deposition. Optimal doping conditions are determined by matching the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD to the valence band maximum of the perovskite, typically achieved at a DMC vapor pressure of approximately 1.0 × 10–5 Torr. The intrinsic layer serves as a buffer to prevent dopant interdiffusion while maintaining efficient charge transport. The p-type doping is accomplished using F4-TCNQ as a dopant, with a concentration of approximately 2 wt.% to achieve optimal Fermi level positioning.  

This multi-layer structure provides several advantages: (1) pinhole-free coverage that protects the perovskite from environmental degradation, (2) optimized energy level alignment that enhances hole extraction efficiency while maintaining high open-circuit voltage, and (3) improved long-term stability through dopant redistribution effects that actually enhance device performance over time. Experimental results demonstrate that devices incorporating this invention maintain stable performance for over 800 hours in ambient air conditions, with some devices showing improved efficiency compared to initial values.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention will now be described in detail with reference to specific embodiments and experimental results. It should be understood that these embodiments are provided by way of illustration only and do not limit the scope of the invention.  

In a preferred embodiment, the perovskite solar cell structure comprises, in sequence: a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) transparent conductive substrate, a compact TiO2 electron transport layer, a methylammonium lead iodide perovskite absorber layer, the novel n-i-p structured hole transport layer, and a gold top electrode. The hole transport layer consists of three distinct sub-layers deposited by vacuum evaporation:  

The n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited first, adjacent to the perovskite surface. This layer is approximately 30 nm thick and is doped with DMC at a vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10–5 Torr during deposition, which produces optimal energy level alignment. The DMC dopant is introduced by heating DMC powder to approximately 100°C in a vacuum chamber with a base pressure of 1.0 × 10–8 Torr. The deposition rate of spiro-OMeTAD is maintained at 0.5 Å/s as measured by a quartz crystal microbalance.  

The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited immediately following the n-type layer in the same vacuum chamber, without breaking vacuum. This layer serves as a buffer to prevent dopant interdiffusion between the n-type and p-type regions while maintaining efficient hole transport. The intrinsic layer has a thickness of approximately 20 nm and is deposited at the same rate as the n-type layer.  

The p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited in a separate vacuum chamber to prevent contamination of the doping sources. This layer is approximately 30 nm thick and is co-evaporated with F4-TCNQ at a deposition rate of 0.1 Å/s (corresponding to 2 wt.% doping concentration) while maintaining the spiro-OMeTAD deposition rate at 0.5 Å/s.  

Energy level measurements using ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) confirm that this structure creates a favorable energy level alignment. The valence band maximum of the perovskite is measured at 2.6 eV below the Fermi level, while the HOMO level of the optimally doped n-type spiro-OMeTAD matches this value at 1.0 × 10–5 Torr DMC vapor pressure. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD shows a HOMO level at 1.5 eV below the Fermi level, while the p-type doped layer exhibits a HOMO level at 0.3 eV below the Fermi level. This staircase energy level profile enhances hole extraction efficiency while maintaining a high built-in potential.  

Device performance testing demonstrates significant improvements in both efficiency and stability. Fresh devices show power conversion efficiencies comparable to conventional spin-coated devices, but with dramatically improved stability. After 840 hours of storage in ambient air, devices incorporating the invention show no degradation in photocurrent and actually exhibit improved open-circuit voltage and fill factor compared to initial values. This improvement is attributed to dopant redistribution effects within the multi-layer structure.  

The invention further encompasses variations in layer thicknesses, doping concentrations, and deposition parameters. For example, the DMC vapor pressure may range from 1.0 × 10–6 to 5.0 × 10–5 Torr, with the optimal range being 5.0 × 10–6 to 1.0 × 10–5 Torr. The F4-TCNQ doping concentration may vary from 1 to 5 wt.%, with 2 wt.% being optimal. Layer thicknesses may range from 10-50 nm for each sub-layer, with total HTL thickness preferably between 60-100 nm.  

The deposition process may be performed in various vacuum systems, including but not limited to thermal evaporation systems, molecular beam epitaxy systems, or organic molecular beam deposition systems. The substrate temperature during deposition may range from room temperature to 100°C, with higher temperatures potentially improving film morphology.  

The invention also encompasses alternative perovskite compositions beyond methylammonium lead iodide, including formamidinium-based perovskites, mixed cation/halide perovskites, and lead-free perovskite analogues. The compact TiO2 layer may be replaced with other electron transport materials such as SnO2 or ZnO. The gold electrode may be substituted with other conductive materials including silver, aluminum, or conductive polymers.  

The described embodiments demonstrate that vacuum-deposited, multi-layer doped hole transport structures represent a significant advancement in perovskite solar cell technology, addressing both efficiency and stability challenges simultaneously. The precise control over doping concentrations and layer sequencing enables optimal energy level alignment while providing superior environmental protection compared to conventional solution-processed alternatives.
