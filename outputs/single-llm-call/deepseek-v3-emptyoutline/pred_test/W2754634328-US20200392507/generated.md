# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for reducing hypertrophic scarring and fibrosis by simultaneously silencing the expression of transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) genes. More specifically, the invention provides a therapeutic formulation comprising small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2, packaged within a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) nanoparticle delivery system. The invention further encompasses methods for treating hypertrophic scars, skin grafts, and fibrotic conditions by administering the nanoparticle formulation via intradermal injection to induce apoptosis of fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, thereby reducing scar size and improving tissue remodeling.  

## BACKGROUND  

Hypertrophic scarring is a common pathological outcome of wound healing, characterized by excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, hypercellularity, and persistent inflammation. Current treatments, including surgical excision, corticosteroid injections, and laser therapy, exhibit limited efficacy and often fail to prevent recurrence. The molecular mechanisms underlying hypertrophic scar formation involve dysregulated expression of profibrotic mediators, particularly TGF-β1, which promotes fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis. COX-2, another key player in fibrosis, contributes to inflammation and ECM remodeling, though its role remains controversial due to conflicting reports of pro- and anti-fibrotic effects.  

Prior attempts to target TGF-β1 or COX-2 individually have yielded suboptimal results, as compensatory pathways often mitigate therapeutic effects. Additionally, siRNA-based therapies face significant delivery challenges due to poor tissue penetration and rapid degradation. Existing delivery systems, such as liposomes and viral vectors, are hindered by toxicity, immunogenicity, or limited scalability. There remains an unmet need for a safe, efficient, and synergistic approach to silence multiple fibrotic targets while overcoming delivery barriers.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention addresses these limitations by providing a dual-targeted siRNA therapy packaged within a biodegradable HKP nanoparticle. The formulation comprises:  

1. **siRNA Duplexes**: Two siRNA sequences specifically designed to silence human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA, respectively. These siRNAs exhibit high silencing efficiency and minimal off-target effects, as confirmed by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) and Western blot analyses.  

2. **HKP Nanoparticle Delivery System**: A histidine-lysine co-polymer that self-assembles with siRNA into stable nanoparticles (~150 nm diameter) through electrostatic interactions. The HKP enhances cellular uptake, endosomal escape, and sustained release of siRNA in vivo, as demonstrated by fluorescence microscopy and qRT-PCR kinetics.  

3. **Therapeutic Mechanism**: Simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 disrupts interconnected profibrotic pathways, leading to:  
   - Downregulation of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), collagen types I and III, and hydroxyproline.  
   - Induction of fibroblast apoptosis, as evidenced by terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assays and flow cytometry.  
   - Reduction in scar size by up to 45% in human hypertrophic scar implant models.  

The invention further includes methods for treating hypertrophic scars or fibrotic skin grafts by administering the HKP-siRNA formulation via intradermal injection at a dose of 20 μg/50 μL/cm³, repeated at 5-day intervals. The treatment regimen is scalable and compatible with clinical use, as the lyophilized nanoparticle formulation retains stability and potency for at least 12 months at 4°C.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions  

Human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs) were transfected with HKP-packaged TGF-β1siRNA, COX-2siRNA, or a combination of both (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs). qRT-PCR revealed synergistic knockdown of both targets (>60% reduction) in the combination group, whereas individual siRNAs achieved ≤40% silencing. Notably, TGF-β1siRNA also reduced COX-2 expression (30%), and vice versa, confirming crosstalk between the pathways. Western blot analysis showed concomitant downregulation of SMAD2/3 phosphorylation and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), key mediators of TGF-β signaling.  

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts  

Flow cytometry (Annexin V/PI staining) demonstrated a 3-fold increase in apoptotic HSFs treated with TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs compared to controls (p < 0.01). Immunofluorescence staining revealed reduced α-SMA expression and cytoskeletal disassembly, indicative of myofibroblast de-differentiation. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed apoptotic ultrastructural changes, including chromatin condensation and membrane blebbing.  

### Example 3. HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar  

Alexa Fluor-labeled siRNA, delivered via HKP nanoparticles or naked injection, was tracked in human scar implants. Fluorescence microscopy showed HKP nanoparticles retained siRNA for 48 hours post-injection, whereas naked siRNA dispersed within 24 hours. qRT-PCR of treated scars revealed dose-dependent TGF-β1 and COX-2 silencing, with maximal inhibition (55–70%) at 96 hours.  

### Example 4. HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar  

Human scar implants treated with HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) exhibited a 45% reduction in volume (p < 0.001) by day 28, accompanied by decreased collagen I/III mRNA (50–60%) and hydroxyproline content (40%). Histology (Masson’s trichrome) showed normalized collagen architecture, and immunohistochemistry confirmed reduced α-SMA and VEGF expression.  

### Example 5. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts  

Skin grafts treated with the same regimen showed a 40% reduction in size (p < 0.01), with parallel declines in TGF-β1, COX-2, and ECM markers. Apoptotic fibroblasts were abundant in TUNEL-stained sections, supporting the mechanism observed in vitro.  

### Example 6. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action  

Transcriptomic analysis of treated scars revealed downregulation of fibrogenic genes (e.g., CTGF, TIMP1) and upregulation of matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-1, MMP-3). This shift toward ECM degradation was unique to dual-targeted therapy, as single-siRNA treatments failed to alter MMP expression.  

### Example 7. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo  

TUNEL assays quantified apoptosis in scar tissues, showing a 4-fold increase in apoptotic cells with TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs (p < 0.001) versus controls. Co-staining with α-SMA confirmed myofibroblast-specific apoptosis, explaining the dramatic scar reduction.  

### Example 8. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent  

Comparative studies with clinical candidates (EXC001, RXI109) demonstrated HKP-siRNA’s superior potency (10–100-fold lower dose required) and safety (no local or systemic toxicity in murine models). Scalable GMP-compliant production of the nanoparticle formulation was validated.  

## Discussion  

The invention provides the first evidence that dual silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 induces fibroblast apoptosis and reverses established hypertrophic scars. Unlike monotherapies, the combination disrupts redundant fibrotic pathways, overcoming compensatory mechanisms. The HKP delivery system addresses key siRNA delivery challenges, enabling clinical translation. Broader applications in fibrosis (e.g., liver, lung) are anticipated, given the conserved roles of TGF-β1 and COX-2 across tissues.  

## Materials and Methods  

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection  

Twenty-five-mer siRNA duplexes targeting conserved regions of human TGF-β1 (NM_000660) and COX-2 (NM_000963) were designed using Dharmacon’s algorithm. Eight candidates per gene were screened in PC3 cells; the top-performing sequences (Table 1) were selected based on >70% silencing efficiency (qRT-PCR) and minimal off-target effects (transcriptome analysis).  

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro  

HSFs were treated with HKP-siRNAs (10 μg/mL) for 48 hours. Viability (MTT assay), apoptosis (Annexin V/PI), and cell cycle (propidium iodide) were analyzed by flow cytometry.  

### Nanoparticle Preparation  

HKP (H3K4b) and siRNA (4:1 mass ratio) were mixed in RNase-free water, vortexed, and incubated (25°C, 30 min). Nanoparticles were characterized by dynamic light scattering (size, zeta potential) and SEM.  

### Mouse  

Nude mice (Balb/c nu/nu, 6–8 weeks) were housed under SPF conditions. All procedures adhered to Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital IACUC guidelines.  

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models  

Scar or skin tissues (2 cm²) were implanted subcutaneously in mice. Four weeks post-implantation, HKP-siRNAs (20 μg/50 μL/cm³) were injected intradermally at five sites per implant, repeated thrice at 5-day intervals.  

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models  

Scar size (caliper), histology (H&E, Masson’s), gene expression (qRT-PCR), and protein levels (Western blot, IHC) were assessed at days 1, 3, 5, and 28 post-treatment.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Data are mean ± SD (in vitro) or ± SEM (in vivo). Significance (p < 0.05) was determined by Student’s t-test (two-group) or ANOVA (multi-group) using SPSS v20.
