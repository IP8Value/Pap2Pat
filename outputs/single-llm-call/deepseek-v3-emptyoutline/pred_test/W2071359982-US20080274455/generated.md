# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Schizophrenia represents a severe neuropsychiatric disorder characterized by disturbances across cognitive, emotional, and motor processes. The dopaminergic system's dysfunction has been implicated as a primary contributor to the development of this disease. Prior research has established that neurotransmitters, neuropeptides, and specific dopamine receptor genes, particularly the D2 subclass (including D2, D3, and D4 subtypes), are expressed in immune cells, notably peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL). These peripheral expressions have been hypothesized to mirror the status of corresponding receptors in the brain.  

Recent investigations have identified upregulation of the D3 subtype of dopamine receptor genes (DRD3) in the PBL of schizophrenic patients, suggesting its utility as a peripheral marker for schizophrenia diagnosis and monitoring. Additionally, decreased mRNA levels of the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7AChR) gene have been reported in schizophrenic PBL. Traditional methodologies for analyzing these expression profiles have relied on dopaminergic ligand binding assays or quantitative RT-PCR (QRT-PCR) to detect RNA levels.  

Advancements in molecular techniques, such as high-throughput microarray analysis and precise QRT-PCR, have enabled more comprehensive gene expression profiling. These methods have been applied to postmortem schizophrenic brain tissue, revealing numerous gene expression alterations. However, the identification of reliable peripheral molecular genetic markers remains critical for early diagnosis, disease monitoring, and therapeutic intervention in schizophrenia.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses a novel method for diagnosing schizophrenia by detecting elevated expression levels of the dopamine receptor D2 (DRD2) gene and the inwardly rectifying potassium channel Kir2.3 gene in peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL). The invention utilizes microarray analysis and quantitative real-time PCR (QRT-PCR) to measure the relative mRNA levels of DRD2 and Kir2.3 in PBL samples obtained from drug-naïve or drug-free schizophrenic patients compared to healthy controls.  

The method involves isolating PBL from blood samples, extracting RNA, and analyzing gene expression through cDNA microarrays and QRT-PCR with SybrGreen dye or TaqMan probes. The invention establishes specific threshold values for DRD2 and Kir2.3 mRNA levels, wherein expression ratios exceeding 2.56-fold for DRD2 and 3.54-fold for Kir2.3 (using SybrGreen) or 2.42-fold for DRD2 and 2.92-fold for Kir2.3 (using TaqMan probes) indicate a positive diagnosis of schizophrenia.  

This diagnostic approach provides a non-invasive, peripheral marker system that reflects central nervous system pathology, enabling early and accurate identification of schizophrenia without confounding effects from antipsychotic medications.  

## DEFINITIONS  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed below:  

- **Peripheral Blood Lymphocytes (PBL):** White blood cells, including T cells and B cells, isolated from peripheral blood samples.  
- **Drug-naïve/drug-free schizophrenic patients:** Individuals diagnosed with schizophrenia who have not been exposed to antipsychotic medications or other drugs that may alter gene expression.  
- **Microarray analysis:** A high-throughput technique for simultaneously measuring the expression levels of thousands of genes using cDNA or oligonucleotide arrays.  
- **Quantitative Real-Time PCR (QRT-PCR):** A molecular biology technique that amplifies and quantifies specific DNA sequences in real time, enabling precise measurement of gene expression levels.  
- **SybrGreen dye:** A fluorescent DNA-binding dye used in QRT-PCR to detect amplified DNA products.  
- **TaqMan probes:** Dual-labeled oligonucleotide probes used in QRT-PCR that emit fluorescence upon cleavage during amplification, providing highly specific detection of target sequences.  
- **DRD2 gene:** The gene encoding the dopamine receptor D2, a G protein-coupled receptor that inhibits adenylyl cyclase activity.  
- **Kir2.3 gene:** The gene encoding the inwardly rectifying potassium channel 2.3, which regulates potassium ion conductance in excitable cells.  
- **Fold change:** The ratio of gene expression levels in test samples compared to control samples, calculated using normalized threshold cycle (Ct) values.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for diagnosing schizophrenia by analyzing the expression levels of DRD2 and Kir2.3 genes in PBL. The method comprises the following steps:  

1. **Sample Collection and Preparation:** Peripheral blood samples are collected from subjects in EDTA-treated tubes to prevent coagulation. The lymphocyte fraction is isolated by lysing erythrocytes using a red blood cell lysis buffer (e.g., 5 mM MgCl2, 10 mM NaCl, 10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.0). The resulting PBL pellet is subjected to RNA extraction using commercially available kits (e.g., Macherey-Nagel RNA isolation kit). RNA quality and quantity are assessed via electrophoresis and spectrophotometry (e.g., NanoDrop).  

2. **Microarray Analysis:** Total RNA (e.g., 2 µg) from pooled PBL samples of schizophrenic patients and healthy controls is reverse-transcribed into cDNA using poly-dT primers. The cDNA is hybridized onto a human cDNA microarray containing thousands of gene sequences. Differential gene expression is detected using fluorescent labeling (e.g., Cy5 for test samples, Cy3 for controls). Microarray data is analyzed to identify genes with at least a two-fold change in expression, with DRD2 and Kir2.3 emerging as consistently upregulated in schizophrenic PBL.  

3. **QRT-PCR Validation:** Individual RNA samples from patients and controls are reverse-transcribed using random hexamer primers and SuperScript II reverse transcriptase. QRT-PCR is performed using SybrGreen or TaqMan probes with gene-specific primers for DRD2 and Kir2.3. Internal control genes (e.g., β-actin or HPRT) are used for normalization. Relative quantification is performed using the Pfaffl method, which accounts for differences in amplification efficiency.  

4. **Diagnostic Thresholds:** For SybrGreen-based QRT-PCR, DRD2 expression ratios >2.56-fold and Kir2.3 ratios >3.54-fold relative to controls are indicative of schizophrenia. For TaqMan probes, thresholds are set at >2.42-fold for DRD2 and >2.92-fold for Kir2.3. Samples exceeding these thresholds are classified as positive for schizophrenia.  

5. **Statistical Analysis:** Gene expression data is analyzed using hierarchical clustering (e.g., agglomerative clustering with Euclidean metric) to group samples based on similarity. Unpaired Student’s t-tests (two-tailed) are applied to determine statistical significance (p < 0.05).  

The invention further encompasses kits for performing the diagnostic method, including primers and probes for DRD2 and Kir2.3, reagents for RNA extraction and reverse transcription, and standardized control samples.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Microarray Screening of Schizophrenic PBL**  
A cDNA microarray containing 3,200 human genes was hybridized with cDNA derived from PBL RNA pools of 13 drug-naïve schizophrenic patients and 10 healthy controls. Microarray analysis revealed a 2.56 ± 0.26-fold increase in DRD2 expression and a 6.75 ± 2.3-fold increase in Kir2.3 expression in schizophrenic patients compared to controls.  

**Example 2: QRT-PCR Confirmation of DRD2 Overexpression**  
Individual PBL RNA samples from 13 schizophrenic patients and 10 controls were analyzed by SybrGreen QRT-PCR. DRD2 expression was elevated 5.7- to 64.7-fold in 12 patients (p = 0.0019). TaqMan probe analysis confirmed DRD2 overexpression (3.7- to 24.1-fold) in 11 patients (p = 0.0023).  

**Example 3: QRT-PCR Confirmation of Kir2.3 Overexpression**  
SybrGreen QRT-PCR detected Kir2.3 overexpression (4- to 133.6-fold) in 12 patients (p = 0.0535). TaqMan probes validated these results, showing 2.8- to 15.8-fold increases in 11 patients (p = 0.0001).  

**Example 4: Diagnostic Classification**  
Hierarchical clustering of QRT-PCR data correctly classified 12/13 schizophrenic samples and all controls into distinct groups, demonstrating the method’s diagnostic accuracy.  

The examples illustrate the invention’s utility in identifying schizophrenia through peripheral gene expression profiling, offering a robust, non-invasive diagnostic tool.
