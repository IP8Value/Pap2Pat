# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to the field of epigenetics and oncology, specifically targeting metabolic vulnerabilities in cancers and developmental disorders associated with mutations in the COMPASS complex proteins MLL3 (KMT2C) and MLL4 (KMT2D). These histone lysine methyltransferases play critical roles in enhancer regulation and gene expression through both catalytic-dependent H3K4 mono-methylation and catalytic-independent cofunctional associations with UTX and p300.  

Recurrent loss-of-function mutations in MLL3 and MLL4 have been identified across a broad spectrum of human cancers and developmental disorders such as Kabuki syndrome. Despite their established roles as tumor suppressors, the molecular mechanisms underlying the oncogenic consequences of MLL3/4 mutations remain poorly understood. Current therapeutic strategies for cancers harboring these mutations lack specificity and fail to exploit potential synthetic lethal interactions arising from MLL3/4 deficiency.  

There exists an unmet need in the art for targeted therapeutic approaches that selectively eliminate cells with compromised MLL3/4 function while sparing normal cells. The present invention addresses this need by identifying and exploiting a novel metabolic vulnerability in MLL3/4-deficient cells, providing composition and method claims for treating MLL3/4-associated disorders.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides methods and compositions for selectively targeting cells with compromised MLL3 or MLL4 function through inhibition of purine nucleotide synthesis pathways. The inventors have discovered that loss of MLL3/4 function creates a synthetic lethal dependency on de novo purine synthesis, making these cells uniquely vulnerable to inhibitors of this pathway.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Identification of purine metabolism as a synthetic lethal pathway in MLL3/4-deficient cells through genome-wide CRISPR screening and metabolomic profiling  
2. Demonstration of increased flux through de novo purine synthesis in MLL3/4-deficient cells using stable isotope tracing  
3. Development of therapeutic strategies using purine synthesis inhibitors (e.g., lometrexol, methotrexate) to selectively target MLL3/4-mutant cancers  
4. Diagnostic methods for identifying cancers likely to respond to purine synthesis inhibition based on MLL3/4 mutation status  
5. Pharmaceutical compositions combining purine synthesis inhibitors with other therapeutic agents for treating MLL3/4-associated disorders  

The invention further encompasses methods of treating cancers harboring MLL3 or MLL4 mutations, particularly truncation mutations that result in complete loss of function. The treatment methods are supported by in vitro and in vivo data demonstrating selective sensitivity of MLL4-mutant colorectal cancer cells to purine synthesis inhibition and significant tumor growth suppression in xenograft models.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention is based on the seminal discovery that cells deficient in MLL3 and MLL4 histone methyltransferases develop a synthetic lethal dependency on purine nucleotide synthesis pathways. This dependency creates a therapeutic window for selectively targeting cancers and other disorders associated with MLL3/4 mutations while sparing normal cells with intact MLL3/4 function.  

### Metabolic Reprogramming in MLL3/4-Deficient Cells  

Through comprehensive CRISPR dropout screening in mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs), the inventors identified that MLL3/4 knockout (KO) cells become dependent on genes involved in purine and pyrimidine synthesis pathways. This emergent dependency was confirmed through metabolic profiling showing increased flux through de novo purine synthesis in MLL3/4-KO cells compared to wild-type controls. Stable isotope tracing with 15N-glutamine demonstrated significantly elevated incorporation into purine nucleotides in the knockout cells, indicating enhanced pathway activity.  

Proteomic analysis revealed upregulation of proteins involved in purine metabolic processes in MLL3/4-KO cells, particularly components of the mitochondrial respiratory chain complex that supports nucleotide metabolism. This metabolic reprogramming creates a vulnerability that can be exploited therapeutically through inhibition of key enzymes in the purine synthesis pathway.  

### Synthetic Lethality with Purine Synthesis Inhibition  

The invention demonstrates that MLL3/4-deficient cells exhibit enhanced sensitivity to inhibitors targeting various steps of purine synthesis. Lometrexol (LTX), which inhibits glycinamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase (GART), showed significantly greater growth inhibition in MLL3/4-KO mESCs compared to wild-type cells. This differential sensitivity was rescued by supplementation with hypoxanthine, confirming the specificity for purine synthesis inhibition.  

Similar synthetic lethal effects were observed with methotrexate (MTX) and SHIN1, which target upstream steps in folate-mediated one-carbon metabolism required for purine synthesis. Importantly, this vulnerability was shown to result from the catalysis-independent functions of MLL3/4, as cells lacking only the SET domains (catalytic mutants) displayed wild-type sensitivity to purine synthesis inhibitors.  

### Mechanistic Basis of Synthetic Lethality  

Integrated transcriptomic and proteomic analyses revealed that loss of MLL3/4 leads to compensatory upregulation of MLL1/COMPASS activity at promoters of purine metabolism genes. Chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) showed increased MLL1 occupancy and H3K4 trimethylation at these loci in knockout cells. Inhibition of the WDR5-MLL1 interaction selectively reduced viability of MLL3/4-KO cells, confirming the importance of this compensatory mechanism.  

Higher-order chromatin structure analysis demonstrated that MLL3/4 deficiency causes global chromatin decompaction, with specific shifts from closed (B) to open (A) compartments at loci containing upregulated genes. This chromatin remodeling likely contributes to the altered gene expression patterns underlying the metabolic dependency.  

### Therapeutic Applications in Cancer  

The invention provides methods for treating cancers harboring MLL3 or MLL4 mutations using purine synthesis inhibitors. In colorectal cancer cell lines, those with MLL4 truncation mutations showed significantly greater sensitivity to LTX treatment compared to wild-type lines. This differential sensitivity correlated with increased flux through de novo purine synthesis in the mutant cells.  

Xenograft studies demonstrated that LTX treatment significantly inhibited tumor growth and extended survival in mice bearing MLL4-mutant HCT116 colorectal cancer cells, while having minimal effect on MLL4 wild-type tumors. The anti-tumor effects were associated with downregulation of mitotic cell cycle genes and induction of cell cycle arrest specifically in the MLL4-mutant cells.  

### Diagnostic and Therapeutic Methods  

The invention encompasses diagnostic methods for identifying patients likely to benefit from purine synthesis inhibition therapy based on MLL3 or MLL4 mutation status. This includes detection of truncation mutations, frameshift mutations, or other loss-of-function alterations in KMT2C (MLL3) or KMT2D (MLL4) genes.  

Treatment methods comprise administering purine synthesis inhibitors at doses effective to selectively target cells with compromised MLL3/4 function. The inhibitors may be used alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents, including chemotherapeutics, targeted therapies, or immunotherapies.  

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise purine synthesis inhibitors formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for administration to patients with MLL3/4-associated disorders. The compositions may include additional active ingredients to enhance efficacy or reduce side effects.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Identification of Purine Synthesis Dependency in MLL3/4-KO Cells  

A genome-wide CRISPR dropout screen was performed in wild-type and MLL3/4 double-knockout mESCs using the Brie CRISPR library. Analysis of negatively selected genes revealed significant enrichment for enzymes in the purine and pyrimidine synthesis pathways in the knockout cells. Validation experiments using individual sgRNAs confirmed that knockout cells were preferentially sensitive to depletion of genes such as Ppat and Mthfd1, which encode key purine synthesis enzymes.  

Metabolomic profiling demonstrated altered abundance of nucleotide intermediates in MLL3/4-KO cells, including increased levels of UMP, CMP, AICAR, and hypoxanthine. Stable isotope tracing with 15N-glutamine showed significantly greater incorporation into purine nucleotides in the knockout cells, confirming increased flux through de novo synthesis. The purine salvage pathway was also slightly elevated in knockout cells, as shown by 13C-hypoxanthine tracing.  

#### Example 2: Therapeutic Targeting of MLL4-Mutant Cancers  

Colorectal cancer cell lines with MLL4 truncation mutations (HCT116, RKO) showed significantly greater sensitivity to LTX treatment compared to wild-type lines (Caco2, HT55). The IC50 for LTX was approximately 3-fold lower in the mutant cells. This differential sensitivity was associated with increased flux through de novo purine synthesis in the mutant lines, as demonstrated by 15N-glutamine tracing.  

In vivo xenograft studies showed that LTX treatment (25 mg/kg) significantly inhibited tumor growth and extended survival in mice bearing MLL4-mutant HCT116 cells. Tumor growth inhibition was accompanied by decreased proliferation and increased apoptosis, as measured by immunohistochemistry for Ki-67 and cleaved caspase-3. In contrast, LTX had minimal effect on MLL4 wild-type HT55 xenografts at the same dose.  

#### Example 3: Mechanism of Action Studies  

RNA-seq analysis revealed that LTX treatment in MLL4-mutant cells specifically downregulated genes involved in mitotic cell cycle progression, including PLK1, AURKA, and CDC20. This effect was not observed in wild-type cells, explaining the selective growth inhibition.  

ChIP-seq demonstrated increased MLL1 occupancy and H3K4me3 at promoters of upregulated purine metabolism genes in MLL3/4-KO cells. Inhibition of the WDR5-MLL1 interaction using the small molecule WDR5-0103 reversed the upregulation of these genes and selectively reduced viability of the knockout cells. These findings establish the mechanistic basis for the synthetic lethal interaction between MLL3/4 loss and purine synthesis inhibition.  

The examples demonstrate the utility of purine synthesis inhibitors for selectively targeting cancers with MLL3 or MLL4 mutations while sparing normal cells. The invention provides both diagnostic methods to identify responsive patients and therapeutic compositions for effective treatment of these cancers.
