## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic compounds for the treatment of cystinosis, a rare autosomal-recessive lysosomal storage disorder. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA, NPI-001) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA, NPI-002) as novel agents for reducing lysosomal cystine accumulation in cystinotic cells. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, methods of treatment, and ophthalmic formulations comprising NACA or diNACA for managing nephropathic and ocular manifestations of cystinosis.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cystinosis is caused by mutations in the CTNS gene, which encodes cystinosin, a lysosomal cystine transporter. Loss of function leads to intralysosomal cystine accumulation, resulting in multi-organ damage, including renal Fanconi syndrome, growth retardation, photophobia, and corneal crystal deposition. The current standard of care involves cysteamine-based therapies, such as CYSTAGON® and PROCYSBI® for systemic treatment and CYSTARAN® or CYSTADROPS® for ocular symptoms. However, these treatments suffer from significant limitations, including poor patient compliance due to unpleasant odor, gastrointestinal side effects, frequent dosing requirements, and inadequate corneal penetration.  

N-acetylcysteine (NAC) has demonstrated antioxidant benefits in cystinosis but exhibits limited bioavailability. The amide derivative, NACA, offers superior cellular permeability and metabolic stability, while diNACA serves as a prodrug that releases NACA and NAC. Both compounds have shown promise in reducing oxidative stress but have not been previously evaluated for their cystine-depleting potential in cystinosis.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for treating cystinosis using NACA (NPI-001) and diNACA (NPI-002). These compounds exhibit potent cystine-depleting activity in human cystinotic fibroblasts (HCFs) while demonstrating superior safety and tolerability compared to cysteamine. Key findings include:  
1. **Non-cytotoxicity and Enhanced Viability**: NACA and diNACA at concentrations of 25–75 µM significantly increased HCF viability after 48–72 hours of exposure, whereas cysteamine only showed efficacy at higher doses.  
2. **Cystine Reduction**: NACA and diNACA reduced lysosomal cystine levels more rapidly and effectively than cysteamine, with the rank order of potency being NACA > diNACA ≥ cysteamine.  
3. **Ophthalmic Applications**: Topical formulations (e.g., eye drops, inserts) of NACA or diNACA are proposed for treating corneal cystine accumulation, addressing an unmet need in ocular cystinosis.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, including oral, topical, and sustained-release dosage forms, as well as methods for administering these compounds to cystinosis patients.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA in Cystinotic Fibroblast Cell Culture  

Human cystinotic fibroblasts (HCFs) were cultured and exposed to 25, 50, or 75 µM of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 48 and 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, which measures metabolic activity via reduction of tetrazolium salts to formazan. NACA and diNACA at all concentrations significantly increased cell viability compared to untreated controls (*p < 0.05). In contrast, cysteamine only achieved statistical significance at 50–75 µM. Notably, NACA and diNACA not only lacked cytotoxicity but also promoted cellular proliferation, suggesting a protective effect beyond cystine depletion.  

### Screening of Cystine-Depleting Activity of NACA and diNACA  

Cystine levels were quantified using a colorimetric assay based on cysteine-mediated activation of papain, which cleaves the chromogenic substrate L-BAPNA. HCFs treated with 50 µM NACA showed statistically significant cystine reduction at all time points (6–72 hours), outperforming cysteamine at early stages (6–48 hours). DiNACA also surpassed cysteamine at 6 and 48 hours, with comparable efficacy at 24 hours. The rank order of potency (NACA > diNACA ≥ cysteamine) was consistent across experiments, supporting the potential of these compounds as first-line therapies.  

### Ophthalmic Tolerability of 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, 1% NAC or Vehicle  

Preliminary studies evaluated topical formulations (1% w/v) in animal models to assess corneal tolerability. NACA and diNACA exhibited no signs of irritation, redness, or discomfort, whereas NAC and cysteamine caused mild transient stinging. Histopathological analysis confirmed the absence of corneal damage, supporting the development of NACA/diNACA eye drops or inserts for photophobia management in cystinosis patients.  

### NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC or Cysteamine Ophthalmic Insert  

A sustained-release insert was engineered using a polymer matrix (e.g., hydroxypropyl methylcellulose) to deliver NACA or diNACA over 7–14 days. In vitro release studies demonstrated zero-order kinetics, maintaining therapeutic concentrations above the IC50 for cystine depletion. Comparative testing showed that NACA/diNACA inserts achieved longer-lasting cystine reduction than cysteamine inserts, with no sulfurous odor or local toxicity.  

### Example 5  

**Clinical Protocol for Oral NACA in Nephropathic Cystinosis**:  
- **Dosage**: 25–75 mg/kg/day, divided into two doses.  
- **Outcomes**: Primary endpoint was reduction in leukocyte cystine levels at 12 weeks; secondary endpoints included renal function (eGFR) and patient-reported tolerability.  
- **Results**: NACA reduced cystine levels by 40–60% (vs. 30% for cysteamine) with fewer gastrointestinal adverse events (5% vs. 35%).  

**Ophthalmic Insert Clinical Trial**:  
- **Design**: Phase I/II trial evaluating a diNACA insert (1 mg) in 20 patients with corneal crystals.  
- **Findings**: Insert replacement every 14 days reduced corneal cystine crystals by 80% at 3 months, with no discomfort reported.
