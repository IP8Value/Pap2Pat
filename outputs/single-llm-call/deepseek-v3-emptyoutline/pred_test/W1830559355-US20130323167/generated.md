# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer therapeutics and more specifically to methods for enhancing tumor-selective drug delivery. The invention provides compositions and methods for improving the accumulation of therapeutic agents in tumors through pharmacologically-induced vascular modulation. Specifically, the invention describes the use of vascular-active agents such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) or anaerobic bacterial spores to enhance the permeability of tumor vasculature, thereby facilitating selective accumulation of macromolecular drug formulations including antibodies, nanoparticles, and liposomal drug carriers. The enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect observed with these methods, referred to herein as Enhanced EPR (E2PR), provides significant improvements in tumor-to-normal tissue ratios of therapeutic agents, leading to improved therapeutic outcomes in various cancer models.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The abnormal vasculature of tumors has long been recognized as distinct from that of normal tissues. This distinction has been exploited therapeutically through the phenomenon known as the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect, where macromolecules and nanoparticles preferentially accumulate in tumors due to their leaky vasculature and poor lymphatic drainage. While the EPR effect has enabled the development of several nanoparticle-based cancer therapeutics, the degree of tumor selectivity achieved through natural EPR remains suboptimal for many clinical applications.  

Current approaches to enhance drug delivery to tumors face several limitations. Small molecule chemotherapeutic agents distribute widely throughout the body, causing substantial toxicity to normal tissues. Targeted therapies that bind specifically to tumor cell surface markers are limited to cancers expressing those particular markers. The natural EPR effect, while useful, provides only modest improvements in tumor selectivity for nanoparticle formulations. There exists a significant unmet need for methods that can dramatically improve the selective accumulation of therapeutic agents in tumors while minimizing exposure to normal tissues.  

The vascular system's response to infection provides clues for potential solutions to this problem. During wound infection, vascular permeability increases markedly to allow immune system components to engage pathogens. This observation led to the hypothesis that creating an inflammatory environment within tumors could similarly enhance vascular permeability and thereby improve drug delivery. Prior attempts to modulate tumor vasculature have focused primarily on vascular disrupting agents that cause thrombosis and necrosis, but these approaches have shown limited therapeutic benefit in clinical settings.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing methods and compositions for pharmacologically enhancing tumor vascular permeability to improve drug delivery. The invention is based on the discovery that certain vascular-active agents, when administered in combination with macromolecular therapeutic formulations, can dramatically increase tumor-selective accumulation of these formulations through a phenomenon termed Enhanced EPR (E2PR).  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Methods for treating cancer comprising administering to a subject in need thereof:  
   a) a vascular-active agent selected from TNF-α, anaerobic bacterial spores (such as C. novyi-NT), or microtubule-interacting agents (such as combretastatin A4P or vinorelbine); and  
   b) a macromolecular therapeutic formulation selected from antibodies, liposomal nanoparticles, or other drug carriers in the size range of approximately 10 nm to 1 μm.  

2. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising both a vascular-active agent and a macromolecular therapeutic formulation, formulated for simultaneous or sequential administration.  

3. Methods wherein the vascular-active agent is administered at a dose and timing that maximizes tumor vascular permeability while minimizing systemic toxicity.  

4. Methods where the macromolecular therapeutic formulation comprises chemotherapeutic agents (such as doxorubicin), radioactive isotopes, or other therapeutic payloads.  

5. Applications of these methods for treating various cancers, including but not limited to colorectal carcinoma, glioblastoma multiforme, and other solid tumors.  

The invention provides several advantages over existing approaches. First, it dramatically improves tumor-to-normal tissue ratios of therapeutic agents, enabling higher effective doses at the tumor site while reducing systemic toxicity. Second, it is broadly applicable to many existing therapeutic agents when formulated appropriately. Third, it can overcome delivery barriers even in challenging tumor environments such as the brain, where the blood-brain barrier normally limits drug access.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides novel methods for enhancing tumor-selective drug delivery through pharmacologic modulation of tumor vasculature. The following sections describe various embodiments of the invention in detail.  

### Example 1  

**Enhanced Antibody Accumulation in Tumors Following Bacterial Infection**  

In one embodiment, the invention provides methods for enhancing antibody accumulation in tumors through combination with anaerobic bacterial spores. Experimental studies demonstrated that intravenous administration of C. novyi-NT spores together with radiolabeled antibodies resulted in significant tumor-selective antibody accumulation.  

Specifically, mice bearing subcutaneous CT26 tumors were treated with C. novyi-NT spores and either an anti-liposomase antibody or control IgG. Both antibodies showed significant enrichment in infected tumors compared to uninfected controls, with tumor-to-normal tissue ratios reaching approximately 4:1. Similar results were obtained with an anti-CD20 antibody (rituximab analog), demonstrating that the effect is not antibody-specific.  

These findings indicate that the inflammatory response to bacterial infection increases tumor vascular permeability, creating an enhanced EPR (E2PR) effect. The methods are particularly useful for antibody-based therapies, including radioimmunotherapy and antibody-drug conjugates, where improved tumor selectivity can significantly enhance therapeutic efficacy while reducing side effects.  

### Example 2  

**TNF-α-Induced Enhancement of Macromolecular Drug Accumulation**  

In another embodiment, the invention provides methods using TNF-α as the vascular-active agent to enhance tumor drug delivery. Experimental studies showed that systemic administration of murine TNF-α with radiolabeled murine IgG resulted in significant tumor accumulation, with kinetics showing peak accumulation between 72-96 hours post-injection.  

The TNF-α-induced E2PR effect was particularly pronounced with sterically stabilized liposomal nanoparticles (SSLs) of approximately 100 nm diameter. When 125I-labeled SSLs were administered with TNF-α, tumor-to-blood ratios reached 22:1, significantly higher than achieved with antibodies alone. SPECT/CT imaging revealed that SSL accumulation peaked earlier than antibodies (at 24 hours versus 72-96 hours), suggesting different mechanisms or kinetics of extravasation.  

This embodiment is valuable for liposomal drug formulations such as Doxil (pegylated liposomal doxorubicin), where enhanced tumor accumulation can improve therapeutic outcomes. The methods are also applicable to other nanoparticle formulations in the size range that benefits from E2PR (approximately 10 nm to 1 μm).  

### Example 3  

**Therapeutic Applications of E2PR**  

The invention further provides therapeutic methods exploiting the E2PR phenomenon. In one embodiment, mice bearing CT26 tumors were treated with TNF-α plus either Doxil or 131I-labeled IgG. While single-agent treatments showed limited efficacy, the combinations produced complete tumor regression in all animals and long-term cures in >75% of cases.  

Dose-response studies demonstrated that therapeutic efficacy correlated with TNF-α dose, with 100 μg/kg showing superior results to 25 μg/kg. Importantly, the higher dose corresponds to levels tolerated in human clinical trials (approximately 3 infusions/week), suggesting ready translatability to clinical applications.  

Similar results were obtained with 131I-labeled SSLs, where combination with TNF-α produced complete tumor regression while SSLs alone only retarded tumor growth. These findings demonstrate that E2PR can significantly enhance the therapeutic efficacy of various macromolecular formulations, including both chemical and radiotherapeutic agents.  

### Example 4  

**Application to Brain Tumors and Blood-Brain Barrier Modulation**  

In another important embodiment, the invention provides methods for treating brain tumors by overcoming the blood-brain barrier. Experimental studies in an orthotopic glioblastoma (GL261) model demonstrated that TNF-α administration enabled significant accumulation of 125I-labeled SSLs in brain tumors, which was not observed without TNF-α treatment.  

Therapeutic studies showed that Doxil plus TNF-α significantly prolonged survival in this aggressive brain tumor model (up to 103 days versus approximately 30 days for controls), while either agent alone provided minimal benefit. This demonstrates the particular value of E2PR for treating central nervous system tumors, where conventional therapies often fail due to poor drug penetration.  

The methods are applicable to various brain tumors, including glioblastoma multiforme, and may be combined with surgical or radiation therapies. The timing of TNF-α administration can be optimized based on tumor characteristics and therapeutic agent properties, with administration within 12 hours of the therapeutic agent showing efficacy in experimental models.  

The invention thus provides comprehensive methods for enhancing cancer therapy through pharmacologic modulation of tumor vasculature. By combining vascular-active agents with appropriately formulated macromolecular therapeutics, significant improvements in tumor selectivity and therapeutic outcomes can be achieved across a broad range of cancer types and therapeutic agents.
