# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel macrocyclic compounds capable of selectively inhibiting insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) activity. IDE is a zinc metalloprotease that plays a critical role in the degradation of insulin and other peptide hormones, including glucagon and amyloid-β. Dysregulation of IDE activity has been implicated in metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes, as well as neurodegenerative conditions like Alzheimer's disease. Existing IDE inhibitors suffer from poor selectivity, low potency, or inadequate pharmacokinetic properties, limiting their therapeutic utility. There remains an unmet need for highly specific, potent, and bioavailable IDE inhibitors that can modulate metabolic pathways without off-target effects.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides macrocyclic compounds that bind IDE with high affinity and inhibit its proteolytic activity. These compounds are derived from a DNA-templated library and exhibit selective inhibition of both human and mouse IDE. The macrocycles demonstrate improved metabolic stability and bioavailability compared to prior art inhibitors. In particular, the compound designated as 6bK, a lysine-modified derivative of the lead macrocycle 6b, shows potent inhibition of IDE in biochemical assays and exhibits favorable pharmacokinetic properties in vivo. The invention further encompasses methods for identifying IDE-binding compounds, detecting IDE activity, and using macrocyclic inhibitors for therapeutic applications in metabolic and neurodegenerative disorders.  

## DEFINITIONS  

### Chemical Definitions  

As used herein, the term "macrocycle" refers to a cyclic compound comprising at least 12 atoms in the ring structure, wherein the ring includes a combination of peptide and non-peptide linkages. The term "IDE-binding compound" refers to a molecule that interacts with insulin-degrading enzyme with a dissociation constant (Kd) of less than 10 µM. The term "fluorogenic substrate" denotes a peptide substrate whose cleavage by IDE produces a detectable fluorescent signal, such as Mca-RPPGFSAFK(Dnp)-OH.  

### Other Definitions  

The term "incremental effect" refers to the enhanced insulin secretion observed after oral glucose administration compared to intravenous or intraperitoneal glucose delivery. The term "hyperinsulinaemic phenotype" describes a condition characterized by elevated basal insulin levels, commonly observed in diet-induced obese (DIO) mice. The term "isotope dilution mass spectrometry" (IDMS) denotes a quantitative analytical method wherein a heavy isotope-labeled internal standard is used to measure the concentration of a target analyte in biological samples.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

### Methods for Identifying IDE-Binding Compounds  

The invention provides a method for identifying IDE-binding macrocycles through in vitro selection using a DNA-templated library. The method involves immobilizing His6-tagged mouse IDE on cobalt magnetic beads and incubating the enzyme with a diverse macrocycle library. Enriched compounds are decoded via DNA barcode sequencing, and their inhibitory activity is validated using a fluorogenic peptide cleavage assay. Secondary confirmation is achieved using time-resolved FRET and LC-MS assays to measure insulin and CGRP degradation inhibition.  

### Methods for Detecting IDE  

A homogeneous time-resolved fluorescence (HTRF) assay is disclosed for detecting IDE activity in biological samples. The assay employs an anti-insulin antibody pair to monitor IDE-mediated insulin degradation in the presence or absence of macrocyclic inhibitors. Additionally, an LC-MS-based method is provided for quantifying CGRP cleavage fragments in plasma, enabling ex vivo assessment of IDE inhibition.  

### Labeled IDE Inhibitor Probes  

The invention includes labeled derivatives of macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, such as 6bK synthesized with 13C6,15N2-lysine, for use in pharmacokinetic and biodistribution studies. These probes permit quantitative measurement of inhibitor concentrations in tissues using isotope dilution mass spectrometry.  

### Clinical Applications of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors  

The macrocyclic inhibitors are useful for treating metabolic disorders by enhancing insulin signaling and suppressing glucagon secretion. In vivo studies demonstrate that 6bK improves glucose tolerance in diet-induced obese mice without altering brain amyloid-β levels. The inhibitors also slow gastric emptying via amylin receptor signaling, suggesting applications in obesity management.  

### Kits  

The invention provides kits comprising macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, fluorogenic substrates, and detection reagents for high-throughput screening or diagnostic use. Optional components include isotopically labeled internal standards for quantitative mass spectrometry.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

In vitro selection was performed using a 13,824-member DNA-templated macrocycle library against His6-tagged mouse IDE. Compounds enriched at least twofold in two independent selections were synthesized and tested for IDE inhibition. The trans-isomer 6b exhibited potent inhibition (IC50 < 1 µM) in a fluorogenic peptide assay. Structure-activity studies revealed that modifications to the linker, scaffold, or building blocks reduced potency, confirming the specificity of 6b.  

### Example 2  

Pharmacokinetic analysis of 6bK in lean mice demonstrated rapid absorption and tissue distribution following intraperitoneal administration (80 mg/kg). Peak plasma concentrations were achieved within 30 minutes, with sustained levels detectable for over 4 hours. Notably, 6bK was undetectable in brain tissue, indicating a peripheral restriction that may minimize central nervous system side effects.  

## EQUIVALENTS AND SCOPE  

While the invention has been described with reference to specific embodiments, modifications such as alternative macrocycle scaffolds, additional isotopic labels, or derivatizations for improved bioavailability fall within the scope of the claims. The methods of use may be extended to other IDE-associated pathologies, including but not limited to diabetes, obesity, and neurodegenerative diseases.
