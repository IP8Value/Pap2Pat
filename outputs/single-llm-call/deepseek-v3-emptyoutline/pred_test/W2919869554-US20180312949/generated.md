# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) based on gold alloys, particularly those designed for applications requiring controlled color and enhanced mechanical properties. More specifically, the invention pertains to gold-based MGMCs comprising finely dispersed gold-rich microdendrites embedded within a metallic glass matrix, wherein the composite exhibits a visually uniform color determined by the relative proportions and compositions of the dendritic and matrix phases. The invention further encompasses methods for processing such alloys into ingots, rods, and plates, as well as techniques for characterizing their color coordinates and thermal properties.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Metallic glasses, or amorphous metals, are known for their unique mechanical properties, including high strength, exceptional damage tolerance, and superior corrosion resistance. However, the formation of bulk metallic glasses is typically restricted to narrow compositional ranges, limiting the ability to tailor their properties for specific applications. One approach to overcome this limitation involves the development of metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs), wherein crystalline dendrites are precipitated within a metallic glass matrix. These composites allow for the fine-tuning of mechanical and functional properties by controlling the morphology, volume fraction, and distribution of the dendritic phase.  

Gold alloys have long been utilized in luxury goods such as jewelry and ornamental articles due to their aesthetic appeal and corrosion resistance. However, conventional gold alloys are often soft and prone to wear, necessitating alloying with other metals to enhance hardness. Traditional gold alloys are classified by hallmark purity and color, with variations such as yellow gold, white gold, and rose gold achieved through specific alloying additions. While bulk metallic glasses based on precious metals have been explored for jewelry applications, their color options have been limited, typically exhibiting a pale white appearance due to the presence of silicon and other bleaching elements.  

Prior efforts in developing gold-based metallic glasses have focused on compositions containing silicon along with noble or near-noble metals such as copper, silver, and palladium. These alloys exhibit critical casting dimensions of up to 5 mm and often suffer from tarnishing upon exposure to air. There remains a need for gold-based materials that combine the aesthetic versatility of traditional gold alloys with the superior mechanical properties of metallic glasses, particularly in applications where controlled color and high hardness are desired.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention provides gold-based metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) with tailored color and enhanced hardness. The composites comprise a metallic glass matrix phase and a dendritic primary gold phase, wherein the microstructure is finely dispersed to achieve a visually uniform color. The invention leverages the lattice mismatch between the primary gold phase and the metallic glass matrix to facilitate composite formation while enabling precise control over chromaticity through compositional adjustments.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A compositional formula for the MGMCs, wherein the alloy composition lies along a tie-line connecting a eutectic metallic glass-forming composition and a primary gold solid solution.  
2. Methods for processing the alloys into ingots, rods, and plates with controlled microstructures.  
3. Techniques for measuring the CIELAB color coordinates of the composites to verify color uniformity.  
4. Differential scanning calorimetry methods for characterizing the thermal properties of the alloys.  

The resulting MGMCs exhibit a broad range of colors, from pale white to yellow gold, while achieving hardness values significantly exceeding those of conventional gold alloys. The invention is particularly suited for applications in jewelry, watches, and decorative articles where both aesthetic and mechanical performance are critical.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Methods of Processing the Alloy Ingots of the Sample Metallic Glass-Forming Alloys  

The alloy ingots of the metallic glass-forming compositions are prepared by inductive melting of high-purity elemental constituents under an inert atmosphere. The constituent elements, including gold, copper, silver, palladium, and silicon, are combined in stoichiometric proportions corresponding to the desired alloy composition. The melting process is conducted in a quartz tube to minimize contamination, and the molten alloy is homogenized by repeated melting and stirring.  

Following homogenization, the alloy is cast into ingots by cooling within the quartz tube. The ingots are subsequently remelted and quenched to form rods or plates of the desired dimensions. For rod samples, the alloy is melted in a cylindrical quartz tube with a wall thickness of 0.5 mm, while plate coupons are produced using rectangular quartz tubes of similar dimensions. Rapid quenching is achieved by immersing the molten alloy in a room-temperature water bath, ensuring the formation of the desired microstructure.  

The ingots and as-cast samples are subjected to polishing to achieve a mirror-like surface finish. Cross-sectional surfaces of rods and plates are finely polished using diamond abrasives down to a 1 μm finish to facilitate subsequent characterization. The polished surfaces are examined for uniformity and absence of defects prior to further processing.  

### Methods of Processing the Sample Metallic Glass Plates  

The metallic glass plates are processed from the alloy ingots using a two-step melting and quenching procedure. The ingots are first melted in a furnace at a temperature of 950°C under high-purity argon to prevent oxidation. The molten alloy is then transferred into rectangular quartz molds with internal dimensions of 20 mm × 20 mm × 1 mm. The molds are rapidly quenched in a water bath to achieve cooling rates sufficient for metallic glass formation.  

The as-quenched plates are removed from the molds and subjected to surface finishing. The plates are polished on all faces to a 1 μm diamond finish, ensuring optical clarity for color measurements. The edges of the plates are beveled to remove any sharp corners, and the surfaces are cleaned ultrasonically to eliminate polishing residues. The final plate dimensions are verified to ensure consistency across samples.  

### Method for Measuring the CIELAB Color Coordinates  

The CIELAB color coordinates of the metallic glass plates are measured using a spectrophotometer with an 8 mm aperture. Measurements are performed at four distinct locations on each plate to account for potential spatial variations in color. The spectrophotometer is calibrated using a white reference standard prior to each measurement session.  

The CIELAB coordinates (L*, a*, b*) are recorded for each measurement location, with L* representing luminosity, a* representing the red-green chromaticity, and b* representing the blue-yellow chromaticity. The average values of L*, a*, and b* are calculated for each plate and compared against reference standards to verify color uniformity. The measurements are conducted under controlled lighting conditions to minimize external influences on the results.  

### Method for Performing Differential Scanning Calorimetry  

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) is employed to characterize the thermal properties of the metallic glass alloys. Samples for DSC analysis are prepared by sectioning discs from the as-cast rods or plates. The discs are polished to ensure flat and parallel surfaces, and their masses are recorded to an accuracy of 0.1 mg.  

The DSC measurements are performed at a heating rate of 20 K/min under a continuous flow of inert gas. The thermograms are analyzed to identify key transition temperatures, including the glass transition temperature (Tg), crystallization temperature (Tx), solidus temperature (Ts), and liquidus temperature (Tl). The enthalpy changes associated with these transitions are quantified to assess the thermal stability of the alloys.  

The DSC data are used to construct pseudo-binary phase diagrams for the alloy system, illustrating the relationship between composition and phase stability. These diagrams aid in optimizing the alloy compositions for desired microstructures and properties.  

The invention thus provides a comprehensive framework for designing, processing, and characterizing gold-based metallic glass matrix composites with tailored color and enhanced mechanical properties. The methods described herein enable the production of alloys suitable for high-end applications where both aesthetic and functional performance are paramount.
