# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of cellular reprogramming and stem cell technology. Specifically, the invention provides methods for the rapid and efficient isolation of fully reprogrammed induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from heterogeneous cell populations using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) with a combination of positive and negative surface markers. The disclosed methods enable the purification of iPSCs at early stages of reprogramming, thereby improving the yield, purity, and quality of derived iPSC lines for research, drug discovery, and therapeutic applications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The discovery that somatic cells can be reprogrammed into pluripotent stem cells by the forced expression of transcription factors (Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and cMyc) has revolutionized regenerative medicine. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) exhibit properties similar to embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and hold immense potential for disease modeling, drug screening, and cell-based therapies. However, current reprogramming methods suffer from inefficiencies, including low yields (≤0.01% of fibroblasts), prolonged culture periods (4–6 weeks), and contamination with partially reprogrammed or non-reprogrammed cells.  

Existing approaches for isolating iPSCs rely primarily on manual colony picking or positive selection for pluripotency markers such as SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. These methods are labor-intensive, require specialized expertise, and often result in heterogeneous cultures containing residual fibroblasts or partially reprogrammed cells. While fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) has been employed to enrich pluripotent cells, prior strategies lacked robust negative selection to exclude contaminating cell populations, leading to variability in iPSC quality.  

There remains a critical need for standardized, high-throughput methods to isolate fully reprogrammed iPSCs with minimal contamination. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel FACS-based strategy combining negative selection against the fibroblast marker CD13 with positive selection for pluripotency markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60). This approach enables the isolation of pure iPSC populations as early as 7 days post-reprogramming, significantly improving the efficiency and reproducibility of iPSC derivation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for isolating fully reprogrammed induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from a heterogeneous cell population, comprising:  
1. Reprogramming somatic cells (e.g., fibroblasts) using viral vectors (retroviral or Sendai viral) expressing reprogramming factors (Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and cMyc).  
2. Detecting the emergence of cells expressing pluripotency markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60) and lacking the fibroblast marker CD13 during reprogramming.  
3. Isolating CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive cells by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) at 7–14 days post-reprogramming.  
4. Culturing the sorted cells under conditions conducive to iPSC expansion and pluripotency maintenance.  

Key advantages of the method include:  
- **Early isolation**: iPSCs can be purified as early as 7 days post-reprogramming, reducing culture time.  
- **High purity**: Negative selection against CD13 eliminates contaminating fibroblasts and partially reprogrammed cells.  
- **Versatility**: Compatible with integrating (retroviral) and non-integrating (Sendai viral) reprogramming systems.  
- **Scalability**: Enables high-throughput derivation of iPSC lines from diverse somatic cell sources, including patient-specific fibroblasts.  

The invention further encompasses iPSC lines generated by the disclosed method, as well as their use in research, drug screening, and regenerative medicine applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies  

The invention enables the rapid derivation of iPSCs by identifying and isolating fully reprogrammed cells at early stages of the reprogramming process. Traditional methods rely on manual colony picking after 3–4 weeks of culture, whereas the disclosed FACS-based approach allows for isolation as early as 7 days post-infection (dpi).  

Reprogramming is initiated by transducing somatic cells (e.g., fibroblasts) with viral vectors encoding the Yamanaka factors (Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and cMyc). Both integrating (retroviral) and non-integrating (Sendai viral) vectors can be used. Following transduction, cells are cultured in pluripotency-supporting medium supplemented with small-molecule inhibitors (e.g., ALK5 inhibitor SB431542, MEK inhibitor PD0325901, and ROCK inhibitor Thiazovivin) to enhance reprogramming efficiency.  

By 7–14 dpi, a subset of cells begins to express pluripotency markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60) while downregulating the fibroblast marker CD13. These CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive cells are isolated by FACS and plated onto feeder layers (e.g., murine embryonic fibroblasts) or extracellular matrix-coated surfaces (e.g., Matrigel). Within 3 days post-sorting, distinct iPSC colonies emerge, exhibiting morphology and growth characteristics consistent with pluripotent stem cells.  

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy  

Immunofluorescence staining and microscopy are employed to validate the pluripotent status of FACS-derived iPSCs. Cells are fixed and stained for pluripotency markers (e.g., Nanog, Oct4, SSEA4, TRA-1-60) and fibroblast markers (e.g., CD13).  

FACS-derived iPSCs exhibit homogeneous expression of pluripotency markers and minimal contamination with CD13-positive fibroblasts. In contrast, manually picked colonies often contain heterogeneous populations, including residual fibroblasts or partially reprogrammed cells. Microscopic analysis reveals that FACS-derived colonies display compact, ESC-like morphology with well-defined borders, whereas unsorted cultures exhibit mixed morphologies with differentiated cells interspersed among iPSC colonies.  

### Primers  

Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) is used to assess the expression of endogenous pluripotency genes (e.g., Nanog, Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, cMyc) and viral transgenes in FACS-derived iPSCs. Custom primer sets are designed to distinguish endogenous from exogenous transcripts.  

FACS-derived iPSCs show robust activation of endogenous pluripotency genes and silencing of viral transgenes by passage 10, comparable to human ESCs. In contrast, manually derived clones often retain high levels of viral transgene expression, indicating incomplete reprogramming. ### Comparison of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors in Reprogramming  

The invention is compatible with both integrating (retroviral) and non-integrating (Sendai viral) reprogramming systems. Comparative studies demonstrate that Sendai virus-mediated reprogramming yields a higher proportion of SSEA4-positive/TRA-1-60-positive cells but exhibits delayed downregulation of CD13 compared to retroviral reprogramming.  

Key differences include:  
- **Retroviral reprogramming**: CD13-negative iPSCs emerge by 7–14 dpi, but viral transgene integration poses risks of insertional mutagenesis.  
- **Sendai viral reprogramming**: CD13-negative iPSCs appear later (11–14 dpi), but the non-integrating nature eliminates genomic modification concerns.  

Despite these differences, FACS purification of CD13-negative/SSEA4-positive/TRA-1-60-positive cells consistently yields high-quality iPSCs from both systems, as confirmed by pluripotency assays (e.g., embryoid body formation, teratoma assays).
