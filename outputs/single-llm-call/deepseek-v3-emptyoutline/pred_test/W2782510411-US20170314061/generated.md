# **DESCRIPTION**  

## **FIELD OF THE INVENTION**  
The present invention relates to bioluminescence imaging (BLI) systems, particularly engineered luciferase enzymes and novel naphthyl-based luciferin analogs that produce near-infrared (NIR) bioluminescence. The invention provides improved substrates and mutant luciferase enzymes, such as CBR2, which enable enhanced deep-tissue imaging with superior sensitivity and resolution compared to conventional firefly luciferase (Luc2) and D-luciferin (D-LH2) systems. Applications include preclinical imaging, gene expression analysis, and bioluminescence tomography (BLT) in mammalian models.  

## **BACKGROUND**  
Bioluminescence imaging using firefly luciferase (Luc2) and D-luciferin (D-LH2) is widely employed for in vivo gene expression studies and therapeutic evaluations in animal models. However, conventional Luc2/D-LH2 systems suffer from limited tissue penetration due to photon absorption by hemoglobin, melanin, and other tissue components, particularly in deep tissues such as the brain, lung, and bone.  

Efforts to shift bioluminescence emission into the NIR range (650–900 nm) have included mutagenesis of luciferases and synthesis of extended π-conjugated luciferin analogs. While some progress has been made, existing NIR-emitting systems remain limited by weak signal intensity, poor biodistribution, and insufficient spectral shifts. For example, AkaLumine-HCl (Aka-HCl) produces NIR emission (~677 nm) but only at restricted substrate concentrations.  

Thus, there remains a need for improved bioluminescent systems that combine high photon flux, deep-tissue penetration, and stable enzyme-substrate interactions for sensitive and high-resolution imaging.  

## **SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION**  
The present invention addresses these limitations by providing:  

1. **Novel Naphthyl-Luciferin Analogs**: Two synthetic substrates, amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH2-NpLH2) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH2), designed with extended π conjugation to enable NIR emission.  
2. **Engineered Luciferase (CBR2)**: A mutant click beetle red (CBR) luciferase optimized for stability and catalytic efficiency with both D-LH2 and naphthyl-luciferins.  
3. **Codon-Optimized Gene (CBR2opt)**: A mammalian codon-optimized sequence encoding CBR2 for improved cellular expression.  
4. **Enhanced Imaging Systems**:  
   - CBR2/D-LH2 provides higher photon flux than Luc2/D-LH2 in vivo.  
   - CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 emits at ~730 nm, enabling superior deep-tissue and tomographic imaging.  
   - The system is compatible with multispectral acquisition for 3D bioluminescence reconstruction.  

These advancements enable high-sensitivity imaging in challenging models, including black-furred mice and deep brain structures, with applications in preclinical research and biomedical diagnostics.  

## **DETAILED DESCRIPTION**  

### **1. DEFINITIONS**  
- **NH2-NpLH2**: Amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin, a synthetic substrate emitting NIR light (~730 nm) when oxidized by CBR2.  
- **OH-NpLH2**: Hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin, a synthetic substrate emitting NIR light (~743 nm) with CBR2.  
- **CBR2**: A mutant click beetle red luciferase (R334S+G351R) with enhanced stability and catalytic efficiency for naphthyl-luciferins.  
- **CBR2opt**: A codon-optimized gene sequence encoding CBR2 for high expression in mammalian cells.  
- **BLT (Bioluminescence Tomography)**: A 3D imaging technique for localizing bioluminescent sources in deep tissues.  

### **2. CBR VARIANTS**  
The invention provides engineered CBR variants, particularly CBR2 (R334S+G351R), designed via homology modeling and rational mutagenesis. Key features include:  
- **Improved Stability**: CBR2 exhibits a half-life >10 hours at 37°C, compared to <30 minutes for Luc2.  
- **Enhanced Catalysis**: Despite lower biochemical brightness, CBR2 accumulates to higher levels in cells, yielding 2–3× greater signal than Luc2/D-LH2.  
- **Substrate Versatility**: CBR2 efficiently utilizes D-LH2, NH2-NpLH2, and OH-NpLH2, enabling multimodal imaging.  

### **3. NEAR-INFRARED BIOLUMINESCENCE SYSTEMS**  
The invention provides two NIR-emitting substrate-enzyme pairs:  
1. **CBR2/NH2-NpLH2**:  
   - Peak emission: 730 nm (65 nm red-shift vs. CBR/NH2-NpLH2).  
   - High cell permeability and low cytotoxicity.  
2. **CBR2/OH-NpLH2**:  
   - Peak emission: 743 nm (most red-shifted bioluminescence reported).  
   - Lower brightness but useful for specialized NIR applications.  

### **4. METHODS OF USING THE CBR VARIANTS AND/OR BIOLUMINESCENT SYSTEMS**  
Applications include:  
- **In Vivo Imaging**: Intravenous or intraperitoneal administration of substrates (e.g., 150–300 mg/kg D-LH2; 220 mg/kg NH2-NpLH2).  
- **Deep-Tissue Tomography**: Multispectral acquisition using 700–740 nm filters for 3D reconstruction.  
- **Orthogonal Imaging**: Selective detection of CBR2-expressing cells in multiplexed assays.  

### **5. SAMPLE**  
A sample protocol for brain imaging:  
1. **Cell Transplantation**: Inject 1×10^5 CBR2opt-expressing HEK-293 cells into mouse brain (3 mm depth).  
2. **Substrate Administration**: Deliver NH2-NpLH2 (220 mg/kg IV) or D-LH2 (300 mg/kg IV).  
3. **Image Acquisition**: Use an IVIS Spectrum system with NIR filters (700–740 nm).  

### **6. KIT**  
A kit for bioluminescence imaging may include:  
- **CBR2opt Lentiviral Vector**: For stable cell line generation.  
- **NH2-NpLH2 or OH-NpLH2 Substrates**: Lyophilized or in solution.  
- **Imaging Buffer**: Optimized for in vitro or in vivo assays.  

### **7. EXAMPLES**  

#### **Example 1: Characterization of CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 in Cells**  
- HEK-293 cells expressing CBR2opt were treated with NH2-NpLH2 (1 mM).  
- Peak emission: 728 nm (live cells) vs. 730 nm (purified enzyme).  
- Signal intensity was 33-fold lower than Luc2/D-LH2 but provided superior deep-tissue penetration.  

#### **Example 2: In Vivo Brain Imaging**  
- C57BL/6 mice received intracranial CBR2opt-expressing cells.  
- NH2-NpLH2 (220 mg/kg IV) yielded a 3-fold lower signal than D-LH2 but enabled precise BLT reconstruction.  
- Co-registration with CT confirmed accurate 3D localization of cells.  

#### **Example 3: Comparison with Aka-HCl**  
- CBR2/Aka-HCl produced 6-fold lower signal than CBR2/D-LH2.  
- NH2-NpLH2 provided superior resolution for tracking cell migration in brain ventricles.
