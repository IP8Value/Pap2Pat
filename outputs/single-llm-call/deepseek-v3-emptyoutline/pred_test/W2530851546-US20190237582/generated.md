# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to high-voltage thin-film transistors (HVTFTs) and methods of fabricating the same. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel ring-structured HVTFT comprising a modulation-doped magnesium zinc oxide (MZO) channel layer with an integrated transition layer, which significantly enhances thermal stability, electrical performance, and high-voltage blocking capability. The disclosed HVTFT is particularly suitable for use in photovoltaic self-powered smart glass (PV-SOG) applications, including building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) and solar inverters.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Conventional thin-film transistors (TFTs) with rectangular channel designs suffer from non-uniform electrical field distribution, particularly at the channel corners, which limits their voltage-blocking capabilities. Additionally, ZnO-based TFTs exhibit poor thermal stability due to high trap densities in the bulk channel and at the interface with the gate dielectric layer. These limitations hinder their application in high-voltage environments, such as photovoltaic inverters, where stable operation under elevated temperatures and high bias conditions is critical.  

Previous attempts to improve ZnO-based TFTs have included doping with magnesium to form MgZnO (MZO) channels, which reduces oxygen vacancy-related traps due to stronger Mg-O bonding compared to Zn-O. However, interdiffusion of Zn and Si across the channel-dielectric interface remains a significant issue, leading to increased interface trap densities and degraded device performance. There exists a need for an improved HVTFT design that addresses these challenges while providing enhanced thermal stability, high blocking voltage, and reliable operation under high bias conditions.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a high-voltage thin-film transistor (HVTFT) with a ring-structured channel design that improves electrical field distribution uniformity and reduces peak field concentrations. The HVTFT comprises a modulation-doped magnesium zinc oxide (m-MZO) channel layer incorporating a graded-composition MgyZn1−yO transition layer (MZO-TL) between the channel and the gate dielectric. This transition layer acts as a diffusion barrier, preventing Zn and Si interdiffusion, thereby reducing interface trap density and improving device performance.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Enhanced thermal stability due to reduced bulk and interface trap densities.  
- Superior high-voltage blocking capability, with demonstrated operation at drain biases exceeding 200 V and blocking voltages up to 609 V.  
- Improved subthreshold slope (S.S.) and on-current characteristics compared to conventional MZO and ZnO HVTFTs.  
- Stable operation under high-temperature conditions, making the device suitable for integration into PV-SOG applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Material Preparation and Device Fabrication Process  

The HVTFTs of the present invention are fabricated on commercially available glass substrates with a thickness of 0.4 mm. A bottom gate electrode is formed by depositing a 50 nm chromium (Cr) layer via sputtering, followed by patterning using a dry etching process. A 200 nm silicon dioxide (SiO2) layer is subsequently deposited by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) to serve as the gate dielectric.  

The channel layer is deposited using metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) at 400°C, with diethyl zinc (DeZn) and bis(methylcyclopentadienyl) magnesium (MCp2Mg) serving as the precursors for Zn and Mg, respectively. Three variants of the channel layer are disclosed: (i) pure ZnO, (ii) Mg0.03Zn0.97O (MZO), and (iii) modulation-doped Mg0.03Zn0.97O (m-MZO). In the m-MZO configuration, a 10 nm MgyZn1−yO transition layer (MZO-TL) is inserted between the MZO channel and the SiO2 dielectric. The Mg composition (y) in the transition layer is graded, decreasing from y = 1 (adjacent to SiO2) to y = 0.03 (adjacent to the channel).  

Source and drain electrodes are formed by depositing a bilayer of 100 nm titanium (Ti) and 50 nm gold (Au) via electron beam evaporation, followed by a lift-off process. A photoresist passivation layer is applied to the channel to prevent environmental interference during electrical testing. The HVTFTs are fabricated with varying channel lengths (10 μm, 15 μm, and 25 μm) and offset regions to optimize blocking voltage and on-current performance.  

### Device Testing Conditions  

Electrical characterization of the HVTFTs is performed under both low and high bias conditions. Low-bias measurements utilize an HP-4156C semiconductor parameter analyzer with an HP-41501B pulse generator, providing a voltage range up to 200 V and current resolution of 1 × 10−15 A. High-bias testing employs a Tektronix 370 system configured with a custom high-voltage probe station, with devices immersed in Fluorinert FC-40 to prevent arcing.  

Thermal stability assessments are conducted using an Agilent 1500B system, with transfer characteristics measured across a temperature range of 294 K to 367 K. Material characterization includes transmission electron microscopy (TEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to analyze interfacial properties and elemental distributions.  

### Material Characterizations  

Cross-sectional TEM analysis reveals significant differences in interfacial morphology between MZO and m-MZO HVTFTs. In MZO devices, Zn diffusion into the SiO2 dielectric layer is observed, manifested as gray dots in TEM images and confirmed by EDS elemental mapping. In contrast, m-MZO devices exhibit abrupt interfaces with negligible Zn penetration into the dielectric, demonstrating the effectiveness of the MZO-TL as a diffusion barrier.  

XPS depth profiling further corroborates these findings, showing overlapping Zn and Si profiles in MZO devices versus sharply defined interfaces in m-MZO structures. The graded Mg composition in the transition layer is confirmed by a narrow Mg peak in XPS spectra, with atomic percentages consistent with the designed composition gradient.  

### Device Design  

The HVTFT features a ring-structured channel that replaces conventional rectangular designs to improve electric field distribution. Simulation results demonstrate that the ring structure reduces peak electric field intensity by approximately 50% compared to rectangular counterparts, enabling higher blocking voltages. The device employs a bottom-gate inverted-staggered configuration with offset regions between the gate and source/drain electrodes to further enhance voltage withstand capability.  

Three channel length variants are disclosed (10 μm, 15 μm, and 25 μm), with corresponding gate-to-drain offsets of 5 μm, 10 μm, and 20 μm respectively. The gate-to-source offset is maintained at 3 μm across all configurations. This design provides a tunable trade-off between blocking voltage and on-current performance, allowing optimization for specific application requirements.  

### Transfer Characteristics and Thermal Stability  

The m-MZO HVTFT demonstrates superior transfer characteristics compared to both pure ZnO and undoped MZO devices. At room temperature, the m-MZO device exhibits an on-current one order of magnitude higher than MZO counterparts, with a steeper subthreshold slope (0.246 eV/V vs. 0.177 eV/V for MZO).  

Thermal stability testing reveals significantly reduced threshold voltage shifts (ΔVth) in m-MZO devices. When temperature increases from 294 K to 367 K, the m-MZO HVTFT shows a ΔVth of only -6 V, compared to -10.5 V for pure ZnO devices. This improvement is attributed to lower bulk and interface trap densities resulting from the Mg doping and transition layer architecture.  

### High Voltage Blocking Capability  

The m-MZO HVTFT demonstrates exceptional high-voltage performance, with blocking voltages exceeding 600 V in the 25 μm channel length configuration. Comparative testing shows MZO devices failing at 90 V drain bias, while m-MZO devices maintain leakage currents below 10−12 A at 200 V. The blocking voltage scales with channel length, with measured values of 300 V (10 μm), 427 V (15 μm), and 609 V (25 μm).  

Notably, the m-MZO devices maintain high on/off ratios (>107) even at maximum operating biases (70 V for 10 μm, 110 V for 15 μm, and 200 V for 25 μm devices). This stable high-bias operation makes the invention particularly suitable for photovoltaic inverter applications.  

### Statistical Data of Electrical Performance  

Comprehensive electrical characterization demonstrates consistent performance advantages of the m-MZO architecture. Key metrics include:  
- On-current values of 3.5 × 10−5 A (10 μm), 6.61 × 10−6 A (15 μm), and 4.57 × 10−6 A (25 μm)  
- Off-currents consistently below 10−12 A across all device variants  
- On/off ratios exceeding 108 in optimal configurations  
- Subthreshold slopes of 0.246 eV/V for m-MZO versus 0.177 eV/V for MZO and 0.107 eV/V for ZnO  

Statistical analysis of multiple devices confirms reproducible performance with less than 5% variation in key parameters, demonstrating the robustness of the fabrication process.  

### Simulation of Electrical Field Distribution  

SILVACO software simulations reveal that the m-MZO architecture reduces maximum electric field intensity by approximately 7% compared to conventional MZO devices (1,410 kV/cm vs. 1,520 kV/cm). This reduction is attributed to decreased interface trap density and the elimination of Zn-related positive oxide charges in the dielectric layer.  

The simulations further demonstrate that the ring structure provides more uniform field distribution along the channel compared to rectangular designs. The combination of optimized device geometry and advanced material engineering enables the exceptional high-voltage performance of the invention.  

The complete implementation demonstrates a novel HVTFT architecture that overcomes limitations of conventional designs, providing superior performance for high-voltage applications in photovoltaic systems and smart glass technologies.
