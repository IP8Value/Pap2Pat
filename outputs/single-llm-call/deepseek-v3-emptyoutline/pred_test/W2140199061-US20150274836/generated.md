# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  
The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics and diagnostics, specifically to monoclonal antibodies targeting mesothelin. More particularly, the invention provides novel mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies that bind to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, demonstrating complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) against mesothelin-expressing cancer cells. The invention further encompasses antibody fragments, immunoconjugates, fusion proteins, pharmaceutical compositions, and methods of using these antibodies for the treatment and diagnosis of cancers associated with mesothelin overexpression, including but not limited to malignant mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, and breast cancer.  

## BACKGROUND  
Mesothelin is a cell surface glycoprotein that is overexpressed in various malignancies while exhibiting limited expression in normal tissues, making it an attractive target for cancer therapy. The mesothelin gene encodes a 71 kDa precursor protein that is processed into a 31 kDa N-terminal secreted protein and a 40 kDa C-terminal membrane-bound mature mesothelin. Current mesothelin-targeted therapies include immunotoxins and monoclonal antibodies such as SS1P and MORAb-009 (amatuximab), which primarily target the N-terminal region (Region I) of mesothelin. These existing therapies rely predominantly on ADCC as their mechanism of action and show limited CDC activity.  

A significant limitation of current mesothelin-targeted therapies is their inability to induce CDC, which has been demonstrated to be an important mechanism for several successful cancer therapeutic antibodies, including rituximab and ofatumumab. The lack of CDC activity in existing mesothelin antibodies is likely due to their binding to epitopes located far from the cell membrane. Antibodies that bind closer to the cell membrane, such as ofatumumab against CD20, have shown superior CDC activity. However, generating antibodies against the C-terminal region of mesothelin has proven challenging due to the poor immunogenicity of this region.  

There remains an unmet need for mesothelin-targeted antibodies that can induce both CDC and ADCC, potentially offering improved therapeutic efficacy against mesothelin-expressing cancers. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, close to the cell membrane, and demonstrate potent CDC and ADCC activity against cancer cells.  

## SUMMARY  
The present invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies, particularly human single-domain antibodies (VH), that specifically bind to the C-terminal region of mesothelin. These antibodies, exemplified by the SD1 antibody, recognize an epitope within residues 539-588 of mesothelin, close to the cell membrane, and demonstrate unique biological properties not seen in existing mesothelin-targeted antibodies.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. An isolated monoclonal antibody or antigen-binding fragment thereof that specifically binds to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, wherein the antibody demonstrates complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) against mesothelin-expressing cancer cells.  
2. The SD1 antibody, a human single-domain antibody (VH) that binds to residues 539-588 of mesothelin with a dissociation equilibrium constant (KD) of approximately 13-16 nM.  
3. Antibody fusion proteins, particularly SD1-hFc, comprising the SD1 VH domain fused to human IgG1 Fc, which retains high affinity binding to mesothelin and demonstrates both CDC and ADCC activity.  
4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies or fusion proteins of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.  
5. Methods of treating mesothelin-expressing cancers by administering a therapeutically effective amount of the antibodies or fusion proteins of the invention.  
6. Methods of detecting mesothelin-expressing cancer cells using the antibodies of the invention.  

The antibodies of the invention show superior antitumor activity compared to existing mesothelin-targeted antibodies, including:  
- Potent CDC activity against mesothelin-expressing cancer cell lines (30-40% specific lysis)  
- Strong ADCC activity (20-40% specific lysis) using both human PBMCs and purified NK cells  
- Significant inhibition of tumor growth in xenograft mouse models (approximately 70% reduction in tumor volume)  
- Specific binding to native mesothelin on cancer cell surfaces without binding to mesothelin-negative cells  

These properties make the antibodies of the invention promising candidates for improved mesothelin-targeted cancer therapy, particularly for cancers such as mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, and cholangiocarcinoma where mesothelin is highly expressed.

### II. Terms and Methods  
The term "mesothelin" refers to the 40 kDa mature, cell surface-bound form of the mesothelin protein (residues 296-598 of the precursor protein) and includes naturally occurring variants and isoforms.  

"Specific binding" refers to binding with an affinity (KD) of at least 100 nM, preferably at least 50 nM, more preferably at least 20 nM, and most preferably about 13-16 nM as demonstrated for SD1.  

"Complement-dependent cytotoxicity" (CDC) refers to the ability of an antibody to induce target cell lysis through activation of the complement cascade, measured by standard LDH release assays with at least 10%, preferably at least 20%, more preferably at least 30% specific lysis at antibody concentrations of 100 μg/mL or less.  

"Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity" (ADCC) refers to the ability of an antibody to induce target cell lysis through engagement of Fc receptor-bearing effector cells, measured by standard LDH release assays with at least 10%, preferably at least 20%, more preferably at least 30% specific lysis at effector:target ratios of 5:1 or greater.  

"Single-domain antibody" refers to an antibody fragment consisting of a single VH domain that retains antigen-binding capacity, such as the SD1 VH domain of the invention.  

### III. Introduction  
The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that antibodies targeting the C-terminal region of mesothelin (residues 539-588) demonstrate unique biological properties, particularly the ability to induce both CDC and ADCC against mesothelin-expressing cancer cells. This contrasts with existing mesothelin-targeted antibodies that bind to the N-terminal region (Region I) and primarily induce only ADCC.  

The invention encompasses the SD1 antibody, a human single-domain antibody isolated from a phage display library using a C-terminal mesothelin peptide (residues 539-588) as the target antigen. SD1 was converted to an IgG-like format (SD1-hFc) by fusion to human IgG1 Fc for therapeutic applications. SD1-hFc demonstrates:  
1. High affinity binding to both the C-terminal peptide (KD = 16.08 nM) and full-length mesothelin (KD = 13.59 nM)  
2. Specific binding to native mesothelin on cancer cell surfaces without cross-reactivity to mesothelin-negative cells  
3. Potent CDC activity (30-40% specific lysis) against multiple mesothelin-expressing cancer cell lines  
4. Strong ADCC activity (20-40% specific lysis) mediated by both human PBMCs and purified NK cells  
5. Significant inhibition of tumor growth in xenograft models (approximately 70% reduction in tumor volume)  

These properties make SD1 and related antibodies of the invention superior to existing mesothelin-targeted therapeutics, particularly for cancers where mesothelin is highly expressed, such as mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, and cholangiocarcinoma.  

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies  
The invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the C-terminal region of mesothelin (residues 539-588), exemplified by the SD1 antibody. These antibodies are characterized by:  

1. Binding specificity:  
   - Bind to residues 539-588 of mesothelin  
   - Do not compete with N-terminal binding antibodies (e.g., SS1, HN1)  
   - Do not inhibit mesothelin-MUC16 interaction  
   - Bind native, cell surface-expressed mesothelin but not denatured mesothelin in Western blots  

2. High affinity binding:  
   - KD of approximately 13-16 nM for both full-length mesothelin and C-terminal peptide  
   - Specific binding demonstrated by ELISA, flow cytometry, and immunoprecipitation  

3. Biological activity:  
   - Induce C1q binding to mesothelin-expressing cancer cells  
   - Demonstrate dose-dependent CDC activity  
   - Show no direct inhibition of tumor cell proliferation  

The antibodies may be in various formats including:  
- Full-length IgG (e.g., human IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, or IgG4)  
- Single-domain antibodies (VH)  
- Fc fusion proteins (e.g., SD1-hFc)  
- Antibody fragments (Fab, F(ab')2, scFv)  

Preferred embodiments include human or humanized antibodies to minimize immunogenicity for therapeutic applications. The antibodies may be produced by recombinant DNA technology in mammalian expression systems (e.g., HEK-293F cells) with yields exceeding 70 mg/L.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Production and Characterization of SD1-hFc**  

The SD1 human single-domain antibody was isolated from a phage display library by panning against a C-terminal mesothelin peptide (residues 539-588). The SD1 VH domain was fused to human IgG1 Fc (SD1-hFc) and expressed in HEK-293F cells.  

Key results:  
- SD1-hFc was produced at >70 mg/L in culture supernatant  
- Purity exceeded 95% by SDS-PAGE  
- Binds full-length mesothelin with KD = 13.59 nM  
- Binds C-terminal peptide with KD = 16.08 nM  
- Does not bind mouse mesothelin or irrelevant proteins  

### Example 2  
**In Vitro Antitumor Activity of SD1-hFc**  

SD1-hFc was tested for CDC and ADCC activity against mesothelin-expressing cancer cell lines:  

1. CDC activity:  
   - 40% specific lysis of A431/H9 cells  
   - 30% specific lysis of NCI-H226 mesothelioma cells  
   - No activity against mesothelin-negative A431 cells  
   - Dose-dependent C1q binding to target cells  

2. ADCC activity:  
   - 40% specific lysis of A431/H9 cells with human PBMCs  
   - 20% specific lysis with purified human NK cells (E:T = 5:1)  
   - No activity against mesothelin-negative cells  

## DISCUSSION  

The SD1 antibody of the invention represents a significant advance in mesothelin-targeted therapy by demonstrating unique CDC activity not seen with existing mesothelin antibodies. This activity results from SD1's binding to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, close to the cell membrane, enabling efficient complement activation.  

Key advantages of SD1 include:  
1. Fully human sequence minimizing immunogenicity  
2. Dual mechanism of action (CDC + ADCC)  
3. High affinity and specificity for mesothelin  
4. Potent antitumor activity in vitro and in vivo  

These properties suggest SD1-based therapeutics may offer improved efficacy against mesothelin-expressing cancers compared to current therapies like MORAb-009.  

## Example 3  

### Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies for Detecting Cancer in a Subject or Confirming the Diagnosis of Cancer in a Subject  

The SD1 antibody was evaluated for its ability to detect mesothelin-expressing cancer cells in patient samples:  

Methods:  
- Flow cytometry analysis of tumor cell suspensions  
- Immunohistochemistry of tumor tissue sections  
- ELISA detection of shed mesothelin in serum  

Results:  
- SD1 specifically stained mesothelin-expressing tumor cells  
- No staining of mesothelin-negative cells or normal tissues  
- Detected mesothelin in 90% of mesothelioma cases  
- Detected mesothelin in 70% of ovarian cancer cases  

These results demonstrate the utility of SD1 for cancer diagnosis and monitoring.  

### Example 4  
**In Vivo Antitumor Activity of SD1-hFc**  

SD1-hFc was tested in a xenograft model of A431/H9 tumors in nude mice:  

Methods:  
- Mice were treated with SD1-hFc (50 mg/kg) or PBS control  
- Tumor volumes measured over 20 days  

Results:  
- SD1-hFc treatment reduced tumor volume by ~70%  
- No significant toxicity observed  
- Demonstrated both CDC and ADCC activity in mice  

These results confirm the therapeutic potential of SD1-based antibodies for treating mesothelin-expressing cancers.
