# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Glaucoma remains the leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide, with elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) being the primary modifiable risk factor. Current surgical interventions, including trabeculectomy, glaucoma drainage implants (GDIs), and minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS), aim to facilitate aqueous humor (AH) outflow but are frequently compromised by fibrotic encapsulation. Fibrosis around conventional GDIs, for instance, obstructs fluid drainage and diminishes long-term efficacy. Despite advances in surgical techniques and adjunctive antifibrotic agents such as mitomycin-C and 5-fluorouracil, postoperative scarring persists as a significant limitation.  

Existing GDIs, such as the Ahmed Glaucoma Valve (AGV), employ rigid or semi-rigid plates that mechanically disrupt surrounding tissues, provoking chronic inflammation and fibrous capsule formation. Modifications to implant materials, including flexible silicone designs, have yielded marginal improvements but fail to address the fundamental issue of mechanical mismatch between the implant and ocular tissues. Furthermore, conventional implants disrupt local cellular and vascular networks, perpetuating an inflammatory microenvironment. There exists a critical unmet need for a glaucoma drainage device that minimizes fibrotic responses while maintaining effective AH outflow.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses a novel glaucoma drainage implant comprising a microfluidic meshwork designed to suppress fibrotic encapsulation. The meshwork integrates two key innovations: (1) interconnected, cellular-scale microfluidic channels that facilitate AH drainage while mimicking native tissue architecture, and (2) ultra-flexible materials that conform to ocular curvature and movements, eliminating mechanical strain at the implant-tissue interface.  

In one embodiment, the meshwork features a grid period of 100 μm, channel outer diameters of 20 μm, and inner diameters of 8 μm, optimized via finite element simulations to achieve an AH outflow rate of 2 μL/min at 10 mmHg pressure. The meshwork’s sub-10 μm thickness and flexibility (<1 MPa elastic modulus) reduce interfacial forces that trigger fibrosis. Preclinical studies in rabbit models demonstrate near-complete suppression of fibrotic capsule formation (1.3 ± 0.6 μm vs. 89 ± 6.6 μm for conventional AGV plates). The invention further includes methods for fabricating the meshwork via photolithography and deploying it surgically without sutures, leveraging its self-adhering properties.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

- **Microfluidic meshwork**: A porous, flexible structure comprising interconnected channels with cellular-scale dimensions (≤50 μm), fabricated from biocompatible polymers (e.g., SU-8 photoresist) and capable of conducting fluids.  
- **Fibrotic encapsulation**: The formation of a collagen-rich scar tissue layer around an implant, typically exceeding 50 μm in thickness, that impedes fluid flow.  
- **Mechanical mismatch**: A disparity in stiffness between an implant (>1 GPa for conventional GDIs) and surrounding tissues (~1 kPa for episclera), leading to chronic inflammation.  

### Glaucoma Drainage Meshwork  

The meshwork is a monolithic structure with a 7 mm × 7 mm footprint and 4 μm thickness, patterned as a grid with 100 μm periodicity (FIG. 2). Each microchannel comprises a lumen of 8 μm diameter, enclosed by 6 μm-thick walls, yielding a porosity of 35%. The meshwork’s Young’s modulus is engineered to match episcleral tissue (0.5–1.5 kPa) via selective polymer crosslinking. Channels are arranged in a fractal branching pattern to maximize fluid conductance while minimizing shear stress on AH (<0.1 Pa). Surface modifications, such as plasma-treated hydrophilic coatings, further enhance wettability and prevent protein adhesion.  

### Methods of Making  

The meshwork is fabricated via a multi-step photolithographic process:  
1. A silicon wafer is coated with a nickel sacrificial layer and patterned with SU-8 photoresist to define channel walls.  
2. Sacrificial photoresist (LOR 5A/AZ1505) is deposited and lithographically structured to form channel cores.  
3. A final SU-8 layer encapsulates the channels, followed by nickel etching in dilute hydrochloric acid to release the meshwork.  
4. The device is sterilized via autoclaving (121°C, 15 psi) and stored in phosphate-buffered saline until implantation.  

### Methods of Use  

Surgical implantation involves:  
1. Conjunctival peritomy at the supratemporal limbus, followed by blunt dissection to create a sub-Tenon’s pocket.  
2. Unfolding the meshwork (pre-loaded on a delivery cartridge) and positioning it 6 mm posterior to the limbus without sutures.  
3. Closing the conjunctiva with 8-0 Vicryl sutures. Postoperative care includes topical steroids (e.g., prednisolone acetate 1%) tapered over 4 weeks.  

## EXPERIMENTAL EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: A Novel Flexible Microfluidic Meshwork to Reduce Fibrosis in Glaucoma Surgery  

**Objective**: To compare fibrotic responses to the microfluidic meshwork versus conventional AGV plates in rabbits.  

**Methods**:  
- **Implants**: Meshwork (7 mm × 7 mm, 4 μm thick) vs. AGV PF7 silicone plates.  
- **Animal model**: Bilateral implantation in 3 New Zealand rabbits (n=6 eyes), randomized to treatment.  
- **Histology**: Capsule thickness measured via hematoxylin-eosin staining at 3 months.  

**Results**:  
- Meshwork elicited minimal fibrosis (1.3 ± 0.6 μm) vs. AGV (89 ± 6.6 μm; p=0.002).  
- Inflammation was localized to <10% of meshwork area where folding occurred.  

**Conclusion**: The microfluidic meshwork virtually eliminates fibrotic encapsulation, addressing a major limitation of current GDIs.
