# **DESCRIPTION**  

## **BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION**  

### **1. Field of the Invention**  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, particularly breast cancer prognosis and therapeutic decision-making. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel biomarkers, namely pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc), for predicting clinical outcomes in hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) luminal breast cancer. The invention further encompasses diagnostic methods, prognostic stratification tools, and therapeutic applications based on the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in breast cancer patients.  

### **2. The Prior Art**  

Breast cancer (BC) is a highly heterogeneous disease with distinct molecular subtypes that influence prognosis and treatment response. Among these, luminal breast cancers—particularly HR+/HER2− tumors—exhibit variable clinical outcomes, with luminal B tumors demonstrating higher aggressiveness and worse prognosis compared to luminal A tumors. Current prognostic tools rely on clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, histological grade, and multigene expression assays (e.g., OncotypeDx, MammaPrint). However, these methods have limitations in accurately predicting recurrence risk, especially in premenopausal women and patients with low-risk genomic profiles.  

Metabolic reprogramming, including aerobic glycolysis (Warburg effect) and enhanced O-GlcNAcylation, plays a crucial role in cancer progression. PKM2, a key glycolytic enzyme, is overexpressed in breast tumors and associated with poor clinical outcomes. Similarly, O-GlcNAc modification, regulated by the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP), contributes to tumor malignancy and therapy resistance. Despite these findings, prior art lacks a validated metabolic biomarker panel for HR+/HER2− breast cancer prognosis. Existing methods do not integrate PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression with clinical parameters to refine risk stratification.  

Thus, there remains an unmet need for improved prognostic tools that leverage metabolic markers to enhance predictive accuracy and guide personalized treatment strategies in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.  

## **SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION**  

The present invention addresses the limitations of prior art by providing a novel prognostic method based on the combined assessment of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. **Identification of Prognostic Biomarkers**: High expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are significantly associated with poor clinical outcomes, including reduced disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS).  
2. **Diagnostic and Prognostic Applications**: Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in tumor tissues serves as a cost-effective and clinically feasible prognostic tool.  
3. **Integration with Clinical Parameters**: Combining PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression with tumor stage (T stage) or clinical treatment score post-5 years (CTS5) improves prognostic accuracy, as demonstrated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.  
4. **Therapeutic Implications**: The invention enables risk stratification to guide adjuvant therapy decisions, particularly in premenopausal women and patients with luminal B subtype.  

The invention thus provides a robust, metabolism-based prognostic assay that enhances existing clinical tools for HR+/HER2− breast cancer management.  

## **DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT**  

### **Definition**  

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:  

- **PKM2 (Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2)**: A glycolytic enzyme that regulates Warburg metabolism in cancer cells, with roles in tumor progression and metastasis.  
- **O-GlcNAc (O-linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine)**: A post-translational modification involved in nutrient sensing, signaling, and cancer cell survival.  
- **HR+/HER2− Luminal Breast Cancer**: A molecular subtype of breast cancer characterized by estrogen receptor (ER) and/or progesterone receptor (PR) positivity and HER2 negativity.  
- **CTS5 (Clinical Treatment Score Post-5 Years)**: A prognostic tool incorporating tumor size, grade, nodal status, and patient age to estimate late recurrence risk.  

### **High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer**  

Analysis of the METABRIC dataset revealed that high PKM2 expression (top quartile) correlates with significantly worse 10-year overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) in luminal breast cancer patients (p = 0.005 and p = 0.045, respectively). Immunohistochemical (IHC) validation in a retrospective cohort (n = 162) demonstrated that PKM2-high tumors exhibit increased recurrence risk (p < 0.001) and reduced DFS (p = 0.032). These findings establish PKM2 as an independent prognostic marker in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.  

### **High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2− Breast Cancer**  

IHC analysis revealed that high O-GlcNAc expression is significantly associated with poor DFS (p = 0.038). Strikingly, the combined high expression of both PKM2 and O-GlcNAc further enhances prognostic discrimination, with double-positive tumors showing the worst DFS outcomes (p = 0.004). This synergistic effect suggests that PKM2 and O-GlcNAc contribute to tumor aggressiveness through interconnected metabolic pathways.  

### **Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis**  

Multivariate Cox regression and logistic regression models were employed to evaluate the prognostic performance of PKM2, O-GlcNAc, and clinical parameters. The triple-marker model incorporating **T stage, PKM2, and O-GlcNAc** demonstrated the highest predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.722), outperforming individual markers or CTS5-based models. ROC analysis confirmed that the integration of metabolic biomarkers with clinical parameters enhances risk stratification in HR+/HER2− breast cancer.  

### **Conclusion**  

The invention provides a novel prognostic assay based on PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression, offering improved accuracy over existing tools. By leveraging metabolic dysregulation in breast cancer, the invention enables personalized treatment strategies and better clinical outcomes for HR+/HER2− patients.
