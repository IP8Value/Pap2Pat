# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) targeting the prolactin receptor (PRLR), compositions comprising such ADCs, and methods of using the same for the treatment of cancer, particularly breast cancer. More specifically, the invention provides human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies conjugated to cytotoxic maytansinoid agents through non-cleavable linkers, wherein the ADCs demonstrate high binding affinity to PRLR, efficient internalization into PRLR-expressing cells, and potent antitumor activity against breast cancer cells with low-to-moderate PRLR expression levels. The invention further encompasses methods of treating PRLR-positive cancers by administering these ADCs alone or in combination with hormonal therapies.  

## BACKGROUND  

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with triple-negative breast cancers (TNBC) and hormone-refractory cases presenting particular therapeutic challenges. Current targeted therapies predominantly address tumors expressing estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), leaving significant unmet medical needs for patients whose tumors lack these biomarkers.  

The prolactin receptor (PRLR), a member of the class I cytokine receptor family, has emerged as a potential therapeutic target due to its overexpression in malignant breast epithelium compared to normal tissues. While PRLR signaling contributes to mammary gland development and lactation, accumulating evidence suggests its involvement in breast cancer pathogenesis. Several studies have demonstrated that PRL can stimulate proliferation of human breast cancer cells in vitro and in vivo, implicating the PRL/PRLR axis in tumor progression.  

Previous attempts to target PRLR in cancer have employed various strategies including PRL-based antagonists and monoclonal antibodies. However, these approaches have shown limited clinical efficacy, likely due to insufficient disruption of PRLR signaling pathways alone. Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) represent an alternative strategy that combines the target specificity of antibodies with the potent cytotoxicity of chemotherapeutic agents. The clinical success of ADCs such as ado-trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla®) in HER2-positive breast cancer demonstrates the potential of this approach when applied to appropriately selected targets.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel anti-PRLR antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) exhibiting superior properties for the treatment of PRLR-expressing cancers. The ADCs comprise human monoclonal antibodies specifically binding PRLR, conjugated to cytotoxic maytansinoid agents through stable linkers. Key features of the invention include:  

1) Human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies (e.g., REGN2878) demonstrating high affinity binding to human PRLR (KD ≈ 1.05 nM) and effective blockade of PRL-mediated signaling;  

2) Antibody-drug conjugates (e.g., REGN2878-DM1) wherein the antibodies are conjugated to DM1 via a non-cleavable SMCC linker, with drug-to-antibody ratios (DAR) averaging approximately 3.4;  

3) ADCs that specifically internalize into PRLR-expressing cells and undergo lysosomal degradation to release cytotoxic payloads;  

4) Potent in vitro cytotoxicity against breast cancer cell lines expressing varying levels of PRLR, including cells with relatively low PRLR expression (5,000-8,000 receptors/cell);  

5) Significant antitumor activity in multiple breast cancer xenograft models, including complete tumor regression in some models at therapeutic doses;  

6) Enhanced antitumor effects when combined with hormonal therapies such as fulvestrant; and  

7) Favorable pharmacokinetic properties with sustained serum levels following administration.  

The invention further provides methods of treating PRLR-positive cancers by administering these ADCs, either as monotherapy or in combination with other anticancer agents. Diagnostic methods for identifying patients likely to respond to the therapy based on PRLR expression levels are also encompassed.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"PRLR" refers to the human prolactin receptor, a single-pass transmembrane protein of the class I cytokine receptor family, including all naturally occurring isoforms, variants, and polymorphic forms thereof.  

"Antibody-drug conjugate" or "ADC" refers to a monoclonal antibody chemically linked to a cytotoxic agent, wherein the antibody specifically binds to PRLR and delivers the cytotoxic agent to PRLR-expressing cells.  

"DM1" refers to the maytansinoid derivative N2'-deacetyl-N2'-(3-mercapto-1-oxopropyl)-maytansine, a potent microtubule-targeting agent.  

"SMCC" refers to succinimidyl-4-(N-maleimidomethyl)cyclohexane-1-carboxylate, a heterobifunctional crosslinker used to conjugate antibodies to cytotoxic agents.  

"Drug-to-antibody ratio" or "DAR" refers to the average number of cytotoxic drug molecules conjugated per antibody molecule.  

"Internalization" refers to the process by which the antibody-PRLR complex is taken up into the cell following binding to cell surface PRLR, including both constitutive and ligand-induced internalization pathways.  

### Anti-PRLR Antibodies Comprising Fc Variants  

The invention provides human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies comprising Fc variants optimized for specific properties. In preferred embodiments, the antibodies comprise human IgG1 constant regions with modifications to enhance stability, pharmacokinetics, or effector functions. The Fc region may include amino acid substitutions that modulate antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), though these effector functions are not required for ADC activity.  

Particular embodiments utilize Fc regions engineered to prolong serum half-life through increased binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). The antibodies may further include modifications to reduce immunogenicity, such as removal of potential T-cell epitopes from the framework regions.  

### Biological Characteristics of the Antibodies  

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention exhibit several biologically important characteristics:  

1) High affinity binding to human PRLR with dissociation constants (KD) in the low nanomolar range (1.05-1.24 nM);  

2) Specificity for human and non-human primate PRLR, with no detectable binding to rodent PRLR;  

3) Effective blockade of PRL binding to PRLR, with IC50 values of approximately 4-5 nM in competitive binding assays;  

4) Inhibition of PRL-mediated signaling through the JAK2/STAT5 pathway, with IC50 values of approximately 0.4 nM in reporter gene assays;  

5) Rapid internalization following binding to cell surface PRLR, with intracellular localization observable within 1 hour of binding;  

6) Efficient trafficking to lysosomes for degradation, facilitating release of cytotoxic payloads from ADCs; and  

7) Stability in physiological conditions, maintaining binding affinity and structural integrity under conditions encountered during manufacturing, storage, and administration.  

### Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)  

The antibody-drug conjugates of the invention comprise anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies covalently linked to cytotoxic maytansinoid agents through stable linkers. In preferred embodiments:  

1) The antibody component is a human monoclonal antibody (e.g., REGN2878) that specifically binds PRLR with high affinity;  

2) The cytotoxic agent is DM1 (a maytansine derivative) or related maytansinoids;  

3) The linker is a non-cleavable SMCC linker that remains stable in circulation but allows intracellular release of the cytotoxic payload following lysosomal degradation;  

4) The average drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) ranges from 3 to 4, with preferred embodiments having DAR values of approximately 3.4;  

5) The conjugates are >95% monomeric with <0.5% free linker-drug, as determined by size-exclusion HPLC and reverse-phase HPLC;  

6) The ADCs maintain high binding affinity to PRLR following conjugation, with KD values comparable to unconjugated antibodies (1.24 nM for REGN2878-DM1); and  

7) The ADCs demonstrate potent cytotoxicity against PRLR-expressing cells while showing minimal activity against PRLR-negative cells.  

### Targeting ADCs to Cells Expressing Low Levels of PRLR  

A surprising and advantageous feature of the invention is the ability of the anti-PRLR ADCs to effectively target and kill cells expressing relatively low levels of PRLR. While many ADCs require high target expression levels for efficacy (typically >50,000 receptors/cell), the present ADCs demonstrate:  

1) Potent cytotoxicity against cells expressing as few as 5,000-8,000 PRLR molecules per cell (e.g., MCF7 cells);  

2) Significant antitumor activity in xenograft models derived from cells with endogenous PRLR expression levels (e.g., T47D cells with ~27,000 receptors/cell);  

3) Complete tumor regressions in models with engineered PRLR overexpression (e.g., MCF7/PRLR cells with ~185,000 receptors/cell); and  

4) Activity in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models with heterogeneous PRLR expression.  

This ability to target cells with low-to-moderate PRLR expression significantly expands the potential patient population that may benefit from the therapy, as many breast tumors express PRLR at levels below those typically considered adequate for ADC targeting.  

### Class I Cytokine Receptor Targeting  

The invention demonstrates for the first time the successful application of ADC technology to target a class I cytokine receptor. PRLR shares structural features with other members of this receptor family, including:  

1) A single transmembrane domain;  

2) Extracellular cytokine receptor homology domains;  

3) Lack of intrinsic kinase activity;  

4) Dependence on associated kinases (e.g., JAK2) for signal transduction; and  

5) Constitutive internalization and lysosomal degradation pathways.  

The successful targeting of PRLR with ADCs suggests that similar approaches may be applicable to other class I cytokine receptors implicated in cancer, including growth hormone receptor, erythropoietin receptor, and leptin receptor.  

### Epitope Mapping and Related Technologies  

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention bind specific epitopes on the extracellular domain of PRLR that are critical for their functional properties. Key features of the epitope binding include:  

1) Binding to regions of PRLR involved in PRL binding, enabling effective blockade of ligand-receptor interaction;  

2) Engagement of epitopes that do not prevent constitutive internalization of the receptor;  

3) Binding to regions conserved between human and non-human primate PRLR but distinct from rodent PRLR; and  

4) Recognition of epitopes that remain accessible in the context of PRLR dimerization or oligomerization.  

Epitope mapping may be performed using techniques such as hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry, alanine scanning mutagenesis, or crystallography of antibody-antigen complexes.  

### Preparation of Human Antibodies  

The human anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention may be prepared using various technologies:  

1) Immunization of transgenic mice (e.g., VelocImmune® mice) expressing human immunoglobulin genes with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain;  

2) Generation of hybridomas or direct isolation of antigen-binding B cells from immunized animals;  

3) Cloning of human immunoglobulin variable region genes from selected antibodies and joining to human constant region genes;  

4) Production of antibodies in mammalian expression systems (e.g., CHO cells) under conditions ensuring proper folding and post-translational modifications;  

5) Purification using affinity chromatography (e.g., protein A/G) followed by additional polishing steps to remove aggregates and impurities; and  

6) Characterization of binding affinity, specificity, and functional activity through biochemical and cell-based assays.  

### Bioequivalents  

The invention encompasses not only the specific anti-PRLR antibodies and ADCs described herein, but also bioequivalent molecules with similar structural and functional characteristics. Bioequivalents include:  

1) Antibodies with amino acid sequences having at least 90%, 95%, or 99% identity to the reference sequences while maintaining PRLR binding and internalization properties;  

2) Antibodies with conservative amino acid substitutions in framework regions that do not significantly affect antigen binding;  

3) Antibody fragments (e.g., Fab, scFv) capable of binding PRLR and being conjugated to cytotoxic agents;  

4) ADCs utilizing different maytansinoid derivatives (e.g., DM4) or other cytotoxic payloads (e.g., auristatins, calicheamicins) with similar mechanisms of action;  

5) ADCs with alternative linker chemistries (e.g., cleavable peptide linkers, hydrazone linkers) that maintain stability in circulation while allowing intracellular drug release; and  

6) Antibodies or ADCs with modified glycosylation patterns that alter pharmacokinetic or effector functions without compromising target binding or internalization.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1. Generation of Anti-PRLR Antibodies  

Human anti-PRLR monoclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing VelocImmune® mice (harboring human immunoglobulin gene segments) with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain. Following immunization, splenocytes were harvested and used to generate hybridomas or for direct isolation of antigen-binding B cells.  

From over 300 antigen-binding clones identified, REGN2878 was selected as a lead candidate based on superior performance in comprehensive screening assays evaluating:  

1) Binding affinity to human PRLR by surface plasmon resonance (KD = 1.05 nM);  

2) Specificity for human versus rodent PRLR;  

3) Inhibition of PRL binding to PRLR (IC50 = 5.0 nM);  

4) Blockade of PRL-mediated signaling (IC50 = 0.4 nM in STAT5 reporter assay);  

5) Efficient internalization into PRLR-expressing cells; and  

6) Favorable biophysical properties for manufacturing and conjugation.  

The variable region genes of REGN2878 were cloned and joined to human IgG1 constant region genes for production in CHO cells. The resulting antibody demonstrated high expression yields (>1 g/L) and maintained stability during purification and storage.

### Control Constructs Used in the Following Examples  

For comparative studies, the following control constructs were generated:  

1) Isotype control antibody: A human IgG1 monoclonal antibody of irrelevant specificity, produced and purified using identical methods as REGN2878;  

2) Control ADC: The isotype control antibody conjugated to DM1 via SMCC linker using identical conjugation protocols as REGN2878-DM1, with comparable DAR (3.4);  

3) Unconjugated REGN2878: The parental anti-PRLR antibody without cytotoxic conjugation; and  

4) PRLR-negative cell lines: Engineered to confirm target-specificity of ADC effects.  

These controls enabled differentiation of target-specific effects from nonspecific or Fc-mediated activities in subsequent experiments.  

### Example 3. Surface Plasmon Resonance Derived Binding Affinities and Kinetic Constants of Human Monoclonal Anti-PRLR Antibodies  

The binding kinetics of REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 to human PRLR were characterized by surface plasmon resonance using a Biacore T200 instrument. Antibodies were captured on sensor chips coated with anti-human Fc antibody, and binding to monomeric human PRLR extracellular domain was measured at concentrations ranging from 3.125 to 200 nM.  

Key findings:  

1) REGN2878 exhibited high affinity binding to human PRLR with KD = 1.05 nM (ka = 4.2 × 10^5 M^-1s^-1, kd = 4.4 × 10^-4 s^-1);  

2) REGN2878-DM1 maintained strong binding affinity post-conjugation (KD = 1.24 nM; ka = 3.8 × 10^5 M^-1s^-1, kd = 4.7 × 10^-4 s^-1);  

3) Both molecules bound cynomolgus monkey PRLR with similar affinity (KD ≈ 1.6-1.9 nM), supporting translational studies;  

4) No detectable binding to mouse or rat PRLR was observed at concentrations up to 1 μM; and  

5) Binding was specific to PRLR, with no cross-reactivity to other class I cytokine receptors tested.  

These results confirm that conjugation of DM1 to REGN2878 does not significantly impair its binding to PRLR, a critical requirement for ADC functionality.  

### Example 4A. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Bind to Endogenous and Overexpressed PRLR Cell Lines  

Flow cytometry was used to evaluate REGN2878 binding to various breast cancer cell lines with differing PRLR expression levels:  

1) MCF7 cells: Moderate endogenous PRLR expression (~5,000-8,000 receptors/cell);  

2) T47D cells: Higher endogenous PRLR expression (~27,000 receptors/cell);  

3) MCF7/PRLR cells: Engineered to overexpress PRLR (~185,000 receptors/cell); and  

4) PRLR-negative controls: Confirmed lack of binding.  

Results demonstrated:  

1) Dose-dependent binding of REGN2878 to all PRLR-expressing cell lines;  

2) Binding intensity correlated with PRLR expression levels;  

3) No binding to PRLR-negative controls;  

4) Comparable binding patterns between REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1; and  

5) No binding of isotype control antibodies to any cell line.  

These findings confirm the specificity of REGN2878 for PRLR and its ability to recognize both endogenous and overexpressed receptor across different cellular contexts.  

### Example 4B. Anti-PRLR Antibodies are Internalized by PRLR-Expressing Cells In Vitro  

The internalization kinetics of REGN2878 were evaluated using fluorescence microscopy with Alexa488-labeled antibody. Key observations:  

1) Rapid internalization within 1 hour at 37°C in both T47D and MCF7/PRLR cells;  

2) Intracellular localization consistent with lysosomal trafficking;  

3) Minimal internalization at 4°C (control for energy-dependent processes);  

4) Internalization occurred in the absence of PRL stimulation, indicating constitutive receptor turnover;  

5) Comparable internalization rates between REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1; and  

6) No internalization in PRLR-negative cells or with control antibodies.  

These results demonstrate that anti-PRLR antibodies are efficiently internalized following binding to cell surface PRLR, a critical property for effective ADC-mediated drug delivery.  

### Example 5. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Inhibit PRL-Mediated Receptor Activation in Cells Expressing Human PRLR  

The ability of REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 to inhibit PRL-mediated signaling was assessed using a HEK293 cell line engineered to express PRLR and a STAT5-responsive luciferase reporter.  

Key findings:  

1) PRL stimulated dose-dependent luciferase activity (EC50 = 2.1 nM);  

2) Both REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 potently inhibited PRL-induced signaling (IC50 ≈ 0.4 nM);  

3) Complete inhibition of signaling was achieved at antibody concentrations ≥10 nM;  

4) Inhibition was specific, as control antibodies showed no effect; and  

5) Similar potency was observed in T47D cells assessing endogenous STAT5 phosphorylation.  

These results confirm that the anti-PRLR antibodies functionally block PRLR signaling while demonstrating that conjugation to DM1 does not impair this activity.  

### Example 6. Preparation and Characterization of Anti-PRLR Antibody Drug Conjugates  

REGN2878-DM1 was prepared by conjugating REGN2878 to DM1 via SMCC linker chemistry:  

1) Antibody (10 mg/mL) in HEPES buffer (pH 8.0) was reacted with 6-fold molar excess of SMCC-DM1 for 1 hour at ambient temperature;  

2) The conjugate was purified by size-exclusion chromatography and sterile filtered;  

3) Drug loading was determined by UV spectroscopy and mass spectrometry (average DAR = 3.4);  

4) Purity was assessed by SE-HPLC (>95% monomeric) and RP-HPLC (<0.5% free drug);  

5) Binding affinity was confirmed by Biacore (KD = 1.24 nM); and  

6) Stability was verified under various storage conditions.  

The resulting ADC maintained the target-binding properties of the parental antibody while incorporating sufficient cytotoxic payload for potent antitumor activity.  

### Example 7. Anti-PRLR Antibody-Drug Conjugates Effectively Kill Cells with Low-to-Moderate PRLR Expression Levels as Well as Cells with High PRLR Expression Levels  

The cytotoxic activity of REGN2878-DM1 was evaluated against breast cancer cell lines with varying PRLR expression:  

1) MCF7/PRLR (high expression): IC50 = 0.06 nM;  

2) T47D (moderate expression): IC50 = 0.97 nM;  

3) MCF7 (low expression): IC50 > 100 nM (minimal activity);  

4) PRLR-negative cells: No cytotoxicity at concentrations up to 100 nM;  

5) Control ADC: No cytotoxicity against any PRLR-expressing cell line; and  

6) Unconjugated REGN2878: No cytotoxicity despite PRLR binding.  

Notably, in vivo studies revealed significant antitumor activity even against MCF7 xenografts (low PRLR expression), suggesting that longer exposure times or tumor microenvironment factors may enhance ADC efficacy in vivo compared to in vitro.  

Complete tumor regressions were observed in MCF7/PRLR and T47Dv11 models at doses of 10-15 mg/kg, with combination therapy using fulvestrant showing enhanced efficacy in ER-positive models. These results demonstrate the potent and specific antitumor activity of REGN2878-DM1 across a range of PRLR expression levels relevant to human breast cancers.
