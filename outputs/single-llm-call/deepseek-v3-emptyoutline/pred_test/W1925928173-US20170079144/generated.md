# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of flexible and stretchable electronics, and more specifically to methods of fabricating adhesive-integrated sensors without requiring sacrificial layers or intermediate transfer printing steps. The disclosed technology enables direct integration of thin-film sensors into adhesives through a simplified peel-off process, reducing fabrication complexity while maintaining sensor performance. This approach is particularly applicable to wearable electronics, epidermal sensors, and flexible strain gauges for industrial and consumer applications.  

## BACKGROUND  

Conventional fabrication of flexible electronic sensors typically involves multi-step transfer printing processes that require sacrificial layers, solvent treatments, and intermediate substrates such as polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS). These methods, while effective, introduce complexity, reduce yield, and increase production time due to the need for precise alignment and controlled release rates during transfer. Additionally, the use of sacrificial layers necessitates additional processing steps such as solvent immersion and cleaning, which can damage delicate sensor structures or introduce defects.  

Existing approaches for producing ultra-thin flexible sensors often rely on transfer printing techniques where sensors are first fabricated on a donor substrate, released using a sacrificial layer, and then transferred to an intermediate stamp before final placement on the target adhesive. This multi-step process is time-consuming and requires specialized equipment and expertise to achieve high yields. Furthermore, the variability in release rates during sacrificial layer dissolution can lead to inconsistent sensor quality and deformation of the intended patterns.  

There exists a need for a simplified fabrication method that eliminates the need for sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer steps while maintaining the performance and fidelity of flexible sensors. The present invention addresses this need by providing a direct peel-off process that enables efficient integration of sensors into adhesives with reduced processing time and improved yield.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel method for fabricating adhesive-integrated flexible sensors through an inverted fabrication scheme and direct peel-off process. The method involves depositing sensor materials onto a weakly-adhering donor substrate, such as PDMS-coated silicon or glass, in a reversed layer sequence compared to conventional approaches. This inverted structure allows the sensor to be directly peeled off from the donor substrate and integrated into an adhesive without requiring sacrificial layers or intermediate transfer steps.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of a weakly-adhering donor substrate that provides sufficient adhesion to retain the sensor during fabrication but allows easy release during peel-off.  
2. An inverted fabrication sequence where the conductive layer (e.g., gold or chromium) is deposited first onto the donor substrate, followed by a polymer backing layer (e.g., photodefinable polyimide).  
3. Direct integration of the sensor into an adhesive through a mechanical peel-off process, eliminating the need for solvents or intermediate stamps.  
4. Compatibility with various sensor types, including electrical sensors for biopotential measurement and mechanical strain sensors for deformation monitoring.  

The disclosed method reduces fabrication steps by approximately 50% compared to conventional transfer printing approaches while maintaining sensor performance. Electrical sensors produced using this method demonstrate high fidelity in biopotential measurements, such as electroencephalography (EEG), with correlation coefficients exceeding 95% when compared to sensors fabricated using traditional transfer printing.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a streamlined fabrication process for adhesive-integrated flexible sensors, referred to herein as Adhesive-Peeled (AP) sensors. The method involves constructing sensor layers on a weakly-adhering donor substrate in an inverted sequence, followed by direct peel-off onto a target adhesive. This approach eliminates the need for sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer steps, significantly reducing processing time and complexity.  

### Exemplary Embodiments  

In one embodiment, the fabrication process begins with preparation of the weakly-adhering donor substrate. A silicon or glass wafer is coated with a thin layer of PDMS, which serves as the weakly-adhering interface. The PDMS layer is formulated with a reduced ratio of pre-polymer to crosslinker (e.g., 3:1) to increase stiffness and prevent excessive buckling of metal films during subsequent processing.  

The sensor layers are then deposited onto the PDMS-coated donor substrate in an inverted sequence. First, a conductive layer comprising gold (Au) and chromium (Cr) is deposited using physical vapor deposition techniques such as sputtering or electron beam evaporation. The Au film interfaces directly with the PDMS layer, while the Cr film remains exposed at the top surface. This reversed metallization sequence is critical for enabling the subsequent peel-off process.  

Following metallization, a photodefinable polyimide (PPI) layer is applied to form the polymer backing of the sensor. The PPI serves multiple functions: (1) it provides mechanical support for the thin metal films, (2) acts as an electrical insulator, and (3) can be patterned simultaneously during development to define the sensor geometry. After PPI patterning, an etch-back process removes exposed Cr-Au regions, leaving only the desired sensor pattern on the donor substrate.  

The completed AP sensor is then ready for direct integration into an adhesive through a simple mechanical peel-off process. Any adhesive material with sufficient adhesion strength (typically >0.2 N) can be used to peel the sensor from the donor substrate. This includes medical-grade adhesives like Tegaderm or consumer adhesives like Scotch tape. The peel-off process takes approximately 35 seconds, compared to 27 minutes for conventional transfer printing methods.  

### Additional Exemplary Embodiments  

In another embodiment, the fabrication process is adapted for producing mechanical strain sensors. The same inverted fabrication sequence is employed, but the photolithography pattern is modified to create strain-sensitive geometries. These AP strain sensors exhibit a gauge factor of approximately 0.79 and maintain their structural integrity during axial loading. The peel-off process for strain sensors is identical to that for electrical sensors, enabling easy integration into adhesive substrates.  

The method can also be implemented using flexible donor substrates, such as Kapton films, to enable roll-to-roll processing. In this configuration, the weakly-adhering interface is created on the flexible substrate, and the same inverted fabrication sequence is followed. AP sensors produced on flexible donors show no performance differences compared to those made on rigid substrates, while offering potential for high-volume manufacturing through roll-to-roll peel-off processes.  

For electrical sensors, the invention demonstrates particular utility in biopotential measurement applications. AP sensors fabricated using this method successfully record electrophysiological signals including EEG, ECG, and EMG with fidelity comparable to conventional transfer-printed sensors. Time-frequency analysis of simultaneously recorded EEG signals shows strong correlation (r > 0.95) between AP sensors and traditional TP sensors, confirming that the simplified fabrication process does not compromise sensor performance.  

The invention further encompasses variations in materials and processing techniques. For example, alternative conductive materials such as silver or platinum can be used in place of gold, with appropriate modifications to the deposition parameters. Similarly, different polymer backings may be employed depending on application requirements, though photodefinable polyimide is preferred for its combination of electrical insulation, mechanical stability, and biocompatibility.  

The disclosed method represents a significant advancement in flexible sensor fabrication by reducing process complexity while maintaining sensor performance. By eliminating sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer steps, the invention enables more efficient production of adhesive-integrated sensors for wearable, medical, and industrial applications.
