# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The detection of intracellular pathogens, such as *Mycobacterium avium* subspecies *paratuberculosis* (MAP), presents significant challenges in clinical diagnostics due to the pathogen's residence within host immune cells. Conventional methods, including serological immunoassays and polymerase chain reaction (PCR), are hindered by labor-intensive protocols, lengthy processing times, and the requirement for high-purity DNA samples. Furthermore, culture-based detection methods, while effective for some pathogens, are impractical for slow-growing microorganisms like MAP, often requiring weeks or months to yield results. These limitations delay accurate diagnosis and appropriate therapeutic intervention, particularly in diseases such as Crohn’s disease, where MAP has been implicated as a potential causative agent.  

Nanotechnology has emerged as a promising avenue for overcoming these diagnostic hurdles. Among the most promising nanomaterials are magnetic relaxation nanosensors (MRS), which leverage the unique magnetic properties of iron oxide nanoparticles to detect specific biological targets. Traditional MRS-based assays rely on nanoparticle clustering to induce changes in water proton relaxation times (T2). However, these methods often suffer from slower kinetics and reduced sensitivity. A novel approach utilizing low-valency hybridizing MRS (hMRS) has demonstrated rapid and sensitive detection of genomic markers without requiring nanoparticle clustering. This innovation enables the detection of single genome copies of intracellular pathogens in minimally processed clinical samples, offering a transformative solution for point-of-care diagnostics.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a hybridizing magnetic relaxation nanosensor (hMRS) system for the rapid and sensitive detection of intracellular pathogens, particularly *Mycobacterium avium* subspecies *paratuberculosis* (MAP). The hMRS comprises polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles functionalized with oligonucleotides complementary to a conserved genomic marker (IS900) of MAP. Upon binding to the target DNA, the hMRS induces measurable increases in water proton relaxation times (T2), enabling detection without the need for amplification or extensive sample processing.  

Key advantages of the hMRS system include:  
1. **Unprecedented Sensitivity**: The hMRS detects single genome copies of MAP, outperforming nested PCR (nPCR) in sensitivity and speed.  
2. **Minimal Sample Processing**: Crude DNA extracts from clinical samples (e.g., blood, tissue homogenates) are sufficient for detection, eliminating the need for labor-intensive purification steps.  
3. **Rapid Results**: The assay provides results within 30 minutes, compared to hours or weeks required for PCR or culture-based methods.  
4. **Specificity**: The hMRS exhibits no cross-reactivity with non-target mycobacteria or common Gram-positive/-negative bacteria.  
5. **Clinical Utility**: The system accurately correlates MAP presence with Crohn’s disease symptomology, offering superior diagnostic performance over existing methods.  

The invention further encompasses methods for synthesizing hMRS, protocols for detecting MAP in clinical samples, and applications for diagnosing Johne’s disease in cattle and Crohn’s disease in humans.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Design and Synthesis of hMRS  

The hMRS nanoparticles are synthesized via carbodiimide chemistry, wherein propargyl-functionalized polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles are conjugated to azide-modified oligonucleotides complementary to the IS900 genomic marker of MAP. The conjugation is achieved through copper(I)-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition ("click" chemistry), yielding nanoparticles with an average of 55 oligonucleotides per particle. The resulting hMRS exhibit a diameter of 78 ± 3 nm, a zeta potential of –35 mV, and relaxivities (R1 and R2) of 45 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ and 60 mM⁻¹s⁻¹, respectively, at 0.47 Tesla.  

The low valency of the hMRS (55 oligonucleotides per nanoparticle) is critical for achieving high sensitivity. Unlike high-valency systems that require nanoparticle clustering, the hMRS induces T2 increases upon binding to target DNA, enabling rapid detection at low target concentrations. Dynamic light scattering confirms the absence of nanoparticle aggregation, with hMRS-DNA complexes measuring 81 ± 5 nm in diameter.  

### Discussion  

The hMRS system represents a paradigm shift in pathogen detection by leveraging the magnetic relaxation amplification effect. Each binding event between hMRS and target DNA alters the T2 relaxation times of surrounding water molecules, enabling single-genome-copy detection without enzymatic amplification. This contrasts with traditional MRS assays reliant on nanoparticle clustering, which exhibit slower kinetics and higher detection limits.  

The system’s clinical utility is demonstrated in three key applications:  
1. **Detection in Cultured Clinical Isolates**: hMRS identifies MAP in minimally processed cultures with 100% concordance to culture-based nPCR, resolving false negatives caused by low pathogen loads.  
2. **Tissue Screening**: hMRS detects MAP in homogenized tissues from Johne’s disease cattle and Crohn’s disease biopsies, correlating with epitope-based MRS results.  
3. **Blood Testing**: In peripheral blood samples, hMRS outperforms direct and culture-based nPCR in predicting Crohn’s disease, with a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis confirming its diagnostic superiority.  

### Methods  

**Sample Preparation**:  
- Crude DNA is extracted from clinical samples (blood, tissue) via a 30-minute boiling protocol.  
- Pure DNA is isolated using phenol/chloroform/isoamyl alcohol precipitation for comparative PCR studies.  

**hMRS Assay Protocol**:  
1. Incubate 200 µL of hMRS (6 µg Fe/mL) with 1 µL of crude or pure DNA.  
2. Heat samples at 95°C for 3 minutes (twice) to facilitate DNA denaturation and hMRS hybridization.  
3. Cool samples to room temperature and measure T2 relaxation times using a 0.47 T relaxometer.  

**Data Analysis**:  
- Positive samples exhibit ΔT2 > 1.5 ms versus negative controls.  
- Statistical validation is performed via two-proportion z-tests and ROC analysis.  

The invention’s portability is underscored by compatibility with compact NMR instruments, enabling deployment in point-of-care settings.
