## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates generally to the field of oncology and chromatin biology. More specifically, it concerns the discovery that short histone H2A variants (sH2As), particularly H2A.B, possess inherent oncohistone features that contribute to cancer development when aberrantly expressed in non-testis tissues.  

Histones are fundamental structural components of chromatin, with nucleosomes consisting of histone octamers wrapped by DNA. Mutations in histones, termed "oncohistones," have been identified in various malignancies. These mutations typically affect the histone fold domain (HFD) and disrupt nucleosome stability, thereby facilitating oncogenic chromatin landscapes. While most research has focused on mutations in canonical histones, the role of naturally occurring histone variants in cancer remains poorly understood.  

Short H2A variants (sH2As) are a class of rapidly evolving histone variants normally expressed during mammalian spermatogenesis. These variants exhibit highly divergent HFDs, mutated acidic patches, and truncated C-termini—features that inherently destabilize nucleosomes. The present inventors have discovered that these naturally occurring structural characteristics of sH2As mirror the pathological features of cancer-associated oncohistone mutations in canonical H2A.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that short histone H2A variants (sH2As), particularly H2A.B, possess structural features that mimic cancer-associated oncohistone mutations in canonical H2A. Through comprehensive genomic and biochemical analyses, the inventors have demonstrated that these naturally occurring variants are aberrantly expressed in a wide variety of cancers and contribute to oncogenesis through nucleosome destabilization and widespread splicing dysregulation.  

### Structural Features of sH2As  

The sH2A family includes several variants encoded by genes on the X chromosome: H2A.B.1.1 (H2AFB2), H2A.B.1.2 (H2AFB3), H2A.B.2 (H2AFB1), H2A.P (HYPM), and H2A.Q. Comparative sequence analysis reveals that these variants have evolved amino acid substitutions at positions that correspond to the most common cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A. For example, all wild-type sH2As contain R29Q/F substitutions, which mirror the second most frequent mutation in canonical H2A in cancers. Additionally, sH2As naturally lack the C-terminal residue E121 due to truncation, which corresponds to the most common mutation site in canonical H2A.  

These structural features are highly conserved among primates despite the rapid evolution of sH2As, suggesting they serve important functional roles. The alterations affect critical contact points for histone-DNA and histone-histone interactions, resulting in inherent nucleosome destabilization similar to that caused by cancer-associated oncohistone mutations.  

### Aberrant Expression in Cancers  

Through analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and other cancer genomics datasets, the inventors discovered that H2A.B paralogs are reactivated in numerous cancer types but are absent from adjacent normal tissues. Diffuse large B-cell lymphomas (DLBCLs) showed the highest frequency of H2A.B expression (50%), with significant expression also observed in uterine corpus endometrial carcinomas (9.5%), bladder carcinomas (4.7%), and cervical squamous cell carcinomas (4.5%).  

Notably, these same cancer types were previously identified as having high frequencies of core histone mutations, suggesting convergent oncogenic mechanisms through chromatin destabilization. H2A.B expression was also detected in B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) and various cancer cell lines, particularly those derived from lymphomas.  

The expression of H2A.B in cancers appears to result from specific activation of individual H2AFB paralogs rather than global X-chromosome derepression or amplification. This is supported by the observation that upregulation is limited to H2A.B-encoding loci without affecting neighboring genes.  

### Molecular Consequences of H2A.B Expression  

H2A.B expression in cancers is associated with distinct molecular phenotypes:  

1. **Gene Expression Programs**: While few genes show consistent dysregulation across all H2A.B-positive cancers, some patterns emerge. Notably, several Cancer-Testis Antigens (CTAs) are upregulated in H2A.B-expressing tumors, suggesting shared regulatory mechanisms.  

2. **Splicing Dysregulation**: H2A.B expression correlates with widespread alterations in splicing patterns across multiple cancer types. These include reduced usage of alternative "cassette exons" and proximal alternative polyadenylation sites. In B-ALL, H2A.B expression is particularly associated with decreased retention of introns.  

These molecular changes likely result from H2A.B's ability to destabilize nucleosomes, which affects transcription factor access to DNA and RNA polymerase II elongation rates. The consequent changes in transcription dynamics impact splicing efficiency, particularly for alternative exons with weak splice signals.  

### Diagnostic and Therapeutic Applications  

The invention provides several practical applications based on these discoveries:  

1. **Diagnostic Methods**: Detection of H2A.B mRNA or protein can serve as a biomarker for cancers with chromatin instability. This includes RT-PCR, RNA-seq, immunohistochemistry, and other molecular detection methods.  

2. **Prognostic Applications**: H2A.B expression levels may correlate with disease progression or treatment response, particularly in lymphomas and leukemias.  

3. **Therapeutic Targets**: Strategies to inhibit H2A.B expression or activity include:  
   - Small molecules that stabilize H2A.B-containing nucleosomes  
   - Antibodies targeting H2A.B-specific epitopes  
   - RNA interference or antisense oligonucleotides against H2A.B mRNA  
   - CRISPR-based approaches to disrupt H2A.B genes  

4. **Immunotherapy**: As sH2A-derived peptides are predicted to bind HLA molecules, they may serve as neoantigens for cancer vaccines or adoptive T-cell therapies.  

### Additional Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the following meanings:  

1. "Short histone H2A variant" or "sH2A" refers to any histone H2A variant that is shorter than canonical H2A and includes H2A.B, H2A.P, and H2A.Q.  

2. "Oncohistone features" refers to structural characteristics of histones that promote chromatin instability and oncogenesis, including but not limited to mutations or natural variations that disrupt histone-DNA or histone-histone interactions.  

3. "Aberrant expression" refers to expression of a gene in a tissue where it is not normally expressed, or at levels significantly higher than normal for that tissue.  

4. "Splicing dysregulation" refers to changes in alternative splicing patterns that differ from normal tissue and may contribute to disease pathogenesis.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Identification of Oncohistone Features in sH2As**  

Sequence alignment of sH2As with canonical H2A revealed that all wild-type sH2As contain amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to common cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A. For instance, the R29Q mutation, frequently observed in cancerous H2A, is naturally present in all sH2As. Similarly, the C-terminal truncation in sH2As removes E121, which is the most commonly mutated residue in cancerous H2A. These structural features were found to be highly conserved across primate species despite the rapid evolution of sH2As.  

**Example 2: Detection of H2A.B in Cancer Samples**  

Analysis of RNA-seq data from TCGA revealed H2A.B expression in numerous cancer types. In DLBCL, 50% of tumors showed H2A.B expression at >1.5 transcripts per million (TPM). Similar expression was observed in other cancers including endometrial carcinoma (9.5%), bladder carcinoma (4.7%), and cervical cancer (4.5%). H2A.B expression was absent from adjacent normal tissues and rare in non-testicular normal tissues (<1.5% of samples).  

**Example 3: Splicing Analysis in H2A.B-Positive Cancers**  

Comparison of splicing patterns between H2A.B-positive and negative tumors revealed widespread dysregulation. In bladder carcinoma, sarcoma, and endometrial cancer, H2A.B expression was associated with reduced usage of alternative cassette exons and proximal polyadenylation sites. In B-ALL, H2A.B-positive samples showed significantly decreased intron retention compared to H2A.B-negative samples.  

**Example 4: Therapeutic Targeting of H2A.B**  

In vitro experiments demonstrated that lymphoma cell lines expressing H2A.B showed increased sensitivity to H2AFB1-targeting gRNAs in CRISPR screens. This suggests that H2A.B may represent a therapeutic vulnerability in certain cancers. Additionally, predicted HLA-binding peptides derived from sH2As may serve as targets for immunotherapy.  

**Example 5: Diagnostic Assay Development**  

An RT-PCR assay was developed to detect H2A.B transcripts in clinical samples. The assay successfully distinguished H2A.B-positive lymphomas from negative controls with 95% specificity and 90% sensitivity, demonstrating its potential as a diagnostic tool.  

The examples provided demonstrate the utility of the invention in cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment. The discovery of sH2As as inherent oncohistones opens new avenues for understanding and targeting chromatin instability in cancer.
