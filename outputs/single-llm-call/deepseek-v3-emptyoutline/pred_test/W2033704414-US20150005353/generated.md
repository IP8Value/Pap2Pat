# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel compounds that selectively inhibit sphingosine kinase-2 (SphK2), methods of synthesizing said compounds, and their use in the treatment of diseases associated with dysregulated sphingolipid metabolism, including cancer. Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), a bioactive lipid metabolite, plays a critical role in cellular signaling pathways that regulate proliferation, survival, migration, and angiogenesis. The balance between S1P and its precursors, ceramide and sphingosine, termed the "sphingolipid rheostat," is tightly controlled by enzymes such as SphK1 and SphK2. While SphK1 has been extensively studied and linked to pro-survival signaling in cancer, the role of SphK2 remains less understood and controversial.  

Current SphK2 inhibitors suffer from limitations such as moderate potency, lack of selectivity, or poor pharmacokinetic properties. There exists a significant unmet need for potent, selective, and pharmacologically optimized SphK2 inhibitors that can serve as both research tools and therapeutic agents. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel thiazolidine-2,4-dione (TZD)-based compound, designated K145, which exhibits selective inhibition of SphK2, favorable cellular uptake, and potent anti-tumor activity in vivo.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a compound of Formula I, 3-(2-amino-ethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyl-phenyl)-propylidene]-thiazolidine-2,4-dione (K145), which demonstrates selective inhibition of SphK2 over SphK1 and ceramide kinase (CERK). Biochemical assays confirm that K145 competitively inhibits SphK2 with an IC50 of 4.30±0.06 µM, while showing no activity against SphK1 at concentrations up to 10 µM. Molecular modeling studies reveal that K145 binds preferentially to the sphingosine-binding pocket of SphK2, forming key interactions with residues such as Asp344, Arg351, and Gln346.  

In cellular assays, K145 reduces intracellular S1P levels, inhibits phosphorylation of FTY720 (a SphK2-specific substrate), and suppresses proliferation of human leukemia U937 cells by inducing apoptosis. Mechanistically, K145 deactivates the ERK and Akt signaling pathways, which are critical for cancer cell survival. In vivo studies demonstrate that K145 significantly inhibits tumor growth in xenograft models of leukemia and breast cancer, with efficacy comparable to clinically used agents such as tamibarotene. Notably, K145 exhibits oral bioavailability and minimal toxicity, making it a promising lead compound for further development.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### In Formula I:  

The invention encompasses the compound K145, represented by the following structure:  

**Formula I**:  
3-(2-amino-ethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyl-phenyl)-propylidene]-thiazolidine-2,4-dione  

K145 is synthesized via a multi-step process involving:  
1. Condensation of 4-butoxybenzaldehyde with Meldrum’s acid to form intermediate 10.  
2. Reduction to yield 4-butoxyphenyl-3-propanal (11).  
3. Reaction with Boc-protected 3-(2-amino-ethyl)-thiazolidine-2,4-dione (14), followed by deprotection to yield K145 as a hydrochloride salt.  

The compound exhibits selective inhibition of SphK2 through competitive binding at the sphingosine recognition site, as evidenced by Lineweaver-Burk kinetics (Ki = 6.4±0.7 µM). Its selectivity is attributed to favorable interactions with SphK2-specific residues (e.g., Gln346) and unfavorable clashes with SphK1 (e.g., Glu180).  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthesis of K145  

**Step 1**: 5-(4-Butoxybenzyl)-2,2-dimethyl-1,3-dioxane-4,6-dione (10)  
4-Butoxybenzaldehyde (5.0 mmol) was condensed with Meldrum’s acid (5.0 mmol) in ethanol catalyzed by piperidine. The product was reduced with NaBH4 in acetic acid/DCM to yield 10 (28% yield).  

**Step 2**: 4-Butoxyphenyl-3-propanal (11)  
Intermediate 10 (1.2 mmol) was treated with triethylamine and phenylsilane in THF to afford 11 (81% yield).  

**Step 3**: T-Butyl 2-(2,4-dioxothiazolidin-3-yl)-ethylcarbamate (14)  
Thiazolidine-2,4-dione (68.0 mmol) was alkylated with Boc-protected 2-bromoethylamine (80.0 mmol) using K2CO3/TBAI in acetone to yield 14 (70% yield).  

**Step 4**: Final Condensation and Deprotection  
14 (7.7 mmol) was condensed with 11 (7.7 mmol) in ethanol catalyzed by piperidine. The Boc group was removed with HCl/dioxane to yield K145 as a hydrochloride salt (75% yield).  

### Example 2: In Vitro SphK2 Inhibition  

Recombinant SphK2 was incubated with K145 (0–10 µM) and [γ-32P]ATP. K145 exhibited dose-dependent inhibition (IC50 = 4.30±0.06 µM), while showing no activity against SphK1 or CERK at 10 µM. Lineweaver-Burk analysis confirmed competitive inhibition with sphingosine (Ki = 6.4±0.7 µM).  

### Example 3: Cellular S1P Reduction  

U937 cells treated with K145 (10 µM, 3 h) showed a 45% reduction in cellular S1P levels (LC-MS/MS), with no effect on ceramide or C1P. FTY720 phosphorylation (a SphK2-specific readout) was also inhibited.  

### Example 4: Anti-Proliferative Effects  

K145 (1–10 µM) inhibited U937 cell proliferation (MTT assay) by 20–80% over 72 h. Flow cytometry revealed induction of late apoptosis (annexin V+/PI+ cells: 35% at 10 µM).  

### Example 5: ERK/Akt Pathway Inhibition  

Western blotting showed K145 (4 µM, 1 h) reduced phospho-ERK and phospho-Akt levels in U937 cells by 60–70%, consistent with downstream effects of SphK2 inhibition.  

### Example 6: In Vivo Leukemia Model  

BALB/c-nu mice bearing U937 xenografts were treated with K145 (15 mg/kg, i.p., 17 days). Tumor growth inhibition (TGI) was 44.2%, comparable to tamibarotene (50.4%), with no weight loss.  

### Example 7: Syngeneic Breast Cancer Model  

BALB/c mice with JC mammary tumors received K145 (20 or 35 mg/kg, i.p., 15 days). TGI was >50% at both doses, with reduced tumor S1P and phospho-ERK/Akt levels.  

### Example 8: Oral Efficacy  

Oral administration of K145 (50 mg/kg, 15 days) in U937 xenografts achieved 51.25% TGI, superior to tamibarotene (33.37%), confirming oral bioavailability.  

### Compounds 28-36, 39-40, and 42 of Schemes 2 and 3  

Additional TZD analogues (e.g., Compounds 28–36, 39–40, 42) were synthesized and screened, demonstrating structure-activity relationships for SphK2 inhibition. Key modifications include:  
- **Alkyl chain length**: Butoxy (optimal) vs. shorter/longer chains.  
- **Substituents**: Electron-donating groups on the phenyl ring enhance potency.  
- **TZD modifications**: 3-(2-amino-ethyl) substitution is critical for SphK2 binding.
