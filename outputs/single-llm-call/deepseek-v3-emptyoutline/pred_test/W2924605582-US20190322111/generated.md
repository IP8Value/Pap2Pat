## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to functional printing of metallic inks, and more specifically to a novel nickel (Ni) precursor ink formulation capable of producing aligned nanowires through thermal reduction in the presence of a magnetic field. The invention further encompasses methods of printing said ink using aerosol-based deposition techniques, the resulting aligned nanowire structures, and their applications in electronic and magnetic devices.  

## BACKGROUND  

Functional printing of metallic inks has gained significant attention for applications in printed electronics, sensors, antennas, and other devices requiring conductive or magnetic properties. Traditional approaches utilize nanoparticle-based inks containing pre-formed metal particles (e.g., Cu, Ag, or Ni nanoparticles). However, such inks suffer from instability issues including particle agglomeration and solvent evaporation, which adversely affect print quality and shelf life.  

Precursor inks, also known as metal-organic decomposition (MOD) inks, present an alternative by employing dissolved metal complexes that undergo thermal reduction to form metallic structures upon curing. While precursor inks avoid particle-related instability, they typically exhibit lower metal content compared to nanoparticle inks. Moreover, formulating Ni precursor inks has been particularly challenging due to nickel's propensity to oxidize upon reduction in ambient conditions.  

Existing methods for producing Ni nanowires involve multi-step processes such as hydrothermal synthesis, chemical reduction, or electrochemical deposition—often requiring templates, inert atmospheres, or extreme conditions. These methods are impractical for scalable manufacturing and limit substrate compatibility. There remains an unmet need for a simple, template-free process capable of producing aligned Ni nanowires on diverse substrates under ambient conditions.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a solution to the aforementioned challenges through a novel Ni precursor ink formulation and associated printing/reduction methodology. The ink comprises a nickel formate complex with ethylenediamine in an aqueous solvent, optionally including additives such as polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and surfactants to optimize rheological properties for printing.  

Key innovations include:  
1. **Ink Composition**: A homogeneous Ni precursor ink stable under ambient conditions, capable of thermal reduction to pure metallic Ni at temperatures as low as 235°C.  
2. **Magnetic Field-Assisted Reduction**: Thermal curing in the presence of a weak magnetic field (e.g., 250 Oe) induces alignment of reduced Ni nanoparticles into nanowires oriented along the field lines, without requiring templates or post-processing.  
3. **Printed Nanowire Structures**: The aligned nanowires exhibit enhanced anisotropic electrical conductivity (resistivity as low as 560 µΩ·cm) and magnetic properties (remanent magnetization of 20 emu·g⁻¹ along the alignment axis).  
4. **Versatile Deposition**: Compatible with aerosol printing, inkjet, and other deposition techniques, enabling patterning on planar/non-planar substrates, including flexible and thermally sensitive materials.  

Applications span printed electronics (e.g., antennas, circuits), magnetic storage devices, sensors, optical polarizers, and biomedical devices. The invention further enables fabrication of nanowire grids by alternating magnetic field directions during multilayer printing, yielding isotropic conductivity and tailored magnetic responses.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Ink Formulation and Properties  

The Ni precursor ink is synthesized by dissolving nickel formate (0.8 g, 5.38 mmol) in a solvent mixture of ethylenediamine (0.72 mL, 10.77 mmol) and distilled water (4 mL). Ethylenediamine acts as a bidentate ligand, enhancing complex stability and solubility while facilitating low-temperature reduction. Optional additives include:  
- **PVP (1% w/w)**: Improves ink stability and film formation.  
- **Surfactants (e.g., BYK 333)**: Enhances substrate wettability.  

The ink exhibits a surface tension of 44.01 mN/m and viscosity of 3.2 cP, optimized for aerosol printing. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) confirms a two-stage decomposition: solvent evaporation below 100°C and Ni reduction at 235°C, yielding 5.7 wt% metallic Ni. The formate counterion promotes a reducing atmosphere during decomposition, preventing Ni oxidation.  

### Printing and Reduction Process  

The ink is deposited via aerosol printing (e.g., ultrasonic atomization) using a NanoJet system. Key parameters include:  
- **Nozzle Tip**: 25-gauge dispensing tip with 5 mm standoff distance.  
- **Print Speed**: 2 mm/s, producing ~4 µm wet film thickness per layer.  
- **Substrate Treatment**: Plasma activation improves ink adhesion.  

Post-printing, the substrate is heated to 240°C for 15 minutes under a homogeneous magnetic field (250 Oe). The field aligns Ni nanoparticles during reduction, forming nanowires via sintering ("string of pearls" morphology, 100–250 nm particle diameters). Without the field, reduction yields porous Ni films with random particle orientation.  

### Structural and Functional Characteristics  

**Aligned Nanowires**:  
- Exhibit preferential orientation quantified by 2D FFT analysis (peaks at 90°/270°).  
- Nanowire grids are fabricated by alternating magnetic field directions between layers, producing crosshatched structures (FFT peaks at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°).  

**Electrical Properties**:  
- Resistivity of aligned nanowires: 560 µΩ·cm (parallel to alignment), 80× bulk Ni.  
- Nanowire grids show isotropic resistivity (~600 µΩ·cm in both axes).  

**Magnetic Properties**:  
- Saturation magnetization: 30 emu·g⁻¹ (comparable to bulk Ni).  
- Remanent magnetization: 20 emu·g⁻¹ (aligned axis) vs. 7 emu·g⁻¹ (perpendicular).  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Fabrication of Aligned Ni Nanowires on Glass Substrates**:  

1. **Ink Preparation**:  
   - Dissolve 0.72 mL ethylenediamine in 4 mL distilled water.  
   - Add 0.8 g nickel formate and stir for 15 minutes.  
   - Filter through a 0.2 µm syringe filter; add 1% PVP and 20 µL BYK 333 surfactant.  

2. **Aerosol Printing**:  
   - Print 1 cm² patterns on plasma-treated glass using a 25-gauge nozzle (2 mm/s speed).  

3. **Thermal Reduction**:  
   - Cure at 240°C for 15 minutes under a 250 Oe magnetic field (parallel to substrate plane).  

**Results**:  
- SEM confirms aligned nanowires (diameter: 100–250 nm; length: >10 µm).  
- Resistivity: 580 µΩ·cm (parallel); 850 µΩ·cm (perpendicular).  
- Remanent magnetization: 19.5 emu·g⁻¹ (aligned axis).
