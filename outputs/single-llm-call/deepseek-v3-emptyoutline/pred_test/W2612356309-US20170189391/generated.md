# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology therapeutics, specifically to methods and compositions for treating triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). More particularly, the invention concerns the use of protein kinase C (PKC) inhibitors, particularly chelerythrine and related compounds, as selective therapeutic agents against TNBC through targeted inhibition of PKN2 and other PKC isoforms. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing these inhibitors, methods of treatment using such compositions, and combination therapies with conventional chemotherapeutic agents.  

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION  

Triple-negative breast cancer represents a particularly aggressive subtype of breast cancer characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression. This molecular profile renders TNBC unresponsive to hormonal therapies or HER2-targeted treatments, leaving chemotherapy as the primary systemic treatment option. However, TNBC patients frequently experience rapid disease relapse following initial chemotherapy response, with limited effective treatment options available post-relapse. The aggressive nature of TNBC is further demonstrated by its propensity for early metastasis to distant sites such as lungs and brain, resulting in poorer prognosis and shorter overall survival compared to other breast cancer subtypes.  

Current therapeutic strategies under investigation for TNBC target various molecular pathways including poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), angiogenesis pathways, mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and others. However, none of these approaches have yielded approved targeted therapies for TNBC to date, highlighting the urgent need for novel treatment modalities.  

Protein kinase C has emerged as a potential therapeutic target in cancer due to its established roles in various disease processes. In breast cancer specifically, an inverse relationship exists between ER expression and PKC activity, with PKC overexpression observed in malignant versus normal breast tissue. Among PKC isoforms, PRKCA (PKCα) has been particularly associated with antiestrogen resistance and tumor aggressiveness. However, the potential of PKC inhibitors as targeted therapies for TNBC remains unexplored, despite evidence suggesting PKC's involvement in TNBC pathogenesis.  

Chelerythrine, a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid and potent PKC inhibitor, has demonstrated anti-cancer activity in various contexts but has not been investigated specifically for TNBC treatment. The present invention addresses this unmet need by establishing chelerythrine and related PKC inhibitors as selective therapeutic agents against TNBC through novel mechanisms targeting specific PKC isoforms, particularly PKN2.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for treating triple-negative breast cancer through selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms, particularly PKN2. The invention is based on the discovery that chelerythrine and related PKC inhibitors demonstrate selective anti-proliferative activity against TNBC cell lines compared to non-TNBC breast cancer cells, mediated through specific induction of apoptosis in TNBC cells.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. The use of chelerythrine as a selective therapeutic agent against TNBC, wherein said selectivity is achieved through preferential inhibition of PKN2 and other PKC isoforms overexpressed in TNBC cells.  

2. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising chelerythrine or its derivatives formulated for administration to TNBC patients, optionally in combination with chemotherapeutic agents such as taxanes.  

3. Methods of treating TNBC comprising administering effective amounts of PKC inhibitors to patients identified as having TNBC, particularly those overexpressing PKN2 or other relevant PKC isoforms.  

4. Combination therapies wherein PKC inhibitors are administered with conventional chemotherapeutic agents, demonstrating synergistic or additive effects in TNBC treatment.  

5. Diagnostic methods for identifying TNBC patients likely to respond to PKC inhibitor therapy based on PKN2 expression levels.  

The invention is supported by experimental data demonstrating that chelerythrine selectively inhibits TNBC cell proliferation in vitro with IC50 values ranging from 2.6 μM to 4.2 μM, while showing minimal activity against non-TNBC cell lines (IC50 >10 μM). This selective activity correlates with PKN2 overexpression in TNBC cells and is mediated through induction of apoptosis, as evidenced by characteristic morphological changes, cleaved PARP detection, and annexin V staining. In vivo xenograft studies further confirm the selective anti-tumor activity of chelerythrine against TNBC.  

The invention further encompasses the use of other PKC inhibitors demonstrating similar selectivity profiles, methods for identifying such inhibitors, and biomarkers for predicting treatment response. The therapeutic approach described herein addresses the critical unmet need for targeted therapies in TNBC while potentially overcoming limitations of current treatment modalities.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The present invention provides detailed methods and compositions for treating triple-negative breast cancer through selective inhibition of protein kinase C isoforms. The following embodiments describe the invention in sufficient detail to enable practice by those skilled in the art, without undue experimentation.  

**Chelerythrine and Related Compounds**  

The primary active agent of the invention is chelerythrine, a benzophenanthridine alkaloid having the chemical name 1,2-dimethoxy-12-methyl[1,3]benzodioxolo[5,6-c]phenanthridin-12-ium. The compound may be used in its natural form or as pharmaceutically acceptable salts, derivatives, or prodrugs designed to enhance bioavailability or reduce side effects. Structural analogs maintaining the core PKC inhibitory activity while potentially improving pharmacokinetic properties are also encompassed within the invention.  

The invention further includes other PKC inhibitors demonstrating selective activity against TNBC cells, particularly those showing preferential inhibition of PKN2. Such inhibitors may be identified through screening assays comparing activity against TNBC versus non-TNBC cell lines, with verification of PKN2 dependency through knockdown experiments as described herein.  

**Pharmaceutical Compositions**  

The active compounds of the invention may be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for various routes of administration, including oral, parenteral, topical, or localized delivery to tumor sites. Preferred formulations for systemic administration include tablets, capsules, or injectable solutions containing the active compound with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients.  

For intravenous administration, the compounds may be formulated in sterile isotonic solutions at concentrations ranging from 0.1 mg/mL to 10 mg/mL. Oral formulations may include enteric coatings to protect the active ingredient from gastric degradation. Sustained-release formulations providing prolonged drug exposure are particularly advantageous given the mechanism of action.  

**Dosing Regimens**  

Effective dosing of chelerythrine for TNBC treatment ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg body weight, administered daily or intermittently (e.g., every 3-4 days) based on patient tolerance and therapeutic response. A preferred dosing schedule for monotherapy comprises 5 mg/kg administered intraperitoneally every 3-4 days, as demonstrated effective in xenograft models.  

For combination therapy with taxanes or other chemotherapeutic agents, the chelerythrine dose may be reduced to 2 μM equivalent concentrations (approximately 0.7 mg/kg) to achieve synergistic effects while minimizing toxicity. Dose adjustments may be made based on individual patient factors including body surface area, renal/hepatic function, and concomitant medications.  

**Combination Therapies**  

The invention specifically encompasses combination therapies wherein PKC inhibitors are administered with conventional chemotherapeutic agents. Particularly effective combinations include chelerythrine with taxanes (e.g., paclitaxel, docetaxel), with demonstrated synergy evidenced by combination index values ranging from 0.75 to 1.14 at ED50.  

The combination may be administered sequentially or concurrently, with timing optimized to maximize therapeutic synergy while minimizing overlapping toxicities. For example, chelerythrine may be administered 24 hours prior to taxane treatment to prime tumor cells for enhanced apoptosis induction.  

**Patient Selection and Monitoring**  

The invention includes methods for identifying TNBC patients likely to respond to PKC inhibitor therapy based on PKN2 expression levels in tumor tissue. Diagnostic assays include immunohistochemistry, Western blotting, or quantitative PCR of tumor biopsies to assess PKN2 protein or mRNA levels relative to established thresholds.  

Treatment response may be monitored through serial imaging, circulating tumor cell analysis, or biomarker assessment including cleaved PARP levels. Dose adjustments or therapy modifications may be implemented based on response monitoring and toxicity assessments.  

**Experimental Support**  

The therapeutic efficacy of the invention is supported by extensive in vitro and in vivo data:  

1. In vitro proliferation assays demonstrated selective inhibition of TNBC cell growth by chelerythrine (IC50 2.6-4.2 μM for TNBC vs >10 μM for non-TNBC cells).  

2. Colony formation assays showed near-complete suppression of TNBC colony growth at 5 μM chelerythrine, with minimal effect on non-TNBC cells.  

3. Apoptosis assays confirmed selective induction of apoptosis in TNBC cells, with annexin V-positive fractions reaching 67.8% in MDA-MB-231 cells versus minimal apoptosis in non-TNBC lines.  

4. PKN2 knockdown experiments validated its essential role in TNBC cell growth, with significant reduction in colony formation and xenograft growth upon PKN2 suppression.  

5. Xenograft studies demonstrated significant tumor growth inhibition in TNBC models (MDA-MB-231) compared to non-TNBC models (ZR-75-1) at 5 mg/kg dosing.  

6. Combination studies showed enhanced efficacy when chelerythrine (2 μM) was combined with taxol (10 nM), with combination index values indicating additive to synergistic effects.  

These experimental results collectively support the utility of PKC inhibitors, particularly chelerythrine, as selective therapeutic agents for TNBC, with PKN2 serving as a key molecular target and potential predictive biomarker. The invention thus provides a novel targeted approach for this difficult-to-treat breast cancer subtype.  

**Additional Embodiments**  

Further embodiments of the invention include:  

1. Methods for synthesizing chelerythrine derivatives with improved pharmaceutical properties while maintaining selective PKN2 inhibition.  

2. Companion diagnostic tests for identifying TNBC patients likely to respond to PKC inhibitor therapy based on PKC isoform expression profiles.  

3. Treatment protocols combining PKC inhibitors with radiation therapy or immunotherapy for enhanced TNBC control.  

4. Methods for overcoming chemotherapy resistance in TNBC through PKC inhibition-mediated sensitization.  

The invention represents a significant advancement in TNBC treatment by providing the first targeted therapeutic approach based on PKC isoform specificity, with particular emphasis on PKN2 as a novel molecular target in this aggressive breast cancer subtype.
