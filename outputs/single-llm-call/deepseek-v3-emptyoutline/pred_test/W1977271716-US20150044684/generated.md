## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods for immobilizing and preparing living microorganisms, particularly those derived from soil and sediment samples. More specifically, the invention pertains to techniques involving the application of weak negative electric potentials to attract living microorganisms to an electrode surface, followed by their detachment using high-frequency triangular wave potentials. The disclosed methods enable efficient isolation and analysis of microbial communities while maintaining cell viability.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Microorganisms present in soil and sediments are a rich source of novel therapeutic compounds, including antibiotics, anticancer agents, and immunosuppressants, as well as valuable biotechnological products. However, a significant challenge in microbial research is that the majority of these microorganisms cannot be cultured under conventional laboratory conditions.  

Metagenomics has emerged as a powerful tool to study microbial diversity without requiring cultivation. However, conventional DNA extraction methods often co-extract inhibitory substances such as humic acids, which interfere with downstream applications like PCR and DNA ligation. Indirect DNA extraction techniques, where microorganisms are first separated from soil or sediment before lysis, yield higher purity DNA but only capture a fraction (25–35%) of the total microbial population.  

Existing microbial single-cell isolation techniques include:  
1. **Dilution-to-extinction** – Serial dilution until single cells remain.  
2. **Micromanipulation** – Mechanical or optical trapping of individual cells.  
3. **Flow cytometry** – Rapid analysis based on single-cell characteristics.  
4. **Microfluidics** – Isolation via microflow channels.  
5. **Compartmentalization** – Encapsulation of single cells for screening.  

Despite these advances, isolating living microorganisms directly from soil or sediment remains challenging due to strong adhesion between microbes and soil aggregates. Current methods often fail to preserve cell viability or introduce bias by selecting only loosely attached cells.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Achieved by the Invention  

The primary object of the invention is to provide a novel method for efficiently isolating living microorganisms from soil and sediment samples while maintaining their viability. A further object is to enable controlled immobilization and detachment of microorganisms using electrical modulation, facilitating high-throughput microbial analysis.  

### Means for Achieving the Objects  

The invention achieves these objects through the following means:  
1. **Application of a weak negative electric potential** (-0.2 to -0.5 V vs. Ag/AgCl) to attract living microorganisms to an electrode surface.  
2. **Use of non-nutritive buffers** (e.g., PBS(−), MOPS buffer, tricine buffer, or artificial seawater) to enhance selective attachment.  
3. **Detachment via high-frequency triangular wave potential** (±4 to ±10 mV, 9 MHz) to release microorganisms while preserving viability.  
4. **Patterned electrode arrays** for spatially controlled microbial immobilization.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention provides several key advantages:  
- **High recovery rates**: Enables isolation of a broad spectrum of microorganisms, including previously unculturable species.  
- **Preserved viability**: Detachment via high-frequency waves minimizes cell damage.  
- **Reduced bias**: Captures both loosely and strongly adhered microbes, improving representation of microbial communities.  
- **Scalability**: Suitable for high-throughput applications in metagenomics and bioprospecting.  

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

### <Method for Immobilizing Living Microorganisms>  

The method comprises the following steps:  
1. **Sample Preparation**: Soil or sediment is suspended in a non-nutritive buffer (e.g., PBS(−), MOPS, or artificial seawater) and homogenized.  
2. **Electrode Setup**: A patterned indium tin oxide (ITO) electrode is prepared and sterilized.  
3. **Potential Application**: A negative constant potential (-0.3 to -0.5 V vs. Ag/AgCl) is applied for 2–24 hours, inducing microbial attachment.  
4. **Washing**: Non-adherent particles are removed by rinsing the electrode.  

### <Method for Preparing Living Microorganisms>  

Following immobilization, microorganisms are detached using:  
1. **High-Frequency Triangular Wave Potential**: A ±10 mV, 9 MHz signal is applied for 30–60 minutes, releasing cells into suspension.  
2. **Collection**: Detached microorganisms are harvested for downstream applications (e.g., cultivation, DNA extraction).  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Electrical Attachment of E. coli to a Patterned ITO Electrode**  
- **Conditions**: -0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl in PBS(−) for 24 hours at RT.  
- **Results**: 46-fold increase in attached cell density compared to open circuit.  

### Example 2  

**Detachment of Soil Microorganisms**  
- **Conditions**: ±10 mV, 9 MHz triangular wave for 60 minutes.  
- **Results**: 99.8% detachment rate with 91% viability.  

### Example 3  

**Deep-Sea Sediment Microbial Recovery**  
- **Conditions**: -0.3 V in artificial seawater, followed by ±10 mV detachment.  
- **Results**: 1.3 × 10⁸ cells recovered from 5 g sediment.  

### Example 4  

**Comparison of Buffers for Microbial Attachment**  
- **Tested Buffers**: PBS(−), MOPS, tricine, LB medium.  
- **Results**: Highest attachment in non-nutritive buffers (PBS(−), MOPS).  

### Example 5  

**Viability After Electrical Detachment**  
- **Test Organisms**: E. coli, B. subtilis, soil microbes.  
- **Results**: 83% viability for E. coli, 47% for B. subtilis.  

### (1) Various Buffers  

- **PBS(−)**: Optimal for most gram-negative and gram-positive bacteria.  
- **Artificial Seawater**: Preferred for marine samples (e.g., deep-sea sediment).  

### (2) Artificial Seawater  

- **Composition**: 30 g NaCl, 0.7 g KCl, 5.3 g MgSO₄·7H₂O, 10.8 g MgCl₂·6H₂O, 1 g CaCl₂·2H₂O per liter.  
- **Use Case**: Enhanced attachment of marine bacteria at -0.3 V.  

### Example 6  

**Anaerobic Conditions**  
- **Setup**: Anaerobic chamber with -0.4 V application.  
- **Results**: 68-fold increase in E. coli attachment vs. aerobic.  

### Example 7  

**Phylogenetic Analysis of Retrieved Microbes**  
- **Method**: 16S rRNA gene sequencing.  
- **Findings**: 19 phyla detected, matching direct DNA extraction results.
