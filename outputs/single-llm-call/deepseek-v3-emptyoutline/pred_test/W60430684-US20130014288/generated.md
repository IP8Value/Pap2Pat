## BACKGROUND  

### Field of the Invention  
The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and medicine, specifically to compositions and methods for detecting and correcting protein trafficking defects associated with genetic disorders. More particularly, the invention provides novel fluorescent reporter systems for monitoring cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) protein localization and function, along with improved corrector compounds and combinations thereof for treating cystic fibrosis caused by the F508del mutation in CFTR.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides a novel platform for detecting and quantifying cell surface expression of CFTR proteins using fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) tags. The system comprises genetically engineered CFTR constructs fused with FAP modules that enable selective fluorescence detection of properly trafficked CFTR at the plasma membrane. Two specific embodiments are disclosed: an N-terminal FAP-CFTR fusion construct and a fourth extracellular loop (EL4) insertion construct (CFTR EL4-FAP).  

The invention further provides improved corrector compounds, including C18 and CFFT-002, and demonstrates that combinations of correctors (particularly C18 with either C4 or CFFT-002) produce synergistic effects in rescuing F508del-CFTR trafficking defects. These corrector combinations achieve significantly greater rescue of F508del-CFTR to the cell surface compared to individual compounds, as demonstrated through multiple experimental approaches including fluorescence detection, functional assays, and biochemical characterization.  

The FAP-CFTR platform enables rapid, sensitive detection of CFTR surface expression without requiring wash steps or cell permeabilization. This system provides substantial advantages over existing methods for drug screening and corrector efficacy evaluation, including improved dynamic range, reduced artifacts, and increased throughput. The invention further demonstrates that the FAP reporter system accurately recapitulates the behavior of endogenous CFTR in primary human bronchial epithelial cells, validating its utility for preclinical drug development.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses critical limitations in current methods for studying CFTR trafficking and function by providing a novel fluorescent detection platform based on fluorogen-activating proteins (FAPs). The FAP system comprises an engineered antibody fragment (scFv) that binds specifically to small organic fluorogen dyes. When unbound, these fluorogens are non-fluorescent in solution, but upon binding to the FAP, fluorescence increases by 15,000-20,000-fold, enabling highly sensitive detection.  

The invention specifically describes two distinct CFTR-FAP fusion constructs. In the first embodiment, the FAP (dNP138 variant) is fused to the N-terminus of CFTR along with an additional transmembrane segment derived from the PDGF receptor. In the second embodiment, the FAP is inserted into the fourth extracellular loop (EL4) of CFTR between residues K894 and N900, preserving critical glycosylation sites while enabling extracellular presentation of the FAP module. Both wild-type (WT) and F508del mutant versions of these constructs were generated and characterized.  

The N-terminal FAP-CFTR construct demonstrates superior performance characteristics, including:  
1) Proper trafficking to the plasma membrane in WT form  
2) Complete glycosylation maturation (evidenced by distinct B and C bands in immunoblots)  
3) Robust functional activity in anion transport assays  
4) Greater corrector-mediated rescue of F508del mutant trafficking compared to the EL4 construct  

The invention further characterizes novel corrector compounds and combinations. Corrector C18 demonstrates particularly strong single-agent efficacy, achieving 3.5-fold greater rescue of F508del-CFTR surface expression compared to reference corrector C4. Remarkably, combinations of C18 with either C4 or CFFT-002 produce synergistic effects, with the C18+CFFT-002 combination showing 8.6-fold improvement over C4 alone in rescuing N-terminal FAP-F508del-CFTR surface expression.  

The FAP-CFTR platform enables multiple experimental approaches for studying CFTR:  
1) Selective cell surface labeling using cell-impermeant fluorogen MG-11p  
2) Total cellular distribution analysis using cell-permeant MG-ester  
3) Quantitative flow cytometry measurements of surface expression  
4) Functional characterization through iodide efflux assays  
5) Biochemical analysis of glycosylation states  

The system's modular design allows adaptation to other membrane proteins affected by trafficking defects in various genetic disorders, including but not limited to glucose transporters in diabetes, hERG channels in long QT syndrome, LDL receptors in familial hypercholesterolemia, aquaporins in diabetes insipidus, and bestrophins in retinal degeneration.  

## EXPERIMENTAL  

### EXAMPLE 1  
**Development and Characterization of FAP-CFTR Constructs**  

The N-terminal FAP-CFTR fusion was generated by inserting the dNP138 FAP into the Sac module of pBabeSacLac2 plasmid, followed by insertion of CFTR cDNA (WT or F508del) into the Lac module. The EL4 insertion construct was created by introducing the FAP between KpnI and EcoRV sites engineered into CFTR's fourth extracellular loop. Both constructs were stably expressed in HEK293 cells.  

Confocal microscopy demonstrated that WT versions of both constructs properly localized to the plasma membrane, as evidenced by specific labeling with cell-impermeant MG-11p fluorogen. Functional analysis using SPQ-based iodide efflux assays confirmed that both FAP-tagged WT constructs maintained cAMP-stimulated anion transport activity comparable to untagged CFTR.  

Immunoblot analysis revealed distinct differences between the constructs. The N-terminal FAP-CFTR showed complete glycosylation maturation (clear B and C bands), while the EL4 insertion construct exhibited incomplete glycosylation despite preservation of N-glycosylation sites. This difference likely contributes to the superior performance of the N-terminal construct in subsequent corrector studies.  

### EXAMPLE 2  
**Corrector Efficacy Studies Using FAP-CFTR Platform**  

HEK293 cells stably expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR or F508del-CFTR EL4-FAP were treated with various corrector conditions:  
1) Vehicle control (DMSO)  
2) 10 μM C4  
3) 5 μM CFFT-002  
4) 5 μM C18  
5) 10 μM C4 + 5 μM C18  
6) 5 μM CFFT-002 + 5 μM C18  

After 24-hour treatment, cells were analyzed by:  
1) Flow cytometry using MG-11p to quantify surface expression  
2) Confocal microscopy to visualize cellular distribution  
3) Iodide efflux assays to measure functional rescue  
4) Immunoblotting to assess glycosylation maturation  

Results demonstrated that C18 was the most potent single-agent corrector, achieving 3.5-fold greater surface expression than C4 for the N-terminal construct. Correctors C4 and CFFT-002 showed more modest effects individually. Strikingly, combinations of C18 with either C4 or CFFT-002 produced synergistic rescue, with C18+CFFT-002 showing 8.6-fold improvement over C4 alone.  

Parallel studies in primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells from F508del-CFTR patients confirmed that the corrector efficacy patterns observed in HEK293 cells translated to endogenous CFTR in physiologically relevant cells. Ussing chamber measurements demonstrated that C18 alone and particularly in combination with CFFT-002 significantly rescued F508del-CFTR function in HBE cells.  

### Summary of Corrector Efficacy from FAP Labeling and Functional Assays  

The FAP-CFTR platform enabled comprehensive characterization of corrector compounds through multiple orthogonal methods:  

1) **Surface Expression Quantification**: Flow cytometry with MG-11p provided precise measurements of corrector efficacy in rescuing F508del-CFTR to the cell surface. The N-terminal FAP construct showed greater dynamic range and more robust response to correctors compared to the EL4 insertion construct.  

2) **Functional Rescue**: Iodide efflux assays and Ussing chamber measurements confirmed that increased surface expression translated to functional anion transport activity. Corrector combinations showed particularly strong functional rescue.  

3) **Biochemical Maturation**: Immunoblot analysis demonstrated that effective correctors promoted glycosylation maturation of F508del-CFTR, with the N-terminal construct showing more complete processing than the EL4 variant.  

4) **Cellular Distribution**: Confocal microscopy visualized the intracellular retention of F508del-CFTR under control conditions and its redistribution to the plasma membrane after corrector treatment.  

This multi-modal characterization establishes the FAP-CFTR platform as a powerful tool for drug discovery and development, providing sensitive, quantitative data on corrector mechanisms and efficacy while maintaining relevance to endogenous CFTR biology in primary human cells.  

The invention demonstrates that combination corrector therapies targeting multiple aspects of CFTR folding and trafficking may be required to achieve clinically meaningful rescue of F508del-CFTR. The disclosed C18+CFFT-002 combination represents a particularly promising therapeutic approach warranting further development.
