## DESCRIPTION

### INTRODUCTION

The present invention relates to a novel DNA vaccine for Spring Viremia of Carp Virus (SVCV), specifically targeting the glycoprotein (G) gene of the North American strain of SVCV. SVCV is a highly contagious rhabdoviral pathogen that causes significant mortality in cyprinid fish species, particularly common carp (Cyprinus carpio carpio) and koi (Cyprinus carpio koi). The virus has been responsible for devastating outbreaks in aquaculture facilities and wild populations across North America since its first detection in 2002. Current methods for controlling SVCV outbreaks rely primarily on eradication of infected fish populations and strict hygiene measures, as no commercially available vaccine exists. The invention provides an effective prophylactic solution through a DNA vaccine construct containing the complete G-gene sequence from the North Carolina isolate of SVCV (SVCVnc), demonstrating significant protection in vaccinated koi against homologous viral challenge.

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses a DNA vaccine composition comprising a plasmid vector containing the complete glycoprotein (G) gene sequence from the North American isolate of Spring Viremia of Carp Virus (SVCVnc). The vaccine construct, designated pSGnc, was developed by cloning the full-length G-gene into the pcDNA3.1 expression vector. When administered via intramuscular injection to susceptible fish species, particularly koi (Cyprinus carpio koi), the vaccine induces protective immunity against SVCV infection. Experimental trials demonstrated relative percent survival (RPS) values ranging from 50% to 88% in vaccinated fish challenged with homologous virus 28 days post-vaccination. The vaccine represents the first effective DNA-based immunization against SVCV in North America and provides significant advantages over previous vaccine attempts, including consistent efficacy across multiple trials, complete viral clearance in most survivors, and protection against a currently circulating viral strain. The invention further encompasses methods of vaccine production, administration protocols, and challenge models for evaluating vaccine efficacy in susceptible fish species.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution to the problem of SVCV infection in susceptible fish populations through development of a DNA vaccine targeting the glycoprotein antigen of the North American SVCV strain. The vaccine construct was developed by isolating the complete G-gene sequence from the SVCVnc isolate (GenBank accession AY527273) and cloning it into the pcDNA3.1 mammalian expression vector. The G-gene was selected as the target antigen due to its role as the primary viral surface protein responsible for eliciting protective immune responses in fish hosts. The complete 1,518 nucleotide G-gene sequence was amplified using specific primers (forward: 5'-CACCATGTCTATCATCAGC-TACATC-3'; reverse: 5'-CTAAACGAAGGACCGCATTTCGTG-3') and directionally cloned into the expression vector. Sequence verification confirmed preservation of the authentic viral sequence without mutations introduced during the cloning process.

The vaccine is produced by propagating the pSGnc construct in Escherichia coli followed by purification using alkaline lysis methodology. For administration, the plasmid DNA is suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and delivered via intramuscular injection into the epaxial muscle of susceptible fish species. A dose of 10 μg plasmid DNA in 50 μL volume has been shown to be effective in fish weighing approximately 3.5-10 grams. Optimal vaccination conditions include water temperatures of 19-20°C during the immunization period to enhance immune response development. The vaccine induces protective immunity within 28 days post-vaccination (approximately 546 degree-days), with protection lasting through at least the duration of testing (56 days post-challenge).

#### Susceptibility Testing of Goldfish and Koi

Development of the invention included establishment of a reliable challenge model using susceptible host species. Comparative testing revealed significant differences in susceptibility between goldfish (Carassius auratus) and koi (Cyprinus carpio koi) to the North American SVCV strain. While goldfish demonstrated 96% survival following both immersion and injection challenges with SVCVnc, koi showed 100% mortality after injection challenge and 75% mortality after immersion challenge. These findings established koi as the preferred model species for vaccine efficacy testing due to their consistent susceptibility and relevance as an economically important species affected by SVCV outbreaks. The challenge protocols developed include either intraperitoneal injection of 1 × 10^6 plaque forming units (PFU) per fish or immersion in water containing 1 × 10^5 PFU/mL for one hour, both of which produce predictable mortality patterns in unvaccinated koi.

#### Example 2

In a series of controlled experiments, the efficacy of the pSGnc vaccine was evaluated under varying conditions. Trial A utilized 4-month-old koi (average weight 3.5 g) vaccinated with 10 μg pSGnc and challenged 28 days later with 1 × 10^6 PFU/fish by intraperitoneal injection. Vaccinated fish showed 20% cumulative mortality compared to 100% mortality in both non-vaccinated controls and fish receiving the pLuc control vaccine, resulting in a relative percent survival (RPS) of 80%. Virus was undetectable in 75% of vaccinated survivors, while all control survivors remained virus-positive. Trial B employed similar conditions but with a higher challenge dose (1 × 10^7 PFU/fish), demonstrating 50% mortality in vaccinated fish versus 100% in controls (RPS = 50%). These trials established that the vaccine provides significant protection against varying challenge doses of homologous virus.

#### Example 3

Additional trials evaluated vaccine performance under different environmental and host conditions. Trial C used 6-month-old koi (average weight 10 g) with a challenge dose of 1 × 10^6 PFU/fish, resulting in 10% mortality in vaccinated fish versus 70% in controls (RPS = 86%). Trial D conducted during winter months with 7-month-old koi (average weight 15 g) showed 20% vaccinated mortality versus 90% control mortality (RPS = 78%). All trials demonstrated statistically significant protection (p ≤ 0.05) by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Notably, vaccinated survivors in later trials (C and D) showed complete viral clearance (0/23 positive), suggesting enhanced immune competence in older fish. The consistent protection across multiple trials with varying conditions confirms the reliability and practical applicability of the vaccine invention.
