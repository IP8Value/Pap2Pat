# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for treating inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, by sequestering high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) protein. More specifically, the invention provides DNA-coated sepharose beads capable of binding HMGB1 with high affinity, thereby neutralizing its pro-inflammatory activity in the gastrointestinal tract. The invention further encompasses methods of administering these DNA beads to reduce inflammation, improve intestinal barrier function, and ameliorate symptoms associated with IBD in mammalian subjects.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract, characterized by periods of remission and relapse. Despite advances in therapeutic strategies, current treatments such as immune-suppressive regimens and cytokine-targeting biologics have limited efficacy and are often associated with significant side effects. The pathogenesis of IBD involves dysregulated immune responses, impaired intestinal barrier function, and elevated levels of pro-inflammatory mediators, including HMGB1.  

HMGB1 is a nuclear protein that, when released extracellularly during cellular stress or injury, acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) molecule, exacerbating inflammation. In IBD, HMGB1 is abundantly present in inflamed intestinal tissues and fecal matter, where it contributes to mucosal inflammation and epithelial dysfunction. Neutralizing HMGB1 activity has been shown to attenuate colitis in animal models, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. However, existing approaches, such as anti-HMGB1 antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors, face challenges related to specificity, stability, and delivery to the intestinal lumen.  

The redox state of HMGB1, determined by modifications of its cysteine residues (C23, C45, and C106), influences its biological activity. For instance, disulfide HMGB1 (with C23 and C45 forming a disulfide bond) exhibits cytokine-stimulating activity, while fully reduced HMGB1 acts as a chemotactic factor. Current therapies do not account for the diversity of HMGB1 isoforms present in IBD, limiting their effectiveness.  

There remains an unmet need for a targeted, stable, and orally administrable therapy that can sequester HMGB1 in the gastrointestinal tract, irrespective of its redox state, to mitigate inflammation and improve clinical outcomes in IBD.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing therapies by providing DNA-coated sepharose beads capable of selectively binding and sequestering HMGB1 in the gastrointestinal tract. The DNA beads comprise high-affinity DNA oligonucleotides immobilized on sepharose beads, which bind HMGB1 with high specificity and capacity, irrespective of its redox or acetylation state.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **High Affinity and Specificity**: The DNA beads bind HMGB1 with dissociation constants (Kd) in the nanomolar range, ensuring efficient sequestration of HMGB1 from biological fluids.  
2. **Redox-State Independence**: The beads capture all major HMGB1 isoforms, including disulfide HMGB1, fully reduced HMGB1, and sulfonyl HMGB1, making them effective across diverse inflammatory conditions.  
3. **Oral Administration**: The beads are stable under acidic conditions (pH 1–2), enabling oral delivery and targeted action in the intestinal lumen.  
4. **Safety**: The DNA beads are non-immunogenic, non-toxic to epithelial cells, and do not induce type I interferon responses, minimizing off-target effects.  
5. **Versatility**: The beads can be used in acute and chronic colitis models, as demonstrated by their efficacy in dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis and IL-10 knockout mice.  

The invention further provides methods for treating IBD by administering the DNA beads to subjects in need thereof, resulting in reduced HMGB1 levels, improved colon histology, and attenuated cytokine production.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Composition of DNA Beads  
The DNA beads of the invention comprise sepharose beads conjugated to DNA oligonucleotides selected for their high affinity to HMGB1. Preferred oligonucleotides include:  
- Linear DNA sequences (e.g., S1 and S2) with phosphorothioate backbones to resist nuclease degradation.  
- Structured DNA constructs (e.g., 4-way junction DNA (S3) or kinked duplex DNA (S4)) for enhanced binding kinetics.  

The oligonucleotides are covalently linked to CNBr-activated sepharose beads via a C6 amino linker, with coupling efficiencies exceeding 90%. Each milliliter of drained beads typically carries 8 nmol of linear DNA (B1, B2) or 4 nmol of structured DNA (B3, B4).  

### Binding Properties  
The DNA beads exhibit the following binding characteristics:  
- **Capacity**: B1 and B2 beads bind HMGB1 at a molar ratio of 2:1 (HMGB1:DNA), while B3 beads bind at a 4:1 ratio.  
- **Kinetics**: Saturation is achieved within 15–30 minutes, with maximal binding observed at 1 hour.  
- **pH Stability**: B1 and B2 beads retain binding capacity at pH 1–7, making them suitable for oral delivery.  

### Therapeutic Efficacy  
In murine models of colitis, oral administration of DNA beads (e.g., B2) resulted in:  
- Reduced fecal and circulating HMGB1 levels.  
- Improved body weight and colon morphology.  
- Attenuated expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β).  
- Enhanced histological scores, indicating reduced mucosal damage.  

### Experimental Details  

#### Preparation of DNA Beads  
1. Oligonucleotides (S1–S4) were synthesized with 5′ amino linkers and conjugated to CNBr-activated sepharose beads.  
2. Control beads were prepared identically but without DNA conjugation.  
3. Binding efficiency was quantified by measuring pre- and post-coupling OD260.  

#### In Vitro Binding Assays  
1. Recombinant HMGB1 (0–3 µg) was incubated with DNA beads (5 µL) for 1 hour.  
2. Bound HMGB1 was eluted and quantified by Western blot.  

#### Animal Studies  
1. **DSS-Induced Colitis**: BALB/c mice received 4% DSS in drinking water for 5 days, followed by B2 or control beads (300 µL of 50% slurry) on days 0, 1, 2, 4, and 6.  
2. **IL-10 KO Mice**: Beads were administered every other day for 6 weeks.  
3. Outcomes included body weight, colon histology, and cytokine levels (ELISA/qPCR).  

#### Safety Profiling  
1. Cytotoxicity was assessed in Caco-2 and Hela cells using LDH release assays.  
2. Immunogenicity was evaluated by measuring TNF and IFN-β responses in macrophages.  

### Conclusions  
The DNA beads of the invention represent a novel, targeted therapy for IBD, capable of sequestering HMGB1 and mitigating inflammation without systemic immunosuppression. Their stability, specificity, and oral bioavailability make them ideal for clinical translation.
