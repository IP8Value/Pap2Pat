# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of regenerative medicine and stem cell therapy for diabetes. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for treating insulin-dependent diabetes through transplantation of genetically modified human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) expressing vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). The invention further encompasses mechanisms of endogenous β-cell regeneration mediated by VEGF-expressing hBMSCs through activation of insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathways.  

## BACKGROUND  

Diabetes mellitus remains a significant global health challenge, with current treatments focusing on exogenous insulin administration failing to achieve optimal glycemic control. While whole pancreas or islet transplantation demonstrates physiologic advantages, these approaches are limited by donor scarcity and immune rejection. Stem cell therapy has emerged as a promising alternative, with bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) being particularly attractive due to their multipotency, ease of isolation, and potential for autologous transplantation.  

Previous attempts to differentiate BMSCs into functional insulin-producing cells have shown limited success, with poor β-cell differentiation efficiency and transient therapeutic effects. While some studies demonstrated partial reversal of hyperglycemia following BMSC transplantation, the mechanisms underlying β-cell recovery remained unclear. The present invention addresses these limitations by demonstrating that hBMSCs genetically modified to express VEGF can induce sustained recovery from diabetes through a novel mechanism involving endogenous β-cell regeneration rather than direct stem cell differentiation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for treating insulin-dependent diabetes comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) genetically modified to express vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF). The genetically modified hBMSCs are capable of engrafting in the pancreatic tissue and differentiating into vascular structures, creating a microenvironment conducive to endogenous β-cell regeneration.  

A key aspect of the invention is the discovery that VEGF-expressing hBMSCs activate the insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathway in pancreatic β-cells, leading to increased expression of downstream targets including PDX1 and decreased expression of cell cycle inhibitor p27Kip1. This activation results in β-cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival while inhibiting apoptosis. The method achieves sustained normoglycemia primarily through regeneration of endogenous β-cells rather than through differentiation of transplanted stem cells into insulin-producing cells.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising VEGF-expressing hBMSCs, methods for preparing said cells through genetic modification with VEGF-encoding vectors, and diagnostic methods for monitoring treatment efficacy through measurement of insulin/IGF pathway activation markers.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for treating insulin-dependent diabetes through transplantation of VEGF-expressing human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells. The therapeutic approach involves several key components: isolation and expansion of hBMSCs from a patient or donor, genetic modification of the cells to express VEGF, and administration of the modified cells to the diabetic subject.  

Human BMSCs are isolated from bone marrow aspirates and cultured in α-MEM medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum, glutamine, and antibiotics. The adherent cell population is expanded through serial passaging while maintaining multipotency as confirmed by differentiation into adipogenic and osteogenic lineages and expression of characteristic surface markers (CD44+, CD105+, CD31-, CD34-).  

Genetic modification of hBMSCs is achieved through transduction with an adenoviral vector encoding VEGF, preferably the VEGF165 isoform. The viral vector utilizes a CMV promoter to drive high-level transgene expression. Transfected cells demonstrate robust VEGF expression at both mRNA and protein levels, confirmed by quantitative PCR and Western blot analysis.  

Administration of VEGF-expressing hBMSCs is performed systemically, preferably through intracardiac injection to ensure widespread distribution. The cells home to pancreatic tissue where they engraft and differentiate primarily into vascular structures. Surprisingly, while some differentiation into insulin-producing cells occurs, the primary therapeutic mechanism involves creation of a vascular niche that supports endogenous β-cell regeneration.  

The invention further elucidates the molecular mechanisms underlying β-cell regeneration, including activation of the insulin/IGF receptor signaling pathway. Transplantation of VEGF-expressing hBMSCs restores intra-islet VEGF expression that is typically depleted in diabetic conditions. This leads to phosphorylation and activation of insulin receptor substrates, PI3-kinase, and AKT. Downstream effects include nuclear localization of PDX1, downregulation of p27Kip1, and inhibition of caspase-3 mediated apoptosis.  

The therapeutic effects are monitored through measurement of blood glucose levels, serum insulin (both human and endogenous), and pancreatic β-cell mass. Successful treatment results in sustained normoglycemia, increased endogenous insulin production, and expansion of β-cell populations with normal islet morphology.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Cell Isolation and Culture  

Human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells were obtained from a commercial source (AllCells, LLC) and cultured in α-MEM medium supplemented with 17% fetal bovine serum, 2 mM glutamine, and antibiotics. Adherent cells were expanded through serial passaging with a doubling time of approximately 48 hours. Cell surface marker expression was confirmed by flow cytometry showing positive expression of CD44 and CD105, and negative expression of CD31 and CD34. Multipotency was verified through differentiation into adipogenic and osteogenic lineages under appropriate culture conditions.  

### Cell Culture and Differentiation  

For genetic modification, hBMSCs at passage 3-6 were transfected with adenoviral vectors encoding either VEGF165 or PDX1. The adenoviral vectors were constructed by subcloning the respective cDNAs into Adeno-X viral DNA (BD Biosciences Clontech) under control of a CMV promoter. Viral particles were expanded in HEK293 cells and titered before use. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by quantitative PCR showing significant increases in VEGF or PDX1 mRNA levels, and Western blot analysis demonstrating corresponding protein expression.  

### Quantitative Real-Time PCR  

Gene expression analysis was performed on pancreatic tissue from treated and control mice using RT2 Profiler PCR Arrays for insulin signaling pathways (SABiosciences). Total RNA was extracted using RNeasy kits (Qiagen) and converted to cDNA. Real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a LightCycler 480 system (Roche). Data analysis revealed significant upregulation of insulin receptor pathway components including Ins1, Igf2, Igfbp1, and Dok3 in mice treated with VEGF-expressing hBMSCs compared to diabetic controls.  

### Immunocytochemistry  

Pancreatic tissues were fixed in paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned for immunohistochemical analysis. Sequential staining was performed using primary antibodies against insulin, glucagon, VEGF, PDX1, AKT, p27Kip1, and cleaved caspase-3, followed by appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies. Confocal microscopy demonstrated restoration of VEGF expression in β-cells, nuclear localization of PDX1, membrane and cytoplasmic distribution of AKT, and reduced p27Kip1 expression in treated animals compared to diabetic controls.  

### Example 5  

In vivo therapeutic efficacy was evaluated in a streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic mouse model (NOD/SCID strain). Diabetes was induced by three intraperitoneal injections of STZ (50 mg/kg). One week after STZ treatment, mice with blood glucose >250 mg/dL received intracardiac injections of 1×10^6 hBMSCs, hBMSCs-VEGF, or hBMSCs-PDX1. Control groups included untreated healthy mice, STZ-diabetic mice, and mice receiving sham injections.  

Mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF showed sustained reversal of hyperglycemia (blood glucose <200 mg/dL) in 55% of cases, compared to transient improvement in hBMSCs-PDX1 recipients and no improvement in unmodified hBMSC recipients. The hBMSCs-VEGF group demonstrated significantly higher survival rates (100% vs 50% in diabetic controls) and weight gain comparable to healthy mice.  

Histological analysis at 6 weeks post-transplantation revealed preserved islet morphology and increased β-cell mass in hBMSCs-VEGF treated mice, similar to healthy controls. Engraftment of human cells was confirmed by detection of human β2-microglobulin and human genomic DNA in pancreatic tissue. While some human-derived insulin-positive cells were detected, the majority of insulin production came from endogenous mouse β-cells, indicating regeneration rather than replacement as the primary mechanism.  

These results demonstrate that VEGF-expressing hBMSCs can induce sustained recovery from diabetes through activation of endogenous β-cell regeneration pathways, providing a novel therapeutic approach for insulin-dependent diabetes.
