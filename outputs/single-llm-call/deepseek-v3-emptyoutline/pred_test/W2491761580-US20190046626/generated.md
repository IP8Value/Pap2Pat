# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates to recombinant expression systems and methods for producing Chlamydia trachomatis major outer membrane protein (MOMP) in bacterial outer membranes. More specifically, the invention provides codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences, optimized expression vectors, leader sequences, and culture conditions for high-yield outer membrane expression of recombinant MOMP (rMOMP). The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing the recombinantly expressed MOMP and methods of using such compositions for inducing protective immune responses against Chlamydia infection.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
Chlamydia trachomatis infections represent a significant global health burden, causing ocular trachoma, lymphogranuloma venereum, and various oculogenital diseases. Current treatment options fail to address the need for durable prevention, highlighting the necessity for an effective vaccine. The major outer membrane protein (MOMP) constitutes approximately 60% of the Chlamydia elementary body's outer membrane mass and contains both B-cell and T-cell epitopes, making it an attractive vaccine candidate.  

Previous attempts to develop MOMP-based vaccines have faced substantial challenges. Formalin-inactivated whole cell vaccines provided only transient protection. While native MOMP (nMOMP) purified from elementary bodies demonstrated protective efficacy, its large-scale production is impractical. Recombinant expression of MOMP in E. coli cytoplasm resulted in inclusion body formation, and subsequent refolding yielded proteins with inferior immunogenicity compared to native MOMP. Prior efforts to express MOMP in bacterial outer membranes encountered problems with host cell viability and low expression yields.  

The present invention overcomes these limitations through comprehensive optimization of the recombinant expression system, including codon harmonization, vector selection, leader sequence optimization, and culture condition refinement. These innovations enable high-yield outer membrane expression of properly folded rMOMP that maintains structural and immunological properties comparable to native MOMP.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The invention provides a novel system for recombinant expression of Chlamydia MOMP in bacterial outer membranes. Key aspects include:  

1) Codon-harmonized MOMP nucleotide sequences that match native Chlamydia codon usage patterns while remaining compatible with E. coli expression systems;  

2) Optimized expression vectors combining moderate-strength promoters with low copy numbers to balance transcription rates and protein folding;  

3) Identification of effective leader sequences (particularly PelB, OmpA, and OmpP) that direct proper MOMP localization to the outer membrane;  

4) Refined culture conditions including specific media (Cinnabar medium), induction parameters (0.4 mM IPTG at OD590 ~0.5), and temperature profiles (4-hour induction at 30°C) that maximize expression while maintaining cell viability;  

5) Efficient purification methods employing sequential detergent extraction and chromatographic steps to isolate properly folded rMOMP;  

6) Pharmaceutical compositions containing the recombinant MOMP combined with immunostimulatory adjuvants; and  

7) Methods of using the recombinant MOMP to induce protective immune responses against Chlamydia infection.  

The recombinant MOMP produced by this system maintains β-barrel secondary structure and elicits antibody responses comparable to native MOMP in animal models. Immunization with the recombinant protein significantly reduces bacterial shedding following Chlamydia challenge, demonstrating its potential as an effective vaccine component.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  
The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions containing recombinantly expressed Chlamydia MOMP. These compositions typically comprise purified rMOMP in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or adjuvants. Preferred adjuvants include combinations of CpG oligonucleotides and Montanide ISA 720 VG, which have been shown to enhance both humoral and cellular immune responses to MOMP.  

The compositions may contain rMOMP from various Chlamydia strains and serovars, including but not limited to C. trachomatis serovars D and E, and C. muridarum. The rMOMP may be formulated at concentrations between 1-100 μg/dose, with 10 μg/dose being particularly effective in mouse models. The compositions may be prepared as injectable formulations, typically for subcutaneous administration, though other routes of administration are also contemplated.  

### Methods of Use  
The invention provides methods for inducing immune responses against Chlamydia infection by administering the recombinant MOMP compositions. In preferred embodiments, the methods involve prime-boost immunization regimens, typically comprising three doses administered at 0, 20, and 30 days.  

The methods are particularly useful for preventing or reducing the severity of Chlamydia infections, including ocular trachoma, lymphogranuloma venereum, and oculogenital diseases. The induced immune responses include production of antibodies that recognize native Chlamydia elementary bodies and provide protection against challenge infections.  

The methods may be adapted for various target populations, including humans at risk of Chlamydia infection. The immunization protocols may be combined with other preventive or therapeutic measures against sexually transmitted infections or ocular diseases.  

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences  
The invention provides optimized nucleotide sequences encoding Chlamydia MOMP that have been codon-harmonized for expression in E. coli. Unlike conventional codon optimization that maximizes usage of host-preferred codons, codon harmonization preserves the relative codon frequency patterns of the native Chlamydia genes while adjusting for expression in E. coli.  

The harmonized sequences avoid rare codon clusters that could cause ribosomal stalling while maintaining appropriate translation kinetics for proper protein folding. The sequences are further optimized by eliminating restriction sites (particularly NdeI and XhoI) that might interfere with cloning procedures.  

Specific examples include harmonized sequences for:  
- C. muridarum Nigg (strain MoPn) MOMP  
- C. trachomatis strain D/UW-3/CX MOMP (serovar D)  
- C. trachomatis strain E/12-94 MOMP (serovar E)  

These sequences enable approximately 2-fold higher outer membrane expression compared to conventionally codon-optimized sequences, as demonstrated by flow cytometry analysis of surface-expressed protein.  

## EXAMPLE 1  

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP  
The codon-harmonized MOMP genes were cloned into the pAVE029 expression vector (MSD Biologics UK) using NdeI and XhoI restriction sites. The recombinant plasmids were transformed into E. coli K12 W25113 by electroporation.  

Transformants were grown in Cinnabar medium with 10 μg/mL tetracycline at 37°C with shaking until OD590 reached approximately 0.5. Protein expression was induced by adding 0.4 mM IPTG and incubating for 4 hours at 30°C with shaking.  

Whole cell flow cytometry using anti-Chlamydia EB antibodies demonstrated successful outer membrane expression of rMOMP. The expression level was approximately 300-400 geomean fluorescence intensity units under optimized conditions. SDS-PAGE and western blot analysis confirmed the presence of properly processed rMOMP.  

## EXAMPLE 2  

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP  
Cells expressing rMOMP were harvested by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 15 minutes. Cell pellets were resuspended in 50 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0 buffer with protease inhibitors and disrupted by microfluidization.  

The membrane fraction was isolated by sequential centrifugation steps:  
1) 9,700 x g for 15 minutes to remove unbroken cells  
2) 23,800 x g for 90 minutes to pellet membranes  
3) High-salt wash (1 M NaCl, 0.05% Tween 20) followed by centrifugation at 23,800 x g for 90 minutes  

Membrane proteins were fractionated using:  
1) 1% Triton X-100 to remove inner membrane proteins  
2) 3% β-octyl-glucoside to remove non-MOMP outer membrane proteins  

rMOMP was extracted with 1% sarkosyl and 5 mM DTT, then purified by:  
1) Size exclusion chromatography (Sephacryl S300) in 10 mM HEPES pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% zwittergent 3-14  
2) Ion exchange chromatography (Hitrap Q FF)  

The final yield was approximately 6 mg purified rMOMP per liter of culture. Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed the protein maintained β-strand secondary structure characteristic of native MOMP.  

## EXAMPLE 3  

### Mouse Immunogenicity and Challenge Study  
Female C57BL/6 mice (6-8 weeks old) were immunized subcutaneously on days 0, 20, and 30 with:  
1) 10 μg rMOMP + CpG/Montanide adjuvant  
2) 10 μg native MOMP + CpG/Montanide adjuvant  
3) 1x10^6 live EB (positive control)  
4) Adjuvant only (negative control)  

Serum samples collected two weeks post-immunization showed comparable IgG titers against Chlamydia EB in both rMOMP and nMOMP groups (p>0.05 by ANOVA). The IgG1/IgG2c ratios indicated similar Th1/Th2 bias profiles.  

Mice were challenged intravaginally with 1x10^5 Ct serovar D EBs approximately one month after final immunization. Quantitative PCR analysis of vaginal swabs showed:  
- Significant reduction in bacterial shedding in both rMOMP and nMOMP groups compared to adjuvant control (p<0.01)  
- No significant difference in protection between rMOMP and nMOMP groups  

This demonstrates that outer membrane-expressed rMOMP elicits protective immune responses comparable to native MOMP.
