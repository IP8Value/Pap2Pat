# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of plant biotechnology and genetic engineering, specifically to a novel male sterility-fertility restoration system for hybrid seed production. More particularly, the invention provides a genetically engineered system comprising: (1) a female parent line exhibiting complete male sterility through tapetum-specific, high-level expression of a sterility-inducing gene (such as BECLIN1/ATG6) under control of a modified transcriptional regulation system, and (2) a male parent line capable of restoring fertility in F1 hybrid progeny through controlled degradation of essential transcriptional components. The invention further relates to methods of creating and utilizing said system for commercial hybrid seed production in plants, particularly in crop species.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION AND PRIOR ART  

Hybrid seed technology has revolutionized modern agriculture by harnessing heterosis (hybrid vigor) to significantly increase crop yields. Conventional hybrid breeding relies on cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) systems, which have several limitations including maternal inheritance patterns, susceptibility to environmental factors, and restricted applicability across species. Genetic engineering approaches have attempted to overcome these limitations through various artificial male sterility systems, though each presents its own challenges.  

Prior art includes several engineered male sterility systems:  
1) The barnase/barstar system, where tapetum-specific expression of the ribonuclease barnase induces male sterility, with fertility restored by co-expression of its inhibitor barstar. This system suffers from incomplete fertility restoration and potential yield penalties due to leaky barnase expression.  
2) Cytotoxic gene systems (e.g., BAX, diphtheria toxin) which often cause pleiotropic effects beyond male sterility.  
3) RNA interference-based systems that silence essential pollen development genes, though these frequently show incomplete penetrance.  
4) Site-specific recombination systems (e.g., Cre/loxP) that can restore fertility but require complex genetic engineering.  

### LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ART  

Existing systems face several critical limitations:  
1) Incomplete male sterility in female parent lines, leading to self-pollination contamination.  
2) Incomplete fertility restoration in F1 hybrids, resulting in suboptimal seed set.  
3) Leaky expression of sterility genes in non-target tissues causing yield penalties.  
4) Requirement for species-specific fertility restorer genes.  
5) Complex multi-component systems that are difficult to transfer across species.  
6) Dependence on conditional (e.g., temperature-sensitive) systems that are environmentally unstable.  

### Objectives of the Present Invention  

The present invention addresses these limitations through several key objectives:  
1) To provide a complete male sterility system with no detectable pollen viability.  
2) To enable complete fertility restoration in F1 hybrids through transcriptional control.  
3) To achieve stringent, tapetum-specific expression without leaky expression in other tissues.  
4) To develop a modular system adaptable to various sterility-inducing genes.  
5) To create a genetically stable system independent of environmental conditions.  
6) To provide a method for efficient maintenance of male-sterile female lines.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel two-component system for male sterility and fertility restoration comprising:  

1) A female expression cassette containing:  
   - A sterility-inducing gene (e.g., Arabidopsis BECLIN1/ATG6) under control of a tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., TA29) with a modified TGTA TATA-box  
   - A translationally fused HFR1NT131-TBPm3 protein that enables transcription from the TGTA-modified promoter  

2) A male expression cassette containing:  
   - A COP1 or COP1L105A gene under control of a tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., A9)  
   - The COP1 protein mediates degradation of HFR1NT131-TBPm3, abolishing sterility gene expression  

Key features include:  
- The TGTA-TBPm3 complementation system provides 4-6 fold higher expression than native promoters  
- HFR1NT131 fusion allows light-regulated control through COP1 interaction  
- COP1L105A mutant provides complete transcriptional abolition through enhanced nuclear localization  
- Complete male sterility (0.76% viability vs 77.7% in controls)  
- Complete fertility restoration (74.58% viability vs 77.7% in controls)  
- Stringent tapetum-specific expression without detectable expression in other tissues  

The system has been demonstrated in tobacco, showing:  
- Normal plant growth and development except for male sterility  
- Complete pollen abortion with deformed microspores visible by TEM/SEM  
- Normal female fertility allowing cross-pollination  
- Complete restoration of pollen viability and seed set in F1 hybrids  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a genetically encoded system for controlled male sterility and fertility restoration comprising two principal components:  

1) Female Sterility Component:  
The female parent line contains an expression cassette with:  
- A tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., TA29) modified to contain a TGTA TATA-box instead of the native TATAA sequence  
- A sterility-inducing gene (e.g., BECLIN1/ATG6) operably linked to said promoter  
- A HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion gene under constitutive expression  

The modified promoter cannot be recognized by native TBP proteins. The TBPm3 mutant (Ile152Phe, Val161Thr, Leu163Val) specifically binds the TGTA sequence, while the HFR1NT131 domain confers COP1-mediated degradation control. This system provides:  
- High-level expression (4-6× native promoter levels)  
- Stringent tapetum-specificity (no detectable expression in leaves, stems, roots)  
- Post-meiotic temporal control (stages 2-3 in tobacco)  

2) Male Fertility Restoration Component:  
The male parent line contains an expression cassette with:  
- A tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., A9)  
- A COP1 or COP1L105A gene operably linked to said promoter  

The COP1L105A mutant shows enhanced nuclear localization while maintaining dimerization and degradation activity. In F1 hybrids:  
- COP1L105A binds HFR1NT131, targeting the entire fusion for proteasomal degradation  
- Depletion of TBPm3 prevents transcription initiation at TGTA promoters  
- Complete abolition of sterility gene expression (21-fold reduction vs partial systems)  

Molecular Mechanism:  
The system operates through:  
1) In female lines:  
   - Dedicated pool of HFR1NT131-TBPm3 drives high-level sterility gene expression  
   - BECLIN1 overexpression causes abnormal tapetal development and microspore abortion  
   - Tapetum fails to degenerate normally, leading to pollen collapse  

2) In F1 hybrids:  
   - Paternal COP1L105A expression leads to HFR1NT131-TBPm3 degradation  
   - TGTA promoters become transcriptionally inactive  
   - Normal tapetal development and pollen formation occur  

### EXAMPLES  

Example 1: Vector Construction  
Four expression cassettes were constructed in plant transformation vectors:  
1) 1370: TGTA-PTA29-gusA + Pcec-HFR1NT131-TBPm3 (reporter control)  
2) 1371: TGTA-PTA29-BECLIN1 + Pcec-HFR1NT131-TBPm3 (sterility line)  
3) 1372: PA9-COP1 (partial restoration line)  
4) 1373: PA9-COP1L105A (complete restoration line)  

Example 2: Male Sterility Phenotype  
1371 transgenic tobacco lines showed:  
- Normal vegetative growth and female fertility  
- Complete male sterility (0.76% pollen viability vs 77.7% in controls)  
- No detectable in vitro pollen germination  
- SEM showing deformed pollen wall architecture  
- TEM showing abnormal tapetal development and microspore collapse  

Example 3: Fertility Restoration  
F1 hybrids (1371 × 1373) showed:  
- Complete abolition of BECLIN1 expression (qRT-PCR)  
- Normal pollen viability (74.58% vs 77.7% in controls)  
- Normal in vitro germination (69% vs 70% in controls)  
- SEM showing normal pollen morphology  
- Normal seed set (71.9 mg/pod vs 74.23 mg in controls)  

Example 4: Molecular Analysis  
- Fluorimetric GUS assays showed complete abolition in F1 (1370 × 1373)  
- qRT-PCR confirmed stage-specific COP1L105A expression (peak at stage 1)  
- Western blot showed depletion of HFR1NT131-TBPm3 in F1 anthers  
- Histochemical staining showed stringent tapetum-specific expression  

Example 5: System Stability  
- Phenotype stable over 5 generations  
- No detectable expression in vegetative tissues  
- No yield penalty in female parent lines  
- Consistent fertility restoration in F1 hybrids  

The examples demonstrate the complete male sterility and complete fertility restoration achieved by the invention, representing a significant improvement over prior art systems. The modular design allows substitution of sterility genes (e.g., barnase, BAX) and promoters for adaptation to different crop species.
