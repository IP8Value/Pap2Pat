# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for controlling gene expression in mammalian cells through modulation of RNA self-cleavage. Conventional gene regulation systems predominantly rely on transcriptional control or RNA interference mechanisms. While these approaches have proven useful, they exhibit limitations in terms of specificity, efficiency, and applicability across diverse biological contexts.  

There exists an unmet need for alternative gene regulation strategies that operate through distinct molecular mechanisms, thereby expanding the toolkit available for biomedical research and therapeutic applications. Ribozymes, or self-cleaving RNA motifs, present an attractive platform for such alternative regulation systems. When incorporated into mRNA transcripts, ribozymes can mediate self-cleavage, leading to mRNA degradation and consequent suppression of gene expression. Modulation of ribozyme activity through small-molecule inhibitors offers a means to precisely control gene expression at the post-transcriptional level.  

Prior attempts to develop ribozyme-based regulation systems have been hindered by the lack of efficient ribozyme sequences capable of functioning in mammalian cells and the absence of suitable small-molecule inhibitors that can effectively modulate ribozyme activity in vivo. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing optimized ribozyme sequences derived from Schistosoma mansoni (Sm1) and identifying novel small-molecule inhibitors capable of potently inhibiting ribozyme self-cleavage in mammalian cells.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides compositions and methods for regulating gene expression in mammalian cells through modulation of RNA self-cleavage. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Modified hammerhead ribozyme sequences, particularly the N79 ribozyme derived from Sm1, which exhibit efficient self-cleavage activity when embedded in mRNA transcripts in mammalian cells.  

2. Small-molecule inhibitors of ribozyme self-cleavage, including adenosine analogs (e.g., toyocamycin, tubercidin) and uridine analogs (e.g., 5-fluorouridine), which act through incorporation into ribozyme-containing mRNAs to inhibit self-cleavage.  

3. Cell-based screening assays for identifying ribozyme inhibitors, utilizing stable cell lines harboring reporter genes under the control of ribozyme sequences.  

4. Methods for regulating gene expression in vitro and in vivo by administering ribozyme inhibitors, including synergistic combinations of adenosine and uridine analogs.  

5. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising ribozyme inhibitors for therapeutic applications.  

The invention enables precise control of gene expression through an RNA-only regulatory mechanism, offering advantages over conventional transcription-based systems. The identified small-molecule inhibitors provide tools for research applications and potential therapeutic interventions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs  

The invention provides modified hammerhead ribozyme sequences capable of efficient self-cleavage in mammalian cells. Particularly, the N79 ribozyme, derived from Schistosoma mansoni (Sm1), has been optimized for function in mammalian systems. This ribozyme maintains stable secondary structure and exhibits robust self-cleavage activity when embedded in mRNA transcripts.  

The N79 ribozyme contains characteristic stem-loop structures that facilitate proper folding and catalytic activity in the cellular environment. Key structural features include conserved core sequences required for self-cleavage and variable regions that can be modified to adjust cleavage efficiency. The ribozyme can be incorporated in single or multiple copies within mRNA transcripts to achieve varying degrees of expression control.  

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines  

The invention encompasses expression vectors designed for ribozyme-mediated gene regulation. These vectors typically comprise:  
1) A promoter element (e.g., CMV promoter) for transcriptional initiation  
2) A gene of interest  
3) One or more copies of the N79 ribozyme sequence embedded within the transcript  
4) Appropriate transcriptional termination signals  

Stable cell lines harboring such expression vectors have been developed for screening and research applications. For example, the HEK79 cell line contains a luciferase reporter gene under control of the N79 ribozyme, providing a sensitive system for monitoring ribozyme activity and inhibitor effects. These cell lines exhibit low basal expression due to ribozyme-mediated mRNA cleavage and can be induced to high expression levels upon administration of ribozyme inhibitors.  

### Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids  

The invention provides methods for delivering ribozyme-containing nucleic acids to target cells. Delivery approaches include:  
1) Viral vector systems (e.g., AAV, lentivirus) for stable integration  
2) Lipid-based transfection reagents  
3) Electroporation  
4) Nanoparticle formulations  

For in vivo applications, adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors have been successfully employed to deliver ribozyme-regulated transgenes to specific tissues. The choice of delivery method depends on the target cell type, duration of expression required, and therapeutic context.  

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators  

A high-throughput screening platform has been developed to identify small-molecule inhibitors of ribozyme self-cleavage. The screening method involves:  
1) Culturing reporter cell lines (e.g., HEK79) in multi-well formats  
2) Exposing cells to compound libraries  
3) Measuring reporter gene expression as an indicator of ribozyme inhibition  
4) Validating hits in secondary assays  

This approach has identified several classes of ribozyme inhibitors, including adenosine analogs (toyocamycin, tubercidin) and uridine analogs (5-fluorouridine). The screening platform enables rapid evaluation of large compound libraries and structure-activity relationship studies.  

### Methods of Treatment and Administration  

The invention provides therapeutic methods utilizing ribozyme-mediated gene regulation. Treatment protocols may involve:  
1) Administration of ribozyme-containing vectors to target tissues  
2) Delivery of small-molecule ribozyme inhibitors to modulate gene expression  
3) Combination therapies with other pharmacological agents  

For systemic delivery, ribozyme inhibitors can be administered via intravenous, subcutaneous, or oral routes. Localized delivery (e.g., intratumoral injection) may be employed for site-specific effects. Dosage regimens are tailored based on the pharmacokinetic properties of the inhibitors and the desired duration of gene expression modulation.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

Pharmaceutical formulations of ribozyme inhibitors have been developed for research and therapeutic applications. These compositions typically comprise:  
1) An active inhibitor (e.g., toyocamycin, 5-fluorouridine)  
2) Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers  
3) Optional excipients to enhance stability or bioavailability  

The formulations may be prepared as solutions, suspensions, tablets, or controlled-release preparations depending on the intended route of administration. Combination formulations containing multiple ribozyme inhibitors (e.g., adenosine and uridine analogs) have shown particular utility due to synergistic effects.
