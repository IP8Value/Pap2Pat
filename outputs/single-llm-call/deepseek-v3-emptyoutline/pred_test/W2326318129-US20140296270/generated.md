# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to novel small molecule inhibitors of Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) and their use in treating cancers characterized by STAT3 activation, particularly head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). More specifically, the invention provides optimized derivatives of the STAT3 inhibitor C188, particularly compound C188-9, which demonstrates superior binding affinity, specificity, and therapeutic efficacy against STAT3-dependent cancers. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing these inhibitors, methods of treatment using these compounds alone or in combination with radiation therapy or chemotherapy, and kits for implementing such treatments.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a member of a family of seven closely related proteins that transmit peptide hormone signals from cell surface receptors to the nucleus. STAT3 serves as a master regulator of multiple oncogenic processes including cell proliferation, apoptosis resistance, metastasis, immune evasion, angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, DNA damage response, and metabolic reprogramming. Constitutive activation of STAT3 occurs in approximately 50% of all human cancers through various mechanisms including upstream activation of receptor tyrosine kinases, cytokine receptors, or Src family kinases.  

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) represents a particularly relevant therapeutic target for STAT3 inhibition, as it was the first human cancer demonstrated to depend on constitutively activated STAT3 for growth. While EGFR inhibitors have shown some efficacy in HNSCC treatment, many tumors demonstrate STAT3 activation independent of EGFR signaling through alternative pathways such as IL-6/gp130 autocrine loops. Furthermore, both STAT3 and the related STAT1 protein have been implicated in radiation resistance, creating an urgent need for effective inhibitors that can target both proteins.  

Current small molecule STAT3 inhibitors suffer from limitations including poor potency, lack of specificity, or unfavorable pharmacokinetic properties. For example, Stattic demonstrates radiation-sensitizing effects but has a low maximum tolerated dose due to off-target effects from its covalent mechanism of action. There remains a significant unmet need for STAT3 inhibitors with improved pharmacological properties suitable for clinical development.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these needs by providing novel small molecule STAT3 inhibitors, particularly the lead compound C188-9, which was developed through structure-activity optimization of the parent compound C188. C188-9 demonstrates superior properties including:  

1) High binding affinity for the STAT3 SH2 domain (KD = 4.7 ± 0.4 nM)  
2) Potent inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation (IC50 = 3.7 μM against G-CSF-induced pSTAT3)  
3) Specificity for STAT3 over related STAT family members  
4) Oral bioavailability and favorable pharmacokinetic profile  
5) Effective tumor penetration with tumor:plasma concentration ratio of ~3:1  
6) Dual activity against both STAT3 and STAT1 pathways  
7) Enhanced efficacy in HNSCC xenograft models compared to parent compound  
8) Favorable safety profile with no observed toxicity at therapeutic doses  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing these STAT3 inhibitors, methods for treating STAT3-dependent cancers (particularly HNSCC), and combination therapies with radiation or chemotherapeutic agents. The compounds are particularly useful for overcoming radiation resistance in cancer therapy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### I. DEFINITIONS  

As used throughout this specification, the following terms shall have the following meanings unless context clearly indicates otherwise:  

"STAT3" refers to Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3, including all isoforms, variants, and post-translationally modified forms thereof.  

"SH2 domain" refers to the Src homology 2 domain of STAT3 responsible for phosphotyrosine peptide binding and dimerization.  

"pSTAT3" refers to STAT3 phosphorylated at tyrosine 705, the activated form of STAT3 capable of dimerization and nuclear translocation.  

"HNSCC" refers to head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and includes all subtypes and stages of this malignancy.  

"IC50" refers to the half-maximal inhibitory concentration of a compound in a specified biological assay.  

"KD" refers to the equilibrium dissociation constant measuring binding affinity between two molecules.  

"Radiation therapy" refers to any therapeutic use of ionizing radiation including but not limited to external beam radiotherapy, brachytherapy, and stereotactic radiosurgery.  

### II. DERIVATIVES  

The invention encompasses derivatives of the parent compound C188 having the general structure of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide with modifications at key positions that enhance STAT3 inhibitory activity. Structural analysis revealed three classes of derivatives:  

Group I derivatives contain substitutions at the 3-position of the naphthylamine ring, including but not limited to triazole-3-yl-mercapto and binaphthyl groups. These derivatives demonstrate the highest potency with IC50 values in the low micromolar range.  

Group II derivatives incorporate a 5-membered ring combining the 3-R and 4-OR2 groups, such as furan derivatives. These compounds show intermediate activity approximately 5-fold less potent than Group I derivatives.  

Group III derivatives lack favorable substitutions and demonstrate the lowest activity.  

Particularly preferred is the lead compound C188-9 containing a binaphthyl substitution at the 3-position, which demonstrates superior pharmacological properties including:  

1) STAT3 pY-peptide binding inhibition IC50 = 2,500 nM  
2) G-CSF-induced pSTAT3 inhibition IC50 = 3.7 μM  
3) STAT3 binding affinity KD = 4.7 nM  
4) Calculated inhibition constant Ki = 12.4 nM  

### III. EMBODIMENTS FOR TARGETING STAT3  

The compounds of the invention specifically target the SH2 domain of STAT3, blocking both phosphopeptide binding and subsequent dimerization. This targeting occurs through:  

1) High-affinity binding to the pY705 binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain  
2) Competitive displacement of endogenous phosphopeptide ligands  
3) Prevention of STAT3 homodimerization  
4) Inhibition of nuclear translocation of activated STAT3  

The compounds demonstrate specificity for STAT3 over other STAT family members, particularly STAT1, while maintaining the ability to inhibit both proteins when desired. This dual specificity is particularly advantageous for overcoming radiation resistance in cancer therapy.  

### IV. TARGETING CANCER STEM CELLS  

The compounds of the invention demonstrate particular efficacy against cancer stem cells through:  

1) Inhibition of STAT3-mediated self-renewal pathways  
2) Disruption of tumor-initiating cell maintenance  
3) Prevention of cancer stem cell expansion  
4) Reduction of tumor recurrence potential  

In xenograft models, treatment with C188-9 significantly reduced cancer stem cell markers and prevented tumor regrowth after initial regression.  

### V. COMBINATION THERAPY  

The invention encompasses combination therapies employing the STAT3 inhibitors with:  

1) Radiation therapy - for overcoming radiation resistance mediated by STAT3/STAT1 pathways  
2) Chemotherapeutic agents - including but not limited to platinum compounds, taxanes, and antimetabolites  
3) Targeted therapies - including EGFR inhibitors, angiogenesis inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors  
4) Other signal transduction inhibitors - particularly those targeting upstream activators of STAT3  

Combination regimens may involve concurrent or sequential administration of the therapeutic agents. Preferred dosing schedules maintain therapeutic levels of the STAT3 inhibitor during radiation therapy sessions.  

### VI. PHARMACEUTICAL COMPOSITIONS  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions containing the STAT3 inhibitors in formulations suitable for:  

1) Oral administration - including tablets, capsules, and liquid formulations  
2) Parenteral administration - including intravenous, subcutaneous, and intramuscular injections  
3) Topical administration - for mucosal and dermatological applications  
4) Implantable devices - for sustained local delivery  

Compositions typically contain the active compound in concentrations ranging from 0.1 mg/mL to 100 mg/mL combined with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, carriers, or diluents.  

### VII. KITS OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides kits containing:  

1) One or more STAT3 inhibitor compounds  
2) Instructions for use in cancer treatment  
3) Optional additional therapeutic agents  
4) Optional diagnostic tools for assessing STAT3 activation status  

Kits may be configured for specific treatment regimens including monotherapy or combination therapy protocols.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Exemplary Materials and Methods  

Cell lines were maintained under standard culture conditions appropriate for each cell type. STAT3 inhibition assays included surface plasmon resonance binding studies, phosphoflow analysis of phosphorylation status, Luminex bead-based quantification of phosphoproteins, and microscale thermophoresis binding measurements. Animal studies employed xenograft models in immunodeficient mice with tumor volume monitoring and molecular analysis of excised tumors.  

### Identification by VLS of Compounds that Blocked Stat3 Binding to its Phosphopeptide Ligand and Inhibited IL-6-Mediated Phosphorylation of STAT3  

Virtual ligand screening of 920,000 compounds identified three initial hits (C3, C30, and C188) with STAT3 inhibitory activity. Subsequent 2D fingerprint screening and 3D pharmacophore analysis of C188 derivatives identified C188-9 as the lead compound with improved potency in both pY-peptide binding assays (IC50 = 2,500 nM) and cellular phosphorylation assays (IC50 = 3.7 μM).  

### Compound-Mediated Inhibition of Ligand-Stimulated Phosphorylation of STAT3 is Specific for Stat3 Vs. Stat1  

While C188-9 demonstrated primary specificity for STAT3, it also showed significant activity against STAT1 (IC50 = 9.5 μM against IFN-γ-induced STAT1 phosphorylation). This dual activity proved particularly advantageous in overcoming radiation resistance mediated by STAT1-dependent gene expression.  

### Sequence Analysis and Molecular Modeling of the Interaction of Each Compound with the Stat3 Vs. Stat1 SH2 Domain  

Molecular modeling revealed key structural features responsible for STAT3 binding affinity and selectivity. The binaphthyl group of C188-9 occupies a hydrophobic pocket adjacent to the phosphotyrosine binding site, while maintaining favorable hydrogen bonding interactions with conserved SH2 domain residues.  

### Example 5  

In vivo studies demonstrated that C188-9 treatment (50 mg/kg/day) significantly inhibited growth of UM-SCC-17B xenografts compared to vehicle controls (p = 0.027). Tumor analysis revealed 57% reduction in pSTAT3 levels (p = 0.017) and 80% reduction in pSTAT1 levels (p = 0.000003), accompanied by significant modulation of STAT3/STAT1-regulated gene expression.
