# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The field of polymer science has witnessed significant advancements in the design and synthesis of bottlebrush polymers, which exhibit unique properties suitable for applications in drug delivery, molecular imaging, and self-assembly. Traditional linear triblock copolymers have been extensively studied for their utility in forming hydrogels and micelles, yet their bottlebrush counterparts remain underexplored, particularly in the context of biomedical applications.  

Current hydrogel systems often suffer from limitations such as rapid erosion, poor mechanical stability, and inadequate control over drug release kinetics. Linear triblock copolymers, while useful, are constrained by their inability to prevent primary loop formation, which compromises network integrity. Additionally, existing drug delivery platforms frequently fail to provide sustained localized release, leading to systemic toxicity and suboptimal therapeutic outcomes.  

There exists a need for novel polymeric architectures that combine the advantages of bottlebrush topology with the functional versatility of triblock copolymers. Such materials should enable precise control over self-assembly, gelation behavior, and drug encapsulation while maintaining biocompatibility and tunable release profiles.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses thermoresponsive triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) comprising polylactic acid (PLA), polyethylene glycol (PEG), and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) blocks. These ABC-sequenced TBCs self-assemble into stable micelles at room temperature and undergo rapid gelation upon heating above the lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of PNIPAM (~32°C).  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Facile synthesis via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers.  
- Formation of uniform micelles (~100 nm) capable of encapsulating diverse therapeutics, including hydrophobic small molecules and proteins.  
- Injectable hydrogel formation at physiological temperatures, enabling localized drug depot formation.  
- Sustained release kinetics with reduced systemic exposure and improved therapeutic indices.  

The TBCs are particularly suited for immunochemotherapy, as demonstrated by their ability to co-deliver paclitaxel (PTX) and resiquimod (R848) with enhanced efficacy and reduced cytokine-mediated toxicity in murine tumor models.  

## Definitions  

### Chemical Definitions  

- **Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymer (TBC):** A macromolecule comprising three distinct polymer blocks (A, B, C) grafted onto a central backbone, where each block contributes specific physicochemical properties (e.g., hydrophobicity, thermoresponsiveness).  
- **ROMP (Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization):** A living polymerization technique employing Grubbs catalysts to polymerize cyclic olefins, such as norbornene derivatives.  
- **LCST (Lower Critical Solution Temperature):** The temperature above which a polymer (e.g., PNIPAM) undergoes phase separation due to dehydration and chain collapse.  

### Other Definitions  

- **Micelleplexes:** Higher-order aggregates formed by micelle fusion, typically induced by environmental triggers (e.g., temperature).  
- **Therapeutic Index:** The ratio of the dose required for efficacy to the dose causing toxicity, where higher values indicate safer drugs.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS  

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers  

The ABC TBCs of the invention comprise:  
1. A hydrophobic PLA block (A) driving micelle core formation.  
2. A hydrophilic PEG block (B) providing steric stabilization and biocompatibility.  
3. A thermoresponsive PNIPAM block (C) enabling temperature-triggered gelation.  

The block sequence is critical; ABC TBCs outperform reverse (CBA) or statistical (ABC-stat) analogues in micelle uniformity and gel robustness.  

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers  

Synthesis involves:  
1. **Macromonomer Preparation:**  
   - Nb-PLA: Synthesized via ring-opening polymerization of lactide initiated by norbornene-alcohol.  
   - Nb-PEG: Obtained by coupling amine-terminated PEG to norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) ester.  
   - Nb-PNIPAM: Prepared by conjugating amine-terminated PNIPAM to norbornene-NHS.  
2. **Sequential ROMP:**  
   - Polymerization initiated by Grubbs 3rd generation catalyst (G3).  
   - Blocks added in order A→B→C, with real-time monitoring via size exclusion chromatography (SEC).  
   - Termination with ethyl vinyl ether (EVE) yields TBCs with narrow dispersity (Ð < 1.2).  

### Particles and Hydrogels  

- **Micelles:** Formed in aqueous solution (1 mg/mL) with Dh ~100 nm (PDI < 0.1), as confirmed by DLS and cryoTEM. Core-shell morphology features a PLA core (20 nm) and PEG/PNIPAM corona.  
- **Hydrogels:** Formed at ≥10 wt% TBC concentration upon heating above LCST. Rheology shows G′ > G′′ above 36°C, indicating elastic network formation via PLA/PNIPAM domain crosslinking.  

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration  

- **Drug Loading:** Hydrophobic drugs (e.g., PTX, R848) are encapsulated by dissolving TBC and drug in DMSO, followed by dialysis into buffer. Encapsulation efficiency correlates with drug log D.  
- **Injectable Formulations:** 10 wt% TBC solutions gel in situ post-injection, enabling localized delivery. Kits may include lyophilized TBC and reconstitution buffers.  

### Methods of Treatment and Use  

- **Cancer Immunotherapy:** Intratumoral injection of PTX/R848-loaded TBC hydrogels in CT26 murine models achieved 80% cure rates vs. 10% for free drugs.  
- **Sustained Release:** Linear release profiles (t½ ~3–5 days) for small molecules and proteins (e.g., ovalbumin).  

## EXAMPLES  

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles  

ABC TBCs (Mn ~150 kDa) were synthesized via ROMP of Nb-PLA (DP=20), Nb-PEG (DP=45), and Nb-PNIPAM (DP=30). SEC confirmed monomodal distributions (Fig. 3b).  

### General Methods and Instruments  

- **SEC:** Agilent PL-GPC 220, THF eluent, 1 mL/min.  
- **DLS:** Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS, 25°C.  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester  

Norbornene-COOH (5 g) was reacted with NHS (3.8 g) and DCC (4.1 g) in DCM to yield Nb-NHS (87%).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)  

NH₂-PEG (5 kDa, 10 g) and Nb-NHS (1.2 eq) in PBS yielded Nor-PEG (92%) after dialysis.  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)  

NH₂-PNIPAM (5.5 kDa) was conjugated to Nb-NHS as above (Mn confirmed by ¹H NMR).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol  

Norbornene anhydride (10 g) was reduced with LiAlH₄ to give norbornene-propanol (95%).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PLA Macromonomer (Nor-PLA)  

Norbornene-propanol initiated lactide polymerization (Sn(Oct)₂ catalyst) to yield Nor-PLA (Mn=3 kDa, Ð=1.1).  

### Ring-Opening Metathesis (ROMP) Polymerization  

Nor-PLA (0.1 mmol), G3 (0.002 mmol) in THF, 30 min; sequential addition of Nor-PEG and Nor-PNIPAM. Quenched with EVE, precipitated in hexane.

The invention encompasses variations in block chemistry (e.g., substituting PLA with PCL), macromonomer length, and drug combinations. The claims cover:  
1. ABC TBCs and derivatives.  
2. Methods of synthesis and drug encapsulation.  
3. Therapeutic uses, including immunochemotherapy.
