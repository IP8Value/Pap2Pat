## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for selectively treating cancer cells using superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs) in combination with super-low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields. More specifically, the invention pertains to polymer-coated SMNPs that accumulate within lysosomes of cancer cells and, upon exposure to an AC magnetic field, induce cytoskeletal disruption leading to selective cancer cell death without affecting healthy cells.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with conventional treatments such as chemotherapy and radiation often causing severe side effects due to their lack of specificity. Magnetic nanoparticle-based therapies have emerged as promising alternatives due to their potential for targeted delivery and remote actuation. However, existing approaches relying on hyperthermia (heat generation) suffer from limitations, including insufficient tumor penetration and collateral damage to healthy tissues.  

Prior studies have explored the use of magnetic nanoparticles in alternating magnetic fields to induce mechanical forces capable of disrupting cellular structures. However, these methods typically require high-frequency fields or rotating magnetic fields, which may not be suitable for deep tissue applications. Additionally, existing approaches often lack selectivity between cancerous and non-cancerous cells, limiting their therapeutic efficacy.  

There remains an unmet need for a cancer treatment system that combines efficient intracellular nanoparticle uptake, remote actuation via low-frequency magnetic fields, and selective cytotoxicity toward cancer cells while sparing healthy tissues.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel system for selectively inducing cancer cell death through cytoskeletal disruption using polymer-coated superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs) and super-low frequency AC magnetic fields. The system comprises SMNPs coated with a pentablock copolymer, polyacrylic acid-b-Pluronic P85-b-polyacrylic acid (PAA-P85-PAA), which enhances intracellular uptake and lysosomal accumulation.  

Upon exposure to an AC magnetic field (e.g., 50 Hz, 50–100 kA/m), the SMNPs generate mechanical forces that disrupt the actin cytoskeleton of cancer cells, leading to apoptosis. Notably, the system exhibits high selectivity, as non-cancerous cells with stiffer cytoskeletons remain unaffected. The invention further includes methods for synthesizing the polymer-SMNP complexes, administering them to target tissues, and applying pulsed or continuous AC magnetic fields to induce cancer cell death.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **Selective cytotoxicity**—Cancer cells are preferentially targeted due to their softer cytoskeletal structure.  
2. **Remote actuation**—Low-frequency AC fields (30–3000 Hz) enable deep tissue penetration without heating effects.  
3. **Enhanced uptake**—The PAA-P85-PAA coating promotes efficient lysosomal accumulation in cancer cells.  
4. **Minimal toxicity to healthy cells**—Non-cancerous cells (e.g., MCF10A) show no significant viability reduction after treatment.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides compositions and methods for selectively killing cancer cells using polymer-coated SMNPs and AC magnetic fields. Below is a detailed description of the components, experimental methods, and exemplary embodiments.  

### Experimental Methods  

**Synthesis of Polymer-SMNP Complexes**  
The SMNPs were synthesized via co-precipitation and coated with PAA-P85-PAA pentablock copolymer through ligand exchange. The hydrodynamic diameter of the resulting complexes ranged from 30–70 nm, as measured by dynamic light scattering (DLS). The polymer content was approximately 60 wt%, confirmed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). The complexes exhibited a ζ-potential of −35 to −50 mV, ensuring colloidal stability.  

**Cell Culture and Treatment**  
Human breast cancer cell lines (MDA-MB-231, BT474) and non-tumorigenic mammary cells (MCF10A) were cultured in DMEM or DMEM/F12 media supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS) and antibiotics. Cells were treated with polymer-SMNP complexes (0.005–0.5 mg/mL) for 24 hours, followed by exposure to AC magnetic fields (50 Hz, 50–100 kA/m) in either continuous or pulsed (10 min on, 5 min off) regimes.  

**Characterization of Uptake and Cytotoxicity**  
Intracellular uptake was quantified using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) to measure iron content. Cytotoxicity was assessed via MTT assay, and cytoskeletal disruption was visualized using confocal microscopy (actin staining with ActinGreen 488). Lysosomal membrane integrity was evaluated using acridine orange staining.  

### Example 2  

**Selective Uptake in Cancer Cells**  
PAA-P85-SMNPs exhibited significantly higher uptake in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cancer cells compared to non-cancerous MCF10A cells after 24 hours (Fig. 1a). The uptake was concentration-dependent, with maximal accumulation observed at 0.5 mg/mL. Confocal microscopy confirmed lysosomal localization, with colocalization rates exceeding 80% at low concentrations (0.05–0.1 mg/mL).  

### Example 3  

**Cytotoxicity After AC Field Exposure**  
Exposure to a 50 Hz, 50 kA/m AC field induced significant cell death in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells (Fig. 3a–b), while MCF10A viability remained unaffected (Fig. 3c). The pulsed field regime was more effective than continuous exposure, reducing viability to 25% in BT474 cells. No cytotoxicity was observed in the absence of SMNPs or in non-cancerous cells.  

### Example 4  

**Mechanism of Cytoskeletal Disruption**  
Confocal microscopy revealed severe actin filament disorganization in cancer cells post-exposure (Fig. 6a–b), whereas MCF10A cells retained normal cytoskeletal structure. Treatment with cytochalasin D (CD), a cytoskeletal destabilizer, sensitized MCF10A cells to the AC field, confirming the role of mechanical properties in selectivity.  

### Example 5  

**Lack of Lysosomal Membrane Permeabilization**  
Acridine orange staining confirmed that lysosomal membranes remained intact after field exposure (Fig. 4), ruling out lysosomal leakage as a mechanism of cell death. This distinguishes the invention from prior methods relying on lysosomal disruption.  

### Example 6  

**Dependence on Field Parameters**  
The cytotoxic effect was frequency- and amplitude-dependent, with optimal results observed at 50 Hz and 50 kA/m. Higher field strengths (100 kA/m) did not significantly enhance efficacy, suggesting saturation of mechanical force generation.  

### Example 7  

**Role of Polymer Coating**  
Comparative studies with PAA-PEG-SMNPs demonstrated that the PAA-P85-PAA coating was critical for high uptake and lysosomal accumulation. The hydrophobic Pluronic P85 block facilitated membrane interaction and intracellular trafficking.  

### Example 8  

**In Vivo Applicability**  
The low-frequency AC field (50 Hz) enables deep tissue penetration, making the system suitable for treating solid tumors. Preliminary studies in murine models show promising tumor regression without systemic toxicity (data not shown).
