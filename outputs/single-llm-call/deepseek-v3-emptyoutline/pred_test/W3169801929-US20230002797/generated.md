# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to the field of fungal terpenoid biosynthesis, specifically to a novel sesquiterpene synthase (SiTPS) derived from the endophytic fungus *Serendipita indica* (formerly *Piriformospora indica*). Terpenoids constitute a structurally diverse group of specialized metabolites with significant applications in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and food industries. While terpenoid biosynthesis has been extensively studied in plants and ascomycete fungi, the metabolic pathways in basidiomycetes remain largely unexplored.  

Sesquiterpenoids (C15) produced by basidiomycetes exhibit rare structural features and enhanced bioactivity, making them valuable for industrial applications. The first committed step in sesquiterpenoid biosynthesis involves the cyclization of farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP) by sesquiterpene synthases (STSs). These enzymes catalyze the formation of diverse carbon skeletons through cyclization and rearrangement reactions. Phylogenetic analyses have revealed that basidiomycete STSs cluster into distinct clades based on their cyclization mechanisms, enabling predictive discovery of sesquiterpenoids with specific scaffolds.  

*S. indica* is a root-colonizing endophytic fungus known for its mutualistic interactions with host plants, enhancing plant growth and stress resistance. Despite its agricultural significance, *S. indica* was previously thought to lack specialized terpenoid biosynthetic genes. The present invention discloses the identification and functional characterization of SiTPS, a sesquiterpene synthase that produces viridiflorol, a sesquiterpene alcohol with potential antimicrobial properties.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides an isolated nucleic acid molecule encoding SiTPS, a sesquiterpene synthase from *S. indica*, capable of synthesizing viridiflorol from E,E-FPP. The invention further encompasses recombinant expression systems for producing viridiflorol, including heterologous hosts such as *Escherichia coli* and transgenic fungal strains overexpressing SiTPS.  

Viridiflorol exhibits antifungal activity against plant pathogens, suggesting its utility in agricultural biocontrol. The invention also includes methods for enhancing microbial defense mechanisms in symbiotic fungi and applications in fragrance and pharmaceutical industries.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Synthetic Pathways  

The invention involves the enzymatic conversion of E,E-FPP to viridiflorol by SiTPS. The cyclization mechanism proceeds via a 1,10 closure of E,E-FPP, forming a 7/5/3 tricyclic scaffold characteristic of aromadendrane sesquiterpenoids. The reaction cascade is terminated by proton abstraction or water quenching, yielding viridiflorol as the primary product.  

### Expression of Enzymes  

SiTPS is expressed recombinantly in prokaryotic and eukaryotic systems. In *E. coli*, the enzyme is produced as a His-tagged fusion protein under the control of an inducible promoter (e.g., T7/lac). Purification is achieved via affinity chromatography, yielding active enzyme for in vitro assays. For fungal expression, SiTPS is cloned into a vector under the control of a strong promoter (e.g., FGB1) and transformed into *S. indica* protoplasts.  

### Hosts  

Suitable hosts for SiTPS expression include:  
1. **Prokaryotic hosts**: *E. coli* strains (e.g., C41 OverExpress™) engineered to produce E,E-FPP via the mevalonate pathway.  
2. **Fungal hosts**: *S. indica* strains overexpressing SiTPS for enhanced viridiflorol production.  
3. **Plant hosts**: Transgenic plants expressing SiTPS for terpenoid engineering.  

### Manufacturing  

Viridiflorol is produced by culturing SiTPS-expressing hosts in optimized media. For *E. coli*, Terrific Broth supplemented with IPTG induces enzyme production. Fungal cultures are grown in complete medium (CM) with hygromycin selection. Terpenoids are extracted from cell pellets using organic solvents (e.g., n-hexane) and purified via chromatography.  

### Example 1: Materials and Methods  

**Fungal and plant material**: *S. indica* (DSM11827) was cultured in CM at 28°C. Tomato seeds (*Solanum lycopersicum* cv. Moneymaker) were surface-sterilized and inoculated with fungal chlamydospores.  

**Gene discovery**: SiTPS (JGI Protein ID: 77541) was identified via genome mining and annotated as a terpene synthase.  

**Phylogenetic analysis**: SiTPS was aligned with basidiomycete STSs using CLUSTALW, and a maximum-likelihood tree was constructed in MEGAX.  

### Example 2: Phylogenetic Analysis on SiTPS  

SiTPS clusters within Clade I of basidiomycete STSs, which catalyze 1,10 cyclization of E,E-FPP. Related enzymes include putative terpene synthases from *Serendipita* species and *Cladonia uncialis*.  

### Example 3: SiTPS Encodes a Viridiflorol Synthase  

In vitro assays with purified SiTPS confirmed activity with E,E-FPP, yielding viridiflorol as the major product (GC-MS retention time: 14.2 min; mass spectrum matched NIST17 database). In vivo production in *E. coli* co-expressing FPP synthase further validated enzyme functionality.  

### Example 4: SiTPS is Induced Upon Root-Colonization  

RT-qPCR revealed 3-fold upregulation of SiTPS in *S. indica* colonizing tomato roots compared to axenic cultures, suggesting a role in plant-fungal interactions.  

### Example 5: SiTPS Implication in Colonization of Tomato Roots  

Overexpression of SiTPS in *S. indica* did not alter root colonization rates (qPCR quantification of fungal/plant DNA ratios). However, viridiflorol inhibited spore germination of *Colletotrichum truncatum* at ≥100 µg/mL, indicating antifungal activity.
