## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  
The field of this invention relates generally to cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs) and their use in delivering therapeutic payloads to target cells. More specifically, the invention concerns novel CPP compositions comprising nuclear localization signal (NLS) peptides derived from transcription factors combined with glutamate-rich peptide sequences, and their application for targeted delivery of peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) to cancer cells.  

Current approaches for delivering therapeutic oligonucleotides face significant challenges regarding cellular uptake and intracellular trafficking. While cell-penetrating peptides show promise as delivery vehicles, existing CPPs often suffer from inefficient nuclear localization and endosomal sequestration. The art lacks CPP compositions that effectively combine efficient cellular uptake with precise nuclear targeting, particularly for delivering PNA therapeutics to cancer cells.  

Prior attempts to utilize transcription factor NLS peptides as CPPs have demonstrated suboptimal performance, with many peptides showing poor cellular uptake and significant endosomal trapping. Furthermore, existing CPP-PNA conjugates often require impractical concentrations (in the millimolar range) for therapeutic effect, making clinical translation economically unfeasible. There remains an unmet need for CPP compositions that provide efficient cellular uptake at nanomolar concentrations while achieving effective nuclear localization of therapeutic payloads.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention provides novel cell-penetrating peptide compositions and methods for their use in delivering therapeutic agents to target cells. The invention specifically discloses a glutamate-rich peptide-Oct6 NLS conjugate (Glu-Oct6) that demonstrates unexpectedly superior properties for cellular uptake and nuclear localization compared to either component alone.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A cell-penetrating peptide composition comprising a glutamate-rich peptide sequence (EEEAA) linked to the nuclear localization signal (NLS) of the Oct6 transcription factor.  
2. The Glu-Oct6 conjugate demonstrates approximately 100-fold greater cellular uptake compared to the glutamate-rich peptide alone and significantly enhanced nuclear localization compared to the Oct6 NLS alone.  
3. The composition enables efficient delivery of therapeutic payloads (including peptide nucleic acids) to cancer cells at nanomolar concentrations (as low as 30 nM).  
4. The Glu-Oct6 conjugate maintains its cell-penetrating activity under physiological conditions (including in the presence of serum), unlike many existing CPPs.  
5. The invention includes methods for using the Glu-Oct6 conjugate to deliver therapeutic PNAs targeting specific genes (such as STAT3) to cancer cells, resulting in effective gene silencing and induction of apoptosis.  

The Glu-Oct6 composition of the invention represents a significant advance over prior CPP technologies by combining efficient cellular uptake with precise nuclear targeting, enabling effective delivery of therapeutic payloads at clinically feasible concentrations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel cell-penetrating peptide compositions and methods for their use in therapeutic applications. The core innovation involves the unexpected discovery that conjugating a glutamate-rich peptide (EEEAA) to the nuclear localization signal (NLS) of the Oct6 transcription factor creates a CPP with superior properties for cellular uptake and nuclear localization compared to either component alone.  

The glutamate-rich peptide portion (EEEAA) of the invention serves multiple functions. The glutamate residues provide enhanced cellular uptake properties, while the alanine residues (AA) serve as a flexible linker separating the glutamate-rich domain from the NLS domain. This spatial arrangement allows both domains to function optimally without steric interference. The glutamate-rich portion specifically enhances cellular uptake through mechanisms that may involve interaction with cell surface receptors, though the invention is not bound by any particular mechanism of action.  

The Oct6 NLS portion of the conjugate provides nuclear targeting capability. Surprisingly, while the Oct6 NLS alone shows poor cellular uptake and tends to accumulate in endosomal compartments, conjugation with the glutamate-rich peptide dramatically improves both uptake efficiency and nuclear localization. Without being bound by theory, this synergy may result from the glutamate-rich portion facilitating escape from endosomal compartments while the NLS directs nuclear accumulation.  

The invention encompasses the Glu-Oct6 peptide sequence itself, as well as conjugates of this peptide with various therapeutic payloads. A particularly valuable application involves conjugation to peptide nucleic acids (PNAs), which are synthetic DNA analogs with superior binding affinity and nuclease resistance compared to natural oligonucleotides. The Glu-Oct6-PNA conjugates of the invention demonstrate efficient cellular uptake and nuclear localization at concentrations as low as 30 nM, making them practical for therapeutic applications.  

The temperature dependence and NaN3 sensitivity of Glu-Oct6 uptake suggest that the conjugate utilizes both energy-dependent and energy-independent uptake mechanisms. This dual mechanism may contribute to the superior performance of the invention compared to existing CPP technologies that rely on a single uptake pathway.  

Specific embodiments of the invention include:  
1. The Glu-Oct6 peptide having the sequence EEEAAKRSPRTLL (SEQ ID NO:1).  
2. Variants of this sequence maintaining at least 80% sequence identity while preserving the glutamate-rich N-terminal portion and the Oct6 NLS core sequence.  
3. Conjugates of the Glu-Oct6 peptide with therapeutic payloads including but not limited to PNAs, oligonucleotides, small molecules, and proteins.  
4. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the Glu-Oct6 peptide or its conjugates in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers.  
5. Methods for treating cancer by administering Glu-Oct6 conjugates targeting oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes.  

### Examples  

Example 1 demonstrates the superior uptake properties of Glu-Oct6 compared to control peptides. Flow cytometry analysis shows that the Glu-Oct6-FITC conjugate achieves cellular uptake at 30 nM concentration, staining 60-70% of DU-145 prostate cancer cells. This represents a 100-fold improvement over the glutamate-rich peptide alone and a 10-fold improvement over the lysine-substituted variant (Glu-Lys-FITC).  

Example 2 illustrates the sequence specificity of Glu-Oct6 uptake. Substitution of glutamate residues with phenylalanine, tyrosine, or asparagine completely abrogates cellular uptake, while coincubation with these substituted peptides does not inhibit uptake of the original Glu-Oct6-FITC. This confirms that uptake is sequence-specific and not due to nonspecific membrane interactions.  

Example 3 shows the nuclear localization capability of Glu-Oct6. Imaging flow cytometry reveals that approximately 50% of internalized Glu-Oct6-FITC localizes to the nucleus within 1 hour of incubation, with the remainder distributed in the cytoplasm. This represents a dramatic improvement over the Oct6 NLS alone, which shows minimal nuclear localization.  

Example 4 demonstrates therapeutic application of the invention. Conjugation of Glu-Oct6 to an anti-STAT3 PNA (Glu-Oct6-13410a) results in efficient delivery to DU-145 and PANC-1 cancer cells, inducing significant apoptosis (48-69% at 600 nM) compared to control PNA without CPP (≤1% at 1000 nM).  

## Experimental Procedures  

Peptide Synthesis:  
All peptides were synthesized using standard FMoc chemistry on an automated peptide synthesizer. After cleavage and deprotection, peptides were purified by reverse-phase HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry. FITC-labeled peptides were synthesized by incorporating FITC-lysine at the C-terminus.  

Cell Culture:  
DU-145 prostate cancer cells and PANC-1 pancreatic cancer cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum at 37°C in 5% CO2. Cells were passaged at 80% confluence and used for experiments between passages 5-20.  

Uptake Studies:  
Cells were incubated with FITC-labeled peptides at specified concentrations (0-1000 nM) for 1-48 hours at 37°C. After washing, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry using logarithmic amplifiers. Geometric mean fluorescence intensities (MFIs) were calculated from at least 10,000 live cell events per sample.  

Nuclear Localization:  
Cells incubated with FITC-peptides were stained with DRAQ5 nuclear dye and analyzed by imaging flow cytometry. Nuclear and cytoplasmic fluorescence intensities were quantified using image analysis software, with nuclear localization expressed as the percentage of total cellular fluorescence.  

Apoptosis Assays:  
Cells treated with Glu-Oct6-PNA conjugates were stained with FITC-annexin V and propidium iodide after 48 hours. Flow cytometry was used to quantify apoptotic cells (annexin V positive) and necrotic cells (PI positive). Statistical significance was determined by ANOVA with post-hoc testing.  

Temperature and Energy Dependence:  
Uptake studies were performed at 4°C, 25°C, and 37°C to assess temperature dependence. Energy dependence was evaluated by pretreatment with 0.1-1% sodium azide (NaN3) for 30 minutes prior to peptide addition.
