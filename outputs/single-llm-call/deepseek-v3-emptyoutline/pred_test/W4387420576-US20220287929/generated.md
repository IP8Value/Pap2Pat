# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of dermatology and cosmetic science, specifically to compositions and methods for mitigating oxidative stress-induced skin aging. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel lipid particle matrix, referred to herein as SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, which encapsulates antioxidant agents such as vitamins A, C, and E, and demonstrates superior efficacy in reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated skin damage compared to conventional free or encapsulated vitamin formulations. The invention further encompasses methods of preparing the lipid particles, their incorporation into topical formulations, and their use in improving skin hydration, barrier function, and anti-aging effects.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Skin aging is a complex biological process influenced by intrinsic and extrinsic factors. Intrinsic aging results from genetic and biochemical mechanisms, including reduced cellular proliferative capacity, diminished extracellular matrix synthesis, and increased expression of matrix-degrading enzymes. Extrinsic aging is driven by environmental stressors such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation and pollution, which exacerbate oxidative damage. The cumulative effect of these factors manifests as wrinkles, pigmentary changes, loss of elasticity, and impaired barrier function.  

Under physiological conditions, cells maintain homeostasis by balancing ROS production with antioxidant defenses. However, excessive ROS generation overwhelms these defenses, leading to oxidative stress and macromolecular damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA. Antioxidants such as vitamins A, C, and E play critical roles in neutralizing ROS. Vitamin E protects polyunsaturated fatty acids in cell membranes, vitamin C safeguards carbohydrates and other macromolecules, and vitamin A preserves membrane integrity and combats free radicals. Despite their efficacy, the direct application of these vitamins is limited by instability, poor skin penetration, and potential irritation.  

Encapsulation technologies, particularly lipid-based nanoparticles, have emerged as promising solutions to these challenges. Lipid nanoparticles enhance stability, improve bioavailability, and enable controlled release of active ingredients. Their chemical affinity to skin lipids (e.g., ceramides, cholesterol) further promotes penetration and retention. However, existing systems exhibit suboptimal ROS sensitivity and fail to fully exploit the synergistic potential of combined antioxidants.  

Thus, there remains an unmet need for a lipid particle system that optimally encapsulates and delivers antioxidants, exhibits high ROS sensitivity, and provides measurable improvements in skin hydration, barrier function, and anti-aging outcomes.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a lipid particle matrix, SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, comprising a blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax, combined with polyunsaturated fatty acids from safflower oil and liposoluble active ingredients (e.g., retinyl palmitate as vitamin A). The particles are prepared via a solvent-free emulsification method, yielding a stable emulsion with a uniform particle diameter of ≤25 μm. The matrix demonstrates exceptional ROS sensitivity, as evidenced by in vitro assays showing a 44.5% reduction in ROS production at 5 mg/mL compared to controls.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **Enhanced Antioxidant Delivery**: The lipid matrix co-encapsulates hydrophilic (e.g., sodium ascorbyl phosphate as vitamin C) and lipophilic (e.g., retinyl palmitate) actives, ensuring synchronized release and synergistic effects.  
2. **Superior Skin Benefits**: In vivo studies confirm significant improvements in stratum corneum hydration (+20.17% vs. placebo), transepidermal water loss reduction, and wrinkle depth (quantified via Ra and Rz parameters).  
3. **Stability and Safety**: The particles exhibit oxidation stability (iodine index <60 g/100 g) and tolerate accelerated storage conditions (40°C, 3 months) without degradation.  

The invention further provides topical formulations (e.g., gels) incorporating the lipid particles and methods for treating oxidative skin damage by applying such formulations.  

## DESCRIPTION  

### Composition and Preparation of Lipid Particles  
The SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ system is prepared as follows:  
1. **Lipid Phase Preparation**: A blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax is melted at 55°C under magnetic stirring (200 rpm, 10 min). Polyunsaturated fatty acids (from safflower oil) and liposoluble actives (e.g., retinyl palmitate) are added to the molten wax mixture, maintaining homogeneity at 55°C.  
2. **Aqueous Phase Preparation**: Decyl glucoside (surfactant) and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (vitamin C derivative) are dissolved in water at 45–50°C under stirring (500 rpm, 10 min).  
3. **Emulsification**: The lipid phase is added to the aqueous phase at 48–50°C under high-shear mixing (3500 rpm, 10 min), forming an emulsion. The emulsion is sieved through a 25 μm mesh to ensure uniform particle size.  
4. **Solidification**: The emulsion is cooled to 10°C to solidify the lipid particles, which are stored at 4°C until use.  

### In Vitro Efficacy  
The antioxidant capacity of the lipid particles was evaluated using human keratinocytes (HaCaT) exposed to menadione-induced oxidative stress. Key findings:  
- **ROS Inhibition**: At 5 mg/mL, the SIREN formulation reduced ROS production by 55.5% (vs. 97.83% for placebo and 88.51% for encapsulated vitamins).  
- **Dose Dependency**: ROS inhibition was concentration-dependent, with 78.59% reduction at 1.25 mg/mL.  

### In Vivo Performance  
Clinical studies on 30 volunteers (36–58 years) demonstrated:  
1. **Hydration**: Corneometer measurements showed a 20.17% increase in stratum corneum water content after 4 weeks (p < 0.001).  
2. **Barrier Function**: TEWL values decreased significantly, indicating improved barrier integrity.  
3. **Anti-Aging Effects**: 3D topographic analysis revealed reductions in skin roughness (Ra) and wrinkle depth (Rz), with statistical significance (p < 0.01).  

### Formulations and Applications  
The lipid particles are incorporated into gel-based topical formulations (e.g., 1–10% w/w particle loading). Preferred applications include daily-use anti-aging creams, serums, and protective lotions.
