# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to monoclonal antibodies that selectively bind to oligomeric forms of pyroglutamate-modified amyloid-beta peptides (AβpE3), compositions comprising such antibodies, and methods for diagnosing and treating neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's disease (AD). Amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides are known to aggregate into various oligomeric and fibrillar forms, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of AD. Among these, N-terminally truncated and modified Aβ peptides, particularly those with pyroglutamate modification at position 3 (AβpE3), have been identified as highly neurotoxic species that accumulate in the brains of AD patients.  

Current therapeutic strategies targeting Aβ have largely focused on reducing overall Aβ burden or preventing fibril formation. However, these approaches have shown limited clinical efficacy, likely due to their inability to specifically neutralize the most toxic Aβ species. There remains an unmet need for agents that selectively target pathogenic Aβ oligomers, particularly those derived from AβpE3, which are believed to play a critical role in synaptic dysfunction and neuronal loss in AD.  

Existing antibodies against Aβ often recognize generic epitopes present in multiple Aβ isoforms or aggregation states, leading to non-specific binding and potential off-target effects. Moreover, conventional detection methods fail to distinguish between different oligomeric forms of AβpE3, which vary in their pathogenic potential. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing highly specific monoclonal antibodies that selectively recognize low molecular weight (LMW) oligomers of AβpE3, enabling precise targeting of the most toxic Aβ species for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies, or antigen-binding fragments thereof, that specifically bind to oligomeric forms of AβpE3, particularly low-n oligomers (4-10×) of AβpE3-42, without cross-reacting with full-length Aβ1-42 or other Aβ isoforms. In preferred embodiments, the antibody is monoclonal antibody 9D5 (IgG2b), which exhibits high specificity for LMW oligomers of AβpE3-42 as demonstrated by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC), dot blot, and Western blot analyses.  

The antibodies of the invention are characterized by their ability to: (i) selectively detect oligomeric AβpE3 in biological samples, including brain tissue and plasma; (ii) inhibit the aggregation of AβpE3 peptides into higher-order oligomers; and (iii) neutralize the neurotoxic effects of AβpE3 oligomers in vitro and in vivo. Notably, antibody 9D5 shows minimal binding to monomeric or fibrillar forms of AβpE3, distinguishing it from conventional Aβ antibodies.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-AβpE3 oligomer antibodies, optionally in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or excipients. These compositions are useful for passive immunization therapies aimed at reducing AβpE3 oligomer burden in AD patients.  

Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic methods for detecting AβpE3 oligomers in biological samples, such as plasma or cerebrospinal fluid, using the antibodies of the invention. These methods enable early diagnosis of AD and monitoring of disease progression.  

Therapeutic methods are also provided, comprising administering an effective amount of the anti-AβpE3 oligomer antibodies to a subject with or at risk of developing AD. In vivo studies demonstrate that passive immunization with antibody 9D5 reduces plaque burden, decreases intracellular AβpE3 oligomer accumulation, and improves cognitive function in transgenic AD mouse models.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The monoclonal antibodies of the invention are generated using standard hybridoma technology, wherein mice are immunized with AβpE3-38 peptides to elicit an immune response. Hybridomas producing antibodies specific for AβpE3 oligomers are selected through ELISA screening against various N-terminal Aβ epitopes. Preferred antibodies, such as 9D5, are characterized by their binding to AβpE3-38 and AβpE3-42 but not to AβpE1-42, as confirmed by immunoassays.  

Antibody 9D5 exhibits unique binding properties as demonstrated by SEC under native conditions. When analyzed by SEC, AβpE3-42 forms low-n oligomers (4-6×) and smaller aggregates (1-2×), distinct from the aggregation profile of Aβ1-42. Antibody 9D5 specifically detects low-n oligomeric fractions (4-10×) of AβpE3-42 in dot blot assays, with no cross-reactivity to Aβ1-42 fractions. Under denaturing conditions, 9D5 recognizes a single LMW band of AβpE3-42, further confirming its specificity for oligomeric forms.  

The inhibitory effects of antibody 9D5 on AβpE3 aggregation are demonstrated using thioflavin T (ThT) fluorescence assays. While AβpE3-42 rapidly forms intermediate oligomeric assemblies, the presence of 9D5 at a 1:76 (antibody:Aβ) ratio effectively blocks the formation of higher aggregates without affecting the initial oligomerization step. This suggests that 9D5 binds to LMW oligomers of AβpE3, preventing their further aggregation into more toxic species.  

The neuroprotective effects of 9D5 are shown in vitro using SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells. Treatment with AβpE3-42 alone induces significant toxicity, whereas co-incubation with 9D5 completely abolishes this effect. No protective effect is observed when 9D5 is applied with Aβ1-42, confirming the specificity of 9D5 for AβpE3 oligomers.  

In human AD brain tissue, antibody 9D5 exhibits a distinct staining pattern, with strong immunoreactivity in intraneuronal deposits and cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) but minimal staining in non-demented controls. This pattern differs from that of generic Aβ antibodies (e.g., 4G8), supporting the unique specificity of 9D5 for pathogenic AβpE3 oligomers.  

Diagnostically, an ELISA using 9D5 as a capture antibody detects significantly lower levels of AβpE3 oligomers in the plasma of AD patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting its utility as a biomarker for AD.  

Therapeutic efficacy is demonstrated in transgenic AD mouse models (5XFAD and APP/PS1KI). Passive immunization with 9D5 (250 μg weekly for 6 weeks) significantly reduces plaque load, decreases AβpE3 levels in brain lysates, and improves performance in behavioral tests such as the elevated plus maze. Immunohistochemical analysis reveals reduced intraneuronal AβpE3 oligomer accumulation in treated mice, further supporting the therapeutic potential of 9D5.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Generation and Characterization of Antibody 9D5**  
Monoclonal antibody 9D5 was generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with AβpE3-38 peptides. Hybridomas were screened by ELISA, and clones producing antibodies specific for AβpE3-38 and AβpE3-42 (but not AβpE1-42) were selected. Antibody 9D5 was purified using protein G-agarose chromatography and characterized by SEC and Western blot.  

**Example 2: Specificity of 9D5 for AβpE3 Oligomers**  
SEC analysis of AβpE3-42 revealed dominant peaks corresponding to low-n oligomers (4-6×). Dot blot assays using 9D5 detected only these oligomeric fractions, with no signal for Aβ1-42. Under denaturing conditions, 9D5 recognized a single LMW band of AβpE3-42 by Western blot.  

**Example 3: Inhibition of AβpE3 Aggregation**  
ThT assays showed that AβpE3-42 rapidly forms oligomers, but 9D5 (1:76 ratio) blocked further aggregation into higher-order species. No effect was observed on Aβ1-42 aggregation.  

**Example 4: Neuroprotective Effects in Vitro**  
SH-SY5Y cells treated with AβpE3-42 exhibited reduced viability, but co-incubation with 9D5 abolished this toxicity. No protection was seen with Aβ1-42.  

**Example 5: Diagnostic ELISA for AβpE3 Oligomers**  
Plasma from AD patients showed 46% lower levels of AβpE3 oligomers compared to healthy controls when measured using a 9D5-based ELISA (p < 0.05).  

**Example 6: Therapeutic Efficacy in Transgenic Mice**  
5XFAD mice passively immunized with 9D5 (250 μg/week for 6 weeks) showed reduced plaque load, decreased AβpE3 levels in brain lysates, and improved performance in the elevated plus maze test.  

The foregoing demonstrates the utility of the invention for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications in AD.
