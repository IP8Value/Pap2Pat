**PATENT APPLICATION**  

**Title:**  
**Methods and Compositions for Extending Lifespan Through Mitochondrial Uncoupling via Circadian Regulation of UCP4C**  

### **FIELD OF THE INVENTION**  
The present invention relates to the fields of molecular biology, aging, and metabolic regulation. Specifically, it pertains to methods and compositions for extending lifespan and improving healthspan by modulating mitochondrial uncoupling through the circadian-regulated expression of uncoupling protein UCP4C. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic interventions targeting mitochondrial uncoupling in intestinal tissues to mitigate age-related decline in gut homeostasis and cellular proliferation.  

### **BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION**  
Circadian rhythms are endogenous biological oscillations that regulate physiological processes, including metabolism, in alignment with environmental light-dark cycles. These rhythms are governed by transcriptional feedback loops involving activators (e.g., Clock, Cycle) and repressors (e.g., Period, Timeless). Disruption of circadian regulation has been linked to metabolic dysfunction and accelerated aging, though the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood.  

Prior studies have demonstrated that loss of circadian transcriptional activators (Clock, Cycle) shortens lifespan, while the effects of disrupting repressors (Period, Timeless) have been inconsistent. Some reports suggest that loss of Period (Per) accelerates aging, whereas others indicate metabolic benefits, including increased metabolic rate and reduced fat storage. The role of mitochondrial uncoupling—a process that dissipates the proton gradient to generate heat instead of ATP—in circadian-regulated longevity has not been elucidated.  

Mitochondrial uncoupling proteins (UCPs) regulate energy metabolism by uncoupling oxidative phosphorylation from ATP synthesis, thereby increasing respiration and reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) production. While UCP1 is well-studied in mammals, the function of Drosophila UCP4 homologs (UCP4A, UCP4B, UCP4C) remains underexplored. Given the association between mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and aging, there is a need for novel interventions that modulate mitochondrial uncoupling to promote longevity.  

### **SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION**  
The present invention discloses that disruption of the circadian repressor Period (Per) extends lifespan in Drosophila through increased mitochondrial uncoupling mediated by upregulation of UCP4C. Key findings include:  
1. **Lifespan Extension via Loss of Per**: Male flies lacking Per exhibit a 15-20% increase in lifespan, independent of dietary restriction or canonical longevity pathways (e.g., insulin/TOR signaling).  
2. **Mitochondrial Uncoupling Mechanism**: Per mutants display elevated metabolic rates, reduced mitochondrial membrane potential, and increased leak respiration, indicative of mitochondrial uncoupling.  
3. **UCP4C as a Critical Mediator**: Genetic ablation of UCP4C reverts the longevity and metabolic phenotypes of Per mutants, while overexpression of UCP4C in wild-type flies recapitulates lifespan extension.  
4. **Intestinal-Specific Effects**: Lifespan extension is driven by loss of Per and upregulation of UCP4C specifically in intestinal stem cells (ISCs) and enteroblasts (EBs), leading to reduced ROS levels and delayed age-related gut dysfunction.  
5. **Pharmacological Recapitulation**: Low-dose mitochondrial uncoupling agents (e.g., 2,4-DNP) extend lifespan and improve gut homeostasis, mimicking genetic UCP4C activation.  

The invention provides methods for extending lifespan by targeting mitochondrial uncoupling through genetic, pharmacological, or dietary interventions. Compositions include UCP4C activators, Per inhibitors, and mitochondrial uncoupling compounds for therapeutic use.  

### **DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION**  

#### **Definitions**  
- **Mitochondrial Uncoupling**: Dissipation of the proton gradient across the mitochondrial inner membrane, reducing ATP synthesis and increasing heat production.  
- **UCP4C**: A Drosophila mitochondrial uncoupling protein homolog whose expression is circadian-regulated and necessary for Per mutant longevity.  
- **Circadian Repressor Arm**: Transcriptional repressors (e.g., Period, Timeless) that inhibit Clock/Cycle-mediated gene expression in the circadian feedback loop.  
- **Intestinal Homeostasis**: Maintenance of gut barrier function, stem cell proliferation, and ROS balance during aging.  

#### **Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects**  
The invention demonstrates that mitochondrial uncoupling confers metabolic and longevity benefits, including:  
- **Increased Metabolic Rate**: Per mutants exhibit hyperphagia yet reduced lipid storage, consistent with elevated energy expenditure.  
- **Reduced ROS Production**: Uncoupling attenuates mitochondrial ROS, mitigating oxidative damage and age-related stem cell overproliferation.  
- **Improved Gut Integrity**: Intestinal-specific UCP4C activation preserves barrier function and delays dysplasia, key predictors of lifespan.  

#### **Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling**  
The invention encompasses:  
1. **Genetic Interventions**:  
   - Knockout or knockdown of Per in intestinal tissues.  
   - Overexpression of UCP4C, particularly in ISCs/EBs, using tissue-specific promoters (e.g., *esg-GAL4*).  
2. **Pharmacological Agents**:  
   - Low-dose mitochondrial uncouplers (e.g., 2,4-DNP, BHT).  
   - UCP4C activators (e.g., fatty acids, palmitate).  
3. **Dietary Regimens**:  
   - Caloric restriction protocols timed to circadian UCP4C expression peaks.  

#### **Pharmaceutical Compositions and Methods of Administration**  
Therapeutic formulations may include:  
- **Oral Compositions**: Encapsulated uncoupling agents (e.g., 2,4-DNP) for sustained intestinal delivery.  
- **Gene Therapy**: Viral vectors (e.g., AAV) encoding UCP4C under intestine-specific promoters.  
- **Dosing Protocols**: Intermittent administration to mimic circadian UCP4C oscillations.  

### **EXAMPLES**  

#### **Example 1**  
**Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan Independent of Dietary Protein Restriction and Reduced Insulin-like Signaling**  
- **Methods**: Lifespan analysis of *per01* and *tim01* mutants on varying yeast extract diets.  
- **Results**: Lifespan extension persisted across diets except at high protein (10% yeast), confirming independence from dietary restriction. Genetic inhibition of insulin/TOR signaling did not further extend *per01* longevity.  

#### **Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling**  
- **Methods**: Respirometry, mitochondrial membrane potential assays, and cold shock recovery tests.  
- **Results**: *per01* mutants showed elevated CO₂ production, reduced JC-1 staining (indicative of uncoupling), and faster recovery from cold stress.  

#### **Endogenous Uncoupling Protein UCP4C was Necessary for Period Mutant Lifespan Extension and Sufficient to Extend Wild-Type Lifespan**  
- **Methods**: CRISPR-mediated *UCP4C* knockdown and overexpression.  
- **Results**: *UCP4C* ablation reverted *per01* longevity, while overexpression in wild-type flies extended lifespan.  

#### **Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Loss of Per Specifically in the Intestine**  
- **Methods**: Tissue-specific *per* rescue and knockout using *esg-GAL4* drivers.  
- **Results**: Intestinal *per* knockout alone was sufficient for lifespan extension, whereas neuronal rescue had no effect.  

### **CONCLUSIONS**  
The invention establishes mitochondrial uncoupling via UCP4C as a novel pathway for lifespan extension, with therapeutic potential for age-related metabolic and intestinal disorders. Claims cover genetic, pharmacological, and dietary interventions targeting this mechanism.
