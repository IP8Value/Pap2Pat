# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods for pretreating lignocellulosic biomass to enhance enzymatic digestibility. More specifically, the invention pertains to an alkaline twin-screw extrusion (ATSE) pretreatment process that effectively disrupts the lignin-carbohydrate complex in biomass materials such as corn stover, rice straw, wheat straw, bagasse, and sawdust. The invention further relates to the recovery of fermentable sugars from the pretreated biomass for subsequent conversion into biofuels or biochemicals.  

## BACKGROUND  

The depletion of petroleum resources has intensified research into alternative renewable feedstocks for fuel and chemical production. Lignocellulosic biomass, including agricultural and forestry waste, represents a promising sustainable resource due to its abundance and low cost. However, the enzymatic conversion of carbohydrates in lignocellulosic biomass into fermentable sugars is hindered by the recalcitrance of the biomass structure, primarily due to the presence of lignin and hemicellulose.  

Various pretreatment methods have been explored to overcome this recalcitrance, including acid hydrolysis, alkali hydrolysis, steam explosion, ammonia fiber explosion (AFEX), and organosolv processes. However, these methods suffer from drawbacks such as high energy consumption, long processing times, chemical recovery challenges, and the formation of inhibitory byproducts. Extrusion pretreatment has emerged as a promising alternative due to its ability to combine mechanical and chemical disruption of biomass. Nevertheless, existing extrusion methods often require high temperatures, extensive size reduction of feedstock, or excessive chemical usage, which increase operational costs and reduce efficiency.  

There remains a need for an improved pretreatment method that operates at lower temperatures, minimizes chemical usage, and maximizes sugar recovery while maintaining scalability for industrial applications.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides an alkaline twin-screw extrusion (ATSE) pretreatment method for lignocellulosic biomass that overcomes the limitations of existing techniques. The method involves subjecting biomass to mechanical disruption and chemical treatment in a co-rotating twin-screw extruder, wherein the biomass is sequentially compressed and sheared by transport screw elements (TSE) and reversed screw elements (RSE). The process is conducted at a relatively low temperature (approximately 99°C) without requiring external heating, as the mechanical forces generate sufficient heat for the reaction.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The use of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) at a loading of 0.04 to 0.1 g/g biomass, with an optimal loading of 0.06 g/g biomass.  
2. A heat preservation step following extrusion, wherein the pretreated biomass is held at ambient temperature for 0 to 10 hours, preferably 1 hour, to enhance delignification.  
3. A high biomass-to-liquid ratio (1:2 w/w), reducing water consumption and improving waste stream management.  
4. The recovery of lignin from the spent liquor for value-added applications, such as concrete additives or phenol-formaldehyde resins.  

The ATSE pretreatment achieves a delignification of up to 83% and a total sugar yield of 80.2%, significantly improving enzymatic digestibility compared to untreated biomass. The method is scalable, energy-efficient, and compatible with existing pulp mill infrastructure for chemical recovery.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The ATSE pretreatment process involves several critical steps, each contributing to the disruption of lignocellulosic biomass and the enhancement of enzymatic hydrolysis.  

### Extruder Design and Operation  
The pretreatment is performed using a co-rotating twin-screw extruder comprising alternating transport screw elements (TSE) and reversed screw elements (RSE). The TSE convey the biomass forward, while the RSE generate counteracting forces that compress and shear the biomass, leading to fibrillation and size reduction. The mechanical action generates heat, raising the temperature to approximately 99°C, eliminating the need for external heating.  

### Alkali Treatment  
Sodium hydroxide solution is introduced into the extruder at a loading of 0.04 to 0.1 g/g biomass. The alkali facilitates lignin dissolution and hemicellulose degradation while preserving cellulose integrity. At the optimal NaOH loading of 0.06 g/g biomass, the process achieves a balance between delignification (71%) and carbohydrate retention (96% glucan recovery). Higher NaOH loadings (e.g., 0.1 g/g biomass) increase lignin removal but may degrade xylan, reducing overall sugar yield.  

### Heat Preservation  
After extrusion, the biomass is held at ambient temperature for a specified duration (0 to 10 hours) to allow further chemical reactions. A 1-hour preservation period is sufficient to maximize delignification and enzymatic digestibility, while longer durations offer diminishing returns.  

### Enzymatic Hydrolysis  
The pretreated biomass is enzymatically hydrolyzed using cellulase (e.g., Celluclast 1.5 L) and β-glucosidase (e.g., Novozyme 188) to release glucose and xylose. The ATSE pretreatment increases glucan conversion from 17.5% (untreated) to 83% and xylan conversion from 5.1% to 89%.  

### Lignin Recovery  
The spent liquor from the pretreatment is processed to recover lignin, which can be utilized in high-value applications. The recovered lignin exhibits a weight-average molecular weight (Mw) of 22,780 and a polydispersity index (PDI) of 1.386, making it suitable for further modification.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Corn stover was pretreated using the ATSE method with an NaOH loading of 0.06 g/g biomass and a heat preservation time of 1 hour. The pretreated biomass exhibited a glucan recovery of 96%, a xylan recovery of 78.3%, and a lignin reduction of 71%. Enzymatic hydrolysis yielded 83% glucan conversion and 89% xylan conversion, with a total sugar yield of 77.6%.  

### Example 2  

Corn stover was pretreated with an NaOH loading of 0.1 g/g biomass and a heat preservation time of 10 hours. While delignification reached 83%, xylan recovery dropped to 60.7% due to excessive degradation. The total sugar yield was 71.3%, lower than that achieved with 0.06 g/g NaOH.  

### Example 3  

Corn stover was pretreated with an NaOH loading of 0.04 g/g biomass and no heat preservation. The pretreatment resulted in poor delignification (14%) and low enzymatic digestibility (42% glucan conversion, 29% xylan conversion), demonstrating the importance of sufficient alkali loading and heat preservation.
