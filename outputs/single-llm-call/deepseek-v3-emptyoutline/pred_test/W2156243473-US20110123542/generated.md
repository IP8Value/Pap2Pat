# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular biology, specifically to the role of chemokine receptor CXCR4 and chemokine ligand CCL20 in cancer progression. More particularly, the invention discloses methods for inhibiting tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis by targeting the CXCR4/CCL20/CCR6 signaling axis, including the use of neutralizing antibodies against CCL20 and compositions for treating cancers characterized by overexpression of CXCR4 and CCL20.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Chemokines are a family of small cytokines primarily known for their role in leukocyte chemotaxis during inflammation. However, emerging evidence indicates that chemokines and their receptors play critical roles in cancer progression, including tumor growth, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Among chemokine receptors, CXCR4 is particularly significant as it is overexpressed in numerous cancer types, including prostate, breast, pancreatic, and hematological malignancies.  

The chemokine CCL20 and its receptor CCR6 have been implicated in inflammatory responses and immune cell recruitment. While some studies suggest CCL20 may have anti-tumor effects by attracting dendritic cells, other evidence indicates it promotes tumor progression through autocrine signaling in cancer cells expressing CCR6. The relationship between CXCR4 and CCL20 in cancer biology has not been previously elucidated.  

Current cancer therapies often fail to address the complex signaling networks that drive tumor progression and metastasis. There remains an unmet need for targeted therapies that disrupt critical chemokine-mediated pathways in cancer while minimizing systemic toxicity. The present invention addresses this need by identifying the CXCR4/CCL20/CCR6 axis as a novel therapeutic target in cancer.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating cancers characterized by overexpression of CXCR4 and CCL20. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. The discovery that CXCR4 upregulates CCL20 expression in cancer cells, establishing a novel signaling axis between these molecules.  

2. Demonstration that CCL20 promotes cancer cell proliferation, adhesion, and invasion through autocrine signaling via CCR6.  

3. Development of therapeutic strategies targeting this pathway, including:  
   - Neutralizing antibodies against CCL20  
   - Inhibitors of CXCR4-mediated CCL20 upregulation  
   - Combination therapies targeting both CXCR4 and CCL20/CCR6 signaling  

4. Diagnostic methods for identifying cancers likely to respond to anti-CCL20 therapies based on CXCR4 and CCR6 expression profiles.  

The invention is supported by experimental data showing that:  
- CXCR4 overexpression increases CCL20 mRNA and protein levels in prostate cancer cells  
- CCL20 stimulates proliferation and adhesion of CCR6-expressing cancer cells  
- CCL20 overexpression enhances tumor growth, vascularization, and invasion in vivo  
- Neutralizing anti-CCL20 antibodies inhibit tumor growth in multiple cancer models  
- CXCR4 and CCL20 are co-expressed in human prostate cancer specimens  

These findings establish the CXCR4/CCL20/CCR6 axis as a novel therapeutic target in cancer and provide specific compositions and methods for targeting this pathway.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that chemokine receptor CXCR4 upregulates expression of chemokine CCL20 in cancer cells, and that this CCL20 promotes tumor progression through autocrine signaling via its receptor CCR6. This previously unrecognized signaling axis represents a novel target for cancer therapy.  

**CXCR4-Mediated Upregulation of CCL20**  
Experimental data demonstrate that CXCR4 activation increases both mRNA and protein levels of CCL20 in prostate cancer cells. In PC3 prostate cancer cells engineered to overexpress CXCR4 (PC3-CXCR4.5), CCL20 mRNA levels were 41-fold higher than in wild-type PC3 cells. CXCL12, the ligand for CXCR4, stimulated CCL20 production in a dose-dependent manner in both wild-type and CXCR4-overexpressing cells.  

The mechanism of CXCR4-mediated CCL20 upregulation involves JAK/STAT signaling, as treatment with the JAK2 inhibitor AG-490 completely blocked CCL20 secretion. This suggests that CXCR4 activates CCL20 expression through a G-protein independent pathway involving JAK/STAT signaling.  

**CCL20 Promotes Cancer Cell Proliferation and Adhesion**  
CCL20 stimulated proliferation of CCR6-expressing PC3 prostate cancer cells, with maximal effect at 50 ng/ml. Higher concentrations (250 ng/ml) did not further increase proliferation, likely due to receptor desensitization. CCL20 also enhanced adhesion of PC3 cells to extracellular matrix components, particularly collagen I, in a dose-dependent manner.  

**Autocrine Effects of CCL20**  
PC3 cells stably transfected to overexpress CCL20 showed increased proliferation and adhesion compared to control cells, demonstrating autocrine effects of CCL20. Interestingly, clones producing moderate CCL20 levels (100-320 pg/ml) showed greater proliferative effects than clones producing higher levels (1000-5000 pg/ml), consistent with receptor desensitization at high ligand concentrations.  

**In Vivo Tumor Promotion by CCL20**  
In mouse xenograft models, tumors formed by CCL20-overexpressing PC3 cells were larger, more vascularized, and more invasive than control tumors. Functional MRI showed increased vascular perfusion in CCL20-expressing tumors. Histological analysis revealed invasion into adjacent tissues and extensive aberrant angiogenesis.  

**Therapeutic Inhibition of CCL20**  
Neutralizing antibodies against CCL20 significantly inhibited tumor growth in multiple models:  
- Reduced growth of CCL20-overexpressing PC3 xenografts  
- Delayed appearance and reduced growth of CXCR4-overexpressing PC3 tumors  
- Inhibited growth and invasion of HT-29 colon cancer xenografts  

These effects were associated with reduced tumor vascularization and increased necrosis.  

**Co-Expression in Human Cancers**  
Immunohistochemical analysis of human prostate cancer specimens revealed:  
- 96% expressed CCR6  
- 65% expressed CCL20  
- High CCL20 expression correlated with higher Gleason scores  
- CXCR4 and CCL20 showed coordinated expression patterns in 79.5% of samples  

These findings support the clinical relevance of the CXCR4/CCL20/CCR6 axis in human cancer.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Experimental Procedures  

**Cell Culture and Transduction**  
Human cancer cell lines (PC3 prostate cancer, HT-29 colon cancer, NB4 and HL60 leukemia) were cultured under standard conditions. Stable transfectants overexpressing CXCR4 or CCL20 were generated using pcDNA3 vectors and selected with appropriate antibiotics. Single-cell clones were isolated by limiting dilution.  

**RNA Interference**  
CXCR4 was silenced in PC3-CXCR4.5 cells using Dicer-substrate siRNA targeting CXCR4 mRNA. Control cells received non-specific siRNA. Transfection efficiency was monitored by flow cytometry and real-time PCR.  

**ELISA Assays**  
CCL20 protein levels in cell supernatants and lysates were quantified using commercial ELISA kits according to manufacturer protocols.  

**Cell Proliferation Assays**  
Cells were treated with varying CCL20 concentrations (5-500 ng/ml) for 6 days. Viability was assessed by propidium iodide staining and FACS analysis. Proliferation was also measured by BrdU incorporation.  

**Cell Adhesion Assays**  
Cancer cells were allowed to adhere to fibronectin- or collagen I-coated plates in the presence or absence of CCL20. After washing, adherent cells were quantified by FACS.  

**Xenograft Models**  
Cancer cells (5×10^6) were injected subcutaneously into immunodeficient mice. Tumor size was measured regularly. For therapeutic experiments, mice received anti-CCL20 antibodies (20 μg, 3× weekly) starting 24 hours post-injection.  

**Immunohistochemistry**  
Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded human prostate cancer specimens were stained with antibodies against CXCR4, CCL20, and CCR6. Staining intensity was scored by two independent observers.  

### Example 1  

**CXCR4 Upregulates CCL20 in Prostate Cancer Cells**  
PC3 prostate cancer cells and CXCR4-overexpressing PC3-CXCR4.5 cells were stimulated with CXCL12 (0-1000 ng/ml) for 48 hours. CCL20 mRNA levels increased up to 41-fold in CXCR4-overexpressing cells compared to wild-type. ELISA showed corresponding increases in CCL20 protein secretion, from 8.6±4.5 pg/ml in wild-type to 433±30 pg/ml in CXCR4-overexpressing cells.  

Time-course experiments revealed that CXCL12 stimulation increased intracellular CCL20 within 6 hours, with secretion detectable by 24 hours. By 48 hours, both intracellular and secreted CCL20 levels were significantly elevated.  

### Example 2  

**CCL20 Promotes Prostate Cancer Cell Proliferation and Adhesion**  
PC3 cells were treated with CCL20 (5-250 ng/ml) for 6 days. At 50 ng/ml, CCL20 increased viable cell numbers by 1.75-fold (p<0.0004) and BrdU incorporation by 1.76-fold (p<0.0002).  

In adhesion assays, CCL20 (500 ng/ml) increased PC3 cell adhesion to collagen I by 2.2-fold (p<0.001). This effect was blocked by pertussis toxin, indicating G-protein coupled receptor involvement.  

### Example 3  

**Anti-CCL20 Antibodies Inhibit Tumor Growth In Vivo**  
SCID/bg mice bearing PC3-CCL20 xenografts were treated with anti-CCL20 antibodies (20 μg, 3× weekly). Antibody treatment:  
- Reduced tumor incidence from 9/10 to 5/10  
- Inhibited growth of established tumors  
- Increased tumor necrosis  
- Reduced aberrant vascularization  

Similar effects were observed in CXCR4-overexpressing PC3 tumors and HT-29 colon cancer xenografts.  

### Example 4  

**CXCR4 and CCL20 are Co-Expressed in Human Prostate Cancer**  
Analysis of 52 human prostate cancer specimens revealed:  
- 96% expressed CCR6  
- 65% expressed CCL20  
- High CCL20 expression correlated with higher Gleason scores (p=0.02)  
- 79.5% showed coordinated CXCR4 and CCL20 expression patterns  

These findings confirm the clinical relevance of the CXCR4/CCL20/CCR6 axis in human cancer.
