## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of vector-borne disease control. More specifically, the invention provides novel compositions and methods utilizing Chromobacterium sp. Panama (Csp_P) for controlling mosquito populations and blocking transmission of malaria and dengue viruses. The invention encompasses biological control agents, transmission-blocking formulations, and therapeutic compounds derived from Csp_P metabolites.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Mosquito-borne diseases including malaria and dengue fever continue to pose significant global health burdens, with hundreds of millions of cases reported annually. Current control strategies relying on chemical insecticides face challenges including insecticide resistance and environmental concerns. There remains an urgent need for novel control approaches that can complement or replace existing methods.  

The mosquito gut microbiota has emerged as an important factor influencing vector competence. Previous studies have shown that certain gut bacteria can reduce pathogen infection through immune stimulation or production of anti-pathogen compounds. However, most identified bacterial strains show limited efficacy or specificity. Chromobacterium species are known to produce diverse bioactive metabolites, but their potential for vector control applications remains largely unexplored.  

Existing biological control agents suffer from several limitations including poor gut colonization, narrow target specificity, and environmental instability. There remains a need for microbial agents that can effectively colonize mosquito vectors, demonstrate broad-spectrum activity against multiple pathogens and vector species, and maintain efficacy under field conditions. The present invention addresses these needs through the discovery and characterization of a novel Chromobacterium isolate with unique properties.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides isolated Chromobacterium sp. Panama (Csp_P) strains and compositions comprising the same for controlling mosquito populations and blocking transmission of vector-borne pathogens. Csp_P demonstrates several unique and advantageous properties including:  

1) Rapid and persistent colonization of mosquito midguts in both Anopheles and Aedes species;  
2) Broad-spectrum mosquitocidal activity against larval and adult stages;  
3) Potent inhibition of Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus infection in mosquitoes;  
4) Production of stable anti-pathogen metabolites active against both malaria parasites and dengue viruses;  
5) Growth inhibition of competing mosquito gut bacteria.  

The invention encompasses live Csp_P formulations for biological control, purified anti-pathogen metabolites for therapeutic applications, and methods for using these compositions to reduce mosquito populations and disease transmission. The Csp_P biofilm-derived compounds maintain activity after desiccation and resist degradation, making them particularly suitable for field applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### I. Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the following meanings:  

"Biofilm" refers to a structured community of bacterial cells enclosed in a self-produced polymeric matrix adherent to an inert or living surface.  

"Colony forming unit (CFU)" refers to a measure of viable bacterial cells capable of forming colonies under specified conditions.  

"Entomopathogenic" refers to the ability to cause disease or death in insects.  

"Midgut colonization" refers to the establishment and persistence of bacteria in the digestive tract of mosquitoes.  

"Transmission-blocking" refers to the ability to prevent acquisition, development or transmission of pathogens by disease vectors.  

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)  

The present invention provides isolated strains of Chromobacterium sp. Panama (Csp_P), a Gram-negative bacterium originally isolated from field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. Csp_P can be cultured in standard media including Luria Bertani (LB) broth and agar, forming opaque tan colonies that darken with age.  

Molecular characterization of the 16S rRNA gene sequence shows 98% similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum. Unlike Chromobacterium violaceum, Csp_P does not produce violacein pigment. Csp_P demonstrates exceptional ampicillin resistance, growing at concentrations up to 10,000 μg/ml.  

Csp_P possesses unique metabolic characteristics including the ability to form robust biofilms and produce stable secondary metabolites with anti-pathogen activity. The biofilm matrix contains heat-sensitive compounds that maintain activity after desiccation and rehydration.  

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects  

The invention provides methods for administering Csp_P compositions to target mosquito populations. Administration routes include:  

1) Larval exposure through breeding water treatment with Csp_P cultures at 10^8 CFU/ml concentration;  
2) Adult mosquito exposure through sugar baits containing 10^6-10^10 CFU/ml Csp_P;  
3) Blood meal supplementation with 10^6-10^8 CFU/ml Csp_P;  
4) Environmental application of Csp_P biofilm preparations to mosquito resting sites.  

Optimal dosing varies by mosquito species, with Anopheles gambiae typically requiring higher concentrations than Aedes aegypti for equivalent effects. The compositions may be administered as liquid cultures, lyophilized powders, or stabilized biofilm preparations.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes  

This example demonstrates Csp_P's transmission-blocking activity against Plasmodium falciparum in Anopheles gambiae and dengue virus in Aedes aegypti.  

Mosquitoes were rendered aseptic by antibiotic treatment and then exposed to Csp_P through sugar feeding (10^8 CFU/ml for An. gambiae, 10^6 CFU/ml for Ae. aegypti). After 48 hours, mosquitoes were challenged with infectious blood meals containing either P. falciparum gametocytes or dengue virus serotype 2.  

Analysis at 7 days post-infection showed significant reductions in pathogen infection. An. gambiae exhibited 80% lower P. falciparum oocyst counts compared to controls (p<0.001). Ae. aegypti showed 75% reduction in dengue virus titers (p<0.001). Direct addition of Csp_P to infectious blood meals at 10^6 CFU/ml enhanced the anti-Plasmodium effect to 90% inhibition.  

### CONCLUSION  

The results demonstrate that Csp_P colonization of mosquito midguts significantly reduces susceptibility to both malaria parasites and dengue viruses. The transmission-blocking effect occurs through multiple mechanisms including immune stimulation and direct anti-pathogen activity.  

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent  

This example characterizes Csp_P's entomopathogenic and transmission-blocking properties across mosquito life stages.  

Larval assays: Second instar An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti larvae were exposed to Csp_P cultures (10^8 CFU/ml) in breeding water. Complete larval mortality occurred within 3 days for An. gambiae and 2 days for Ae. aegypti.  

Adult assays: Non-antibiotic treated adult mosquitoes were exposed to Csp_P through sugar feeding. An. gambiae showed 95% mortality within 2 days at 10^8 CFU/ml. Ae. aegypti required higher doses (10^10 CFU/ml) to achieve 70% mortality within 3 days.  

In vitro pathogen inhibition: Csp_P biofilm preparations demonstrated direct anti-Plasmodium activity, inhibiting asexual stages by 90% and ookinetes by 95%. Dengue virus infectivity was reduced by 99% after exposure to Csp_P biofilm supernatants.  

These results establish Csp_P as a multi-functional control agent with mosquitocidal and transmission-blocking activities against major disease vectors.
