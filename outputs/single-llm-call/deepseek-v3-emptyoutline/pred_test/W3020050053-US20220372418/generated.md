# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates to the field of microfluidic cell culture technology, specifically to organs-on-chips devices. More particularly, the invention pertains to a pumpless microfluidic organ-on-chip system that utilizes hydrophilic threads to drive fluid flow within microfluidic channels, eliminating the need for external pumps or tubing.  

### Description of Related Art  

Microfluidic organs-on-chips have emerged as powerful tools for in vitro modeling of human tissues, offering improved physiological relevance compared to traditional static cell cultures. These devices typically rely on external pumps connected via tubing to perfuse cell culture medium through microfluidic channels. However, this configuration presents several drawbacks, including the risk of bubble formation, leakage, biosafety concerns when handling hazardous agents, and high capital costs associated with precision pumps.  

Alternative methods to eliminate external pumps have been explored, such as hydrostatic pressure systems, gravity-induced bidirectional flow (rocking platforms), and gravity-driven siphon flow using intravenous infusion sets. While these approaches offer certain advantages, they suffer from limited flexibility in modulating flow conditions, inhomogeneous shear stress distribution, and high implementation costs.  

There remains a need for a simple, robust, and cost-effective perfusion system that enables precise control of flow conditions in organs-on-chips without requiring external pumps or complex setups.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a pumpless organ-on-chip device that utilizes hydrophilic threads to drive continuous flow of cell culture medium through microfluidic channels. The system comprises:  

1. A microfluidic device having at least one inlet reservoir and at least one outlet;  
2. A hydrophilic thread positioned within the outlet, configured to wick fluid from the microfluidic channel;  
3. Wherein evaporation of fluid from the thread provides a driving force for continuous flow through the microfluidic channel.  

The flow rate can be precisely controlled by adjusting the length and/or diameter of the hydrophilic thread. The system operates autonomously within standard cell culture incubators (typically at 37°C, 5% CO₂, and 90% humidity) without requiring external pumps, power sources, or tubing.  

The invention further provides methods for culturing cells in the pumpless organ-on-chip device, including intestinal epithelial cells (e.g., Caco-2) and lung epithelial cells (e.g., A549), under controlled shear stress conditions. The device enables long-term culture (up to 5 days or more) and facilitates studies of cellular responses to fluid shear stress, including epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The pumpless organ-on-chip device of the present invention comprises several key components and features that enable its unique functionality:  

1. **Microfluidic Device Structure**: The device is typically fabricated from polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) bonded to a glass substrate, forming microfluidic channels with dimensions suitable for cell culture (e.g., 1 mm width × 150 µm height). The device includes at least one inlet reservoir for holding cell culture medium and at least one outlet for fluid removal.  

2. **Hydrophilic Thread**: A superhydrophilic thread (e.g., Spunlace thread composed of 70% rayon and 30% polyester) is inserted into the outlet. The thread acts as a capillary channel, wicking fluid from the microfluidic channel through capillary action. The constant evaporation of fluid from the thread surface provides the driving force for continuous flow through the system.  

3. **Flow Control Mechanism**: The flow rate is determined by the balance between wicking rate (dependent on thread diameter) and evaporation rate (dependent on thread length and environmental conditions). By selecting appropriate thread dimensions, flow rates can be precisely controlled within the range of 0.15 µL/min to 6 µL/min.  

4. **Cell Culture Compatibility**: The device maintains sterility by eliminating external tubing connections. The entire system can be housed within a standard Petri dish during incubation. The device supports culture of various cell types, including but not limited to intestinal epithelial cells (Caco-2) and lung epithelial cells (A549), under physiologically relevant shear stress conditions.  

5. **Shear Stress Modulation**: The device enables controlled application of fluid shear stress to cultured cells, ranging from 0.002 dyn/cm² to 0.135 dyn/cm². This allows investigation of shear-dependent cellular responses, such as differentiation of intestinal epithelial cells or EMT in lung epithelial cells.  

6. **Multiplex Capability**: The invention includes embodiments with multiple parallel channels, each with independently controlled flow rates through different thread configurations. This enables simultaneous testing of multiple shear stress conditions within a single device.  

### Examples  

**Example 1: Thread Characterization and Flow Rate Control**  

Superhydrophilic Spunlace threads (70% rayon, 30% polyester) were characterized for their capillary properties. Threads with diameters of 0.5 mm, 1 mm, 1.5 mm, and 2 mm were tested for wicking rates by measuring dye solution ascent over time. The flow rates induced by these threads in microfluidic channels were quantified by tracking fluorescent nanoparticle movement. Flow rates ranged from 0.15 µL/min (0.5 mm diameter, 1 mm length) to 6 µL/min (2 mm diameter, 28 mm length), demonstrating precise tunability through thread dimension selection.  

**Example 2: Pumpless Culture of Caco-2 Intestinal Epithelial Cells**  

Caco-2 cells were cultured in the pumpless device under a shear stress of 0.02 dyn/cm² (achieved with a flow rate of 0.15 µL/min). After 5 days, the cells formed confluent monolayers exhibiting:  
- Dense F-actin networks  
- High expression of tight junction protein ZO-1  
- Abundant active mitochondria  
These characteristics matched those of Caco-2 monolayers cultured using conventional pump-driven systems, demonstrating the device's ability to support proper cell differentiation.  

**Example 3: Pumpless Culture of A549 Lung Epithelial Cells**  

A549 cells expressing red fluorescent protein-tagged vimentin (A549 VIM RFP) were cultured in the pumpless device under varying shear stresses. At shear stresses above 0.03 dyn/cm², cells exhibited:  
- Increased vimentin expression (marker of mesenchymal phenotype)  
- Spindle-like morphology  
- Reduced cytokeratin expression  
These findings confirmed the device's capability to study shear-induced EMT in lung epithelial cells.  

**Example 4: Multiplexed Pumpless Device**  

A device with five parallel channels, each with different thread configurations, demonstrated simultaneous generation of different flow rates (0.15-6 µL/min) within a single chip. This multiplexed approach enables efficient screening of cellular responses to varying shear stress conditions.
