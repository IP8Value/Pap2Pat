# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of assisted reproductive technology (ART), specifically to methods for predicting pregnancy outcomes in single embryo transfer (SET) procedures. More particularly, the invention provides a novel method for assessing oocyte quality by analyzing gene expression profiles in cumulus cells (CC) surrounding the oocyte. The invention further discloses specific gene markers and predictive models that enable the selection of embryos with the highest likelihood of resulting in successful pregnancy, thereby improving the efficacy of in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) treatments.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Infertility affects a significant proportion of the global population, and ART procedures such as IVF and ICSI have become widely adopted solutions. A critical challenge in ART is the selection of embryos with the highest developmental potential for transfer. Conventional methods rely on morphological assessment of embryos, but this approach has limitations in accurately predicting pregnancy outcomes.  

Recent studies have explored the use of cumulus cells, which are in direct contact with the oocyte during its growth and maturation, as a source of biomarkers for oocyte quality. Gene expression analysis in cumulus cells has shown promise in correlating with embryo development, oocyte maturity, and pregnancy outcomes. However, existing studies have reported inconsistent results, likely due to variations in experimental design, stimulation protocols, and laboratory conditions.  

There remains an unmet need for a reliable, standardized method to predict pregnancy outcomes based on cumulus cell gene expression. The present invention addresses this need by identifying specific gene markers and developing predictive models that enhance the accuracy of embryo selection in ART procedures.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for predicting pregnancy outcomes in ART by analyzing the expression of specific genes in cumulus cells. The method comprises the steps of:  

1. Collecting cumulus cells from individual oocytes during oocyte retrieval.  
2. Isolating RNA from the cumulus cells and quantifying the expression of a panel of genes, including but not limited to ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase ID (CAMK1D), stanniocalcin 1 (STC1), glutathione reductase (GSR), glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3), glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 and 4 (GSTA3 and GSTA4), progesterone receptor (PGR), and solute carrier family 2 member 1 (SLC2A1).  
3. Applying a predictive model to the gene expression data to calculate the probability of pregnancy for each oocyte.  
4. Selecting the oocyte or embryo with the highest predicted probability for transfer.  

The invention further discloses multivariable predictive models incorporating gene expression data and patient-specific factors such as age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol levels. These models achieve high accuracy, with positive predictive values (PPV) and negative predictive values (NPV) exceeding 90%.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the analysis of cumulus cell gene expression to predict the pregnancy potential of oocytes in ART. Cumulus cells are harvested during oocyte retrieval and processed for RNA extraction. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (QPCR) is used to measure the expression levels of target genes, which are normalized to reference genes such as beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC).  

### Key Gene Markers  

The invention identifies the following genes as predictive markers for pregnancy outcomes:  

1. **EFNB2**: Higher expression in cumulus cells is associated with successful pregnancy. EFNB2 is implicated in luteinization processes and may reflect oocyte developmental competence.  
2. **CAMK1D**: Upregulated in cumulus cells linked to viable pregnancies. CAMK1D is involved in steroidogenesis and cellular signaling pathways critical for oocyte maturation.  
3. **GSTA4 and GSTA3**: Members of the glutathione S-transferase family, these genes are associated with detoxification and oxidative stress response, which are vital for oocyte health.  
4. **PGR**: The progesterone receptor plays a role in steroid hormone signaling and is correlated with successful embryo implantation.  
5. **GSR and GPX3**: Genes involved in glutathione metabolism, which protects cells from oxidative damage and supports oocyte viability.  

### Predictive Models  

The invention provides several predictive models for pregnancy outcomes:  

1. **Three-Gene Model**: Incorporates EFNB2, GSTA4, and PGR, achieving an accuracy of 73% and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82.  
2. **Five-Gene Model**: Includes EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3, with an accuracy of 81% and an AUC of 0.93.  
3. **Extended Model**: Combines gene expression data with patient characteristics (age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol levels), yielding a PPV and NPV of 93% and an AUC of 0.95.  

### Intra-Patient Analysis  

A unique aspect of the invention is its application in intra-patient comparisons, where cumulus cells from sibling oocytes (from the same retrieval cycle) are analyzed to predict which embryo will result in pregnancy. This approach eliminates inter-patient variability and enhances prediction accuracy.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Validation of the Three-Gene Model**  

A retrospective analysis was performed on cumulus cells from 47 ICSI patients. The three-gene model (EFNB2, CAMK1D, and STC1) from a previous study was applied, demonstrating a positive predictive value (PPV) of 62%, a negative predictive value (NPV) of 86%, and an accuracy of 72%. These results confirm the robustness of the model in an independent patient cohort.  

### Example 2  

**Development of the Five-Gene Model**  

A stepwise multiple regression analysis identified EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3 as the most predictive genes. The resulting model achieved an accuracy of 81% and an AUC of 0.93, significantly outperforming the three-gene model.  

### Example 3  

**Integration of Patient Characteristics**  

The five-gene model was further refined by incorporating patient-specific factors (age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol levels). This extended model achieved a PPV, NPV, and accuracy of 93%, demonstrating the synergistic effect of combining gene expression and clinical data.  

### Example 4  

**Intra-Patient Prediction**  

Cumulus cells from seven patients were analyzed to compare oocytes that resulted in pregnancy after frozen embryo transfer (FRET) with those that did not. The five-gene model correctly ranked the pregnancy potential of oocytes in six out of seven patients, highlighting its utility in clinical decision-making.
