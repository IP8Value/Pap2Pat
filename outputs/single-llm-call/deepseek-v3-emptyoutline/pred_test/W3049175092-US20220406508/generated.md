# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates generally to spintronic devices and more specifically to systems and methods for controlling magnetic spin textures through chemisorbed oxygen-induced Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI). In modern spintronics, the ability to control chiral spin textures such as domain walls and skyrmions is crucial for developing next-generation memory and logic devices. Conventional approaches rely on heavy metal interfaces to generate sufficient DMI for stabilizing these spin textures, which presents material limitations and cost barriers due to the scarcity of suitable heavy metals.  

Prior art systems have demonstrated DMI effects primarily through ferromagnet/heavy metal interfaces, where the spin-orbit coupling in heavy elements like platinum, iridium, and tungsten induces the necessary asymmetric exchange interaction. While these systems have proven effective, they suffer from several disadvantages including high material costs, limited tunability, and challenges in device integration. Additionally, the strong spin-orbit coupling required for substantial DMI typically restricts material choices to rare and expensive 5d transition metals.  

A significant unmet need exists for alternative DMI mechanisms that can provide comparable or superior performance while utilizing more abundant and cost-effective materials. The present invention addresses this need by leveraging the unexpected discovery that chemisorbed oxygen on ferromagnetic surfaces induces a substantial DMI effect at room temperature, comparable in magnitude to that achieved through conventional heavy metal interfaces.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides systems and methods for inducing and controlling Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction in magnetic materials through chemisorbed oxygen interfaces. In one embodiment, the invention comprises a multilayer structure including a ferromagnetic material layer with chemisorbed oxygen atoms on its surface, where the oxygen coverage is precisely controlled to tune the magnitude and sign of the resulting DMI.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. A tunable platform comprising ferromagnetic layers (such as Ni/Co bilayers) adjacent to spacer layers (such as Pd) of controlled thickness, where the spacer thickness adjusts the baseline DMI to enable precise measurement and control of oxygen-induced DMI contributions.  

2. Methods for controlling oxygen coverage on ferromagnetic surfaces to achieve desired DMI effects, including techniques for maintaining sub-monolayer oxygen coverage in the range of 0 to 0.5 monolayers where optimal DMI tuning occurs.  

3. Applications of the oxygen-induced DMI for controlling chiral spin textures including domain walls and skyrmions, with particular utility in spintronic memory and logic devices.  

The invention provides several technical advantages over conventional approaches. First, it enables substantial DMI effects using abundant oxygen rather than scarce heavy metals. Second, it offers precise electrical and chemical control over DMI through adjustable oxygen coverage. Third, it facilitates room temperature operation of chiral spin textures without requiring heavy metal components. These advantages make the invention particularly valuable for developing practical spintronic devices with improved performance and reduced manufacturing costs.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description provides specific implementations of the invention with reference to particular material systems and experimental configurations. However, those skilled in the art will recognize that the principles disclosed herein may be applied to various alternative embodiments within the scope of the invention.  

### Multilayer Structure with Tunable DMI  

In a preferred embodiment, the invention utilizes a multilayer structure comprising a tungsten substrate, palladium spacer layer, and ferromagnetic bilayer (such as Ni/Co), with chemisorbed oxygen on the surface. The tungsten substrate provides an epitaxial growth template for high-quality crystalline films. The palladium spacer layer thickness is precisely controlled between approximately 2 to 3 monolayers to establish a tunable baseline DMI.  

The thickness-dependent DMI tuning arises from competing contributions from the Pd/W and Pd/ferromagnet interfaces. Density functional theory calculations confirm that the W interface contributes a right-handed DMI while the Pd interface contributes a left-handed DMI. By adjusting the Pd thickness, the net DMI can be tuned through zero to either positive or negative values, creating a sensitive platform for measuring additional DMI contributions from surface adsorbates.  

### Oxygen Chemisorption and Coverage Control  

The invention employs controlled oxygen exposure to create well-defined chemisorbed layers on the ferromagnetic surface. Oxygen atoms preferentially occupy threefold hollow sites on Ni(111) surfaces with a bond length of approximately 1.88 Å. The coverage is maintained below 0.5 monolayers to prevent oxide formation while enabling substantial DMI effects.  

Key aspects of oxygen coverage control include:  

1. Ultra-high vacuum deposition and exposure systems maintaining base pressures below 4×10<sup>-11</sup> torr to ensure clean interfaces.  

2. Precise oxygen dosing using high-purity (99.993%) oxygen at controlled pressures between 5×10<sup>-9</sup> to 1×10<sup>-8</sup> torr.  

3. Real-time monitoring of oxygen coverage through work function measurements or low-energy electron diffraction patterns.  

### Measurement and Quantification of Oxygen-Induced DMI  

The invention provides methods for quantifying oxygen-induced DMI through analysis of domain wall chirality in perpendicularly magnetized films. Using spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy (SPLEEM), the chirality of domain walls can be determined with high precision.  

The oxygen-induced DMI is calculated by comparing the Pd thickness variation required to produce equivalent chirality changes through:  

D<sub>O</sub> = (D<sub>Pd</sub> × Δd<sub>Pd</sub>)/θ<sub>O</sub>  

Where D<sub>O</sub> is the oxygen-induced DMI per monolayer, D<sub>Pd</sub> is the Pd-induced DMI variation per monolayer, Δd<sub>Pd</sub> is the Pd thickness variation, and θ<sub>O</sub> is the oxygen coverage. Experimental measurements yield D<sub>O</sub> values of approximately 0.63 ± 0.26 meV/atom for full oxygen monolayer coverage.  

### Applications in Spin Texture Control  

The invention enables precise control over various chiral spin textures through oxygen-induced DMI:  

1. **Domain Wall Chirality Switching**: The system can reversibly switch between left-handed, right-handed, and achiral domain walls by adjusting either oxygen coverage or Pd spacer thickness.  

2. **Skyrmion Engineering**: Oxygen coverage can modify skyrmion winding numbers and transition between hedgehog-type and Bloch-type configurations at room temperature.  

3. **Voltage-Controlled DMI**: The invention may be combined with magneto-ionic approaches to enable voltage-controlled oxygen migration for electrically tunable DMI effects.  

### Alternative Embodiments  

While the preferred embodiment uses Ni/Co/Pd/W multilayers, the invention may be implemented with various alternative material systems:  

1. Different ferromagnetic materials including Fe, Co, Ni, or their alloys.  

2. Alternative substrates such as Ru(0001) or Cu(111).  

3. Other adsorbates with strong electronegativity differences that may induce similar DMI effects.  

The invention thus provides a versatile platform for controlling chiral spin textures through surface chemistry, offering significant advantages over conventional heavy-metal-based approaches for spintronic applications.
