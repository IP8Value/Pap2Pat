# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Reinforcement learning (RL) agents operating in complex environments face significant challenges when extrinsic rewards are sparse or difficult to obtain. Traditional exploration strategies often prove inefficient in such scenarios, as they lack mechanisms to identify and prioritize meaningful areas of the environment for exploration. Curiosity-driven exploration methods have emerged as a promising solution, wherein an agent learns a predictive model of its environment (a "world model") and uses discrepancies between predicted and actual experiences to generate intrinsic rewards. These intrinsic rewards drive the agent to explore states where the world model's predictions are inaccurate, thereby facilitating continuous improvement of both the world model and the exploration policy.  

Existing approaches to curiosity-driven exploration typically treat the learning of the world model and the exploration policy as separate problems, leading to suboptimal performance and increased computational complexity. Additionally, conventional methods often rely on predicting raw observations or fixed random projections, which may not efficiently capture the underlying structure of the environment. There remains a need for a unified framework that jointly optimizes the world model and exploration policy while leveraging advanced self-supervised learning techniques to improve representation quality and exploration efficiency.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses BYOL-Explore, a novel curiosity-driven exploration algorithm that addresses the limitations of prior methods by unifying the learning of a world model and an exploration policy under a single self-supervised objective. BYOL-Explore employs a latent-predictive world model inspired by Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) principles, wherein an online network predicts future latent representations generated by an exponential moving average (EMA) target network. The prediction error serves as both a training signal for the world model and an intrinsic reward for the exploration policy, enabling simultaneous improvement of both components.  

Key innovations of BYOL-Explore include:  
1. **Unified Learning Objective**: The same self-supervised prediction loss is used to train the world model and drive exploration, eliminating the need for separate optimization objectives.  
2. **Latent-Predictive World Model**: The world model operates in latent space, predicting future representations of observations rather than raw observations themselves, improving computational efficiency and generalization.  
3. **Bootstrapped Target Mechanism**: The target network generates latent representations using an EMA of the online network's weights, ensuring stable and meaningful prediction targets.  
4. **Reward Normalization and Prioritization**: Intrinsic rewards derived from prediction errors are normalized to account for non-stationarity and optionally prioritized to focus exploration on high-uncertainty states.  

BYOL-Explore demonstrates superior performance across diverse domains, including complex 3D environments with sparse rewards (DM-HARD-8 suite) and challenging 2D exploration games (Atari). Notably, it achieves human-level or superhuman performance without relying on human demonstrations or task-specific reward shaping. The algorithm's generality stems from its ability to handle partially observable, procedurally generated, and multi-task environments while maintaining a simple and scalable architecture.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

**System Architecture**  
BYOL-Explore comprises three primary components:  
1. **Latent-Predictive World Model**: A neural network architecture that predicts future latent representations of observations.  
2. **Reward Normalization and Prioritization Module**: A mechanism for stabilizing and focusing intrinsic rewards.  
3. **Reinforcement Learning Agent**: A policy optimization framework that leverages intrinsic rewards for exploration.  

**Latent-Predictive World Model**  
The world model consists of:  
- **Online Network**: Comprising an encoder (fθ) that maps observations (ot) to latent representations (fθ(ot) ∈ ℝN) and a recurrent neural network (RNN) cell (hcθ) that maintains a history representation (bt ∈ ℝM).  
- **Target Network**: An EMA version of the encoder (fφ) that generates prediction targets (fφ(ot+k)).  
- **Predictor Network**: (gθ) that projects history representations to the latent space for multi-step open-loop predictions.  

The world model is trained to minimize the cosine distance between predictions (gθ(bt,k)) and targets (fφ(ot+k)) over a horizon K:  

LBYOL-Explore(θ) = 𝔼[∑k=1K(t) ‖gθ(bt,k) − sg(fφ(ot+k))‖²]  

where sg denotes the stop-gradient operation.  

**Intrinsic Reward Computation**  
The intrinsic reward for transition (ot, at, ot+1) is the cumulative prediction error:  
rit = ∑k=1K(t) LBYOL-Explore(θ, t, k)  

Rewards are normalized by an EMA estimate of their standard deviation (σr) and optionally prioritized by clipping values below an adaptive threshold (μξ/σr):  
rit = max(rit/σr − μξ/σr, 0)  

**Policy Optimization**  
The RL agent (e.g., VMPO) optimizes a combined reward:  
rt = re,t + λrit  

where re,t is the extrinsic reward and λ controls their mixing. The world model and policy can share representations (fθ = fψ, hcθ = hcψ) for improved sample efficiency.  

**Implementation Details**  
- **Atari Settings**:  
  - Horizon K=8, EMA α=0.99, λ=0.1  
  - ResNet encoder, LSTM RNN, 128-dimensional latent space  
  - VMPO optimizer with γ=0.999, learning rate 10⁻⁴  

- **DM-HARD-8 Settings**:  
  - Horizon K=10, EMA α=0.99, λ=0.01  
  - 256D observation embeddings, 512D history representations  
  - Multi-task training with shared parameters across 8 environments  

**Advantages Over Prior Art**  
1. **Simplified Architecture**: Eliminates separate networks for exploration and representation learning.  
2. **Improved Exploration**: Multi-step latent prediction captures long-term dependencies better than pixel-space or fixed-projection methods.  
3. **Generality**: Performs well across 2D/3D, fully/partially observable, and single/multi-task domains.  
4. **Sample Efficiency**: Shared representations and prioritized rewards reduce required interaction steps.  

**Experimental Results**  
- **Atari**: Achieves superhuman performance on 10 hardest exploration games (median HNS=331.98)  
- **DM-HARD-8**: Solves 5/8 tasks without demonstrations, surpassing prior state-of-the-art (e.g., Throw-across)  
- **Ablations**: Confirms necessity of bootstrapped targets (vs. fixed) and multi-step prediction (vs. K=1)  

The complete system enables autonomous agents to efficiently explore complex environments by continuously identifying and investigating areas where their understanding is incomplete, advancing the state of the art in reinforcement learning and artificial intelligence.
