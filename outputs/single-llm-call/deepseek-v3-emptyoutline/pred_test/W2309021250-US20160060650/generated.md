# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of plant biotechnology, specifically to methods for restoring female fertility in sterile potato cultivars through the expression of cellulose binding domain (CBD) proteins. Conventional potato breeding is hindered by the natural sterility of many commercially important cultivars, such as Bintje, which fail to produce viable ovaries or seeds even when pollinated. Prior attempts to overcome this sterility have relied on hormonal treatments or genetic modifications targeting auxin pathways, but these approaches often result in undesirable pleiotropic effects or fail to restore full fertility.  

Cellulose binding domains, particularly those derived from phytopathogens such as *Phytophthora infestans*, have been studied primarily for their role in cellulose substrate adherence and microfibril dissociation. While certain CBDs from saprophytic organisms have been explored in transgenic plants to modulate growth characteristics, their application in altering reproductive morphology has not been previously demonstrated. The present invention addresses this gap by disclosing the unexpected utility of *P. infestans* CBD 1 in enabling ovary formation and seed production in otherwise sterile potato plants. This breakthrough facilitates the introgression of agronomically valuable traits—such as late blight resistance and enhanced tuber qualities—into elite cultivars through conventional crossing, without requiring the persistence of the transgene in progeny.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a transgenic potato plant comprising a heterologous nucleic acid encoding a *Phytophthora infestans* cellulose binding domain 1 (CBD 1) protein, wherein expression of said protein confers female fertility to a sterile potato cultivar. The fertility restoration enables the production of seed berries capable of developing viable seeds upon outcrossing with fertile pollen donors. Notably, the invention demonstrates that CBD 1 expression alone is sufficient to overcome the developmental block preventing ovary formation in sterile cultivars like Bintje, without altering susceptibility to pathogens such as *P. infestans*.  

Further embodiments include methods for breeding potato plants by crossing the transgenic CBD 1-expressing female parent with a male parent possessing desirable traits, such as disease resistance or pigmentation. Progeny derived from such crosses segregate for the CBD 1 transgene, allowing selection of improved cultivars that retain the introduced traits while reverting to the original sterile phenotype. The invention also encompasses the use of CBD 1 to induce parthenocarpic ovary development in solanaceous crops, including tomatoes, and the application of CBD 1-derived sequences as molecular tools to investigate cell wall dynamics during reproductive tissue development.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is premised on the discovery that constitutive expression of *P. infestans* CBD 1 in the sterile potato cultivar Bintje restores female fertility, enabling the production of seed berries and viable seeds upon pollination. The CBD 1 protein, encoded by the gene sequence EU179903, is localized to the cell walls of developing ovarian tissues, where it likely modulates cellulose microfibril interactions to permit ovary expansion. Unlike prior art involving hormonal manipulations, the present method does not disrupt endogenous auxin signaling pathways, thereby avoiding unintended growth aberrations.  

Transformation of potato plants is achieved using *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* vector systems, such as pBI121, wherein the CBD 1 coding sequence is operably linked to a constitutive promoter (e.g., CaMV 35S). Stable integration events are confirmed via PCR and genome walking, with preferred embodiments including single-copy insertions in non-coding genomic regions (e.g., introns of putative ODORANT 1 protein genes). Immunodetection assays validate CBD 1 accumulation in ovarian tissues, correlating with the observed fertility phenotype.  

The utility of the invention is demonstrated through crosses between CBD 1-expressing Bintje and late blight-resistant cultivars (e.g., Sarpo Mira) or pigmented varieties (e.g., Peter Wilcox). Progeny inherit multigenic traits such as disease resistance and tuber color while losing the CBD 1 transgene through Mendelian segregation. This allows for the development of improved cultivars without transgenic elements, addressing regulatory and market constraints.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Plant Transformation and Selection  

Transgenic Bintje potato plants were generated by infecting leaf explants with *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* LBA4404 harboring the pBI121-CBD 1 construct. Regenerated plantlets were screened via PCR using 35S promoter-specific primers, and integration sites were mapped using genome walking. Two independent transgenic lines (B7 and B48) exhibited stable CBD 1 expression, with B48 showing insertion within an intron of a non-essential genomic locus (NW_006239126). Immunohistochemistry confirmed CBD 1 localization in ovarian cell walls, coinciding with seed berry formation.  

### Example 2  

Female fertility was assessed by pollinating transgenic Bintje flowers with pollen from Sarpo Mira. Control Bintje plants abscised flowers post-bloom, whereas CBD 1-expressing lines developed seed berries containing up to 200 viable seeds per berry. Progeny from these crosses were evaluated for late blight resistance using detached leaf assays inoculated with *P. infestans* zoospores. Resistant progeny (e.g., B7SM lines 1–16) retained Sarpo Mira’s immunity while exhibiting tuber color variations attributable to anthocyanin pathway complementation.  

### Example 3  

Crosses between CBD 1-expressing Bintje and the pigmented cultivar Peter Wilcox yielded progeny with purple-skinned, yellow-fleshed tubers, demonstrating successful introgression of nutritional quality traits. Segregation analysis confirmed that non-transgenic progeny reverted to Bintje’s sterile phenotype, enabling the development of improved cultivars free of recombinant DNA.
