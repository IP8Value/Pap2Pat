# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) remains a significant global health challenge despite advances in combination antiretroviral therapy (cART). While cART has transformed HIV/AIDS into a manageable chronic condition, the emergence of drug resistance mutations (DRMs) and adverse side effects associated with current anti-HIV drugs necessitate the development of novel therapeutic agents targeting alternative viral mechanisms. The HIV-1 capsid protein (CA) plays a critical role at multiple stages of the viral replication cycle, including uncoating, reverse transcription, nuclear import, and integration of viral DNA into the host genome. Given its multifunctional role, CA represents an attractive target for antiviral intervention.  

Several small molecule inhibitors and peptides targeting CA have been reported, including benzodiazepines (BD), benzimidazoles (BM), pyrrolopyrazolones (BI-1 and BI-2), and PF74. However, none of these compounds has progressed to clinical trials due to limitations in potency, binding affinity, or pharmacokinetic properties. Recent developments have identified GS-CA1 and GS-6207 as highly potent CA inhibitors with long-acting therapeutic potential. These compounds exhibit superior binding affinity and antiviral activity compared to existing inhibitors, making them promising candidates for clinical development.  

Despite these advances, there remains a need for novel CA-targeting compounds with improved pharmacological profiles, reduced toxicity, and enhanced resistance profiles. The present invention addresses this need by providing cyclic and linear peptides designed to mimic the structural interactions of known CA inhibitors while offering superior binding affinity and therapeutic efficacy.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention relates to novel cyclic and linear peptides that inhibit HIV-1 replication by targeting the capsid protein (CA). These peptides are designed based on structural features shared by small molecule inhibitors (e.g., GS-CA1, PF74, BI-2) and host-derived peptides (e.g., CPSF6, NUP153) that bind CA. The disclosed peptides exhibit high binding affinity to CA, as demonstrated by MicroScale thermophoresis (MST) assays, and inhibit viral replication at both early and late stages.  

In one embodiment, the invention provides a cyclic peptide (Pep-1) that binds the CA hexamer with approximately 7-fold greater affinity than PF74, a well-characterized CA inhibitor. The peptide incorporates key structural moieties that facilitate interactions with critical CA residues, including L56, M66, Q67, N74, and A105. Additional embodiments include linear peptides with modified sequences to optimize binding and antiviral activity.  

The peptides of the invention are useful as therapeutic agents for treating HIV-1 infection, either alone or in combination with existing antiretroviral drugs. They may also serve as research tools for studying CA function and viral replication mechanisms.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms have the meanings ascribed to them unless specified otherwise:  

- **Capsid protein (CA):** The structural protein of HIV-1 that forms the conical core surrounding the viral RNA and associated enzymes. CA consists of an N-terminal domain (CA-NTD) and a C-terminal domain (CA-CTD).  
- **Cyclic peptide:** A peptide in which the N- and C-termini are linked, forming a circular structure that enhances stability and binding affinity.  
- **Linear peptide:** A peptide with a free N-terminus and C-terminus, which may be modified to improve pharmacokinetic properties.  
- **Binding affinity (Kd):** The equilibrium dissociation constant, representing the concentration of ligand required to occupy half of the binding sites on a target protein.  
- **MicroScale thermophoresis (MST):** A biophysical technique used to measure binding affinities by monitoring the movement of molecules in a temperature gradient.  

### Overview  

The invention is based on the discovery that certain structural features of known CA inhibitors and host-derived peptides can be incorporated into designed peptides to achieve high-affinity binding to CA. Computational modeling and docking studies were employed to identify key interactions between CA and small molecule inhibitors (e.g., GS-CA1, PF74, BI-2) as well as host factors (e.g., CPSF6, NUP153). These interactions were used to design cyclic and linear peptides that mimic the binding modes of these ligands while offering improved pharmacological properties.  

### Cyclic Peptides  

The cyclic peptide Pep-1 was designed to incorporate structural motifs present in GS-CA1, PF74, and host-derived peptides. Pep-1 binds the CA hexamer at a site shared by these ligands, with a Kd of 32 ± 3 nM, as determined by MST. This represents a 7-fold improvement over PF74 (Kd = 212 ± 7 nM). The peptide's cyclic structure enhances its stability and resistance to proteolytic degradation, making it suitable for therapeutic applications.  

Key residues in Pep-1 interact with CA-NTD residues L56, M66, Q67, N74, and A105, which are critical for viral replication. Mutations at these residues confer resistance to known CA inhibitors, underscoring their functional importance. The peptide's binding mode was validated by computational docking and experimental affinity measurements.  

### Linear Peptides  

In addition to cyclic peptides, the invention encompasses linear peptides designed to target the same binding pocket. These peptides may include modifications such as N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation, or incorporation of non-natural amino acids to enhance binding affinity and metabolic stability. Linear peptides may also be conjugated to carrier molecules (e.g., polyethylene glycol) to improve pharmacokinetics.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  
**Design and Docking of Cyclic Peptide Pep-1**  

The structure of Pep-1 was generated using computational modeling software (Prime, Schrödinger Suite) and energy-minimized using the MM/GBSA method. Docking simulations were performed using Induced-Fit Docking (IFD) to predict the binding pose of Pep-1 within the CA hexamer (PDB: 4XFZ). The best-scoring complex revealed interactions with critical CA residues, including L56, M66, Q67, N74, and A105.  

### Example 2  
**Synthesis and Purification of Pep-1**  

Pep-1 was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) with Fmoc chemistry. The peptide was cyclized via a disulfide bridge between cysteine residues and purified by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC). Purity was confirmed by mass spectrometry (>95%).  

### Example 3  
**Binding Affinity Measurement by MST**  

The binding affinity of Pep-1 for CA was determined using MST. Fluorescently labeled CA (200 nM) was incubated with increasing concentrations of Pep-1 (1–2000 nM), and thermophoresis was monitored. Data were fitted to a quadratic equation, yielding a Kd of 32 ± 3 nM.  

### Example 4  
**Antiviral Activity Assay**  

Pep-1 was tested for antiviral activity in MT-4 cells infected with HIV-1. The peptide exhibited potent inhibition of viral replication, with an EC50 of 50 pM, comparable to GS-6207. No cytotoxicity was observed at concentrations up to 10 μM.
