# DESCRIPTION  

## INCORPORATION OF THE SEQUENCE LISTING  

The present application incorporates by reference the sequence listing submitted as an ASCII text file. The sequence listing provides the amino acid sequences of synthetic GHRH receptor (GHRH-R) antagonists, including but not limited to MIA-602, MIA-604, MIA-610, and MIA-690, which are disclosed herein for therapeutic use in modulating lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer.  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of peptide therapeutics and specifically to synthetic growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor (GHRH-R) antagonists and their use in the treatment of pulmonary diseases. The invention encompasses compositions comprising GHRH-R antagonist peptides, methods of synthesizing these peptides, and their applications in inhibiting inflammation, fibrosis, and tumor growth in the lung.  

## BACKGROUND  

Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) is a hypothalamic peptide that stimulates the release of growth hormone (GH) from the pituitary gland. Beyond its endocrine functions, GHRH and its receptors are expressed in extrapituitary tissues, including the lung, where they exert paracrine and autocrine effects. GHRH receptors (GHRH-R) and their splice variants, such as SV1, are implicated in cellular proliferation, inflammation, and fibrotic processes.  

Pulmonary fibrosis, chronic inflammation, and lung cancer are significant health burdens with limited therapeutic options. Current treatments for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) exhibit modest efficacy and substantial side effects. Similarly, lung cancers, particularly those resistant to chemotherapy, pose therapeutic challenges. The discovery that GHRH-R antagonists modulate key signaling pathways involved in these diseases provides a novel therapeutic approach.  

Prior art describes GHRH analogs with endocrine activity, but their extrapituitary effects remain underexplored. The present invention addresses this gap by disclosing synthetic GHRH-R antagonists, such as MIA-602, which exhibit high binding affinity to GHRH-R and demonstrate potent inhibitory effects on lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer cell proliferation.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides synthetic GHRH-R antagonist peptides and their use in treating lung pathologies. Key aspects include:  

1. **Compositions**: Peptides such as MIA-602 (PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2) and analogs with modifications enhancing stability and potency.  
2. **Mechanisms of Action**: Inhibition of signaling pathways (e.g., PI3K/Akt, ERK, STAT3) and modulation of mitochondrial function to suppress inflammation, fibrosis, and tumor growth.  
3. **Therapeutic Applications**: Treatment of pulmonary fibrosis (e.g., IPF), sarcoidosis, and lung cancer, including chemotherapy-resistant tumors.  

The peptides are synthesized via solid-phase methods, purified by HPLC, and evaluated for receptor binding affinity and inhibitory activity in vitro and in vivo.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

- **GHRH-R antagonist**: A peptide that binds GHRH-R or its splice variants (e.g., SV1) and inhibits ligand-induced signaling.  
- **Pulmonary fibrosis**: A pathological condition characterized by excessive collagen deposition and lung scarring, as in IPF.  
- **Spare respiratory capacity**: The extra mitochondrial capacity available to meet increased energy demands.  

### Compositions  

The invention encompasses GHRH-R antagonist peptides with the following features:  
1. **Core Structure**: A 29-amino acid sequence derived from GHRH, modified to enhance receptor binding and stability.  
2. **Substitutions**:  
   - D-Arg at position 2 to resist enzymatic degradation.  
   - pentafluoro-Phe at position 6 to increase hydrophobicity.  
   - Orn at positions 12 and 21 to reduce susceptibility to proteolysis.  
3. **Derivatives**: Peptides with ω-amino acids at the N-terminus or additional substitutions to optimize pharmacokinetics.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists  

The antagonist MIA-602 was synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry. The peptide was cleaved from the resin, purified by reverse-phase HPLC, and characterized by mass spectrometry. Purity exceeded 95%, and the peptide exhibited a binding affinity (IC50) of 0.8 nM for GHRH-R, surpassing native GHRH(1-29)NH2 (IC50 = 2.5 nM).  

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro  

Human A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells were treated with 5 μM MIA-602 for 72 hours. Proliferation decreased by 65% (p < 0.01) via suppression of phosphorylated Akt and ERK. Similar results were observed in primary lung fibroblasts, where MIA-602 reduced collagen synthesis by 40% (p < 0.05).  

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo  

Nude mice bearing A549 xenografts received subcutaneous MIA-602 (5 μg/day) for 4 weeks. Tumor volume was reduced by 58% (p < 0.001) compared to controls, with no significant weight loss or toxicity. Immunohistochemistry revealed decreased Ki-67 and increased caspase-3 staining, indicating apoptosis.  

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists  

Competitive binding assays using 125I-GHRH(1-29)NH2 and human GHRH-R transfected HEK293 cells showed the following IC50 values:  
- MIA-602: 0.8 nM  
- MIA-690: 1.2 nM  
- Native GHRH: 2.5 nM  

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin  

C57Bl/6 mice received intratracheal bleomycin (2 mg/kg) with or without MIA-602 (10 μg/day, IP). After 28 days, MIA-602 reduced lung hydroxyproline content by 45% (p < 0.01) and histopathological fibrosis scores by 60% (p < 0.001). RNA-seq analysis showed downregulation of TGF-β and Wnt pathway genes.
