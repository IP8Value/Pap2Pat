# DESCRIPTION  

## EXAMPLE 1  

### Secretion ELISA for Detecting the Effector Protein SptP when Bound to its Cognate Chaperone SicP  

The present invention provides a method for detecting the effector protein SptP in complex with its cognate chaperone SicP using a secretion enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The method involves isolating needle complexes from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium strains expressing functional type III secretion systems (T3SS). The needle complexes are immobilized on a solid support, such as a microtiter plate, and incubated with bacterial culture supernatants containing secreted effector proteins.  

A primary antibody specific to SptP is then applied to capture the effector protein. The presence of SicP bound to SptP is confirmed using a secondary antibody conjugated to an enzyme, such as horseradish peroxidase (HRP), which generates a detectable signal upon addition of a chromogenic substrate. This assay is particularly advantageous for studying the secretion dynamics of effector proteins, as it allows for the quantitative measurement of SptP-SicP complexes under varying physiological conditions.  

The specificity of the assay is ensured by using genetically modified bacterial strains lacking functional T3SS components as negative controls. Additionally, the method can be adapted to detect other effector-chaperone pairs by substituting the primary antibody with one specific to the desired effector protein. The secretion ELISA provides a robust and reproducible means of monitoring effector protein secretion, which is critical for understanding bacterial pathogenesis and developing therapeutic interventions.  

## EXAMPLE 2  

### Structure ELISA to Detect the Attachment of PrgI to the PgH-Containing Base Element  

The invention further encompasses a structure-based ELISA for detecting the attachment of the needle filament protein PrgI to the PrgH-containing base element of the *Salmonella* T3SS needle complex. This method involves purifying needle complexes from bacterial strains expressing functional T3SS and immobilizing them on a solid support. The complexes are then probed with an antibody specific to PrgI, followed by a secondary antibody conjugated to a detection enzyme.  

The assay is designed to assess the structural integrity of the needle complex by measuring the binding affinity between PrgI and the base element. This is particularly useful for studying mutations or chemical perturbations that disrupt needle complex assembly. For example, needle complexes isolated from strains with deletions in *invG* or *prgH* show impaired PrgI attachment, as demonstrated by reduced signal intensity in the ELISA.  

The structure ELISA can also be employed to screen for small-molecule inhibitors that interfere with needle complex assembly. By comparing the binding signals of treated and untreated samples, the assay provides a high-throughput means of identifying potential antimicrobial agents targeting the T3SS. The method is highly specific, as it relies on antibodies that recognize conformational epitopes unique to the assembled needle complex.  

## EXAMPLE 3  

### Immunodetection of the Translocators SipB and SipC (Western Blot)  

The invention additionally provides a method for the immunodetection of the translocator proteins SipB and SipC using Western blot analysis. This technique involves isolating bacterial culture supernatants or purified needle complexes from *Salmonella* strains, separating the proteins by SDS-PAGE, and transferring them to a nitrocellulose membrane. The membrane is then probed with primary antibodies specific to SipB or SipC, followed by secondary antibodies conjugated to HRP or another detection enzyme.  

The Western blot method is particularly valuable for characterizing the secretion profiles of translocator proteins under different experimental conditions. For instance, strains with mutations in *prgK* or *prgH* exhibit reduced secretion of SipB and SipC, as evidenced by diminished band intensity on the blot. The assay can also be used to monitor the effects of environmental stimuli, such as pH or temperature, on translocator protein secretion.  

The specificity of the immunodetection is confirmed by including controls such as strains lacking the T3SS or pre-adsorbing the primary antibodies with purified SipB or SipC. The method provides a sensitive and quantitative means of analyzing translocator protein expression and secretion, which is essential for studying bacterial virulence mechanisms and evaluating potential therapeutics.
