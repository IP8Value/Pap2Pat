# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION  

Organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) have become a cornerstone of modern display technology due to their high efficiency, wide color gamut, and flexibility. A critical advancement in OLED performance has been the implementation of microcavity structures, which manipulate the spectral characteristics of emitted light to enhance color saturation and light extraction efficiency. However, existing microcavity OLED designs exhibit significant discrepancies in luminance enhancements across different colors, particularly between green and blue-emitting devices. While green microcavity OLEDs have demonstrated substantial improvements in current efficiency, blue-emitting devices often show negligible enhancements. These inconsistencies cannot be solely attributed to differences in photon outcoupling, suggesting that photopic luminosity—the human eye's sensitivity to light at different wavelengths—plays a crucial role in the observed efficiency variations.  

Conventional microcavity OLEDs rely on semitransparent metal electrodes, such as thin silver layers, which suffer from high absorption losses and insufficient reflectivity. Dielectric distributed Bragg reflectors (DBRs) offer superior reflectivity and lower absorption but introduce challenges in tuning cavity length and resonant wavelength alignment. Furthermore, prior art fails to account for the interplay between cavity length, electrode reflectivity, and photopic luminosity, leading to suboptimal efficiency enhancements. There remains an unmet need for a microcavity OLED design that systematically optimizes these parameters to achieve uniform and significant efficiency improvements across all primary colors.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a microcavity OLED structure that maximizes light extraction efficiency and luminance output by precisely controlling the reflectivity of semitransparent electrodes and the cavity length. The invention utilizes dielectric DBR substrates composed of alternating layers of titanium oxide (TiO₂) and silicon oxide (SiO₂), tuned to specific wavelengths for red, green, and blue emission. By optimizing the reflectivity of the DBR electrodes and the optical cavity length, the invention achieves enhanced photon outcoupling and photopic luminosity, resulting in superior current efficiency and quantum efficiency.  

Key innovations include:  
1. The use of high-reflectivity, low-absorption DBR substrates with peak reflectivities aligned to the emission wavelengths of red (610 nm), green (550 nm), and blue (474 nm) OLEDs.  
2. A method for tuning cavity length to align the resonant wavelength with the peak photopic luminosity (555 nm), thereby maximizing luminance perception.  
3. A double-emitting-layer (DEL) structure that confines excitons within the center of the emitting layer, preventing quenching and further enhancing efficiency.  

The invention demonstrates a green microcavity OLED with a record current efficiency of 224 cd/A, achieved by synergistically optimizing photon outcoupling and photopic luminosity. This approach is universally applicable to red, green, and blue OLEDs, enabling displays with wider color gamuts and higher efficiencies.  

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE  

The microcavity OLED of the present invention comprises a multilayer structure fabricated on a dielectric DBR substrate. The DBR substrate consists of two pairs of quarter-wave stacks of alternating TiO₂ and SiO₂ layers, with peak reflectivities tuned to 610 nm (red), 550 nm (green), and 474 nm (blue). The substrate is coated with a transparent indium tin oxide (ITO) electrode, followed by a spin-coated poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-polystyrenesulfonic acid (PEDOT:PSS) hole injection layer.  

The OLED stack includes:  
- A hole transport layer (HTL) of 1,1-bis[(di-4-tolylamino)phenyl]cyclohexane (TAPC), with thicknesses varied between 20–45 nm to tune cavity length.  
- An emitting layer (EML) comprising 4,4'-N,N-dicarbazole-biphenyl (CBP) doped with phosphorescent emitters: 7 wt% tris(2-phenylpyridine)iridium (Ir(ppy)₃) for green, 15 wt% iridium(III)bis[2-methyldibenzo-(f,h)quinoxaline](acetylacetonate) (Ir(MDQ)₂(acac)) for red, and 5 wt% iridium(III) bis[(4,6-difluorophenyl)pyridinato-N,C²⁹] picolinate (FIrpic) for blue.  
- An electron transport layer (ETL) of tris[3-(3-pyridyl)-mesityl]borane (3TPYMB) for green/red devices or 3,5,3',5'-tetra-3-pyridyl-[1,1';3',1'']terphenyl (B3PyPB) for blue devices.  
- A LiF/Al cathode.  

The cavity length is optimized by adjusting the thicknesses of the TAPC HTL and EML. For green devices, a 30 nm TAPC layer and 25 nm EML yield a resonant wavelength of 550 nm, aligning with the DBR's peak reflectivity and the photopic luminosity maximum. This configuration achieves a 140% enhancement in current efficiency compared to non-cavity devices.  

For blue devices, a trade-off exists between color purity and efficiency. Shorter cavity lengths shift the resonant wavelength toward deeper blue but reduce luminosity due to the eye's lower sensitivity at shorter wavelengths. The invention addresses this by employing a DEL structure, where a TCTA/CBP interface confines excitons, improving carrier injection and reducing quenching. The DEL green device achieves a current efficiency of 224 cd/A, with a 2.4× total enhancement when combined with a macrolens for substrate mode extraction.  

### METHODS AND MATERIALS  

**Fabrication of DBR Substrates:**  
1. Deposit alternating quarter-wave stacks of TiO₂ and SiO₂ via sputtering to form DBR substrates with peak reflectivities at 474 nm (blue), 550 nm (green), and 610 nm (red).  
2. Coat the DBR with ITO (25 nm) via sputtering, followed by UV-ozone treatment for 15 minutes.  

**OLED Fabrication:**  
1. Spin-coat PEDOT:PSS (25 nm) onto ITO and bake at 180°C for 15 minutes.  
2. Thermally evaporate the following layers in sequence:  
   - TAPC HTL (20–45 nm).  
   - EML: CBP doped with Ir(ppy)₃ (green), Ir(MDQ)₂(acac) (red), or FIrpic (blue).  
   - ETL: 3TPYMB (green/red) or B3PyPB (blue).  
   - LiF (1 nm) and Al (100 nm) cathode.  

**Characterization:**  
- Measure current-luminance-voltage characteristics using a Keithley source meter and calibrated photodiode.  
- Record electroluminescence spectra with an Ocean Optics HR4000 spectrometer.  
- Calculate photopic luminosity using the standard CIE luminosity function, integrating over the EL spectrum.  

**Optimization Criteria:**  
1. For green devices, maximize reflectivity at 550 nm and align cavity length to this wavelength.  
2. For blue devices, balance resonant wavelength (470–490 nm) with luminosity by adjusting B3PyPB thickness (45–53 nm).  
3. For red devices, tune 3TPYMB thickness (52–61 nm) to match the DBR's 610 nm peak.  

The invention’s DEL structure is fabricated by inserting a TCTA layer between two CBP:Ir(ppy)₃ layers, confining excitons at the TCTA/CBP interface. This structure achieves a 27% external quantum efficiency, the highest reported for a single emitting unit without additional light extraction techniques.
