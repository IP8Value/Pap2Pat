# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is a metabolic disorder affecting approximately 1 in 15,000 individuals worldwide. The condition arises from inherited mutations in the gene encoding phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH), the hepatic enzyme responsible for converting phenylalanine (phe) to tyrosine. In the absence of functional PAH, phe and its metabolites accumulate to neurotoxic levels in the bloodstream, leading to severe neurological damage including tremors, seizures, and mental retardation if left untreated. Current treatment strategies rely primarily on strict dietary restriction of phe, which presents significant challenges for patients.  

Medical foods and supplements designed for low-phe diets are often expensive, unpalatable, and time-consuming to prepare. These factors contribute to poor long-term compliance, particularly among adolescent and adult patients. While enzyme replacement therapies using phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) have been investigated, these approaches face challenges including immunogenicity, rapid protease degradation in the gastrointestinal tract, and the need for invasive administration methods. There exists a critical need for improved therapeutic options that are more affordable, patient-friendly, and effective at maintaining safe blood phe levels.  

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies  

The present invention provides significant advantages over existing PKU treatments. Unlike dietary restriction approaches, the disclosed technology allows for more normal dietary intake while still maintaining safe blood phe levels. Compared to injectable PAL therapies, the oral probiotic delivery system eliminates injection-related discomfort and reduces systemic immune responses. The engineered probiotic survives gastric transit and establishes itself in the intestinal environment where it can continuously metabolize dietary phe.  

This technology offers improved stability compared to naked enzyme therapies, as the PAL enzyme remains protected within the bacterial cells during gastrointestinal transit. The probiotic formulation provides sustained enzyme activity without requiring frequent dosing. Additionally, the production costs are substantially lower than current medical foods or purified enzyme therapies, potentially making treatment more accessible to a broader patient population. The palatable oral administration format significantly improves patient compliance compared to restrictive diets or injection-based therapies.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention discloses a genetically modified probiotic composition for the treatment of phenylketonuria (PKU). The composition comprises Lactobacillus reuteri bacteria engineered to express phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) from Anabaena variabilis (AvPAL). The probiotic is formulated for oral administration and functions by metabolizing phenylalanine in the gastrointestinal tract, thereby reducing systemic phenylalanine levels.  

Key features of the invention include:  
- A codon-optimized AvPAL gene under control of a constitutive Lactobacillus promoter  
- The complete genetic construct inserted into a stable plasmid vector  
- Methods for large-scale production and lyophilization of the probiotic  
- Demonstration of efficacy in reducing blood phenylalanine levels in a PKU mouse model  
- Absence of significant systemic immune response to the bacterial-delivered enzyme  

The probiotic, designated "pHENOMMenal," represents a novel approach to PKU treatment that combines the benefits of enzyme replacement therapy with the advantages of probiotic delivery. Clinical applications include use as a primary therapy or as an adjunct to dietary management in PKU patients.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The following detailed description provides specific implementations of the invention, including methods of construction, administration, and therapeutic use. While particular embodiments are described, it will be understood that various modifications may be made without departing from the scope of the invention.  

The foundation of the invention is the genetic engineering of Lactobacillus reuteri 100-23C to express functional AvPAL enzyme. The native AvPAL gene from Anabaena variabilis ATCC 29413 was modified through codon optimization for Lactobacillus expression and placed under control of a high-activity constitutive promoter from the erythromycin resistance B gene (ermB). The complete expression cassette includes the ermB promoter, optimized AvPAL coding sequence, and ermGT transcriptional terminator.  

The genetic construct was inserted into plasmid pSLERGT, derived from pSLER1 and pNCKH103 plasmids, to create the final expression vector. This plasmid provides erythromycin resistance for selection and stable maintenance in Lactobacillus hosts. Transformation was achieved through electroporation using optimized conditions of 12.5 kV/cm, 200 ohm parallel resistance, and 25 μFD capacitance.  

For therapeutic use, the engineered bacteria are grown in MRS media under anaerobic conditions to mid-log phase (OD600 0.55-0.75), harvested by centrifugation, and processed into freeze-dried powder. The lyophilization protocol involves suspension in PBS, rapid freezing at -80°C, and drying under vacuum at -40°C for 72 hours. The final product contains approximately 1×10^9 colony forming units per gram of powder.  

Administration is achieved by mixing the freeze-dried probiotic with food or reconstituting in liquid. In murine studies, effective dosing ranged from 1-2×10^7 CFU per gram of food, administered daily. The probiotic survives gastric transit and establishes temporary colonization in the small intestine, where it actively metabolizes dietary phenylalanine.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Construction of pHENOMMenal Probiotic**  

The AvPAL gene from Anabaena variabilis ATCC 29413 was codon-optimized for expression in Lactobacillus reuteri and synthesized as a DNA fragment (BioBasic Inc.). The synthetic gene, designated FuzErmAvPAL, was cloned into plasmid pSLERGT using NcoI and SmaI restriction sites. The resulting construct was transformed into E. coli Top10 for propagation, then introduced into L. reuteri 100-23C via electroporation.  

Transformants were selected on MRS agar containing 5 μg/ml erythromycin and verified by plasmid extraction and restriction analysis. A positive clone was expanded in MRS broth under anaerobic conditions and stored as glycerol stocks at -80°C. Functional expression of AvPAL was confirmed by spectrophotometric detection of trans-cinnamate production from phenylalanine substrate.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

**In Vitro Characterization**  

Lysates from pHENOMMenal cells demonstrated significant AvPAL activity compared to control L. reuteri (p<0.0001 at 30 minutes). Enzyme activity was measured by monitoring trans-cinnamate production at 280 nm in reactions containing 10 mM phenylalanine. The pHENOMMenal lysate produced 125±15 μM trans-cinnamate after 30 minutes incubation at 37°C, while control lysates showed no detectable activity.  

Stability studies revealed the engineered bacteria maintain AvPAL expression through at least 20 generations when grown under selective pressure. Freeze-dried preparations retained >90% viability after 6 months storage at -20°C and showed no significant loss of enzymatic activity.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

**In Vivo Efficacy in PKU Mouse Model**  

PAHenu2 mice (C57BL6/J background) were treated with pHENOMMenal mixed into powdered chow at 1-2×10^7 CFU/g food. Blood phenylalanine levels were monitored by fluorometric assay. After 3-4 days treatment, plasma phe decreased by 511.6±179.3 μM (p<0.0005). Extended treatment for 7 days produced a 598.6±289.5 μM reduction (p<1.0×10^-5), while 14-day treatment maintained similar efficacy.  

Control animals receiving non-engineered L. reuteri showed no significant change in plasma phe levels. Fecal analysis confirmed temporary colonization by pHENOMMenal during treatment periods, with clearance occurring within 2 days after cessation of dosing. No permanent colonization was observed at 8 months post-treatment.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

**Immunogenicity Assessment**  

Plasma from treated mice was analyzed for anti-AvPAL IgG antibodies by ELISA. Compared to positive controls receiving injected AvPAL (56,000-807,000 units), pHENOMMenal-treated animals showed no significant increase in antibody titers (all <9,000 units). This confirms the probiotic delivery system successfully avoids systemic immune detection of the therapeutic enzyme.  

Long-term safety studies revealed no adverse effects on animal health, growth, or behavior during or after treatment periods. Histological examination showed normal intestinal architecture with no signs of inflammation or pathology associated with probiotic administration.
