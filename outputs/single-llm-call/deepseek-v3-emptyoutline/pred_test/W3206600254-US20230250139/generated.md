# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and cellular recording systems. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for recording biological information along growing protein chains within living cells, enabling temporal tracking of cellular states and activities. The invention further relates to genetically encoded self-assembling protein systems capable of continuous longitudinal growth while incorporating labeled subunits that encode different cellular states or functions over time.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Current methods for monitoring biological processes in living cells suffer from significant limitations. Live imaging techniques, while providing real-time data, require specialized equipment and are limited in scalability. Fixed-tissue imaging methods offer greater scalability but only capture snapshots at single time points. Nucleic acid-based recording systems require cell lysis and sequencing, destroying spatial context. There exists an unmet need for systems that combine continuous temporal recording with scalable post-fixation readout while preserving cellular integrity.  

Prior attempts to create molecular recording devices have focused on DNA-based systems that modify nucleic acid sequences in response to biological signals. However, these systems require cell dissociation and sequencing for readout, preventing correlation of recorded data with spatial, structural, and morphological information in intact cells and tissues. Protein-based systems have been explored for information storage but have not achieved continuous, time-ordered recording of cellular states that can be read out through conventional microscopy techniques.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a protein-based cellular recording system termed Expression Recording Islands (XRIs). XRIs comprise self-assembling protein chains that grow continuously within living cells, incorporating differentially labeled subunits that reflect cellular states over time. The system includes:  

1) Engineered filament-forming protein monomers capable of longitudinal self-assembly into linear chains within cells;  
2) Insulator components that prevent lateral growth and promote ordered longitudinal assembly;  
3) Epitope-tagged variants that are expressed in response to specific cellular states or stimuli;  
4) Methods for reading out temporal information through immunostaining and imaging of fixed cells.  

Key advantages include:  
- Continuous recording of cellular states over days to weeks  
- Preservation of temporal order along the growing protein chain  
- Readout through standard immunostaining and microscopy  
- Minimal perturbation of normal cell physiology  
- Compatibility with intact cells and tissues

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Compositions and Components  

The XRI system comprises engineered self-assembling proteins derived from human-designed filament-forming proteins. The core component is the 1POK(E239Y) protein, which forms stable linear assemblies in mammalian cells. This scaffold is modified with:  
- Epitope tags (HA, FLAG, V5) for immunodetection  
- Insulator proteins (mEGFP, MBP) to prevent lateral growth  
- Cleavable linkers (P2A) for coordinated expression  

### Expression and Use of XRI Components  

XRIs function by continuous addition of protein monomers to growing filament ends. Constitutively expressed HA-tagged monomers provide a temporal scaffold, while stimulus-dependent expression of alternatively tagged monomers (e.g., FLAG or V5) creates time-resolved signatures along the chain. The relative density of different tags at positions along the filament encodes the timing and duration of cellular events.  

### Molecules and Compounds  

Key molecular components include:  
- Filament-forming protein monomers with engineered binding interfaces  
- Epitope tags for specific antibody recognition  
- Insulator domains that sterically block lateral interactions  
- Inducible expression systems (e.g., Cre/lox, chemical inducers)  

### Delivery of XRI-Based Recorder Components  

XRIs are delivered to cells via:  
- Viral vectors (AAV, lentivirus)  
- Plasmid transfection  
- Transgenic expression  

Optimal delivery methods maintain expression levels sufficient for filament growth without cellular toxicity.  

### Vectors, Plasmids, and Molecules  

Exemplary constructs include:  
- pAAV-UBC-XRI-HA: Constitutive expression of HA-tagged monomers  
- pAAV-UBC-FLEX-XRI-FLAG: Cre-dependent FLAG-tagged monomers  
- pAAV-cFos-XRI-V5: Activity-dependent V5-tagged monomers  

### Cells and Subjects  

The system is optimized for:  
- Primary neurons  
- Non-dividing cells  
- In vivo applications in rodent brains  

### Controls  

Essential controls include:  
- Untagged filament controls  
- Non-induced controls for inducible systems  
- Time calibration standards  

### Administration Means  

XRIs are administered via:  
- Direct intracranial injection  
- Systemic viral delivery  
- In vitro transduction  

### Assessment Methods  

Recording readout involves:  
- Immunostaining for epitope tags  
- Confocal microscopy  
- Quantitative image analysis of label distributions  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

**Chemically Induced Temporal Recording in Cultured Neurons**  

Primary mouse hippocampal neurons were co-transduced with:  
1) AAV-UBC-XRI-HA (constitutive expression)  
2) AAV-UBC-FLEX-XRI-FLAG (Cre-dependent)  
3) AAV-Syn-ERT2-iCre-ERT2 (4-OHT inducible)  

Cells were treated with 4-OHT at defined timepoints (days 1-6 post-transduction), then fixed at day 7. Immunostaining revealed polarized FLAG signal distributions corresponding to induction times, demonstrating successful temporal recording.

The complete system enables continuous molecular recording of cellular states with temporal resolution maintained through ordered protein assembly, readable through conventional microscopy techniques.
