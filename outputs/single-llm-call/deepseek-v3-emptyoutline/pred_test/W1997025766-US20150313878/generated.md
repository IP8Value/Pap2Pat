# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for diagnosing and treating prostate carcinoma. More specifically, the invention pertains to the identification and modulation of soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) as a therapeutic target in prostate cancer.  

Prostate carcinoma remains a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality in men. Current diagnostic and therapeutic approaches often lack specificity, leading to overdiagnosis and suboptimal treatment outcomes. The molecular mechanisms underlying prostate cancer progression are complex and involve dysregulated signaling pathways that promote uncontrolled cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis.  

Cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) is a ubiquitous second messenger that regulates diverse cellular processes, including proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Traditionally, cAMP synthesis was attributed to transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which are activated by G protein-coupled receptors. However, recent studies have identified soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) as an additional source of cAMP, localized intracellularly and regulated independently of tmACs. While the role of tmAC-derived cAMP in cancer has been explored, the contribution of sAC to tumorigenesis, particularly in prostate carcinoma, remains poorly understood.  

There is an unmet need for novel diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets that can specifically identify and treat aggressive prostate cancer. The present invention addresses this need by demonstrating that sAC is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma and plays a critical role in promoting cell proliferation through a unique signaling pathway involving EPAC (exchange protein activated by cAMP) and Rap1.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides methods and compositions for diagnosing and treating prostate carcinoma by targeting soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC). The invention is based on the discovery that sAC is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma compared to benign prostatic tissue and that inhibition of sAC suppresses tumor cell proliferation and induces apoptosis.  

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for diagnosing prostate carcinoma in a subject by detecting the level of sAC in a biological sample, wherein an elevated level of sAC compared to a control indicates the presence of prostate carcinoma. The method may involve immunohistochemical staining of prostate tissue using an anti-sAC antibody and quantifying the staining intensity using a histology scoring system (e.g., H-score).  

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating prostate carcinoma by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an sAC inhibitor, such as KH7 or a derivative thereof, to a subject in need thereof. The sAC inhibitor may be formulated as a pharmaceutical composition for oral, intravenous, or localized delivery.  

The invention further discloses that sAC promotes prostate cancer cell proliferation through an EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling pathway, independent of protein kinase A (PKA). Inhibition of sAC leads to cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase, downregulation of cyclin B1 and CDK1, and subsequent apoptosis. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for targeting sAC as a novel therapeutic strategy in prostate carcinoma.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma and plays a critical role in promoting tumor cell proliferation. The following sections describe the invention in detail, including experimental data supporting the utility of sAC as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target.  

### Example 1: Detection of sAC Overexpression in Prostate Carcinoma  

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded prostate tissue sections using a monoclonal anti-sAC antibody (R21-IHC). Tissue samples included benign prostatic glands and malignant prostate carcinoma. The staining intensity was quantified using an H-score, which accounts for the percentage of cells staining at weak (1+), moderate (2+), and strong (3+) intensities.  

The results demonstrated that prostate carcinoma glands exhibited significantly higher sAC expression compared to benign glands, with an average H-score of 189.2 versus 144.3, respectively. Notably, sAC upregulation was particularly pronounced at the leading edge of the tumor near the prostatic capsule, suggesting a role in tumor invasiveness. Subcellular localization analysis revealed diffuse cytoplasmic staining, with occasional nuclear or apical/luminal border staining, but no significant differences between benign and malignant samples.  

### Example 2: Inhibition of sAC Suppresses Prostate Cancer Cell Proliferation  

The functional role of sAC in prostate carcinoma was investigated using two human prostate cancer cell lines: androgen-sensitive LNCaP and androgen-insensitive PC3. Both cell lines showed high sAC expression compared to normal prostate epithelial cells (PNT2).  

Treatment with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 (20 µM) significantly reduced intracellular cAMP levels and suppressed cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner. The antiproliferative effect was accompanied by apoptosis, as evidenced by increased lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, caspase-3 cleavage, and a rise in the sub-G1 cell population. In contrast, the inactive KH7 analog (KH7.15) had no effect, confirming the specificity of KH7 for sAC.  

To further validate these findings, sAC expression was knocked down using siRNA or shRNA. Both methods achieved ≥80% reduction in sAC protein and replicated the effects of KH7, including suppressed proliferation, LDH release, and caspase-3 activation. Scrambled siRNA or shRNA controls had no effect, ruling out off-target effects.  

### Example 3: sAC Inhibition Induces G2/M Cell Cycle Arrest  

Cell cycle analysis revealed that sAC inhibition with KH7 or sAC knockdown caused a significant increase in the percentage of cells in G2 phase, indicating arrest at the G2/M checkpoint. Kinetic studies showed that G2 arrest occurred within 12 hours, whereas apoptosis (caspase-3 cleavage) was delayed until 24 hours, suggesting that cell cycle arrest precedes apoptosis.  

Western blot analysis demonstrated that sAC inhibition downregulated cyclin B1 and CDK1 (including phosphorylated CDK1), key regulators of the G2/M transition. In contrast, cyclins D1, D3, and E2 (G1/S regulators) were unaffected, highlighting the specificity of sAC for the G2/M checkpoint.  

### Example 4: sAC Signals Through EPAC/Rap1 Independently of PKA  

The downstream mechanisms of sAC-dependent proliferation were investigated. Unlike tmAC-derived cAMP, which typically activates PKA, sAC-derived cAMP did not influence proliferation through PKA. Treatment with PKA activators (N6-benzoyl-cAMP) or inhibitors (H-89, Rp-cAMP-S) had no effect on cell proliferation or the response to sAC inhibition.  

Instead, sAC signals through EPAC and Rap1. Inhibition of sAC depleted GTP-bound Rap1 and suppressed B-Raf phosphorylation, both downstream effectors of EPAC. Treatment with the EPAC-specific activator 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed the effects of sAC inhibition, restoring Rap1-GTP levels, cell proliferation, and cell cycle progression. These data establish EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf as the primary pathway mediating sAC's pro-proliferative effects in prostate carcinoma.
