## BACKGROUND  

The field of additive manufacturing has seen significant advances in recent years, particularly in the area of polymer composite materials. Conventional methods for manufacturing fiber-reinforced composites typically involve molding or layup processes that limit control over fiber orientation and placement within the final structure. While these methods can produce parts with good mechanical properties, they lack the ability to precisely control microstructure at the mesoscale level.  

Short fiber composites present particular challenges as the random orientation of fibers in traditional manufacturing processes leads to isotropic material properties that may not be optimal for specific applications requiring directional strength or conductivity. Previous attempts to align short fibers during additive manufacturing processes have been limited to low fiber volume fractions or low aspect ratio fibers, particularly when using thermoset resin systems suitable for high-performance applications.  

There exists an unmet need in the art for an additive manufacturing process capable of producing high-performance composite materials with controlled fiber alignment at high volume fractions, particularly using thermoset resin systems suitable for aerospace and other demanding applications. The present invention addresses this need through novel material formulations and processing techniques that enable unprecedented control over fiber orientation and composite microstructure.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides systems and methods for the additive manufacturing of high-performance, fiber-reinforced composite materials with controlled microstructure. The invention encompasses novel ink formulations comprising high aspect ratio carbon fibers dispersed in a modified thermoset resin system, along with specialized direct ink writing (DIW) processes that enable precise control over fiber alignment and placement within three-dimensional structures.  

Key aspects of the invention include a rheologically modified epoxy resin system containing colloidal silica nanoparticles that provide the necessary thixotropic properties for DIW processing while enabling high volume fractions of aligned carbon fibers. The resin system incorporates latent thermal cure catalysts that allow for extended working times at ambient temperatures while enabling rapid curing when heated.  

The DIW process of the invention produces composite materials with meso-structurally ordered arrangements of carbon fibers that exhibit unprecedented orthotropic physical properties. Through controlled shear alignment during extrusion, the invention achieves fiber alignment at volume fractions up to 8% and aspect ratios (l/d) greater than 50, with demonstrated capability to potentially reach 20% volume fractions. The resulting materials show significantly improved mechanical properties compared to conventional random fiber composites at equivalent fiber loadings, with demonstrated increases in Young's modulus of up to 37%.  

The invention further provides methods for modeling and optimizing the DIW process, including computational fluid dynamics simulations that predict fiber alignment behavior during extrusion. These modeling techniques enable the design of nozzle geometries that maximize fiber alignment while minimizing clogging or other processing defects.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF SPECIFIC EMBODIMENTS  

The present invention provides specific embodiments of novel ink formulations and additive manufacturing processes for producing high-performance fiber-reinforced composites. The ink formulations comprise an oligomerized bisphenol-F epoxy resin (BPF) modified with colloidal silica nanoparticles and loaded with high aspect ratio carbon fibers. The silica modification transforms the Newtonian resin into a thixotropic, non-Newtonian fluid suitable for DIW processing, while simultaneously providing sufficient viscous drag to maintain fiber suspension and enable alignment during extrusion.  

In preferred embodiments, the ink contains carbon fibers with lengths of approximately 600 μm and diameters of 6 μm (aspect ratio ≈ 100) at volume fractions between 3% and 8%. The resin system incorporates latent thermal cure catalysts, specifically boron trifluoride salts, at concentrations between 0.1% and 2% by weight. These catalysts enable extended working times at room temperature while allowing rapid curing when heated to temperatures between 70°C and 200°C.  

The DIW process employs specialized nozzle geometries that promote shear alignment of fibers during extrusion. Nozzle diameters typically range from 250 μm to 610 μm, with conical geometries that gradually decrease in diameter from the syringe barrel to the tip. This geometry promotes smooth flow transitions and minimizes fiber aggregation. The printing process maintains a nozzle height of approximately 0.75 times the nozzle diameter to ensure proper layer adhesion while preventing excessive spreading of extruded filaments.  

Post-processing involves a two-stage cure cycle, beginning with 12 hours at 80°C followed by 24 hours at 120°C. This thermal profile ensures complete crosslinking of the epoxy matrix while minimizing residual stresses in the printed structure. The resulting composites exhibit a highly aligned fiber microstructure with individual filament resolutions as fine as 250 μm.  

Mechanical testing demonstrates the superior properties of these aligned fiber composites compared to conventional random fiber materials. At equivalent fiber volume fractions, the aligned composites show increases in Young's modulus up to 37% in the alignment direction. The materials also exhibit significant orthotropy in electrical conductivity, with measured conductivity parallel to the fiber direction approximately 30,000 times greater than perpendicular conductivity.  

Computational modeling plays a key role in optimizing the manufacturing process. The invention employs two complementary modeling approaches: (1) high-resolution discrete fiber simulations using a Distributed Lagrange Multiplier technique to model individual fiber behavior during extrusion, and (2) global flow modeling using computational fluid dynamics to analyze bulk flow behavior and identify potential clogging points in nozzle geometries. These modeling techniques enable prediction and optimization of fiber alignment while minimizing processing defects.  

Alternative embodiments of the invention include variations in resin chemistry, fiber type and loading, and nozzle geometry. The resin system may utilize different epoxy formulations or alternative thermoset resins compatible with the DIW process. Fiber types may include other high-performance fibers such as glass, aramid, or ceramic fibers. Nozzle geometries may be optimized for specific fiber aspect ratios or volume fractions to maximize alignment while maintaining printability.  

The invention further encompasses composite structures with spatially varying fiber orientation and loading, enabled by the precise control offered by the DIW process. Such structures may include regions of differing mechanical properties or electrical conductivity tailored for specific functional requirements. Examples include parts with graded stiffness or thermal conductivity, or structures with designed electrical pathways for sensing or energy transmission applications.  

The detailed embodiments described herein demonstrate the capability to produce additively manufactured composite materials with unprecedented control over microstructure and properties. The combination of specialized material formulations and optimized manufacturing processes enables the production of high-performance composites suitable for demanding applications in aerospace, automotive, and other advanced engineering fields.
