# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of metabolic disorders, particularly insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for diagnosing, monitoring, and treating insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes by targeting nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) activity and its metabolic product 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA). The invention further provides biomarkers and therapeutic strategies based on the association between adipose tissue NNMT expression, circulating MNA levels, and insulin resistance.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention  

Insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes represent significant global health burdens, with increasing prevalence linked to obesity and sedentary lifestyles. Despite extensive research, the molecular mechanisms underlying the development of insulin resistance remain incompletely understood, limiting the development of targeted therapies. Current diagnostic methods for insulin resistance, such as the hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp or homeostatic model assessment (HOMA), are either invasive or lack sufficient sensitivity for early detection. Moreover, existing therapeutic approaches often fail to address the root causes of insulin resistance, focusing instead on symptom management.  

A critical gap in the field is the lack of reliable biomarkers that can predict insulin resistance development or monitor therapeutic responses. While various circulating factors have been proposed as biomarkers, none have shown consistent correlation with tissue-specific molecular changes driving insulin resistance. Additionally, the relationship between nicotinamide metabolism and insulin sensitivity has been observed but not mechanistically explained, leaving this pathway unexplored for therapeutic intervention.  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the need for:  
1) Novel biomarkers that accurately reflect tissue-level changes associated with insulin resistance  
2) Diagnostic methods for early detection of insulin resistance development  
3) Therapeutic targets that directly modulate the molecular pathways underlying insulin resistance  
4) Monitoring tools to assess treatment efficacy in improving insulin sensitivity  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides solutions to the aforementioned technical problems through the discovery that nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) expression in adipose tissue and circulating levels of its metabolic product 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) are significantly elevated in insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. The invention demonstrates that these markers correlate with the degree of insulin resistance and respond to interventions that improve insulin sensitivity.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1) The use of adipose tissue NNMT expression levels as a diagnostic marker for insulin resistance  
2) The measurement of circulating MNA concentrations as a biomarker for insulin resistance and NNMT activity  
3) Methods for monitoring therapeutic interventions by tracking changes in NNMT expression or MNA levels  
4) Therapeutic strategies targeting NNMT activity to improve insulin sensitivity  
5) Pharmaceutical compositions comprising NNMT inhibitors for treating insulin resistance  

The invention is based on clinical evidence showing that adipose tissue NNMT expression is approximately 2-fold higher in individuals with type 2 diabetes compared to non-diabetic controls, and that this elevation correlates with the severity of insulin resistance. Furthermore, interventions that improve insulin sensitivity, including exercise and bariatric surgery, are associated with significant reductions in adipose tissue NNMT expression and circulating MNA levels.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:  

"Nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT)" refers to the enzyme encoded by the NNMT gene in humans, which catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to nicotinamide, producing 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) and S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH).  

"1-methylnicotinamide (MNA)" refers to the product of NNMT-mediated methylation of nicotinamide, which may circulate in bodily fluids including blood, plasma, or serum.  

"Insulin resistance" refers to a physiological condition in which cells fail to respond normally to the hormone insulin, typically characterized by reduced glucose uptake in response to insulin stimulation.  

"Adipose tissue" refers to body fat, including both subcutaneous adipose tissue (located beneath the skin) and omental adipose tissue (located in the abdominal cavity surrounding internal organs).  

"Biomarker" refers to a measurable indicator of a biological state or condition, which may be used for diagnostic, prognostic, or monitoring purposes.  

### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The invention encompasses several embodiments for diagnosing, monitoring, and treating insulin resistance and related metabolic disorders:  

1. Diagnostic Methods:  
   - Measuring NNMT expression levels in adipose tissue biopsies  
   - Determining circulating MNA concentrations in blood, plasma, or serum  
   - Combined assessment of NNMT expression and MNA levels for improved diagnostic accuracy  

2. Monitoring Methods:  
   - Tracking changes in NNMT expression following therapeutic interventions  
   - Monitoring MNA level variations during treatment  
   - Correlating biomarker changes with improvements in insulin sensitivity  

3. Therapeutic Methods:  
   - Administration of NNMT inhibitors to reduce MNA production  
   - Modulation of NNMT expression through pharmacological or genetic means  
   - Combination therapies targeting both NNMT activity and other aspects of insulin resistance  

4. Pharmaceutical Compositions:  
   - Formulations containing NNMT inhibitors  
   - Compositions for targeted delivery to adipose tissue  
   - Combined preparations with other antidiabetic agents  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific applications of the invention without limiting its scope:  

Example 1: Diagnostic Application  
A clinical study was conducted with 199 individuals undergoing abdominal surgery, including 88 with type 2 diabetes. NNMT mRNA expression was measured in omental and subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies using quantitative RT-PCR. Results showed approximately 2-fold higher NNMT expression in diabetic patients compared to non-diabetic controls (p<0.01), establishing NNMT as a tissue biomarker for insulin resistance.  

Example 2: Monitoring Therapeutic Response  
In an exercise intervention study with 60 participants (20 each with normal glucose tolerance, impaired glucose tolerance, and type 2 diabetes), a 12-week exercise program significantly reduced subcutaneous adipose tissue NNMT expression in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance (-21%, p<0.001) and type 2 diabetes (-16%, p<0.001). These changes correlated with improvements in insulin sensitivity (r=0.78, p<0.001), demonstrating the utility of NNMT as a monitoring biomarker.  

Example 3: Therapeutic Intervention  
In a bariatric surgery cohort (n=55), NNMT expression in adipose tissue decreased significantly following surgery (subcutaneous: -50%, omental: -41%, p<0.001). Parallel reductions in plasma MNA concentrations (-24%, p=0.001) were observed, suggesting that interventions reducing NNMT activity may improve metabolic parameters.  

Example 4: Biomarker Correlation  
Analysis of 85 individuals undergoing hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp testing revealed that omental adipose tissue NNMT expression was 3.5-fold higher in insulin-resistant individuals (p<0.001). A strong correlation was found between plasma MNA levels and the degree of insulin resistance (r=0.44, p=0.01), supporting the use of MNA as a circulating biomarker.  

The examples demonstrate that measurement of adipose tissue NNMT expression and circulating MNA levels provides valuable diagnostic and monitoring tools for insulin resistance, and that targeting NNMT activity represents a novel therapeutic approach for metabolic disorders.
