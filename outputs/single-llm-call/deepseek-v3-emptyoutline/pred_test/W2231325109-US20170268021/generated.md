# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to cellular reprogramming and regenerative medicine. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for directly converting somatic cells into induced dopaminergic neural progenitor cells (iDPs) through ectopic expression of transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The resulting iDPs exhibit midbrain identity, dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential, and functional integration capacity when transplanted into animal models of Parkinson's disease. The invention further encompasses methods for expanding iDP populations through conditional expression of L-Myc and their therapeutic use in treating neurodegenerative disorders characterized by dopaminergic neuron loss.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the loss of dopaminergic (DA) neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta. Current treatments provide symptomatic relief but fail to halt disease progression. Cell replacement therapy using stem cell-derived DA neurons has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy. However, existing approaches relying on embryonic stem cells (ESCs) or induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) face challenges including low differentiation efficiency, tumorigenic potential, and incomplete functional integration.  

Direct lineage conversion offers an alternative approach by bypassing the pluripotent state. Previous attempts to generate induced neural progenitor cells (iNPCs) using factors such as Brn2, Sox2, TLX, Bmi1 and c-Myc yielded cells with limited DA differentiation capacity (<5% TH+ neurons). While Foxa2 and Lmx1a are known regulators of midbrain DA development, their role in cellular reprogramming remained unexplored. There exists an unmet need for methods to efficiently produce stable, expandable, and safe dopaminergic neural progenitors with high differentiation potential for therapeutic applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention overcomes limitations of prior approaches by providing a novel combination of transcription factors (Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2) that directly converts somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) with midbrain identity. The iDPs exhibit:  

1) Specific expression of dopaminergic progenitor markers (Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Msx1, Ngn2, Otx2, Mash1, Pitx3 and Nkx6.1)  
2) Neuronal lineage restriction with minimal glial differentiation potential  
3) High efficiency differentiation into functional DA neurons (>90% TH+) independent of SHH/FGF8 patterning  
4) Mature electrophysiological properties including voltage-gated currents and action potentials  
5) Safe expansion capacity through conditional L-Myc expression  
6) Functional integration in MPTP mouse models with partial recovery of motor deficits  

The invention further provides methods for generating, expanding, and differentiating iDPs, as well as their use in cell therapy for Parkinson's disease and related disorders.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein:  
"Direct reprogramming" refers to conversion of a somatic cell directly into another cell type without passing through a pluripotent state.  
"iDPs" means induced dopaminergic progenitor cells generated by the methods of this invention.  
"Midbrain identity" denotes expression of molecular markers characteristic of mesencephalic floor plate-derived dopaminergic progenitors.  
"Neuronal lineage restriction" indicates commitment to neuronal differentiation with minimal potential for glial differentiation.  

### Somatic Cells  

The invention utilizes somatic cells as starting material, preferably fibroblasts isolated from skin biopsies. Fibroblasts may be obtained from various sources including adult dermis, embryonic tissue, or genetically modified animals (e.g., Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice). The cells are cultured in standard fibroblast medium (DMEM with 10% FBS) and used within passages 2-5 to avoid replicative senescence.  

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)  

Direct conversion is achieved through ectopic expression of three transcription factors: Brn2 (POU domain transcription factor), Sox2 (SRY-box containing transcription factor), and Foxa2 (Forkhead box protein A2). These factors are delivered via retroviral vectors (e.g., pMXs-based) packaged in Plat-E cells. Fibroblasts are infected with viral supernatants containing all three factors in the presence of polybrene (10 μg/ml).  

The reprogramming process occurs in neural progenitor medium (NeuroCult NSC Basal Medium with EGF/bFGF) over 9-14 days, with colony formation visible by day 14. Successful reprogramming is indicated by Nestin-EGFP expression (in transgenic systems) and morphological changes from fibroblastic to neural progenitor-like appearance.  

### iDP Production  

The resulting iDP colonies exhibit:  
- Stable expansion capacity (>20 passages)  
- Expression of neural progenitor markers (Sox1, PAX6, ZBTB16, Sox3, CD133, Nestin)  
- Specific dopaminergic progenitor markers (Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Msx1, Ngn2, Otx2)  
- Minimal expression of forebrain markers (FoxG1, GSX2, Nkx2.1)  
- Neuronal lineage restriction (PSA-NCAM+, DCX+, Corin+)  

iDPs can be clonally expanded from single cells and maintain their properties through multiple passages.  

### Human iDP Upon Ectopic Expression of Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2  

While the examples focus on mouse cells, the same principle applies to human cells. Human fibroblasts can be converted into iDPs using the same combination of factors (Brn2, Sox2, Foxa2) delivered via appropriate human-compatible vectors (e.g., lentivirus). The resulting human iDPs would similarly exhibit midbrain dopaminergic progenitor characteristics and high-efficiency differentiation into functional DA neurons.  

## DISCUSSION  

The invention provides several advantages over existing approaches:  

1) **Lineage Restriction**: Unlike conventional iNPCs, iDPs are committed to the dopaminergic neuronal lineage with minimal glial potential, reducing risks of inappropriate differentiation after transplantation.  

2) **Midbrain Specification**: Foxa2 confers specific midbrain identity, enabling generation of authentic mesencephalic DA neurons rather than generic neuronal types.  

3) **Differentiation Efficiency**: The >90% TH+ differentiation efficiency surpasses current methods and occurs without requiring SHH/FGF8 patterning.  

4) **Functional Maturity**: iDP-derived neurons exhibit mature electrophysiological properties including action potentials and neurotransmitter release machinery.  

5) **Safety**: Conditional L-Myc expression enables controlled expansion without tumorigenesis, and transplanted cells show no evidence of overgrowth at 6 weeks post-engraftment.  

The invention establishes a platform for patient-specific PD modeling and therapy by enabling direct conversion of patient fibroblasts into transplantable dopaminergic progenitors. The methods are scalable for clinical translation and avoid ethical concerns associated with embryonic stem cells.  

## Methods  

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming  

Fibroblasts are isolated from Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice by standard protocols and cultured in DMEM/10% FBS. For retroviral production, Plat-E cells are transfected with pMXs vectors encoding Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2 using Lipofectamine LTX. Viral supernatants are collected at 48-72 hours post-transfection, filtered (0.45 μm), and applied to fibroblasts in the presence of polybrene (10 μg/ml). After overnight infection, medium is changed to neural progenitor medium (NeuroCult NSC Basal with EGF/bFGF). Emerging colonies are monitored by EGFP fluorescence and isolated at day 14 for expansion.  

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR  

Total RNA is extracted with TRIzol/RNeasy kits and reverse transcribed using Roche cDNA synthesis kit. qPCR is performed with SYBR Green Master Mix using primer sets specific for neural progenitor markers (Sox1, PAX6), dopaminergic markers (TH, Pitx3), and glial markers (GFAP, Olig2). Cycling conditions: 50°C for 2 min, 95°C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 sec, gene-specific annealing for 15 sec, and 72°C for 1 min. Data are normalized to housekeeping genes and analyzed by ΔΔCt method.  

### Immunocytochemistry  

Cells are fixed in 4% formaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100, and blocked with 2% BSA. Primary antibodies (anti-TH, anti-βIII-tubulin, anti-GFAP) are applied overnight at 4°C, followed by appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies. Images are acquired using confocal microscopy (Zeiss 710) and analyzed for marker co-expression.  

### Differentiation  

For neuronal differentiation, iDPs are plated on poly-L-ornithine/laminin in DMEM/F12 with N2/B27 supplements, BDNF (10 ng/ml), GDNF (10 ng/ml), cAMP (1 mM), and ascorbic acid (0.2 mM) for 4 weeks. Medium is changed every 3-4 days. For glial differentiation, cells are exposed to 10% FBS (astrocytes) or PDGF/forskolin followed by T3 (oligodendrocytes).  

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents  

Whole-cell patch clamp recordings are performed using Axopatch 200B amplifier. For action potentials, cells are current-clamped and depolarized with 20-100 pA injections. For voltage-gated currents, cells are held at -80 mV and stepped to test potentials (-70 to +70 mV). Data are sampled at 10 kHz and analyzed using pClamp software.  

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests  

MPTP-treated mice are assessed on accelerating rotarod (2-12 rpm) pre- and post-transplantation. Performance is measured as latency to fall over 90 sec trials, with weekly averages normalized to baseline. Statistical comparisons are made between iDP-grafted and control groups.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Data are expressed as mean ± SD and analyzed by ANOVA with Tukey's post-test (p<0.05 considered significant). All experiments include at least triplicate biological replicates.  

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology  

iDPs are labeled with GFP-expressing lentivirus and injected into striatum (0.25 million cells/site) of MPTP-treated mice at coordinates: 0.22 mm posterior to bregma, 3.25 mm lateral, 2.9 mm depth. After 6 weeks, brains are sectioned and analyzed for TH immunoreactivity and GFP+ cell survival.
