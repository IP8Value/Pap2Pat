# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Traditional two-dimensional (2D) cell culture systems, while widely used in biological research, fail to accurately replicate the complex three-dimensional (3D) architecture of the extracellular matrix (ECM) found in vivo. Cells cultured in 2D environments exhibit unnatural morphology, proliferation rates, gene expression patterns, and drug sensitivities due to the homogeneous distribution of nutrients and growth factors. While animal models provide more physiologically relevant 3D environments, they are costly, time-consuming, and raise ethical concerns.  

Hydrogel scaffolds have emerged as promising alternatives for 3D cell culture due to their high water content and structural similarity to natural ECM. Natural hydrogels derived from collagen, fibrin, hyaluronic acid, and other biological sources offer biocompatibility but suffer from batch-to-batch variability. Synthetic hydrogels provide consistency but often lack the structural and mechanical properties required to mimic native ECM. Peptide-based hydrogels represent an attractive middle ground, combining the biocompatibility of natural materials with the tunability of synthetic polymers.  

A critical limitation of existing hydrogel systems is the harsh gelation conditions required, such as extreme pH or temperature changes, which compromise cell viability during encapsulation. Additionally, isolating cells from hydrogel matrices typically involves reversing these harsh conditions, further threatening cell survival. There remains an unmet need for a hydrogel system that enables gentle cell encapsulation and safe, efficient cell isolation while maintaining physiological relevance for drug testing and other biomedical applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses a novel peptide-based hydrogel system for three-dimensional (3D) cell culture that overcomes the limitations of existing technologies. The hydrogel is formed by a self-assembling peptide, designated h9e, which undergoes gelation under physiological conditions (neutral pH, 37°C) when mixed with standard cell culture media such as Minimum Essential Medium (MEM).  

Key advantages of the h9e hydrogel system include:  
1. **Mild Encapsulation**: Cells can be encapsulated during gelation without exposure to extreme pH or temperature changes, ensuring high viability.  
2. **Tunable Mechanical Properties**: The storage modulus (G′) can be precisely controlled (100–700 Pa) by adjusting peptide concentration (1–3 mM).  
3. **Reversible Gel-Sol Transition**: The hydrogel exhibits unique shear-thinning and self-recovery properties, allowing it to be liquefied by mechanical disruption (e.g., pipetting) and subsequently reassembled.  
4. **Facile Cell Isolation**: Cells can be safely isolated by diluting the hydrogel with culture medium and centrifuging at 4°C, eliminating the need for harsh chemical or enzymatic treatments.  
5. **Physiological Relevance**: The hydrogel supports the formation of tumor-like cell clusters with morphology and drug response profiles more representative of in vivo conditions compared to 2D cultures.  

The invention further encompasses methods for drug testing using the h9e hydrogel system, including evaluating drug efficacy, diffusion kinetics, and dose-response relationships in a 3D environment.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Composition and Formation of the h9e Hydrogel  
The h9e peptide is synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry. The lyophilized peptide is dissolved in 100 mM sodium bicarbonate (pH 7–8) to a stock concentration of 10 mM. For hydrogel formation, the peptide solution is mixed with cell culture medium (e.g., MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum) at room temperature, resulting in final peptide concentrations of 1–3 mM (0.17–0.51% w/v). Gelation occurs within 15 minutes, forming a nanofibrous network with fiber diameters of approximately 20 nm, as confirmed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM).  

### Rheological Properties  
The hydrogel exhibits viscoelastic behavior characterized by a storage modulus (G′) exceeding the loss modulus (G″), indicating solid-like properties. The G′ scales with peptide concentration, ranging from ~100 Pa (1 mM) to ~700 Pa (3 mM). Under shear strain (500%), the hydrogel transitions to a liquid state (G′ < 0.2 Pa) but rapidly recovers its original stiffness (73–100% recovery within 10 minutes) upon cessation of shear. This property enables repeated pipetting without permanent damage to the hydrogel structure.  

Temperature sensitivity studies reveal reversible changes in stiffness, with G′ increasing 2–3-fold at 50°C compared to 4°C. This thermal response facilitates cell isolation: the hydrogel weakens at 4°C, allowing cells to be pelleted by centrifugation, while remaining stable at 37°C for long-term culture.  

### 3D Cell Culture Applications  
Cells (e.g., MCF-7 breast cancer cells) are encapsulated by mixing a cell suspension with the peptide solution prior to gelation. The hydrogel supports homogeneous cell distribution and the formation of tumor-like spheroids (>50 µm diameter) within 5–7 days. Cells exhibit rounded, clustered morphologies distinct from the flattened appearance of 2D cultures, as confirmed by confocal microscopy and SEM.  

Proliferation rates in 3D are slower than in 2D but can be modulated by peptide concentration (e.g., faster migration and division in 1 mM vs. 3 mM hydrogels). Cell viability remains high (>90%) throughout culture and after isolation, demonstrating the system’s biocompatibility.  

### Drug Testing Platform  
The hydrogel permits efficient drug diffusion, as demonstrated by the dose-dependent cytotoxicity of cisplatin. Three administration methods are disclosed:  
1. **Top Surface Delivery**: Drug is added to medium overlaying the hydrogel.  
2. **Transwell Diffusion**: Drug is applied to both top and bottom chambers of a transwell system.  
3. **Pre-Mixed Encapsulation**: Drug is incorporated during gelation for uniform distribution.  

The pre-mixed method yields the highest efficacy (85% reduction in viability at 10 days), highlighting the system’s utility for evaluating drug penetration kinetics. IC50 values (e.g., 31.25 µM for cisplatin) align with literature reports, validating physiological relevance.  

### Cell Isolation and Downstream Analysis  
Cells are isolated by diluting the hydrogel 15-fold with cold MEM, pipetting to liquefy the matrix, and centrifuging at 4°C. Recovered cells retain viability and are suitable for proteomic analysis (e.g., Western blotting for apoptotic markers) or functional assays.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Hydrogel Formation and Characterization  
A 10 mM h9e peptide solution (pH 7.5) was mixed with MEM + 10% FBS to final concentrations of 1, 2, and 3 mM. Rheological analysis at 37°C showed G′ values of 100, 400, and 700 Pa, respectively, with gelation complete within 15 minutes. SEM revealed nanofiber diameters of 20 nm and tighter mesh spacing at higher peptide concentrations.  

#### Example 2: 3D Culture of MCF-7 Cells  
MCF-7 cells (2.8 × 10⁵/well) were encapsulated in 1–3 mM hydrogels. After 7 days, cells formed compact spheroids (50–200 µm diameter) with viability >90%. Actin staining showed peripheral localization in 3D clusters versus elongated fibers in 2D cultures.  

#### Example 3: Cisplatin Efficacy Testing  
Cells cultured for 5 days in 3 mM hydrogel were treated with 40 µM cisplatin. Viability decreased by 85% (pre-mixed) vs. 40% (top surface) at 10 days, demonstrating method-dependent drug diffusion. IC50 was 31.25 µM, consistent with in vivo data.  

#### Example 4: Cell Isolation and Recovery  
Hydrogels (3 mM) were diluted 15-fold with cold MEM, pipetted to liquefy, and centrifuged (2000 rpm, 5 min, 4°C). Cell recovery exceeded 95% with no loss in viability. Western blotting of isolated cells confirmed detection of apoptotic markers (cleaved caspase-3) post-treatment.  

The invention is not limited to the examples described, and modifications (e.g., alternative peptide sequences, cell types, or culture media) are within the scope of the claims.
