# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions comprising marine-derived omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) containing long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), particularly eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). More specifically, the invention encompasses methods of obtaining, formulating, and utilizing these marine n-3 PLs for nutritional, pharmaceutical, and cosmetic applications. The marine n-3 PLs of the invention demonstrate superior bioavailability and tissue distribution compared to triglyceride (TG)-bound omega-3 fatty acids, making them particularly effective for enhancing brain function, cardiovascular health, and metabolic regulation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Omega-3 fatty acids, particularly EPA and DHA, have been extensively studied for their health benefits, including roles in cardiovascular protection, cognitive function, and inflammatory regulation. Traditionally, these fatty acids have been administered in triglyceride (TG) or ethyl ester forms. However, emerging evidence indicates that omega-3 fatty acids bound to phospholipids (PLs) exhibit enhanced bioavailability and tissue-specific delivery, particularly to the brain, liver, and other critical organs.  

Marine organisms such as krill, fish roe, and certain fish species are rich sources of n-3 PLs, where EPA and DHA are predominantly esterified to phosphatidylcholine (PC) and other phospholipid classes. Unlike vegetable-derived PLs, marine PLs contain long-chain n-3 PUFAs, which confer unique structural and functional advantages. Despite their potential, marine PLs have not been fully exploited due to challenges in extraction, stabilization, and formulation.  

There remains a need for optimized compositions and methods to harness the full therapeutic and nutritional potential of marine n-3 PLs. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel formulations, extraction techniques, and applications that maximize the health benefits of these compounds.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides marine-derived omega-3 phospholipid (n-3 PL) compositions, methods of production, and applications thereof. The compositions are characterized by a high content of EPA and DHA esterified to phospholipids, particularly phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), and other glycerophospholipids.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Source Materials**: Utilization of marine sources such as krill, fish roe, and fish by-products, which are enriched in n-3 PLs.  
2. **Extraction and Purification**: Methods for isolating n-3 PLs while preserving their structural integrity and bioactivity.  
3. **Formulations**: Pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, and cosmetic formulations incorporating marine n-3 PLs for enhanced delivery and efficacy.  
4. **Health Applications**: Use of n-3 PLs for improving cognitive function, cardiovascular health, metabolic regulation, and inflammatory responses.  

The invention further demonstrates that n-3 PLs exhibit superior tissue distribution compared to TG-bound omega-3s, particularly in the brain, liver, and adipose tissue. This property makes them particularly valuable for neurological and metabolic disorders.  

## DEFINITIONS  

- **Marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs)**: Phospholipids derived from marine organisms, wherein the fatty acid moieties comprise long-chain omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), particularly EPA and DHA.  
- **Phosphatidylcholine (PC)**: A phospholipid class with a choline headgroup, constituting the predominant phospholipid in marine n-3 PL sources.  
- **Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE)**: A phospholipid class with an ethanolamine headgroup, often the second most abundant phospholipid in marine sources.  
- **Krill oil (KO)**: Oil extracted from Antarctic krill (*Euphausia superba*), rich in n-3 PLs, primarily in the form of PC.  
- **Fish roe**: Eggs and ovaries of fish, particularly herring, salmon, and mackerel, which are high in n-3 PL content.  
- **Bioavailability**: The proportion of a nutrient that enters circulation and is available for physiological use. Marine n-3 PLs exhibit higher bioavailability than TG-bound omega-3s.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Sources and Composition of Marine n-3 PLs  

The invention utilizes marine sources rich in n-3 PLs, including:  
- **Krill Oil**: KO contains approximately 40% phospholipids, predominantly PC, with EPA and DHA esterified at high levels.  
- **Fish Roe**: Roe from herring, salmon, and mackerel contains 38–75% phospholipids, with PC as the major class.  
- **Fish By-Products**: Processing by-products from fish such as salmon and tuna provide additional sources of n-3 PLs.  

The phospholipid composition varies by source, but PC and PE are consistently the dominant classes, with minor contributions from phosphatidylinositol (PI), phosphatidylserine (PS), and lysophosphatidylcholine (lyso-PC).  

### Extraction and Purification Methods  

The invention encompasses methods for isolating n-3 PLs from marine biomass, including:  
1. **Solvent Extraction**: Using ethanol or hexane to separate phospholipids from triglycerides and other lipids.  
2. **Supercritical Fluid Extraction**: Employing CO₂ under high pressure to obtain phospholipid-rich fractions.  
3. **Chromatographic Purification**: Further refining phospholipid fractions using column chromatography to isolate specific classes (e.g., PC, PE).  

Stabilization techniques, such as nitrogen flushing and antioxidant addition, are employed to prevent oxidation of PUFAs during processing.  

### Formulations and Delivery Systems  

The invention provides multiple formulations for marine n-3 PLs, including:  
1. **Nutraceutical Compositions**: Softgel capsules, powders, or liquids containing standardized doses of n-3 PLs.  
2. **Pharmaceutical Formulations**: Liposomal or micellar delivery systems for enhanced bioavailability.  
3. **Cosmetic Applications**: Emulsions and creams incorporating n-3 PLs for skin hydration and anti-aging effects.  

### Mechanisms of Enhanced Bioavailability  

Marine n-3 PLs exhibit superior tissue uptake due to:  
1. **Digestive Efficiency**: Phospholipids are hydrolyzed by pancreatic phospholipase A2, yielding lysophospholipids that are readily absorbed.  
2. **Micelle Formation**: Unlike TGs, PLs do not require bile salts for micelle formation, facilitating intestinal uptake.  
3. **Tissue-Specific Delivery**: PL-derived EPA and DHA are preferentially incorporated into brain, liver, and adipose tissue.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Extraction of n-3 PLs from Krill Oil  

Krill biomass was subjected to supercritical CO₂ extraction, yielding an oil fraction containing 40% phospholipids. The phospholipid fraction was further purified by ethanol fractionation to isolate PC-enriched n-3 PLs. The final product contained 50% EPA and DHA by weight, with PC constituting 80% of the phospholipid content.  

### Example 2: Formulation of a Nutraceutical Softgel  

A softgel formulation was prepared by blending krill-derived n-3 PLs (500 mg per capsule) with tocopherols as antioxidants. The formulation demonstrated stability over 24 months under accelerated aging conditions.  

### Example 3: Comparative Bioavailability Study  

A randomized trial compared the tissue distribution of DHA from krill oil (PL-bound) versus fish oil (TG-bound) in rats. After 8 weeks, the krill oil group exhibited 96% higher DHA incorporation in heart tissue and 47% higher levels in the liver compared to the fish oil group.  

### Example 4: Cognitive Benefits of n-3 PLs  

A murine model of aging was supplemented with fish roe-derived n-3 PLs. Maze tests revealed a 30% improvement in learning capacity compared to controls, correlating with elevated hippocampal DHA levels.  

### Example 5: Metabolic Regulation in Obesity  

Obese Zucker rats were administered KO or FO for 12 weeks. The KO group showed significant reductions in hepatic triglycerides (45%) and plasma cholesterol (25%), alongside improved insulin sensitivity.  

### Example 6: Human Clinical Trial for Cardiovascular Health  

A 12-week trial in hyperlipidemic patients demonstrated that 1 g/day of n-3 PLs from salmon roe increased HDL by 15% and reduced LDL by 20%, with no adverse effects reported.  

### Example 7: Cosmetic Application for Skin Hydration  

A topical emulsion containing 2% n-3 PLs was tested in a human panel. After 4 weeks, skin hydration improved by 35%, with reduced transepidermal water loss.  

### Example 8: Stabilization of Marine n-3 PLs  

A lyophilized powder of n-3 PLs was prepared using cryoprotectants, maintaining >90% EPA/DHA content after 18 months at room temperature.
