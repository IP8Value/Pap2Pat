# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics and prognostics, particularly to methods and systems for evaluating the severity of critical illness in patients. More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel biomarker system combining blood adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels and serum lactate concentrations to form an ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES), which serves as a real-time prognostic indicator for patients in intensive care units (ICUs). The invention further encompasses improved methods for extracting and measuring ATP from blood samples, as well as clinical applications of the A-LES for predicting patient outcomes and monitoring disease progression.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

### PRIOR ART DOCUMENTS  

Current clinical practice utilizes several scoring systems to assess the severity of illness in ICU patients, including the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) II score and the Simplified Acute Physiology Score (SAPS) II. These systems, while widely adopted, suffer from significant limitations. The APACHE II score requires extensive data collection, including physiological parameters, laboratory values, and chronic health evaluations, making it time-consuming to calculate. Furthermore, inter-examiner variability and the unavailability of certain automated data contribute to inconsistencies in scoring. As a result, these scoring systems are not universally employed in daily ICU practice.  

ATP serves as the primary energy currency in biological systems, playing a central role in cellular metabolism, viability, and function. Recent studies have identified ATP release from damaged cells as an alarm signal (alarmin), which triggers immune responses. However, extracellular ATP is rapidly degraded, making its direct measurement in serum unreliable. Consequently, intracellular ATP levels in blood cells have emerged as a more stable indicator of cellular energy status.  

Existing methods for ATP extraction from biological samples rely on chaotropic reagents such as trichloroacetic acid and perchloric acid. While effective for low-protein samples, these methods suffer from poor efficiency in high-protein tissues due to ATP co-precipitation with insoluble proteins. This limitation has hindered accurate ATP measurement in clinical settings.  

Lactate accumulation, resulting from impaired mitochondrial ATP production, is a well-established marker of tissue hypoxia and metabolic dysfunction. Hyperlactatemia is common in ICU patients and correlates with organ failure and increased mortality. However, lactate measurement alone lacks specificity for predicting clinical outcomes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  

The present invention addresses several unmet needs in critical care medicine. First, it provides a rapid, reliable alternative to complex scoring systems like APACHE II for assessing illness severity. Second, it overcomes the technical challenges of accurate ATP measurement in blood samples. Third, it establishes a novel combined biomarker (A-LES) that offers superior prognostic value compared to existing parameters. Finally, the invention enables real-time monitoring of patient status throughout the ICU stay, facilitating timely clinical interventions.  

### Means to Solve the Object  

The invention solves these problems through several key innovations:  

1. A novel phenol-based ATP extraction method that prevents protein co-precipitation and enables accurate measurement of blood ATP levels, even in samples with high protein content.  

2. A standardized protocol for measuring both blood ATP and serum lactate concentrations, including normalization of ATP values to total hemoglobin content to account for variations in red blood cell counts.  

3. The creation of the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES), calculated as the ratio of serum lactate concentration (mM) to blood ATP concentration (mM), which serves as a sensitive prognostic indicator.  

4. Establishment of reference ranges for blood ATP and A-LES in healthy populations across different age groups, enabling meaningful interpretation of patient values.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention provides multiple clinical benefits:  

1. The A-LES score demonstrates comparable predictive value to APACHE II for mortality risk assessment at ICU admission, while being simpler and faster to calculate.  

2. Unlike static scoring systems, A-LES enables dynamic monitoring of patient status throughout the ICU stay, with values <5.49 associated with survival and values >20 predicting poor outcomes.  

3. The phenol-based ATP extraction method provides more accurate and reproducible measurements than conventional techniques, particularly in critically ill patients who often have abnormal blood composition.  

4. The combined evaluation of ATP (reflecting cellular energy status) and lactate (indicating metabolic stress) offers superior prognostic information compared to either parameter alone.  

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention is implemented through the following steps:  

1. Blood Sample Collection: Blood is collected from patients via arterial, central venous, or peripheral venous access using standard anticoagulant tubes (e.g., EDTA or heparin). For ICU patients, samples are typically drawn from existing arterial lines to minimize additional procedures.  

2. ATP Extraction: A precisely measured blood aliquot (typically 0.1 mL) is added to 1.3 mL of Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol (phenol-TE) extraction reagent. The mixture is vigorously shaken for 20 seconds to ensure complete cell lysis and ATP release, then stored at -20°C until analysis.  

3. ATP Measurement: After thawing, samples are centrifuged (10,000 × g, 5 minutes at 4°C) to separate phases. The aqueous phase is diluted (typically 10,000-fold) and mixed with luciferin/luciferase reagent. Bioluminescence is measured using a luminometer, with ATP concentration calculated from a standard curve.  

4. Lactate Measurement: Serum lactate concentration is determined using standard blood gas analyzers or other clinical laboratory methods.  

5. A-LES Calculation: The A-LES score is computed as [serum lactate (mM)] / [blood ATP (mM)]. For enhanced accuracy, ATP values may be normalized to total hemoglobin content (ATP/tHb ratio) to account for variations in red blood cell counts due to transfusion or anemia.  

6. Interpretation: A-LES values are compared to established reference ranges:  
   - Healthy individuals: <2.00  
   - Moderately ill ICU patients: 2.00-20.00  
   - Severely ill/non-surviving patients: >20.00  

The method is particularly valuable for monitoring patients with septic shock, multiple organ failure, or other critical conditions where mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to disease progression. Serial measurements (e.g., every 6-24 hours) provide dynamic assessment of therapeutic response and prognosis.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Establishment of Reference Ranges:  
Blood samples were collected from 155 healthy individuals (ages 0-92 years). Using the phenol-TE extraction method, mean blood ATP concentration was 0.62 mM (SD 0.19), with no significant gender differences. Lactate levels averaged <1.19 mM, yielding A-LES values <2.00. These values serve as baseline references for interpreting patient data.  

### Example 2  

ICU Patient Monitoring:  
In 42 consecutive ICU patients, median admission values were:  
- ATP: 0.38 mM (IQR 0.29-0.56)  
- Lactate: 2.72 mM (IQR 1.92-5.78)  
- A-LES: 8.00 (IQR 4.74-13.37)  

Non-survivors showed significantly lower ATP (0.23 mM vs 0.52 mM in survivors) and higher A-LES (38.9 vs 2.7) at discharge (p<0.01). ROC analysis demonstrated similar predictive value for A-LES (AUC=0.71) and APACHE II (AUC=0.83).  

### Example 3  

Case Study - Septic Shock:  
A 58-year-old male with septic shock showed:  
- Day 1: ATP 0.29 mM, lactate 5.1 mM, A-LES 17.6  
- Day 3: ATP 0.18 mM, lactate 8.9 mM, A-LES 49.4  
The rising A-LES predicted poor outcome despite stable APACHE II scores (22-24), with the patient expiring on day 5. This demonstrates A-LES's superior sensitivity for detecting clinical deterioration compared to conventional scoring systems.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The invention has broad applications in healthcare:  

1. ICU Prognostics: Enables rapid risk stratification and continuous monitoring of critically ill patients.  

2. Therapeutic Monitoring: Guides treatment adjustments by providing real-time feedback on metabolic status.  

3. Clinical Trials: Serves as an objective biomarker for evaluating new critical care therapies.  

4. Point-of-Care Devices: The method can be adapted to automated analyzers for bedside testing.  

5. Companion Diagnostics: May guide personalized therapy in sepsis, shock, and other critical conditions.  

The invention represents a significant advance in critical care medicine by combining robust biochemical measurements with practical clinical utility, offering improvements over existing scoring systems in terms of speed, accuracy, and dynamic monitoring capability.
