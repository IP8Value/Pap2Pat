# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to environmental remediation technologies, and more specifically to systems and methods for removing heavy metal contaminants from soil. The invention combines a recirculating soil washing system with an electrochemical filtration device utilizing asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry (AACE) to achieve efficient and sustainable remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil.  

The disclosed system and method are particularly suited for treating soils contaminated with heavy metals such as copper (Cu), lead (Pb), and cadmium (Cd), among others. The invention addresses several critical limitations of existing remediation technologies by providing: (1) efficient removal of heavy metals through electrochemical reduction to their zero-valent metallic states, (2) recycling of chelating agents to minimize chemical consumption, (3) preservation of soil nutrients, and (4) elimination of secondary pollution. The technology is applicable to both agricultural and urban land remediation, as well as industrial waste treatment applications where heavy metal recovery is desired.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Soil contamination by heavy metals represents a significant global environmental challenge, particularly in areas with intensive industrial activity, mining operations, or historical pollution. Conventional remediation techniques face substantial limitations in efficiency, cost, and environmental impact.  

Existing soil washing techniques utilizing chelating agents such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) can extract heavy metals from soil but suffer from high chemical consumption, inability to treat the washing effluent, and excessive loss of soil nutrients. Adsorption-based methods using high-surface-area sorbents are limited by slow capture kinetics, low capacity, and concerns about long-term stability of immobilized heavy metals. Phytoremediation methods, while energy-efficient, require extremely long treatment times and risk secondary pollution through biomass accumulation.  

Electrochemical approaches have been explored for heavy metal remediation, but conventional direct current (DC) methods require impractically high voltages (~100 V/m) to maintain sufficient electroosmotic flow, suffer from low ion migration speeds in soil, and experience substantial energy losses due to water splitting at electrodes. These limitations have prevented widespread adoption of electrochemical remediation technologies.  

There exists an unmet need for a soil remediation system that can efficiently remove heavy metals while: (1) minimizing chemical consumption through recycling, (2) preventing soil nutrient loss, (3) avoiding secondary pollution, and (4) operating at practical energy requirements. The present invention addresses these needs through a novel combination of recirculating soil washing with an advanced electrochemical filtration system based on asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system and method for remediating heavy metal-contaminated soil through an integrated approach combining chemical washing with advanced electrochemical treatment. The system comprises two primary components: (1) a recirculating soil washing subsystem utilizing chelating agents such as EDTA, and (2) an asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry (AACE) filtration device for heavy metal recovery and chelating agent recycling.  

Key innovations of the invention include:  

The AACE filtration device employs specially designed amidoxime-functionalized porous carbon (Ami-PC) electrodes that provide both high conductivity and strong chelation sites for heavy metal cations. These electrodes are fabricated by coating carbon felt with a polyacrylonitrile (PAN) and activated carbon slurry, followed by hydrothermal reaction to substitute nitrile groups with amidoxime functional groups.  

The system applies an asymmetrical alternating voltage waveform (e.g., +5V for 0.5 ms followed by -10V for 2 ms) that enables efficient electrodeposition of heavy metals while minimizing energy losses. This waveform creates a two-step process where heavy metal-EDTA complexes are first concentrated at the electrode surface through electrostatic attraction and chelation, then reduced to their zero-valent metallic state during the negative voltage phase.  

The recirculating design allows continuous reuse of the chelating solution, dramatically reducing chemical consumption compared to conventional soil washing. Nutrient cations (e.g., Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) remain in solution due to their lower reduction potentials, preventing soil degradation.  

Experimental results demonstrate the system's ability to reduce heavy metal concentrations below regulatory safety levels (e.g., California Human Health Screening Levels) while maintaining soil fertility. The technology achieves >90% filtration efficiency for heavy metals like Cu, Pb, and Cd, with the added benefit of recovering these metals in reusable metallic form.  

The invention represents a significant advance over prior remediation methods by combining high efficiency with sustainability, addressing simultaneously the technical challenges of heavy metal removal, chemical consumption, nutrient preservation, and energy efficiency.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a complete system and methodology for remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil. What follows is a detailed description of the system components, operational principles, and exemplary embodiments.  

**System Architecture**  
The remediation system comprises two primary subsystems:  
1. A recirculating soil washing system featuring:  
   - A treatment vessel for containing contaminated soil  
   - Reservoirs for chelating agent solution (e.g., EDTA)  
   - Pumping mechanisms for solution circulation  
2. An AACE filtration device consisting of:  
   - Parallel Ami-PC electrodes with interposed separator  
   - AC power supply generating asymmetrical waveform  
   - Collection mechanism for recovered heavy metals  

**Ami-PC Electrode Fabrication**  
The electrodes are fabricated through a multi-step process:  
1. Carbon felt substrates are cut to appropriate dimensions  
2. A precursor slurry is prepared by mixing PAN, activated carbon (e.g., Super P), and DMF solvent  
3. The carbon felt is dip-coated with the slurry and dried  
4. Hydrothermal treatment converts nitrile groups to amidoxime functional groups using hydroxylamine hydrochloride and sodium carbonate  

The resulting electrodes combine:  
- High surface area (pore sizes ranging from tens to hundreds of micrometers)  
- Excellent electrical conductivity from the carbon matrix  
- Strong heavy metal chelation capacity from amidoxime groups  

**Operational Methodology**  
The remediation process proceeds through the following steps:  
1. Contaminated soil is excavated and placed in the treatment vessel  
2. Chelating solution (e.g., 30 mM EDTA) is circulated through the soil column, extracting heavy metals as anionic complexes (MEDTA²⁻)  
3. The metal-laden solution passes through the AACE filter where:  
   a. During the positive voltage phase (+5V, 0.5 ms), MEDTA²⁻ complexes are concentrated at the electrode surface via:  
      - Electrostatic attraction in the electrical double layer  
      - Competitive chelation by amidoxime groups  
   b. During the negative voltage phase (-10V, 2 ms):  
      - Heavy metal cations are reduced to zero-valent state  
      - EDTA anions are repelled from the now-neutral metal particles  
      - Nutrient cations remain in solution due to higher reduction potentials  
4. The regenerated chelating solution returns to the soil washing cycle  
5. The process continues until heavy metal concentrations reach target levels  

**Performance Characteristics**  
The system demonstrates exceptional remediation performance:  
- Achieves >90% removal efficiency for Cu, Pb, and Cd  
- Reduces heavy metal concentrations below regulatory limits (e.g., CHHSL)  
- Maintains filtration efficiency >90% through multiple cycles  
- Preserves soil nutrients (e.g., Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺) by selective deposition  
- Enables chelating agent recycling with <20% capacity loss after 25 cycles  

**Mechanistic Advantages**  
The AACE approach provides critical advantages over DC methods:  
1. The alternating waveform prevents electrode passivation observed in DC systems  
2. The asymmetrical timing (shorter positive phase) optimizes energy efficiency  
3. The amidoxime functionalization enables selective heavy metal capture  
4. The reduction to metallic state provides superior immobilization compared to hydroxide precipitation  

**Exemplary Embodiments**  
Several specific implementations demonstrate the invention's versatility:  
1. Treatment of Cu-contaminated soil (10,000 ppm) achieving reduction to <3000 ppm  
2. Simultaneous remediation of mixed contamination (Cu, Pb, Cd)  
3. Agricultural land remediation preserving soil fertility for crop growth  
4. Industrial-scale operation with periodic electrode regeneration  

**Regeneration and Maintenance**  
The system includes provisions for long-term operation:  
- Acid elution (e.g., 0.1 M HCl) with reverse bias regenerates fouled electrodes  
- Periodic replacement of separator materials maintains flow characteristics  
- Monitoring systems track filtration efficiency and solution composition  

**Environmental Benefits**  
The invention provides multiple environmental advantages:  
- Eliminates secondary pollution through complete metal recovery  
- Preserves soil structure and nutrient content  
- Minimizes chemical usage through closed-loop operation  
- Enables potential recovery and reuse of valuable heavy metals
