# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) presents a significant challenge for delivering therapeutic agents to targeted regions of the brain. The BBB is a selective barrier formed by endothelial cells lining cerebral microvessels, which prevents the systemic delivery of over 98% of small-molecule drugs and nearly all large-molecule drugs to the brain. Current methods for bypassing the BBB, such as intracranial injections or chemical modification of therapeutic agents, are either invasive, drug-specific, or lack spatial specificity. These limitations are particularly problematic when targeting deep subcortical structures like the basal ganglia, as invasive approaches risk damaging not only the target area but also overlying brain tissue.  

Existing techniques that modify the BBB's permeability often increase permeability throughout the entire brain, posing significant risks due to uncontrolled drug distribution. There remains an unmet need for a non-invasive, region-specific method to temporarily and reversibly increase BBB permeability in precisely targeted brain areas without causing collateral damage. Focused ultrasound (FUS) has emerged as a promising approach, but existing systems often rely on complex MRI-guided phased arrays, limiting their accessibility and practicality in clinical settings.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a non-invasive system and method for targeted drug delivery across the blood-brain barrier using a single-element focused ultrasound transducer operating at an intermediate frequency, preferably 500 kHz. The system combines brief FUS pulses with systemically injected microbubbles to induce localized, reversible BBB opening in deep subcortical structures with high spatial precision.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A portable FUS system using a single spherical transducer at 500 kHz frequency, optimized for transcranial application without requiring MRI guidance  
2. A stereotaxic targeting system that enables precise positioning of the ultrasound focus in three-dimensional space  
3. Real-time monitoring of microbubble cavitation using passive acoustic detection to ensure safe and effective BBB opening  
4. Methods for verifying BBB opening using contrast-enhanced MRI  
5. Parameters for safe and effective BBB opening including ultrasound pressure ranges (0.20-0.30 MPa), microbubble characteristics (4-5 μm diameter), and treatment duration (2 minutes)  

The invention achieves targeting accuracy of approximately 3.1 mm, sufficient for distinguishing between substructures of the basal ganglia. The BBB opening is temporary, with permeability typically restored within 2-4 days, and the procedure has been shown to be safe with no evidence of tissue damage. This system represents a significant advance over existing technologies by providing a practical, portable solution for targeted drug delivery that can be implemented in standard clinical settings.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides a comprehensive system for non-invasive, targeted drug delivery across the blood-brain barrier. The system comprises three main components: (1) a focused ultrasound delivery apparatus, (2) a microbubble preparation and delivery system, and (3) a monitoring and verification system.  

The focused ultrasound apparatus consists of a single-element, spherically focused transducer operating at 500 kHz center frequency. This intermediate frequency provides an optimal balance between targeting accuracy and safety, producing a wavelength of approximately 3 mm that minimizes skull-induced phase aberrations while maintaining a sufficiently small focal region. The transducer is mounted on a stereotaxic manipulator with nine degrees of freedom for precise targeting, including medio-lateral, anterior-posterior, and dorsoventral positioning controls as well as azimuth and elevation angle adjustments.  

Microbubbles for the procedure are monodispersed with diameters of 4-5 μm, prepared using differential centrifugation for size isolation. These microbubbles are administered intravenously immediately prior to sonication and function as acoustic cavitation nuclei, interacting with the ultrasound field to produce localized mechanical effects on capillary walls. The half-life of these microbubbles in circulation is approximately 5-10 minutes, after which they are cleared through pulmonary and renal pathways.  

Real-time monitoring is achieved through passive cavitation detection (PCD) using a confocally aligned hydrophone. The PCD system analyzes harmonic and broadband components of backscattered acoustic signals to distinguish between stable cavitation (desired for safe BBB opening) and inertial cavitation (associated with potential tissue damage). The system monitors harmonic energy increase (HEI) and broadband energy increase (BEI) during treatment, with HEI values between 15-25 dB indicating successful stable cavitation without harmful inertial effects.  

Verification of BBB opening is performed using gadodiamide-enhanced T1-weighted MRI, with pre- and post-sonication imaging to identify regions of increased contrast agent extravasation. The opening volume typically measures approximately 115 mm³ and correlates with applied ultrasound pressure within the 0.20-0.30 MPa range. Additional MRI sequences (T2-weighted and susceptibility-weighted imaging) confirm the absence of edema or hemorrhage following the procedure.  

The method involves the following steps:  
1. Stereotaxic positioning of the subject and transducer alignment using anatomical MRI guidance  
2. Administration of microbubble contrast agent via intravenous injection  
3. Application of focused ultrasound at predetermined parameters (500 kHz, 0.20-0.30 MPa, 2 min duration)  
4. Real-time acoustic monitoring to ensure stable cavitation and safety  
5. Post-sonication MRI verification of BBB opening  
6. Administration of therapeutic agents during the window of BBB permeability  

The system is particularly effective for targeting deep brain structures such as the caudate nucleus and putamen, with demonstrated accuracy sufficient to distinguish between these adjacent basal ganglia components. The temporary nature of the BBB opening (typically 2-4 days) allows for controlled drug delivery while minimizing prolonged exposure of non-targeted brain regions to systemic agents.  

### Example 1  

In a representative implementation, a rhesus macaque was positioned in a stereotaxic frame under general anesthesia. A 500 kHz transducer was aligned to target the left putamen using MRI-guided stereotaxic coordinates. After intravenous administration of 4-5 μm microbubbles, the target region was sonicated for 2 minutes at 0.25 MPa peak negative pressure.  

Real-time PCD monitoring showed a harmonic energy increase of 18 dB without significant broadband emission, indicating stable cavitation. Post-sonication gadodiamide-enhanced MRI revealed a 98 mm³ region of BBB opening centered 1.2 mm ventral and 0.8 mm posterior to the intended target. T2-weighted and susceptibility-weighted MRI sequences confirmed the absence of edema or hemorrhage.  

The subject showed no behavioral deficits following the procedure, and follow-up imaging demonstrated complete BBB restoration within 48 hours. This example illustrates the system's capability for precise, safe, and reversible BBB opening in deep brain structures.  

### Example 2  

In another implementation targeting the caudate nucleus, the same system parameters were applied with the transducer repositioned to the caudate coordinates. Sonications at 0.30 MPa produced a 142 mm³ opening volume with 2.1 mm targeting accuracy.  

PCD monitoring showed a 22 dB HEI with negligible BEI. The BBB remained permeable for 72 hours as verified by serial contrast-enhanced MRI, demonstrating the pressure-dependent duration of BBB opening. Therapeutic antibodies administered during this window showed significant accumulation in the target region with minimal distribution to surrounding brain areas.  

This example highlights the system's utility for delivering large-molecule therapeutics and the relationship between ultrasound parameters and both opening volume and duration. The precise targeting enables delivery to specific functional subdivisions of the basal ganglia while sparing adjacent structures.
