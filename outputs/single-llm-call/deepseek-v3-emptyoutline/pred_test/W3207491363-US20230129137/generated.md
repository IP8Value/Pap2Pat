# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Cardiovascular diseases, particularly myocardial infarction (MI), remain a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. The adult mammalian heart possesses limited regenerative capacity, as cardiomyocytes undergo cell cycle arrest shortly after birth. Consequently, the loss of functional cardiomyocytes due to ischemic injury results in irreversible scarring and progressive heart failure. Current therapeutic strategies focus primarily on mitigating symptoms rather than restoring lost cardiac tissue, highlighting an unmet need for interventions that promote endogenous cardiomyocyte proliferation and functional recovery.  

The Hippo signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in regulating cardiomyocyte proliferation during development and has emerged as a promising target for cardiac regeneration. Downstream effectors of this pathway, YES-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), modulate cell cycle activity and survival. Small-molecule compounds such as TT-10, a fluorine-substituted derivative of TAZ-12, have demonstrated potential in activating YAP/TAZ signaling and promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation. However, systemic administration of TT-10 has been limited by rapid clearance, insufficient local bioavailability, and transient therapeutic effects.  

Poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) nanoparticles (NPs) offer a solution to these challenges by enabling controlled and sustained drug release at the target site. Previous studies have shown that encapsulating therapeutic agents within PLGA NPs enhances drug retention, reduces systemic toxicity, and prolongs pharmacological activity. Despite these advantages, the application of NP-mediated delivery for cardiac regenerative therapies remains underexplored.  

Thus, there exists a critical need for novel pharmaceutical compositions and methods that leverage NP technology to enhance the efficacy and durability of cardiomyocyte proliferation-inducing agents such as TT-10. The present invention addresses this need by providing TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs for targeted myocardial repair following ischemic injury.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions comprising TT-10 encapsulated within poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) nanoparticles (NPs) for use in treating myocardial infarction and other cardiac disorders. The invention further provides methods of preparing TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs and administering them to subjects in need thereof.  

The TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs exhibit a mean diameter of approximately 120–160 nm and demonstrate sustained release kinetics, with approximately 47% of the encapsulated TT-10 released within the first 24 hours and 90% released by day 9. These NPs significantly enhance cardiomyocyte proliferation, reduce apoptosis, and improve cardiac function in preclinical models of MI. Notably, intramyocardial injection of TT-10-loaded NPs preserves left ventricular ejection fraction, reduces infarct size, and promotes angiogenesis in the peri-infarct region, outperforming free TT-10 solution or empty NPs.  

The invention also encompasses methods of differentiating induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) into cardiomyocytes for in vitro testing of TT-10-NP efficacy, as well as protocols for NP characterization, drug loading, and release profiling. The therapeutic benefits of TT-10-NPs are mediated, at least in part, by activation of YAP signaling, which drives cell cycle re-entry and cytoprotection in cardiomyocytes.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms shall have the meanings ascribed below:  

- **"TT-10"** refers to the fluorine-substituted small-molecule compound having the chemical formula C11H10FN3OS2, which acts as an activator of YAP/TAZ signaling and inducer of cardiomyocyte proliferation.  
- **"PLGA nanoparticles (NPs)"** denotes biodegradable polymeric particles composed of poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid, typically with a diameter of 50–200 nm, suitable for encapsulating and delivering therapeutic agents.  
- **"Loading efficiency"** describes the amount of TT-10 (μg) incorporated per milligram of PLGA NPs, typically ranging from 2.0 to 3.0 μg/mg.  
- **"Myocardial infarction (MI)"** refers to ischemic injury resulting from coronary artery occlusion, characterized by cardiomyocyte death and scar formation.  
- **"Border zone (BZ)"** indicates the region of viable myocardium adjacent to the infarct core, where regenerative therapies exert their primary effects.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise TT-10 encapsulated within PLGA NPs. The NPs are prepared via a single oil-in-water emulsion technique, wherein PLGA and TT-10 are dissolved in an organic solvent (e.g., dichloromethane), emulsified by sonication, and stabilized with polyvinyl alcohol (PVA). The resulting NPs are purified by centrifugation, lyophilized, and stored at −80°C until use.  

Key attributes of the TT-10-NP formulation include:  
- Mean particle diameter of 123–155 nm, as measured by nanoparticle tracking analysis.  
- Loading efficiency of 2.50 ± 0.08 μg TT-10 per mg PLGA.  
- Sustained release profile, with 47% of TT-10 released within 24 hours and 90% released by day 9 in physiological buffer.  

The compositions may further include pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as dimethylamine borane, coumarin-6 (for imaging), or albumin to enhance stability.  

### ASPECTS  

The invention provides the following aspects:  

1. **Therapeutic Efficacy**: TT-10-NPs significantly improve left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), reduce infarct size by ~20%, and attenuate adverse remodeling compared to free TT-10 or vehicle controls.  
2. **Mechanism of Action**: TT-10-NPs promote cardiomyocyte proliferation (evidenced by Ki67, PH3, and Aurora B expression) and reduce apoptosis (TUNEL staining) via YAP/TAZ pathway activation.  
3. **Angiogenic Effects**: TT-10-NPs enhance vascularity in the border zone, as demonstrated by increased isolectin B4 and α-smooth muscle actin staining.  
4. **Dosage Optimization**: A single intramyocardial dose of 67.2 ng TT-10 delivered via NPs achieves superior outcomes compared to systemic doses exceeding 10 mg/kg.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Preparation of TT-10-Loaded PLGA NPs**  
PLGA (100 mg) and TT-10 (0.6 mg) were dissolved in dichloromethane (5 mL), sonicated, and emulsified in 1% dimethylamine borane. The emulsion was stirred for 4 hours, centrifuged at 45,000 × g, and lyophilized to yield TT-10-NPs with a mean diameter of 155.4 ± 0.7 nm.  

**Example 2: In Vitro Cardiomyocyte Proliferation Assay**  
Human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs) treated with 10 μM TT-10-NPs exhibited a 3-fold increase in Ki67+ cells and 2.5-fold increase in BrdU incorporation compared to controls (p < 0.05).  

**Example 3: In Vivo Myocardial Repair**  
Mice subjected to LAD ligation and treated with TT-10-NPs showed LVEF of 45.2 ± 3.1% at 4 weeks, versus 28.5 ± 2.8% in free TT-10-treated mice (p < 0.01). Infarct size was reduced by 19.7% in the TT-10-NP group.  

**Example 4: YAP Signaling Activation**  
Western blot analysis confirmed reduced phosphorylated YAP (p-YAP) and increased nuclear YAP localization in TT-10-NP-treated cardiomyocytes, consistent with pathway activation.
