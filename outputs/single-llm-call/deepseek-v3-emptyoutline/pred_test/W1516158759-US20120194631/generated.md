# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Videoconferencing systems have become ubiquitous in modern communication, yet they fail to faithfully reproduce the spatial characteristics of gaze, body orientation, and pointing gestures that are fundamental to face-to-face interaction. This deficiency disrupts the natural link between attention projection and attention awareness, leading to inefficiencies in communication. In particular, hub-and-satellite meetings—where most participants are collocated except for one remote participant—suffer from a lack of shared physical environment, making it difficult for participants to use spatial cues to direct attention.  

Existing solutions, such as stationary video displays, do not adequately address the "newscaster effect," where a remote participant appears to look directly at all hub participants simultaneously, or the "skip-over effect," where the remote participant is inadvertently excluded from turn-taking. Prior attempts to improve gaze awareness in videoconferencing, including robotic telepresence systems and multi-camera setups, have either been mechanically complex or failed to provide an intuitive sense of directional attention.  

Thus, there remains a need for an improved videoconferencing system that enhances attention projection and awareness, particularly in hub-and-satellite configurations, while minimizing cognitive effort and maintaining a natural interaction flow.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a kinetic proxy system for videoconferencing that enhances attention projection and awareness between a remote participant (the satellite) and collocated participants (the hub). The system includes a motorized turntable supporting a display screen, camera, speaker, and microphone, which collectively represent the satellite participant within the hub environment. The turntable allows the display to swivel horizontally, enabling the satellite to direct the proxy toward specific hub participants, thereby improving gaze awareness and conversational engagement.  

The system supports two modes of control: explicit control, where the satellite manually adjusts the proxy's orientation using a mouse or similar input device, and implicit control, where the proxy's motion is automatically synchronized with the satellite's head movements via head-tracking software. Both modes enhance the hub participants' ability to discern the satellite's focus of attention, reducing instances of miscommunication and exclusion.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- Improved conversational engagement, as measured by speaking time, speech energy, and turn-taking frequency.  
- More accurate responses to deictic prompts, ensuring that hub participants correctly identify when they are being addressed.  
- Reduced cognitive effort compared to static videoconferencing setups, as the kinetic motion provides intuitive spatial cues.  
- A more natural interaction flow, mitigating the newscaster and skip-over effects.  

The invention is particularly well-suited for hub-and-satellite meetings, where a single remote participant interacts with a group of collocated individuals, but it can also be extended to multi-satellite configurations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The kinetic proxy system comprises a motorized turntable supporting a display screen, a wide-angle camera, a speaker, and a microphone. The display screen presents a live video feed of the satellite participant, while the wide-angle camera captures a panoramic view of the hub environment, which is transmitted to the satellite's monitor. The turntable is capable of rotating the display horizontally within a range of ±90 degrees, allowing the satellite to direct the proxy toward specific hub participants.  

### System Components  

1. **Motorized Turntable:** The turntable is driven by a servo motor controlled via a wired or wireless connection to the satellite's computing device. The motor provides smooth and precise rotation, ensuring that the display's movement accurately reflects the satellite's intended focus of attention.  

2. **Display Screen:** A high-resolution tablet or monitor is mounted in portrait orientation on the turntable, positioned at approximate eye level for seated hub participants. The screen displays a real-time video feed of the satellite participant's face and upper body, ensuring visibility of facial expressions and gestures.  

3. **Wide-Angle Camera:** A fixed-position camera with a wide field of view captures the entire hub environment, transmitting this panoramic image to the satellite's display. This allows the satellite to maintain awareness of all hub participants and their spatial relationships.  

4. **Audio Components:** A speakerphone or directional microphone array ensures clear audio transmission between the satellite and hub participants. The audio system is synchronized with the display's motion to maintain spatial consistency.  

### Control Mechanisms  

1. **Explicit Control Mode:**  
   - The satellite participant uses a mouse or touch input to position a cursor over the panoramic view of the hub environment.  
   - The proxy's display rotates in real time to align with the cursor's horizontal position, ensuring that the screen faces the intended hub participant.  
   - This mode provides deliberate and unambiguous attention projection, as each movement is consciously initiated by the satellite.  

2. **Implicit Control Mode:**  
   - A head-tracking system monitors the satellite's head movements via a webcam or dedicated motion sensor.  
   - The proxy's display rotates in synchrony with the satellite's head orientation, creating a natural correspondence between the satellite's gaze and the proxy's direction.  
   - This mode reduces the cognitive load on the satellite by automating attention projection, though it may introduce incidental motion that requires calibration.  

### Operational Workflow  

1. **Initialization:**  
   - The system is positioned at the hub site, with the display centered and the wide-angle camera capturing the meeting space.  
   - The satellite participant connects to the system via a videoconferencing application, receiving the panoramic hub view while transmitting their video feed to the proxy display.  

2. **Conversational Interaction:**  
   - During discussion, the satellite adjusts the proxy's orientation using either explicit or implicit control, directing attention toward specific hub participants as needed.  
   - Hub participants perceive the satellite's focus of attention through the display's motion, reducing ambiguity in turn-taking and deictic references.  

3. **Feedback and Calibration:**  
   - The system may include feedback mechanisms, such as visual indicators on the satellite's screen, confirming the proxy's current orientation.  
   - Calibration ensures that head movements or cursor positions map accurately to the turntable's rotation, preventing misalignment.  

### Advantages Over Prior Art  

Unlike stationary videoconferencing setups, the kinetic proxy system enhances attention awareness by introducing physical motion cues. Unlike robotic telepresence systems, it avoids mechanical complexity by focusing solely on horizontal rotation, which is sufficient for most meeting scenarios. The dual control modes (explicit and implicit) provide flexibility, accommodating different user preferences and interaction contexts.  

### Alternative Embodiments  

1. **Multi-Satellite Configurations:** Multiple kinetic proxies can be deployed in a single hub environment, each representing a different remote participant. The system may include collision avoidance algorithms to prevent physical interference between proxies.  

2. **Augmented Feedback:** Haptic or auditory feedback could be integrated to notify the satellite when the proxy reaches its rotational limits or when a hub participant initiates eye contact.  

3. **Convex Display Surfaces:** Instead of a flat screen, a curved display could provide a wider viewing angle, reducing the exclusionary effect of swiveling toward one participant.  

### Conclusion  

The kinetic proxy system represents a significant advancement in videoconferencing technology, addressing long-standing challenges in attention projection and awareness. By incorporating motorized motion and intuitive control mechanisms, the invention enhances conversational engagement, reduces miscommunication, and fosters a more natural interaction dynamic in hub-and-satellite meetings. Future refinements may explore additional degrees of freedom, enhanced feedback systems, and integration with emerging telepresence technologies.
