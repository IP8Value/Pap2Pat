# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of viral vectors for gene therapy and more specifically to novel adeno-associated virus (AAV) capsid sequences derived from endogenous viral elements (EVEs) integrated into marsupial genomes. The invention provides reconstructed ancestral AAV capsid proteins, nucleic acids encoding such capsid proteins, recombinant AAV vectors incorporating the capsid proteins, host cells for producing such vectors, and methods of using the vectors for gene delivery. The invention further encompasses methods of diversifying AAV capsids through ancestral sequence reconstruction and applications of the novel AAV vectors in therapeutic contexts.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  

Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) have emerged as promising vectors for gene therapy due to their non-pathogenic nature, ability to infect both dividing and non-dividing cells, and capacity for long-term transgene expression. Current AAV vectors are derived from primate isolates, which may exhibit limited tropism for certain cell types and trigger immune responses in human patients. There exists an unmet need for novel AAV capsids with improved transduction efficiency, reduced immunogenicity, and enhanced tissue specificity.  

The present invention addresses this need by reconstructing functional AAV capsid sequences from endogenous viral elements (EVEs) preserved in marsupial genomes. These EVEs represent "molecular fossils" of ancient AAVs that circulated in ancestral marsupials tens of millions of years ago. Through phylogenetic analysis and maximum likelihood sequence reconstruction, the inventors have resurrected a functional AAV capsid (designated mAAV-EVE1) from an endogenous viral element integrated into the genome of macropodoid marsupials. This ancient capsid exhibits unique structural features distinct from contemporary AAVs while maintaining the fundamental architecture required for viral assembly and infectivity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE  

The invention provides isolated nucleic acids encoding the capsid proteins of an ancestral marsupial AAV (mAAV-EVE1), including VP1, VP2 and VP3 polypeptides. The nucleic acid sequences were reconstructed through phylogenetic analysis of endogenous viral elements present in multiple macropodoid species. The invention further encompasses:  

Recombinant AAV vectors comprising the mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins and a heterologous nucleic acid of interest. These vectors maintain the ability to package recombinant genomes while exhibiting novel tropism profiles due to their unique capsid structure.  

Host cells engineered to produce the recombinant mAAV-EVE1 vectors, including packaging cell lines providing AAV rep genes, adenoviral helper functions, and the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene.  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the mAAV-EVE1 vectors and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, suitable for administration to subjects in need of gene therapy.  

Methods of using the mAAV-EVE1 vectors for delivering therapeutic genes to target cells in vitro or in vivo. The vectors may be administered systemically or locally to transduce specific tissues.  

Methods of capsid diversification through ancestral sequence reconstruction, enabling generation of novel AAV variants with desirable properties for gene therapy applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Vectors  

The invention provides recombinant nucleic acid constructs for producing mAAV-EVE1 viral vectors. These include plasmid vectors comprising:  

1) A recombinant AAV genome containing inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) flanking a heterologous nucleic acid sequence of interest (transgene). The transgene may encode a therapeutic protein, regulatory RNA, or other biologically active molecule.  

2) A rep gene encoding AAV replication proteins (Rep78, Rep68, Rep52, Rep40) required for viral genome replication and packaging. The rep gene may be derived from any known AAV serotype.  

3) An mAAV-EVE1 cap gene encoding the VP1, VP2 and VP3 capsid proteins. The cap gene sequence was reconstructed through phylogenetic analysis of endogenous viral elements from multiple macropodoid species.  

The vectors may further include regulatory elements such as promoters, enhancers, polyadenylation signals, and introns to optimize transgene expression. The ITRs may be derived from any AAV serotype and are preferably identical to enable efficient replication and packaging.  

### Recombinant AAV  

The invention provides recombinant AAV (rAAV) particles comprising:  

1) A viral capsid formed by mAAV-EVE1 VP1, VP2 and VP3 proteins in approximately 1:1:10 ratio. The capsid exhibits a T=1 icosahedral symmetry with characteristic surface loops that determine receptor binding and tropism.  

2) A packaged viral genome containing ITRs flanking a heterologous transgene. The genome may be single-stranded or self-complementary.  

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid differs from known AAV serotypes in several variable regions (VRs), particularly VR-VII which is substantially larger in mAAV-EVE1 compared to AAV2 and AAV8. These structural differences confer novel biological properties including altered receptor binding, cellular trafficking, and antigenic profile.  

### Host Cells  

The invention provides host cells for producing recombinant mAAV-EVE1 vectors. Suitable host cells include mammalian cell lines such as HEK293, HeLa, or A549 cells engineered to:  

1) Express AAV rep genes required for viral replication  
2) Provide adenoviral helper functions (E1a, E1b, E2a, E4, VA RNA)  
3) Express the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene to produce capsid proteins  

The host cells may be transiently transfected with packaging plasmids or stably transformed to constitutively express required viral components. Production methods may involve adherent or suspension culture, with optimization of media conditions, temperature, and harvest timing to maximize vector yield.  

### Compositions and Methods  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising mAAV-EVE1 vectors in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including intravenous, intramuscular, intrathecal, or direct tissue injection.  

The invention further provides methods of gene delivery comprising administering an effective dose of mAAV-EVE1 vectors to a subject in need thereof. The vectors may be used to treat genetic disorders, cancers, infectious diseases, or other conditions amenable to gene therapy.  

### Capsid Diversification  

The invention provides methods for generating diverse AAV capsids through ancestral sequence reconstruction. The methods involve:  

1) Identifying endogenous viral elements in host genomes  
2) Determining phylogenetic relationships among EVEs  
3) Reconstructing ancestral sequences using maximum likelihood algorithms  
4) Engineering functional viral capsids from reconstructed sequences  

This approach enables resurrection of ancient viral variants with unique properties not found in extant viruses. The diversified capsids may be screened for desirable characteristics such as enhanced transduction, reduced immunogenicity, or tissue-specific targeting.  

### Other Uses  

The mAAV-EVE1 vectors have additional applications including:  

1) Delivery of gene editing components (CRISPR-Cas, TALENs, zinc finger nucleases)  
2) Expression of therapeutic antibodies or cytokines  
3) Production of viral vaccines  
4) Basic research tools for studying gene function  
5) Diagnostic applications through reporter gene expression  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins  

Genomic DNA from Macropus giganteus was analyzed using PCR with primers recognizing conserved AAV sequences. An endogenous viral element (mAAV-EVE1) was identified and fully sequenced through genome walking. The element showed 96.4% identity to an AAV-EVE in Macropus eugenii and was found to be lineage-restricted to macropodoid marsupials.  

Maximum likelihood reconstruction was used to infer the ancestral mAAV-EVE1 sequence from alignments of sixteen macropodoid orthologs. The reconstructed genome contained rep and cap genes with frameshifts and stop codons corrected based on conserved AAV protein domains. The cap gene encoded VP1 (82.2 kDa), VP2 (not present), and VP3 (60.9 kDa) proteins. Structural modeling revealed conservation of key AAV features including β-barrel core and surface loops, with notable expansion of VR-VII.  

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid  

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 cap gene was codon-optimized for human expression and cloned into a plasmid vector. Site-directed mutagenesis was performed to:  

1) Restore the histidine residue in the VP1 phospholipase A2 domain  
2) Introduce a start codon for VP2 translation  
3) Optimize surface loops for enhanced transduction  

The modified cap gene was used to produce recombinant AAV vectors as described in Example 3.  

### Example 3. Production of rAAV  

HEK293 cells were co-transfected with:  

1) A packaging plasmid expressing AAV2 rep and mAAV-EVE1 cap  
2) An AAV genome plasmid containing GFP flanked by AAV2 ITRs  
3) An adenoviral helper plasmid  

Cells were harvested after 72 hours, lysed by freeze-thaw, and purified by iodixanol gradient centrifugation. Vector titers were determined by qPCR and averaged 1×10^12 vg/mL.  

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV  

The mAAV-EVE1 vectors were evaluated for:  

1) Transduction efficiency in various cell lines  
2) Neutralization by human sera  
3) Tissue tropism in murine models  

Results showed robust transduction of neuronal and muscle cells, reduced neutralization compared to AAV2, and preferential liver targeting following systemic administration. These properties support use of mAAV-EVE1 vectors for gene therapy applications.
