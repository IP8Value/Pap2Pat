# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Prion diseases represent a class of fatal neurodegenerative disorders affecting both humans and animals, characterized by the conversion of normal cellular prion protein (PrPC) into an abnormal, pathogenic isoform (PrPSc). These diseases include Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) in humans, bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE) in cattle, scrapie in sheep, and chronic wasting disease (CWD) in deer. The accumulation of PrPSc leads to progressive neurodegeneration, resulting in severe clinical symptoms such as ataxia, dementia, and ultimately death. Despite extensive research efforts, no effective treatments currently exist for prion diseases.  

Current therapeutic strategies have focused on targeting various aspects of prion propagation, including inhibiting the conversion of PrPC to PrPSc, reducing PrPC expression, or enhancing the clearance of PrPSc. However, existing compounds have shown limited efficacy in clinical settings, often due to poor blood-brain barrier penetration, toxicity, or insufficient potency. There remains an urgent need for novel therapeutic agents capable of effectively reducing PrPSc levels and halting disease progression.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for the treatment of prion diseases through the administration of AR-12 (OSU-03012) and its analogues, particularly AR-14. These compounds exhibit potent anti-prion activity by significantly reducing PrPSc levels in prion-infected cells through the induction of autophagy.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
- The use of AR-12 and AR-14 to reduce PrPSc in neuronal and non-neuronal cell models of prion infection.  
- The demonstration that these compounds induce autophagy, as evidenced by increased LC3-II levels, contributing to PrPSc clearance.  
- The ability of AR-12 and AR-14 to completely eradicate prion infection in long-term treatment studies, as confirmed by immunoblot analysis and real-time quaking-induced conversion (RT-QuIC).  
- The favorable safety profile of AR-12 and AR-14, with effective concentrations well below cytotoxic thresholds.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising AR-12, AR-14, or their derivatives, optionally in combination with other anti-prion agents, for the treatment of prion diseases and related protein misfolding disorders.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that AR-12 and its analogue AR-14 exhibit potent anti-prion effects in multiple prion-infected cell models, including neuronal (ScN2a, ScCAD5) and non-neuronal (ScMEF) cell lines. These compounds induce autophagy, leading to enhanced degradation of PrPSc, and can achieve complete clearance of prion infection with prolonged treatment.  

**Mechanism of Action**  
AR-12 and AR-14 function by stimulating autophagy, a cellular degradation pathway that facilitates the clearance of misfolded proteins, including PrPSc. Treatment with these compounds results in increased levels of LC3-II, a marker of autophagosome formation, confirming autophagy induction. The anti-prion effect is partially dependent on autophagy, as demonstrated by reduced efficacy in ATG5-knockout cells lacking functional autophagy.  

**Efficacy in Prion-Infected Cell Models**  
Short-term treatment (72 hours) with AR-12 (0.5–3 µM) or AR-14 (0.1–2 µM) significantly reduces PrPSc levels in ScN2a, ScCAD5, and ScMEF cells, with dose-dependent responses observed. Effective concentrations (EC50) range from 1.5–4 µM for AR-12 and 1.5 µM for AR-14, well below cytotoxic thresholds (LD50 = 5–9 µM).  

Long-term treatment (20 days) with AR-12 (3 µM) or AR-14 (2 µM) completely clears PrPSc, as confirmed by immunoblot and RT-QuIC analysis. Notably, prion seeding activity does not reappear after discontinuation of treatment in ScMEF cells, indicating sustained eradication of infection.  

**Pharmaceutical Compositions and Administration**  
The invention includes pharmaceutical formulations comprising AR-12, AR-14, or derivatives thereof, formulated for oral, intravenous, or other routes of administration. The compounds are particularly advantageous due to their ability to cross the blood-brain barrier, enabling effective CNS delivery.  

Combination therapies with other anti-prion agents (e.g., autophagy inducers, PrPC inhibitors) are also envisioned to enhance efficacy and reduce the risk of resistance. Encapsulation strategies, such as acetalated dextran microparticles, may further improve drug safety and bioavailability.  

**Therapeutic Applications**  
The invention is applicable to the treatment of various prion diseases, including:  
- Human prion diseases: sporadic, genetic, and acquired forms of CJD, Gerstmann-Sträussler-Scheinker syndrome (GSS), and fatal familial insomnia (FFI).  
- Animal prion diseases: scrapie, BSE, and CWD.  

Additionally, the methods may extend to other protein misfolding disorders, such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases, where autophagy induction could mitigate pathogenic protein accumulation.  

**Experimental Validation**  
The anti-prion effects of AR-12 and AR-14 were validated using:  
1. **Immunoblot Analysis**: Quantification of PrPSc levels after treatment.  
2. **RT-QuIC**: Sensitive detection of prion seeding activity.  
3. **Immunofluorescence**: Visualization of PrPSc reduction and lysosomal activation.  
4. **Cytotoxicity Assays**: Confirmation of safety margins for therapeutic concentrations.  

In summary, the invention provides a groundbreaking approach to prion disease treatment through the use of AR-12 and AR-14, offering a promising pathway to effective therapy for these currently incurable disorders.
