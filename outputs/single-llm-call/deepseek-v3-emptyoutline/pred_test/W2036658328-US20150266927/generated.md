# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and vaccine development, specifically concerning improved methods for producing virus-like particles (VLPs) with enhanced immunogenicity. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel culture method for Saccharomyces cerevisiae (S. cerevisiae) that yields high-quality human papillomavirus type 16 L1 protein (HPV16 L1) VLPs through optimized carbon source concentrations during fermentation. The resulting VLPs exhibit superior structural stability, antigenicity, and immunogenicity compared to those produced under conventional culture conditions.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Virus-like particles have emerged as a promising vaccine platform due to their ability to mimic native virions while being non-infectious. Traditional VLP production methods using S. cerevisiae expression systems typically employ carbon source concentrations between 2-4%, with culture durations limited to 48-72 hours to avoid alcohol accumulation and osmotic stress. However, VLPs produced under these standard conditions often demonstrate suboptimal structural integrity and reduced immunogenicity.  

Prior attempts to improve VLP quality have focused primarily on post-production modifications such as redox refolding, salt treatments, and maturation protocols. While these in vitro strategies have shown some success, they add complexity and cost to the manufacturing process without addressing the fundamental limitations of intracellular VLP assembly during cell culture.  

The relationship between culture conditions and VLP quality remains poorly understood, particularly regarding the impact of extended culture durations and elevated carbon source concentrations. Conventional wisdom suggests that carbon sources above 4% would be detrimental to yeast viability and protein production. There exists an unmet need for improved culture methods that enhance both the yield and quality of VLPs during bioprocessing, without requiring extensive post-production modifications.  

## DISCLOSURE  

### Technical Problem  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the suboptimal quality of VLPs produced in conventional yeast expression systems, characterized by inadequate structural stability, reduced antigenicity, and limited immunogenicity. These deficiencies stem from standard culture conditions that fail to support proper protein folding and VLP assembly during intracellular production.  

### Technical Solution  

The invention provides a solution through a novel culture method wherein S. cerevisiae is grown in media containing elevated carbon source concentrations between 6-8%, with an extended culture duration of approximately 6 days. This approach unexpectedly yields HPV16 L1 VLPs with superior structural characteristics and enhanced immunological properties compared to those produced under standard conditions.  

The method comprises transforming S. cerevisiae with an expression vector containing a codon-optimized HPV16 L1 gene, culturing the transformed yeast in media containing 6-8% total carbon source (with a 1:1 glucose to galactose ratio) for 6 days at 30°C, followed by purification of the resulting VLPs through a series of precipitation and chromatography steps.  

### Advantageous Effects  

The invention provides multiple advantageous effects including:  
1) Production of HPV16 L1 VLPs with enhanced conformational epitope presentation, as demonstrated by increased reactivity with neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70  
2) Improved immunogenicity evidenced by higher neutralizing antibody titers in immunized animal models  
3) Superior structural characteristics including reduced hydrodynamic diameter compared to conventionally produced VLPs  
4) Increased production yields while maintaining high purity  
5) Elimination of the need for extensive post-production modifications to enhance VLP quality  
6) Cost-effective production through optimized culture conditions rather than additional processing steps  

## MODES OF THE INVENTION  

The invention may be practiced through various embodiments, including but not limited to:  

1) A method for producing HPV16 L1 VLPs comprising culturing S. cerevisiae transformed with an HPV16 L1 expression vector in media containing 6-8% total carbon source for approximately 6 days, followed by purification of the VLPs.  

2) The method of embodiment 1 wherein the carbon source comprises equal parts glucose and galactose.  

3) The method of embodiment 1 or 2 wherein the culture is maintained at 30°C with constant agitation.  

4) The method of any preceding embodiment wherein the purification process comprises ammonium sulfate precipitation followed by cation-exchange chromatography.  

5) A composition comprising HPV16 L1 VLPs produced by the method of any preceding embodiment, characterized by enhanced reactivity with neutralizing antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70 compared to VLPs produced in 2-4% carbon source.  

6) A vaccine formulation comprising the VLP composition of embodiment 5 in combination with an adjuvant.  

7) The vaccine formulation of embodiment 6 wherein the adjuvant is aluminum hydroxide.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Production and Purification of HPV16 L1 VLPs  

S. cerevisiae Y2805 was transformed with a YEGα-MCS vector containing a codon-optimized HPV16 L1 gene. Transformants were selected and cultured in YPDG media containing either 2%, 4%, 6%, or 8% total carbon source (1:1 glucose:galactose) for 6 days at 30°C with shaking at 230 rpm. Cells were lysed using glass bead disruption, and HPV16 L1 protein was purified through ammonium sulfate precipitation followed by cation-exchange chromatography using P-11 phosphocellulose resin.  

### Example 2: Characterization of VLP Quality  

Transmission electron microscopy confirmed VLP formation across all carbon source concentrations. Dynamic light scattering revealed that VLPs from 6% and 8% carbon source cultures had smaller hydrodynamic diameters compared to those from 2% and 4% cultures. ELISA demonstrated significantly higher reactivity with neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70 for VLPs produced in 6% and 8% carbon source.  

### Example 3: Immunogenicity Assessment  

BALB/c mice were immunized three times with 10 ng or 1000 ng doses of VLPs produced under different culture conditions. VLPs from 6% and 8% carbon source cultures induced significantly higher anti-HPV16 L1 IgG titers and neutralizing antibody responses compared to those from 2% and 4% cultures, particularly at the lower 10 ng dose.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The present invention has significant industrial applicability in the field of vaccine production and biotechnology. The improved culture method enables cost-effective production of high-quality HPV VLPs suitable for prophylactic vaccines against HPV infection and associated diseases. The enhanced immunogenicity of the resulting VLPs may allow for reduced dosing requirements in vaccine formulations.  

Furthermore, the principles disclosed herein may be applied to the production of VLPs for other viruses beyond HPV, potentially revolutionizing the development of VLP-based vaccines across multiple disease targets. The invention represents a significant advancement in bioprocessing technology with broad applications in the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries.
