# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Structural biology has long relied on X-ray crystallography as a primary method for determining the three-dimensional structures of proteins and other biological macromolecules. However, conventional crystallography techniques face significant limitations when applied to membrane proteins and large protein complexes, which are often difficult to crystallize in sufficiently large quantities. Serial femtosecond crystallography (SFX) has emerged as a transformative approach, enabling structure determination from micro- and nanocrystals at room temperature using intense femtosecond X-ray pulses from free-electron lasers (XFELs).  

A critical challenge in SFX is the efficient delivery of protein crystals into the X-ray beam path. Existing methods, such as gas dynamic virtual nozzles (GDVNs), consume substantial amounts of protein due to high flow rates required to replenish crystals between X-ray pulses. Alternative viscous media, including lipidic cubic phase (LCP), mineral oil-based grease, and petroleum jelly, have been explored but suffer from drawbacks such as high background scattering, limited compatibility with diverse crystallization conditions, and instability under experimental conditions.  

There remains an unmet need for a versatile, low-background, and protein-efficient crystal delivery medium suitable for SFX applications across a broad range of protein types, including soluble proteins, membrane proteins, and large multi-protein complexes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel crystal delivery medium for serial femtosecond crystallography (SFX) based on agarose. The invention encompasses compositions comprising agarose and optional additives such as glycerol, as well as methods for embedding pre-formed protein crystals into the agarose medium and delivering them to an X-ray free-electron laser (XFEL) beam.  

Key advantages of the agarose-based delivery system include:  
1) Dramatically reduced protein consumption compared to liquid jet injectors, with flow rates as low as 160 nl/min;  
2) Compatibility with a wide range of crystallization conditions, including high salt concentrations, polyethylene glycols (PEGs), and organic precipitants;  
3) Low X-ray scattering background, particularly advantageous for large unit cell crystals and low-resolution data collection;  
4) Operational stability across a temperature range from 4°C to 30°C;  
5) Applicability to both vacuum and ambient pressure experimental setups;  
6) Successful demonstration with challenging membrane protein complexes such as photosystem I and II.  

The invention further provides methods for preparing the agarose medium, embedding crystals, and optimizing delivery parameters for specific protein systems.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for delivering protein crystals in serial femtosecond crystallography (SFX) experiments using an agarose-based medium. The agarose medium typically comprises between 5% to 9% (w/v) agarose, preferably about 5.6% to 7%, dissolved in an appropriate buffer solution. In embodiments designed for vacuum operation, the medium preferably includes about 30% (v/v) glycerol as a cryoprotectant. For ambient pressure operation, alternative cryoprotectants such as sucrose may be employed.  

The agarose medium is prepared by dissolving ultralow-gelling-temperature agarose in the desired buffer system at elevated temperature (typically above 85°C), followed by cooling to form a gel. The specific buffer composition is selected to match the crystallization conditions of the target protein, maintaining crystal stability during delivery. Compatible buffers include those containing various salts (e.g., ammonium sulfate, sodium chloride, calcium chloride), PEGs of different molecular weights (e.g., PEG 2000, PEG 4000), or organic precipitants such as 2-methyl-2,4-pentanediol (MPD).  

Protein crystals are embedded into the agarose medium post-crystallization using a specialized mixing technique. In preferred embodiments, this involves:  
1) Preparing a concentrated crystal suspension in crystallization mother liquor;  
2) Loading the agarose medium and crystal suspension into separate syringes;  
3) Connecting the syringes with a coupler device;  
4) Mixing by repeated reciprocal transfer between syringes (typically 40 or more exchanges) to achieve homogeneous crystal distribution.  

The crystal-loaded agarose medium is then delivered to the XFEL beam using an injection system, preferably at flow rates between 100-200 nl/min. The invention is compatible with both vacuum and ambient pressure delivery environments, with optional modifications to the medium composition (e.g., addition of cryoprotectants for vacuum operation).  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Delivery of Phycocyanin (PC) Crystals in Agarose Medium**  

Phycocyanin (PC) from Thermosynechococcus elongatus was purified and crystallized by free interface diffusion in a precipitant solution containing 1.0 M ammonium sulfate and 40 mM MES buffer (pH 6.4). Crystals of 1-5 μm size were filtered through a 10 μm stainless steel filter prior to embedding.  

The agarose delivery medium was prepared by dissolving 7% (w/v) ultralow-gelling-temperature agarose in a solution containing 30% glycerol and crystallization buffer (15% PEG 2000, 30 mM MgCl₂, 75 mM HEPES pH 7.0). The mixture was heated in a boiling water bath for 30 minutes to ensure complete dissolution.  

For embedding, 20 μl of hot agarose solution was drawn into a pre-warmed syringe and allowed to cool to room temperature. A second syringe was loaded with 5 μl of concentrated PC crystal suspension. The syringes were connected via a coupler, and the contents were mixed through 40 reciprocal transfers to achieve homogeneous crystal distribution (final crystal density ≈ 2×10¹⁰ crystals/ml).  

The crystal-loaded agarose was extruded through a 50 μm capillary at 160 nl/min into the XFEL beam path at the Linac Coherent Light Source (LCLS). Data collection yielded 41,100 diffraction patterns in 72 minutes, with 14,143 patterns successfully indexed to 2.5 Å resolution. The resulting structure was refined to Rwork = 18.7% and Rfree = 25.2%, demonstrating the effectiveness of the agarose delivery method.
