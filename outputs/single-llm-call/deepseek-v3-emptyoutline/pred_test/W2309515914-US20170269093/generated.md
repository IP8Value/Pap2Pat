## BACKGROUND  

Breast cancer metastasis remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women worldwide. The metastatic cascade involves multiple steps wherein cancer cells breach the basement membrane, migrate through the dense extracellular matrix (ECM), intravasate into blood vessels, and ultimately colonize distant organs. A critical aspect of this process involves the formation of specialized membrane protrusions that enable invasive migration through stromal tissues.  

Current understanding indicates that carcinoma cells migrating in mesenchymal mode form elongated membrane protrusions driven by branched F-actin networks. These actin polymerization-driven migration and invasion processes are coordinated by proto-oncogenes including c-Src and c-Abl kinases along with cytoskeletal regulatory proteins such as Rac GTPase, the Scar/WAVE complex, and Ena/VASP proteins. While these molecular players have been individually implicated in cancer progression, the precise mechanisms coordinating their activities during invasive migration remain incompletely understood.  

Ena/VASP proteins (Mena, EVL and VASP) are known to enhance processive filament elongation and are upregulated in breast cancer, where they promote invasion. Lamellipodin (Lpd) has been identified as a binding partner of Ena/VASP proteins that localizes to lamellipodial membrane protrusions in migrating cells. The interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP is regulated by Abl kinase-mediated phosphorylation of Lpd, which drives Ena/VASP recruitment to lamellipodia.  

While Lpd is known to be required for lamellipodium formation and binds directly to the Scar/WAVE complex, the specific roles of these interactions in three-dimensional cancer cell invasion have not been elucidated. There remains an unmet need in the art for targeted therapeutic approaches that can specifically inhibit the molecular mechanisms driving metastatic progression in breast cancer.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting breast cancer metastasis by targeting Lamellipodin (Lpd)-mediated invasive migration. The invention is based on the discovery that Lpd promotes metastatic progression through coordinated interactions with both Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex, and that these interactions are differentially regulated by Src and Abl kinase phosphorylation.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The identification of Lpd as a critical mediator of breast cancer metastasis, where increased Lpd expression correlates with poor clinical outcomes  
2. The demonstration that Lpd promotes invasive migration through both Ena/VASP-dependent and Scar/WAVE-dependent mechanisms  
3. The discovery that Src kinase phosphorylation specifically regulates Lpd-Scar/WAVE interactions while Abl kinase phosphorylation regulates both Lpd-Scar/WAVE and Lpd-Ena/VASP interactions  
4. Therapeutic strategies targeting Lpd phosphorylation sites or protein-protein interaction domains to inhibit metastatic progression  

The invention provides specific inhibitors of Lpd function, including small molecules, peptides, antibodies, and nucleic acids that disrupt Lpd's interactions with Ena/VASP proteins or the Scar/WAVE complex. These inhibitors are useful for treating metastatic breast cancer and other cancers where Lpd-mediated invasion contributes to disease progression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting cancer metastasis by targeting Lamellipodin (Lpd)-mediated invasive migration. As demonstrated in the Examples, Lpd plays a critical role in breast cancer metastasis by coordinating actin cytoskeletal dynamics through regulated interactions with Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex.  

Lpd expression levels correlate with metastatic progression and poor clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients. Analysis of clinical samples reveals that moderately increased Lpd levels are associated with elevated risk of breast cancer mortality, while highly increased Lpd expression at the plasma membrane correlates with reduced disease-free and metastasis-free survival. These clinical correlations are supported by experimental data showing that Lpd depletion significantly reduces lung metastasis in orthotopic mouse models without affecting primary tumor growth.  

At the cellular level, Lpd promotes multiple steps of the metastatic cascade. Lpd is required for local tumor invasion, as demonstrated by reduced stromal infiltration in Lpd-depleted tumors. Zebrafish models reveal that Lpd enhances intravasation, while experimental metastasis assays show it is dispensable for extravasation. These findings position Lpd as a key mediator of early metastatic steps involving invasive migration through stromal tissues.  

Mechanistically, Lpd coordinates actin cytoskeletal dynamics during invasive migration through regulated interactions with two distinct classes of actin regulators. The interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP proteins is positively regulated by Abl kinase phosphorylation and is required for chemotactic sensing of EGF gradients. In contrast, Lpd's interaction with the Scar/WAVE complex is regulated by both Src and Abl kinase phosphorylation and is essential for Arp2/3-mediated actin nucleation.  

The differential regulation of these interactions allows Lpd to balance actin filament elongation (via Ena/VASP) and branching/nucleation (via Scar/WAVE-Arp2/3) to optimize protrusive activity during invasion. This is particularly evident in three-dimensional environments, where both interactions are required for efficient chemotactic invasion through matrigel and collagen matrices.  

Lpd also promotes metastasis by enhancing invadopodia formation and function. Lpd localizes to invadopodia and is required for their maturation and matrix degradation activity. This may involve Lpd-mediated recruitment of Mena to invadopodia, linking protrusive activity with ECM remodeling.  

The invention provides specific strategies for inhibiting Lpd-mediated invasion by targeting:  
1. Src and/or Abl kinase phosphorylation of Lpd  
2. The Lpd-Ena/VASP protein interaction interface  
3. The Lpd-Scar/WAVE complex interaction interface  
4. Lpd expression or plasma membrane localization  

These strategies may be implemented using small molecule inhibitors, peptides, antibodies, or nucleic acid-based therapeutics designed to specifically disrupt pro-metastatic Lpd functions while preserving other cellular activities.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Correlation Between Lpd Expression and Clinical Outcomes**  

Analysis of breast cancer patient datasets revealed that high Lpd mRNA expression correlates with reduced metastasis-free and disease-free survival across multiple independent cohorts (FIGS. 1B-1C). Immunohistochemical staining of tumor microarrays showed that moderately increased Lpd levels in the cytoplasm (Histoscore 2; Hazard ratio: 1.765) and at the plasma membrane (Histoscore 2; Hazard ratio: 2.231) were significantly associated with increased risk of breast cancer mortality (FIGS. 1D-1E). Highly increased Lpd staining intensity at the plasma membrane (Histoscore 3) correlated with reduced disease-free (Hazard ratio: 1.652) and metastasis-free survival (Hazard ratio: 1.515).  

**Example 2: Lpd Depletion Inhibits Metastasis In Vivo**  

Orthotopic implantation of Lpd-depleted LM2 cells (a metastatic variant of MDA-MB-231) into mammary fat pads of immunodeficient mice resulted in primary tumors similar in size to controls (FIG. 2A). However, only 15% of mice bearing Lpd-depleted tumors developed macroscopic lung metastases compared to 90% of controls (FIG. 2B). Metastatic burden was significantly reduced in the few Lpd-depleted tumors that did metastasize (FIGS. 2C-2D). Intravital imaging revealed that Lpd knockdown tumors had fewer motile cells with reduced directional migration and protrusive activity (FIGS. 2F-2I).  

**Example 3: Lpd Mediates EGF-Induced Protrusion and Chemosensing**  

Lpd depletion in MTLn3 cells reduced lamellipodial protrusion persistence and distance following EGF stimulation without affecting protrusion speed (FIGS. 3A-3B). Lpd was rapidly recruited to the cell edge within 30 seconds of EGF stimulation (FIGS. 3C-3D), and its depletion delayed lamellipodial initiation (FIGS. 3E-3F). Lpd-deficient cells showed impaired directional sensing in EGF gradient assays (FIGS. 4G-4H), which could be rescued by wild-type Lpd but not by mutants defective in Ena/VASP binding (FIG. 4I).  

**Example 4: Lpd Promotes 3D Invasion Through Both Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE**  

Inverted chemotaxis assays demonstrated that Lpd depletion significantly reduced invasion of MDA-MB-231 and SUM-159 cells through matrigel (FIGS. 5A-5B) and collagen matrices. Invasion promoted by Lpd overexpression persisted even with MMP inhibition (FIG. 5C), indicating both migration-dependent and MMP-dependent mechanisms. Mutational analysis revealed that both Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE binding were required for Lpd-mediated 3D invasion (FIGS. 5D-5E), unlike 2D migration which only requires Scar/WAVE binding.  

**Example 5: Lpd Regulation by Src and Abl Kinases**  

Lpd was phosphorylated by Src kinases independently of Abl activity (FIGS. 6A-6C). While Src phosphorylation specifically enhanced Lpd-Scar/WAVE interactions (FIGS. 6D-6G), Abl phosphorylation regulated both Lpd-Scar/WAVE and Lpd-Ena/VASP binding. Mutation of six c-Src (Y6F) or eight c-Abl (Y8F) phosphorylation sites abolished EGF-induced phosphorylation (FIGS. 7B-7C) and impaired Scar/WAVE binding (FIGS. 7F-7G). These mutants failed to promote protrusive activity (FIG. 7H) or 3D invasion (FIGS. 7D-7E), demonstrating the functional importance of regulated phosphorylation.
