## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to virology, molecular biology, and infectious disease therapeutics. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel systems and methods for producing infectious hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in vitro, particularly for genotypes that are difficult to propagate in conventional cell culture systems, such as genotype 1. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising such particles, methods of using them for research, diagnostics, and vaccine development, and related pharmaceutical applications.  

## BACKGROUND  

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infects approximately 2–3% of the global population, with a majority of infected individuals failing to clear the virus and consequently facing risks of severe liver complications. HCV belongs to the genus *Hepacivirus* within the *Flaviviridæ* family and comprises at least six distinct genotypes, among which genotypes 1a and 1b account for over two-thirds of global infections. Despite its clinical significance, there is no approved vaccine for HCV, and existing treatments exhibit reduced efficacy against genotype 1 compared to other genotypes.  

A major obstacle in HCV research has been the lack of robust in vitro models for viral propagation. The primary animal model, chimpanzees, presents ethical and logistical challenges, while small animal models often fail to replicate true infection. Additionally, most human hepatic cell lines poorly support HCV propagation from patient isolates. Although subgenomic replicons and cell culture-adapted strains (e.g., JFH-1, genotype 2a) have enabled some in vitro studies, these systems are limited by the high error rate of HCV RNA replicase, leading to adaptive mutations that impair viral fitness and infectivity.  

Flaviviruses, a related genus within the *Flaviviridæ* family, share certain features with HCV, including cytoplasmic replication and induction of endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-derived membrane rearrangements. Prior studies demonstrated that flavivirus particles could be produced by providing structural genes in trans, as seen with West Nile virus (WNV) reporter particles (WNVrp). However, analogous trans-complementation systems for HCV have not been successfully developed in hepatic cell lines.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a novel system for producing infectious HCV particles in non-hepatic mammalian cells, particularly baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon (e.g., WNV). The system circumvents the need for HCV RNA replication, thereby avoiding adaptive mutations that compromise viral fitness. Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. **Production System**: A dual-plasmid bacteriophage polymerase (p2B) system for cytoplasmic transcription of HCV genomic RNA, coupled with expression of HCV structural proteins in trans.  
2. **Cell Line**: BHK cells bearing a flavivirus subgenomic replicon (e.g., WNV), which provide a permissive environment for HCV particle assembly and release.  
3. **Particles**: Infectious HCV particles (HCVbp, HCVwt) containing genomic or subgenomic RNA, suitable for infecting target cells (e.g., Huh-7.5, HepG2-CD81, human liver slices).  
4. **Applications**: Use of the particles for studying HCV entry, replication, and neutralization; screening antiviral compounds; and developing vaccines.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS  

### Production System  
The invention employs a cytoplasmic transcription system comprising:  
- A dual-plasmid bacteriophage polymerase (p2B) system encoding T7 and SP6 RNA polymerases under reciprocal promoters.  
- A plasmid encoding HCV genomic or subgenomic RNA flanked by a T7 promoter and hepatitis delta virus ribozyme (HDVrbz) for precise 3´-end formation.  

Upon co-transfection into BHK-WNV cells, the p2B system drives high-level cytoplasmic production of HCV RNA, which is translated into structural (core, E1, E2) and non-structural proteins. The WNV replicon induces membrane rearrangements conducive to HCV particle assembly and release.  

### Particle Characterization  
HCV particles produced in this system exhibit:  
- Buoyant densities of 1.05–1.20 g/cm³ by sucrose gradient centrifugation.  
- Diameters of 50–60 nm by electron microscopy.  
- Infectivity for Huh-7.5 cells, HepG2-CD81 cells, and human liver slices, as demonstrated by NS5A protein expression and RNA replication.  

### Receptor Dependence  
Infectivity assays confirm the involvement of known HCV entry receptors (CD81, SR-B1, ASGP-R) and sensitivity to neutralizing antibodies (e.g., anti-HVR1).  

## EXAMPLES  

### Materials and Methods  

#### Cell Cultures  
- **BHK-WNV cells**: Derived from BHK-21 cells harboring a WNV subgenomic replicon encoding Renilla luciferase. Cultured in D-MEM with 10% FBS and blasticidin.  
- **Huh-7.5 cells**: Permissive for HCV replication. Maintained in standard conditions.  

#### Plasmid Constructs  
- **p2B system**: pCR-T7p/SP6pol and pSL-SP6p/T7pol for cytoplasmic polymerase amplification.  
- **HCV constructs**: p684-SG(L+I)-HDV (HCVbp), p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV (HCVwt), and derivatives with tetracysteine tags (HCVbp-4cys, HCVwt-4cys).  

#### Particle Production  
BHK-WNV cells were transfected with HCV constructs and p2B system. Supernatants were harvested, clarified, and concentrated by ultracentrifugation. Particles were purified on sucrose gradients.  

### Example 2: Assembly and Release of HCV Particles  

BHK-WNV cells transfected with HCVbp constructs released particles containing HCV RNA and structural proteins (core, E1, E2). Electron microscopy revealed 50–60 nm vesicles within dilated ER protrusions, distinct from WNV replicon-induced membrane rearrangements. Sucrose gradient analysis showed HCV RNA enrichment in supernatants (100-fold over WNV RNA).  

### Example 3: Infectivity of HCVbp for Huh-7.5 Cells  

HCVbp infectivity was demonstrated by:  
- RT-qPCR detection of replicating HCV RNA in target cells.  
- NS5A protein expression by confocal microscopy.  
- Inhibition by interferon-α/ribavirin and neutralizing sera.  

### Example 4: Receptor-Mediated Entry  

HCV reporter particles (HCVrp) were used to quantify entry dependence on CD81, SR-B1, and ASGP-R. siRNA knockdown or antibody blockade of these receptors reduced infectivity, confirming their roles in HCV entry.  

### Example 5: Production of JFH-1-Based HCV  

BHK-WNV cells produced infectious particles from JFH-1 (genotype 2a) and Con1-JFH1 chimeric RNAs. Infectivity was validated in Huh-7.5 cells and inhibited by antiviral treatment.  

### Example 6: Authentic HCVwt Production  

Full-length consensus H77 (genotype 1a) RNA yielded HCVwt particles infectious for human liver slices, as shown by NS5A staining and live-cell imaging with tetracysteine tags.  

### Example 7: Infection of HepG2-CD81 Cells  

HCVwt of genotypes 1a, 1b, and 2a infected HepG2-CD81 cells, with RNA replication detectable by RT-qPCR. Interferon-ribavirin treatment abolished replication.  

### Example 8: Applications  

The system enables:  
- Production of patient-derived HCV isolates without adaptive mutations.  
- High-throughput screening of entry inhibitors and neutralizing antibodies.  
- Development of vaccines using authentic HCV particles.
