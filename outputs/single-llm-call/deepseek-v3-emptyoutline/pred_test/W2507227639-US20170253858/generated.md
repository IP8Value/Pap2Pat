## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  
The present invention relates generally to the field of cardiac tissue regeneration and stem cell technology. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for promoting maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes (iPSC-CMs) using three-dimensional (3D) extracellular matrix (ECM) scaffolds derived from adult cardiac tissue. The invention provides novel biomaterial compositions and culture systems that significantly enhance the functional maturation of iPSC-CMs, making them more suitable for therapeutic applications, drug screening, and disease modeling. The disclosed technology addresses critical limitations in current cardiac regeneration approaches by providing mature, functional cardiomyocytes that better recapitulate adult heart tissue characteristics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  
Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction being a predominant manifestation. The ischemic events characteristic of myocardial infarction result in irreversible loss of millions to billions of cardiomyocytes (CMs). Unlike many other cell types, adult mammalian cardiomyocytes have extremely limited regenerative capacity, leading to permanent tissue damage that often progresses to heart failure. Current therapeutic approaches fail to address the fundamental problem of cardiomyocyte loss, creating an urgent need for innovative strategies to regenerate functional cardiac tissue.

Recent advances in stem cell technology have enabled the derivation of cardiomyocytes from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), offering a potential unlimited source of cells for cardiac regeneration. However, iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes generated through current protocols exhibit immature characteristics resembling fetal cardiomyocytes. These immature cells display disorganized sarcomere structures, weak contractile force, spontaneous automaticity, improper calcium handling, and altered electrophysiological properties compared to adult cardiomyocytes. Such immature characteristics pose significant limitations for therapeutic applications, as they increase arrhythmia risk and fail to provide adequate contractile function. Furthermore, immature iPSC-CMs are suboptimal for drug screening and toxicity testing due to their divergent responses compared to adult cardiomyocytes.

Various approaches have been attempted to promote iPSC-CM maturation, including hormonal treatment, mechanical stimulation, electrical pacing, and co-culture with other cell types. While these methods show some promise, they have not succeeded in generating fully mature cardiomyocytes that recapitulate adult cardiac function. The extracellular matrix (ECM) environment has been recognized as an important factor in cell development and tissue maturation, but previous studies have primarily utilized simplified two-dimensional (2D) culture systems or single-component ECM scaffolds that fail to replicate the complex three-dimensional (3D) microenvironment of native heart tissue.

There remains a critical unmet need for reliable methods to generate mature, functional cardiomyocytes from iPSCs that can be used safely and effectively for cardiac regeneration therapies. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel compositions and methods that utilize decellularized adult cardiac ECM in a 3D configuration to promote robust iPSC-CM maturation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  
The present invention provides compositions and methods for promoting maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes (iPSC-CMs) using three-dimensional extracellular matrix (ECM) scaffolds derived from adult cardiac tissue. The detailed description that follows provides specific embodiments and implementations of the invention, but it should be understood that these are provided as examples and the invention is not limited to these particular embodiments.

### Preparation of Decellularized Cardiac ECM  
The invention utilizes extracellular matrix derived from decellularized cardiac tissue, with particular emphasis on adult cardiac ECM due to its superior maturation-promoting properties compared to fetal cardiac ECM. The decellularization process involves several critical steps to remove cellular components while preserving the native ECM structure and composition.

Cardiac tissue, preferably from bovine sources, is first processed by removing excess fat and connective tissue. The cleaned tissue is then cut into small pieces (approximately 3 mm³) and subjected to a series of detergent treatments. For adult cardiac tissue, a solution containing 1% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) is used for decellularization, while fetal cardiac tissue requires only 0.5% SDS due to its more delicate structure. The tissue is incubated in SDS solution with continuous stirring for approximately 72 hours at room temperature, followed by treatment with 1% Triton X-100 for 90 minutes to remove residual cellular material and detergents.

Following detergent treatment, the tissue undergoes extensive washing in distilled water (7 washes of 30 minutes each) to remove all traces of detergents and cellular debris. The washed tissue is then treated with antibiotic-antimycotic solution in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 18 hours at 4°C to ensure sterility. The decellularized tissue is lyophilized and milled into a fine powder using mechanical grinding.

The ECM powder is digested using pepsin (2 mg/mL) in 0.01 M hydrochloric acid (HCl) with continuous stirring at room temperature for 48-72 hours until fully solubilized. The digested ECM solution is divided into aliquots and stored at -80°C until use. This preparation method preserves the native composition of cardiac ECM proteins including collagens, fibrinogen, periostin, fibrillin, fibulin, and fibronectin, while completely removing cellular components that could trigger immune responses.

### Formation of 3D ECM Hydrogels  
The solubilized cardiac ECM can be reconstituted into three-dimensional hydrogels with controlled mechanical properties. For hydrogel formation, the digested ECM solution is neutralized to physiological pH using NaOH and diluted to the desired protein concentration (typically 4-8 mg/mL) in PBS. The neutralized ECM solution is supplemented with NaCl to achieve final salt concentrations that promote proper fibrillogenesis.

The ECM solution is allowed to polymerize into a hydrogel at 37°C for 30-60 minutes. Rheological measurements demonstrate that adult cardiac ECM forms hydrogels with approximately 10-fold greater stiffness (storage modulus G' ≈ 67.5 Pa) compared to fetal cardiac ECM hydrogels (G' ≈ 7.2 Pa). This difference in mechanical properties is critical for promoting cardiomyocyte maturation, as the increased stiffness of adult ECM better mimics the mechanical environment of native adult heart tissue.

Second-harmonic generation (SHG) microscopy and multiphoton imaging reveal distinct structural differences between adult and fetal cardiac ECM hydrogels. Adult ECM displays higher collagen fiber signal intensity, larger fiber bundles, and more organized elastin fiber networks compared to fetal ECM. These structural characteristics contribute to the maturation-promoting properties of the adult cardiac ECM.

### Culture of iPSC-Derived Cardiomyocytes in 3D ECM  
The invention provides methods for differentiating iPSCs into cardiomyocytes and culturing them in the 3D cardiac ECM hydrogels to promote maturation. Human iPSCs are maintained in feeder-free conditions on growth factor-reduced Matrigel with mTeSR1 medium. For cardiac differentiation, iPSCs are seeded as single cells in Matrigel-coated plates with Rho kinase inhibitor (Y-27632) to enhance survival.

Cardiac induction is initiated at 75-90% confluence using a defined protocol involving sequential treatment with Wnt pathway modulators. Specifically, cells are treated with the GSK3 inhibitor CHIR99021 (6 μM) in RPMI/B27 medium without insulin on day 0. After 24 hours (day 1), the medium is replaced with fresh RPMI/B27 without insulin. On day 3, the Wnt inhibitor IWP2 (5 μM) is added in RPMI/B27 without insulin. From day 5 onward, cells are maintained in RPMI/B27 with insulin, with medium changes every 3 days. Spontaneous beating is typically observed by day 12 of differentiation.

For 3D culture, iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes are harvested and mixed with the neutralized cardiac ECM solution prior to gelation. The cell-ECM mixture is plated in appropriate culture vessels and allowed to polymerize at 37°C. Alternatively, pre-formed ECM hydrogels can be seeded with cardiomyocytes by injecting cell suspensions into the gel matrix. The 3D cultures are maintained in cardiac culture medium with regular medium changes.

### Characterization of Mature iPSC-CMs  
iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM exhibit molecular, structural, and functional characteristics of mature cardiomyocytes. At the gene expression level, these cells show significant upregulation of maturation markers compared to cells cultured in 2D or in fetal ECM. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis demonstrates increased expression of calcium-handling genes including Junctin (JCN), Calsequestrin 2 (CASQ2), L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel (CACNA1C), Triadin (TRDN), sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺ ATPase (ATP2A2/SERCA2a), and potassium/sodium hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated channel 4 (HCN4).

Protein analysis by immunocytochemistry and Western blot confirms increased expression of key cardiac proteins including cardiac troponin T (cTNT), CaV1.2, MYL2, and Connexin 43 (Cx43) in cells cultured in 3D adult ECM compared to 2D controls. These molecular changes correlate with improved functional maturation as assessed by calcium handling and contractile properties.

Calcium imaging using genetically encoded reporters (e.g., GCaMP6) reveals that iPSC-CMs in 3D adult ECM exhibit more mature calcium handling kinetics compared to 2D cultures. Specifically, cells in 3D show increased calcium transient amplitude, faster maximum upslope and downslope rates, and shorter time to 50% decay. These parameters indicate improved sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium storage and release, characteristic of mature cardiomyocytes.

### Functional Assessment and Applications  
The matured iPSC-CMs produced using the methods of the invention demonstrate appropriate pharmacological responses to cardiac drugs, making them valuable for drug screening and toxicity testing. Treatment with the β-adrenergic agonist isoproterenol (1 μM) induces increased beat rate, which can be reversed by the β-blocker propranolol (10 μM), recapitulating the responses of adult cardiomyocytes. Exposure to caffeine, which acts on ryanodine receptors to increase calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, induces more rapid calcium transients in 3D-cultured cells compared to 2D controls, indicating more mature calcium handling machinery.

The reduced spontaneous automaticity and improved electrical stability of iPSC-CMs matured in 3D adult ECM make them safer candidates for cell therapy applications. Unlike immature iPSC-CMs that display erratic spontaneous beating, the matured cells exhibit more stable electrophysiological properties with lower arrhythmia risk upon transplantation.

The invention also provides methods for scaling production of mature iPSC-CMs suitable for clinical applications. The 3D culture system can be adapted to bioreactor formats for large-scale cardiomyocyte production. The cells can be delivered as 3D constructs or as dissociated cells for cardiac repair, optionally in combination with the cardiac ECM hydrogel as a delivery vehicle.

### EXAMPLES  
The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.

**Example 1: Preparation of Adult Cardiac ECM Hydrogel**  
Adult bovine cardiac tissue was decellularized using 1% SDS for 72 hours followed by 1% Triton X-100 for 90 minutes. After extensive washing, the tissue was lyophilized and milled into powder. The powder was digested with pepsin (2 mg/mL in 0.01 M HCl) for 48 hours at room temperature. The solubilized ECM was neutralized to pH 7.4 with NaOH and diluted to 6 mg/mL in PBS. The solution was allowed to polymerize at 37°C for 1 hour, forming a hydrogel with storage modulus G' = 67.5 ± 12.6 Pa.

**Example 2: iPSC-CM Culture in 3D Adult ECM**  
Human iPSCs were differentiated into cardiomyocytes using a small molecule protocol. On day 12 of differentiation, beating cardiomyocytes were dissociated and mixed with neutralized adult cardiac ECM solution (4 mg/mL). The cell-ECM mixture (1×10⁶ cells/mL) was plated in 24-well plates and allowed to gel at 37°C. Cultures were maintained in RPMI/B27 with insulin, with medium changes every 3 days.

**Example 3: Maturation Marker Expression**  
After 7 days in 3D culture, iPSC-CMs showed significant upregulation of maturation markers compared to 2D controls. qRT-PCR analysis revealed 5.2-fold increase in Junctin (JCN), 3.8-fold increase in Calsequestrin 2 (CASQ2), and 4.1-fold increase in MYL2 expression (p<0.01, n=3). Immunostaining demonstrated increased CaV1.2 and Connexin 43 protein expression in 3D cultures.

**Example 4: Calcium Handling Properties**  
GCaMP6-expressing iPSC-CMs in 3D adult ECM showed improved calcium transients compared to 2D. Calcium transient amplitude increased by 2.3-fold, maximum upslope increased by 1.8-fold, and time to 50% decay decreased by 35% (p<0.01, n=5). Cells responded appropriately to isoproterenol (1 μM) with increased beat rate (35% increase) that was blocked by propranolol (10 μM).

**Example 5: Drug Response Profiling**  
iPSC-CMs cultured in 3D adult ECM for 7 days were treated with various cardiac drugs. The cells showed concentration-dependent responses to verapamil (L-type calcium channel blocker) and E-4031 (hERG channel blocker) that closely matched published data from adult cardiomyocytes. The 3D-cultured cells demonstrated improved predictive accuracy compared to 2D-cultured cells in detecting known cardiotoxic compounds.
