# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and allergy therapeutics, specifically concerning immunoglobulin E (IgE) and its interactions with therapeutic antibodies. More particularly, the invention pertains to the structural characterization of the complex formed between IgE and the therapeutic antibody omalizumab, including the identification of key binding epitopes and conformational states that influence this interaction. The disclosure further encompasses engineered IgE variants resistant to omalizumab binding, methods for exchanging cell-bound IgE with such variants, and therapeutic applications thereof in the treatment of allergic disorders.  

## BACKGROUND  

Immunoglobulin E plays a central role in allergic diseases through its ability to bind the high-affinity Fcε receptor (FcεRI) on mast cells and basophils. Crosslinking of receptor-bound IgE by allergens triggers cellular activation and release of inflammatory mediators, leading to allergic symptoms. Omalizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting IgE, has been used therapeutically to neutralize free IgE and reduce allergic responses. However, the precise structural mechanisms underlying omalizumab's inhibitory effects on IgE interactions with both FcεRI and CD23 receptors have remained incompletely understood.  

Previous studies have identified that omalizumab binds the Cε3 domain of IgE and competes with FcεRI binding, but the structural basis for this competition and the potential for omalizumab to disrupt preformed IgE:FcεRI complexes has not been fully elucidated. Moreover, while IgE exists in both open and closed conformational states associated with receptor binding, the impact of these conformations on omalizumab binding has not been characterized. There remains a need in the field for detailed structural understanding of the omalizumab:IgE interaction to enable development of improved anti-IgE therapeutics and combination therapies.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a comprehensive structural and functional characterization of the omalizumab:IgE complex, revealing novel aspects of its binding mechanism and therapeutic implications. Through X-ray crystallography, the inventors have determined the three-dimensional structure of omalizumab bound to an engineered IgE-Fc3-4 fragment, identifying a perpendicular binding orientation of the omalizumab Fab relative to the IgE Cε3 domain. The structure demonstrates that omalizumab contacts symmetric binding sites on the face of the two Cε3 domains, predominantly interacting with β-sheet residues below the FcεRIα-binding loops.  

Contrary to previous models, the structural data show that omalizumab does not inhibit IgE:FcεRI interactions by distorting the FcεRI-binding site, but rather through steric blocking of both FcεRI and CD23 binding sites. Kinetic analyses reveal that the Cε2 domains of full-length IgE mask an omalizumab-binding epitope that becomes exposed in IgE-Fc3-4 fragments, explaining omalizumab's specificity for free IgE. The invention further demonstrates that omalizumab can bind both open and closed conformations of IgE with similar affinity, and characterizes the extensive network of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions at the omalizumab:IgE interface.  

The disclosure includes the generation of an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant (IgE-R419N-Fc3-4) through introduction of a glycosylation site at the core of the omalizumab epitope. This variant maintains FcεRI binding while being refractory to omalizumab neutralization. The inventors demonstrate that co-administration of this resistant IgE variant with omalizumab enables effective exchange of native IgE on human basophils and B cells, maintaining receptor occupancy while removing allergen-specific IgE. Furthermore, combination treatment shows synergistic inhibition of basophil activation, suggesting improved therapeutic potential over omalizumab monotherapy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### I. DEFINITIONS  

As used throughout this specification, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

The term "IgE" refers to immunoglobulin E, including full-length IgE molecules comprising Cε1, Cε2, Cε3, and Cε4 domains, as well as fragments thereof such as IgE-Fc3-4 comprising only the Cε3 and Cε4 domains.  

The term "omalizumab" refers to the humanized monoclonal antibody that binds IgE, also known by the trade name Xolair, including both intact antibody and Fab fragments thereof.  

The term "FcεRI" refers to the high-affinity IgE receptor expressed on mast cells and basophils, including both membrane-bound and soluble forms of the α chain subunit.  

The term "CD23" refers to the low-affinity IgE receptor (FcεRII) expressed on B cells and other leukocytes.  

The term "IgE-R419N-Fc3-4" refers to an engineered IgE fragment comprising the Cε3 and Cε4 domains with an arginine to asparagine mutation at position 419 that introduces a novel glycosylation site rendering the molecule resistant to omalizumab binding.  

### II. MODES OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention encompasses several embodiments relating to the structural characterization of the omalizumab:IgE interaction and its therapeutic applications. In one embodiment, the invention provides the crystal structure of the complex between omalizumab and an IgE-Fc3-4 fragment at 2.5 Å resolution. The structure reveals that omalizumab binds perpendicularly to the IgE Cε3 domain, contacting symmetric binding sites on the face of both Cε3 domains in the IgE dimer.  

The binding interface comprises 23 IgE residues burying approximately 725 Å² of surface area, with interactions mediated through a network of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic contacts. Key interacting residues include R376, S378, K380, Q417, and R419 on IgE, which form specific contacts with omalizumab complementarity-determining regions (CDRs). The structure demonstrates minimal overlap with FcεRI binding sites, with only peripheral contact at residues P426 and R427, indicating that omalizumab primarily inhibits receptor binding through steric blocking rather than direct competition for binding residues.  

In another embodiment, the invention provides kinetic analyses comparing omalizumab binding to full-length IgE versus IgE-Fc3-4 fragments. These studies reveal that the association rate (ka) for full-length IgE is 15-30-fold slower than for IgE-Fc3-4 fragments, while the dissociation rate (kd) is 4.5-10.9-fold slower, resulting in only minor differences in overall binding affinity. This kinetic profile is attributed to masking of one omalizumab epitope by the Cε2 domains in full-length IgE, while both epitopes remain accessible in IgE-Fc3-4 fragments.  

The invention further encompasses characterization of omalizumab binding to different conformational states of IgE. Using an engineered IgE-G335C-Fc3-4 mutant locked in the closed conformation, the inventors demonstrate that omalizumab binds with similar kinetics to both closed and open (FcεRI-bound) conformations of IgE. This finding indicates that the omalizumab epitope remains stable across different IgE conformational states relevant to receptor binding.  

In a therapeutic embodiment, the invention provides an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant (IgE-R419N-Fc3-4) generated by introducing an N-linked glycosylation site at position R419 in the core of the omalizumab epitope. This variant completely abolishes omalizumab binding while maintaining FcεRI binding capability. The invention demonstrates that co-administration of this resistant IgE variant with omalizumab enables effective exchange of native IgE on human basophils and B cells, maintaining receptor occupancy while removing allergen-specific IgE.  

### III. EXPERIMENTAL  

The experimental data supporting this invention include crystallographic, biophysical, and cellular studies. The omalizumab:IgE-G335C-Fc3-4 complex was crystallized in the P21 space group and its structure solved by molecular replacement to 2.5 Å resolution. The asymmetric unit contained two IgE-Fc3-4 molecules and four omalizumab-Fab copies, providing four independent views of the binding interface.  

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) studies were conducted to measure binding kinetics between omalizumab and various IgE constructs. Full-length IgE (clone Sus11) showed a 15-30-fold slower association rate (ka = 1.1 × 10⁵ M⁻¹s⁻¹) compared to IgE-Fc3-4 fragments (ka = 1.6-3.3 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹), while dissociation rates (kd) were 4.5-10.9-fold slower for full-length IgE (kd = 1.3 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹) versus fragments (kd = 1.2-2.9 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹). These measurements were performed at 25°C using a BIAcore X100 instrument with omalizumab immobilized on a CM5 chip.  

Basophil activation tests were conducted to evaluate the therapeutic potential of IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 in combination with omalizumab. Basophils from healthy donors were stripped of native IgE using disruptive inhibitors (E2_79 or bi53_79 DARPins), reloaded with NP-specific JW8-IgE, and treated with various combinations of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3-4. After 6 days of treatment, combination therapy with 500 nM omalizumab and 1 nM IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 nearly completely inhibited basophil activation upon NP-antigen challenge, demonstrating synergistic effects compared to either agent alone.  

Flow cytometry experiments confirmed successful exchange of cell-bound IgE with IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 in the presence of omalizumab. Human basophils and CD23+ B cells treated with both agents showed complete removal of native IgE while maintaining surface levels of the resistant IgE variant. These cellular studies were performed using biotinylated IgE variants and specific antibody staining for different IgE species, with analysis conducted on a 10-color flow cytometer.  

The invention further includes computational analysis of steric overlap between omalizumab and IgE receptors. Using structural alignment and volume calculation tools, the inventors quantified substantial steric conflicts between omalizumab and FcεRI (1,183 Å³ overlap) as well as even greater overlap with CD23, explaining the inhibitory effects on both receptor interactions. These calculations were performed using the CCP4 software suite with structures aligned based on the Cε3 domain.
