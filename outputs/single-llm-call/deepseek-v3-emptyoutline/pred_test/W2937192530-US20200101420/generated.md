# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to photothermal carbon membranes and methods for their preparation. More specifically, the invention pertains to a fluorosilane-coated, pore-controlled graphite foam (GF) membrane exhibiting enhanced photothermal absorption properties. The membrane is particularly suited for solar-driven applications, including water purification, desalination, and thermal energy conversion. The invention further encompasses the fabrication process involving the deposition of fluorosilane molecules on a GF substrate, followed by coating with a graphite-phenolic resin slurry to achieve controlled porosity and optimized light absorption.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Conventional photothermal materials often suffer from inefficiencies in solar absorption, poor thermal conductivity, or limited durability under prolonged exposure to high-intensity light. Existing membranes may also exhibit inadequate hydrophobicity, leading to reduced performance in applications requiring selective liquid permeation. Prior attempts to enhance photothermal conversion have included the use of carbon-based materials, such as graphene or carbon nanotubes, but these solutions frequently involve complex synthesis methods or high production costs.  

Graphite foam (GF) has emerged as a promising candidate due to its high thermal conductivity and porous structure. However, unmodified GF membranes typically lack the necessary surface chemistry to maximize solar absorption and repel contaminants. There remains a need for a cost-effective, scalable photothermal membrane that combines high solar absorption efficiency, controlled porosity, and long-term stability under operational conditions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the limitations of prior art by providing a photothermal carbon membrane comprising a fluorosilane-coated graphite foam (GF) substrate with a pore-controlling graphite-phenolic resin layer. The membrane exhibits superior solar absorption (A) efficiency, calculated as A = 1−R−T, where R is reflectance and T is transmission efficiency. In preferred embodiments, the GF substrate has a thickness of approximately 5 mm, rendering transmission negligible.  

Key features of the invention include:  
1. **Fluorosilane Deposition**: The GF substrate is treated with (heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl) trichlorosilane via solution immersion, enhancing hydrophobicity and light-trapping properties.  
2. **Pore Control**: A slurry of graphite particles and phenolic resin (weight ratio 2:1) is applied to the GF surface, followed by thermal curing at 300 °C to stabilize the pore structure.  
3. **High Absorption Efficiency**: The membrane achieves optimal light absorption across the 330–2500 nm spectrum under AM 1.5 G solar irradiation (100 mW cm⁻²).  

The invention further includes methods for preparing the membrane, such as machining GF into desired geometries (e.g., tubes or chambers), coating with fluorosilane, and applying the graphite-resin slurry. The resulting membrane is durable, scalable, and suitable for industrial applications requiring efficient solar-thermal conversion.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Example  

In a representative embodiment, the photothermal membrane is fabricated as follows:  

1. **Substrate Preparation**: Graphite foam (GF) with tailored pore sizes is machined into a chamber or tube configuration using electrical saws and drills. The GF is derived from heat-treated PI Mesophase Pitch, sieved to 250 mesh, and blended with 7% Ground Foam Graphene platelets for structural reinforcement.  

2. **Fluorosilane Functionalization**: The GF substrate is immersed in a 0.1 wt% solution of (heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl) trichlorosilane in hexane for 12 hours. After coating, the substrate is oven-dried at 100 °C for 1 hour and rinsed with water and ethanol to remove unbound silanes.  

3. **Pore-Stabilizing Layer**: A slurry is prepared by mixing graphite particles (Asbury Grade 4827, 225–275 m² g⁻¹) with phenolic resin (Durite SC1008) at a 2:1 weight ratio. The slurry is applied to the GF chamber and lid using a knife-coating technique, dried overnight, and thermally cured at 300 °C for 1 hour to form a robust, pore-controlled layer.  

4. **Performance Characterization**:  
   - **Solar Absorption**: Under 100 mW cm⁻² AM 1.5 G irradiation, the membrane exhibits minimal reflectance (R) and negligible transmission (T), yielding absorption efficiencies exceeding 90%.  
   - **Hydrophobicity**: Water contact angles exceed 150°, confirming superhydrophobicity.  
   - **Durability**: Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS) confirms stable fluorosilane bonding after prolonged solar exposure.  

The invention is not limited to the above example. Variations may include alternative carbon precursors, resin binders, or coating methods without departing from the core inventive concept.
