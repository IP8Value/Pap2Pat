# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology diagnostics, specifically to methods for detecting breast cancer through minimally invasive means. More particularly, the invention pertains to the identification and use of circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) as diagnostic biomarkers for Luminal A-like breast cancer. The invention encompasses methods for profiling miRNA expression in blood samples, statistical validation of miRNA signatures, and their application in clinical settings to distinguish between healthy individuals and those with Luminal A-like breast cancer. The disclosed biomarkers offer a non-invasive alternative to traditional diagnostic techniques such as mammography and tissue biopsy, enabling early detection and improved patient outcomes.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality among women worldwide, with over 1.3 million new cases diagnosed annually. Despite advances in targeted therapies, such as hormonal treatments for estrogen receptor (ER)-positive tumors and trastuzumab for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu)-positive tumors, metastatic progression continues to be the primary cause of death. Current diagnostic methods rely heavily on mammography and invasive tissue biopsies, which are not only uncomfortable for patients but also limited in their ability to provide real-time monitoring of disease progression or subtype-specific detection.  

Circulating biomarkers present a promising avenue for non-invasive cancer detection. Blood-based diagnostics are advantageous due to their minimal invasiveness, ease of collection, and ability to reflect systemic physiological and pathological states. Among potential biomarkers, microRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as particularly promising candidates due to their stability in circulation and their established roles in tumorigenesis. MiRNAs regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally and are frequently dysregulated in cancer, functioning either as oncogenes or tumor suppressors. Previous studies have demonstrated altered miRNA expression patterns in breast cancer tissue, correlating with molecular subtypes and receptor status. However, the translation of these findings into reliable circulating biomarkers for clinical use has remained elusive.  

A critical unmet need exists for a minimally invasive, subtype-specific diagnostic tool that can complement existing imaging techniques and reduce reliance on tissue biopsies. The Luminal A subtype, characterized by ER positivity, progesterone receptor (PR) positivity, and HER2/neu negativity, accounts for over 70% of breast cancer cases. Early and accurate detection of this subtype is essential for timely intervention and personalized treatment strategies. The present invention addresses this need by identifying a panel of circulating miRNAs that are differentially expressed in Luminal A-like breast cancer, providing a foundation for the development of a blood-based diagnostic test.  

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION  

The primary object of the invention is to provide a method for the non-invasive detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer by analyzing circulating miRNA profiles in blood samples. Specifically, the invention aims to identify a set of miRNAs whose expression levels are significantly altered in patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. A further object is to validate these miRNAs as robust diagnostic biomarkers through statistical and clinical analyses, ensuring high sensitivity and specificity.  

An additional object is to develop a miRNA-based assay that can be integrated into routine clinical practice, offering a complementary tool to mammography for early cancer detection. The invention also seeks to elucidate the relationship between circulating miRNA levels and clinicopathological parameters, such as tumor grade, nodal status, and disease stage, to enhance prognostic accuracy.  

Ultimately, the invention aims to improve patient outcomes by enabling earlier diagnosis, reducing the need for invasive procedures, and facilitating subtype-specific treatment decisions. By leveraging advancements in miRNA profiling and data analysis, the invention provides a scalable and reproducible diagnostic platform with broad clinical applicability.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention discloses a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer by detecting and quantifying specific circulating miRNAs in blood samples. Through comprehensive microarray profiling and validation studies, a panel of miRNAs—including miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-223, and miR-652—was identified as differentially expressed in patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer. These miRNAs exhibit consistent dysregulation in both blood and tumor tissue, supporting their utility as biomarkers.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **MicroRNA Profiling**: Utilizing microarray technology to screen for miRNAs with altered expression in the blood of Luminal A-like breast cancer patients.  
2. **Validation by RQ-PCR**: Confirming miRNA expression patterns in independent cohorts using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RQ-PCR).  
3. **Statistical Analysis**: Applying advanced data-mining algorithms, such as artificial neural networks (ANNs), to identify miRNAs with high predictive power for cancer detection.  
4. **Diagnostic Panels**: Combining multiple miRNAs (e.g., miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652) to achieve superior sensitivity (77%) and specificity (74%) compared to single biomarkers.  
5. **Clinical Correlation**: Demonstrating associations between miRNA levels and clinicopathological features, such as tumor size and nodal status, to enhance diagnostic and prognostic utility.  

The invention also encompasses kits and reagents for performing the disclosed methods, including primers and probes for miRNA detection, as well as software tools for data analysis and interpretation. By providing a minimally invasive and highly accurate diagnostic approach, the invention represents a significant advancement in the early detection and management of Luminal A-like breast cancer.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that specific circulating miRNAs are dysregulated in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer. The following sections detail the experimental methodologies, validation studies, and statistical analyses that underpin the invention.  

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis  

The validation of candidate miRNAs involved a multi-step analytical process to ensure robustness and reproducibility. Blood samples were collected from 110 women, including 54 patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer and 56 healthy controls. Total RNA was extracted from 1 mL of whole blood using TRI Reagent BD, and miRNA expression was profiled using TaqMan Human miRNA Arrays.  

Microarray data were analyzed using a nonlinear artificial neural network (ANN) algorithm to identify miRNAs with altered expression in cancer patients. The ANN employed a three-layer architecture with sigmoid transfer functions and Monte Carlo cross-validation to mitigate overfitting and false discovery. This approach generated a rank-ordered list of miRNAs based on their classification performance in blind datasets.  

For validation, ten miRNAs were selected for RQ-PCR analysis in an independent cohort of 44 patients and 46 controls. PCR amplification efficiencies were calculated, and cycle threshold (CT) values were normalized to the endogenous control miR-16. Statistical significance was assessed using Mann-Whitney U tests, ANOVA, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.  

### Results  

The ANN algorithm identified 76 miRNAs with detectable and altered expression in Luminal A-like breast cancer. Validation by RQ-PCR confirmed significant dysregulation of miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-223, and miR-652. Key findings include:  
- **miR-181a and miR-652**: Downregulated in both blood and tumor tissue of cancer patients (p = 0.004 and p = 0.009, respectively).  
- **miR-29a and miR-223**: Consistently under-expressed in the circulation of cancer patients (p < 0.001 and p = 0.004, respectively).  

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that a combination of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80, with sensitivity and specificity of 77% and 74%, respectively. These miRNAs were also associated with clinicopathological parameters, such as tumor size and nodal status, further supporting their diagnostic utility.  

The invention thus provides a reliable and minimally invasive method for detecting Luminal A-like breast cancer, with potential applications in early diagnosis, monitoring, and personalized treatment strategies.
