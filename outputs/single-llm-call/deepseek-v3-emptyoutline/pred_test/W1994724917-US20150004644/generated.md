# DESCRIPTION  

## EXAMPLES  

### 1. Equipment  

The present invention utilizes a Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) system for the rapid and accurate detection of delta-toxin in *Staphylococcus aureus* isolates. The MALDI-TOF MS system comprises an Axima Assurance® mass spectrometer (Shimadzu Biotech) equipped with LaunchPad® software (version 2.8.4.20081127) for spectral acquisition and analysis. The system is calibrated using *Escherichia coli* ATCC 8739 as a reference standard, ensuring consistent and reproducible mass measurements.  

For sample preparation, disposable target plates (Fleximass DS®, bioMérieux) are employed to deposit bacterial samples. The matrix solution, consisting of α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid (CHCA) or sinapinic acid, is applied to facilitate ionization. The mass spectrometer operates in positive reflectron mode, scanning a mass-to-charge (*m/z*) range of 2000 to 20000 Thomson (Th), with a specific focus on the 2800–3200 Th range for delta-toxin detection.  

The system further includes a MALDI Time-of-Flight/Time-of-Flight (TOF/TOF) module (Ultraflex II, Bruker Daltonics) for confirmatory peptide fragmentation analysis. This module enables high-resolution tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) to validate the identity of delta-toxin peaks. The MALDI-TOF/TOF spectra are processed using Mascot® software (Matrix Science) for database searching against the UniProtKB/SwissProt database, ensuring accurate peptide identification.  

### 2. Methodology  

The methodology for detecting delta-toxin and assessing *agr* functionality in *S. aureus* isolates comprises the following steps:  

**Sample Preparation:** Bacterial colonies are cultured on trypticase soy agar with horse blood (TSH) or other suitable media (e.g., Columbia agar with 5% sheep blood, chocolate agar with Polyvitex®) for 18–48 hours at 35±2°C under aerobic conditions. A single colony is smeared onto a MALDI target plate and overlaid with matrix solution (CHCA or sinapinic acid).  

**Mass Spectrometry Analysis:** The MALDI-TOF MS system is calibrated in the 2800–3200 Th range using polyethylene glycol (PEG3000) as an internal standard. Spectra are acquired in positive reflectron mode, and the presence of delta-toxin is determined by identifying peaks at 3005±5 Th (wild-type delta-toxin) or 3035±5 Th (G10S variant).  

**Confirmation of Delta-Toxin Identity:** For ambiguous peaks, MALDI-TOF/TOF MS is performed to obtain fragmentation spectra. The resulting MS/MS data are analyzed using Mascot® to confirm peptide sequences.  

**Correlation with *agr* Functionality:** The absence of delta-toxin peaks (3005±5 Th or 3035±5 Th) indicates *agr* dysfunction. This is further validated by Northern blot analysis of *agr*-RNAIII expression or synergistic hemolysis assays with *Staphylococcus intermedius* N890223 as a beta-toxin producer.  

**Clinical Interpretation:** Delta-toxin-negative isolates are flagged for potential chronic infections or glycopeptide resistance. The results are integrated into diagnostic workflows to guide therapeutic decisions.  

### 3. Results  

The invention demonstrates robust and reproducible detection of delta-toxin in *S. aureus* isolates, with the following key findings:  

1. **Specificity of Delta-Toxin Peaks:** Purified delta-toxin (1 fg to 500 ng) yields a peak at 3005±1 Th, matching the theoretical mass of monoprotonated delta-toxin (3005.3 Th). Isogenic *agr*-negative strains (e.g., RN6911) lack this peak, confirming specificity.  

2. **Robustness Across Culture Conditions:** Delta-toxin detection is unaffected by incubation time (18–48 hours) or growth media (TSH, COS, PVX, CHROM ID SA®). Signal intensity remains stable, though slightly reduced at 48 hours.  

3. **Clinical Isolate Analysis:** Among 168 clinical isolates, 139 (82.7%) exhibit the 3005±5 Th peak, 12 (7.1%) show the 3035±5 Th peak (G10S variant), and 17 (10.1%) lack both peaks, indicating *agr* dysfunction.  

4. **Association with Chronic Infections:** Delta-toxin-negative isolates are significantly associated with chronic infections (p = 0.005), including cystic fibrosis and diabetic foot infections.  

5. **Glycopeptide Resistance Correlation:** Delta-toxin deficiency is independently linked to glycopeptide-intermediate *S. aureus* (GISA/hGISA) phenotypes (p = 0.023).  

6. **Within-Host Selection of *agr* Dysfunction:** Sequential isolates from patients reveal shifts from delta-toxin-positive to -negative status, suggesting in vivo selection of *agr* dysfunction during chronic infection.  

The invention thus provides a rapid (<1 hour), high-throughput method to assess *agr* functionality and predict clinical outcomes, enhancing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for *S. aureus* infections.
