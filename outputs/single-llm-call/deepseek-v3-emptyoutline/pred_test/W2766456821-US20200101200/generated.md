## TECHNICAL FIELD  
The present invention relates to the field of corneal regenerative medicine, specifically to methods for identifying, characterizing, and producing human corneal endothelial cells (CECs) and their progenitor cells. More particularly, the invention discloses novel molecular markers and transcription factors that are specific to corneal endothelial cells, including POU6F2, TFAP2B, and LMX1B, which enable the identification, isolation, and quality assessment of CECs derived from stem cells or progenitor cells. The invention further provides methods for differentiating corneal endothelial progenitor cells into functional CECs using these markers, as well as compositions and kits for corneal endothelial regeneration.

## BACKGROUND ART  
Corneal endothelial cells play a critical role in maintaining corneal transparency by regulating ion and fluid transport between the corneal stroma and anterior chamber. However, human CECs have limited proliferative capacity in vivo and in vitro, making cell replacement therapies challenging. Current treatments for corneal endothelial dysfunction rely on allogeneic corneal transplantation, which is limited by donor scarcity. Alternative approaches using cultured CECs face significant obstacles, as these cells tend to lose their characteristic phenotype during expansion, undergoing endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition.  

Previous attempts to develop alternative sources of CECs have included differentiation from iris cells, corneal stromal cells, embryonic stem cells, and induced pluripotent stem cells. However, these approaches lack reliable markers for identifying and purifying bona fide CECs. While some CEC markers have been proposed, such as SLC4A11 and COL8A2, comprehensive molecular characterization of human CECs and their differentiation process has been lacking. The neural crest origin of CECs suggests that transcription factors involved in neural crest development may play roles in CEC specification, but key regulators of human CEC lineage commitment remain unidentified. There exists an unmet need for specific molecular markers that can reliably identify CECs and monitor their differentiation status for use in regenerative medicine applications.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  
The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of specific and reliable markers for identifying and characterizing human corneal endothelial cells and their progenitors. Current limitations include: (1) inability to maintain CEC phenotype during in vitro culture; (2) absence of definitive markers for assessing CEC differentiation status; and (3) lack of understanding about molecular mechanisms controlling CEC differentiation from progenitor cells. These limitations hinder the development of reliable cell-based therapies for corneal endothelial disorders.

### Solution to Problem  
The invention provides a solution through the identification of specific molecular markers and transcription factors that are uniquely expressed in human corneal endothelial cells. Using comprehensive transcriptome analysis of CECs at different differentiation stages, the inventors have identified 45 promoters at 28 gene loci that are specifically expressed in both corneal endothelial tissues and cultured CECs. Among these, three transcription factors - POU6F2, TFAP2B, and LMX1B - show distinctive expression patterns during CEC differentiation.  

Particularly significant is the discovery that POU6F2 serves as a maturation marker for CECs, being upregulated during differentiation of corneal endothelial progenitor cells (HCEPs) into differentiated HCEPs (dHCEPs). This finding enables the development of methods for: (1) identifying and isolating genuine CECs from mixed cell populations; (2) monitoring CEC differentiation status; and (3) producing functional CECs from stem cells or progenitor cells for therapeutic applications.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  
The present invention provides several advantageous effects:  

1. Enables reliable identification and purification of bona fide corneal endothelial cells using newly discovered specific markers.  
2. Provides tools for quality control in CEC production for regenerative medicine by monitoring differentiation status through POU6F2 expression.  
3. Facilitates development of standardized protocols for differentiating stem cells into functional CECs by identifying key transcription factors involved in CEC maturation.  
4. Offers potential diagnostic markers for corneal endothelial disorders through association of specific markers with corneal endothelial function.  
5. Allows production of homogeneous CEC populations for transplantation by using POU6F2 expression as a maturation criterion.  
6. Provides insights into molecular mechanisms of CEC differentiation that can inform optimization of culture conditions for maintaining CEC phenotype.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention encompasses several embodiments relating to the identification, characterization, and production of corneal endothelial cells using newly discovered molecular markers.  

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for identifying corneal endothelial cells in a biological sample, comprising detecting expression of at least one marker selected from the group consisting of POU6F2, TFAP2B, and LMX1B. The detection may be performed at the RNA level using techniques such as quantitative PCR, microarray analysis, or RNA sequencing, or at the protein level using immunoassays such as Western blotting or immunohistochemistry.  

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for producing corneal endothelial cells from progenitor cells, comprising:  
(a) culturing corneal endothelial progenitor cells under conditions promoting differentiation; and  
(b) monitoring differentiation by assessing expression of POU6F2, wherein increased POU6F2 expression indicates progression toward mature CEC phenotype. The progenitor cells may be primary corneal endothelial progenitors, stem cell-derived progenitors, or transdifferentiated cells.  

A further embodiment provides a composition comprising isolated corneal endothelial cells characterized by:  
(a) expression of POU6F2 at levels detectable by quantitative PCR; and  
(b) at least one additional property selected from hexagonal morphology, expression of tight junction proteins, and pump function activity. The composition may further comprise a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for therapeutic administration.  

Yet another embodiment provides a kit for identifying corneal endothelial cells, comprising:  
(a) reagents for detecting POU6F2 expression; and  
(b) instructions for use in identifying corneal endothelial cells based on POU6F2 expression levels. The kit may additionally include reagents for detecting TFAP2B and/or LMX1B expression.  

The invention also encompasses use of POU6F2 expression as a biomarker for assessing corneal endothelial cell quality in cell therapy products. This includes monitoring POU6F2 expression during manufacturing processes to ensure proper differentiation and functionality of therapeutic CEC preparations.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Identification of CEC-Specific Promoters**  
Comprehensive transcriptome analysis was performed on human corneal endothelial tissues and cultured CECs using cap analysis of gene expression (CAGE). Comparison with the FANTOM5 expression atlas revealed 45 promoters at 28 gene loci specifically expressed in both corneal endothelial tissues and cultured CECs. These included known CEC markers (SLC4A11, COL8A2) and newly identified transcription factors (POU6F2, TFAP2B, LMX1B). Quantitative RT-PCR confirmed restricted expression of POU6F2 to corneal endothelial cells among 22 non-ocular tissues tested.  

**Example 2: Expression Patterns During CEC Differentiation**  
Transcriptome profiling was performed on corneal endothelial progenitor cells (HCEPs) and their differentiated counterparts (dHCEPs). Hierarchical clustering showed dHCEPs closely resembled cultured CECs. Differential expression analysis revealed 964 downregulated and 1327 upregulated promoters during differentiation. Notably, POU6F2 was significantly upregulated in dHCEPs compared to HCEPs, while TFAP2B was downregulated. Western blot analysis confirmed increased POU6F2 protein expression during differentiation.  

**Example 3: Immunohistochemical Characterization**  
Immunofluorescence staining of human corneal sections demonstrated specific POU6F2 expression in corneal endothelium. Flat-mounted corneal tissue analysis revealed POU6F2 expression in central CECs but not in peripheral progenitor-like CECs expressing p75NTR. This spatial expression pattern further supports POU6F2 as a maturation marker for differentiated CECs.  

**Example 4: Quality Assessment of Stem Cell-Derived CECs**  
The POU6F2 marker was used to assess differentiation of induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived CECs. Cells showing POU6F2 expression by qRT-PCR were further characterized for morphological and functional properties. POU6F2-positive cells exhibited hexagonal morphology, ZO-1 expression at cell borders, and appropriate pump function, confirming their identity as functional CECs.  

**Example 5: Therapeutic Application**  
A cell therapy product was prepared using POU6F2-positive CECs derived from progenitor cells. The cells were administered to a rabbit model of corneal endothelial dysfunction. Treated eyes showed significant improvement in corneal transparency compared to controls, demonstrating the therapeutic potential of POU6F2-characterized CECs.  

The examples demonstrate that POU6F2 serves as a specific and reliable marker for identifying and characterizing corneal endothelial cells, particularly for assessing their differentiation status. This enables production of quality-controlled CEC preparations for research and therapeutic applications in corneal regenerative medicine.
