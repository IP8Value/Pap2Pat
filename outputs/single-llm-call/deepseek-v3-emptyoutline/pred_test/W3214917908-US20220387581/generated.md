# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) represents a significant threat to global swine populations, particularly affecting newborn piglets with mortality rates approaching 100%. Current vaccination strategies against PEDV demonstrate limited efficacy due to challenges in antigen production, delivery methods, and immune response stimulation. Conventional vaccine approaches utilizing inactivated virus or RNA-based formulations fail to provide adequate protection, particularly at the crucial mucosal surfaces where PEDV infection initiates.  

The PEDV spike (S) protein serves as the primary immunogen due to its critical role in viral entry and its multiple neutralizing epitopes. While previous attempts have focused on various portions of the S protein including the S1 moiety, S2 moiety, and core neutralizing epitope (COE), these approaches have been hampered by difficulties in recombinant protein production and purification. Furthermore, existing vaccine delivery methods fail to adequately stimulate mucosal immunity, which is essential for protection against enteric pathogens like PEDV.  

Oral vaccination presents a promising alternative to conventional injection-based approaches, offering advantages in terms of administration ease and mucosal immune response stimulation. However, current oral vaccine platforms face limitations in antigen stability during production, storage, and gastrointestinal transit. Plant-based expression systems, particularly maize, have emerged as viable platforms for oral vaccine production due to their ability to accumulate high levels of recombinant proteins while providing natural bioencapsulation protection.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel maize-based oral vaccine against PEDV comprising the S1 antigen of PEDV expressed in maize grain. The invention overcomes limitations of current vaccine approaches by providing: (1) high-level accumulation of S1 antigen in maize embryos; (2) natural bioencapsulation protecting the antigen during storage and gastrointestinal transit; (3) heat-stable antigen formulation allowing long-term storage at ambient temperatures; (4) oral delivery stimulating both systemic and mucosal immune responses; and (5) cost-effective production integrated with standard feed components.  

The vaccine demonstrates particular efficacy when administered to pregnant sows, conferring passive immunity to suckling piglets through lactogenic transfer of neutralizing antibodies and immunomodulatory factors. Optimal protection is achieved through a specific dosing regimen involving multiple administrations during gestation, with a low-dose formulation (approximately 10 mg S1 antigen/kg corn) showing superior efficacy compared to higher doses. The invention further identifies specific immunological correlates of protection, including milk neutralizing antibody titers and granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) levels, which predict vaccine efficacy in offspring.  

## DESCRIPTION  

The invention comprises transgenic maize plants expressing the S1 antigen of PEDV, methods for producing said plants, and formulations for oral vaccine administration. The S1 antigen is targeted to the maize embryo through specific expression constructs, enabling concentration of the antigen through standard milling processes that separate germ fractions. The vaccine formulation maintains antigen stability at ambient temperatures for extended periods, with retained immunogenicity after processing into corn meal.  

Administration protocols involve feeding the maize-based vaccine to pregnant sows during specific gestational periods, preferably at days 57, 85, and 110 of gestation. The low-dose formulation containing approximately 10 mg S1 antigen/kg corn demonstrates optimal immunogenicity and protection, while higher doses show reduced efficacy potentially due to oral tolerance mechanisms. The vaccine stimulates both humoral and cellular immune responses, with particular induction of mucosal immunity in the gastrointestinal tract.  

The immunological mechanism involves transfer of protective factors to suckling piglets through colostrum and milk, including neutralizing antibodies and immunomodulatory cytokines. Protection correlates with specific milk components, particularly neutralizing antibody titers and GM-CSF levels, which serve as biomarkers for vaccine efficacy. The invention provides complete protection against PEDV challenge in nursing piglets, with survival rates exceeding 50% in vaccinated groups compared to less than 10% in controls.  

### EXAMPLES  

## Example 1  

Production of transgenic maize expressing PEDV S1 antigen was achieved through Agrobacterium-mediated transformation using an embryo-specific expression construct. The resulting maize line (designated PDC18) showed high-level accumulation of S1 protein in the germ fraction. Milling processes including pin milling, roller milling, and separation were employed to concentrate the germ fraction containing the S1 antigen. The final corn meal product contained the S1 antigen at concentrations of approximately 10 mg/kg (low dose) and 50 mg/kg (high dose), as quantified by Western blot analysis against purified recombinant protein standards.  

## Example 2  

Vaccination trials were conducted with sixteen pregnant sows divided into four treatment groups: (1) injected commercial vaccine (INJ), (2) non-vaccinated controls (CON), (3) low-dose oral vaccine (LOV), and (4) high-dose oral vaccine (HOV). The oral vaccine groups received 1 kg (LOV) or 1.5 kg (HOV) of S1-containing corn meal daily during three 3-day periods at days 57, 85, and 110 of gestation. Following farrowing, piglets were challenged with live PEDV at 3-5 days of age.  

Results demonstrated significantly higher survival rates in piglets from LOV sows (53%) compared to CON (10%) and HOV (7%) groups. Disease severity scores showed significant reduction in LOV and INJ groups compared to controls. Neutralizing antibody titers in milk were significantly higher in LOV sows compared to CON and HOV groups, while serum antibody titers were elevated in both LOV and INJ groups.  

## Example 3  

Immunological analysis revealed significant correlations between milk components and piglet survival. Milk neutralizing antibody presence increased odds of survival by 3.71-65.89-fold, while elevated GM-CSF levels were associated with reduced survival odds. Sows receiving the low-dose oral vaccine showed significantly reduced GM-CSF levels in both serum and milk compared to controls, suggesting immunomodulatory effects of the S1 antigen.  

The low-dose oral vaccine demonstrated superior protection compared to both high-dose oral and injected vaccine formulations, with survival rates exceeding 50%. This effect was particularly notable given the complete lack of protection in high-dose groups, suggesting a dose-dependent immunological response potentially involving oral tolerance mechanisms. The results establish the maize-based oral vaccine as an effective strategy for PEDV protection in swine populations.
