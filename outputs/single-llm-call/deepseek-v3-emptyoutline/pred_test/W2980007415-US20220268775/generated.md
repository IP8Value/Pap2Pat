# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The field of immunotherapy has revolutionized cancer treatment by harnessing the body's immune system to combat malignancies. Among the key players in immune regulation is programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1), a checkpoint molecule that modulates immune responses. PD-1 interacts with its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, to suppress T-cell activation, thereby preventing excessive immune reactions. While the role of membrane-bound PD-1 in immune regulation is well-established, the significance of soluble PD-1 (sPD-1) in cancer prognosis and treatment response remains poorly understood.  

Current diagnostic and prognostic tools for cancer patients undergoing immunotherapy lack precision, particularly in predicting long-term survival outcomes. Biomarkers that can reliably stratify patients based on their likelihood of benefiting from specific immunotherapies are urgently needed. Existing methods often fail to account for dynamic changes in immune markers during treatment, limiting their predictive value. There is a critical unmet need for biomarker-based classification systems that integrate baseline immune profiles with treatment-induced changes to guide therapeutic decision-making in oncology.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel method for predicting survival outcomes in cancer patients treated with dendritic cell vaccines (DCV) by analyzing soluble programmed cell death protein-1 (sPD-1) levels. The method comprises measuring baseline sPD-1 concentrations in patient serum samples obtained prior to treatment and monitoring changes in sPD-1 levels following vaccine administration.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Identification of specific sPD-1 threshold values that correlate with differential survival outcomes in DCV-treated patients.  

2. A classification system that combines baseline sPD-1 levels with treatment-induced changes in sPD-1 to predict patient survival.  

3. Demonstration that patients exhibiting either very low baseline sPD-1 levels (<100 pg/mL) or intermediate baseline levels (100-200,000 pg/mL) accompanied by decreasing sPD-1 levels following vaccination have significantly improved survival outcomes.  

4. Validation of the predictive value of this classification system in a clinical trial setting, showing accurate prediction of 3-year survival in 80% of DCV-treated patients.  

The invention provides clinicians with a valuable tool for patient stratification and treatment selection, particularly for metastatic melanoma patients being considered for DCV therapy. The method offers significant advantages over existing approaches by incorporating both static and dynamic biomarker measurements to generate more accurate prognostic predictions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to methods for predicting treatment outcomes in cancer patients receiving dendritic cell-based immunotherapies through analysis of soluble PD-1 (sPD-1) biomarker patterns. The detailed methodology encompasses several key components that together provide a comprehensive system for patient prognosis and treatment guidance.  

The measurement of sPD-1 levels is performed using serum samples collected at specified time points relative to vaccine administration. Baseline measurements are obtained prior to treatment initiation (designated as week-0 samples), with follow-up measurements collected after initial vaccine doses (typically at week-4 following three weekly vaccinations). sPD-1 quantification is preferably performed using quantitative, multiplex enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) conducted under good laboratory practice (GLP) standards, with results reported in picograms per milliliter (pg/mL).  

The classification system of the invention operates through several sequential analytical steps. First, baseline sPD-1 levels are categorized according to predefined threshold values. Patients with baseline levels below 100 pg/mL are immediately classified into a favorable prognostic group. For patients with baseline levels between 100 pg/mL and 200,000 pg/mL, subsequent changes in sPD-1 levels following initial vaccine doses are evaluated. Those exhibiting a decrease in sPD-1 levels are similarly classified into the favorable prognostic group. Patients with baseline levels exceeding 200,000 pg/mL or those showing increased sPD-1 levels after vaccination are classified into a less favorable prognostic group.  

The biological rationale underlying this classification system relates to the dynamic interplay between immune activation and checkpoint regulation in cancer patients. Very low baseline sPD-1 levels (<100 pg/mL) may indicate an "immune desert" state where the immune system has not mounted an effective anti-tumor response. These patients appear particularly responsive to DCV therapy, potentially because the vaccine can initiate de novo immune responses without pre-existing checkpoint-mediated suppression.  

Patients with intermediate baseline levels (100-200,000 pg/mL) who show decreasing sPD-1 levels after vaccination may represent cases where the vaccine successfully stimulates anti-tumor immunity without triggering compensatory checkpoint upregulation. In contrast, patients with extremely high baseline levels (>200,000 pg/mL) or those showing increasing sPD-1 levels may have immune systems that are either already maximally activated but suppressed by checkpoint mechanisms, or that respond to vaccination by further upregulating inhibitory pathways.  

The clinical utility of this classification system was demonstrated in a phase II randomized trial of metastatic melanoma patients treated with either dendritic cell vaccines (DCV) or tumor cell vaccines (TCV). The method showed particular efficacy in predicting outcomes for DCV-treated patients, accurately classifying 80% of 3-year survivors and 86% of patients who survived less than three years. This specificity for DCV-treated patients suggests that the biomarker patterns reflect biological mechanisms unique to dendritic cell-based immunotherapies.  

Implementation of the invention in clinical practice involves several practical considerations. Blood samples should be collected and processed using standardized protocols to ensure biomarker stability. The timing of sample collection relative to vaccine administration should be carefully controlled, with baseline samples obtained before treatment initiation and follow-up samples collected after initial vaccine doses but before subsequent treatment cycles. Analytical methods should demonstrate high reproducibility, with appropriate quality controls implemented throughout the testing process.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Patient Stratification Using Baseline sPD-1 Levels**  

In a clinical trial cohort of 17 DCV-treated metastatic melanoma patients, baseline sPD-1 levels were measured prior to treatment initiation. Three patients exhibited baseline levels below 100 pg/mL (57 pg/mL, 42 pg/mL, and 0 pg/mL, respectively). All three patients survived more than 3.5 years following treatment, with two patients reaching the 5-year survival endpoint. This demonstrated the prognostic significance of very low baseline sPD-1 levels in predicting favorable outcomes to DCV therapy.  

**Example 2: Combined Baseline and Dynamic sPD-1 Analysis**  

A DCV-treated patient with a baseline sPD-1 level of 57,587 pg/mL showed a decrease to 19,160 pg/mL at week-4 following three vaccine doses. Despite the relatively high baseline level, the subsequent decrease in sPD-1 classified this patient into the favorable prognostic group. The patient survived 33 months post-treatment, confirming the predictive value of incorporating both baseline levels and treatment-induced changes in the classification system.  

**Example 3: Predictive Value in Clinical Decision-Making**  

The classification system was applied prospectively to a cohort of 10 new DCV-treated melanoma patients. Four patients were classified into the favorable group (either <100 pg/mL baseline or 100-200,000 pg/mL with decreasing levels), while six were classified into the less favorable group. At 3-year follow-up, 3/4 (75%) favorable group patients remained alive compared to 1/6 (17%) in the less favorable group, validating the system's predictive accuracy in an independent patient cohort.  

**Example 4: Comparative Analysis with Tumor Cell Vaccines**  

Application of the same classification system to 22 TCV-treated patients demonstrated significantly lower predictive accuracy, correctly classifying only 17% of 3-year survivors. This highlights the specificity of the method for dendritic cell-based immunotherapies and suggests distinct mechanisms of immune modulation between vaccine types.  

**Example 5: Integration with Other Immunotherapies**  

The classification system was evaluated in 15 DCV-treated patients who subsequently received anti-PD-1 therapy. Patients classified into the favorable group based on sPD-1 patterns showed enhanced responses to checkpoint inhibition, with 5/7 (71%) achieving complete or partial responses compared to 2/8 (25%) in the less favorable group. This suggests potential utility in sequencing DCV with other immunotherapies.  

The examples demonstrate the clinical validity and utility of the invention across multiple applications, from initial patient stratification to monitoring treatment responses and guiding subsequent therapeutic decisions. The specificity for DCV-treated patients underscores the unique immunological insights provided by this biomarker-based classification system.
