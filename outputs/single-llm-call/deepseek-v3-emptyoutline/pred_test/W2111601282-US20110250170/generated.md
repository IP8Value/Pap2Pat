# DESCRIPTION  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic compositions and methods for treating glioblastoma using an immunocytokine fusion protein in combination with temozolomide. The immunocytokine comprises an antibody specific to the extradomain A1 of tenascin-C (F16) fused to human interleukin-2 (IL2). This combination therapy demonstrates superior efficacy in both subcutaneous and orthotopic glioblastoma xenograft models compared to monotherapy approaches.  

The F16-IL2 immunocytokine specifically targets tumor vasculature in glioblastoma lesions, as evidenced by immunohistochemical staining of human glioblastoma specimens and mouse xenografts. Quantitative biodistribution analysis revealed preferential accumulation of radiolabeled F16-IL2 in tumors (2.3% ID/g) with a tumor-to-blood ratio of 11.5 and favorable tumor-to-organ ratios.  

In therapeutic experiments, the combination of F16-IL2 with temozolomide produced complete tumor remission in subcutaneous models that persisted for over 160 days. Intracranial models showed a 73% reduction in tumor volume and significantly prolonged survival compared to monotherapy groups. Immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated enhanced infiltration of natural killer cells and macrophages in tumors following combination therapy, without inflammatory effects in normal organs.  

### VL CDR3 SEQ ID NO: 10  

The variable light chain complementarity determining region 3 (VL CDR3) of the F16 antibody comprises the amino acid sequence as set forth in SEQ ID NO: 10, which confers specific binding to the extradomain A1 of tenascin-C. This sequence represents a critical component of the antigen-binding site that enables selective targeting of glioblastoma vasculature.  

## TERMINOLOGY  

### Antibody Molecule  

As used herein, the term "antibody molecule" refers to an immunoglobulin or functional fragment thereof that specifically binds to an antigen. This includes full-length antibodies, single-chain variable fragments (scFv), small immunoproteins (SIP), and other engineered antibody formats. The F16 antibody described herein may be utilized in any of these formats while maintaining binding specificity to tenascin-C A1 domain.  

### Antigen-Binding Site  

The term "antigen-binding site" refers to the structural region of an antibody molecule responsible for specific recognition and binding to its target antigen. In the context of this invention, the antigen-binding site comprises the variable regions of the F16 antibody that interact with the extradomain A1 of tenascin-C, including the VL CDR3 sequence disclosed as SEQ ID NO: 10.  

## EXPERIMENTS  

### Summary  

A series of experiments were conducted to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of F16-IL2 immunocytokine in combination with temozolomide against glioblastoma. These included immunohistochemical characterization of target expression, biodistribution studies, therapeutic efficacy assessments in subcutaneous and orthotopic models, and mechanistic investigations of immune cell infiltration and tumor cell apoptosis/proliferation.  

### Immunohistochemistry on Human Glioblastoma Samples and on Glioblastoma Xenografts  

Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated strong expression of the tenascin-C A1 domain in both human glioblastoma surgical specimens and U87MG xenografts in nude mice. Tissue sections were stained using biotinylated F16 antibody in small immunoprotein format (2 μg/ml), detected with streptavidin-alkaline phosphatase complex and Fast Red substrate. The staining pattern and intensity for tenascin-C A1 domain were comparable to control staining of fibronectin extradomain B using L19 antibody.  

### Cell Lines and Animals  

The human glioblastoma cell line U87MG (ATCC HTB-14) was cultured in MEM supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, L-glutamine, sodium pyruvate, and ampicillin. For subcutaneous xenografts, female BALB/c nude mice received flank injections of 5×10^6 U87MG cells. Orthotopic models were established in male BALB/c nude mice by intracranial implantation of 5×10^4 U87MG cells. All procedures were performed in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines.  

### Antibodies and Therapeutic Agents  

The F16 antibody specific to tenascin-C A1 domain and the F16-IL2 fusion protein were prepared as previously described. Temozolomide was dissolved in saline containing 10% DMSO. For therapy studies, F16-IL2 was administered intravenously (20 μg/dose, equivalent to 6.6 μg IL2) and temozolomide was given intraperitoneally (0.525 mg/dose, equivalent to 75 mg/m^2 human dose).  

### Biodistribution Experiments  

Biodistribution of radioiodinated F16-IL2 was assessed in nude mice bearing subcutaneous U87MG tumors. Animals were injected intravenously with 10 μg (3.6 μCi) F16-IL2 and sacrificed 24 hours post-injection. Radioactivity in organs was quantified using a gamma counter, with tumor accumulation reaching 2.3% ID/g and tumor-to-blood ratio of 11.5.  

### Subcutaneous Glioma Mouse Models  

Therapeutic efficacy was evaluated in subcutaneous glioblastoma xenografts measuring approximately 300 mm^3 at treatment initiation. Animals received five administrations of F16-IL2, temozolomide, combination therapy, or vehicle control every third day. Tumor volumes were measured by caliper using the formula (length × width^2)/2. Combination therapy resulted in complete tumor regression by day 40, with animals remaining tumor-free for over 160 days.  

### Immunofluorescence Assessment of Tumour-Infiltrating Cells and of Microvascular Density in Subcutaneous Glioma Xenografts  

Immunofluorescence analysis of tumor sections from treated animals revealed increased infiltration of natural killer cells (asialo GM1+) and macrophages (F4/80+) in combination therapy groups compared to monotherapies. Vascular structures were identified by CD31 staining. Quantitative image analysis demonstrated significant enhancement of immune cell recruitment specifically in tumor tissue without effects on normal organs.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Data were analyzed using two-tailed Student's t-test for tumor volume comparisons and log-rank test for survival analysis. All pairwise comparisons between combination therapy and monotherapy groups showed statistically significant differences (p<0.05). Kaplan-Meier survival curves demonstrated prolonged survival in the combination treatment group (p<0.002 vs monotherapies).  

This patent application describes novel compositions and methods for treating glioblastoma through targeted delivery of IL2 to tumor vasculature in combination with standard chemotherapy. The invention provides unexpected synergistic effects leading to complete tumor eradication in preclinical models, representing a significant advancement in glioblastoma therapy.
