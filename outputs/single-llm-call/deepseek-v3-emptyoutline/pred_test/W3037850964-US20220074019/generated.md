# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to methods for extracting rare earth elements (REEs) from geological materials, and more particularly to an improved process for recovering REEs from clay-rich sedimentary rocks associated with coal formations using organic acid-based leaching solutions. The invention specifically discloses a novel hydrometallurgical process employing citrate-based solutions for selective extraction of REEs from underclay formations while minimizing co-dissolution of gangue elements and radioactive constituents. This technology addresses critical needs in domestic REE production by enabling economical recovery from abundant coal-related byproducts and waste materials.  

## BACKGROUND  

Rare earth elements have become indispensable components in modern technologies including renewable energy systems, advanced electronics, and defense applications. Current global REE production is dominated by a limited number of sources, creating supply chain vulnerabilities. Domestic coal and coal utilization byproducts represent promising alternative sources of recoverable REEs, particularly clay-rich strata such as underclays associated with coal seams.  

Conventional REE extraction methods typically employ strong inorganic acids or salts such as ammonium sulfate, which often result in excessive dissolution of matrix minerals and co-extraction of undesirable elements. These processes generate significant waste streams and require extensive downstream purification. There exists a need for more selective and environmentally benign extraction methods that can efficiently recover REEs from clay-rich materials while minimizing environmental impact and processing costs.  

Organic acids have shown potential as alternative lixiviants due to their ability to complex with metal ions while operating at milder pH conditions compared to mineral acids. However, prior approaches using organic acids have not demonstrated sufficient selectivity or recovery efficiency for commercial viability in REE extraction from geological materials. The present invention overcomes these limitations through a carefully optimized citrate-based leaching system that provides superior REE recovery with reduced gangue element dissolution.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a novel method for extracting rare earth elements from clay-rich geological materials, particularly underclays associated with coal formations. The method comprises contacting the material with an aqueous leaching solution comprising citrate anions, wherein the solution is buffered to maintain a pH between approximately 3 and 6.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. A citrate-based leaching solution formulation comprising citric acid and sodium citrate in specific molar ratios, optionally amended with sodium chloride or ammonium sulfate to enhance ion exchange processes.  

2. A pH-controlled leaching process that selectively extracts REEs while minimizing dissolution of matrix minerals and co-extraction of gangue elements such as aluminum, silicon, and iron.  

3. Demonstration that the citrate solution achieves significantly higher REE recovery compared to conventional ammonium sulfate or mineral acid lixiviants, particularly for exchangeable and weakly-bound REE fractions.  

4. Optimization of leaching parameters including pH, contact time, and liquid-to-solid ratios to maximize REE recovery while maintaining solution selectivity.  

5. Integration with downstream REE recovery processes including solid-phase extraction techniques for purification of REEs from the pregnant leach solution.  

The invention represents a significant advancement in REE extraction technology by providing an environmentally benign, selective, and cost-effective alternative to conventional acid leaching methods. The process is particularly well-suited for application to abundant coal-related byproducts and waste materials, offering potential for domestic REE production from these underutilized resources.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive method for recovering rare earth elements from clay-rich geological materials, with particular applicability to underclays associated with coal formations. The detailed description that follows provides specific embodiments and experimental data demonstrating the efficacy of the invention, but it should be understood that these represent preferred implementations and not limitations on the scope of the inventive concept.  

### Leaching Solution Composition  

The core innovation resides in the formulation of the organic acid-based leaching solution. The preferred embodiment utilizes a citrate buffer system comprising citric acid and its conjugate base, sodium citrate tribasic dihydrate. The total citrate concentration ([citric acid + Na-citrate]) in the leaching solution typically ranges between 0.05 M and 0.2 M, with 0.1 M being the optimal concentration demonstrated in experimental studies.  

The solution is pH-buffered to maintain stability within the range of 3 to 6, preferably between 4 and 5. This pH range has been found to optimize REE recovery while minimizing dissolution of gangue elements. The buffering capacity is achieved through careful balancing of citric acid (pKa values at 3.1, 4.7, and 5.4) and sodium citrate components.  

Optionally, the leaching solution may be amended with additional ionic constituents to enhance REE recovery. Sodium chloride at concentrations between 0.1 M and 1.0 M has been shown to improve REE extraction, likely through effects on clay particle dispersion and surface charge balancing. Ammonium sulfate may also be incorporated at similar concentrations to provide ammonium ions for ion exchange processes.  

### Material Preparation  

The target geological materials for processing include clay-rich sedimentary rocks associated with coal formations, particularly underclays that occur as roof or floor strata adjacent to coal seams. These materials typically contain significant concentrations of REEs (250-500 ppm) with a substantial fraction present in exchangeable or weakly-bound forms.  

Prior to leaching, the raw material undergoes preparatory steps including:  

1. Size reduction through crushing and grinding to achieve particle sizes below 150 μm, preferably below 75 μm.  
2. Homogenization to ensure representative sampling and consistent leaching performance.  
3. In some embodiments, thermal pretreatment such as calcination may be employed to modify clay mineral structures and enhance REE accessibility.  

### Leaching Process Parameters  

The leaching process may be conducted under various configurations including:  

1. Batch leaching in stirred tanks with liquid-to-solid ratios between 5:1 and 20:1 (mL solution:g solids).  
2. Column leaching or heap leaching configurations for larger-scale operations.  
3. Flow-through systems for in situ or ex situ leaching applications.  

Optimal contact times range from 4 to 24 hours at ambient temperature (20-25°C), though shorter or longer times may be employed depending on specific material characteristics. Elevated temperatures up to 70°C may be used to accelerate leaching kinetics, though this is generally not required for effective REE recovery.  

Agitation methods include mechanical stirring, air sparging, or other mixing techniques to ensure adequate contact between the leaching solution and solid particles. In flow-through systems, solution residence times and flow rates are optimized based on material permeability and desired REE recovery levels.  

### Experimental Studies  

Extensive experimental studies have been conducted to validate the efficacy of the citrate-based leaching process. Key findings from these studies include:  

1. Comparative leaching tests demonstrated that the citrate solution (0.1 M citrate, pH 5) recovered approximately 33% of total REEs from underclay samples, significantly outperforming conventional ammonium sulfate solutions which typically recovered less than 10%.  

2. pH optimization studies showed consistent REE recovery across the pH range 3-6, with optimal selectivity (minimal gangue dissolution) achieved at pH 5. At this pH, the ratio of REE to base cations (Al, Si, Fe) in the leachate was approximately 2.5, compared to values below 0.3 for conventional acid leaching methods.  

3. Time-dependent studies revealed that the majority of REE recovery occurs within the first 20-40 minutes of contact, though extended contact times up to 24 hours can incrementally improve total recovery.  

4. Flow-through experiments demonstrated the feasibility of applying the citrate leaching process in continuous or semi-continuous systems, with optimal performance achieved using periodic "shut-in" periods to allow solution-rock interaction.  

5. Mineralogical analysis confirmed that the citrate leaching process primarily targets exchangeable and weakly-bound REE fractions with minimal dissolution of refractory REE-bearing minerals such as monazite and xenotime.  

### Downstream Processing  

Following leaching, the pregnant leach solution (PLS) containing dissolved REEs undergoes further processing for REE recovery and purification. The citrate-based PLS is particularly amenable to solid-phase extraction methods due to:  

1. Lower concentrations of competing gangue elements compared to conventional acid leachates.  
2. The formation of stable REE-citrate complexes that can be selectively captured by appropriate sorbent materials.  

Experimental results have demonstrated 60-80% recovery of light and middle REEs from citrate PLS using amine-based sorbents, with potential for further optimization. The citrate lixiviant can potentially be regenerated and recycled through the process, offering significant cost and environmental benefits.  

### Advantages Over Prior Art  

The invention provides several distinct advantages over conventional REE extraction methods:  

1. Superior selectivity for REEs over gangue elements, reducing downstream processing requirements.  
2. Minimal co-extraction of radioactive elements (Th, U), addressing a major environmental concern in REE production.  
3. Operation at near-neutral pH conditions, reducing equipment corrosion and environmental impact.  
4. Compatibility with various feedstocks including raw underclays, coal mining wastes, and processing byproducts.  
5. Potential for integration with in situ recovery methods due to the benign nature of the leaching solution.  

These advantages position the invention as a transformative technology for domestic REE production from abundant coal-related resources, addressing critical supply chain vulnerabilities while minimizing environmental impact.
