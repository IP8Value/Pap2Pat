# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of antimicrobial resistance detection. More specifically, the invention pertains to immunoassays for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria by identifying the presence of MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, which confer resistance to colistin through enzymatic modification of bacterial lipopolysaccharides. The invention provides novel recombinant MCR proteins, specific antibodies against these proteins, and sensitive immunoassay methods for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria in clinical, environmental, and food samples.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Colistin, also known as polymyxin E, represents a last-line antibiotic against multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacterial infections. The recent emergence of plasmid-mediated colistin resistance genes, particularly mcr-1 and mcr-2, poses a significant global health threat as these resistance determinants can spread horizontally between bacterial species. Current methods for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria suffer from various limitations. Phenotypic methods based on minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) determinations are time-consuming and prone to technical variability due to colistin's polycationic nature. Genotypic methods such as PCR and whole genome sequencing, while faster, only detect the presence of resistance genes rather than the functional resistance proteins and may yield false results due to various technical limitations.  

There exists an unmet need for direct, rapid, and reliable detection methods that identify functional MCR proteins responsible for colistin resistance. Such methods would facilitate timely surveillance and intervention to prevent the spread of colistin-resistant bacteria in healthcare settings, food production systems, and the environment. The present invention addresses this need by providing specific immunological reagents and assays for detecting MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides isolated and purified recombinant MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, including full-length proteins and their catalytic domains. These proteins serve as standards and immunogens for antibody production. The invention further discloses polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to MCR-1 and/or MCR-2 proteins. Among these antibodies, some demonstrate cross-reactivity with both MCR-1 and MCR-2, while others show specificity for MCR-1 alone.  

A key aspect of the invention is a sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for detecting MCR proteins. The assay exhibits high sensitivity, with a detection limit of 0.01 ng/mL for MCR-1 and 0.1 ng/mL for MCR-2 in buffer systems. The ELISA demonstrates excellent specificity and can detect MCR-producing bacteria at concentrations as low as 0.4 × 10^6 CFU/mL in culture. Importantly, the assay maintains functionality in complex food matrices, enabling detection of colistin-resistant bacteria in meat samples at levels below 0.4 CFU/g following enrichment.  

The immunological reagents and methods of the invention provide significant advantages over existing detection technologies. The direct detection of MCR proteins confirms functional resistance mechanisms rather than merely indicating the presence of resistance genes. The assays are rapid, sensitive, specific, and adaptable to various testing platforms and sample types. These features make the invention particularly valuable for surveillance programs aimed at controlling the spread of plasmid-mediated colistin resistance in clinical, agricultural, and food safety contexts.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Materials and Methods  

The invention encompasses recombinant MCR proteins produced through molecular cloning and bacterial expression systems. Genes encoding full-length MCR-1 (residues 1-541) and MCR-2 (residues 1-538), as well as their catalytic domains (MCR-1 residues 214-541; MCR-2 residues 212-538), were cloned into expression vectors with N-terminal hexahistidine tags. These constructs were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) cells induced with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG). The full-length proteins were purified under denaturing conditions using nickel affinity chromatography, while the catalytic domains were further purified by size-exclusion chromatography under native conditions.  

Polyclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing rabbits with the MCR-1 catalytic domain. Monoclonal antibodies were produced through hybridoma technology following immunization of mice with the same antigen. Antibody specificity was characterized through Western blotting and ELISA against both MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins. Isotyping revealed the monoclonal antibodies were predominantly IgG1 with kappa light chains, except for one IgG2b clone.  

The sandwich ELISA was developed using polyclonal antibodies as capture reagents and selected monoclonal antibodies for detection. The assay protocol involves coating microtiter plates with capture antibody, blocking with milk-based buffer, incubating with samples or standards, and detecting bound MCR proteins with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies. Chemiluminescent substrates provide sensitive signal detection.  

For food sample analysis, meat specimens were inoculated with known quantities of MCR-producing bacteria, enriched in selective media, and processed for ELISA analysis. Cell lysis was performed using bacterial protein extraction reagents supplemented with protease inhibitors and DNase.  

### Results and Discussion  

The recombinant MCR proteins produced in this invention were characterized by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry. While full-length proteins exhibited lower than expected mobility on gels due to their hydrophobic transmembrane domains, mass spectrometry confirmed their integrity with 60.6% sequence coverage. The catalytic domains, being more soluble, were obtained in higher purity and yield.  

Antibody development yielded high-affinity polyclonal and monoclonal reagents. The polyclonal antibodies recognized both recombinant and native MCR-1 in bacterial lysates without cross-reacting with MCR-negative strains. Among nine monoclonal antibodies characterized, six showed cross-reactivity with MCR-1 and MCR-2, while three were specific for MCR-1. One particularly high-affinity monoclonal antibody (MCR-1-7, IgG2b) was selected as the detection antibody in the sandwich ELISA due to its strong signal generation and broad reactivity.  

The optimized sandwich ELISA demonstrated excellent analytical performance. Linear detection ranges were 0.01-4 ng/mL for MCR-1 and 0.1-6 ng/mL for MCR-2, with coefficients of determination (R^2) exceeding 0.98. Intra-assay precision was below 5% coefficient of variation (CV), while inter-assay precision remained under 15% CV, meeting regulatory guidelines for immunoassay validation.  

Application of the ELISA to bacterial cultures showed detection limits of 0.4 × 10^6 CFU/mL for MCR-1-producing strains. Importantly, the assay showed no reactivity with MCR-negative strains even at high bacterial concentrations (8 × 10^8 CFU/mL), confirming specificity. In food matrices, the ELISA successfully detected MCR-1 in ground beef, chicken, and pork samples inoculated with as few as 10 CFU/25 g following overnight enrichment. Signal intensities in food samples ranged from 7-13% of those in buffer systems, demonstrating the assay's tolerance to complex matrices.  

The immunoassay platform described in this invention provides several advantages over existing detection methods. Unlike PCR, it detects functional resistance proteins rather than genes that may not be expressed. Compared to phenotypic methods, it offers faster turnaround times and greater consistency. The assay's sensitivity and specificity make it suitable for surveillance applications in clinical microbiology, food safety, and agricultural settings. Furthermore, the antibodies developed in this invention can be adapted to other diagnostic platforms, including lateral flow devices and biosensors, for point-of-care testing.  

The invention's significance lies in its potential to improve monitoring of colistin resistance spread, particularly in food production systems where MCR genes are believed to have originated. Early detection of MCR-producing bacteria can inform intervention strategies to limit resistance dissemination. The reagents and methods disclosed herein therefore represent an important advancement in the global effort to combat antimicrobial resistance.
