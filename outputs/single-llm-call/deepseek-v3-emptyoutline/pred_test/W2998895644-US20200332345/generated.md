## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics, specifically to quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays for the rapid and accurate identification and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes. More particularly, the invention provides singleplex and multiplex qPCR assays capable of distinguishing virulent select agent *F. tularensis* strains from non-select agent strains, including *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*, as well as near-neighbor bacterial species.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

*Francisella tularensis* is a highly virulent, facultative intracellular pathogen responsible for tularemia, a zoonotic disease with significant public health and biodefense implications. The bacterium is classified as a Tier 1 select agent due to its potential for deliberate misuse, with an infectious dose as low as 10 organisms. *F. tularensis* comprises four subspecies: *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis* (Type A), *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica* (Type B), *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*, and *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. Among these, *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis* subtype A.I is the most virulent, with fatality rates as high as 35% if untreated.  

Current diagnostic methods for *F. tularensis* lack the ability to rapidly and accurately differentiate between subspecies and subtypes, which is critical for appropriate medical management and public health response. Existing PCR-based assays either require complex scoring matrices, exhibit sporadic amplification failures, or fail to distinguish all four subspecies and two subtypes. Furthermore, environmental near-neighbor species, such as *Francisella persica* and other *Francisella*-like endosymbionts, complicate accurate identification due to shared genomic content.  

There is an urgent need for a robust, sensitive, and specific diagnostic platform that can definitively identify *F. tularensis* subspecies and subtypes without the limitations of current methodologies. The present invention addresses this need by providing a suite of qPCR assays capable of rapid, accurate, and sensitive detection and differentiation of *F. tularensis* strains.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel singleplex and multiplex qPCR assays for the rapid and accurate identification and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes. The assays target unique genomic signatures specific to each subspecies and subtype, enabling definitive discrimination between virulent select agent strains and non-select agent strains, including *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. A universal 16S rRNA gene qPCR assay (U16S) serving as an endogenous control for bacterial detection.  
2. A pan-*F. tularensis* qPCR assay (4Pan1) targeting the *ostA* gene, capable of detecting all four subspecies.  
3. A select agent-specific qPCR assay (3Pan) targeting a hypothetical protein gene, detecting only virulent subspecies (*F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis*, *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica*, and *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*).  
4. Subtype- and subspecies-specific qPCR assays (A1d, A2c, B2, M3, N1) for precise identification of *F. tularensis* A.I, A.II, type B, *mediasiatica*, and *novicida*, respectively.  
5. Two-tier multiplex qPCR platforms (Tier 1 and Tier 2) combining the above assays for streamlined diagnostics.  

The assays exhibit high sensitivity, with limits of detection (LOD) as low as 2 fg (1-3 genomic copies) in singleplex format and 10-100 fg (5-49 genomic copies) in multiplex format. The invention also includes a sequencing-based confirmation method for strain-level discrimination.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a comprehensive suite of qPCR assays for the identification and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes. Below is a detailed description of the assays and their applications.  

### Universal 16S rRNA Gene qPCR Assay (U16S)  

The U16S assay targets a conserved region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene, serving as an endogenous control to confirm the presence of bacterial DNA in a sample. The assay utilizes forward and reverse primers and a hydrolysis probe slightly modified to accommodate the *Francisella* genus and *Francisella*-like organisms. The LOD for the singleplex U16S assay is approximately 0.1 pg of bacterial DNA, with an average Ct value of 24.97 for inclusivity strains.  

### Pan-*F. tularensis* qPCR Assay (4Pan1)  

The 4Pan1 assay detects all four *F. tularensis* subspecies by targeting a 116-bp region within the *ostA* gene (SCHU S4 locus tag FTT_0467), encoding an organic solvent tolerance protein. The assay exhibits high specificity, with no cross-reactivity to near-neighbor species. The LOD for the singleplex 4Pan1 assay is 3 fg (~2 genomic copies), with an average Ct value of 32.73 for inclusivity strains at 30 fg.  

### Select Agent-Specific qPCR Assay (3Pan)  

The 3Pan assay detects only virulent select agent subspecies (*F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis*, *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica*, and *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*) by targeting an 83-bp region within a hypothetical protein gene (LVS locus tag FTL_1858). The LOD for the singleplex 3Pan assay is 5 fg (~2-3 genomic copies), with an average Ct value of 32.87 for inclusivity strains at 50 fg.  

### Subtype- and Subspecies-Specific qPCR Assays  

1. **A1d Assay**: Targets a 114-bp region within an oxidoreductase gene (SCHU S4 locus tag FTT_0516) specific to *F. tularensis* A.I strains. LOD: 7 fg (~3 genomic copies).  
2. **A2c Assay**: Targets a 101-bp region within the *mviN* gene (WY96-3418 locus tag FTW_1702) specific to *F. tularensis* A.II strains. LOD: 5 fg (~2-3 genomic copies).  
3. **B2 Assay**: Targets an 80-bp region within a hypothetical protein gene (FSC200 locus tag FTS_0806) specific to *F. tularensis* type B strains. LOD: 5 fg (~3 genomic copies).  
4. **M3 Assay**: Targets a 112-bp region within a major facilitator superfamily transporter gene (FSC147 locus tag FTM_1104) specific to *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*. LOD: 2 fg (~2 genomic copies).  
5. **N1 Assay**: Targets a 140-bp region within a metabolite:H+ symporter family protein gene (U112 locus tag FTN_0003) specific to *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. LOD: 3 fg (~2 genomic copies).  

### Two-Tier Multiplex qPCR Platforms  

**Tier 1 Multiplex Platform**: Combines U16S, 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d assays to rapidly determine whether a sample contains:  
- Hypervirulent select agent A.I strains (U16S+, 4Pan1+, 3Pan+, A1d+).  
- Other select agent strains (U16S+, 4Pan1+, 3Pan+, A1d-).  
- Non-select agent *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida* (U16S+, 4Pan1+, 3Pan-, A1d-).  

**Tier 2 Multiplex Platform**: Combines U16S, A2c, B2, and M3 assays to further differentiate select agent strains that are not A.I.  

### Sequencing-Based Confirmation  

The invention also includes a sequencing-based method using barcoded primers for the tier 1 multiplex targets. This allows for strain-level discrimination and confirmation of qPCR results, providing additional epidemiological information.  

### Example 1  

A clinical isolate suspected to be *F. tularensis* is tested using the tier 1 multiplex platform. The sample yields positive results for U16S, 4Pan1, and 3Pan but negative for A1d, indicating a select agent strain that is not A.I. The tier 2 multiplex platform is then employed, yielding positive results for B2, confirming the isolate as *F. tularensis* type B.  

### Example 2  

An environmental sample from a tick is tested using the tier 1 multiplex platform. The sample yields positive results for U16S and 4Pan1 but negative for 3Pan and A1d, indicating *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. The N1 assay is used for confirmation, yielding a positive result. Sequencing of the 4Pan1 amplicon further confirms the strain identity.  

The invention provides a robust, sensitive, and specific diagnostic platform for *F. tularensis*, addressing critical gaps in current methodologies. Its applications include clinical diagnostics, public health surveillance, and biodefense.
