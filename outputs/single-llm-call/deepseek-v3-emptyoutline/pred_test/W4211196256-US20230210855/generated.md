# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The prevalence of obesity has dramatically increased over the past four decades, with recent reports indicating that over 73% of the adult US population is overweight and 42.5% are classified as obese. This rise in obesity has been accompanied by a marked increase in the incidence of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), with more than 85% of T2DM patients also being obese. T2DM is a complex metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion, increased hepatic glucose production, and decreased insulin-mediated glucose uptake, a condition known as insulin resistance.  

Adipose tissue plays a critical role in systemic metabolic health by storing excess energy in the form of triglycerides. When adipocytes reach their lipid storage capacity, they undergo either hypertrophy (increase in cell size) or hyperplasia (increase in cell number). While hyperplasia is associated with metabolically healthy obesity, hypertrophy is linked to insulin resistance, inflammation, and ectopic lipid deposition in non-adipose tissues such as the liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas. Notably, smaller adipocytes retain insulin sensitivity, whereas enlarged adipocytes contribute to systemic metabolic dysfunction.  

Current therapeutic strategies for obesity and insulin resistance primarily focus on weight loss, which is often difficult to sustain. An alternative approach involves inducing adipocyte hyperplasia to redistribute lipid storage and improve metabolic health. However, mechanisms to safely and effectively promote adipocyte hyperplasia remain underexplored. The monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), a proton-linked transporter of lactate and other monocarboxylates, has been implicated in cell proliferation and metabolic regulation. While MCT1 is known to be upregulated in proliferating cells and during adipocyte differentiation, its role in adipocyte hyperplasia and metabolic regulation has not been previously elucidated.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel method for inducing adipocyte hyperplasia and improving metabolic health by modulating MCT1 activity. Specifically, inhibition of MCT1 in mature adipocytes promotes re-entry into the cell cycle, enhances insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and increases lipid storage capacity. These effects are mediated through alterations in intracellular lactate and pyruvate levels, redox homeostasis, and activation of cell cycle regulators such as cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1).  

In one embodiment, the invention comprises a method of treating metabolic disorders such as obesity and insulin resistance by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an MCT1 inhibitor, such as AZD3965, to a subject in need thereof. The inhibitor induces adipocyte proliferation, reduces lipid accumulation in hypertrophic adipocytes, and enhances insulin sensitivity.  

Another embodiment includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising an MCT1 inhibitor and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for use in metabolic therapy. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and prognostic methods for assessing adipocyte hyperplasia and metabolic health by measuring MCT1 expression or activity in adipose tissue.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:  

- **Adipocyte hyperplasia**: An increase in the number of adipocytes due to cell proliferation.  
- **Adipocyte hypertrophy**: An increase in adipocyte size due to lipid accumulation.  
- **MCT1 (Monocarboxylate transporter 1)**: A proton-linked transporter encoded by the SLC16A1 gene, responsible for the cellular uptake and efflux of lactate, pyruvate, and other monocarboxylates.  
- **AZD3965**: A selective small-molecule inhibitor of MCT1 currently under clinical investigation for cancer therapy.  
- **Insulin resistance**: A condition in which cells fail to respond normally to insulin, leading to impaired glucose uptake.  
- **Redox homeostasis**: The balance between oxidative and reductive reactions within a cell, often reflected in the NAD+/NADH ratio.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Inhibition of MCT1 Reduces Lipid Accumulation in Adipocytes**  

Murine 3T3-L1 preadipocytes were differentiated into mature adipocytes in the presence or absence of 1 µM AZD3965, a selective MCT1 inhibitor. By day six of differentiation, control adipocytes exhibited significant lipid droplet formation, as visualized by Oil Red O staining. In contrast, adipocytes treated with AZD3965 or genetically modified to knockdown MCT1 (shMCT1) showed markedly reduced lipid accumulation. Quantification confirmed a statistically significant decrease in lipid content in MCT1-inhibited adipocytes compared to controls.  

**Example 2: MCT1 Inhibition Alters Adipogenic Gene Expression**  

Differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes treated with AZD3965 exhibited attenuated expression of key adipogenic markers, including peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ), fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4), and hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL). RT-qPCR and immunoblotting demonstrated that MCT1 inhibition suppressed the induction of adipogenic genes during differentiation, suggesting a role for MCT1 in adipocyte maturation.  

**Example 3: Mature Adipocytes Retain Differentiation Status After MCT1 Inhibition**  

Fully differentiated adipocytes treated with AZD3965 showed a progressive decrease in lipid content without evidence of de-differentiation. Viability assays confirmed that the reduction in lipid was not due to cytotoxicity. Instead, MCT1 inhibition increased intracellular glycerol levels, indicating enhanced lipolysis. Notably, adipokine secretion profiles remained unchanged, further supporting that adipocytes retained their differentiated state.  

**Example 4: Transcriptional Profiling Reveals Cell Cycle Re-entry**  

RNA sequencing of AZD3965-treated adipocytes identified 277 differentially expressed genes, with significant upregulation of cell cycle promoters such as CDK1, polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1), and DNA topoisomerase 2-α (TOP2A). Pathway analysis predicted activation of cytokinesis, suggesting that MCT1 inhibition promotes adipocyte proliferation.  

**Example 5: Adipocytes Proliferate Following MCT1 Inhibition**  

Proliferation assays demonstrated a significant increase in cell number in AZD3965-treated mature adipocytes but not in preadipocytes. Confocal microscopy confirmed elevated expression of the proliferation marker Ki67 in lipid-laden adipocytes after MCT1 inhibition. Additionally, CDK1 phosphorylation at Tyr15, which maintains CDK1 in an inactive state, was reduced, indicating increased CDK1 activity and cell cycle progression.  

**Example 6: Enhanced Insulin-Stimulated Glucose Uptake**  

MCT1 inhibition significantly increased insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in mature adipocytes after 48–72 hours of treatment. Co-treatment with the CDK1 inhibitor RO-3306 attenuated this effect, confirming that the metabolic benefits of MCT1 inhibition are dependent on cell cycle re-entry.  

**Example 7: Increased Triglyceride Storage Capacity**  

Adipocytes pretreated with AZD3965 accumulated significantly more triglycerides upon subsequent insulin stimulation, demonstrating an enhanced lipid storage capacity consistent with hyperplasia.  

### Conclusion  

The invention demonstrates that MCT1 inhibition induces adipocyte hyperplasia, improves insulin sensitivity, and enhances metabolic health. These findings support the use of MCT1 inhibitors, such as AZD3965, as a novel therapeutic strategy for obesity and related metabolic disorders.
