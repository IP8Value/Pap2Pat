## DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to a novel microelectrode array (MEA) comprising three-dimensional nanostructured boron-doped diamond (3D-nanostructured BDD) microelectrodes for bidirectional neural interfacing. The invention provides improved neural recording and stimulation capabilities through enhanced electrochemical properties while maintaining biocompatibility and structural stability.  

The 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes comprise vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) embedded between two boron-doped diamond nanolayers, forming a conductive and porous scaffold that significantly increases the effective surface area of each microelectrode. This nanostructured configuration reduces interfacial impedance by approximately 40-fold compared to conventional BDD microelectrodes while maintaining diamond's characteristic wide potential window and chemical inertness.  

The microelectrode arrays of the invention feature 20-μm-diameter electrodes arranged in various configurations, including a 4×15 layout covering 900×12600 μm² for embryonic hindbrain-spinal cord preparations and a 1×60 linear array with 100-150 μm spacing for cell culture applications. Each microelectrode demonstrates a double layer capacitance of approximately 3 mF/cm² and a charge storage capacity of about 10 mC/cm², enabling sensitive detection of neural signals as small as 10-20 μV while providing safe charge injection for neural stimulation.  

The fabrication process involves growing a first BDD layer on a fused silica substrate, depositing nickel catalyst patterns, growing VACNTs via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), coating the nanotubes with diamond nanoparticles, and growing a second BDD layer to encapsulate the nanostructure. Metal tracks (Cr/Au) provide individual electrode connections, and a SU-8 insulating layer defines the final electrode geometry.  

The invention further demonstrates that 3D-nanostructured BDD substrates support neural cell adhesion, survival, and neurite extension comparable to conventional BDD, confirming their biocompatibility. The MEAs have been successfully used to record spontaneous and evoked activity from embryonic mouse hindbrain-spinal cord preparations and hippocampal cell cultures, as well as to deliver effective electrical microstimulation.  

Compared to existing neural interface technologies, the 3D-nanostructured BDD MEAs combine the advantages of diamond's chemical stability and wide potential window with the enhanced electrochemical performance of nanostructured materials, while avoiding the mechanical fragility and potential toxicity concerns associated with other high-performance electrode materials. The invention represents a significant advance in neural interface technology with applications in basic neuroscience research, neural prosthetics, and brain-computer interfaces.  

The detailed description that follows provides specific embodiments of the invention, including materials, fabrication methods, electrochemical characterization, and neural interface performance data. These embodiments are presented to illustrate the invention but should not be construed as limiting its scope, which is defined by the appended claims.
