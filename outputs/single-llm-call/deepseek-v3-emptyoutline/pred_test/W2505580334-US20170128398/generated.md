# DESCRIPTION  

## PRIOR APPLICATION INFORMATION  

The present application claims priority to U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. [To Be Assigned], filed on [Filing Date], entitled "METHODS AND COMPOSITIONS FOR TREATING SEPTIC SHOCK AND LACTIC ACIDEMIA USING GENTISIC ACID SODIUM SALT," the contents of which are incorporated herein by reference in their entirety.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Septic shock (SS) is a life-threatening condition characterized by systemic hypotension, organ dysfunction, and metabolic disturbances, including lactic acidemia (LA). The development of LA in SS is associated with poor prognosis, as elevated blood lactate concentrations reflect impaired tissue perfusion and mitochondrial dysfunction. Lactate metabolism is regulated by hepatic uptake through monocarboxylate transporters (MCTs), followed by conversion to glucose via gluconeogenesis or oxidation to CO₂ and water in mitochondria.  

Hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction has been postulated as a contributor to LA in SS, though the precise mechanisms remain debated. Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, driven by the proton motive force (pmf) across the inner membrane, is critical for ATP synthesis. Dysfunction in electron transport chain (ETC) complexes, particularly Complex IV, has been implicated in sepsis-related mitochondrial impairment. However, existing studies report conflicting results due to methodological variations, and the relationship between mitochondrial dysfunction and LA remains unclear.  

Current treatments for SS, such as norepinephrine (NE), aim to restore blood pressure but do not consistently address mitochondrial dysfunction or LA. There is a need for novel therapies that simultaneously improve hemodynamics, mitochondrial function, and lactate clearance in SS.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating septic shock (SS) and lactic acidemia (LA) using gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS). GSS, a phenolic compound with antioxidant properties, has been found to reverse systemic hypotension, restore hepatic mitochondrial function, and reduce blood lactate concentrations in a canine model of SS.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Restoration of Mitochondrial Function**: GSS enhances oxygen consumption rates (OCR) in hepatic mitochondria, particularly coupled respiration (ATP-linked) and maximal uncoupled respiration, surpassing the effects of norepinephrine (NE).  
2. **Reduction of Lactic Acidemia**: Unlike NE, GSS treatment significantly attenuates LA, suggesting a mechanism independent of mitochondrial function, potentially involving enhanced hepatic lactate uptake or gluconeogenesis.  
3. **Hemodynamic Stabilization**: GSS increases mean arterial pressure (MAP) to baseline levels while lowering heart rate (HR), reducing myocardial strain compared to NE.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations of GSS for intravenous administration in SS, including dosage regimens and combination therapies with vasopressors or other supportive treatments.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### RESULTS  

In a canine model of SS induced by *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* bacteremia, GSS demonstrated superior efficacy compared to NE in reversing hemodynamic collapse, mitochondrial dysfunction, and LA. Key findings include:  

1. **Hemodynamic Effects**:  
   - GSS restored MAP to baseline levels (≈100 mmHg) within minutes of infusion, comparable to NE.  
   - Systemic vascular resistance (SVR) increased post-treatment, though not statistically significant.  
   - Heart rate (HR) was significantly lower in the GSS-treated group at 3 and 5 hours post-treatment compared to NE.  

2. **Lactate Clearance**:  
   - Blood lactate concentrations decreased in the GSS-treated group, while they increased in NE-treated and untreated septic groups.  
   - This effect was independent of oxygen delivery (DO₂), which was higher in the NE group due to increased hemoglobin.  

3. **Mitochondrial Function**:  
   - **Basal OCR**: Reduced in septic controls but restored by GSS.  
   - **Coupled Respiration**: Markedly enhanced by GSS, exceeding responses in other groups.  
   - **Maximal OCR**: Attenuated in septic controls but restored by both GSS and NE.  
   - **Spare Respiratory Capacity**: Preserved in GSS-treated animals, indicating improved metabolic reserve.  
   - **Complex IV Activity**: Partially restored by GSS, though Seahorse OCR measurements provided more sensitive functional assessment.  

### METHODS  

#### Animal Preparation  

Canines (18–26 kg) were anesthetized with sufentanil citrate and midazolam, ventilated with 100% O₂, and instrumented with vascular catheters for hemodynamic monitoring. SS was induced by infusing ~10¹⁰ CFU/h of *P. aeruginosa*, while non-septic controls received saline. Hemodynamic parameters (MAP, CO, SVR) and blood samples (lactate, troponin, liver function tests) were collected at baseline, septic shock (MAP ≈60 mmHg), and post-treatment intervals.  

#### Experimental Protocols  

Five groups were studied:  
1. **Septic Control**: SS + dextrose (D5W).  
2. **Non-Septic Control**: Saline + D5W.  
3. **NE Septic Group**: SS + NE (0.51 ± 0.22 μg/kg/min).  
4. **GSS Septic Group**: SS + GSS (0.55 ± 0.29 mg/kg/min).  
5. **GSS Non-Septic Group**: Saline + GSS.  

Treatments were initiated at shock onset (MAP ≈60 mmHg) and titrated to restore baseline MAP.  

#### Mitochondrial Preparation  

Liver tissue was homogenized in mitochondrial isolation buffer (MSHE: 70 mM sucrose, 210 mM mannitol, 5 mM HEPES, 1 mM EGTA, BSA). Mitochondria were pelleted by centrifugation, resuspended in assay solution (MAS), and plated (~20 μg protein/well) for Seahorse XF24 analysis.  

#### Mitochondria Measurement of Oxygen Consumption Rates (OCR)  

OCR was measured sequentially under:  
1. **Basal Conditions**: Pyruvate (10 mM) + malate (2 mM).  
2. **Coupled Respiration**: ADP (4 mM).  
3. **ATP Synthase Inhibition**: Oligomycin (2 μM).  
4. **Maximal Respiration**: FCCP (4 μM).  
5. **ETC Inhibition**: Rotenone (2 μM) + antimycin A (2 μM).  

Spare respiratory capacity was calculated as (maximal OCR − basal OCR).  

#### To Determine Whether MCT1 Protein is Altered in Septic Shock  

MCT1 expression was assessed via Western blot in liver homogenates. No significant changes were observed, suggesting lactate transport impairment is not primary to LA in this model.  

#### Gallic Acid Septic Group  

For comparison, ethyl gallate (a trihydroxy phenolic analog) was tested but did not reduce LA, highlighting GSS’s unique efficacy.  

#### Statistics  

Data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA (split-plot) with Student-Newman-Keuls post-hoc tests. Significance was set at *P* < 0.05.
