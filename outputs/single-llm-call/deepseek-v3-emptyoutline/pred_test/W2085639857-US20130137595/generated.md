# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of diagnostic medicine, particularly to methods and compositions for the early detection of Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) in human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)-infected individuals. More specifically, the invention provides a panel of protein biomarkers detectable in plasma that are differentially expressed in HIV-infected individuals with HL compared to those without HL. The invention further encompasses diagnostic assays, kits, and methods for screening, prognosis, and monitoring of HL in immunocompromised patients.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) represents a significant clinical challenge in HIV-infected individuals, who exhibit a 5.6- to 14.7-fold increased risk of developing HL compared to the general population. HL is characterized by a unique pathological composition, wherein malignant B lymphocytes constitute only 1–2% of the tumor mass, with the remainder comprising infiltrating inflammatory cells. This inflammatory microenvironment suggests that HL is not merely a neoplastic disorder but also a dysregulated immune response, often associated with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) reactivation in immunocompromised hosts.  

Current diagnostic approaches for HL rely on invasive tissue biopsies and imaging studies, typically initiated only after symptomatic presentation. Early detection remains elusive, despite evidence that early-stage HL responds favorably to less aggressive therapies, thereby reducing treatment-related morbidity. Moreover, HIV-infected patients face additional complications due to concurrent immunosuppression, necessitating adjunctive therapies such as highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) and growth factor support during cancer treatment.  

Proteomic analyses of plasma have identified numerous biomarkers for malignancies, but no validated screening tool exists for HL in HIV-positive individuals. Prior studies in non-HIV populations have reported elevated levels of C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase in HL patients, but these findings have not been systematically evaluated in the context of HIV co-infection. There is thus an urgent need for non-invasive, high-specificity biomarkers to enable early HL detection in this high-risk population.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention discloses a novel panel of 60 plasma protein biomarkers for the detection of Hodgkin's lymphoma (HL) in HIV-infected individuals. These biomarkers were identified through comparative proteomic analysis using accurate mass tag (AMT) liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) of plasma samples from HIV-positive subjects with and without HL. The biomarkers exhibit statistically significant differential expression (p ≤ 0.05) and a minimum 1.5-fold change in abundance between the two groups.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Biomarker Panel**: A group of 60 proteins associated with inflammatory and oncogenic pathways, including but not limited to C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase, which collectively serve as a diagnostic signature for HL in HIV-infected patients.  
2. **Diagnostic Methods**: Assays for detecting the biomarkers, including mass spectrometry, immunoassays (e.g., ELISA, multiplex bead arrays), and point-of-care lateral flow devices.  
3. **Clinical Applications**: Use of the panel for early screening, disease monitoring, and therapeutic response assessment in HIV-associated HL.  

The invention addresses the unmet need for a non-invasive screening tool in immunocompromised populations, enabling earlier intervention and improved clinical outcomes.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Materials and Methods  

**Sample Preparation**:  
Plasma samples were obtained from HIV-infected individuals with and without HL, matched for age and gender. Samples were processed by immunodepletion of the 12 most abundant plasma proteins using Proteome Purify™ 12 resin (R&D Systems), followed by trichloroacetic acid precipitation. Protein pellets were solubilized in urea/thiourea buffer, reduced with dithiothreitol, and digested with sequencing-grade trypsin. Peptides were purified via C18 solid-phase extraction and reconstituted in ammonium bicarbonate prior to analysis.  

**Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS)**:  
Peptides were separated using a custom-built nanoLC system coupled to an LTQ-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. A 65 cm × 75 µm C18 capillary column was employed with a 115-minute exponential gradient (0–55% acetonitrile). Mass spectra were acquired in the Orbitrap at 100,000 resolution (400–2000 m/z range). Data were processed using the AMT tag approach, matching spectra against a database of 30,573 known human plasma peptides.  

**Statistical Analysis**:  
Peptide abundances were normalized using median absolute deviation and subjected to non-parametric Mann-Whitney testing for intensity differences and G-testing for detection frequency. Proteins were inferred from peptide data using RRollup and Protein Prophet algorithms, retaining only those identified by ≥2 unique peptides.  

**Pathway Analysis**:  
Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) was used to annotate biological functions. Proteins with ≥1.5-fold change and p ≤ 0.05 were evaluated for enrichment in inflammatory and oncogenic pathways.  

### Results and Discussion  

The proteomic analysis identified 60 plasma proteins significantly dysregulated in HIV-infected HL patients compared to controls. Key findings include:  

1. **Validation of Known Biomarkers**:  
   - C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase, previously associated with HL in non-HIV populations, were confirmed in this cohort, supporting their utility as universal HL markers.  

2. **Novel Biomarker Discovery**:  
   - Fifty-seven additional proteins exhibited differential expression, many linked to immune activation (e.g., serum amyloid proteins) and cancer-associated pathways (e.g., fibronectin, galectin-3-binding protein).  

3. **Biological Relevance**:  
   - IPA revealed strong enrichment for "inflammatory response" (p = 3.2×10⁻⁸) and "cancer" (p = 1.7×10⁻⁵) pathways, aligning with HL’s pathophysiology. The biomarkers likely originate from tumor-infiltrating leukocytes or systemic inflammation rather than malignant B cells.  

4. **Clinical Utility**:  
   - The panel’s composition (e.g., acute-phase reactants, liver-derived proteins) suggests feasibility for routine clinical assays. Multiplexed immunoassays could quantify subsets of markers (e.g., 5–10 proteins) for cost-effective screening.  

**Advantages Over Prior Art**:  
- **HIV-Specific Context**: Unlike prior studies in immunocompetent HL patients, this panel accounts for HIV-related confounders (e.g., chronic inflammation, HAART effects).  
- **Early Detection Potential**: Biomarkers reflect pre-symptomatic disease biology, enabling intervention before advanced tumor progression.  

**Example Embodiments**:  
1. A diagnostic kit comprising antibodies against CRP, beta-2-microglobulin, and ≥3 novel markers (e.g., SAA1, FN1, LGALS3BP) for ELISA-based detection.  
2. A mass spectrometry protocol quantifying ≥10 biomarkers from the panel in 100 µL of plasma.  

**Industrial Applicability**:  
The invention is amenable to commercialization as laboratory-developed tests (LDTs) or FDA-approved in vitro diagnostics. Potential applications include:  
- Routine screening for HIV-positive patients during clinical monitoring.  
- Companion diagnostics for clinical trials of HL therapies in immunocompromised cohorts.
