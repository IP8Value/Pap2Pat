# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) biology and regenerative medicine. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for expanding hematopoietic stem cells through modulation of transcription factors that regulate Musashi-2 (MSI2) expression. The invention further relates to methods of identifying compounds that promote HSPC expansion by targeting the cytochrome P450 1B1 (CYP1B1) pathway. The disclosed methods and compositions have therapeutic applications in bone marrow transplantation, treatment of blood disorders, and ex vivo expansion of HSPCs for clinical use.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Hematopoietic stem cells possess the unique ability to self-renew while maintaining the capacity to differentiate into all blood cell lineages. This dual functionality makes them crucial for lifelong blood production and valuable for therapeutic applications. However, the limited availability of functional HSPCs presents a significant barrier to effective transplantation therapies. Current methods for HSPC expansion often result in loss of stem cell properties or uncontrolled differentiation.  

The RNA-binding protein Musashi-2 (MSI2) has been identified as a key regulator of HSPC function. While MSI2 overexpression enhances stem cell self-renewal, its repression leads to diminished reconstitution capacity. Despite its importance, the mechanisms controlling MSI2 expression in HSPCs remain poorly understood. Similarly, the cytochrome P450 enzyme CYP1B1 has been implicated as a negative regulator of HSPC maintenance through its role in the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) pathway.  

There exists an unmet need in the art for methods to reliably expand functional HSPCs while maintaining their stem cell properties. The present invention addresses this need by identifying and targeting specific transcription factors that regulate MSI2 expression and by providing novel approaches to modulate the CYP1B1 pathway for therapeutic HSPC expansion.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for expanding hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) through modulation of key regulatory pathways. The invention is based on the discovery that transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1 cooperatively regulate MSI2 expression in HSPCs, and that inhibition of CYP1B1 activity promotes HSPC expansion.  

In one aspect, the invention provides methods for expanding HSPCs by modulating the activity or expression of USF2 and/or PLAG1 transcription factors. The methods may involve overexpression of USF2 and PLAG1, either individually or in combination, to enhance MSI2 expression and promote HSPC self-renewal.  

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for expanding HSPCs by inhibiting CYP1B1 activity or expression. These methods may involve the use of small molecule inhibitors, genetic knockdown approaches, or other means of CYP1B1 modulation.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising expanded HSPCs prepared according to the disclosed methods, as well as methods of using such compositions for therapeutic purposes. Additionally, the invention provides screening methods for identifying compounds that promote HSPC expansion through modulation of the USF2/PLAG1/MSI2 axis or the CYP1B1 pathway.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs  

The present invention provides methods for expanding hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) ex vivo or in vivo. These methods involve modulating the expression or activity of transcription factors USF2 and PLAG1 to enhance MSI2 expression, or alternatively, inhibiting CYP1B1 activity to promote HSPC maintenance and expansion.  

For ex vivo expansion, HSPCs may be isolated from various sources including cord blood, bone marrow, or peripheral blood using standard isolation techniques. The isolated cells are then cultured in appropriate media supplemented with factors that promote HSPC expansion while maintaining stem cell properties. The culture conditions may include modulation of USF2 and/or PLAG1 activity through genetic engineering approaches such as overexpression vectors or genome editing techniques. Alternatively, the culture may include small molecule inhibitors of CYP1B1 or other components of the AHR pathway.  

For in vivo applications, the methods may involve administering to a subject pharmaceutical compositions that modulate USF2, PLAG1, or CYP1B1 activity. Such compositions may include gene therapy vectors encoding USF2 and/or PLAG1, or small molecule modulators of these transcription factors. The compositions may also include CYP1B1 inhibitors or other compounds that target the AHR pathway.  

The expansion methods may be combined with other known HSPC expansion techniques, such as supplementation with stem cell factor (SCF), thrombopoietin (TPO), or other cytokines. The methods are particularly useful for expanding HSPCs while maintaining their long-term repopulating capacity and multi-lineage differentiation potential.  

### Expanded HSPCs for Therapeutic Use  

The invention provides expanded populations of HSPCs prepared according to the disclosed methods, and methods of using such cells for therapeutic purposes. The expanded HSPCs maintain their stem cell properties and are capable of long-term engraftment and multi-lineage differentiation.  

Therapeutic applications include but are not limited to: bone marrow transplantation for hematologic malignancies or other blood disorders; treatment of genetic blood diseases; immune reconstitution following chemotherapy or radiation therapy; and regenerative medicine applications. The expanded HSPCs may be administered to a patient in need thereof via standard transplantation protocols.  

The invention also provides methods for genetically modifying the expanded HSPCs prior to therapeutic use. Such modifications may include correction of genetic defects, introduction of therapeutic genes, or other genetic manipulations to enhance therapeutic efficacy.  

### Compositions  

The invention provides various compositions for expanding HSPCs and for therapeutic use. These compositions include:

4. Small molecule inhibitors of CYP1B1 or activators of USF2/PLAG1 activity, formulated for in vitro or in vivo use.  

The compositions may be provided as kits for HSPC expansion or therapeutic use, optionally including instructions for use and other necessary components.  

### Methods of Culturing Leukemic Cells  

The invention further provides methods for culturing leukemic cells, particularly for research purposes or drug screening applications. These methods utilize the same principles of USF2/PLAG1/MSI2 regulation and CYP1B1 inhibition, but applied to leukemic cell populations.  

The methods may be used to maintain or expand leukemic stem cells in culture, which are valuable for studying leukemia biology and for screening potential therapeutic compounds. The culture conditions may be modified to either promote or inhibit leukemic cell growth, depending on the intended application.  

These methods are particularly useful for establishing patient-derived xenograft models, studying leukemia stem cell biology, and screening for compounds that specifically target leukemic stem cells while sparing normal HSPCs.  

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells  

In this example, we demonstrate that inhibition of CYP1B1 activity leads to expansion of HSPCs. Cord blood-derived CD34+ cells were cultured in serum-free media supplemented with various cytokines and treated with specific CYP1B1 inhibitors.  

The treated cells showed significantly increased numbers of CD34+ cells compared to untreated controls after 7 days in culture. Flow cytometry analysis revealed maintenance of primitive cell surface markers, and functional assays demonstrated preserved multi-lineage differentiation potential and enhanced engraftment capacity in immunodeficient mice.  

Gene expression analysis showed that CYP1B1 inhibition led to decreased expression of differentiation markers and maintained expression of stem cell-associated genes. These results demonstrate that pharmacological inhibition of CYP1B1 can promote HSPC expansion while maintaining stem cell properties.  

### Results & Discussion  

The results from multiple experiments demonstrate that modulation of the USF2/PLAG1/MSI2 axis or inhibition of CYP1B1 activity effectively promotes HSPC expansion. Key findings include:  

1. Combined overexpression of USF2 and PLAG1 leads to synergistic upregulation of MSI2 expression, resulting in enhanced HSPC self-renewal.  

2. CYP1B1 inhibition phenocopies the effects of MSI2 overexpression, suggesting a common pathway regulating HSPC maintenance.  

3. Expanded HSPCs maintain long-term repopulating capacity and multi-lineage differentiation potential in vivo.  

4. The expansion methods are applicable to various HSPC sources, including cord blood, bone marrow, and mobilized peripheral blood.  

These findings have significant implications for clinical HSPC transplantation and regenerative medicine applications. The disclosed methods provide a reliable means to expand functional HSPCs while maintaining their therapeutic potential.  

### Example 2: CYPB1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs  

This example demonstrates that genetic knockdown of CYP1B1 enhances HSPC expansion. Lentiviral vectors encoding shRNAs targeting CYP1B1 were used to transduce cord blood-derived CD34+ cells.  

Transduced cells showed efficient CYP1B1 knockdown as confirmed by qRT-PCR and western blot analysis. After 7 days in culture, CYP1B1-knockdown cells exhibited significantly higher numbers of CD34+ cells compared to control cells transduced with non-targeting shRNAs.  

Functional assays demonstrated that the expanded cells maintained colony-forming capacity and engraftment potential. These results confirm that genetic inhibition of CYP1B1 expression promotes HSPC expansion, providing an alternative approach to pharmacological inhibition.  

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors  

This example describes a high-throughput screening method to identify compounds that promote HSPC expansion through inhibition of CYP family enzymes. A library of known CYP inhibitors was screened using cord blood-derived CD34+ cells in 384-well plate format.  

Cells were treated with test compounds at various concentrations and cultured for 7 days. Expansion was assessed by automated cell counting and flow cytometry analysis of CD34 expression. Hits were confirmed in secondary screens assessing stem cell functionality.  

The screen identified several specific CYP1B1 inhibitors that effectively promoted HSPC expansion while maintaining stem cell properties. These compounds represent potential candidates for clinical development of HSPC expansion protocols.  

### Example 3: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS  

This example demonstrates the combinatorial effects of StemRegenin 1 (SR1), a known HSPC expansion agent, with TMS, a specific CYP1B1 inhibitor identified in the high-throughput screen.  

Cord blood-derived CD34+ cells were cultured with SR1 alone, TMS alone, or the combination of both compounds. The combination treatment showed synergistic effects on HSPC expansion, with significantly higher numbers of CD34+ cells compared to either treatment alone.  

Functional assays demonstrated that cells expanded with the combination treatment maintained superior engraftment capacity and multi-lineage differentiation potential compared to cells expanded with single agents. These results suggest that combining different mechanisms of HSPC expansion may provide enhanced therapeutic benefits.
