## BACKGROUND  

The field of the invention relates to protein engineering, particularly to antibodies and antibody fragments with pH-dependent binding properties. Receptor-ligand recognition and binding frequently depend on pH-induced changes stemming from the combined protonation states of amino acids within proteins. Histidine is considered a key amino acid driving pH sensitivity due to its side-chain pKa of 5.5-6.5 in protein contexts. Proteins have adapted to function in various subcellular pH environments through non-random placement of histidine residues. These phenomena have been exploited in therapeutic protein design to alter intracellular trafficking patterns.  

The transferrin receptor (TfR) presents a valuable therapeutic target that can be antagonized directly or exploited as an intracellular drug delivery vector. This results from its ubiquitous expression on normal cells, elevated expression on cancer cells, and the endocytotic route used to transport iron-bearing transferrin inside cells. The natural ligand for TfR, transferrin (Tf), circulates in iron-free (apoTf) or iron-bound (holoTf) forms. HoloTf binds TfR tightly at blood pH (7.2-7.4), and the complex is internalized via clathrin-mediated endocytosis. As holoTf-TfR complexes cycle through acidic endosomes (pH 5.0-6.0), pH-induced conformational changes induce iron release to yield apoTf, which has increased affinity for TfR at endosomal pH. The apoTf-TfR complex then recycles to the cell surface where apoTf dissociates back into circulation.  

Previous attempts to modify TfR binding properties have focused on transferrin itself or full-length antibodies. However, these approaches have limitations in terms of engineering flexibility, production costs, and tissue penetration. There remains an unmet need for smaller antibody fragments that can be precisely engineered for pH-dependent TfR binding while maintaining favorable pharmacokinetic properties.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides engineered antibody fragments, particularly single-chain variable fragments (scFvs), with pH-dependent binding to the transferrin receptor (TfR). The scFvs are modified to exhibit rapid dissociation from TfR at endosomal pH (approximately 5.5) while maintaining high affinity binding at neutral pH (approximately 7.4).  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. An anti-TfR scFv comprising histidine substitutions in complementarity-determining region 1 of the heavy chain (CDRH1), wherein the substitutions confer pH-sensitive binding to TfR.  

2. The scFv of claim 1, wherein the histidine substitutions are located at positions corresponding to VH31-VH34 of the CDRH1 loop.  

3. The scFv of claim 1 or 2, further comprising proline substitutions adjacent to the histidine-substituted region.  

4. The scFv of any preceding claim, wherein the scFv exhibits at least 90% dissociation from TfR after 10 minutes at pH 5.5.  

5. The scFv of any preceding claim, wherein the scFv maintains sub-nanomolar affinity for TfR at pH 7.4.  

6. A pharmaceutical composition comprising the scFv of any preceding claim and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.  

7. A method of increasing intracellular accumulation of a therapeutic agent in a cell, comprising administering to a subject the scFv of any of claims 1-5 conjugated to the therapeutic agent.  

8. The method of claim 7, wherein the cell is a cancer cell.  

The engineered scFvs of the invention show substantially improved intracellular accumulation compared to wild-type antibodies, making them particularly useful for drug delivery applications. The pH-sensitive binding properties enable the antibodies to dissociate from TfR in endosomal compartments, avoiding normal recycling pathways and leading to enhanced intracellular retention.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides engineered antibody fragments with pH-dependent binding properties to the transferrin receptor (TfR). In particular embodiments, the invention provides single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) derived from an anti-human TfR antibody (H7) that have been modified to exhibit enhanced dissociation from TfR at endosomal pH while maintaining high affinity binding at neutral pH.  

The scFvs are engineered through histidine saturation mutagenesis of complementarity-determining regions (CDRs), particularly CDRH1. Histidine residues are introduced at multiple positions within CDRH1 to create pH-sensitive binding characteristics. Preferred embodiments include at least three histidine substitutions in the central region of CDRH1 (positions corresponding to VH31-VH34), optionally accompanied by proline substitutions at adjacent positions (VH30 or VH35).  

A particularly preferred embodiment is scFv M16, which contains histidine substitutions at multiple positions in CDRH1 and exhibits the following properties:  

1. Rapid dissociation from TfR at pH 5.5 (≥90% dissociation after 10 minutes)  
2. High affinity binding at pH 7.4 (Kd = 420 ± 100 pM)  
3. Enhanced intracellular accumulation compared to wild-type scFv H7 (2.6-fold increase in internalized fraction)  
4. Distinct intracellular trafficking pattern characterized by accumulation in large vesicular structures  

The pH-sensitive binding properties are achieved through the strategic placement of histidine residues that undergo protonation at endosomal pH, disrupting antibody-TfR interactions. The histidine substitutions are preferably combined with other mutations that maintain structural integrity while optimizing pH-sensitive behavior.  

The engineered scFvs can be produced recombinantly in various expression systems, including yeast (e.g., Saccharomyces cerevisiae) and mammalian cells. Purification can be accomplished through standard techniques such as affinity chromatography (e.g., Ni-NTA for histidine-tagged proteins).  

The scFvs of the invention are particularly useful as targeting moieties for drug delivery. They can be conjugated to therapeutic agents (e.g., cytotoxins, nucleic acids, proteins) either directly or through linker molecules. The pH-sensitive binding properties enable enhanced intracellular delivery of conjugated therapeutic agents by promoting dissociation from TfR in endosomal compartments and subsequent accumulation within cells.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Engineering pH-sensitive anti-TfR scFvs**  

A histidine-saturation mutagenesis library was created by introducing all possible combinations of histidine substitutions into CDRH1 of anti-TfR scFv H7. The library was expressed on yeast surface and screened for clones that maintained high-affinity TfR binding at pH 7.4 but showed increased dissociation at pH 5.5.  

After multiple rounds of sorting, clone M16 was identified as having particularly favorable pH-sensitive properties. Sequence analysis revealed M16 contained histidine substitutions at multiple positions in CDRH1 (Table 1). At pH 7.4, M16 maintained sub-nanomolar affinity for TfR (Kd = 420 ± 100 pM). After 10 minutes at pH 5.5, M16 showed ≥90% dissociation from TfR, compared to only 56% dissociation for wild-type H7.  

**Example 2: Characterization of pH-sensitive binding**  

The pH-dependent binding properties of M16 were characterized in both surface-displayed and soluble forms. In yeast surface display assays, M16 exhibited rapid dissociation at pH 5.5 while maintaining strong binding at pH 7.4. Similar behavior was observed with purified soluble scFv using bead-based binding assays.  

**Example 3: Cellular trafficking studies**  

The intracellular trafficking of M16 was compared to wild-type H7 and pH-insensitive mutant N5 in SK-BR-3 human breast cancer cells. M16 showed:  

1. 1.5-fold greater total cellular association compared to H7  
2. 2.6-fold greater internalized fraction compared to H7  
3. Distinct intracellular distribution characterized by accumulation in large vesicular structures  

Treatment with bafilomycin A1 (an inhibitor of endosomal acidification) abolished the enhanced internalization of M16, confirming the pH-sensitive mechanism of action.  

**Example 4: Co-localization studies**  

Immunocytochemical analysis revealed that internalized M16 showed reduced co-localization with endosomal (EEA1) and lysosomal (LAMP1, LAMP2) markers compared to H7 and N5. This suggests M16 diverges from canonical TfR trafficking pathways, potentially explaining its enhanced intracellular accumulation.  

The examples demonstrate that engineered pH-sensitive anti-TfR scFvs, particularly M16, exhibit favorable properties for therapeutic applications requiring enhanced intracellular delivery. The histidine substitutions in CDRH1 create pH-dependent binding characteristics that promote dissociation in endosomal compartments and subsequent intracellular accumulation.
