## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of protein engineering and therapeutics. More specifically, the invention pertains to engineered monomeric forms of transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling, their methods of production, and their therapeutic applications.  

## BACKGROUND  

The TGF-β superfamily of cytokines plays critical roles in numerous biological processes including cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and immune regulation. Aberrant TGF-β signaling is implicated in various human diseases including fibrosis, cancer, and autoimmune disorders. Despite decades of research, no TGF-β inhibitors have been approved for clinical use, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic approaches.  

Native TGF-β functions as a disulfide-linked homodimer that signals through formation of a heterotetrameric receptor complex consisting of two type I (TβRI) and two type II (TβRII) receptors. Previous attempts to develop inhibitors have focused on antibodies, soluble receptors, or small molecule kinase inhibitors, all of which have significant limitations in terms of specificity, tissue penetration, or off-target effects.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides engineered monomeric TGF-β proteins that function as potent dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. These mini-monomeric TGF-β (mmTGF-β) variants are created through:  

1. Deletion of the α-helix 3 (residues 52-71) which mediates dimerization and TβRI binding  
2. Substitution of Cys-77 with serine to prevent covalent dimerization  
3. Introduction of specific mutations to enhance TβRII binding affinity  

The resulting mmTGF-β proteins retain high affinity binding to TβRII but are incapable of recruiting TβRI or initiating signaling. These inhibitors demonstrate nanomolar potency against all TGF-β isoforms (TGF-β1, -β2, and -β3) and possess superior pharmaceutical properties including small size (~10 kDa), high solubility at physiological pH, and exceptional stability due to their conserved cystine knot structure.

### II. Terms and Methods  

The term "dominant negative inhibitor" refers to a modified form of a protein that retains the ability to bind its natural interaction partners but blocks their normal function.  

The term "mini-monomeric TGF-β" (mmTGF-β) refers to engineered TGF-β monomers comprising:  
(a) Deletion of residues 52-71 corresponding to α-helix 3  
(b) Substitution of Cys-77 with serine  
(c) Optional charge-modifying substitutions to enhance solubility  
(d) Optional affinity-enhancing substitutions in the TβRII binding interface  

Key experimental methods employed in characterizing the invention include:  
1. Protein expression in E. coli and refolding from inclusion bodies  
2. NMR spectroscopy for structural validation  
3. X-ray crystallography for atomic resolution structure determination  
4. Surface plasmon resonance for binding affinity measurements  
5. Cell-based luciferase reporter assays for functional assessment  

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments  

The invention encompasses multiple embodiments of engineered TGF-β monomers with dominant negative activity:  

1. A basic mmTGF-β construct comprising:  
   - Deletion of α-helix 3 (residues 52-71)  
   - C77S substitution  
   - Exemplified by mmTGF-β2 (FIG. 1D)  

2. An enhanced affinity mmTGF-β construct further comprising:  
   - K25R, I92V, and N94R substitutions (TβRII interface mutations)  
   - Optional peripheral substitutions (R26K, L89V, T95K, I98V)  
   - Exemplified by mmTGF-β2-7M (FIG. 1E)  

3. Variants based on different TGF-β isoforms (TGF-β1, -β2, or -β3 backbones)  

4. Tagged variants (e.g., avi-tagged, His-tagged) for detection/purification  

### IV. Administration of Engineered TGF-β Monomers  

The mmTGF-β proteins of the invention may be administered:  

1. As monotherapy for TGF-β-mediated disorders  
2. In combination with other therapeutic agents  
3. Via various routes including intravenous, subcutaneous, or localized delivery  

Dosing will typically range from 0.1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg based on the specific indication and patient factors. The small size of mmTGF-β enables superior tissue penetration compared to antibody-based inhibitors.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling  

### BACKGROUND  

This example demonstrates the creation and characterization of mmTGF-β2-7M, a high-affinity dominant negative inhibitor based on the TGF-β2 isoform.  

### Discussion  

The mmTGF-β2-7M protein was expressed in E. coli and refolded from inclusion bodies. Structural characterization by NMR and X-ray crystallography confirmed proper folding despite deletion of α-helix 3 (FIGS. 2,5).  

SPR binding studies demonstrated that mmTGF-β2-7M binds TβRII with high affinity (KD = 140 nM, Table 2) comparable to native TGF-β1/β3, while being completely unable to recruit TβRI (FIGS. 3G,H).  

In cell-based assays, mmTGF-β2-7M showed no detectable signaling activity at concentrations up to 1 μM (FIG. 6A), but potently inhibited signaling by all TGF-β isoforms with IC50 values of 19-68 nM (FIG. 6B, Supplemental FIG. S4).  

Mechanistic studies revealed that while mmTGF-β2-7M can weakly self-associate (KD = 42 μM by AUC, Table 3), these dimers cannot bind TβRI. TR-FRET assays confirmed complete inability to form signaling-competent receptor complexes (FIG. 7B).  

These data establish mmTGF-β2-7M as a potent, specific inhibitor of TGF-β signaling with potential therapeutic applications in fibrosis, cancer, and other TGF-β-driven pathologies.
