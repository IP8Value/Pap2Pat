# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Polymeric materials derived from nonrenewable resources have traditionally been employed as supporting substrates in various medical and diagnostic applications. However, the widespread commercial use of such materials presents significant environmental concerns due to their non-biodegradable nature and reliance on fossil fuel-based feedstocks. Cellulose and its derivatives have emerged as promising alternatives owing to their inherent properties, including non-toxicity, hydrophilicity, and chemical resistivity. Despite these advantages, the high porosity of cellulose-based materials poses challenges in diagnostic applications, where controlled diffusion and surface interactions are critical.  

Nitrocellulose, a cellulose derivative, has been utilized as a supporting material for antibody conjugation in diagnostic assays. However, nitrocellulose exhibits several limitations, such as relatively low mechanical strength, flammability, and a strong affinity for proteins that leads to non-specific adsorption. These drawbacks hinder its performance in sensitive diagnostic applications. Nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC), produced through mechanical or chemical disintegration of wood fibers, offers superior properties, including high surface area, strong hydrogen bonding capacity, and excellent mechanical strength. Recent advancements have enabled the production of nearly transparent and smooth NFC films, which exhibit reduced porosity and improved structural integrity.  

A critical challenge in adapting NFC films for diagnostic applications is their water resistance. While precursor pulps with low negative charge facilitate reduced water impregnation and enhanced analyte penetration, the surface chemistry of NFC films must be modified to minimize non-specific adsorption. TEMPO-mediated oxidation presents a viable method for selectively converting primary hydroxyl groups of cellulose to carboxyl groups, which can be further functionalized for immunoassays. However, TEMPO-oxidized NFC films typically exhibit high negative charge, resulting in reduced water resistance. Surface-specific post-oxidation of water-resistant NFC films offers a novel approach to creating immunodiagnostic platforms with low porosity, high stability, and optimized conjugation sites for protein immobilization.  

Inkjet printing has been widely adopted in biosensor and immunoassay fabrication due to its precision, minimal reagent consumption, and non-contact deposition method. The ability to control droplet size and deposition topology makes inkjet printing an attractive technique for patterning antibodies and other biomolecules on solid supports. The integration of inkjet printing with functionalized NFC films could enable the development of high-performance diagnostic platforms with enhanced sensitivity and specificity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel method for the topological modification of water-resistant nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films to create robust and functionalized substrates for diagnostic applications. The invention involves the sequential TEMPO-mediated oxidation and EDC/NHS activation of NFC films, followed by the conjugation of antibodies or other biomolecules via inkjet printing or physical adsorption. The resulting biointerfaces exhibit low porosity, high water resistance, and reduced non-specific adsorption, making them suitable for sensitive immunoassays.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. **Preparation of NFC Films**: NFC films are produced from bleached hardwood pulp through mechanical processing and subsequent disintegration. The films are consolidated via filtration, rolling, and drying to achieve a dense and closed structure with reduced porosity.  

2. **TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation**: The NFC films are subjected to TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on their surface. This oxidation is carefully controlled to ensure surface-specific modification while maintaining the film's structural integrity and water resistance.  

3. **EDC/NHS Activation**: The carboxylated NFC films are further activated using EDC/NHS coupling chemistry to convert carboxyl groups into amine-reactive NHS esters. This step enhances the films' ability to covalently conjugate antibodies and other proteins.  

4. **Antibody Conjugation**: Activated NFC films are functionalized with antibodies or other biomolecules through inkjet printing or physical adsorption. The covalent conjugation ensures stable immobilization, while the low porosity of the films minimizes non-specific interactions.  

5. **Characterization and Validation**: The functionalized NFC films are characterized using techniques such as quartz crystal microbalance with dissipation (QCM-D), contact angle measurements, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and atomic force microscopy (AFM) to validate their performance in diagnostic applications.  

The invention addresses the limitations of existing polymeric and cellulose-based substrates by providing a sustainable, high-performance platform for diagnostic assays. The functionalized NFC films exhibit superior mechanical strength, water resistance, and reduced non-specific adsorption, making them ideal for applications such as lateral flow assays, biosensors, and point-of-care diagnostics.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is further illustrated by the following detailed description of preferred embodiments, which provide specific methods and conditions for producing and functionalizing NFC films.  

### Preparation of NFC Films  

NFC films are prepared from bleached hardwood pulp (e.g., birch) through mechanical processing using a Masuko grinder and subsequent disintegration via microfluidization. The resulting NFC gel is filtered to remove excess water, and the film is consolidated by rolling with a smooth metal rolling pin. The films are then dried between blotting boards under external pressure to achieve a dense and uniform structure. The low porosity and compact structure of these films contribute to their enhanced water resistance and mechanical stability.  

### TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation  

The NFC films are oxidized using the TEMPO/NaBr/NaClO system under controlled conditions. A solution containing TEMPO, NaBr, and NaClO is prepared, and the pH is adjusted to 10. The NFC films are immersed in this solution for varying durations (e.g., 10–300 seconds) to achieve surface-specific carboxylation. The reaction is quenched by adding ethanol, and the films are thoroughly rinsed with water to remove residual oxidation chemicals. The degree of carboxylation is quantified using conductometric titration, while changes in surface hydrophilicity are assessed via contact angle measurements.  

### EDC/NHS Activation  

Carboxylated NFC films are activated by immersion in a solution containing EDC and NHS in sodium acetate buffer (pH 5). The activation reaction proceeds for 20 minutes, after which the films are rinsed with water to remove excess reagents. The formation of NHS esters is confirmed using XPS, which detects the presence of nitrogen and carbonyl groups characteristic of NHS esters.  

### Antibody Conjugation  

Activated NFC films are functionalized with antibodies (e.g., antihuman IgG) or other proteins (e.g., bovine serum albumin) through inkjet printing or physical adsorption. For inkjet printing, fluorescence-labeled antibodies are deposited onto the films using a piezoelectric printer. The printed films are treated with ethanolamine to block unreacted NHS esters and rinsed to remove non-covalently bound proteins. Physical adsorption involves incubating the films with protein solutions, followed by similar blocking and rinsing steps.  

### Characterization Techniques  

The functionalized NFC films are characterized using:  
- **QCM-D**: To monitor mass changes during oxidation, activation, and protein conjugation.  
- **Contact Angle Measurements**: To assess changes in surface hydrophilicity.  
- **XPS**: To analyze surface chemistry and confirm the presence of NHS esters and conjugated proteins.  
- **AFM and CLSM**: To evaluate topological changes and protein distribution on the film surface.  

These techniques collectively validate the films' suitability for diagnostic applications by demonstrating their stability, specificity, and performance under relevant conditions.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention and demonstrate its utility in diagnostic applications.  

#### Example 1: Preparation and Oxidation of NFC Films  

NFC films were prepared from bleached hardwood pulp as described above. The films were oxidized using TEMPO/NaBr/NaClO for 30 seconds, resulting in a carboxyl group density of 169 µeq/g, as determined by conductometric titration. Contact angle measurements confirmed increased hydrophilicity, with values dropping below 15° after oxidation.  

#### Example 2: EDC/NHS Activation and Protein Conjugation  

Carboxylated NFC films were activated with EDC/NHS and incubated with BSA (100 µg/mL). QCM-D analysis revealed a mass increase of 9.8 mg/m², with approximately 50% of the adsorbed BSA remaining after alkaline rinsing, indicating covalent conjugation. Similar results were obtained with antihuman IgG, demonstrating the films' suitability for antibody immobilization.  

#### Example 3: Inkjet Printing of Antibodies  

Dansyl-stained antihuman IgG was printed onto activated NFC films using an inkjet printer. Fluorescence imaging confirmed uniform antibody deposition, with minimal non-specific binding after ethanolamine treatment. AFM analysis revealed a smooth, continuous protein layer with reduced surface roughness compared to unmodified films.  

#### Example 4: Diagnostic Performance  

Functionalized NFC films conjugated with antihuman IgG were used to detect human IgG in solution. QCM-D measurements showed a specific adsorption of 0.93 mg/m² hIgG, with negligible non-specific binding, confirming the films' high specificity and suitability for immunoassays.  

These examples highlight the invention's versatility and effectiveness in creating high-performance diagnostic platforms. The functionalized NFC films offer a sustainable, robust, and sensitive alternative to conventional polymeric substrates, with broad applicability in medical and diagnostic fields.
