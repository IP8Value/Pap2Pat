## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel antibiotic compositions and methods for inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis. More particularly, the invention provides synergistic antibiotic combinations comprising lankacidin (LC) and lankamycin (LM) that bind simultaneously to adjacent sites on the bacterial ribosome, pharmaceutical formulations containing these antibiotic combinations, and methods for treating bacterial infections using such combinations. The invention further encompasses structural modifications of LC and LM that enhance their synergistic binding and inhibitory effects.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The increasing prevalence of antibiotic-resistant bacterial strains has created an urgent need for new antimicrobial agents with novel mechanisms of action. Many clinically used antibiotics target the bacterial ribosome, inhibiting protein synthesis by binding to functional regions such as the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) or the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET). While single antibiotics targeting these sites have shown efficacy, combinations of antibiotics that inhibit successive steps in protein synthesis often exhibit synergistic effects, providing enhanced antibacterial activity.  

Streptogramins represent one such synergistic antibiotic combination, where streptogramin A (SA) binds the PTC while streptogramin B (SB) binds the NPET. However, resistance to streptogramins has emerged, limiting their clinical utility. There remains a need for alternative synergistic antibiotic pairs that can overcome existing resistance mechanisms while maintaining high efficacy against pathogenic bacteria.  

Lankacidin and lankamycin are two structurally distinct antibiotics produced by Streptomyces rochei. While lankacidin has demonstrated antibiotic and antitumor activity, lankamycin exhibits only weak antibacterial effects. Prior to the present invention, the ribosomal binding sites and potential interactions between these compounds remained unknown. The discovery that these naturally co-produced antibiotics can simultaneously bind adjacent ribosomal sites and act synergistically provides a new platform for developing enhanced antibacterial therapies.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel antibiotic compositions and methods based on the discovery that lankacidin (LC) and lankamycin (LM) bind simultaneously to adjacent sites on the bacterial ribosome and exhibit synergistic antibacterial activity. Crystallographic studies have revealed that LC binds the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) while LM binds the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET), with their binding sites partially overlapping those of streptogramin components but involving distinct molecular interactions.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

1. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising synergistic combinations of LC and LM, or their derivatives, for treating bacterial infections.  

2. Methods for inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis by administering LC and LM in combination, wherein the compounds bind simultaneously to the ribosome.  

3. Structural modifications of LC and LM that enhance their binding affinity and synergistic effects, including modifications to the LC macrocyclic ring and LM sugar moieties.  

4. Conjugated molecules comprising LC and LM linked through chemical bridges designed to maintain their simultaneous binding to ribosomal sites.  

The synergistic action of LC and LM provides superior antibacterial effects compared to either compound alone, with potential applications against Gram-positive pathogens. The structural insights provided by crystallographic studies enable rational design of enhanced derivatives with improved pharmacological properties.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Lankacidin Binding Site  

Crystallographic studies at 3.5 Å resolution have unequivocally determined that lankacidin binds to the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) in the large ribosomal subunit. The macrocyclic ring of LC fits into a shallow depression in the wall of the PTC A-site, forming van der Waals interactions with nucleotides U2504, G2505 and U2506. The 2-methyl group at the lactone edge inserts into a hydrophobic crevice formed by bases A2451 and C2452, a site also occupied by the aminoacyl moiety of A-site-bound tRNA.  

LC binding induces a unique network of interactions among five re-oriented nucleotides (U2506, G2505, G2581, C2610 and G2576), stabilizing conformations favorable for drug binding. The amino group of G2502 forms a hydrogen bond with O4 of U2506, which shifts toward LC's 1,3-dicarbonyl system. Additional interactions occur with second-shell nucleotides including G2576, A2062, C2530, U2531, C2507, U2584, G2581, C2610 and A2059, exploiting the PTC's inherent flexibility for induced-fit binding.  

The LC binding site partially overlaps with those of other PTC-targeting antibiotics including phenicols, lincosamides, pleuromutilins, oxazolidinones, and streptogramin A compounds. However, LC exhibits distinct interactions despite positional similarities with dalfopristin and virginiamycin M. Notably, LC causes a 45° rotation of the flexible base A2602, which plays a key role in tRNA translocation, while inducing only minor alterations in U2585 orientation.  

Biochemical assays confirm LC's activity as a PTC inhibitor, with an IC50 of 1.5 μM in bacterial cell-free translation systems and 0.32 μM in peptidyl transferase inhibition assays. These findings establish LC as an effective inhibitor of peptide bond formation, likely by interfering with proper positioning of aminoacyl-tRNA in the A-site.  

### Lankamycin Binding Site  

Structural and biochemical evidence demonstrates that lankamycin binds to the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET) in the large ribosomal subunit, adjacent to the PTC. While chemically similar to erythromycin, LM exhibits distinct binding properties that enable simultaneous binding with LC.  

RNA probing experiments reveal that LM binding protects nucleotide U2609 from CMCT modification and nucleotides A2058 and A2059 from DMS modification, characteristic of macrolide binding to the NPET. However, unlike erythromycin, LM does not compete with LC for ribosomal binding, due to structural differences in their sugar moieties. The C5-linked sugar residue of LM (4-acetyl-L-arcanose) appears critical for this non-competitive binding, as modifications to the analogous desosamine sugar in erythromycin significantly affect binding affinity.  

LM demonstrates moderate inhibitory activity in cell-free translation systems (IC50 = 275 μM), substantially weaker than erythromycin. This reduced potency reflects evolutionary optimization for synergistic action with LC rather than individual efficacy. The LM binding site partially overlaps with streptogramin B compounds but involves distinct interactions due to differences in chemical structure.  

### Lankacidin and Lankamycin Can Simultaneously Bind to the Ribosome  

A key discovery of the present invention is that LC and LM can bind simultaneously to adjacent sites on the ribosome without competitive inhibition. This simultaneous binding was demonstrated through RNA probing experiments showing that:  

1. LC (50 μM) protects nucleotides U2506 and U2585 from CMCT modification without affecting LM's protection of U2609.  

2. LM (500 μM) protects U2609 without interfering with LC's protection of U2506 and U2585.  

3. When both compounds are present, all three nucleotides (U2506, U2585 and U2609) remain protected, confirming concurrent binding.  

In contrast, erythromycin partially reverses LC-mediated protections when co-administered, demonstrating competitive binding. The ability of LC and LM to bind simultaneously stems from structural differences between LM and erythromycin, particularly in their sugar moieties, that prevent steric clashes with LC. This simultaneous binding provides the structural basis for their synergistic antibacterial effects.  

### LC and LM Act Synergistically Upon Bacterial Cells  

The simultaneous binding of LC and LM to adjacent ribosomal sites results in synergistic inhibition of bacterial growth. Checkerboard assays against Staphylococcus aureus demonstrate concave fractional inhibitory concentration (FIC) curves characteristic of synergistic interactions, with the combination showing significantly greater efficacy than either compound alone.  

This synergism was confirmed in Escherichia coli cell-free translation systems, where combined LC and LM treatment produced inhibition exceeding the sum of individual effects. The synergistic action mirrors that observed with streptogramins but involves distinct molecular interactions due to structural differences between the antibiotic pairs.  

The evolutionary co-production and co-regulation of LC and LM by Streptomyces rochei suggests natural selection for this synergistic relationship. While LM exhibits only weak antibiotic activity alone, its combination with LC provides potent antibacterial effects, illustrating the biological advantage of this paired inhibition strategy.  

## Materials and Methods  

### Crystallography  

Crystals of Deinococcus radiodurans 50S ribosomal subunits (D50S) were grown and soaked in solutions containing 0.025 mM lankacidin for 20 hours at 20°C before shock-freezing in liquid nitrogen. X-ray diffraction data were collected at 85-100 K using synchrotron radiation at the Advanced Photon Source and European Synchrotron Radiation Facility.  

Data were processed with HKL2000 and the structure refined using CNS software. Ribosome-antibiotic interactions were analyzed with LigPlot and LPC, with molecular images generated using PyMol. The crystal structure revealed LC's binding site in the PTC and enabled docking studies of LM's predicted NPET binding site.  

### Antibiotic Binding  

Binding assays were performed by incubating D50S subunits (0.1 μM) with radiolabeled antibiotics at varying concentrations (0-2 μM) for 30 minutes at 37°C. Ribosome-antibiotic complexes were purified by gel filtration and bound radioactivity measured by scintillation counting. Competition experiments assessed displacement of bound antibiotics by LC or LM.  

### Inhibition of Cell-Free Protein Synthesis by LC and LM  

E. coli cell-free transcription-translation systems were incubated with varying concentrations of LC, LM, or combinations thereof. Reactions used pBestLuc plasmid DNA to produce firefly luciferase, with inhibition measured by luminescence assays. Synergistic effects were quantified by comparing combination treatments to individual antibiotic doses.  

### Inhibition of the Peptidyl Transferase Reaction by LC  

Peptidyl transferase activity was measured using puromycin reactions with Staphylococcus aureus 70S ribosomes or isolated D50S subunits. LC was added at varying concentrations before puromycin addition, with reaction products quantified by HPLC to determine IC50 values.  

### RNA Probing  

Chemical probing of 23S rRNA used D. radiodurans 50S subunits (200 nM) treated with modifying reagents (CMCT or DMS) after pre-incubation with LC (50 μM), LM (500 μM), or erythromycin (50 μM). Protection patterns were analyzed by primer extension to identify antibiotic binding sites and assess simultaneous binding.  

### Cell-Free Translation System Used for Detecting Synergism In Vitro  

A customized E. coli cell-free system expressed GFP from pIVEX.6D plasmid in the presence of LC/LM combinations. Reactions were quenched with erythromycin after 1 hour, with GFP fluorescence measured to quantify translation inhibition. Dose-response curves for individual and combined antibiotics established synergistic interactions.  

## Pharmacology  

### Chemical Derivatives and Conjugates  

The invention encompasses structural modifications of LC and LM that enhance binding affinity or synergistic effects. For LC, modifications may include:  

1. Alterations to the 17-membered carbocyclic ring to optimize PTC interactions  
2. Modifications at the 2-methyl position to enhance hydrophobic crevice binding  
3. Addition of functional groups that stabilize interactions with G2505 and U2506  

For LM, modifications may include:  

1. Alterations to the 14-membered lactone ring  
2. Modifications to the 4-acetyl-L-arcanose sugar to optimize NPET binding  
3. Addition of functional groups that interact with U2609 and A2058  

The invention further includes conjugated molecules where LC and LM are linked through chemical bridges designed to maintain simultaneous binding to their respective ribosomal sites. Such conjugates may employ:  

1. Flexible linkers of optimal length (10-20 Å) to span the PTC-NPET distance  
2. Cleavable linkers that release both active compounds after cellular uptake  
3. Non-cleavable linkers that maintain the conjugated structure during binding  

### Synthetic and Semi-Synthetic Methods for Producing Antibiotics  

The invention provides methods for producing LC, LM, and their derivatives, including:  

1. Fermentation processes using Streptomyces rochei optimized for LC/LM co-production  
2. Chemical synthesis of LC and LM core structures  
3. Semi-synthetic modification of naturally produced compounds  
4. Combinatorial synthesis of derivative libraries for screening enhanced activity  

Purification methods are provided to isolate LC and LM from fermentation broths, with chromatographic techniques optimized to maintain the natural synergistic pairing. Analytical methods ensure proper characterization of derivative compounds and verification of maintained synergistic activity.  

The pharmaceutical formulations of the invention may combine LC and LM derivatives in ratios from 1:10 to 10:1, preferably 1:1 to 1:5, with optimal ratios determined for specific bacterial targets. Formulations may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, solubility enhancers, and stabilizers to maintain antibiotic activity.  

The invention provides methods of treatment comprising administering synergistic LC/LM combinations to patients with bacterial infections, particularly those caused by Gram-positive pathogens. Dosing regimens are designed to maintain simultaneous ribosomal binding for optimal synergistic effects.  

In summary, the present invention provides novel antibiotic combinations, their structural modifications, and therapeutic methods based on the discovery of simultaneous ribosomal binding and synergistic action between lankacidin and lankamycin. These innovations address the critical need for new antibacterial therapies with enhanced efficacy against resistant bacterial strains.
