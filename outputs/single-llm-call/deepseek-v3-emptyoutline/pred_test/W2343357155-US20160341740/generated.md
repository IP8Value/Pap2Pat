# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Bipolar disorder (BD) represents a significant psychiatric condition characterized by alternating episodes of depression and mania or hypomania, interspersed with periods of euthymia. The disorder typically manifests in late adolescence or early adulthood, imposing a substantial economic burden on healthcare systems and society. Despite extensive research into the genetic and environmental factors contributing to BD, the precise pathogenesis remains incompletely understood. Emerging evidence suggests that mitochondrial dysfunction plays a pivotal role in the disorder, with abnormalities observed in calcium regulation, energy metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, and synaptic plasticity.  

Metabolomics, the comprehensive profiling of small-molecule metabolites, offers a powerful approach to identifying metabolic phenotypes associated with disease states. Unlike other omics techniques, metabolomics directly reflects the metabolic changes occurring within an organism, integrating information from genetic, transcriptional, and protein-level alterations. Previous studies have identified metabolic disturbances in post-mortem brain samples from BD patients, including elevated levels of myo-inositol, creatine, glutamate, lactate, and phosphocholine. However, post-mortem analyses are limited by the rapid metabolic degradation of small molecules, making cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) a more suitable medium for in vivo biomarker discovery.  

Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) represents a state-of-the-art metabolomic analysis technique, offering high resolution and the ability to simultaneously quantify a broad spectrum of charged low-molecular-weight compounds. While CE-TOFMS has been applied to plasma samples in other neuropsychiatric disorders, its utility in CSF metabolomics for BD remains unexplored. The present invention addresses this gap by identifying isocitrate as a novel CSF biomarker for BD, linked to mitochondrial dysfunction and altered expression of isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) genes.  

## Non-Patent Literature  

Relevant prior art includes studies demonstrating mitochondrial dysfunction in BD, as well as metabolomic profiling of CSF and plasma samples in neuropsychiatric disorders. Lan et al. (2019) reported metabolic alterations in post-mortem brain samples from BD patients, though the applicability of these findings to in vivo biomarker discovery is limited. Additional studies have employed CE-TOFMS to identify metabolic signatures in autism spectrum disorders and schizophrenia, but none have focused on CSF metabolomics in BD. The present invention builds upon these findings by establishing a direct link between CSF isocitrate levels and BD pathogenesis, supported by gene and protein expression analyses.  

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention discloses a novel method for diagnosing bipolar disorder (BD) by measuring cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) levels of isocitrate. The invention further encompasses the identification of altered expression of IDH3A and IDH3B genes in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of BD patients, as well as reduced IDH3A protein levels in cerebellar tissue. These findings collectively indicate a disruption in mitochondrial isocitrate metabolism, providing a mechanistic basis for the observed biomarker.  

The invention also includes a diagnostic assay utilizing capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) to quantify CSF isocitrate levels, enabling the differentiation of BD patients from healthy controls. Additionally, the invention covers therapeutic applications targeting mitochondrial IDH3 activity to restore normal isocitrate metabolism in BD.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The invention is based on metabolomic profiling of CSF samples from 54 BD patients and 40 healthy controls using CE-TOFMS. Of the 116 metabolites analyzed, 72 were detectable in CSF, with 13 showing significant alterations in BD patients. Multivariate logistic regression identified isocitrate as an independent biomarker, with elevated CSF levels significantly correlating with BD diagnosis (odds ratio: 4.402; 95% confidence interval: 2.249–7.266).  

Isocitrate is a key intermediate in the citric acid cycle, synthesized from citrate via aconitase (ACO) and metabolized by isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH). The invention demonstrates reduced mRNA expression of IDH3A and IDH3B in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of BD patients, alongside decreased IDH3A protein levels in the cerebellum. These findings suggest impaired mitochondrial IDH3 activity, leading to isocitrate accumulation.  

The diagnostic method involves lumbar puncture to collect CSF, followed by CE-TOFMS analysis to quantify isocitrate levels. The assay is optimized for high-throughput clinical use, with a detection threshold established through comparative studies in BD and control populations. The invention further includes genetic and protein expression assays to validate IDH3A and IDH3B as complementary biomarkers.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

CSF samples were collected from 54 BD patients and 40 healthy controls under standardized conditions. Metabolomic analysis via CE-TOFMS revealed significantly elevated isocitrate levels in BD patients (P < 0.0001), with no confounding effects from age, body mass index, or medication.  

### Example 2  

Post-mortem brain analysis showed reduced mRNA expression of IDH3A (P = 0.007) and IDH3B (P = 0.007) in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of BD patients compared to controls. Protein levels of IDH3A were also decreased in cerebellar tissue (P = 0.017).  

### Example 3  

Genetic association studies of IDH and ACO genes in 2251 BD patients and 3364 controls revealed no significant single-nucleotide polymorphisms after multiple testing correction, suggesting that isocitrate dysregulation is not driven by primary genetic variants.  

### Example 4  

Chronic treatment of rats with lithium or valproic acid did not alter CSF isocitrate levels or IDH3A/IDH3B expression, confirming that the biomarker is independent of medication effects.  

### Example 5  

A logistic regression model incorporating isocitrate levels achieved a Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit statistic of P = 0.145, validating its diagnostic utility for BD.  

### Example 6  

Cross-validation using the jack-knife method confirmed the internal validity of the isocitrate biomarker, with consistent odds ratios across subsamples.  

### Example 7  

Protein expression assays in post-mortem cerebellar tissue demonstrated reduced IDH3A levels in BD, major depressive disorder, and schizophrenia, suggesting a shared metabolic pathway in psychiatric disorders.  

### Example 8  

Mitochondrial functional assays confirmed impaired IDH3 activity in BD patient-derived cells, supporting isocitrate as a therapeutic target for restoring energy metabolism.  

The invention thus provides a comprehensive framework for diagnosing and treating BD through mitochondrial isocitrate metabolism, with applications in clinical practice and drug development.
