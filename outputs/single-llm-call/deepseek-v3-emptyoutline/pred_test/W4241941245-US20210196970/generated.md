# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and specifically to a novel combination therapy for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma (CCA). More particularly, the invention pertains to the synergistic use of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) in conjunction with the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy regimen to enhance therapeutic efficacy while reducing systemic toxicity. The invention provides methods, compositions, and systems for administering this combination therapy to target CCA cells selectively, thereby improving patient outcomes.  

### Brief Description of the Related Art  

Cholangiocarcinoma is a rare but aggressive malignancy arising in the biliary tract, characterized by late-stage diagnosis, poor prognosis, and high recurrence rates. Current standard treatments include surgical resection, liver transplantation, and chemotherapy with gemcitabine or fluorouracil (5-FU). However, these approaches are limited by chemoresistance, severe side effects, and insufficient long-term efficacy.  

The FOLFIRINOX regimen, comprising fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin, has shown promise in treating metastatic pancreatic cancer but is associated with significant toxicity, including thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and febrile neutropenia. Modified dosing strategies, such as FOLFOX-4, FOLFOX-5, and FOLFOX-7, have been explored to mitigate adverse effects but remain suboptimal for CCA treatment.  

Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has emerged as a novel anti-cancer modality due to its selective induction of apoptosis and DNA damage in tumor cells without harming normal tissue. The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS), currently under clinical investigation, delivers low-temperature plasma (26–31°C) and has demonstrated efficacy in reducing tumor cell viability across multiple cancer types.  

Despite these advancements, there remains an unmet need for effective, low-toxicity therapies for CCA. The present invention addresses this need by combining CAP with FOLFIRINOX to achieve synergistic anti-tumor effects, thereby reducing the required chemotherapeutic dose and minimizing adverse events.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel combination therapy for cholangiocarcinoma, comprising the administration of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) in conjunction with the FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy regimen. The invention is based on the discovery that CAP enhances the cytotoxic effects of FOLFIRINOX, allowing for dose reduction while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A method for treating cholangiocarcinoma by sequentially administering FOLFIRINOX followed by CAP, wherein the combination demonstrates synergistic anti-tumor activity.  
2. A pharmaceutical composition comprising FOLFIRINOX components (5-FU, leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin) formulated for use in combination with CAP.  
3. A system for delivering CAP to tumor sites, particularly the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, configured for post-chemotherapy application.  

The invention further encompasses optimized dosing regimens, wherein CAP is administered at durations of 1–7 minutes and FOLFIRINOX is dosed at subclinical levels (e.g., 6.7 µM 5-FU equivalent). Preclinical data demonstrate that this combination reduces CCA cell viability by up to 99%, induces cell cycle arrest, and decreases proliferation markers (e.g., Ki-67) without damaging normal tissue.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Experiments  

The invention was evaluated through in vitro experiments using the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line KKU-055. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) and treated with FOLFIRINOX at six dose levels (0.8–53.8 µM 5-FU equivalent) for 24 hours, followed by CAP exposure (1–7 minutes at 28.7 W). Cell viability was assessed via MTT assay, while proliferation and cell cycle dynamics were analyzed using confocal microscopy (Ki-67/DAPI staining) and live-cell imaging (IncuCyte system).  

### Results  

1. **FOLFIRINOX Monotherapy**: Dose-dependent reduction in viability was observed, with the highest dose (53.8 µM 5-FU) reducing viability to 19%.  
2. **CAP Monotherapy**: CAP alone reduced viability to 3% at 7 minutes.  
3. **Combination Therapy**: Synergistic effects were noted, particularly at low FOLFIRINOX doses (6.7 µM 5-FU) combined with 5-minute CAP, achieving 91% viability reduction. Statistical analysis (two-way ANOVA, post hoc Bonferroni) confirmed synergy (p < 0.05).  
4. **Proliferation and Cell Cycle**: Combination treatment significantly decreased Ki-67 expression and induced G1/S arrest, with complete cell death observed within 24 hours at higher doses.  

### Discussion  

The data demonstrate that CAP potentiates the anti-cancer effects of FOLFIRINOX, enabling dose reduction and mitigating toxicity. The synergy is attributed to CAP-induced oxidative stress, which exacerbates FOLFIRINOX-mediated DNA damage and apoptosis. This combination is particularly advantageous for patients ineligible for high-dose chemotherapy or surgery.  

### Conclusion  

The invention represents a breakthrough in CCA treatment by combining CAP with FOLFIRINOX to achieve superior efficacy and tolerability. Future directions include in vivo validation and clinical trials to establish optimal dosing protocols.
