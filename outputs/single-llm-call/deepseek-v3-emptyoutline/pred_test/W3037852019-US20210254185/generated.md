# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and stem cell technology. Specifically, it pertains to methods for detecting residual undifferentiated induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) in differentiated cell populations derived from iPSCs. The invention provides novel marker genes and detection techniques that enable highly sensitive and specific identification of undifferentiated iPSCs across cell lineages originating from all three germ layers (endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm). This technology is particularly valuable for quality control in cell therapy products intended for clinical applications.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

### PRIOR ART LITERATURE  

Current approaches for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations face several limitations. LIN28A has been reported as a marker for residual undifferentiated iPSCs in retinal pigment epithelial cells (RPE), but its applicability to other cell lineages remains uncertain. Other methods include cell sorting with surface markers, growth medium modifications, and the use of cytotoxic viral vectors or microRNA-302. However, these techniques are often lineage-specific and lack the sensitivity required for clinical applications.  

Existing methods such as SOX2, NANOG, or OCT4 detection have relatively high detection limits (1-5%) and may not be sufficiently sensitive for therapeutic applications where even minute quantities of undifferentiated cells could pose teratoma risks. Furthermore, many current markers show variable expression patterns across different differentiation protocols and cell lines, limiting their reliability. The re-seeding method, while sensitive, requires extended culture periods (at least one week) and is labor-intensive.  

There remains an unmet need for a universal, highly sensitive detection method that can identify residual undifferentiated iPSCs across diverse differentiated cell types with minimal processing time and high reliability.  

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION  

### Problem for Solution by the Invention  

The key problem addressed by this invention is the lack of sensitive, robust, and universal methods for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations intended for therapeutic use. Current methods suffer from several limitations: (1) inadequate sensitivity for detecting low levels of contamination, (2) lineage-specific applicability that prevents broad use across different cell types, (3) variable performance across different iPSC lines, and (4) time-consuming procedures that hinder practical implementation in clinical settings.  

### Means to Solve the Problem  

The invention solves these problems through the identification and utilization of novel marker genes that exhibit:  

1. Specific and abundant expression in undifferentiated iPSCs but not in differentiated cells  
2. Immediate downregulation upon differentiation induction  
3. Consistent expression patterns across multiple iPSC lines  
4. High sensitivity for detecting minimal residual undifferentiated cells (as low as 0.005%)  

The primary marker genes identified are ESRG (Embryonic Stem Cell Related) and CNMD (Chondromodulin), with SFRP2 (Secreted Frizzled Related Protein 2) as an additional marker. These markers are detected using quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) or single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH) followed by flow cytometry analysis.  

The invention further provides a comprehensive validation protocol comprising:  

1. Correlation analysis between marker gene expression and actual residual undifferentiated cell counts  
2. Spike-in experiments to establish detection limits  
3. Cross-lineage validation across endodermal, mesodermal, and ectodermal derivatives  
4. Comparison with conventional markers to demonstrate superior sensitivity  

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention provides several significant advantages over existing methods:  

1. **Enhanced Sensitivity**: Detection limits of 0.005% for ESRG and 0.025% for CNMD/SFRP2, representing a 200-1000 fold improvement over conventional markers like SOX2 or NANOG.  

2. **Universal Applicability**: Validated across cell types derived from all three germ layers, including hepatic (endoderm), mesenchymal/endothelial (mesoderm), and neural (ectoderm) lineages.  

3. **Rapid Detection**: The qPCR-based method provides results within hours compared to days required for colony formation assays.  

4. **Robust Performance**: Consistent results across multiple iPSC lines and differentiation protocols.  

5. **Versatile Implementation**: Compatible with various detection platforms including qPCR, digital PCR, and smFISH-flow cytometry.  

These improvements significantly enhance the safety profile of iPSC-derived therapeutic products by enabling reliable exclusion of potentially tumorigenic undifferentiated cells.  

## BEST MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Identification and Validation of Marker Genes**  

Single-cell RNA sequencing data from hepatocyte differentiation was analyzed to identify genes meeting the following criteria:  
- Specific expression in iPSCs  
- High expression levels in iPSCs  
- Significant downregulation in differentiated cells  

Twelve candidate genes were initially selected, with ESRG, CNMD, SFRP2, SOX2, THY1, USP44, VSNL1, and SPP1 showing promising patterns. Further validation across multiple iPSC lines confirmed ESRG, CNMD, and SFRP2 as the most robust markers.  

**Example 2: Correlation with Residual Undifferentiated Cells**  

Using over-passaged iPSCs as a model for residual contamination:  
- ESRG, CNMD, and SFRP2 showed strong correlation (r ≥ 0.6) with actual undifferentiated cell counts  
- LIN28A showed poor correlation (r < 0.3)  
- Spike-in experiments demonstrated linear detection down to 0.005% contamination  

**Example 3: Cross-Lineage Validation**  

The markers were tested in:  
1. Endodermal lineages: Hepatic and pancreatic cells  
2. Mesodermal lineages: Septum transversum mesenchyme and endothelial cells  
3. Ectodermal lineages: Neural crest cells and neural stem cells  

ESRG and CNMD showed consistent downregulation in all lineages, while maintaining high sensitivity for detecting spiked-in undifferentiated cells.  

**Example 4: smFISH-Flow Cytometry Implementation**  

Single-molecule FISH for ESRG followed by flow cytometry:  
- Successfully detected rare ESRG+ cells in differentiated populations  
- Enabled quantitative assessment of contamination levels  
- Provided spatial information about residual undifferentiated cells  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The present invention has significant industrial applicability in several domains:  

1. **Cell Therapy Manufacturing**: Quality control for iPSC-derived therapeutic products across multiple cell types including hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, neural cells, and others.  

2. **Pharmaceutical Development**: Safety assessment of drug screening platforms using iPSC-derived cells.  

3. **Diagnostic Services**: Commercial testing services for research and clinical institutions requiring validation of differentiated cell products.  

4. **Research Tools**: Enhanced experimental protocols for stem cell differentiation studies and basic research.  

5. **Regulatory Compliance**: Standardized safety testing method for regulatory submissions of cell-based therapies.  

The technology is particularly valuable for clinical applications where stringent safety standards require demonstration of minimal risk from residual undifferentiated cells. The ability to detect contamination at 0.005% levels far exceeds current regulatory expectations and positions this invention as a critical component in the translation of iPSC technologies to clinical practice.
