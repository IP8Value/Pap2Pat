## **BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION**  
Apoptosis, or programmed cell death, is a critical biological process that regulates tissue homeostasis and eliminates damaged or infected cells. The BCL-2 family of proteins plays a central role in controlling apoptosis, with pro-apoptotic members such as BAX initiating mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), leading to cytochrome c release and caspase activation. Dysregulation of BAX activity is implicated in various diseases, including cancer (where apoptosis is suppressed) and neurodegenerative disorders (where excessive apoptosis occurs).  

Current therapeutic strategies targeting BAX activation face significant challenges due to the lack of high-affinity, conformation-specific inhibitors that selectively modulate BAX without disrupting other essential cellular functions. Existing approaches, such as BH3 mimetics, often exhibit off-target effects or insufficient binding specificity. There is a pressing need for novel inhibitors that directly and selectively engage BAX to either suppress or promote its activation, depending on the therapeutic context.  

## **SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION**  
The present invention provides novel synthetic antibodies, particularly antigen-binding fragments (Fabs), that specifically bind to monomeric BAX with high affinity and inhibit its activation. These Fabs were identified through phage display screening of a synthetic antibody library, yielding 14 distinct clones with nanomolar binding affinities. Among these, Fab 3G11 demonstrates exceptional inhibitory activity by binding to the N-terminal activation site of BAX, preventing BH3-triggered conformational changes required for mitochondrial translocation and oligomerization.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **High Specificity:** The Fabs selectively target BAX without cross-reacting with other BCL-2 family proteins.  
2. **Mechanistic Inhibition:** Fab 3G11 directly blocks the N-terminal trigger site of BAX, a novel mechanism distinct from existing allosteric inhibitors.  
3. **Functional Validation:** The Fabs inhibit BAX-mediated liposomal permeabilization and mitochondrial cytochrome c release in vitro.  
4. **Therapeutic Potential:** These inhibitors may be developed into drugs for conditions involving dysregulated apoptosis, such as cancer or ischemic injuries.  

## **DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION**  

### **Introduction**  
The invention pertains to recombinant synthetic antibodies and their use in modulating BAX-dependent apoptosis. The Fabs were derived from a phage-displayed synthetic antibody library ("Library F") engineered to encode diverse complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) with binomial Tyr/Ser diversity in CDR-H1/H2 and expanded amino acid variability in CDR-L3/H3. This library was screened against purified monomeric BAX, leading to the isolation of high-affinity binders.  

### **Materials and Methods**  

#### **Production of Recombinant BAX**  
Full-length wild-type human BAX and truncated variants (e.g., BAX ΔC26) were cloned into the pTYB1 vector (New England Biolabs) with an N-terminal chitin-binding domain. Expression was induced in *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3)-RIPL cells using 1 mM isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG). Cells were lysed, and BAX was purified via chitin affinity chromatography, followed by gel filtration (Superdex 75) to isolate monomeric fractions.  

#### **Phage Display Selections**  
A synthetic Fab library was panned against immobilized BAX over three rounds of biopanning. High-affinity clones were identified via phage ELISA, with Fab 3G11 exhibiting the strongest binding (EC50 = 2.6 nM). Selected Fabs were converted into soluble proteins by introducing a hexahistidine tag for nickel-affinity purification.  

#### **Binding and Inhibition Assays**  
- **Direct Binding ELISA:** Confirmed nanomolar affinity of Fabs for BAX.  
- **Competitive ELISA:** Demonstrated overlapping epitopes among Fabs.  
- **Biolayer Interferometry:** Quantified kinetic parameters (ka, kd, KD) for Fab-BAX interactions.  
- **Liposome Permeabilization Assay:** Showed that Fabs (e.g., 3G11) inhibit tBID-triggered BAX pore formation.  
- **Mitochondrial Translocation Assay:** Validated that 3G11 blocks BAX integration into mitochondrial membranes.  

#### **Structural Characterization**  
- **Hydrogen-Deuterium Exchange Mass Spectrometry (HXMS):** Revealed that 3G11 binding protects the N-terminal α1-α2 loop and α6 helix of BAX.  
- **NMR Spectroscopy:** Confirmed 1:1 stoichiometry of the Fab-BAX complex.  
- **Computational Docking (HADDOCK):** Predicted binding interfaces between 3G11 CDRs and BAX helices α1/α6.  

### **Results**  

#### **Discovery of High-Affinity BAX-Binding Fabs**  
Fourteen unique Fabs were isolated, with EC50 values ranging from 2.3 nM (2B1) to 250 nM (2A5). Competitive ELISAs indicated overlapping epitopes, with 3G11, 3E8, and 2B1 showing the strongest inhibition of BAX activation.  

#### **Inhibition of BAX Activation**  
- **Liposomal Assays:** 3G11 (2 μM) completely suppressed tBID-induced BAX pore formation.  
- **Mitochondrial Assays:** 3G11 dose-dependently blocked BAX translocation and cytochrome c release.  

#### **Structural Mechanism**  
HXMS and mutagenesis studies demonstrated that 3G11 engages the N-terminal trigger site of BAX (residues K21, R134), preventing conformational changes required for activation. Computational models supported these findings, showing Fab-CDRI interactions with BAX helices α1/α6.  

### **DISCUSSION**  
The invention provides the first synthetic antibodies that directly inhibit BAX activation by targeting its N-terminal trigger site, a mechanism distinct from BH3 mimetics or allosteric inhibitors. These Fabs serve as valuable tools for:  
1. **Studying BAX Activation:** Enabling structural insights into conformational transitions.  
2. **Drug Development:** Facilitating high-throughput screens for small-molecule BAX modulators.  
3. **Therapeutic Applications:** Potential use in diseases where BAX inhibition (e.g., stroke) or activation (e.g., cancer) is desirable.  

Future work may involve humanization of these Fabs for clinical use or engineering bispecific antibodies targeting both BAX and anti-apoptotic BCL-2 proteins.
