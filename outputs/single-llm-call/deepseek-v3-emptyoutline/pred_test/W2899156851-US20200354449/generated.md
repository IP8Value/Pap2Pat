# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) targeting the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2). More specifically, the invention provides fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to ROR2, compositions comprising these antibodies conjugated to cytotoxic agents, and methods of using these ADCs for the treatment of ROR2-expressing cancers. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, dosing regimens, and therapeutic applications of the anti-ROR2 ADCs.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with existing treatment modalities often limited by toxicity and lack of tumor specificity. Traditional chemotherapeutic agents exhibit dose-limiting toxicities due to their non-selective mechanism of action. Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) represent an emerging class of targeted therapeutics that combine the specificity of monoclonal antibodies with the potent cytotoxic effects of small molecule payloads. By selectively delivering cytotoxic agents to tumor cells expressing specific tumor-associated antigens (TAAs), ADCs aim to maximize anti-tumor efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity.  

ROR2, a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor family, has emerged as a promising therapeutic target due to its restricted expression in normal adult tissues and overexpression in various malignancies including renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and pancreatic cancer. ROR2 plays a critical role in tumor progression through activation of non-canonical Wnt signaling pathways, promoting cell migration, invasion, and tumor growth. The differential expression pattern of ROR2 between normal and malignant tissues makes it an attractive target for ADC-based therapeutic intervention.  

Current ADC development faces several challenges including identification of optimal antibody candidates that combine high target specificity with efficient internalization properties. Traditional antibody discovery approaches often require extensive screening and optimization steps before functional ADC properties can be evaluated. There exists a need in the art for improved methods to identify and develop highly effective anti-ROR2 ADCs with favorable therapeutic indices. The present invention addresses these needs by providing novel fully human anti-ROR2 antibodies identified through an efficient functional screening platform, along with corresponding ADCs demonstrating potent and selective anti-tumor activity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to ROR2, along with antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising these antibodies and methods of using these ADCs for cancer treatment. The antibodies were identified through an innovative functional screening platform that enables simultaneous evaluation of antigen binding and ADC potency early in the discovery process.  

### Definitions  

As used herein, the following terms have the following meanings:  

"Antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule that specifically binds to an antigen through at least one antigen recognition site located in the variable region of the immunoglobulin molecule. The term encompasses intact immunoglobulins as well as antigen-binding fragments thereof, including but not limited to Fab, Fab', F(ab')2, Fv, single-chain Fv (scFv), diabodies, and other antibody fragments that retain antigen-binding function.  

"Antibody-drug conjugate" or "ADC" refers to an antibody conjugated to one or more cytotoxic agents (drugs) through a linker. The antibody portion of the ADC specifically binds to a target antigen (e.g., ROR2) on the surface of target cells (e.g., cancer cells), facilitating delivery of the cytotoxic agent to the target cells.  

"Complementarity determining regions" or "CDRs" refer to the hypervariable regions within the variable domains of antibodies that primarily mediate antigen binding. CDRs are typically defined according to the Kabat numbering system (Kabat et al., Sequences of Proteins of Immunological Interest, 5th Ed. Public Health Service, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, Md. (1991)).  

"Cytotoxic agent" refers to a chemical compound or biological molecule that inhibits or prevents cellular function and/or causes cell death. Cytotoxic agents suitable for use in ADCs include chemotherapeutic agents, toxins, radioactive isotopes, and other molecules capable of killing cells.  

"Fully human antibody" refers to an antibody having variable and constant regions derived from human immunoglobulin sequences. Such antibodies may be produced in transgenic mice engineered to produce human antibodies or through phage display or other in vitro selection methods using human immunoglobulin gene repertoires.  

"ROR2" or "receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2" refers to the human protein encoded by the ROR2 gene (NCBI Gene ID: 4920) and includes naturally occurring variants, isoforms, and fragments thereof, particularly the extracellular domain.  

"Specifically binds" means that an antibody recognizes and binds to a particular antigen (e.g., ROR2) with greater affinity than to unrelated proteins, typically with a dissociation constant (KD) of less than about 10-7 M as measured by surface plasmon resonance.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention provides isolated fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human ROR2. These antibodies were generated by immunizing transgenic mice (H2L2 mice) that produce antibodies with human variable regions and rat constant regions. The mice were immunized with the extracellular domain of human ROR2 (hROR2-ECD), and antigen-specific B cells were isolated from the spleens of immunized animals.  

Variable heavy (VH) and variable light (VL) chain genes from the antigen-specific B cells were amplified by RT-PCR and cloned into transposable expression vectors. These vectors were used to generate cellular libraries in murine progenitor B cells (L11 cells) that stably express the human antibodies on their surface and secrete them into culture supernatants. The libraries were screened for hROR2-binding antibodies using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), and positive clones were further evaluated for their ability to mediate cytotoxic payload delivery in a secondary ADC assay.  

From this screening process, twelve distinct anti-ROR2 antibody clonotypes were identified, representing a diverse panel of fully human monoclonal antibodies with nanomolar affinities for hROR2. These antibodies demonstrated potent and selective cytotoxicity when conjugated to the anthracycline derivative PNU-159682, with IC50 values ranging from 12.3 to 168 ng/ml against ROR2-expressing tumor cells.

The antibodies may be of any immunoglobulin isotype, including IgG (e.g., IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, IgG4), IgM, IgA, IgE, or IgD. In preferred embodiments, the antibodies are IgG1 antibodies. The antibodies may comprise human constant regions, particularly human IgG1 constant regions.  

The invention further provides antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising the anti-ROR2 antibodies conjugated to cytotoxic agents. Suitable cytotoxic agents include but are not limited to auristatins (e.g., monomethyl auristatin E or F), maytansinoids (e.g., DM1, DM4), calicheamicins, duocarmycins, pyrrolobenzodiazepines, and anthracyclines (e.g., doxorubicin, PNU-159682). In preferred embodiments, the cytotoxic agent is PNU-159682 or a derivative thereof.  

The cytotoxic agent may be conjugated to the antibody through a linker. The linker may be cleavable (e.g., by intracellular enzymes such as cathepsins or under acidic conditions) or non-cleavable. Suitable cleavable linkers include valine-citrulline (Val-Cit) dipeptide linkers, phenylalanine-lysine (Phe-Lys) dipeptide linkers, and hydrazone linkers. In preferred embodiments, the linker is a cathepsin-cleavable linker.  

The antibody may be conjugated to the cytotoxic agent at specific sites, such as at engineered cysteine residues or at the C-terminus of the heavy and/or light chains. In some embodiments, the antibody is conjugated to the cytotoxic agent via sortase-mediated transpeptidation, which enables site-specific conjugation at a C-terminal LPETG motif.  

The drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of the ADCs may range from 1 to 8, preferably from 2 to 4. ADCs with DARs in this range typically exhibit optimal balance between potency and pharmacokinetic properties.  

The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-ROR2 ADCs and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions may be formulated for intravenous administration and may include excipients such as buffers, surfactants, and stabilizers.  

Methods of treating ROR2-expressing cancers are also provided, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-ROR2 ADC. The ADCs may be administered at doses ranging from about 0.1 mg/kg to about 10 mg/kg, preferably about 0.5 mg/kg to about 5 mg/kg, at intervals of about 1 to 4 weeks. The ROR2-expressing cancer may be selected from renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer, and multiple myeloma.  

The invention further provides nucleic acids encoding the heavy and light chains of the anti-ROR2 antibodies, expression vectors comprising these nucleic acids, and host cells transformed with these vectors for recombinant antibody production. Methods of making the anti-ROR2 ADCs are also provided, comprising culturing host cells expressing the antibodies, purifying the antibodies, and conjugating them to cytotoxic agents.
