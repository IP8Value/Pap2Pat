# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of reproductive medicine and assisted reproductive technologies. More specifically, it concerns a novel method for improving sperm motility in vitro through treatment with molecular hydrogen (H2). The invention provides compositions and techniques for enhancing motility of human sperm samples, particularly those affected by asthenozoospermia or damage from cryopreservation, thereby increasing their clinical utility for in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) procedures.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

### PRIOR ART DOCUMENTS  

Male infertility affects approximately 7% of the male population, with asthenozoospermia (reduced sperm motility) being a major contributing factor. Various approaches have been attempted to improve sperm motility, including supplementation with antioxidants such as zinc, aspartic acid, coenzyme Q10, myoinositol, and xanthine derivatives. These compounds have shown limited success in stimulating sperm motility through oral administration or in vitro treatment.  

The hydrogen molecule (H2) has been previously investigated for its antioxidant properties in other medical contexts. Studies have demonstrated that H2 can reduce oxidative damage by selectively neutralizing cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals (•OH) while preserving physiologically important reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as nitric oxide (NO•) and superoxide. H2 has shown protective effects against ischemia-reperfusion injury in various organs and has been used to mitigate radiation-induced tissue damage.  

However, prior to the present invention, there was no recognition in the art that H2 treatment could specifically improve sperm motility or that it could be effectively applied to damaged or cryopreserved sperm samples for fertility treatments. Existing methods for improving sperm motility have been limited by inconsistent results, potential toxicity at higher concentrations, and inability to maintain improved motility for clinically relevant durations.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  

The present invention addresses several unmet needs in the field of reproductive medicine. First, it provides a solution for improving motility in sperm samples that have been damaged by oxidative stress or cryopreservation. Second, it offers a method to enhance the clinical utility of sperm samples from patients with asthenozoospermia. Third, the invention provides a treatment that maintains improved sperm motility for sufficient durations to allow successful IVF or ICSI procedures.  

### Means to Solve the Object  

The invention solves these problems through a novel method comprising:  
1) Preparing a sperm suspension from a human semen sample  
2) Exposing said sperm suspension to a gas mixture containing molecular hydrogen (H2) at concentrations between 5-75% by volume  
3) Maintaining said exposure for a period of 30-60 minutes  
4) Optionally washing the treated sperm with a medium containing dissolved H2  
5) Using the treated sperm for assisted reproductive technologies  

The gas mixture preferably contains 20% oxygen (O2) and 5% carbon dioxide (CO2), with the balance being H2 and nitrogen (N2). For frozen-thawed sperm samples, treatment with a sperm-wash medium containing 50-75% saturation of dissolved H2 has shown particularly beneficial effects.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention provides several significant advantages over prior methods:  
1) Marked improvement in forward motility of damaged sperm samples (from 3.6% to 16.8% in experimental models)  
2) Enhancement of mitochondrial membrane potential in treated sperm  
3) Maintenance of improved motility for at least 2.5 hours after treatment  
4) Ability to repeat the treatment effect with subsequent exposures  
5) Specific effectiveness for asthenozoospermic samples while showing minimal effect on already highly motile sperm  
6) No observed negative effects on sperm velocity or hyperactivation  
7) Safety profile suitable for clinical use in reproductive medicine  

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention may be practiced through several embodiments, as described below.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Treatment of Experimentally Damaged Sperm**  
Sperm suspensions were prepared from human semen samples by washing in cleavage medium supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction (PPF). Samples were left at room temperature for >5 days to induce oxidative damage, resulting in average forward motility of 3.6%. The damaged suspensions were placed in an exposure chamber with a gas mixture containing 50% H2, 20% O2, 5% CO2, and 25% N2 for 40 minutes. Following treatment, forward motility increased significantly to 16.8%, while control samples treated with N2-mixed gas showed no significant improvement.  

**Example 2: Treatment Duration Optimization**  
Sperm suspensions with relatively higher initial motility were treated with the H2-containing gas mixture for varying durations. Treatment for 30 minutes resulted in significant motility improvement, while 5-minute treatments showed no effect. Extending treatment to 60 minutes provided no additional benefit over 30-minute treatment.  

**Example 3: Effect on Sperm Survival Rate**  
Sperm suspensions were divided based on viability as assessed by propidium iodide staining. Samples with >20% viability showed significant motility improvement after H2 treatment (from 3.0% to 11.0% forward motility), while samples with <20% viability showed minimal response. However, in 3 of 13 low-viability samples, H2 treatment stimulated previously undetectable weak motility.  

**Example 4: Effect on Sperm Velocity**  
Computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA) of H2-treated samples showed no significant change in sperm swimming speed compared to controls, indicating that H2 treatment improves motility without causing hyperactivation.  

**Example 5: Retention of Treatment Effect**  
The improved motility from H2 treatment persisted for at least 2.5 hours after removal from the H2 environment. When motility began to decline after 24 hours, a second H2 treatment restored motility to approximately 67% of the initial improvement.  

**Example 6: Mitochondrial Membrane Potential**  
Staining with tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester (TMRM) showed significantly increased fluorescence in H2-treated sperm, indicating enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential compared to untreated or N2-treated controls.  

**Example 7: Treatment of Frozen-Thawed Sperm**  
Frozen-thawed sperm samples from 21 patients were divided into normozoospermic (≥50% motility) and asthenozoospermic (<50% motility) groups. Treatment with sperm-wash medium containing 50% saturated H2 significantly improved motility in asthenozoospermic samples, while higher concentrations (75-100% saturation) showed reduced effectiveness.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The present invention has significant industrial applicability in several fields:  

1) **Fertility Clinics**: The method can be directly implemented in IVF and ICSI procedures to improve success rates, particularly for patients with asthenozoospermia or those requiring frozen sperm samples.  

2) **Sperm Banking**: The invention provides a means to enhance the quality and utility of cryopreserved sperm samples, increasing their viability and motility post-thaw.  

3) **Medical Device Manufacturing**: The specialized gas exposure chambers and monitoring systems described in the examples represent novel medical devices that could be commercialized.  

4) **Pharmaceutical Industry**: The development of H2-containing media and solutions for sperm treatment creates new product opportunities in reproductive medicine.  

5) **Research Applications**: The method provides researchers with a tool to study sperm motility mechanisms and test other compounds that might synergize with H2 treatment.
