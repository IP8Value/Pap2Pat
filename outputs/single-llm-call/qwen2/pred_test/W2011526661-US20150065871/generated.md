# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) poses a significant challenge for the delivery of therapeutic agents to the brain. Traditional methods, such as intracranial injections, mixing or attaching agents to BBB-modifying chemicals, and chemical alterations of agents, are either invasive, drug-specific, or lack spatial specificity. These limitations are particularly problematic for deep subcortical structures like the basal ganglia, where alternative invasive approaches can damage not only the target structure but also surrounding grey and white matter. A non-invasive method for targeted drug delivery would be highly beneficial for both clinical applications and neuroscience research.

This invention addresses the need for a non-invasive method to deliver drugs to specific brain regions, particularly the basal ganglia. The method utilizes focused ultrasound (FUS) in combination with systemically injected micro-bubbles to reversibly increase the permeability of localized parts of the BBB. The FUS method is capable of delivering a wide range of compounds, from small molecules to large molecules, and is entirely non-invasive, allowing for targeted delivery through the intact skin and skull. The method also includes real-time monitoring using a passive cavitation detector (PCD) to ensure safety and efficacy.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for non-invasively delivering therapeutic agents to specific brain regions, particularly the basal ganglia, by using focused ultrasound (FUS) in combination with systemically injected micro-bubbles. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Target Selection**: Selecting a specific brain region, such as the basal ganglia, for targeted drug delivery.
2. **Ultrasound Setup**: Using a 500-kHz single-element focused ultrasound transducer attached to a stereotaxic manipulator to precisely target the selected brain region.
3. **Micro-bubble Injection**: Systemically injecting monodispersed micro-bubbles into the bloodstream.
4. **Ultrasound Application**: Applying brief FUS pulses to the targeted brain region to cause reversible increases in the permeability of the BBB.
5. **Real-Time Monitoring**: Using a passive cavitation detector (PCD) to monitor the cavitation behavior of the micro-bubbles in real-time, ensuring the safety and efficacy of the procedure.
6. **Verification**: Using contrast-enhanced MRI to verify the success of the BBB opening and the targeting accuracy.

The invention also includes a method for real-time monitoring of the BBB opening using PCD, which helps in determining the success and safety of the sonication. The method is designed to be portable and independent of MRI guidance, making it suitable for use in less specialized clinical settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1

#### Target Selection and Ultrasound Setup

The method begins with the selection of a specific brain region, such as the caudate nucleus or the putamen, for targeted drug delivery. The target is selected based on the clinical or research objectives. The 500-kHz single-element focused ultrasound transducer is attached to a stereotaxic manipulator, which allows for precise targeting of the ultrasound focus in stereotaxic coordinates. The stereotaxic manipulator is calibrated to ensure accurate positioning of the ultrasound focus.

#### Micro-bubble Injection

Monodispersed micro-bubbles, typically 4-5 µm in diameter, are manufactured in-house and size-isolated using differential centrifugation. These micro-bubbles are systemically injected into the bloodstream of the subject. The micro-bubbles serve as a contrast agent and are rapidly evacuated from the system through the lungs and kidneys, with a half-life time of approximately 5-10 minutes in humans.

#### Ultrasound Application

Brief FUS pulses are applied to the targeted brain region using the 500-kHz transducer. The estimated focal maximum pressures range between 0.20 and 0.30 MPa, and the total duration of the sonication is 2 minutes. The FUS pulses cause reversible increases in the permeability of the BBB, allowing therapeutic agents to pass through the barrier and reach the targeted brain region.

#### Real-Time Monitoring

A passive cavitation detector (PCD) is used to monitor the cavitation behavior of the micro-bubbles in real-time. The PCD detects the acoustic emissions from the micro-bubbles, specifically the harmonic energy increase (HEI) and the broadband energy increase (BEI). The HEI is indicative of stable cavitation, which is safe and necessary for BBB opening, while the BEI is indicative of potentially harmful inertial cavitation. The PCD ensures that the procedure is safe and successful by monitoring the cavitation behavior and providing real-time feedback.

#### Verification

Contrast-enhanced MRI is used to verify the success of the BBB opening and the targeting accuracy. T1-weighted MR images are acquired before and after the injection of a gadodiamide contrast agent. The post-contrast images highlight regions of increased BBB permeability, indicating the success of the FUS treatment. The targeting accuracy is quantified by comparing the observed position of the BBB opening to the intended target position.

### Example 2
