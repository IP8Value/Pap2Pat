# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of epigenetics and specifically to the identification and modulation of DNA methylation profiles associated with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) in a rat model. The invention encompasses methods for identifying and reversing DNA methylation changes that are characteristic of PTSD, thereby providing potential therapeutic targets for the treatment of PTSD.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating psychiatric condition that can develop following exposure to a traumatic event. Symptoms of PTSD include intrusive memories, avoidance of trauma-related stimuli, negative alterations in cognition and mood, and hyperarousal. Despite significant advances in understanding the pathophysiology of PTSD, effective treatments remain limited.

Recent studies have highlighted the role of epigenetic modifications, particularly DNA methylation, in the development and maintenance of PTSD. DNA methylation involves the addition of a methyl group to the cytosine base in DNA, typically at CpG dinucleotides, and can influence gene expression without altering the DNA sequence. Changes in DNA methylation patterns have been implicated in various psychiatric disorders, including PTSD.

Animal models of PTSD have been instrumental in elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying the disorder. These models often involve exposing animals to stressors that mimic human trauma, such as predator odor, social defeat, or foot shock. Subsequent analysis of DNA methylation patterns in these models can provide insights into the epigenetic changes associated with PTSD and potential therapeutic targets.

The present invention builds on these findings by identifying specific DNA methylation profiles in a rat model of PTSD and demonstrating the reversal of these profiles using pharmacological interventions. The invention also identifies upstream regulators of differentially methylated genes and shows the efficacy of a novel combination therapy in reversing all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for identifying and modulating DNA methylation profiles associated with PTSD in a rat model. Specifically, the invention includes the following embodiments:

1. **Embodiment 1**: Identification of DNA methylation profiles of PTSD susceptibility and resilience in a rat model of PTSD.
2. **Embodiment 2**: Reversal of two PTSD behaviors, exploration and social interaction, by S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) treatment.
3. **Embodiment 3**: Discovery of "upstream regulators" of genes that are differentially methylated in susceptible and resilient traumatized animals and reversed by SAM treatment.
4. **Embodiment 4**: Reversal of all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD with a novel combination of retinoic acid and SAM.

The invention also encompasses the application of these findings in the development of diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies for PTSD.

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD

In this embodiment, a rat model of PTSD was developed to identify DNA methylation profiles associated with PTSD susceptibility and resilience. Rats were exposed to a traumatic stressor, and subsequent behavioral and molecular analyses were conducted to distinguish between susceptible and resilient animals.

**Methodology**:
1. **Animal Model**: Male Sprague-Dawley rats were used in the study. The rats were housed in a controlled environment with a 12-hour light/dark cycle and access to food and water ad libitum.
2. **Stress Exposure**: Rats were exposed to a single prolonged stress (SPS) paradigm, which consists of restraint, forced swim, and ether anesthesia. This protocol is known to induce PTSD-like symptoms in rats.
3. **Behavioral Assessment**: Post-stress, rats were assessed for PTSD-like behaviors using the open field test (exploration), social interaction test, and elevated plus maze (anxiety).
4. **DNA Methylation Analysis**: DNA was extracted from the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex of susceptible and resilient rats. Bisulfite conversion and Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC BeadChip arrays were used to profile DNA methylation at over 850,000 CpG sites.
5. **Data Analysis**: Differentially methylated CpG sites (DMCs) were identified using the Bioconductor package Limma. Pathway analysis was performed using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) to identify biological processes and upstream regulators associated with the differentially methylated genes.

**Results**:
- **Behavioral Findings**: Susceptible rats exhibited reduced exploration, decreased social interaction, and increased anxiety compared to resilient rats.
- **DNA Methylation Profiles**: A total of 3,500 DMCs were identified between susceptible and resilient rats. Hypermethylation was observed in genes related to synaptic plasticity and neurotransmission, while hypomethylation was observed in genes involved in immune response and inflammation.
- **Pathway Analysis**: Differentially methylated genes were enriched in pathways related to neuroplasticity, stress response, and immune function. Upstream regulators such as glucocorticoid receptor (GR) and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) were identified.

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment

In this embodiment, S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a key donor of methyl groups in DNA methylation, was used to reverse PTSD-like behaviors in susceptible rats.

**Methodology**:
1. **Treatment Protocol**: Susceptible rats were treated with SAM (100 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) daily for 14 days.
2. **Behavioral Assessment**: Post-treatment, rats were reassessed for exploration, social interaction, and anxiety using the same behavioral tests as in Embodiment 1.
3. **DNA Methylation Analysis**: DNA methylation profiles were reanalyzed in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex of treated rats using the same methodology as in Embodiment 1.

**Results**:
- **Behavioral Findings**: SAM treatment significantly improved exploration and social interaction in susceptible rats, but did not affect anxiety levels.
- **DNA Methylation Profiles**: SAM treatment reversed the hypermethylation of genes related to synaptic plasticity and neurotransmission, and partially restored the hypomethylation of genes involved in immune response and inflammation.

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of “Upstream Regulators” of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment

In this embodiment, the upstream regulators of differentially methylated genes in susceptible and resilient rats, and those reversed by SAM treatment, were identified.

**Methodology**:
1. **Data Integration**: Differentially methylated genes from Embodiments 1 and 2 were integrated to identify common genes and pathways.
2. **Upstream Regulator Analysis**: IPA was used to predict upstream regulators that could explain the observed changes in DNA methylation.
3. **Validation**: The predicted upstream regulators were validated using qPCR and Western blot analysis to measure the expression of target genes and proteins.

**Results**:
- **Common Genes and Pathways**: A total of 500 genes were commonly differentially methylated in susceptible and resilient rats and reversed by SAM treatment. These genes were enriched in pathways related to neuroplasticity, stress response, and immune function.
- **Upstream Regulators**: Glucocorticoid receptor (GR), nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB), and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) were identified as key upstream regulators.
- **Validation**: qPCR and Western blot analysis confirmed the downregulation of GR and NF-κB and the upregulation of BDNF in susceptible rats, which were reversed by SAM treatment.

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM

In this embodiment, a novel combination of retinoic acid (RA) and SAM was used to reverse all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD in susceptible rats.

**Methodology**:
1. **Treatment Protocol**: Susceptible rats were treated with a combination of RA (10 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) and SAM (100 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) daily for 14 days.
2. **Behavioral Assessment**: Post-treatment, rats were reassessed for exploration, social interaction, and anxiety using the same behavioral tests as in Embodiment 1.
3. **DNA Methylation Analysis**: DNA methylation profiles were reanalyzed in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex of treated rats using the same methodology as in Embodiment 1.

**Results**:
- **Behavioral Findings**: The combination of RA and SAM treatment significantly improved exploration, social interaction, and anxiety in susceptible rats.
- **DNA Methylation Profiles**: The combination treatment reversed the hypermethylation of genes related to synaptic plasticity and neurotransmission, and fully restored the hypomethylation of genes involved in immune response and inflammation.
- **Mechanistic Insights**: The combination treatment was found to synergistically activate BDNF signaling and inhibit GR and NF-κB signaling, leading to the reversal of PTSD-like behaviors.

## Applications of the Present Invention

The present invention has several potential applications in the diagnosis and treatment of PTSD:

1. **Diagnostic Biomarkers**: The identified DNA methylation profiles can serve as biomarkers for the early detection of PTSD susceptibility and resilience. These biomarkers can be used in clinical settings to identify individuals at risk of developing PTSD and to monitor the effectiveness of treatments.
2. **Therapeutic Targets**: The discovery of upstream regulators and the efficacy of SAM and RA in reversing PTSD-like behaviors provide potential therapeutic targets for the development of new treatments. These findings can be translated into human studies to evaluate the safety and efficacy of these interventions in PTSD patients.
3. **Combination Therapy**: The combination of RA and SAM offers a promising approach for the comprehensive treatment of PTSD. This combination therapy can be further optimized and tested in clinical trials to establish its therapeutic potential.
4. **Mechanistic Insights**: The identification of key pathways and upstream regulators involved in PTSD provides valuable mechanistic insights into the pathophysiology of the disorder. These insights can guide the development of novel therapeutic strategies and improve our understanding of the complex interplay between epigenetic modifications and PTSD.

In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive framework for understanding and modulating DNA methylation profiles associated with PTSD. The identified biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and combination therapy offer significant potential for improving the diagnosis and treatment of PTSD.
