# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel promoter and enhancer for enhancing the expression of genes in cells, particularly in T-cell lines. More specifically, the invention provides a promoter region from the Human Herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) major immediate-early (MIE) gene, which exhibits significantly stronger activity than the commonly used cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, especially in T-cell lines. The invention also encompasses gene constructs utilizing this promoter, methods for enhancing gene expression, and uses thereof in various biotechnological and medical applications.

## BACKGROUND ART

Human Herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) is a member of the beta-herpesvirus subfamily, which includes human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) and human herpesvirus 7 (HHV-7). HHV-6 was first isolated in 1986 from the peripheral blood of patients with lymphoproliferative disorders and AIDS. Subsequently, it was found to be ubiquitous in healthy adults and is known to cause exanthema subitum in infants. HHV-6 has two variants, A and B (HHV-6A and HHV-6B), with HHV-6B being the causative agent of exanthema subitum. The HHV-6 genome is a double-stranded DNA of approximately 160 kbp, consisting of a unique long region of 140 kbp flanked by 10-kbp direct repeats, and there is 90% identity between the two variants.

The major immediate-early (MIE) promoter of HCMV has been extensively studied and is widely used in molecular biology for driving the expression of various genes. The MIE promoter of HCMV controls the expression of two immediate-early transcripts, designated IE1 (UL123) and IE2 (UL122). HHV-6 has positional homologs of UL123 and UL122, which are U89 and U86, designated as IE1 and IE2, respectively. The HHV-6 IE1 and IE2 transcripts are formed by alternative splicing. Recently, it was reported that the R3 region in the right end of HHV-6 is a strong enhancer of another HHV-6 immediate-early gene, U95. This region is predicted to also contain promoter activity for the IE1 and IE2 genes, making it a positional homolog of the HCMV MIE promoter.

Despite the widespread use of the HCMV MIE promoter, there is a need for promoters with enhanced activity, particularly in specific cell types such as T cells. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel promoter from the HHV-6 MIE gene that exhibits significantly stronger activity than the HCMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the need for a promoter with enhanced activity in T-cell lines. While the HCMV MIE promoter is widely used, its activity is limited in certain cell types, particularly T cells. This limitation restricts its utility in applications requiring high levels of gene expression in T cells, such as gene therapy, vaccine development, and biotechnological processes.

### Solutions to the Problems

The present invention provides a solution to the aforementioned problem by identifying and characterizing a novel promoter from the HHV-6 MIE gene. This promoter, referred to as the HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp), exhibits significantly stronger activity than the HCMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines. Additionally, the inclusion of the first intron of the IE1 gene further enhances the promoter activity, making it a highly effective tool for driving gene expression in T cells.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The advantageous effects of the present invention include:
1. **Enhanced Gene Expression**: The HHV-6 MIE promoter provides significantly higher levels of gene expression in T-cell lines compared to the HCMV MIE promoter.
2. **Versatility**: The promoter can be used in various biotechnological and medical applications, including gene therapy, vaccine development, and protein production.
3. **Stability**: The inclusion of the first intron of the IE1 gene enhances the stability and activity of the promoter, making it suitable for long-term expression studies.
4. **Specificity**: The promoter shows enhanced activity in T-cell lines, making it particularly useful for applications targeting these cells.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### DEFINITION

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:
- **HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp)**: The promoter region of the HHV-6 major immediate-early gene, which includes the R3 region and the first intron of the IE1 gene.
- **T-cell lines**: Cell lines derived from T lymphocytes, including but not limited to Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat cells.
- **Enhancer**: A DNA sequence that increases the rate of transcription of a gene when it is located near the gene's promoter.
- **Intron**: A non-coding sequence of DNA within a gene that is removed during RNA processing to form mature messenger RNA (mRNA).
- **Gene construct**: A DNA molecule that has been artificially assembled and is designed to express a specific gene in a host cell.

## DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### (Enhancer for Promoter)

The enhancer for the HHV-6 MIE promoter is the R3 region, which is located between the U95 and U89 genes. This region contains critical regulatory elements, including an NF-κB-binding site and an AP-1-binding site, which play a significant role in enhancing promoter activity. The deletion of the NF-κB-binding site results in a dramatic decrease in promoter activity, underscoring its importance.

### (Promoter)

The HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp) is a 971-bp region located upstream of the IE1 gene. This promoter region includes the R3 region and the first intron of the IE1 gene. The inclusion of the first intron significantly enhances the promoter activity, making it particularly effective in T-cell lines. The promoter activity of HHV-6 MIEp is stronger than that of the HCMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines, but not in other adherent cell lines.

### (Gene Construct)

The gene construct of the present invention comprises the HHV-6 MIE promoter operably linked to a gene of interest. The gene of interest can be any gene that requires high levels of expression in T-cell lines, such as therapeutic genes, reporter genes, or genes involved in vaccine development. The construct can be introduced into cells using standard transfection methods, and the expression of the gene of interest can be monitored using appropriate reporter assays.

### (Method for Enhancing Expression of Gene in Cell)

The method for enhancing the expression of a gene in a cell involves the following steps:
1. **Construct Preparation**: Prepare a gene construct comprising the HHV-6 MIE promoter operably linked to the gene of interest.
2. **Transfection**: Transfect the gene construct into the target cells, such as T-cell lines.
3. **Expression Analysis**: Analyze the expression of the gene of interest using appropriate methods, such as luciferase assays, Western blotting, or fluorescence microscopy.

### (Use)

The HHV-6 MIE promoter and the gene constructs thereof have a wide range of uses, including:
1. **Gene Therapy**: The promoter can be used to drive the expression of therapeutic genes in T-cell lines, making it suitable for gene therapy applications.
2. **Vaccine Development**: The promoter can be used to express antigens in recombinant viruses, such as varicella-zoster virus (VZV), for vaccine development.
3. **Protein Production**: The promoter can be used to drive the expression of proteins of interest in T-cell lines for biotechnological applications.
4. **Research Tools**: The promoter can be used as a research tool to study gene regulation and expression in T-cell lines.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Objective**: To compare the activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter with the HCMV MIE promoter in various cell lines.

**Methods**: 
- **Plasmid Construction**: The HHV-6 MIE promoter was cloned into the pGL3-basic vector (Promega) to create a luciferase reporter construct. The HCMV MIE promoter was similarly cloned into the same vector.
- **Transfection**: The reporter constructs were transfected into MRC-5, MeWo, U373, Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen).
- **Luciferase Assay**: Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega).

**Results**: The HHV-6 MIE promoter exhibited stronger activity than the HCMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines (Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat cells), but not in other adherent cell lines (MRC-5, MeWo, and U373 cells).

### Example 2

**Objective**: To determine the role of the first intron of the IE1 gene in enhancing the activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter.

**Methods**: 
- **Plasmid Construction**: Various deletion mutants of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, including those with and without the first intron, were constructed and cloned into the pGL3-basic vector.
- **Transfection**: The reporter constructs were transfected into various cell lines.
- **Luciferase Assay**: Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured.

**Results**: The inclusion of the first intron significantly enhanced the promoter activity in all cell lines, particularly in T-cell lines. The promoter construct containing the first intron (HHV-6 MIEp-in1) had the strongest activity of all the promoter variants.

### Example 3

**Objective**: To identify the region within the HHV-6 MIE promoter that contributes to its activity.

**Methods**: 
- **Plasmid Construction**: Various deletion mutants of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, including those with and without the first intron, were constructed and cloned into the pGL3-basic vector.
- **Transfection**: The reporter constructs were transfected into various cell lines.
- **Luciferase Assay**: Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured.

**Results**: The region at nucleotide positions -381 to -552, which lies within the R3 region, was found to be critical for the promoter activity. The deletion of this region resulted in a significant decrease in promoter activity.

### Example 4

**Objective**: To demonstrate the ability of the HHV-6 MIE promoter to drive the expression of a heterologous gene in a recombinant varicella-zoster virus.

**Methods**: 
- **Plasmid Construction**: The HHV-6 MIE promoter, including the first intron, was cloned into the vOka BAC genome along with the DsRed2 gene and the BGH poly(A) signal sequence.
- **Reconstitution of Infectious Virus**: MRC-5 cells were transfected with the recombinant BAC DNA to reconstitute the infectious virus.
- **Expression Analysis**: The expression of DsRed2 was confirmed by fluorescence microscopy and Western blotting.

**Results**: The HHV-6 MIE promoter successfully drove the expression of the DsRed2 gene in the recombinant varicella-zoster virus, confirming its utility for expressing heterologous genes in viral vectors.
