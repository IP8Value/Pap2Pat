# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to microfluidic cell culture technology, particularly to organs-on-chips devices, and more specifically to a novel pumpless system for maintaining dynamic culture conditions within such devices. The invention addresses the limitations of existing organs-on-chips technologies, which often require external pumps and tubing, and provides a simple, robust, and cost-effective solution for achieving continuous flow of cell culture medium.

### Description of Related Art

Microfluidic cell culture technology, commonly referred to as organs-on-chips, has gained significant attention due to its potential to create in vitro models that better mimic the structural and functional aspects of human tissues. These models are crucial for advancing our understanding of biological processes and developing novel therapeutic approaches. A key feature of organs-on-chips is the application of dynamic culture environments, which influence the phenotype and behavior of living cells, including their proliferation, differentiation, and migration.

Most existing organs-on-chips technologies rely on external pumps and tubing to perfuse cell culture medium and buffer within the microfluidic channels. However, this configuration poses several challenges. It is prone to the formation of bubbles and leaks, leading to uncontrolled culture conditions and compromising experimental outcomes. Additionally, the requirement for high-precision pumps increases the initial capital cost and limits the accessibility of these technologies to laboratories with extensive microfluidic expertise. Furthermore, the use of external pumps and tubing introduces biosafety concerns, especially when handling biologically hazardous agents such as pathogens or viruses.

To overcome these limitations, alternative methods have been proposed to eliminate the need for external pumps and tubing. These methods include the application of hydrostatic pressure and resistance circuits, gravity-induced bidirectional flow (rocking platforms), and modifications of gravity-driven siphon flow using intravenous infusion sets. Despite their merits, these methods have significant drawbacks. Devices based on in-built hydrostatic pressure lack flexibility in modulating flow conditions and do not allow for rapid medium exchange. Rocking platforms create inhomogeneous flow conditions and remain expensive to implement. Simpler perfusion systems are needed to encourage and facilitate the use of organs-on-chips by non-microfluidic users.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and robust pumpless system for maintaining dynamic culture conditions within microfluidic organs-on-chips devices. The system utilizes a hydrophilic thread inserted into the outlet of the microfluidic circuit. The constant evaporation of the culture medium wicked by the thread in a controlled environment, such as a cell culture incubator, provides a reliable driving force to achieve continuous flow over extended periods without the need for external pumps, power sources, or tubing.

The invention offers several advantages over existing technologies. It is highly flexible and allows for easy adjustment of flow rates by varying the length and diameter of the thread. This flexibility is crucial for optimizing the shear stress experienced by cellular monolayers, which is essential for maintaining physiological relevance. The system is also straightforward to implement, making it accessible to laboratories with limited microfluidic expertise. Additionally, the absence of tubing reduces the risk of contamination and simplifies the setup, enhancing the overall reliability and reproducibility of experiments.

The invention has been successfully demonstrated using two commonly used cell lines in organs-on-chips applications: Caco-2 and A549 cells. Caco-2 cells, which are a widely used in vitro model of the intestinal barrier, were cultured in a pumpless device and displayed characteristics similar to those cultured using external pumps, indicating efficient differentiation. A549 cells, which are adenocarcinomic human alveolar basal epithelial cells, were used to investigate the effect of fluid shear stress on epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). The results showed that A549 cells adopt mesenchymal phenotypes when exposed to shear stress above a certain threshold, further validating the effectiveness of the pumpless system.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Examples

#### Example 1: Characterization of Thread-Driven Flows within Microfluidic Devices

Non-woven Spunlace threads, commonly used in medical products such as wound dressings, were selected for this application due to their availability in different diameters and their suitability as microfluidic capillary channels. The Spunlace thread structure was characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), revealing a network of twisted fibers with smooth surfaces and diameters of approximately 15 µm. The capillary rise in Spunlace threads was measured by assessing the wicking height of a dye solution at different time points for threads of four different diameters. These measurements confirmed the feasibility of tuning the wicking rates by adjusting the dimensions of the threads.

To demonstrate the feasibility of using the constant evaporation of the medium wicked by a thread to drive flow within a microfluidic device, the flow rate in a constant-width device (1 mm wide and 150 µm high) was measured by tracking the displacement of highly fluorescent polystyrene beads. The results showed that the constant evaporation of a thread wicking the outlet of a microfluidic channel generates a constant velocity over up to 24 hours, thereby ensuring continuous flow of the culture medium. By varying the length and diameter of the thread, the flow rate could be effectively controlled, ranging from 0.15 µL/min to 6 µL/min. Based on these measurements, the most suitable thread dimensions were identified for specific organ-on-chip devices and cell types.

#### Example 2: Pumpless Culture of Caco-2 Cell Monolayers

Caco-2 cells, a widely used in vitro model of the intestinal barrier, were cultured in a pumpless device to evaluate the effectiveness of the system. The cells were seeded in a constant-width microfluidic channel and cultured for 5 days under a shear stress of 0.02 dyn/cm². The resulting monolayers displayed a dense F-actin network, high levels of tight junctions (ZO-1 protein expression), and high numbers of active mitochondria. These characteristics were comparable to those observed in Caco-2 monolayers cultured using external pumps, demonstrating that the pumpless system supports efficient differentiation and maintains the physiological relevance of the model.

#### Example 3: Pumpless Culture of A549 Cell Monolayers

A549 cells, which are adenocarcinomic human alveolar basal epithelial cells, were cultured in a pumpless device to investigate the effect of fluid shear stress on epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). The A549 VIM RFP cell line, where vimentin is constitutively tagged with a red fluorescent protein, was used to monitor vimentin expression. The cells were exposed to different shear stress levels (ranging from 0.135 to 0.008 dyn/cm²) within a Hele-Shaw device. The results showed that vimentin expression decreased significantly from the sections with higher shear stress to those with lower shear stress, indicating that EMT is triggered when cells are exposed to shear stress above approximately 0.03 dyn/cm². The morphology of the A549 VIM RFP cells also changed noticeably, with cells adopting a spindle-like shape in higher shear stress sections and forming less dense monolayers. These findings confirm the utility of the pumpless system for studying the phenotypic changes in response to fluid shear stress.

#### Example 4: Pumpless Cell Culture Device for Multiplexed Studies

To further demonstrate the versatility of the pumpless system, a microfluidic device was designed to allow the simultaneous application of different flow rates and shear stresses within a single chip. The device featured five parallel channels, each perfused with the same cell culture medium but under different flow rates driven by threads of varying lengths. The flow rates in each channel were experimentally verified by measuring the velocity of nanoparticles. This design provides a convenient methodology for testing the influence of shear stress on cellular responses to soluble factors, facilitating multiplexed studies in a single device.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel and robust pumpless system for maintaining dynamic culture conditions within microfluidic organs-on-chips devices. The system is simple, cost-effective, and highly flexible, making it accessible to a broader range of laboratories. The successful demonstration of the system using Caco-2 and A549 cells highlights its potential for advancing in vitro studies and developing more physiologically relevant models of human tissues.
