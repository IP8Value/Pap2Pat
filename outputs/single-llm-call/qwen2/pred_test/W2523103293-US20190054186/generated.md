## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy, specifically to methods and compositions for selectively targeting and disrupting cancer cells using super low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields and polymer-coated superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs). The invention provides a novel approach to cancer treatment that leverages the unique mechanical properties of cancer cells to achieve selective cytotoxicity without affecting healthy cells.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, and the development of effective and selective therapeutic strategies continues to be a significant challenge. Traditional cancer treatments, such as chemotherapy and radiation therapy, often lack specificity and can cause severe side effects due to their impact on healthy tissues. Therefore, there is a critical need for innovative approaches that can selectively target and eliminate cancer cells while minimizing harm to normal cells.

Superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs) have gained considerable attention in recent years for their potential applications in cancer therapy. These nanoparticles can be remotely actuated by external magnetic fields, enabling precise control over their behavior within cells. However, the mechanisms by which SMNPs can selectively target and destroy cancer cells while sparing healthy cells have not been fully elucidated.

Recent studies have shown that the mechanical properties of cancer cells differ significantly from those of healthy cells. Cancer cells are generally softer and more deformable due to their altered cytoskeletal architecture, which is essential for their ability to migrate and invade surrounding tissues. This mechanical difference provides a unique opportunity to develop therapies that exploit the mechanical vulnerabilities of cancer cells.

The present invention builds on this knowledge by providing a method and composition for selectively targeting and disrupting cancer cells using polymer-coated SMNPs and super low frequency AC magnetic fields. The invention leverages the mechanical properties of cancer cells to achieve selective cytotoxicity, offering a promising new approach to cancer therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and composition for selectively targeting and disrupting cancer cells using polymer-coated superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs) and super low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields. The invention is based on the discovery that the mechanical properties of cancer cells, particularly their softer and more deformable cytoskeleton, can be exploited to achieve selective cytotoxicity.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for selectively killing cancer cells comprising the steps of:
1. Providing a composition comprising polymer-coated SMNPs;
2. Administering the composition to a subject such that the polymer-coated SMNPs are internalized by the cancer cells;
3. Exposing the cancer cells to a super low frequency AC magnetic field, wherein the magnetic field causes the SMNPs to generate mechanical forces that disrupt the cytoskeleton of the cancer cells, leading to cell death.

In another aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising polymer-coated SMNPs, wherein the polymer is a pentablock copolymer comprising polyacrylic acid (PAA) and Pluronic P85 (P85). The composition is suitable for administration to a subject and is capable of being internalized by cancer cells.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for assessing the effectiveness of the treatment comprising:
1. Administering the composition to a subject;
2. Exposing the cancer cells to a super low frequency AC magnetic field;
3. Monitoring the viability of the cancer cells and healthy cells to determine the selective cytotoxicity of the treatment.

The invention also provides a kit for performing the method, comprising:
1. A composition comprising polymer-coated SMNPs;
2. Instructions for administering the composition and exposing the cancer cells to a super low frequency AC magnetic field.

The invention offers several advantages over existing cancer therapies, including:
- **Selective Cytotoxicity:** The method selectively targets and kills cancer cells while sparing healthy cells, reducing the risk of side effects.
- **Mechanical Disruption:** The method exploits the mechanical properties of cancer cells, providing a novel mechanism of action.
- **Remote Actuation:** The use of super low frequency AC magnetic fields allows for remote and non-invasive actuation of the SMNPs, enhancing the precision and control of the treatment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Experimental Methods

The present invention is based on the discovery that polymer-coated superparamagnetic nanoparticles (SMNPs) can be used to selectively target and disrupt cancer cells when exposed to super low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields. The invention leverages the unique mechanical properties of cancer cells, particularly their softer and more deformable cytoskeleton, to achieve selective cytotoxicity.

#### Synthesis and Characterization of Polymer-SMNP Complexes

The SMNP complexes were prepared and coated by ligand exchange with polyanion-PEG or polyanion-P85 block copolymers. The effective hydrodynamic diameter, polydispersity, and ζ-potential of the polymer-SMNP complexes were determined by dynamic light scattering (DLS) using a Zetasizer Nano ZS. The polymer content in the polymer-SMNP complexes was determined by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), and the iron content was analyzed by Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS).

#### Labeling of PAA-P85-SMNP with Alexa Fluor®647

PAA-P85 coated SMNP complexes were labeled with the fluorescent dye Alexa-Fluor®647 hydrazine using standard EDC chemistry. The labeled complexes were purified on a size exclusion column with PBS as the eluent and analyzed by ICP-MS to determine the concentration of SMNPs in solution.

#### Cytotoxicity of Polymer-SMNP Complexes

In vitro cytotoxicity of polymer-SMNP complexes was assessed in MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells using a standard MTT assay. Cells were treated with polymer-SMNP complexes at various doses for 24 hours, washed with acidic saline to remove non-internalized complexes, and maintained in complete DMEM for an additional 24 hours. Cell viability was measured by adding MTT dye to each well and measuring the absorbance at 570 nm.

#### Quantitative Uptake of Polymer-SMNP Complexes In Vitro

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with polymer-SMNP complexes at various doses for 1 hour or 24 hours. Cells were rinsed with acidic saline, harvested, and digested using nitric acid. The iron content was analyzed by ICP-MS to determine the uptake of SMNPs.

#### Intracellular Distribution of PAA-P85-SMNPs

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with Alexa Fluor®647-PAA-P85-SMNPs for 24 hours. Cells were stained with Lysotracker™ Green and Hoechst 33342 nuclear stain and imaged using a Zeiss CLSM 710 Spectral Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope. Colocalization of the SMNPs with lysosomes was determined using ImageJ/Fiji.

For transmission electron microscopy (TEM), cell monolayers were treated with polymer-SMNPs for 24 hours, fixed, and embedded in Polybed 812 epoxy resin. Ultrathin sections were collected on copper grids and stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Samples were viewed with a LEO EM910 transmission electron microscope.

#### Effect of Exposure to AC Magnetic Fields on Cell Viability

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNP complexes at various concentrations for 24 hours and exposed to AC magnetic fields of 50 or 100 kA/m and 50 Hz. Cell viability was assessed using a standard MTT assay.

#### Intracellular Distribution of PAA-P85-SMNPs After Exposure to an AC Field

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with Alexa Fluor®647-PAA-P85-SMNPs, exposed to an AC magnetic field (50 Hz, 50 kA/m) using a pulsed exposure regime, and imaged using a Zeiss CLSM 710 Spectral Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope.

#### Assessment of Lysosomal Membrane Permeabilization

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNPs, exposed to an AC magnetic field, and stained with acridine orange. Live cell images were acquired using a Zeiss CLSM 710 Spectral Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope.

#### Effect of Cytoskeleton Modulation on the Response to an AC Magnetic Field

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNPs, exposed to a sub-lethal dosage of cytochalasin D (CD), and exposed to an AC magnetic field. Cell viability was assessed using a standard MTT assay. Confocal microscopy was used to visualize the cytoskeletal damage.

### Example 2

#### Synthesis of PAA-P85-SMNP Complexes

The PAA-P85-SMNP complexes were synthesized by ligand exchange. Briefly, 4.5 mg of PAA-P85-SMNPs were diluted with 0.35 mL of deionized water and sonicated for 30 minutes. EDC (1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide hydrochloride) and N-hydroxysulfosuccinimide (S-NHS) were added to the reaction vial, followed by Alexa Fluor®647 hydrazine. The vial was protected from light and incubated overnight on a shaker at approximately 100 rpm. The labeled complexes were purified on a size exclusion column with PBS as the eluent and analyzed by ICP-MS to determine the concentration of SMNPs in solution.

### Example 3

#### Cytotoxicity of PAA-P85-SMNP Complexes

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were seeded in 96-well plates and allowed to adhere for two days. Cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNP complexes at various concentrations (0.005–0.5 mg/mL) for 24 hours, washed with acidic saline, and maintained in complete DMEM for an additional 24 hours. A standard MTT assay was performed to assess cell viability. The results showed that the PAA-P85-SMNP complexes were minimally toxic to all cell lines at all tested concentrations.

### Example 4

#### Quantitative Uptake of PAA-P85-SMNP Complexes

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNP complexes at various concentrations for 1 hour or 24 hours. Cells were rinsed with acidic saline, harvested, and digested using nitric acid. The iron content was analyzed by ICP-MS to determine the uptake of SMNPs. The results showed that the uptake of PAA-P85-SMNP complexes was time and concentration-dependent in all cell lines, with the highest uptake observed in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells.

### Example 5

#### Intracellular Distribution of PAA-P85-SMNPs

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with Alexa Fluor®647-PAA-P85-SMNPs for 24 hours. Cells were stained with Lysotracker™ Green and Hoechst 33342 nuclear stain and imaged using a Zeiss CLSM 710 Spectral Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope. Colocalization of the SMNPs with lysosomes was determined using ImageJ/Fiji. The results showed that at low concentrations (0.05 mg/mL), the PAA-P85-SMNP complexes were primarily localized in lysosomes, while at high concentrations (0.5 mg/mL), they were distributed throughout the cytoplasm.

### Example 6

#### Effect of Exposure to AC Magnetic Fields on Cell Viability

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNP complexes at various concentrations for 24 hours and exposed to AC magnetic fields of 50 or 100 kA/m and 50 Hz using continuous or pulsed exposure regimes. Cell viability was assessed using a standard MTT assay. The results showed a significant reduction in cell viability in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells, while MCF10A cells remained unaffected. The pulsed exposure regime was more effective than the continuous regime in both cancerous cell lines.

### Example 7

#### Intracellular Distribution of PAA-P85-SMNPs After Exposure to an AC Field

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with Alexa Fluor®647-PAA-P85-SMNPs, exposed to an AC magnetic field (50 Hz, 50 kA/m) using a pulsed exposure regime, and imaged using a Zeiss CLSM 710 Spectral Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope. The results showed that the intracellular distribution of the PAA-P85-SMNPs remained primarily in lysosomes, with no evidence of lysosomal membrane permeabilization.

### Example 8

#### Effect of Cytoskeleton Modulation on the Response to an AC Magnetic Field

MDA-MB-231, BT474, and MCF10A cells were treated with PAA-P85-SMNP complexes, exposed to a sub-lethal dosage of cytochalasin D (CD), and exposed to an AC magnetic field. Cell viability was assessed using a standard MTT assay. Confocal microscopy was used to visualize the cytoskeletal damage. The results showed that the exposure to CD sensitized MCF10A cells to the AC magnetic field, leading to significant cytoskeletal damage and reduced cell viability. The cancerous cells were also sensitized to the continuous AC magnetic field regime, suggesting that the mechanical properties of the cytoskeleton play a crucial role in the selective cytotoxicity of the treatment.

The present invention provides a novel and effective method for selectively targeting and disrupting cancer cells using polymer-coated SMNPs and super low frequency AC magnetic fields. The invention leverages the unique mechanical properties of cancer cells to achieve selective cytotoxicity, offering a promising new approach to cancer therapy.
