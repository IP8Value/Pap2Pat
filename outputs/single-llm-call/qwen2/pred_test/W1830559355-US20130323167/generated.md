# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy and, more specifically, to methods and compositions for enhancing the delivery of therapeutic agents to tumors. The invention leverages the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect, which is a phenomenon where nanoparticles accumulate preferentially in tumors due to the abnormal vasculature and poor lymphatic drainage of tumor tissues. The invention further enhances this effect by inducing an inflammatory response in the tumor microenvironment, thereby increasing vascular permeability and improving the selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within the tumor.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, and the development of effective therapeutic strategies continues to be a major focus of medical research. Traditional chemotherapy and radiation therapy, while effective in some cases, often suffer from significant limitations, including systemic toxicity and the inability to deliver sufficient drug concentrations to the tumor site. The Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect has emerged as a promising approach to overcome these limitations. The EPR effect refers to the tendency of nanoparticles to accumulate in tumors due to the leaky vasculature and impaired lymphatic drainage characteristic of solid tumors. However, the magnitude of the EPR effect can vary widely among different tumors and individuals, limiting its therapeutic potential.

Recent studies have shown that the vascular permeability of tumors can be further enhanced by inducing an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment. This enhanced permeability, referred to as Enhanced EPR (E2PR), can significantly improve the selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within tumors. Various agents, including bacterial infections and pro-inflammatory cytokines, have been shown to induce E2PR, leading to improved therapeutic outcomes in preclinical models.

Despite these advances, there remains a need for methods and compositions that can reliably and effectively enhance the EPR effect in a wide range of tumor types and patient populations. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel approach to induce E2PR and thereby improve the delivery and efficacy of therapeutic agents in cancer treatment.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing the delivery of therapeutic agents to tumors by inducing an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment. The invention is based on the discovery that certain agents, such as bacterial infections and pro-inflammatory cytokines, can significantly enhance the EPR effect, leading to improved selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within tumors.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for treating a subject with a tumor, comprising the steps of:
1. Administering to the subject a vascular-active agent that induces an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment, thereby enhancing vascular permeability and retention (E2PR).
2. Administering to the subject a therapeutic agent formulated as a nanoparticle, wherein the therapeutic agent is selectively accumulated in the tumor due to the enhanced EPR effect.

In another aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising:
1. A vascular-active agent that induces an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment.
2. A therapeutic agent formulated as a nanoparticle, wherein the therapeutic agent is designed to exploit the enhanced EPR effect for selective accumulation in the tumor.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for enhancing the delivery of a therapeutic agent to a tumor, comprising the step of administering to a subject a combination of a vascular-active agent and a therapeutic agent formulated as a nanoparticle, wherein the vascular-active agent induces an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment, thereby enhancing the EPR effect.

The invention further provides methods for optimizing the timing and dosage of the vascular-active agent and the therapeutic agent to achieve maximal therapeutic benefit. The invention also includes methods for selecting the most effective vascular-active agent for a given tumor type based on imaging-based companion tests.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1

#### Induction of E2PR Using Bacterial Infection

In a series of experiments, the inventors demonstrated that bacterial infection can significantly enhance the EPR effect, leading to improved selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within tumors. Specifically, the inventors used C. novyi-NT, an attenuated anaerobic bacterial strain, to infect experimental tumors in mice. C. novyi-NT secretes an enzyme called liposomase at high levels in the infected tumors, which can be targeted by radiolabeled antibodies for therapeutic purposes.

Mice bearing subcutaneous CT26 tumors were intravenously injected with C. novyi-NT spores together with a radiolabeled anti-liposomase antibody or a similarly labeled IgG control antibody. The anti-liposomase antibody was highly enriched in the tumors infected with C. novyi-NT but not in uninfected tumors. Surprisingly, the radiolabeled IgG control antibody was also enriched in the C. novyi-NT infected tumors, albeit to a lesser extent. Biodistribution analyses showed that the level of radioactivity in the tumor was four-fold higher than that in most normal tissues.

To further confirm that the accumulation in the tumors was not antibody-specific, the inventors repeated the experiment with another antibody generated against human CD20, a B-cell antigen. Systemically administered anti-CD20 antibody was also enriched in the tumor if the animal was simultaneously injected with C. novyi-NT spores.

These results demonstrate that bacterial infection can induce an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment, leading to enhanced vascular permeability and selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within the tumor.

### Example 2

#### Induction of E2PR Using Pro-Inflammatory Cytokines

The inventors also explored the use of pro-inflammatory cytokines to induce E2PR. Among the cytokines considered, Tumor Necrosis Factor-α (TNF-α) was of particular interest due to its known role in endotoxin-induced vascular permeabilization. TNF-α has been shown to cause hemorrhagic necrosis in tumors, similar to the effects observed with C. novyi-NT infection.

Mice bearing subcutaneous CT26 tumors were injected with murine TNF-α and radiolabeled murine IgG. Significant IgG accumulation was observed in the tumors but not in the normal tissues. A time course study revealed that the IgG tumor accumulation progressed slowly and peaked between 72 and 96 hours after injection.

The inventors then evaluated whether stericallly stabilized liposomal nanoparticles (SSLs) of ~100 nm in diameter, which are susceptible to the EPR effect, were also susceptible to E2PR. Iodinated SSLs were intravenously injected into tumor-bearing mice in combination with either C. novyi-NT or TNF-α. Both treatments significantly augmented the selective retention of radioactivity within tumors, with the tumor-to-blood ratio of radioactivity reaching as high as 22-fold.

These results demonstrate that pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, can effectively induce E2PR, leading to improved selective accumulation of therapeutic agents within tumors.

### Example 3

#### Therapeutic Efficacy of E2PR in Experimental Tumors

The inventors next investigated whether the enhanced EPR effect (E2PR) could translate into therapeutic benefits. Mice bearing fully developed CT26 tumors were treated by simultaneous intravenous injections of TNF-α plus Doxil (10 mg/kg) or radiolabeled IgG. Treatment with Doxil or 131I-labeled IgG alone retarded tumor growth and prolonged animal survival, but the tumors always grew back. When combined with TNF-α, however, a single administration of these agents led to complete tumor regression in all animals and long-term cures in more than 75% of them.

The inventors also tested SSLs containing 131I, generated using the chemical trapping approach described above. While 131I-labeled SSLs alone retarded tumor growth, complete tumor regression and cures were only observed when they were used in combination with TNF-α.

These results demonstrate that the combination of a vascular-active agent and a therapeutic agent formulated as a nanoparticle can significantly enhance the therapeutic efficacy of cancer treatment.

### Example 4

#### Therapeutic Efficacy of E2PR in a Murine Model of Glioblastoma Multiforme (GBM)

The inventors evaluated the therapeutic potential of E2PR in a murine model of glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), a highly aggressive and recalcitrant form of brain cancer. When implanted orthotopically, the brain tumor cell line GL261 forms very aggressive tumors, killing animals within about a month. Following stereotactic injection of GL261 cells into the frontal lobe, brain tumors were allowed to grow to substantial size, then 125I-labeled SSLs with or without TNF-α were administered. Tumor accumulation of the radiolabeled SSLs was only observed in TNF-α treated animals.

Mice with similar tumors were injected with Doxil, either with or without TNF-α. The combination clearly had a therapeutic benefit, prolonging survival up to 103 days even in this highly challenging pre-clinical model. Both Doxil and TNF-α showed limited therapeutic benefit when used as single agents, with no animal surviving beyond 50 days following tumor implantation.

These results demonstrate that the E2PR approach can be effective in treating highly aggressive and difficult-to-treat cancers, such as glioblastoma multiforme, by enhancing the delivery and efficacy of therapeutic agents.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel and effective approach to enhance the delivery of therapeutic agents to tumors by inducing an inflammatory response within the tumor microenvironment. The invention leverages the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect and further enhances it through the use of vascular-active agents, such as bacterial infections and pro-inflammatory cytokines. The methods and compositions of the invention can be applied to a wide range of tumor types and patient populations, offering the potential for improved therapeutic outcomes in cancer treatment.
