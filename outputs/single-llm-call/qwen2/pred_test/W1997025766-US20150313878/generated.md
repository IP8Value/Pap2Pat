# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and, more particularly, to methods and compositions for inhibiting the proliferation and inducing the apoptosis of prostate carcinoma cells. Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in men, and despite significant advances in diagnosis and treatment, it remains a leading cause of cancer-related deaths. The identification of novel therapeutic targets and pathways is crucial for developing more effective treatments.

Soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is a unique enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), sAC is localized intracellularly and is not directly coupled to G protein-coupled receptors. Recent studies have implicated sAC in various physiological processes, including calcium homeostasis, sperm function, and sensory perception. However, the role of sAC in cancer, particularly prostate cancer, has not been fully elucidated.

This invention provides a method for inhibiting the proliferation and inducing apoptosis in prostate carcinoma cells by targeting sAC. The invention is based on the discovery that sAC is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma and that its inhibition leads to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. The methods and compositions described herein offer a novel approach to treating prostate cancer by modulating the sAC/cAMP/EPAC/Rap1 signaling pathway.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Soluble Adenylyl Cyclase (sAC)

Soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is a unique enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) to cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which are located at the plasma membrane and are coupled to G protein-coupled receptors, sAC is localized intracellularly and is not directly coupled to G protein-coupled receptors. sAC is activated by bicarbonate and is involved in various physiological processes, including calcium homeostasis, sperm function, and sensory perception.

### Overexpression of sAC in Prostate Carcinoma

The present invention is based on the discovery that sAC is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma compared to benign prostatic glands. Immunohistochemical analysis of prostate tissue samples revealed that sAC staining was significantly increased in prostate cancer glands compared to benign glands. Specifically, sAC expression was upregulated at the leading edge of the tumor near the prostatic capsule and in extraprostatic foci of the tumor. This finding suggests a potential role for sAC in the growth and progression of prostate cancer.

### Inhibition of sAC Suppresses Cell Growth and Induces Apoptosis

The inventors have demonstrated that the inhibition of sAC activity can suppress the proliferation and induce apoptosis in prostate carcinoma cells. Two human prostate carcinoma cell lines, PC3 and LNCaP, were used to evaluate the effects of sAC inhibition. Both cell lines showed marked expression of sAC, which was significantly higher compared to normal prostate epithelial cells (PNT2).

#### Pharmacological Inhibition of sAC

Treatment of PC3 and LNCaP cells with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 reduced the intracellular cAMP content and suppressed cell proliferation in a dose-dependent manner. The antiproliferative effect of sAC inhibition was accompanied by the release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and apoptosis, as measured by a rise in the sub-G1 population and cleavage of caspase-3. Treatment with the inactive analog of KH7, KH7.15, had no effect, suggesting that the observed effects are sAC-dependent.

#### Genetic Suppression of sAC

To further substantiate the role of sAC in cell growth and death, the expression of sAC was suppressed in LNCaP cells using two different methods: Lipofectamine-based transfection with sAC-specific siRNA and adenoviral transfection with sAC-specific shRNA. Both treatments equally reduced the expression of the 50-kDa sAC isoform by ≥80%. Similar to pharmacological inhibition of sAC with KH7, both methods of sAC knockdown significantly suppressed the proliferation of LNCaP cells, induced LDH release, and led to caspase-3 cleavage. Treatment with scrambled siRNA or shRNA had no effect on these parameters.

### Inhibition of sAC Induces Cell Cycle Arrest

To understand the mechanisms responsible for the antiproliferative effect of sAC inhibition, cell cycle analysis was performed. In LNCaP cells, pharmacological or genetic suppression of sAC activity significantly increased the percentage of cells in the G2 phase and subsequently reduced the percentage of cells in the G1 phase, suggesting the development of cell cycle arrest at the G2/M checkpoint. Further kinetic analysis of cell cycle arrest progression revealed that the percentage of cells in the G2 phase started to rise 12 hours after treatment with the sAC inhibitor and reached a maximal value at 24 hours. Cleavage of caspase-3 first appeared after 24 hours, indicating that apoptosis is a result, rather than a cause, of the cell cycle arrest.

### sAC Controls the Expression of G2/M Checkpoint Proteins

To investigate how sAC controls the G2/M transition, the expression of cyclin B1 and CDK1 was examined. Treatment with KH7 significantly reduced the expression of cyclin B1 and CDK1 (both the phosphorylated and dephosphorylated forms). Similar effects were observed upon sAC knockdown. In contrast, the expression of cyclins D1, D3, and E2, which are responsible for the G1/S transition, was not altered by sAC inhibition. These data support the specific role of sAC in controlling the G2/M transition.

### sAC Controls Proliferation in an EPAC-Dependent Manner

PKA and EPAC (exchange protein activated by cAMP) are two major downstream targets of cAMP. The inventors investigated whether these proteins are involved in sAC-dependent proliferation control. Treatment with the PKA-specific activator N6-benzoyl-cAMP had no effect on proliferation of control cells or cells treated with the sAC inhibitor KH7. Similarly, treatment with PKA inhibitors had no effect on proliferation, cytotoxicity, or cell cycle distribution. In contrast, treatment with the EPAC-selective activator 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed the effect of KH7 on Rap1-GTP expression and prevented the decrease in cell proliferation, LDH release, and cell cycle block induced by sAC inhibition. These data suggest that sAC controls the cell cycle and proliferation in an EPAC/Rap1-dependent and PKA-independent manner.

### Methods of Treatment

The methods of the invention involve administering to a subject an effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition of the invention to inhibit the proliferation and induce apoptosis in prostate carcinoma cells. The methods may be used alone or in combination with other cancer therapies, such as chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or hormone therapy.

### Example 1

**Pharmacological Inhibition of sAC in Prostate Carcinoma Cells**

Prostate carcinoma cells (PC3 and LNCaP) were treated with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 at a concentration of 20 μM. The cells were incubated for 48 hours, and the intracellular cAMP content was measured using a cAMP direct enzyme immunoassay kit. The results showed a significant reduction in intracellular cAMP content in both cell lines. Cell proliferation was assessed by counting the number of viable cells using a trypan blue exclusion assay. The results showed a dose-dependent reduction in cell proliferation, with a maximal effect at 20 μM KH7. Apoptosis was evaluated by measuring the release of LDH and the presence of cleaved caspase-3. The results showed a significant increase in LDH release and caspase-3 cleavage in cells treated with KH7, indicating the induction of apoptosis.

### Example 2

**Genetic Suppression of sAC in Prostate Carcinoma Cells**

Prostate carcinoma cells (LNCaP) were transfected with sAC-specific siRNA or sAC-specific shRNA using Lipofectamine or adenoviral vectors, respectively. The cells were incubated for 48 hours, and the expression of sAC was assessed by Western blotting. The results showed a significant reduction in sAC expression by ≥80% in both siRNA and shRNA-treated cells. Cell proliferation was assessed by counting the number of viable cells using a trypan blue exclusion assay. The results showed a significant reduction in cell proliferation in both siRNA and shRNA-treated cells. Apoptosis was evaluated by measuring the release of LDH and the presence of cleaved caspase-3. The results showed a significant increase in LDH release and caspase-3 cleavage in cells treated with sAC-specific siRNA or shRNA, indicating the induction of apoptosis.

### Example 3

**Cell Cycle Analysis Following sAC Inhibition**

Prostate carcinoma cells (LNCaP) were treated with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 at a concentration of 20 μM. The cells were incubated for 48 hours, and cell cycle analysis was performed using flow cytometry. The results showed a significant increase in the percentage of cells in the G2 phase and a corresponding decrease in the percentage of cells in the G1 phase, indicating the development of cell cycle arrest at the G2/M checkpoint. Kinetic analysis revealed that the percentage of cells in the G2 phase started to rise 12 hours after treatment and reached a maximal value at 24 hours. Cleavage of caspase-3 was first observed after 24 hours, indicating that apoptosis is a result, rather than a cause, of the cell cycle arrest.

### Example 4

**Role of EPAC in sAC-Dependent Proliferation Control**

Prostate carcinoma cells (LNCaP) were treated with the sAC-selective inhibitor KH7 at a concentration of 20 μM, either alone or in combination with the EPAC-selective activator 8-pCPT-cAMP at a concentration of 100 μM. The cells were incubated for 48 hours, and the expression of Rap1-GTP and the phosphorylated forms of B-Raf were assessed by Western blotting. The results showed that treatment with KH7 significantly reduced the expression of Rap1-GTP and the phosphorylated forms of B-Raf. Treatment with 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed the effect of KH7 on Rap1-GTP expression and prevented the decrease in cell proliferation, LDH release, and cell cycle block induced by sAC inhibition. These data suggest that sAC controls the cell cycle and proliferation in an EPAC/Rap1-dependent and PKA-independent manner.
