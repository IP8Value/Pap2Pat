# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for improving the motility of human sperm, particularly in cases of asthenozoospermia, using hydrogen gas (H2). The invention further encompasses the use of hydrogen gas in the treatment of frozen-thawed sperm to enhance its motility and viability, thereby providing a valuable tool in assisted reproductive technologies such as in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

## BACKGROUND ART

Infertility is a significant health issue affecting many couples worldwide. Male infertility, often characterized by sperm function defects such as asthenozoospermia, is a common cause of reproductive failure. Asthenozoospermia is defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a condition where the percentage of progressively motile sperm is less than 32%. Several factors contribute to asthenozoospermia, including gene defects, mitochondrial DNA haplogroups, systemic disorders, and oxidative stress. Oxidative stress, in particular, is a critical factor that impairs sperm motility by damaging cellular components, including the axoneme and intracellular ATP levels.

Previous studies have explored various methods to improve sperm motility, such as the use of antioxidants like zinc, aspartic acid, and coenzyme Q10. Myoinositol and xanthine derivatives have also shown promise in enhancing sperm motility. However, these treatments have limitations and may not be universally effective.

Hydrogen gas (H2) has emerged as a novel therapeutic agent due to its unique properties. H2 is the smallest molecule and can rapidly diffuse across biological membranes, including those of mitochondria and the nucleus. It selectively reduces cytotoxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as the hydroxyl radical (•OH) without affecting physiologically important ROS. Studies have shown that H2 can protect cells from oxidative damage and improve cellular functions, including ATP production.

Despite the potential benefits of H2, its application in improving sperm motility has not been extensively studied. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a method for treating human sperm with hydrogen gas to enhance motility and viability, particularly in cases of asthenozoospermia.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for improving the motility of human sperm, particularly in cases of asthenozoospermia, using hydrogen gas (H2). The invention aims to address the limitations of existing treatments and offer a novel, safe, and effective approach to enhance sperm motility and viability.

### Means to Solve the Object

The present invention provides a method for improving the motility of human sperm, comprising the step of treating the sperm with a gas mixture containing hydrogen gas (H2). The gas mixture may contain 50% H2, 20% O2, and 30% N2, or other suitable compositions that ensure the effective delivery of H2 to the sperm. The treatment can be applied to fresh or frozen-thawed sperm suspensions.

The method involves the following steps:
1. Preparing a sperm suspension from a human donor or patient.
2. Exposing the sperm suspension to a gas mixture containing hydrogen gas (H2) for a predetermined period, typically 30 minutes.
3. Assessing the motility of the treated sperm using standard techniques such as computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA).

For frozen-thawed sperm, the method further includes:
1. Thawing the frozen sperm suspension.
2. Washing the thawed sperm with a medium containing hydrogen gas (H2).
3. Assessing the motility of the treated sperm.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention demonstrates that treatment with hydrogen gas (H2) significantly improves the motility of human sperm, particularly in cases of asthenozoospermia. The treatment is effective in enhancing the forward motility rate of sperm, even in highly damaged suspensions. Additionally, the method is applicable to both fresh and frozen-thawed sperm, making it a valuable tool in assisted reproductive technologies.

The invention also shows that H2 treatment enhances the mitochondrial membrane potential of sperm, which is crucial for ATP production and sperm motility. The improved motility and viability of sperm following H2 treatment can potentially lead to higher fertilization rates and better outcomes in IVF and ICSI procedures.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Preparation of Sperm Suspensions

Human sperm suspensions are prepared from donors or patients undergoing in vitro fertilization (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). The sperm samples are collected by masturbation and processed according to standard protocols. The semen is washed in cleavage medium supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction (PPF) to remove seminal plasma. The sperm are then concentrated by centrifugation and resuspended in cleavage medium with 10% PPF.

### Treatment of Experimentally Damaged Sperm with Hydrogen Gas

Experimentally damaged sperm suspensions are prepared by leaving the sperm at room temperature for more than 5 days to enhance oxidative stress and reduce motility. The damaged sperm suspensions are divided into three groups: untreated (control), treated with H2-mixed gases, and treated with N2-mixed gases. The H2-mixed gases contain 50% H2, 20% O2, and 30% N2, while the N2-mixed gases contain 20% O2 and 80% N2.

Each sperm suspension is placed in an exposure chamber and exposed to the respective gas mixture for 30 minutes. The exposure chamber is tightly sealed, and the gas concentrations are monitored using needle-type sensors. After exposure, the sperm suspensions are mixed and assessed for motility using a microscope and computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA).

### Treatment of Frozen-Thawed Sperm with Hydrogen Gas

Frozen-thawed sperm suspensions are prepared by mixing equal amounts of freshly prepared sperm suspension with cleavage medium and TEST-yolk buffer. The mixture is dispensed into cryotubes and frozen in liquid nitrogen. To thaw the frozen sperm, the cryotubes are warmed at 37°C for 5 minutes, and the thawed sperm suspension is dispensed into vials.

The thawed sperm suspensions are washed with a medium containing hydrogen gas (H2). The medium is prepared by mixing 50%, 75%, or 100% of cleavage medium saturated with H2 with the medium equilibrated with 5% CO2. The sperm suspensions are washed for 5 minutes and then assessed for motility using a microscope and CASA.

### Fluorescent Staining of Sperm Mitochondria

To evaluate the effect of H2 treatment on sperm mitochondrial function, the sperm are co-stained with MitoTracker Green (MTG) and tetramethylrhodamine methyl ester (TMRM). MTG fluorescence is independent of the membrane potential, while TMRM fluorescence is dependent on the membrane potential. The stained sperm are visualized using a laser-scanning confocal microscope, and the TMRM fluorescence intensity is analyzed to determine the mitochondrial membrane potential.

### Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses are conducted using JMP software. One-way ANOVA followed by a post hoc Dunnett's test and two-way ANOVA are used to compare the motility of sperm before and after treatment. Paired t-tests are used to compare the motility of individual sperm suspensions before and after treatment. Differences are considered significant for P-values < 0.05.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Improvement of Experimentally Damaged Sperm Motility by Treatment with Hydrogen Gas

Experimentally damaged sperm suspensions were prepared by leaving the sperm at room temperature for more than 5 days. The average forward motility rate in 35 suspensions was 3.6% (90% CI: 0.6-6.7%). The sperm suspensions were treated with H2-mixed gases (50% H2, 20% O2, and 30% N2) for 40 minutes. The forward motility rate increased significantly to 16.8% (90% CI: 13.8-19.9%). In contrast, the forward motility rate did not change (7.7%, 90% CI: 4.6-10.8%) after treatment with N2-mixed gases (20% O2 and 80% N2).

#### Example 2: Effect of Sperm Survival Rate on H2 Treatment

Sperm suspensions from 30 patients were divided into two groups based on the percentage of viable sperm: high (>20%) and low (<20%). The average forward motility in the high-viability group was 3.0% (90% CI: 0.0-10.4%). After treatment with H2-mixed gases, the forward motility in 16 suspensions significantly increased. The average forward motilities in the high-viability group after treatment with H2- and N2-mixed gases were 11.0% (90% CI: 2.7-20.0%) and 3.6% (90% CI: 0.0-17.6%), respectively.

#### Example 3: Effect of H2 Treatment on Sperm Swimming Speed

Ten sperm suspensions with a higher survival rate were treated with H2- or N2-mixed gases for 30 minutes. The traveling distances of moving sperm were measured using time-lapse microscopy and CASA. No significant difference was observed in the swimming speed between the control and treated groups.

#### Example 4: Retention Time of H2 Treatment on Sperm Motility

After treatment with H2- or N2-mixed gases for 30 minutes, the sperm suspensions were kept at 25°C in room air. The forward motility in the H2-treated suspension remained higher than that before treatment for at least 2.5 hours. A second H2 treatment after 24 hours further increased the motility.

#### Example 5: Enhancement of Mitochondrial Membrane Potential by H2 Treatment

Sperm suspensions were treated with H2- or N2-mixed gases for 30 minutes and stained with TMRM. The TMRM fluorescence intensity increased in the H2-treated sperm, indicating an enhancement of mitochondrial membrane potential.

#### Example 6: Improvement in Frozen-Thawed Sperm Motility by Treatment with Hydrogen Gas

Frozen-thawed sperm suspensions were divided into two groups based on forward motility: higher (≥50%) and lower (<50%). The lower motility group showed a significant increase in forward motility after treatment with sperm-wash medium containing 50% H2. The higher motility group showed a moderate increase in forward motility after treatment with 75% H2.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention has significant industrial applicability in the field of assisted reproductive technologies. The method for improving sperm motility using hydrogen gas (H2) can be applied in IVF and ICSI procedures to enhance the fertilization rates and overall success of these treatments. The invention is particularly useful for patients with asthenozoospermia, where traditional treatments may be less effective.

The method is simple, cost-effective, and can be easily integrated into existing clinical protocols. The use of hydrogen gas as a therapeutic agent offers a novel and promising approach to address male infertility, potentially leading to better outcomes for couples struggling with reproductive issues.
