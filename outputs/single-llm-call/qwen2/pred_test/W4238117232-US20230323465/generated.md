# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and kit for the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules using a panel of specific gene isoforms. More specifically, the invention provides a method for detecting and quantifying the expression of five specific gene isoforms in fine needle aspiration (FNA) samples to distinguish between benign and malignant thyroid tumors, including the recently reclassified non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Thyroid cancer incidence is rapidly increasing worldwide, particularly in the United States, where the prevalence has nearly quadrupled over the past 15 years, primarily due to the increased incidence of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Thyroid nodules are detectable by ultrasound in over 50% of the adult population, and fine needle aspiration (FNA) cytopathology is the most accurate means of pre-operative diagnosis. However, up to 30% of FNA samples result in indeterminate cytology, complicating the diagnosis and management of thyroid nodules.

The American Thyroid Association has recently reclassified a previously considered malignant subgroup of follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC) to the non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP), an indolent neoplasm with questionable malignant potential. NIFTP cannot be differentiated from invasive FVPTC by cytopathology alone, requiring histopathologic evaluation for diagnosis. This reclassification has further complicated the use of existing molecular tests, which often report NIFTP as "positive" or "suspicious" for malignant disease.

Several molecular diagnostic tests, including RNA-based gene expression and multi-panel mutation genotyping analyses, are currently available for clinical use. However, these tests suffer from relatively low specificity and positive predictive value (PPV), particularly after the introduction of the NIFTP subtype. There is a critical need for a more accurate and reliable method to preoperatively diagnose thyroid nodules, especially to distinguish NIFTP from malignant PTC and FVPTC.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the aforementioned needs by providing a method and kit for the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules using a panel of specific gene isoforms. The method involves detecting and quantifying the expression of five specific gene isoforms in FNA samples to distinguish between benign and malignant thyroid tumors, including NIFTP.

The invention includes the following aspects:
1. A method for detecting and quantifying the expression of specific gene isoforms in FNA samples to distinguish between benign and malignant thyroid tumors.
2. A kit for performing the method, comprising primers and probes for the detection of the specific gene isoforms.
3. A method for guiding the treatment of patients based on the results of the gene isoform expression analysis.

The specific gene isoforms targeted by the invention are:
- CDH3
- FNDC4
- HMGA2
- KLK7
- PLAG1

These isoforms are differentially expressed in benign and malignant thyroid tumors and are not expressed in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), making them suitable for use in FNA samples. The method also includes the use of a thyrocyte-specific load control, TPO1, to normalize the expression data.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

- **Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA):** A minimally invasive procedure used to collect cells from a thyroid nodule for diagnostic purposes.
- **Non-Invasive Follicular Thyroid Neoplasm with Papillary-Like Nuclear Features (NIFTP):** An indolent neoplasm with questionable malignant potential, reclassified from a previously considered malignant subgroup of follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC).
- **Positive Predictive Value (PPV):** The probability that subjects with a positive screening test truly have the disease.
- **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):** The probability that subjects with a negative screening test truly do not have the disease.
- **Thyrocyte-Specific Load Control:** A gene or gene isoform that is specifically expressed in thyroid cells and used to normalize the expression data in FNA samples.

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression

The method of the present invention begins with the collection of FNA samples from thyroid nodules. The FNA samples are collected using a 25-gauge needle syringe and preserved in 95% ethanol at -20°C or in RNAlater at -80°C. The FNA site is marked intraoperatively to ensure correlation with the final pathological diagnosis.

Total RNA is isolated from the FNA samples using a GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit (Sigma-Aldrich) following the manufacturer's instructions. The entire total RNA elution volume (11 µl) is used for cDNA synthesis to account for the variable number of thyroid cells in individual FNA samples. cDNA is synthesized using 500 ng of total RNA and SuperScript III reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen).

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid

The detection of target nucleic acids involves performing PCR assays using 5% of the total cDNA. The PCR assays are conducted using Platinum Taq DNA polymerase (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's instructions. The amplified DNA is analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis to identify the isoforms showing the highest levels of differential expression between cancer and non-cancer samples.

The specific gene isoforms targeted by the invention are:
- CDH3
- FNDC4
- HMGA2
- KLK7
- PLAG1

Additionally, four isoforms of thyroglobulin and thyroid peroxidase (TPO) are assessed as potential thyrocyte-specific load controls. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) serves as a total RNA load control.

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)

The detection of target proteins is not a primary focus of the present invention, as the method primarily relies on the detection and quantification of gene expression at the mRNA level. However, the expression levels of the target gene isoforms can be correlated with the presence or absence of specific proteins using techniques such as immunohistochemistry or Western blotting.

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel

The results of the gene isoform expression analysis can be used to guide the treatment of patients with thyroid nodules. The method provides a high degree of sensitivity and specificity in distinguishing between benign and malignant thyroid tumors, including NIFTP. Patients with a positive result for the 5-gene isoform panel are more likely to have a malignant tumor and may require more aggressive treatment, such as surgical resection. Conversely, patients with a negative result are less likely to have a malignant tumor and may be managed with less invasive approaches, such as observation or minimally invasive procedures.

### VI. Kits for Detection of Target Biomarkers

The invention also provides a kit for performing the method. The kit comprises:
- Primers and probes for the detection of the specific gene isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1).
- Primers and probes for the detection of the thyrocyte-specific load control (TPO1).
- Reagents for RNA isolation and cDNA synthesis.
- Instructions for use.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

#### Sample Collection
FNA samples were collected from thyroid nodules using a 25-gauge needle syringe and preserved in 95% ethanol at -20°C or in RNAlater at -80°C. The FNA site was marked intraoperatively to ensure correlation with the final pathological diagnosis.

#### RNA Isolation and Reverse Transcription
Total RNA was isolated from the FNA samples using a GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit (Sigma-Aldrich) following the manufacturer's instructions. The entire total RNA elution volume (11 µl) was used for cDNA synthesis. cDNA was synthesized using 500 ng of total RNA and SuperScript III reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen).

#### PCR of Thyroid Tumor Tissue and PBMC Samples
PCR assays were performed using 5% of the total cDNA and Platinum Taq DNA polymerase (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's instructions. The amplified DNA was analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis to identify the isoforms showing the highest levels of differential expression between cancer and non-cancer samples.

#### Real-Time Quantitative PCR (qPCR) on FNA Samples
Selected target and reference gene isoforms were tested by real-time qPCR on 159 FNA samples from confirmed surgical histological subtypes of thyroid tumors (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP, FVPTC, and PTC). Gene expression was quantitated in duplicate using 5% of total cDNA in each assay and Power SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) on a Bio-Rad iQ5 thermal cycler for 40 cycles. The Ct values of the duplicates were averaged. Serial dilutions of frozen tumor RNA were used to establish the threshold of detectability of the selected thyrocyte-specific reference gene in the assay, and an amplification threshold cycle (Ct) value > 30 was chosen to exclude samples from further analysis for lack of sufficient thyrocytes.

### Results

#### Candidate Gene Isoform Selection
The 22 selected transcript isoforms were characterized on frozen tumor tissue samples from 15 ANs, 14 FAs, 10 HAs, 5 NIFTPs, 7 FVPTCs, 7 HCs, 7 FCs, and 15 PTCs using semi-quantitative PCR. Five transcript isoforms from the following genes—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—showed the most differential expression among thyroid cancers, NIFTPs, and benign tumors. None of the isoforms were detectable in PBMCs from 31 patients.

Among the candidate thyrocyte load-control isoforms of TPO and thyroglobulin tested, TPO1 was the only isoform not detectable in PBMCs and stably expressed across the well-differentiated thyroid tumor subtypes. Therefore, TPO1 was selected as the load control for thyroid cell content of FNA samples.

#### Validation of the 5 Candidate Gene Isoforms
Seven (4.4%) of the 159 FNA samples tested had no detectable TPO1 expression with 40 qPCR cycles and were excluded from the study. Serial dilutions of thyroid tissue-derived RNA revealed that TPO1 was detectable at 30 qPCR cycles using a minimum of 370 pg of total RNA, corresponding to the total RNA of approximately 12-36 cells. A TPO1 threshold Ct value of 30 was selected to include FNA samples for the study, a criterion met by 137 of 159 (86.2%) FNA samples tested.

The TPO1-normalized ΔCt data were z-transformed to create a z-ΔCt score for the expression of each target gene. The expression profiles of the selected CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 isoforms and the composite z-ΔCt score in the 6 thyroid tumor subtypes tested (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP, FVPTC, and PTC) showed higher expression in malignant thyroid tumors than in benign tumors. The composite z-ΔCt score exhibited a differential profile among thyroid tumors.

#### ROC Analysis
An ROC analysis was used to evaluate the ability of the 5-gene isoform expression panel to differentiate benign tumors (AN, FA, HA, and NIFTP) from malignant tumors (FVPTC and PTC). The AUC was 0.86, indicating a high ability to distinguish between benign and malignant tumors. At a threshold for the composite expression score (z-ΔCt) of -1, the sensitivity was 75% and the specificity was 91%. Using the prevalence rates from a previous large multicenter study, the NPV was 91% and the PPV was 74%.

### Discussion

The present invention provides a method and kit for the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules using a panel of specific gene isoforms. The method addresses the limitations of existing molecular tests by providing a high degree of sensitivity and specificity in distinguishing between benign and malignant thyroid tumors, including NIFTP. The use of a thyrocyte-specific load control (TPO1) ensures reliable normalization of the expression data in FNA samples.

The 5-gene isoform panel (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1) was validated using an independent cohort of FNA samples and demonstrated a high ability to distinguish between benign and malignant thyroid tumors. The method also significantly differentiated NIFTP from malignant PTC and FVPTC, which is crucial for guiding appropriate treatment decisions.

### Conclusions

In conclusion, the present invention provides a robust and reliable method for the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules using a panel of specific gene isoforms. The method offers a significant improvement over existing molecular tests by providing a high degree of sensitivity and specificity, particularly in distinguishing NIFTP from malignant PTC and FVPTC. Further validation trials will be needed to develop this panel as a diagnostic assay to guide preoperative surgical decision-making for thyroid tumors.

### Example 3: Development of a High Sensitivity and Specificity Test to Evaluate Preoperative Thyroid Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology Material

#### Validation of the 5 Candidate Gene Isoforms
The 5 candidate gene isoforms were validated using an independent cohort of 159 FNA samples from confirmed surgical histological subtypes of thyroid tumors (AN, FA, HA, NIFTP, FVPTC, and PTC). The TPO1-normalized ΔCt data were z-transformed to create a z-ΔCt score for the expression of each target gene. An ROC analysis was used to evaluate the ability of the 5-gene isoform expression panel to differentiate benign tumors (AN, FA, HA, and NIFTP) from malignant tumors (FVPTC and PTC). The AUC was 0.86, indicating a high ability to distinguish between benign and malignant tumors. At a threshold for the composite expression score (z-ΔCt) of -1, the sensitivity was 75% and the specificity was 91%. Using the prevalence rates from a previous large multicenter study, the NPV was 91% and the PPV was 74%.

The development of this high sensitivity and specificity test represents a significant advancement in the preoperative diagnosis of thyroid nodules, particularly in distinguishing NIFTP from malignant PTC and FVPTC. Further validation trials will be needed to fully develop this panel as a diagnostic assay for clinical use.
