# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) and their interactions with noggin, a natural inhibitor of BMP activity. Specifically, the invention provides novel modified BMPs with enhanced resistance to noggin inhibition, methods for generating and purifying these modified BMPs, and their use in promoting bone formation and osteoblast differentiation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) are a subgroup of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily and play crucial roles in various biological processes, including bone and cartilage formation, wound healing, and embryonic development. Recombinant human BMP-2 and BMP-7 have been widely used clinically to induce new bone formation in spinal fusion and long bone non-union fractures. However, these proteins face significant challenges, including the need for high doses to achieve clinical success and the activation of strong negative feedback mechanisms that attenuate their function.

One such negative feedback mechanism involves noggin, a naturally occurring extracellular BMP inhibitor. Noggin binds to BMPs and prevents their interaction with BMP receptors, thereby inhibiting BMP signaling. This inhibition is particularly pronounced for BMP-7, which is more susceptible to noggin inhibition compared to BMP-6. Understanding and modulating the interactions between BMPs and noggin can lead to the development of more effective BMP variants for therapeutic applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel modified BMPs with enhanced resistance to noggin inhibition. These modified BMPs are designed to retain or enhance their osteogenic activity while overcoming the limitations imposed by noggin. The invention includes methods for generating, expressing, and purifying these modified BMPs, as well as their use in promoting bone formation and osteoblast differentiation.

In one aspect, the invention provides a modified BMP-7 protein comprising a substitution at position 60 of the mature domain, wherein the substitution is from glutamic acid (Glu) to lysine (Lys). This modification results in a BMP-7 variant with increased resistance to noggin inhibition.

In another aspect, the invention provides a modified BMP-2 protein comprising a substitution at position 36 of the mature domain, wherein the substitution is from proline (Pro) to lysine (Lys). This modification also results in a BMP-2 variant with increased resistance to noggin inhibition.

The invention further provides nucleic acids encoding the modified BMPs, expression vectors containing these nucleic acids, and host cells transformed with the expression vectors. Methods for expressing and purifying the modified BMPs are also disclosed.

The modified BMPs of the invention are useful in various therapeutic applications, including the promotion of bone formation, treatment of bone defects, and enhancement of osteoblast differentiation. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the modified BMPs and methods of using these compositions to treat bone-related disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Bone Morphogenetic Proteins

Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) are a family of proteins belonging to the TGF-β superfamily. They play essential roles in bone and cartilage formation, wound healing, and embryonic development. BMPs signal through specific receptors, leading to the activation of intracellular signaling pathways, primarily the Smad pathway. The binding of BMPs to their receptors initiates a cascade of events that ultimately leads to the expression of target genes involved in osteogenesis and other biological processes.

### Interactions Between BMPs and Their Antagonists

Noggin is a potent inhibitor of BMP activity. It binds to BMPs and prevents their interaction with BMP receptors, thereby inhibiting BMP signaling. The interaction between BMPs and noggin is highly specific and can vary among different BMPs. For example, BMP-7 is more susceptible to noggin inhibition compared to BMP-6. This differential susceptibility to noggin inhibition can significantly impact the biological activity and therapeutic potential of BMPs.

### Generating Modified BMPs

The present invention provides methods for generating modified BMPs with enhanced resistance to noggin inhibition. These methods involve site-directed mutagenesis to introduce specific amino acid substitutions into the mature domain of BMPs. For example, a substitution at position 60 of BMP-7 from glutamic acid (Glu) to lysine (Lys) results in a BMP-7 variant with increased resistance to noggin inhibition. Similarly, a substitution at position 36 of BMP-2 from proline (Pro) to lysine (Lys) results in a BMP-2 variant with increased resistance to noggin inhibition.

### Preparing Nucleic Acids Encoding Modified BMPs

Nucleic acids encoding the modified BMPs can be prepared using standard molecular biology techniques. Site-directed mutagenesis can be performed using the QuikChange protocol or other suitable methods. The nucleic acids can be cloned into appropriate expression vectors for subsequent expression and purification of the modified BMPs.

### Expression Vectors

Expression vectors suitable for the production of the modified BMPs include plasmids, viral vectors, and other vectors known in the art. These vectors typically contain regulatory elements such as promoters, enhancers, and terminators to drive the expression of the modified BMPs. Suitable promoters include constitutive promoters (e.g., CMV promoter) and inducible promoters (e.g., tetracycline-inducible promoter).

### Transfection/Transformation

Host cells can be transfected or transformed with the expression vectors using standard methods such as calcium phosphate precipitation, lipofection, electroporation, or viral transduction. Suitable host cells include mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293T, COS, C2C12, A549), insect cells, yeast cells, and bacterial cells. The choice of host cell depends on the specific requirements of the expression system and the desired level of protein expression.

### Hosts for Expression of Modified BMPs

Suitable host cells for the expression of modified BMPs include mammalian cells such as HEK293T, COS, C2C12, and A549. These cells are preferred for their ability to produce correctly folded and functional proteins. Other suitable host cells include insect cells (e.g., Sf9, Sf21), yeast cells (e.g., Pichia pastoris), and bacterial cells (e.g., E. coli).

### Expression Methods

The expression of modified BMPs can be optimized by adjusting various parameters such as the concentration of the expression vector, the duration of transfection, and the culture conditions. For example, HEK293T cells can be transfected with the expression vector using FuGENE 6 or FuGENE HD, and the culture medium can be replaced with fresh medium containing 0.5% FBS 24 hours post-transfection. Conditioned media can be collected 48-72 hours post-transfection for the purification of the modified BMPs.

### Purification

The modified BMPs can be purified from the conditioned media using various chromatographic techniques. For example, the proteins can be precipitated with ammonium phosphate, reconstituted in a buffer containing HEPES, NaCl, and imidazole, and loaded onto a nickel-IMAC column. Bound proteins can be eluted with a buffer containing HEPES, NaCl, and urea. The fractions containing BMP activity can be further purified by HPLC and analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot. The purified proteins can be lyophilized and stored for future use.

## EXAMPLES

### BMP Induction of Alkaline Phosphatase Activity

To assess the osteogenic activity of the modified BMPs, ROS 17/2.8 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and serum-starved for 24 hours. The cells were then stimulated with the modified BMPs in the presence or absence of noggin. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity was measured 24 hours post-stimulation. The results showed that the modified BMP-7 E60K and BMP-2 P36K variants induced ALP activity more potently than their wild-type counterparts, even in the presence of noggin.

### Noggin Inhibition of a Panel of Exemplary BMPs and Related Proteins

A panel of BMPs and related proteins, including BMP-2, BMP-4, BMP-6, BMP-7, and the modified BMP-7 E60K and BMP-2 P36K variants, were tested for their susceptibility to noggin inhibition using an ALP assay in ROS 17/2.8 cells. The results showed that the modified BMP-7 E60K and BMP-2 P36K variants were significantly more resistant to noggin inhibition compared to their wild-type counterparts.

### BMP-6 Induction of Downstream Genes in Primary Human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells is Less Susceptible to Noggin Inhibition

Primary human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells were stimulated with BMP-6 or BMP-7 in the presence or absence of noggin. The induction of downstream genes, including Id-1, Dlx-5, Msx-2, and noggin, was measured by quantitative PCR. The results showed that the induction of these genes by BMP-6 was less susceptible to noggin inhibition compared to BMP-7.

### BMP-7 Variant Proteins with Marked Resistance to Noggin Inhibition

BMP-7 variant proteins with substitutions at position 60, including E60K, Y65N, and R48Q/E60K/Y65N, were generated and tested for their susceptibility to noggin inhibition using a BRE-luciferase reporter gene assay in A549-BRE cells. The results showed that the E60K variant was significantly more resistant to noggin inhibition compared to the wild-type BMP-7 and the other variants.

### Example 5

To further validate the functional data, the binding characteristics of the modified BMPs to cell surface BMP receptors were investigated. COS cells were transfected with BMPR-II and either ALK2 or ALK6, and the cells were affinity-labeled with radiolabeled BMP-6 or BMP-7 in the presence or absence of noggin. The results showed that the modified BMP-7 E60K variant bound to BMP receptors more efficiently in the presence of noggin compared to the wild-type BMP-7.

In conclusion, the present invention provides novel modified BMPs with enhanced resistance to noggin inhibition, methods for generating and purifying these modified BMPs, and their use in promoting bone formation and osteoblast differentiation. These modified BMPs hold promise for future clinical applications, offering improved therapeutic outcomes and reduced dosing requirements.
