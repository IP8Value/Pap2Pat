# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating type 2 diabetes mellitus. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract as a therapeutic agent for managing hyperglycemia and hyperlipidemia, thereby providing a natural and effective alternative to conventional antidiabetic medications.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an extract of Curculigo latifolia fruit and root in a 1:1 ratio, which is effective in reducing blood glucose levels and improving lipid profiles in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus. The extract is prepared by extracting the fruit and root of Curculigo latifolia with distilled water, followed by lyophilization. The resulting lyophilized extract is administered orally to diabetic subjects to achieve the desired therapeutic effects.

The invention also encompasses a method for treating type 2 diabetes mellitus, which involves administering the Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract to a subject in need thereof. The method is particularly useful for reducing hyperglycemia and hyperlipidemia, enhancing insulin and adiponectin secretion, and modulating the expression of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to a pharmaceutical composition and method for treating type 2 diabetes mellitus using an extract of Curculigo latifolia fruit and root. The invention is based on the discovery that the combined extract of Curculigo latifolia fruit and root, when administered to diabetic subjects, effectively reduces blood glucose levels, improves lipid profiles, and enhances insulin and adiponectin secretion. Additionally, the extract modulates the expression of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism, thereby providing a comprehensive approach to managing type 2 diabetes mellitus.

### Example 1

**Preparation of Curculigo latifolia Fruit:Root Extract**

The Curculigo latifolia plant was collected from Beranang, Selangor, Malaysia, and identified by a taxonomist from the Biodiversity Unit in the Institute of Bioscience, Universiti Putra Malaysia, with voucher number SK 1709/09. The fruits were plucked from the apex of the C. latifolia stem, cleaned with tap water, blotted with tissue paper, and stored at −20°C until further use. The roots were cleaned with tap water and dried overnight in an air oven at 40°C. The dried roots were ground to a fine powder using an electric grinder and stored at 4°C until further use.

To prepare the extract, 50 grams of fresh C. latifolia fruits were mashed using a mortar and pestle and extracted with 2000 mL of distilled water. Similarly, 50 grams of C. latifolia root powder were soaked in 2000 mL of distilled water. Both extractions were carried out for 24 hours with continuous stirring at room temperature. The extracts were then filtered through Whatman number 1 filter paper, and the filtrate was collected and lyophilized. The lyophilized fruit and root extracts were mixed in a 1:1 ratio to obtain the final Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract, which was stored at −80°C until further use.

### Example 2

**Animal Study Design**

The efficacy of the Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract was evaluated in a rat model of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Forty-two male Sprague-Dawley rats, weighing 160-180 grams each, were housed individually in polypropylene cages and maintained under controlled room temperature (22 ± 2°C) and humidity (55 ± 5%) with a 12:12 hour light-dark cycle. All experimental protocols for animal care and use were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee (ACUC) of the Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences, Universiti Putra Malaysia (project approval number UPM/FPSK/PADS/BR-UUH/0030).

Upon receipt, the rats were acclimatized for a week with free access to water and normal pellet diet. After acclimatization, normal rats (n = 6) were maintained on a normal pellet diet, while the remaining rats were given a high-fat diet (HFD) to induce obesity. This treatment was conducted for 4 weeks. After 4 weeks, obese rats were anesthetized with diethyl ether after being fasted overnight and injected with 40 mg/kg body weight of streptozotocin (STZ) via intravenous administration to induce type 2 diabetes. Diabetic rats (fasting blood glucose level > 170 mg/dL after 7 days of STZ injection) were randomly divided into 5 groups, each consisting of 6 rats:

- **Group 1:** Normal (normal pellet diet, untreated) rats.
- **Group 2:** Obese (HFD, obese, untreated) rats.
- **Group 3:** Diabetic control (HFD, induced with STZ, diabetic, untreated) rats.
- **Group 4:** Diabetic test rats (HFD, induced with STZ, diabetic) treated with 50 mg/kg body weight of Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract.
- **Group 5:** Diabetic test rats (HFD, induced with STZ, diabetic) treated with 100 mg/kg body weight of Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract.
- **Group 6:** Diabetic test rats (HFD, induced with STZ, diabetic) treated with 200 mg/kg body weight of Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract.
- **Group 7:** Diabetic test rats (HFD, induced with STZ, diabetic) treated with 10 mg/kg body weight of glibenclamide.

Treatment on diabetic rats was conducted for 4 weeks. Body weight of each rat was recorded before (0 week) and after 4 weeks of treatment. At the end of the experimental period, all rats were fasted for 15 hours prior to sacrifice. Blood samples were collected by cardiac puncture, and adipose and muscle tissues were excised and stored at −80°C for further analysis.

### Example 3

**Biochemical Parameter Analysis of Blood**

Blood samples were collected using K2EDTA blood collection tubes. Plasma was obtained by centrifuging the blood at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes. Biological assays, including glucose, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), urea, creatinine, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and γ-glutamyltransferase (GGT), were measured using a Selectra XL clinical chemistry analyzer. Insulin levels were measured using a rat insulin ELISA kit, and adiponectin levels were measured using a BioVision rat adiponectin ELISA assay.

### Example 4

**Gene Expression Study Using Genomelab**

White adipose tissues (retroperitoneal, subcutaneous, and epididymal) and muscle tissues were harvested from the rats. Total RNA was extracted using the RiboPure isolation of high-quality total RNA kit. Reverse transcription and PCR procedures were performed according to the GenomLab GeXP kit protocol. The amplicons from the PCR reaction were used for quantification of differentially expressed genes between the treatment groups using the GenomeLab GeXP Start kit. Samples were prepared and added to the appropriate wells of a 96-well sample microplate. All samples were run in triplicates, and the data were analyzed using Express analysis software. Multiplex genes were normalized with 18S by dividing the peak area of each gene by the peak area of the 18S gene. The expression level was calculated using the following formula:

\[
\text{Fold change} = \frac{\text{Normalized data of the gene from treated samples}}{\text{Normalized data of the gene from untreated samples}}
\]

### Example 5

**Statistical Analyses**

All results are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. The data were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey's post hoc test. The level of significance was set at P < 0.05.

### Example 6

**Results and Discussion**

**Energy Contribution from HFD:**
The high-fat diet (HFD) used in the study contained 56.9% energy from fat sources, indicating a 47.4% increase in fat composition compared to the normal pellet diet (NPD). This high-fat content is consistent with the requirements for inducing obesity and insulin resistance in the rat model.

**Body Weight:**
Body weight increased significantly in normal and obese rats over the 4-week study period. However, body weight in diabetic control rats increased by only 6%, while body weight in diabetic rats treated with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract increased by 12%, 9%, and 7% for the 50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg doses, respectively. This suggests that the extract has a beneficial effect on weight management in diabetic rats.

**Plasma Glucose Level:**
Plasma glucose levels in diabetic control rats were significantly higher compared to normal rats. Treatment with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract at 200 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 50 mg/kg doses resulted in a significant reduction in glucose levels by 64.5%, 54.3%, and 51.6%, respectively. This indicates that the extract has a potent hypoglycemic effect.

**Plasma Lipid Profiles:**
Diabetic control rats exhibited elevated levels of total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL), while high-density lipoprotein (HDL) levels were reduced. Treatment with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract significantly decreased TC, TG, and LDL levels and increased HDL levels. The most significant improvements were observed in the 200 mg/kg dose group, suggesting a dose-dependent effect.

**Plasma Insulin and Adiponectin:**
Insulin levels in diabetic control rats were not significantly different from baseline, but treatment with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract increased insulin levels by 16%, 13%, and 12% for the 50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg doses, respectively. Adiponectin levels, which were significantly reduced in diabetic control rats, were increased by 48%, 56%, and 41% in the 50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg dose groups, respectively.

**Plasma ALT, GGT, Urea, and Creatinine:**
Diabetic control rats showed elevated levels of ALT, GGT, urea, and creatinine, indicating liver and kidney dysfunction. Treatment with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract reduced these levels, suggesting a protective effect on liver and kidney function.

**Gene Expression Study:**
The expression of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism, such as IRS1, IGF, GLUT4, PPARα, PPARγ, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, leptin, LPL, and lipase, was significantly downregulated in obese and diabetic control rats. Treatment with Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract upregulated the expression of these genes, indicating a mechanism by which the extract improves glucose and lipid metabolism.

**Conclusion:**
The present study demonstrates that Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract is effective in reducing blood glucose levels and improving lipid profiles in diabetic rats. The extract also enhances insulin and adiponectin secretion and modulates the expression of genes involved in glucose and lipid metabolism. These findings support the potential of Curculigo latifolia fruit:root extract as a therapeutic option for managing type 2 diabetes mellitus and its complications.
