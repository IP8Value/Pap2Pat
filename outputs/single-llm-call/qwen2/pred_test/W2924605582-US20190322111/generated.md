## FIELD
The present invention relates to the field of functional printing, specifically to the synthesis, printing, and reduction of nickel (Ni) precursor inks to produce aligned Ni nanowires. More particularly, the invention pertains to a method for printing and thermally reducing a nickel precursor ink in the presence of a magnetic field to form aligned Ni nanowires on various substrates.

## BACKGROUND
Functional printing, particularly involving metallic inks, has gained significant attention due to its applications in printed electronics, magnetic devices, and sensors. Metallic inks, such as those containing copper (Cu), silver (Ag), and nickel (Ni), are commonly used to produce conductive and magnetic features. Transition metals like iron (Fe), cobalt (Co), and nickel (Ni) are favored for their ferromagnetic properties, with Ni being particularly advantageous due to its corrosion resistance, good electrical conductivity, high magnetic permeability, and high thermal coefficient of resistance.

Metallic inks are typically formulated as either nanoparticle inks or precursor inks. Nanoparticle inks, while effective, face challenges such as agglomeration and solvent evaporation, which can affect print quality. Precursor inks, also known as metal organic decomposition (MOD) inks, offer a solution to these issues by dissolving an organic metal complex in a solvent, resulting in a homogeneous solution free from particle instability. However, the metal content in precursor inks is generally lower than in nanoparticle inks, and the reduction process can be complex, especially for Ni due to its tendency to oxidize upon contact with air.

Despite these challenges, there is a significant interest in developing methods to directly fabricate and align nanowires on substrates for various applications, including magnetic data storage, sensors, and optical devices. Traditional methods for fabricating and aligning nanowires often involve multiple steps, the use of templates, and extreme conditions, making them less practical for large-scale production.

## SUMMARY
The present invention provides a novel method for synthesizing, printing, and thermally reducing a nickel precursor ink to produce aligned Ni nanowires. The method involves the following steps:
1. Synthesizing a homogeneous nickel precursor ink in ambient conditions.
2. Printing the ink on a substrate using an aerosol printing technique.
3. Thermally reducing the printed ink in the presence of a magnetic field to form aligned Ni nanowires.

The invention offers several advantages over existing methods:
- **Template-Free Fabrication:** The method does not require the use of templates, simplifying the fabrication process.
- **Ambient Conditions:** The synthesis and reduction processes can be performed under ambient conditions, reducing the complexity and cost of the process.
- **Enhanced Properties:** The aligned Ni nanowires exhibit enhanced electrical and magnetic properties, making them suitable for a wide range of applications.
- **Versatility:** The method can be adapted for various printing techniques and substrates, including flexible and thermally sensitive materials.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION
The present invention relates to a method for synthesizing, printing, and thermally reducing a nickel (Ni) precursor ink to produce aligned Ni nanowires. The method involves the following detailed steps:

### Synthesis of Nickel Precursor Ink
The nickel precursor ink is synthesized by dissolving nickel formate in a solvent mixture containing ethylene diamine. Specifically, 0.8 grams of nickel formate (5.38 mmol) is dissolved in a solution of 0.72 mL of ethylene diamine (10.77 mmol) and 4 mL of distilled water. The mixture is stirred at room temperature for 15 minutes to ensure complete dissolution. The resulting ink is filtered through a 0.2 µm syringe filter to remove any undissolved particles. Additional additives, such as 1% (w/w) polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and 20 µL of BYK 333 surfactant (10% v/v solution in distilled water), are added to improve the ink's rheological properties and wettability.

### Aerosol Printing
The synthesized nickel precursor ink is printed on a substrate using an aerosol printing technique. An aerosol jet printer, such as the NanoJet aerosol printer, is used to dispense the ink. The printer uses ultrasonic energy to atomize the ink, and a carrier gas (air) to transport the aerosol to a series of aerodynamic focusing lenses. The aerosol is then directed towards the substrate through a tapered luer lock dispensing tip. The sheath gas flow is maintained to avoid clogging of the nozzle and to focus the distribution of aerosol droplets onto the substrate.

The printing parameters are carefully controlled to ensure consistent and high-quality prints. The distance between the substrate and the nozzle tip is kept constant at 5 mm, and the translational speed is set to 2 mm/s. A solid 1 cm × 1 cm square pattern is printed using a 25 gauge dispensing tip, resulting in a wet film thickness of approximately 4 µm for a single printed layer.

### Thermal Reduction
The printed nickel precursor ink is thermally reduced to form metallic Ni. The reduction process is carried out in the presence of a magnetic field to align the nanowires. The substrate is placed on a benchtop hot plate and heated to 240 °C for 15 minutes. In the absence of a magnetic field, the reduction process results in a porous Ni film with particles ranging from 100 to 250 nm. However, when a homogeneous magnetic field of 250 Oe is applied during the reduction process, the particles align to form nanowires. The nanowires are composed of individual 100–250 nm nanoparticles that fuse together, creating a "string of pearls" structure.

### Characterization
The morphology and structure of the reduced Ni films are characterized using various techniques:
- **Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM):** SEM images reveal the formation of aligned Ni nanowires in the presence of a magnetic field and a porous Ni film in its absence.
- **Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS):** EDS analysis confirms the presence of Ni and the absence of significant impurities.
- **Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA) and Differential Thermal Analysis (DTA):** TGA and DTA data show that the thermal decomposition of the ink occurs in two stages: solvent evaporation up to 100 °C and reduction of Ni to its elemental state at 235 °C.
- **Electrical Conductivity:** The electrical resistivity of the printed Ni ink is measured using a four-point probe. The resistivity is lower in the direction of the aligned Ni nanowires, indicating enhanced conductivity.
- **Magnetic Properties:** The magnetic properties of the Ni films are studied using a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM). The hysteresis loops show that the aligned Ni nanowires exhibit higher remanent magnetization and coercive field in the direction of the magnetic field.

### Example 1
In this example, the method of the present invention is demonstrated by synthesizing a nickel precursor ink, printing it on a glass substrate, and thermally reducing it in the presence of a magnetic field to form aligned Ni nanowires.

#### Materials
- Nickel formate (Alfa Aesar, Ward Hill, MA, USA)
- Ethylene diamine (Fisher Scientific, Fair Lawn, NJ, USA)
- Distilled water
- Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) (MW: 3500, K12)
- BYK 333 surfactant
- Glass microscope slides (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA)

#### Ink Formulation
1. Dissolve 0.8 grams of nickel formate in a solution of 0.72 mL of ethylene diamine and 4 mL of distilled water.
2. Stir the mixture at room temperature for 15 minutes.
3. Filter the ink through a 0.2 µm syringe filter.
4. Add 1% (w/w) PVP and 20 µL of BYK 333 surfactant to the ink.

#### Aerosol Printing
1. Clean the glass microscope slides with isopropyl alcohol.
2. Treat the substrates with atmospheric plasma to improve wettability.
3. Print the nickel precursor ink on the glass slides using a NanoJet aerosol printer.
4. Set the printing parameters: distance between substrate and nozzle tip = 5 mm, translational speed = 2 mm/s, and wet film thickness = 4 µm.

#### Thermal Reduction
1. Place the printed glass slides on a benchtop hot plate.
2. Heat the slides to 240 °C for 15 minutes.
3. Apply a homogeneous magnetic field of 250 Oe during the reduction process.

#### Characterization
1. **SEM Imaging:** Observe the morphology of the reduced Ni films. The presence of aligned Ni nanowires in the magnetic field and a porous Ni film in its absence should be evident.
2. **EDS Analysis:** Confirm the presence of Ni and the absence of significant impurities.
3. **TGA and DTA:** Analyze the thermal decomposition of the ink to ensure proper reduction.
4. **Electrical Conductivity:** Measure the electrical resistivity using a four-point probe. The resistivity should be lower in the direction of the aligned Ni nanowires.
5. **Magnetic Properties:** Study the magnetic properties using a VSM. The hysteresis loops should show higher remanent magnetization and coercive field in the direction of the magnetic field.

By following the method described in this example, aligned Ni nanowires with enhanced electrical and magnetic properties can be produced, demonstrating the effectiveness and versatility of the present invention.
