# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic agents for treating chronic kidney disease (CKD) and, more particularly, to a novel proteolysis targeting chimera (PROTAC) that targets the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α. The invention leverages the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (UPP) to achieve dual therapeutic effects, thereby providing a promising approach for the treatment of renal fibrosis and renal anemia.

## BACKGROUND ART

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a significant health burden worldwide, characterized by progressive loss of renal function and the development of renal fibrosis. Renal fibrosis is a common pathological process in CKD, involving the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM) and the transformation of tubular epithelial cells into myofibroblasts, leading to the disruption of renal architecture and function. Transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1) and its downstream effector, Smad3, play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of renal fibrosis. Smad3 is a core signal protein that mediates the profibrotic effects of TGF-β1, contributing to ECM deposition, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and inflammation.

Hypoxia-inducible factor-2α (HIF-2α) is a transcription factor that plays a protective role in the progression of CKD. HIF-2α promotes renal repair and ameliorates renal anemia by increasing the production of erythropoietin (EPO) and enhancing iron utilization. However, the degradation of HIF-2α is tightly regulated by the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) protein, an E3 ubiquitin ligase. The VHL protein targets HIF-2α for ubiquitination and subsequent degradation via the UPP.

Proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTACs) are a novel class of therapeutic agents that harness the UPP to degrade target proteins. A typical PROTAC consists of a ligand that binds to the protein of interest (POI), a linker, and a ligand that recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase. By bringing the POI and the E3 ligase into close proximity, PROTACs facilitate the ubiquitination and degradation of the POI. This approach offers several advantages over traditional inhibitors, including the ability to target "undruggable" proteins and the potential for sustained therapeutic effects at lower doses.

Despite the promise of PROTACs, the design and optimization of these molecules require careful consideration of the POI, the E3 ligase, and the linker. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel PROTAC that targets the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α, thereby offering a multifaceted approach to treating CKD.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel proteolysis targeting chimera (PROTAC) that targets the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α. The PROTAC comprises a small molecule compound (SMC) that specifically binds to Smad3, a SMC that specifically binds to the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) protein, and a linker connecting these two SMCs. The PROTAC is designed to degrade Smad3 via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (UPP) and stabilize HIF-2α by inhibiting its degradation, thereby providing dual therapeutic effects for the treatment of chronic kidney disease (CKD).

In one embodiment, the PROTAC is represented by the formula:
\[ \text{SMC}_{\text{Smad3}}-\text{Linker}-\text{SMC}_{\text{VHL}} \]
where:
- \(\text{SMC}_{\text{Smad3}}\) is a small molecule compound that specifically binds to Smad3,
- \(\text{Linker}\) is a chemical linker connecting \(\text{SMC}_{\text{Smad3}}\) and \(\text{SMC}_{\text{VHL}}\),
- \(\text{SMC}_{\text{VHL}}\) is a small molecule compound that specifically binds to the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) protein.

In a preferred embodiment, the \(\text{SMC}_{\text{Smad3}}\) is a chiral molecule with the S configuration, and the \(\text{SMC}_{\text{VHL}}\) is a known VHL-binding compound. The linker is a succinic acid ((CH2COOH)2) linker, which provides optimal flexibility and efficiency in the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α.

The PROTAC of the present invention exhibits high efficacy in degrading Smad3 and stabilizing HIF-2α in vitro and in vivo. In vitro studies demonstrate that the PROTAC significantly reduces Smad3 protein levels and increases HIF-2α protein levels in renal fibroblasts and tubular cells. In vivo studies using mouse models of renal fibrosis (unilateral ureteral obstruction, UUO) and chronic renal failure (5/6 nephrectomy, 5/6Nx) show that the PROTAC effectively ameliorates renal fibrosis, improves renal function, and corrects renal anemia.

The PROTAC of the present invention is safe and well-tolerated in mice, with no significant adverse effects observed in subacute and chronic toxicity studies. Pharmacokinetic studies indicate that the PROTAC has a favorable pharmacokinetic profile, with sustained plasma levels and tissue distribution that support its therapeutic effects.

## EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1

#### Chemical Synthesis and Optimization of the PROTAC

The PROTAC of the present invention was synthesized by combining a Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) and a VHL-binding SMC with a succinic acid ((CH2COOH)2) linker. The Smad3-binding SMC is a chiral molecule with the S configuration, which was previously identified through computer virtual screening and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) technology. The VHL-binding SMC is a known VHL ligand.

The synthesis of the PROTAC was performed as follows:

1. **Synthesis of the Smad3-Binding SMC**:
   - The Smad3-binding SMC was synthesized using standard organic synthesis techniques. The chiral S configuration was confirmed using chiral HPLC and mass spectrometry (MS).

2. **Synthesis of the VHL-Binding SMC**:
   - The VHL-binding SMC was synthesized using known methods and confirmed by HPLC and MS.

3. **Synthesis of the PROTAC**:
   - The Smad3-binding SMC and the VHL-binding SMC were connected using a succinic acid ((CH2COOH)2) linker. The linker was introduced by reacting the carboxylic acid groups of the succinic acid with the amino groups of the SMCs using a coupling agent such as 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) and N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS).
   - The purity and structure of the resulting PROTAC were confirmed by HPLC and MS.

#### In Vitro Evaluation of the PROTAC

The efficacy of the PROTAC in degrading Smad3 and stabilizing HIF-2α was evaluated in vitro using human renal carcinoma (ACHN) cells and rat kidney interstitial fibroblasts (NRK-49F) cells.

1. **Degradation of Smad3**:
   - ACHN cells were treated with the PROTAC at various concentrations (0, 1, 5, 10 μg) for 30 minutes at 37°C. The degradation of Smad3 was assessed by western blot analysis. The results showed a dose-dependent reduction in Smad3 protein levels, with significant degradation observed at 5 μg and 10 μg.
   - To confirm that the degradation of Smad3 was mediated by the UPP, the proteasome inhibitor MG132 was added to the cell lysates. The addition of MG132 rescued the degradation of Smad3, indicating that the PROTAC-mediated degradation occurs via the UPP.

2. **Stabilization of HIF-2α**:
   - NRK-49F cells were treated with the PROTAC at 25 nM for 24 hours. The protein levels of HIF-2α were assessed by western blot analysis. The results showed a significant increase in HIF-2α protein levels, with no significant changes in HIF-1α protein levels.
   - The time course of HIF-2α stabilization was also evaluated, showing a sustained increase in HIF-2α protein levels over 24 hours.

#### In Vivo Evaluation of the PROTAC

The therapeutic effects of the PROTAC were evaluated in mouse models of renal fibrosis (UUO) and chronic renal failure (5/6Nx).

1. **Unilateral Ureteral Obstruction (UUO) Model**:
   - Adult male C57BL/6J mice were subjected to UUO surgery and randomly divided into four groups: Sham + Vehicle, Sham + PROTAC, UUO + Vehicle, and UUO + PROTAC.
   - The PROTAC was administered intraperitoneally (i.p.) at a dose of 30 mg/kg twice daily for 5 days before surgery and 90 mg/kg twice daily for 9 days after surgery.
   - Renal fibrosis was assessed by Masson's trichrome staining, and the results showed a significant reduction in collagen deposition in the UUO + PROTAC group compared to the UUO + Vehicle group.
   - The protein levels of Smad3 and HIF-2α were assessed by western blot analysis, and the results showed a significant reduction in Smad3 protein levels and a significant increase in HIF-2α protein levels in the UUO + PROTAC group.

2. **5/6 Nephrectomy (5/6Nx) Model**:
   - Adult male C57BL/6J mice were subjected to 5/6Nx surgery and randomly divided into four groups: Sham + Vehicle, Sham + PROTAC, 5/6Nx + Vehicle, and 5/6Nx + PROTAC.
   - The PROTAC was administered subcutaneously (s.c.) at a dose of 24 mg/kg every 2 days for 5 weeks.
   - Renal function was assessed by measuring serum cystatin C, creatinine, and blood urea nitrogen (BUN). The results showed a significant improvement in renal function in the 5/6Nx + PROTAC group compared to the 5/6Nx + Vehicle group.
   - Renal anemia was assessed by measuring hemoglobin (HGB) and red blood cell (RBC) counts. The results showed a significant improvement in anemia in the 5/6Nx + PROTAC group, with increased levels of HGB and RBC counts.
   - The protein levels of Smad3, HIF-2α, collagen-I, and α-SMA were assessed by western blot analysis, and the results showed a significant reduction in Smad3 and collagen-I protein levels and a significant increase in HIF-2α protein levels in the 5/6Nx + PROTAC group.

#### Safety and Pharmacokinetic Evaluation

The safety and pharmacokinetic properties of the PROTAC were evaluated in ICR mice.

1. **Subacute and Chronic Toxicity**:
   - ICR mice were divided into 8 groups (male/female control, low-dose (50 mg/kg), middle-dose (150 mg/kg), and high-dose (300 mg/kg)) and administered subcutaneous (s.c.) injections of the PROTAC for 7 consecutive days.
   - No significant signs of toxicity or behavioral abnormalities were observed in any of the groups. Body weight, hematological parameters, and organ/body weight ratios were monitored, and no significant changes were observed.
   - Histopathological examination of liver, kidney, and spleen tissues showed no significant abnormalities, except for an increase in nucleated cells in the spleen, which did not affect red blood cell (RBC) counts.

2. **Pharmacokinetic Profile**:
   - The pharmacokinetic profile of the PROTAC was evaluated in ICR mice after administration by intravenous (i.v.), intraperitoneal (i.p.), subcutaneous (s.c.), and gavage (i.g.) routes.
   - The PROTAC administered by i.p. at 5 mg/kg resulted in a peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 94.1 ng/mL, with a half-life (T1/2) of 0.481 hours.
   - The PROTAC administered by s.c. at 5 mg/kg resulted in a peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 221 ng/mL, with a half-life (T1/2) of 1.58 hours.
   - Tissue distribution studies showed that the PROTAC was detectable in heart, kidney, and liver tissues up to 24 hours after s.c. administration.

In conclusion, the PROTAC of the present invention provides a novel and effective approach for the treatment of chronic kidney disease by targeting the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α. The PROTAC exhibits high efficacy, safety, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties, making it a promising candidate for further clinical development.
