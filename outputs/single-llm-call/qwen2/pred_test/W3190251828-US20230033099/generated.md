# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Dendritic cell (DC)-based immunotherapy approaches have gained significant attention for their ability to induce potent anti-tumor immune responses. The only FDA-approved DC-based vaccine for cancer, Sipuleucel-T, has demonstrated some survival advantages in prostate cancer patients. However, the breadth of immunity induced by a single antigen approach is limited and not widely applicable to cancers without defined target antigens, such as triple-negative breast cancer. Other DC vaccination approaches involve the identification and production of patient-specific neoantigens from peptides restricted to the human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I for presentation to CD8 T cells, and the delivery of mRNA encoding tumor antigens and activating signals to DCs. Despite their promise, these approaches remain resource-intensive. GVAX, another notable approach, utilized irradiated tumor cells modified to express soluble GM-CSF to stimulate antigen-presenting cells and induce an anti-tumor response. However, the difficulty in regulating the amount of soluble GM-CSF secreted resulted in increased myeloid-derived suppressor cell (MDSC) and impaired DC responses in patients. Tumor lysates have also been tested as a source of antigen for DC-based vaccines in cancer, with oxidation of tumor tissue using hypochlorous acid increasing the immunogenicity of the lysate. DCs generated from monocytes and pulsed with the autologous oxidized tumor lysate have been shown to induce robust anti-tumor effects in both murine and human studies.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel tumor membrane vesicle (TMV)-based vaccine immunotherapy approach that can be prepared from frozen tumor tissue. TMVs are 200–600 nm in size, making them ideal for processing by antigen-presenting cells such as DCs and macrophages. To enhance the immune response against the tumor antigens in the TMV, a novel "protein transfer" approach is employed to incorporate glycosyl phosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored immunostimulatory molecules (GPI-ISMs) onto the surface of TMVs. The invention further involves a DC vaccine platform that utilizes autologous DCs pulsed with cytokine-adjuvanted TMVs as a therapeutic in murine breast cancer models. The DCs can take up TMVs when pulsed in vitro, and this uptake is improved when TMVs contain GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12 on their surface. Treatment with DCs pulsed ex vivo with HER-2+ TMV results in tumor growth inhibition and improved survival of tumor-bearing mice. Additionally, in a triple-negative breast cancer model, the DC vaccine can inhibit tumor growth, reduce lung metastasis, and increase immune cell infiltration into the tumor.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Tumor Membrane Vesicles (TMVs)

Tumor membrane vesicles (TMVs) are derived from the plasma membrane of tumor cells and have an average diameter of 200 nm, falling within the optimal size range for immunogenicity and uptake by dendritic cells (DCs). TMVs retain the surface antigens present on tumor cells, making them an effective source of tumor-associated antigens for immunotherapy. The preparation of TMVs involves homogenizing frozen tumor tissue or cultured cell pellets, isolating the plasma membranes by ultracentrifugation over a 41% sucrose gradient, and analyzing the vesicles for particle diameters using a Zetasizer Nano ZS. The polydispersity index of the vesicles is typically around 0.31, similar to that of liposome formulations. Surface marker expression of tumor antigens, such as HER2, is confirmed using flow cytometry.

### TMV Preparation, Characterization, and GPI-ISM Incorporation

TMVs are generated from frozen tumor tissue or cultured cell pellets by homogenizing the tissue and isolating the plasma membranes through ultracentrifugation over a 41% sucrose gradient. The size and polydispersity of the vesicles are characterized using a Zetasizer Nano ZS. To incorporate GPI-ISMs onto the surface of TMVs, a protein transfer method is employed. Purified GPI-ISMs, such as GPI-IL-12 and GPI-GM-CSF, are incubated with the TMVs at 37°C for 4 hours under constant rotation. The TMVs are then pelleted by ultracentrifugation, and unbound GPI-ISMs in the supernatant are removed, followed by a wash with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The successful incorporation of GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12 onto the TMVs is verified using flow cytometry with specific antibodies.

### TMVs Containing the Tumor Antigen HER2 and Protein Transferred GPI-ISMs on Their Surface

Flow cytometry and Zetasizer analysis are used to assess the surface marker expression and size distribution of the TMVs. D2F2/E2 TMVs prepared from cell culture pellets are within 200 nm in diameter, with a polydispersity index of 0.31. Surface marker expression of HER2 is confirmed on D2F2/E2 cells and TMVs from this cell line. Flow cytometry analysis of plain TMVs shows no signal of IL-12 or GM-CSF on their surface, while analysis of GPI-ISM-incorporated TMVs confirms the presence of protein transfer-mediated incorporation of GPI-GM-CSF and GPI-IL-12.

### Uptake of TMVs by DCs is Potentiated by GPI-ISMs

DCs are one of the most potent antigen-presenting cells (APCs) that can take up vaccine antigens at the vaccination site and migrate to lymphoid organs to initiate a T-cell response. To test whether DCs can phagocytose TMVs incorporated with GPI-ISMs, the TMVs are labeled with the pH-sensitive dye pHrodo Deep Red, which fluoresces in the acidic conditions of the late endosome/phagolysosome. Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) are pulsed with pHrodo-labeled TMVs for 30, 90, and 180 minutes, then washed and kept on ice until flow cytometry analysis. DCs pulsed with plain TMVs are able to take up TMVs as early as 30 minutes, albeit at a smaller percentage compared to DCs pulsed with TMVs containing either GPI-ISM. After 180 minutes of incubation, there is a significant increase in the percentage of pHrodo-positive DCs pulsed with GPI-IL-12 TMVs; however, the highest percentages of pHrodo-positive DCs are observed after pulsing with GPI-GM-CSF TMVs or TMVs containing both GPI-ISMs. There is also a significant increase in the pHrodo mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) on DCs pulsed with TMVs containing either GPI-IL-12 or GM-CSF compared to plain TMVs, with the highest MFI observed in DCs pulsed with TMVs containing both GPI-ISMs.

### DC Vaccine Therapy Reduces Tumor Growth

To test the efficacy of the DCs pulsed with TMVs as a vaccine platform, the HER2-positive murine model D2F2/E2 is used. Mice are challenged with D2F2/E2 cells, and BMDCs are delivered subcutaneously (s.c.) on the opposite flank after seven days of tumor growth. Treatment with DCs that are not pulsed with TMVs (DC alone) and DCs that are pulsed with TMVs without GPI-ISMs (plain TMV-pulsed DCs) show no reduction in tumor growth and have similar survival rates compared to PBS controls. However, mice that receive the DC vaccine (DCs pulsed with TMVs incorporated with GPI-IL-12 and GPI-GM-CSF) show a significant inhibition of tumor growth compared to all other groups. Mice from the PBS control group, DC alone, or plain TMV-pulsed DC groups survive until day 35 post-tumor inoculation. Treatment with the DC vaccine results in a significant improvement in survival (day 35 versus day 57) compared to controls.

### DC Vaccine Inhibits 4T1 Tumor Growth, Reduces Lung Metastasis, and Improved Immune Profile of Tumors

The 4T1 triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) model represents a highly metastatic cancer that lacks a defined target antigen. Mice are challenged with 4T1 cells, and DCs pulsed with 4T1 TMV vaccine are injected s.c. on the opposite flank after five days of tumor inoculation. Similar to the observations in the D2F2/E2 model, only treatment with DCs pulsed with 4T1 TMVs containing both GPI-ISMs (DC vaccine) results in a significant inhibition of tumor growth. Given that the 4T1 model is spontaneously metastatic, the metastatic burden in the lungs of tumor-bearing mice is quantified after 20 days of tumor growth. Treatment with the DC vaccine results in a 2 to 3-fold reduction in metastatic 4T1 cells in the lungs compared to PBS controls, DC alone, or plain TMV-pulsed DC therapy. To characterize the immune infiltrates of 4T1 tumors after therapy with the TMV vaccine, tumors are resected and processed to isolate tumor-infiltrating cells. Flow cytometry analysis shows a 2-fold increase in MHC-II+ CD11c+ DCs in 4T1 tumors after mice receive the DC vaccine compared to PBS controls. Further, DC vaccine treatment results in a significant increase in CD4 and CD8 T cells in the tumor compared to PBS controls.

### Bone Marrow-Derived Dendritic Cell (BMDC) Production

Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) are generated according to established protocols. Femurs of female BALB/c mice are removed and cleaned from surrounding muscle tissue. Bone marrow is flushed using RPMI-1640 medium with a 22G needle and syringe. Red blood cells (RBCs) are lysed using RBC lysis buffer, and the resulting cells are cultured in complete RPMI-1640 medium containing 20 ng/mL recombinant murine GM-CSF (rmGM-CSF) at a density of 2 × 10^5 cells/mL. The rmGM-CSF is replenished at days 3, 6, and 8 in culture, and BMDCs are ready for use at day 10.

### 4T1 Lung Metastasis Assay

Lungs are collected from mice bearing 4T1 tumors after 20 days of tumor inoculation. These are processed under sterile conditions, minced, and digested in 1 mg/mL Collagenase IV for 2 hours at 37°C under rotation. The cell suspension is filtered and washed twice in selection media composed of complete DMEM containing 60 µM 6-thioguanine (6-TG). This treatment kills lung fibroblasts without affecting tumor cells. Once one of the wells reaches confluency, all the wells are harvested and counted using a Cellometer T4 Auto counter. The number of metastatic 4T1 cells in the lungs is quantified to assess the efficacy of the DC vaccine in reducing lung metastasis.

In summary, the present invention provides a novel and effective TMV-based DC vaccine platform for the treatment of various types of cancer, including those lacking defined target antigens. The incorporation of GPI-ISMs onto TMVs enhances the uptake and activation of DCs, leading to a robust anti-tumor immune response and improved survival outcomes in preclinical models. This approach holds significant promise for the development of personalized cancer immunotherapies.
