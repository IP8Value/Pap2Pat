# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane and methods for its preparation. More specifically, the invention pertains to a carbon membrane that exhibits enhanced photothermal properties and superhydrophobicity, making it particularly suitable for applications in solar energy conversion, water purification, and other related fields.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The demand for efficient and sustainable energy solutions has driven significant advancements in materials science, particularly in the development of photothermal materials. Photothermal materials convert light energy, especially sunlight, into thermal energy, which can be utilized for various applications such as water desalination, solar steam generation, and thermal management systems. However, traditional photothermal materials often suffer from issues such as low efficiency, poor stability, and limited scalability.

Superhydrophobic surfaces, characterized by their ability to repel water, have gained considerable attention due to their potential to enhance the performance of photothermal systems. By reducing water adhesion and promoting droplet shedding, superhydrophobic surfaces can improve the efficiency of heat transfer and reduce fouling, thereby extending the operational lifespan of photothermal devices.

Despite these advantages, the integration of superhydrophobicity with photothermal properties in a single material remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches often involve complex and costly processes, leading to materials that are either not cost-effective or lack the necessary robustness for practical applications.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel photothermal superhydrophobic carbon membrane. This membrane combines the excellent photothermal properties of carbon materials with the superhydrophobic characteristics of fluorosilane-coated surfaces, resulting in a material that is both efficient and durable.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane and methods for its preparation. The SP carbon membrane comprises a graphene foam (GF) substrate, a fluorosilane coating, and a pore-controlled layer of graphite particles and phenolic resin. The GF substrate serves as the base material, providing a porous structure that enhances the photothermal properties of the membrane. The fluorosilane coating imparts superhydrophobicity to the membrane, while the pore-controlled layer further improves its photothermal efficiency and stability.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for preparing the SP carbon membrane, comprising the following steps:
1. Machining the GF substrate to form tubes and chambers.
2. Depositing fluorosilane molecules on the GF substrate via solution immersion.
3. Drying the fluorosilane-coated GF substrate.
4. Coating the GF substrate with a mixture of graphite particles and phenolic resin.
5. Thermally curing the coated GF substrate.

In another aspect, the invention provides the SP carbon membrane prepared by the aforementioned method. The membrane exhibits high photothermal efficiency, superhydrophobicity, and excellent durability, making it suitable for a wide range of applications, including solar energy conversion, water purification, and thermal management systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Preparation of the Graphene Foam Substrate

The graphene foam (GF) substrate used in the present invention is a porous material that serves as the base for the photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane. The GF substrate is prepared from heat-treated polyimide mesophase pitch, which is sieved to a 250 mesh size. Ground foam graphene platelets are added to the pitch at a concentration of 7% to form the GF substrate. The GF substrate is then machined using an electrical saw and drills to create tubes and chambers of the desired dimensions.

### Deposition of Fluorosilane Molecules

To impart superhydrophobicity to the GF substrate, fluorosilane molecules are deposited on the surface via solution immersion. A 0.1 wt% solution of (heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl) trichlorosilane in hexane is prepared, and the GF substrate is immersed in this solution overnight. The fluorosilane molecules chemically bond to the surface of the GF substrate, creating a superhydrophobic layer that repels water and other liquids.

### Drying and Rinsing of the Fluorosilane-Coated GF Substrate

After the fluorosilane deposition, the coated GF substrate is dried in an oven at 100°C for 1 hour to ensure complete evaporation of the solvent. The substrate is then rinsed with copious amounts of water and ethanol to remove any unbound silanes, leaving a stable and uniform superhydrophobic layer on the surface.

### Coating with Graphite Particles and Phenolic Resin

To further enhance the photothermal properties of the SP carbon membrane, a pore-controlled layer is applied to the fluorosilane-coated GF substrate. A mixture of graphite particles and phenolic resin, at a weight ratio of 2:1, is prepared and coated onto the substrate using a wood stick knife. The coated substrate is then allowed to dry overnight at room temperature.

### Thermal Curing of the Coated GF Substrate

The final step in the preparation of the SP carbon membrane involves thermal curing of the coated GF substrate. The substrate is heated to 300°C for 1 hour in a controlled environment. This thermal treatment ensures that the graphite particles and phenolic resin form a stable and uniform layer on the surface of the GF substrate, enhancing the photothermal efficiency and durability of the membrane.

### Material Characterization

The photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane prepared according to the present invention undergoes extensive characterization to evaluate its properties. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) is used to examine the surface morphology and porosity of the membrane. Water contact angles are measured to confirm the superhydrophobic nature of the membrane. Reflectance measurements are taken over the 330 to 2500 nm spectral region to determine the absorption efficiency of the membrane. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) is employed to analyze the surface chemistry of the coated samples. Solar irradiation processing is conducted under an illumination of 100 mW/cm² AM 1.5 G solar spectrum to assess the photothermal performance of the membrane.

### Applications of the SP Carbon Membrane

The photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane of the present invention finds numerous applications in various fields. In solar energy conversion, the membrane can be used to generate steam from solar radiation, making it an ideal component for solar thermal power plants. In water purification, the membrane's superhydrophobicity and photothermal properties enable efficient desalination and water treatment processes. Additionally, the membrane can be utilized in thermal management systems to improve heat dissipation and reduce thermal resistance.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel photothermal superhydrophobic (SP) carbon membrane and methods for its preparation. The SP carbon membrane combines the excellent photothermal properties of carbon materials with the superhydrophobic characteristics of fluorosilane-coated surfaces, resulting in a material that is both efficient and durable. The invention addresses the challenges associated with existing photothermal materials and offers a promising solution for various applications in solar energy conversion, water purification, and thermal management systems.
