# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics, specifically to the identification and development of small-molecule inhibitors targeting Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) and Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 1 (STAT1). More particularly, the invention provides a novel compound, C188-9, which exhibits potent inhibitory activity against both STAT3 and STAT1, and demonstrates significant therapeutic potential in the treatment of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) and other cancers characterized by aberrant STAT3 and STAT1 activation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3) is a member of a family of seven closely related proteins responsible for transmitting peptide hormone signals from the extracellular surface of cells to the nucleus. STAT3 plays a pivotal role in various cellular processes, including cell proliferation, resistance to apoptosis, metastasis, immune evasion, tumor angiogenesis, epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT), response to DNA damage, and the Warburg effect. Aberrant activation of STAT3 is observed in approximately 50% of all cancers, making it an attractive target for drug development.

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) is a type of cancer that has been shown to depend on constitutively activated STAT3 for growth. Inhibition of STAT3 has been demonstrated to result in growth inhibition of HNSCC cell lines. However, existing STAT3 inhibitors, such as Stattic, have limitations due to low maximum tolerated doses and off-target effects. Therefore, there is a significant need for more potent and selective STAT3 inhibitors that can be used clinically, either alone or in combination with other therapies, to improve treatment outcomes in HNSCC and other cancers.

Recent advancements in virtual ligand screening (VLS) and structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis have led to the identification of novel small-molecule inhibitors of STAT3. One such compound, C188, was identified through VLS and demonstrated promising inhibitory activity. Building on this foundation, the present invention describes the development of a more potent derivative, C188-9, which not only targets STAT3 but also exhibits significant inhibitory activity against STAT1, another key mediator of oncogene addiction and resistance to radiation therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel compound, C188-9, which is a potent inhibitor of both STAT3 and STAT1. C188-9 is a derivative of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide and has been optimized through 2D fingerprint screening and 3D pharmacophore analysis to enhance its inhibitory activity. The compound binds to STAT3 with high affinity (KD = 4.7 ± 0.4 nM) and is more potent than its parent compound, C188, in reducing constitutive pSTAT3 levels and inhibiting anchorage-dependent and independent growth of HNSCC cell lines. Additionally, C188-9 effectively reduces levels of constitutively activated STAT1 in HNSCC cells and tumor xenografts.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising C188-9 and methods for using these compositions to treat cancers, particularly HNSCC, characterized by aberrant STAT3 and STAT1 activation. The compositions can be administered alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents, such as radiation therapy, to enhance treatment efficacy and overcome resistance to conventional therapies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **STAT3**: Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3, a transcription factor involved in various cellular processes, including cell proliferation, resistance to apoptosis, and tumor progression.
- **STAT1**: Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 1, a transcription factor involved in immune responses and resistance to radiation therapy.
- **pSTAT3**: Phosphorylated STAT3, the active form of STAT3.
- **pSTAT1**: Phosphorylated STAT1, the active form of STAT1.
- **HNSCC**: Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, a type of cancer that affects the head and neck region.
- **VLS**: Virtual Ligand Screening, a computational method used to identify potential ligands for a target protein.
- **SAR**: Structure-Activity Relationship, the study of how changes in the chemical structure of a molecule affect its biological activity.
- **QSAR**: Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship, a computational method used to predict the biological activity of a molecule based on its chemical structure.
- **MST**: Microscale Thermophoresis, a technique used to measure the binding affinity of a ligand to a protein.
- **Luminex**: A bead-based assay used to measure the levels of multiple analytes in a single sample.
- **Phosphoflow**: A flow cytometry-based method used to measure the phosphorylation status of intracellular proteins.
- **Xenograft**: A graft of tissue transplanted from one species to another, typically used in preclinical cancer research.

### II. DERIVATIVES

The present invention is based on the identification and optimization of a novel compound, C188-9, which is a derivative of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide. C188-9 was identified through a hit-to-lead program that utilized the scaffold of C188, a previously identified small-molecule inhibitor of STAT3. The optimization process involved 2D fingerprint screening and 3D pharmacophore analysis to identify compounds with improved inhibitory activity.

The general structure of C188-9 is as follows:
\[ \text{N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide} \]

The specific structural modifications that enhance the inhibitory activity of C188-9 include the addition of a triazole-3-yl-mercapto group at the 3-position of the naphthylamine ring. This modification, along with other structural variations, contributes to the high affinity and potency of C188-9 in inhibiting STAT3 and STAT1.

### III. EMBODIMENTS FOR TARGETING STAT3

C188-9 is a potent inhibitor of STAT3, as demonstrated by its ability to reduce constitutive pSTAT3 levels in HNSCC cell lines and tumor xenografts. The compound binds to the pY-binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain with high affinity (KD = 4.7 ± 0.4 nM), as measured by microscale thermophoresis (MST). This binding affinity is comparable to that of the natural ligand, EGFR pY1068-peptide (KD = 1.1 ± 0.1 nM).

In vitro studies using phosphoflow and Luminex assays revealed that C188-9 is more potent than C188 in inhibiting G-CSF-induced pSTAT3 levels (IC50 = 3.7 μM vs. 16.2 μM, respectively). C188-9 also demonstrated superior activity in reducing constitutive pSTAT3 levels in HNSCC cell lines, with IC50 values ranging from 10.5 to 22.8 μM. Additionally, C188-9 inhibited anchorage-dependent and independent growth of HNSCC cell lines, with IC50 values ranging from 0.7 to 14.8 μM.

In vivo studies using HNSCC xenograft models further confirmed the therapeutic potential of C188-9. Treatment of nude mice bearing UM-SCC-17B xenografts with C188-9 (50 mg/kg/day) significantly reduced tumor growth and pSTAT3 levels within the tumors. In contrast, treatment with C188 (50 mg/kg/day) did not significantly affect tumor growth or pSTAT3 levels.

### IV. TARGETING CANCER STEM CELLS

C188-9 not only targets STAT3 but also has the potential to target cancer stem cells (CSCs), which are a subpopulation of cells within tumors that are responsible for cancer initiation, maintenance, and relapse. STAT3 is a key mediator of CSC self-renewal and maintenance, and its inhibition can lead to the eradication of CSCs.

RNA sequencing analysis of tumor xenografts treated with C188-9 revealed the downregulation of several genes involved in CSC maintenance and self-renewal, including SOX2, OCT4, and NANOG. These findings suggest that C188-9 may have the potential to eliminate CSCs and prevent cancer recurrence.

### V. COMBINATION THERAPY

C188-9 can be used in combination with other therapeutic agents to enhance treatment efficacy and overcome resistance to conventional therapies. For example, combination therapy with radiation therapy has shown promise in preclinical models of HNSCC. RNA sequencing analysis of HNSCC xenografts treated with C188-9 revealed the downregulation of several genes involved in radiation resistance, including OASL, IFIT3, MX2, and IRF7. These genes are known to be positively regulated by STAT1, and their downregulation by C188-9 suggests that the compound may enhance the sensitivity of HNSCC cells to radiation therapy.

Additionally, C188-9 can be combined with other targeted therapies, such as EGFR inhibitors, to achieve synergistic effects. Preclinical studies have shown that combination therapy with C188-9 and cetuximab, an EGFR inhibitor, resulted in enhanced growth inhibition of HNSCC xenografts compared to monotherapy with either agent alone.

### VI. PHARMACEUTICAL COMPOSITIONS

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising C188-9 and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and topical. The compositions can be administered in a single dose or in multiple doses over a period of time, depending on the specific treatment regimen.

## EXAMPLES

### Exemplary Materials and Methods

#### Cell Lines

HNSCC cell lines (SCC-9, SCC-15, HN5, UM-SCC-1, HN30, HN31, SCC-61, SQ-20B, SCC-35, and UM-SCC-17B) were obtained from various sources and authenticated by genotyping. The cells were maintained in appropriate media supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and other common additives.

#### 2-D Fingerprint Screening

Using the scaffold of C188 as a reference structure, 2-D fingerprint screening was performed using the Life Chemicals database, which contained over 490,000 compounds. The Tanimoto coefficient/Unity/Sybyl/Tripos was used as the similarity comparing method, and 100% similarity was set as the cutoff.

#### 3-D Pharmacophore Sorting

The 207 compounds resulting from 2-D fingerprint screening were converted into 3-D structures for analysis. The molecular features of these compounds were combined, and the 3D pharmacophore structures were compared using a matrix that incorporated the structural features as distance bins. Compounds were ranked in decreasing order of pharmacophore similarity, and the top 39 compounds with pharmacophore scores greater than 70 were purchased for further testing.

#### QSAR Studies

3-D quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) studies were performed using the program Phase in Schrödinger. The 40 compounds were built and their energy and geometry minimized using the OPLS-2005 force field in Maestro. The aligned ligands were imported into the Phase program, and a partial least squares (PLS) method was applied to correlate the STAT3 inhibitory activities (pIC50) of these compounds with the Phase field data calculated based on their aligned 3-D structures.

#### STAT3/Py-Peptide Binding Using a Surface Plasmon Resonance (SPR) Assay

Binding of STAT3 (200 nM in 20 mM Tris buffer, pH 8) pre-incubated without or with C188/C188-9 to phosphorylated and control non-phosphorylated biotinylated EGFR-derived dodecapeptides based on the sequence surrounding Y1068 was measured using a Biacore 3000 biosensor.

#### Phosphoflow Assay

Phosphoflow analysis was performed using Kasumi-1 cells. The cells were serum-starved, pre-treated with compound or DMSO (1 hour, RT), and then stimulated with G-CSF (10 ng/ml) for 15 minutes at 37°C. The cells were permeabilized and stained with Alexa647-pSTAT3, Alexa488-pSTAT5, and PE-pSTAT1 antibodies and analyzed using a BD LSR2 flow cytometer.

#### Luminex Bead-Based Assay

Luminex bead-based assays were used to determine levels of pSTAT1, pSTAT3, and GAPDH. The assays were performed as described in the literature.

#### Microscale Thermophoresis

Binding of C188-9 or EGFR Tyr(P)-1068 phosphopeptide to WT STAT3β (127-722) was measured by microscale thermophoresis (MST). The movement of STAT3 under a temperature gradient was recorded, and the dissociation constant (KD) was calculated.

#### Anchorage-Independent and Dependent Cell Growth

Cells were cultured in triplicates in ultra-low attachment 96-well plates for 72 hours or cell-culture treated plates for 48 hours in complete DMEM ± drug. Viable cells were quantitated using MTT, and optical density (OD) was measured at 590 nm.

#### UM-SCC-17B Xenografts

UM-SCC-17B cells (1.5 × 10^6) were injected into the tongues of athymic, 8-10 week-old, male, nude mice. Once tumors were established, mice were randomized to receive 5 times a week, intraperitoneal injections of either DMSO or C188 (50 mg/kg) or C188-9 (100 mg/kg). Tumor volumes were measured twice weekly, and average tumor volumes were calculated and normalized to the volume at the first day of treatment.

#### RNA Extraction and Sequencing Using RNA-Seq

Tissue was cryosliced, and total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Microarray tissue kit followed by DNase digestion and Qiagen RNeasy column purification. High-quality RNA was processed using an Illumina TruSeq RNA sample prep kit, and RNA sequencing was performed.

#### PK Studies in Mice

Pharmacokinetic (PK) studies to assess plasma levels of C188-9 following IP and oral routes were performed in C57BL/6 mice. PK studies to assess tumor vs. plasma levels of C188-9 were performed in nude mice bearing xenograft tumors.

#### Statistical Analysis

Student's t-test or a paired t-test was used to compare control- and C188-9 treated groups as indicated.

### Identification by VLS of Compounds that Blocked Stat3 Binding to its Phosphopeptide Ligand and Inhibited IL-6-Mediated Phosphorylation of STAT3

Virtual ligand screening (VLS) of 920,000 compounds from 8 chemical libraries was performed to identify potential inhibitors of STAT3. The pY-binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain was used as the target site. Three hits—C3, C30, and C188—were identified as direct STAT3 inhibitors. C188 demonstrated the greatest activity in inhibiting STAT3 binding to its phosphopeptide ligand and in inhibiting IL-6-mediated phosphorylation of STAT3.

### Compound-Mediated Inhibition of Ligand-Stimulated Phosphorylation of STAT3 is Specific for Stat3 Vs. Stat1

Further studies were conducted to determine the specificity of C188 and its derivatives for STAT3 versus STAT1. Phosphoflow and Luminex assays revealed that C188-9, a derivative of C188, was more potent in inhibiting G-CSF-induced pSTAT3 levels compared to C188. Additionally, C188-9 was effective at reducing levels of constitutively activated STAT1 in HNSCC cells and tumor xenografts, demonstrating its dual inhibitory activity against both STAT3 and STAT1.

### Sequence Analysis and Molecular Modeling of the Interaction of Each Compound with the Stat3 Vs. Stat1 SH2 Domain

Sequence analysis and molecular modeling were performed to understand the interaction of C188-9 with the SH2 domains of STAT3 and STAT1. The high affinity of C188-9 for STAT3 (KD = 4.7 ± 0.4 nM) and its ability to reduce pSTAT3 levels in HNSCC cells and tumor xenografts were confirmed. The molecular modeling also revealed that C188-9 interacts with the SH2 domain of STAT1 with similar affinity, explaining its dual inhibitory activity.

### Example 5

RNA sequencing analysis of tumor xenografts treated with C188-9 revealed the downregulation of several genes involved in oncogenesis, chemoresistance, and radioresistance. The analysis also uncovered the modulation of many STAT1-regulated genes, including those involved in radiation resistance. These findings suggest that C188-9 targets both STAT3 and STAT1, potentially overcoming resistance to radiation therapy in HNSCC.
