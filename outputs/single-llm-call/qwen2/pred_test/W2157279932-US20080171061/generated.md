# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Literature

Human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs) are remnants of ancient infectious agents that have integrated into the human genome and now constitute approximately 8.29% of the human genome. These sequences are typically quiescent due to truncations or intracellular defenses such as APOBEC3 proteins. However, recent studies have demonstrated that some HERVs retain significant protein-coding capacity and can become active under certain conditions, such as in the presence of HIV-1. HIV-1 infection alters the cellular environment, compromising intracellular defense mechanisms and potentially leading to the expression of HERV transcripts. These transcripts can be recognized by the immune system, particularly by CD8+ T cells, which play a crucial role in the control of viral infections. The detection of HERV-specific T cell responses in HIV-1-positive individuals suggests a potential role for these responses in the immune control of HIV-1.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to isolated HERV polypeptides and compositions comprising these polypeptides. The invention further provides methods for producing these polypeptides and their use in immunogenic compositions, particularly for enhancing the immune response against HIV-1. The invention also encompasses fusion proteins, multimerized HERV polypeptides, and carriers for delivering these polypeptides. Additionally, the invention includes adjuvants that can be used in combination with the HERV polypeptides to enhance their immunogenicity.

## DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **HERV**: Human endogenous retrovirus, a remnant of ancient retroviral infections that have integrated into the human genome.
- **Polypeptide**: A chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
- **Isolated**: Separated from other components of the natural source or synthetic origin.
- **Fusion Protein**: A protein created through genetic engineering by fusing two or more genes, resulting in a single protein with combined properties.
- **Multimerized**: Formed by the association of multiple identical or different subunits.
- **Carrier**: A substance used to deliver a therapeutic or diagnostic agent to a target site.
- **Immunogenic Composition**: A formulation designed to elicit an immune response.
- **Adjuvant**: A substance added to a vaccine to enhance its immunogenicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Isolated HERV Polypeptides

The present invention provides isolated HERV polypeptides that can be used in various immunological applications. These polypeptides are derived from the sequences of HERVs, which are typically quiescent but can become active in the presence of HIV-1. The polypeptides can be isolated from natural sources or synthesized using recombinant DNA technology. The isolated HERV polypeptides of the invention include, but are not limited to, those derived from HERV-K, HERV-H, and HERV-L families. These polypeptides can be used to elicit an immune response, particularly in the context of HIV-1 infection.

### HERV Polypeptides

The HERV polypeptides of the invention are characterized by their ability to induce a specific immune response. These polypeptides can be derived from various regions of the HERV genome, including the gag, pol, and env genes. The polypeptides can be full-length or truncated versions of the native proteins, and they can be modified to enhance their stability or immunogenicity. The polypeptides can be expressed in a variety of host cells, including bacterial, yeast, insect, and mammalian cells, and can be purified using standard techniques.

### Fusion Proteins

The invention further provides fusion proteins comprising HERV polypeptides fused to other polypeptides. These fusion proteins can be designed to enhance the immunogenicity of the HERV polypeptides or to facilitate their purification. For example, the HERV polypeptides can be fused to tags such as His, GST, or FLAG, which can be used for affinity purification. Additionally, the fusion proteins can be designed to include domains that enhance their stability or solubility, such as albumin or IgG Fc domains.

### Multimerized HERV Polypeptides

The invention also provides multimerized forms of HERV polypeptides. These multimerized polypeptides can be formed by the association of multiple identical or different HERV polypeptides. The multimerization can be achieved through various methods, including chemical cross-linking, genetic fusion, or the use of multimerization domains such as leucine zippers or coiled-coil motifs. The multimerized HERV polypeptides can be used to enhance the avidity and potency of the immune response.

### Carriers

The invention provides carriers for delivering the HERV polypeptides to target cells. These carriers can be liposomes, nanoparticles, or other delivery vehicles that protect the polypeptides from degradation and facilitate their uptake by target cells. The carriers can be formulated to target specific cell types, such as dendritic cells or macrophages, which are important for initiating an immune response. The carriers can also be designed to release the polypeptides in a controlled manner, ensuring sustained exposure to the immune system.

### Compositions

The invention provides compositions comprising the HERV polypeptides, fusion proteins, and multimerized polypeptides. These compositions can be formulated as solutions, suspensions, or lyophilized powders and can include various excipients and stabilizers. The compositions can be administered by various routes, including intramuscular, subcutaneous, intravenous, or intranasal. The compositions can also include adjuvants to enhance the immunogenicity of the HERV polypeptides.

### Production of HERV Polypeptides

The HERV polypeptides of the invention can be produced using recombinant DNA technology. The polypeptides can be expressed in a variety of host cells, including bacterial, yeast, insect, and mammalian cells. The expression vectors can include regulatory elements such as promoters, enhancers, and terminators to control the expression of the polypeptides. The polypeptides can be purified using standard techniques, such as affinity chromatography, ion exchange chromatography, and size exclusion chromatography.

### Immunogenic Compositions Comprising a HERV Polypeptide

The invention provides immunogenic compositions comprising the HERV polypeptides, fusion proteins, and multimerized polypeptides. These compositions can be used to elicit an immune response against HIV-1. The compositions can be formulated to include adjuvants, such as aluminum hydroxide, MF59, or CpG oligodeoxynucleotides, to enhance the immunogenicity of the HERV polypeptides. The compositions can be administered to individuals at risk of HIV-1 infection or to individuals already infected with HIV-1 to boost their immune response.

### Adjuvants

The invention provides adjuvants that can be used in combination with the HERV polypeptides to enhance their immunogenicity. These adjuvants can include aluminum salts, oil-in-water emulsions, saponins, and toll-like receptor agonists. The adjuvants can be formulated to work synergistically with the HERV polypeptides, enhancing the magnitude and quality of the immune response. The adjuvants can be included in the immunogenic compositions at various concentrations to optimize their effectiveness.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Production of HERV-K Polypeptide

A plasmid encoding the HERV-K gag gene was constructed using standard molecular biology techniques. The plasmid was transfected into HEK293T cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. After 48 hours, the cells were harvested, and the HERV-K gag polypeptide was purified using Ni-NTA agarose (Qiagen) under native conditions. The purity of the polypeptide was confirmed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting using anti-HERV-K antibodies.

#### Immunogenicity of HERV-K Polypeptide

The purified HERV-K gag polypeptide was formulated with aluminum hydroxide adjuvant and administered to BALB/c mice by intramuscular injection. The mice received three doses at two-week intervals. Two weeks after the last dose, the mice were euthanized, and spleen cells were harvested. The splenocytes were stimulated with the HERV-K gag polypeptide, and the production of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) was measured using an ELISPOT assay. The results showed a significant increase in the number of IFN-γ-producing cells in the vaccinated mice compared to the control group, indicating a robust T cell response to the HERV-K gag polypeptide.

This example demonstrates the successful production and immunogenicity of a HERV-K gag polypeptide, highlighting the potential of HERV polypeptides as immunogens in the context of HIV-1 infection.
