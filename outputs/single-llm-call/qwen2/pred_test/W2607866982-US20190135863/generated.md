# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel retro-inverso (RI) peptides that inhibit the interaction between the urinary plasminogen activator receptor (uPAR) and the formyl-peptide receptor 1 (FPR1). These peptides are designed to prevent cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis, which are critical steps in the metastatic process of solid tumors, particularly sarcomas. Despite significant advancements in cancer therapy, metastasis remains a leading cause of mortality in cancer patients. The development of effective anti-metastatic agents is therefore of paramount importance.

The uPAR is a glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored protein that plays a crucial role in cell migration and invasion by promoting proteolysis and intracellular signaling. The uPAR interacts with the formyl-peptide receptor 1 (FPR1), a G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR), to mediate these processes. Disrupting the uPAR/FPR1 interaction is a promising strategy for developing anti-metastatic therapies. However, existing uPAR-targeted therapeutics have limitations, including instability in human serum and potential toxicity.

To address these issues, the present invention utilizes the retro-inverso (RI) approach, where the sequence and chirality of the parent peptide are inverted, resulting in a peptide with high topochemical equivalence but enhanced stability to proteases. The RI peptides of the present invention are designed to inhibit the uPAR/FPR1 interaction, thereby reducing cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis, and ultimately preventing metastasis in solid tumors.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a series of retro-inverso (RI) peptides that inhibit the interaction between the urinary plasminogen activator receptor (uPAR) and the formyl-peptide receptor 1 (FPR1). These peptides are designed to prevent cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis, which are essential steps in the metastatic process of solid tumors, particularly sarcomas. The RI peptides are stable in human serum and exhibit potent inhibitory activity against the uPAR/FPR1 interaction.

### Peptide Design

The RI peptides of the present invention are based on the structure-activity relationship (SAR) of previously described uPAR/FPR1 inhibitors. Specifically, the parent peptides are tetrapeptides of the general formula Ac-Arg-X1-Arg-X2-NH2, where X1 and X2 are amino acids that influence the biological activity. The RI peptides are synthesized by inverting the sequence and chirality of the parent peptides, resulting in all-D amino acid compositions. The sequences of the RI peptides are as follows:

- **RI-1**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Glu-(D)-Arg-NH2
- **RI-2**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Ser-(D)-Arg-NH2
- **RI-3**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-Aib-(D)-Arg-NH2
- **RI-4**: Phenyl-Acetyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2
- **RI-5**: 3-Phenyl-Propionyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2

### Biological Activity

The RI peptides were tested for their ability to inhibit cell migration and invasion using human fibrosarcoma HT1080 cells. The results showed that all RI peptides failed to elicit cell migration, unlike the formyl-methionyl-leucyl-phenylalanine (fMLF) peptide, which is a known agonist of FPR1. The RI peptides also inhibited FBS-directed cell migration and matrigel invasion. Among the RI peptides, RI-3 was the most potent, reducing cell migration by 57% and cell invasion by 51%.

### Conformational Analysis

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed to understand the structural basis of the inhibitory effect of the RI peptides. The simulations revealed that RI-3 forms a stable turn structure, with a characteristic β-turn hydrogen bond between the CO group of D-Tyr1 and the NH group of D-Arg4. This turn structure is similar to that observed in the parent antagonists, suggesting that RI-3 can effectively mimic the pharmacophore of the parent peptides.

### Inhibition of Agonist/FPR1 Interaction

The RI peptides were tested for their ability to block the interaction between fMLF and FPR1 using rat basophilic leukemia (RBL-2H3) cells stably expressing FPR1. RI-3 was found to prevent the binding of FITC-fMLF to FPR1, inhibit FPR1 internalization, and block fMLF-dependent intracellular protein phosphorylation, including Akt, p38 MAPK, and ERK1/2. These results indicate that RI-3 competes with fMLF for binding to FPR1 and effectively inhibits FPR1 signaling.

### Stability in Human Serum

The stability of RI-3 in human serum was evaluated by assessing its inhibitory activity on cell migration and invasion after pre-incubation in serum. RI-3 retained 81% of its inhibitory activity after 18 hours of incubation in human serum, demonstrating its stability to proteolytic degradation.

### Lack of Cytotoxicity

To ensure that the reduction in cell migration and invasion by RI-3 is not due to cytotoxic effects, the peptides were tested for their impact on cell proliferation using the MTS assay. RI-3 did not affect cell proliferation, indicating that its inhibitory effects are specific to cell migration and invasion.

### Prevention of Matrigel Invasion by Sarcoma Cells

The ability of RI-3 to prevent matrigel invasion by sarcoma cells derived from different tissues was evaluated using human fibrosarcoma HT1080, osteosarcoma Saos-2, and chondrosarcoma Sarc cells. RI-3 significantly reduced matrigel invasion by HT1080 and Sarc cells by 64% and 47%, respectively. The effect was less pronounced in Saos-2 cells, which express low levels of uPAR, indicating that the inhibitory activity of RI-3 is dependent on the co-expression of uPAR and FPR1.

### Inhibition of Endothelial Tube Formation and Trans-Endothelial Migration

RI-3 was tested for its ability to inhibit endothelial tube formation and trans-endothelial migration of sarcoma cells. RI-3 significantly reduced FBS- and VEGF-dependent endothelial tube formation by 61% and 75%, respectively. Additionally, RI-3 prevented the adhesion of Sarc cells to the endothelium and reduced trans-endothelial migration by 24%.

### Anti-Metastatic Effects in Vivo

The anti-metastatic effects of RI-3 were evaluated in a xenograft model using nude mice injected with human chondrosarcoma Sarc cells. RI-3 significantly reduced tumor growth, intra-tumoral microvessel density, and the number of circulating tumor cells (CTCs). Furthermore, RI-3 prevented the formation of lung metastases, demonstrating its efficacy in inhibiting the metastatic process in vivo.

## EXAMPLE 1. SYNTHESIS OF THE RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES RI-1, RI-2, RI-3, RI-4, RI-5

The retro-inverso (RI) peptides were synthesized using solid-phase Fmoc/t-Bu chemistry. The peptides were purified by reversed-phase HPLC and characterized by UPLC-MS. The sequences and molecular weights of the RI peptides are as follows:

- **RI-1**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Glu-(D)-Arg-NH2 (MW: 663.7, MH+: 664.4)
- **RI-2**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Ser-(D)-Arg-NH2 (MW: 619.7, MH+: 620.4)
- **RI-3**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-Aib-(D)-Arg-NH2 (MW: 621.3, MH+: 622.3)
- **RI-4**: Phenyl-Acetyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2 (MW: 533.1, MH+: 533.5)
- **RI-5**: 3-Phenyl-Propionyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2 (MW: 547.1, MH+: 547.5)

## EXAMPLE 2. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES DO NOT PROMOTE CELL MIGRATION

The ability of the RI peptides to promote cell migration was assessed using human fibrosarcoma HT1080 cells. The cells were allowed to migrate toward 10 nM fMLF or 10 nM of the indicated RI peptides in Boyden chambers. The results showed that all RI peptides failed to elicit cell migration, unlike fMLF, which induced significant cell migration.

## EXAMPLE 3. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INTERFERES WITH FPR1 BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY BY BLOCKING AGONIST/FPR1 INTERACTION AND BY PREVENTING AGONIST-INDUCED FPR1 INTERNALIZATION

The ability of RI-3 to interfere with FPR1 biological activity was evaluated using rat basophilic leukemia (RBL-2H3) cells stably expressing FPR1. RI-3 was found to prevent the binding of FITC-fMLF to FPR1, inhibit FPR1 internalization, and block fMLF-dependent intracellular protein phosphorylation, including Akt, p38 MAPK, and ERK1/2. These results indicate that RI-3 competes with fMLF for binding to FPR1 and effectively inhibits FPR1 signaling.

## EXAMPLE 4. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL MIGRATION IN A DOSE DEPENDENT MANNER

The dose-dependent inhibitory effect of RI-3 on cell migration was assessed using HT1080 cells. The cells were allowed to migrate toward 10% FBS in the presence of increasing concentrations of RI-3. The results showed that RI-3 inhibits cell migration in a dose-dependent manner, with an overall 50% reduction of cell migration at 1 × 10^-13 M RI-3.

## EXAMPLE 5. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL INVASION IN A DOSE DEPENDENT MANNER

The dose-dependent inhibitory effect of RI-3 on cell invasion was assessed using HT1080 cells. The cells were allowed to invade matrigel in real-time for 20 hours toward 10% FBS in the presence of increasing concentrations of RI-3. The results showed that RI-3 inhibits cell invasion in a dose-dependent manner, with an overall 50% reduction of cell invasion at 1 × 10^-11 M RI-3.

## EXAMPLE 6. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 DOES NOT AFFECT CELL PROLIFERATION

The effect of RI-3 on cell proliferation was assessed using the MTS assay. HT1080 cells were seeded in plates and allowed to proliferate in the presence or absence of 10 μM RI-3. The results showed that RI-3 does not affect cell proliferation, indicating that its inhibitory effects are specific to cell migration and invasion.

## EXAMPLE 7. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 IS STABLE IN HUMAN SERUM

The stability of RI-3 in human serum was evaluated by assessing its inhibitory activity on cell migration and invasion after pre-incubation in serum. HT1080 cells were allowed to migrate or invade matrigel in the presence of 10 nM RI-3 pre-incubated in human serum for 18 hours. The results showed that RI-3 retains 81% of its inhibitory activity after 18 hours of incubation in human serum, demonstrating its stability to proteolytic degradation.

### EXAMPLE 8. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS MATRIGEL INVASION BY HUMAN SARCOMA, MELANOMA AND EPITHELIAL OVARIAN CARCINOMA CELLS

The ability of RI-3 to prevent matrigel invasion by sarcoma cells derived from different tissues was evaluated using human fibrosarcoma HT1080, osteosarcoma Saos-2, and chondrosarcoma Sarc cells. ### EXAMPLE 9. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS IN VITRO TRANS-ENDOTHELIAL MIGRATION BY SARCOMA, MELANOMA AND EPITHELIAL OVARIAN CARCINOMA CELLS AS WELL AS BY HUMAN MONOCYTES

The ability of RI-3 to prevent trans-endothelial migration of sarcoma cells was assessed using human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) and Sarc cells. RI-3 significantly reduced trans-endothelial migration by 24%, indicating its potential to prevent the entry of tumor cells into the bloodstream.

### EXAMPLE 10. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INHIBITS BOTH FBS AND VEGF-TRIGGERED ENDOTHELIAL TUBE FORMATION

The ability of RI-3 to inhibit endothelial tube formation was assessed using HUVECs. The cells were suspended in EBM medium with 10% FBS or 40 ng/mL VEGF165, with or without 10 nM RI-3, and seeded on matrigel. The results showed that RI-3 significantly reduced FBS- and VEGF-dependent endothelial tube formation by 61% and 75%, respectively.
