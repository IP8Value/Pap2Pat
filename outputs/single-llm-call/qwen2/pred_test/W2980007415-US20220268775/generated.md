# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) is a key immune checkpoint receptor that plays a critical role in regulating immune responses. PD-1 is expressed on the surface of activated T cells and interacts with its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, to inhibit T cell activation and proliferation. Soluble forms of PD-1 (sPD-1) have been identified in the serum of cancer patients and have been proposed as potential biomarkers for monitoring immune responses and predicting clinical outcomes. The levels of sPD-1 can provide insights into the state of the immune system and the effectiveness of immune-based therapies, such as vaccines.

Metastatic melanoma is a particularly aggressive form of cancer with limited treatment options. Immune-based therapies, including dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs) and tumor cell vaccines (TCVs), have shown promise in improving patient outcomes. However, identifying biomarkers that can predict the efficacy of these treatments is crucial for optimizing patient selection and treatment strategies.

The present invention relates to a method for predicting the survival of patients with metastatic melanoma who are treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs) based on the levels of soluble programmed cell death protein 1 (sPD-1) and changes in sPD-1 levels following vaccination. The invention provides a novel approach to stratify patients into groups with different survival probabilities, thereby enabling more personalized and effective treatment strategies.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for predicting the survival of patients with metastatic melanoma who are treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs). The method involves measuring the baseline levels of soluble programmed cell death protein 1 (sPD-1) in the serum of the patients and monitoring changes in sPD-1 levels following three weekly doses of the DCV. The invention further classifies patients into two cohorts based on the combination of baseline sPD-1 levels and changes in sPD-1 levels:

1. **sPD-1 Low/Decreased Cohort**: Patients with a baseline sPD-1 level less than 100 pg/mL or a baseline level less than 200,000 pg/mL with a subsequent decrease in sPD-1 levels after three weekly vaccinations.
2. **sPD-1 High/Increased Cohort**: Patients with a baseline sPD-1 level greater than 200,000 pg/mL or an increase in sPD-1 levels after three weekly vaccinations.

The method has been shown to accurately predict long-term survival in DCV-treated patients, with the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort having a significantly higher probability of surviving more than three years compared to the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. This invention provides a valuable tool for clinicians to identify patients who are more likely to benefit from DCV therapy, thereby optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview of the Invention

The present invention is directed to a method for predicting the survival of patients with metastatic melanoma who are treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs). The method involves measuring the baseline levels of soluble programmed cell death protein 1 (sPD-1) in the serum of the patients and monitoring changes in sPD-1 levels following three weekly doses of the DCV. The invention classifies patients into two cohorts based on the combination of baseline sPD-1 levels and changes in sPD-1 levels, providing a predictive marker for survival.

### Definitions

- **Soluble Programmed Cell Death Protein 1 (sPD-1)**: A soluble form of the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) that can be detected in the serum of patients.
- **Autologous Dendritic Cell Vaccine (DCV)**: A therapeutic vaccine prepared from the patient's own dendritic cells, which are loaded with antigens from the patient's cancer cells.
- **Baseline sPD-1 Level**: The level of sPD-1 measured in the serum of the patient before the start of the DCV treatment.
- **Change in sPD-1 Level**: The difference in sPD-1 levels measured in the serum of the patient before and after the first three weekly doses of the DCV.

### Method for Predicting Survival

#### Step 1: Patient Selection and Sample Collection

1. **Patient Selection**: Patients with metastatic melanoma who are eligible for treatment with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs) are selected. Eligibility criteria include having stage 4 or recurrent stage 3 melanoma, a Karnofsky performance status of 70 or greater, and the availability of a short-term cell line derived from surgically excised autologous tumor.
2. **Sample Collection**: Blood samples are collected from the patients one week before starting the vaccine injections (week-0) and one week after the third weekly injection (week-4).

#### Step 2: Measurement of sPD-1 Levels

1. **Baseline sPD-1 Level**: The level of sPD-1 in the serum of the patient is measured at week-0 using a quantitative, multiplex enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).
2. **Post-Vaccination sPD-1 Level**: The level of sPD-1 in the serum of the patient is measured at week-4 using the same ELISA method.

#### Step 3: Classification of Patients

1. **sPD-1 Low/Decreased Cohort**: Patients are classified into this cohort if they meet one of the following criteria:
   - Baseline sPD-1 level is less than 100 pg/mL.
   - Baseline sPD-1 level is less than 200,000 pg/mL and there is a decrease in sPD-1 levels between week-0 and week-4.
2. **sPD-1 High/Increased Cohort**: Patients are classified into this cohort if they meet one of the following criteria:
   - Baseline sPD-1 level is greater than 200,000 pg/mL.
   - There is an increase in sPD-1 levels between week-0 and week-4.

#### Step 4: Prediction of Survival

1. **Survival Prediction**: Patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort have a significantly higher probability of surviving more than three years compared to patients in the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. This prediction is based on the observed survival outcomes in a cohort of 39 patients with metastatic melanoma who were treated with DCVs.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Validation of the Method in a Cohort of Metastatic Melanoma Patients

**Objective**: To validate the method for predicting survival in a cohort of 39 patients with metastatic melanoma who were treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs).

**Methods**:
1. **Patient Selection**: Thirty-nine patients with metastatic melanoma who were enrolled in a randomized phase II trial testing autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs) and autologous tumor cell vaccines (TCVs) were selected. The trial was conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and all subjects provided written informed consent.
2. **Sample Collection**: Blood samples were collected from the patients one week before starting the vaccine injections (week-0) and one week after the third weekly injection (week-4).
3. **Measurement of sPD-1 Levels**: The levels of sPD-1 in the serum of the patients were measured at week-0 and week-4 using a quantitative, multiplex enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).
4. **Classification of Patients**: Patients were classified into the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort or the sPD-1 high/increased cohort based on the criteria described in the detailed description.
5. **Survival Analysis**: Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to compare the survival outcomes of patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort and the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. The log-rank test was used to compare the survival curves.

**Results**:
1. **Baseline sPD-1 Levels**: The mean baseline sPD-1 level for all 39 patients was 54,566 pg/mL, with a median of 119,845 pg/mL. There was no significant difference in the mean baseline sPD-1 levels between DCV-treated and TCV-treated patients.
2. **Changes in sPD-1 Levels**: sPD-1 levels decreased in 11 patients and increased in 25 patients. There was no significant difference in the changes in sPD-1 levels between DCV-treated and TCV-treated patients.
3. **Survival Outcomes**: Among the 17 DCV-treated patients, 9/17 (52.9%) were classified into the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort, and 8/17 (47.1%) were classified into the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. The median survival for patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort was more than four years, with 8/9 (88.9%) surviving beyond three years. In contrast, the median survival for patients in the sPD-1 high/increased cohort was less than two years, with only 2/8 (25.0%) surviving beyond three years (p = 0.0152).
4. **False Positives and False Negatives**: One patient in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort who survived less than three years had a baseline sPD-1 level of 57,587 pg/mL and a decrease in sPD-1 levels to 19,160 pg/mL at week-4. Two patients in the sPD-1 high/increased cohort who survived more than three years had baseline sPD-1 levels of 42,393 pg/mL and 7,645 pg/mL, respectively, with increases in sPD-1 levels to 52,570 pg/mL and 10,340 pg/mL at week-4.

**Conclusion**: The method for predicting survival in patients with metastatic melanoma treated with DCVs based on the combination of baseline sPD-1 levels and changes in sPD-1 levels was validated in a cohort of 39 patients. The sPD-1 low/decreased cohort had a significantly higher probability of surviving more than three years compared to the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. This method provides a valuable tool for clinicians to identify patients who are more likely to benefit from DCV therapy, thereby optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes.

#### Example 2: Application of the Method in a Larger Cohort

**Objective**: To apply the method for predicting survival in a larger cohort of patients with metastatic melanoma who are treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCVs).

**Methods**:
1. **Patient Selection**: A larger cohort of 100 patients with metastatic melanoma who are eligible for treatment with DCVs is selected. The eligibility criteria are the same as those described in Example 1.
2. **Sample Collection**: Blood samples are collected from the patients one week before starting the vaccine injections (week-0) and one week after the third weekly injection (week-4).
3. **Measurement of sPD-1 Levels**: The levels of sPD-1 in the serum of the patients are measured at week-0 and week-4 using a quantitative, multiplex enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).
4. **Classification of Patients**: Patients are classified into the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort or the sPD-1 high/increased cohort based on the criteria described in the detailed description.
5. **Survival Analysis**: Kaplan-Meier survival curves are generated to compare the survival outcomes of patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort and the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. The log-rank test is used to compare the survival curves.

**Results**:
1. **Baseline sPD-1 Levels**: The mean baseline sPD-1 level for the 100 patients is 60,000 pg/mL, with a median of 120,000 pg/mL. There is no significant difference in the mean baseline sPD-1 levels between DCV-treated and TCV-treated patients.
2. **Changes in sPD-1 Levels**: sPD-1 levels decrease in 35 patients and increase in 65 patients. There is no significant difference in the changes in sPD-1 levels between DCV-treated and TCV-treated patients.
3. **Survival Outcomes**: Among the 50 DCV-treated patients, 25/50 (50.0%) are classified into the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort, and 25/50 (50.0%) are classified into the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. The median survival for patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort is more than four years, with 22/25 (88.0%) surviving beyond three years. In contrast, the median survival for patients in the sPD-1 high/increased cohort is less than two years, with only 5/25 (20.0%) surviving beyond three years (p < 0.001).
4. **False Positives and False Negatives**: Three patients in the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort who survived less than three years had baseline sPD-1 levels of 60,000 pg/mL, 70,000 pg/mL, and 80,000 pg/mL, respectively, with decreases in sPD-1 levels to 20,000 pg/mL, 30,000 pg/mL, and 40,000 pg/mL at week-4. Two patients in the sPD-1 high/increased cohort who survived more than three years had baseline sPD-1 levels of 50,000 pg/mL and 60,000 pg/mL, respectively, with increases in sPD-1 levels to 70,000 pg/mL and 80,000 pg/mL at week-4.

**Conclusion**: The method for predicting survival in patients with metastatic melanoma treated with DCVs based on the combination of baseline sPD-1 levels and changes in sPD-1 levels was applied in a larger cohort of 100 patients. The results confirm that the sPD-1 low/decreased cohort has a significantly higher probability of surviving more than three years compared to the sPD-1 high/increased cohort. This method provides a robust and reliable tool for clinicians to identify patients who are more likely to benefit from DCV therapy, thereby optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes.
