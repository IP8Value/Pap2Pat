# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The intestinal epithelium serves as a critical barrier to protect the host from pathogens and toxins present in the gut lumen. Disruption of this barrier can lead to the passage of harmful substances, initiating and exacerbating various diseases and possibly contributing to the aging process. Oxidative stress, particularly the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as superoxide, has been implicated as a significant driver of intestinal barrier dysfunction. However, the specific sources and mechanisms of ROS production in this context remain poorly understood.

This invention addresses the issue of intestinal barrier disruption by targeting the production of superoxide from complex III of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. Specifically, the invention utilizes small-molecule Suppressors of site IIIQo Electron Leak (S3QELs) to inhibit superoxide production, thereby reducing intestinal permeability and extending lifespan in model organisms. The invention provides a novel therapeutic approach to prevent and treat intestinal barrier dysfunction and associated diseases.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and composition for reducing intestinal permeability and extending lifespan by inhibiting the production of superoxide from complex III of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. The invention utilizes specific small-molecule Suppressors of site IIIQo Electron Leak (S3QELs) to achieve this goal. The S3QELs are effective in reducing intestinal permeability, decreasing the number of apoptotic cells, and increasing median lifespan in both Drosophila and mice fed high-nutrient diets. The invention also includes pharmaceutical formulations and kits containing the S3QELs for therapeutic use.

## Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Intestinal Permeability**: The ability of substances to pass through the intestinal epithelial barrier, which can be assessed using various assays such as the "smurf assay" in Drosophila or the uptake of FITC-dextran in mice.
- **Superoxide**: A highly reactive oxygen species (ROS) formed by the one-electron reduction of molecular oxygen.
- **Complex III**: A component of the mitochondrial electron transport chain responsible for transferring electrons from ubiquinone to cytochrome c.
- **Site IIIQo**: The outer ubiquinone-binding site of complex III, which has a large capacity to produce superoxide.
- **S3QELs (Suppressors of site IIIQo Electron Leak)**: Small-molecule compounds that specifically inhibit superoxide production at site IIIQo of complex III without affecting normal electron flow or oxidative phosphorylation.
- **Dietary Yeast Extract (YE)**: A common dietary component in Drosophila studies, used to manipulate nutrient intake and assess its effects on health and lifespan.
- **High-Fat Diet**: A diet high in fat content, typically used in mouse models to induce metabolic disorders and study their effects on intestinal permeability and lifespan.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Celiac Disease and Ulcerative Colitis (UC)

Celiac disease and ulcerative colitis (UC) are chronic inflammatory conditions of the gastrointestinal tract characterized by increased intestinal permeability. In celiac disease, the ingestion of gluten triggers an immune response that damages the lining of the small intestine, leading to malabsorption and various symptoms. UC, on the other hand, is a type of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that causes inflammation and ulcers in the lining of the colon and rectum. Both conditions are associated with a disruption of the intestinal barrier, allowing harmful substances to pass through and exacerbate inflammation.

The present invention provides a novel approach to managing these conditions by targeting the production of superoxide from complex III of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. By using S3QELs to inhibit superoxide production, the invention aims to reduce intestinal permeability and alleviate the symptoms associated with celiac disease and UC.

### Parkinson's Disease and Alzheimer's Disease

Parkinson's disease (PD) and Alzheimer's disease (AD) are neurodegenerative disorders characterized by the progressive loss of neuronal function. While the primary manifestations of these diseases are neurological, recent research has highlighted the role of gut-brain axis dysregulation in their pathogenesis. Intestinal permeability, often referred to as "leaky gut," has been linked to the onset and progression of PD and AD. Increased intestinal permeability allows the passage of harmful substances, including lipopolysaccharides (LPS) and other pro-inflammatory molecules, which can trigger systemic inflammation and contribute to neurodegeneration.

The invention's focus on reducing intestinal permeability through the inhibition of superoxide production at complex III has the potential to mitigate the gut-brain axis dysregulation observed in PD and AD. By maintaining the integrity of the intestinal barrier, the invention aims to reduce systemic inflammation and potentially slow the progression of these neurodegenerative diseases.

### Active Agents

The active agents of the present invention are small-molecule Suppressors of site IIIQo Electron Leak (S3QELs). These compounds are designed to specifically inhibit the production of superoxide at site IIIQo of complex III in the mitochondrial electron transport chain. S3QELs are unique in that they do not affect normal electron flow or oxidative phosphorylation, making them a safe and effective therapeutic option.

Three specific S3QELs have been identified and tested in the present invention:
1. **S3QEL1.2**
2. **S3QEL2.2**
3. **S3QEL3**

These compounds have been shown to effectively reduce intestinal permeability, decrease the number of apoptotic cells, and increase median lifespan in both Drosophila and mice fed high-nutrient diets. The mechanism of action of S3QELs involves the suppression of superoxide production from site IIIQo, which in turn reduces oxidative stress and prevents the stabilization of Hif-1α, a key mediator of intestinal barrier dysfunction.

### Permeability Assays

To assess the effectiveness of S3QELs in reducing intestinal permeability, various assays have been employed. In Drosophila, the "smurf assay" is used, which involves feeding a blue food dye that is normally not absorbed. Flies with permeable intestinal epithelium become blue, indicating increased intestinal permeability. In mice, the uptake of FITC-dextran from the gut into blood plasma and the appearance of plasma-derived albumin in feces are used to measure intestinal permeability.

These assays have demonstrated that S3QELs significantly reduce intestinal permeability in both model organisms. For example, in Drosophila fed a 5% yeast extract (YE) diet, the incidence of intestinal permeability was approximately halved by co-feeding S3QELs. Similarly, in mice fed a high-fat diet, S3QELs strongly protected against the increases in intestinal permeability, as measured by decreased plasma FITC-dextran and fecal albumin levels.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Objective

To evaluate the effectiveness of S3QELs in reducing intestinal permeability and extending lifespan in Drosophila fed a high-nutrient diet.

#### Methods

**Model Organism**: Drosophila melanogaster (w1118 strain)

**Diet**: 
- Control diet: 0.5% yeast extract (YE)
- High-nutrient diet: 5% yeast extract (YE)

**Treatment**: 
- S3QEL1.2 (8 μM)
- S3QEL2.2 (8 μM)
- S3QEL3 (8 μM)

**Assays**:
- **Smurf Assay**: Flies were fed a blue food dye, and the incidence of blue flies was recorded to assess intestinal permeability.
- **Apoptosis Assay**: The number of apoptotic cells in the intestine was counted using TUNEL staining.
- **Lifespan Assay**: Median lifespan was determined by monitoring the survival of flies over time.

#### Results

**Intestinal Permeability**:
- Flies fed a 5% YE diet showed a significantly higher incidence of intestinal permeability compared to those fed a 0.5% YE diet.
- Co-feeding S3QEL1.2, S3QEL2.2, or S3QEL3 reduced the incidence of intestinal permeability by approximately 50%.

**Apoptosis**:
- Flies fed a 5% YE diet had a higher number of apoptotic cells in the intestine compared to those fed a 0.5% YE diet.
- Co-feeding S3QEL1.2, S3QEL2.2, or S3QEL3 significantly reduced the number of apoptotic cells.

**Lifespan**:
- Flies fed a 5% YE diet had a shorter median lifespan compared to those fed a 0.5% YE diet.
- Co-feeding S3QEL1.2, S3QEL2.2, or S3QEL3 increased median lifespan by 10-20%.

#### Conclusion

The results demonstrate that S3QELs effectively reduce intestinal permeability, decrease the number of apoptotic cells, and extend median lifespan in Drosophila fed a high-nutrient diet. These findings support the potential therapeutic value of S3QELs in preventing and treating intestinal barrier dysfunction and associated diseases.
