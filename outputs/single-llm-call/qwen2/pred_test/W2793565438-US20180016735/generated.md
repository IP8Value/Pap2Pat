# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of composite materials, specifically to lignocellulosic composites (LCs) and methods for their preparation. More particularly, the invention pertains to the fabrication of LCs using cold aqueous alkaline solvents, wherein the composites are composed of a cellulose matrix and lignocellulosic reinforcement materials.

## BACKGROUND

Lignocellulosic biomass, composed primarily of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, is highly resistant to degradation and conversion into useful intermediates for biobased fuels and products. Various pretreatment methods have been explored to enhance the accessibility and reactivity of the carbohydrate fraction within lignocellulosic biomass. Cold alkaline solvents, such as sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and lithium hydroxide monohydrate (LiOH·H₂O) solutions, have been shown to effectively break down the lignocellulosic bonds, making the cellulose more accessible for further processing.

Prior studies have focused on the use of cold aqueous alkaline solvents to prepare all-cellulose composites (ACCs) by dissolving cellulose and reinforcing it with undissolved cellulose materials. However, these ACCs are composed entirely of cellulose, limiting their mechanical properties and potential applications. The present invention expands on these methods by preparing lignocellulosic composites (LCs) that incorporate a semidissolved cellulose matrix with nondissolved lignocellulose reinforcement materials. These LCs offer several advantages over traditional composites, including the use of readily available and inexpensive biomass materials, the absence of adhesives or resins, and complete biodegradability and compostability.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for preparing lignocellulosic composites (LCs) using cold aqueous alkaline solvents. The method involves the following steps:

1. Preparing a cold aqueous alkaline solvent solution.
2. Immersing a cellulose source, such as cotton, in the solvent solution to form a cellulose gel matrix.
3. Adding lignocellulosic reinforcement materials, such as osage orange wood (OOW) particles and blue agave fibers (AF), to the cellulose gel matrix.
4. Stirring the mixture to ensure uniform distribution of the reinforcement materials within the matrix.
5. Transferring the mixture to a mold and subjecting it to a series of solvent-water exchange treatments.
6. Drying the composite under controlled conditions to remove the solvent and form a solid LC panel.

The invention also encompasses the lignocellulosic composites (LCs) prepared by the aforementioned method, characterized by their composition, mechanical properties, and environmental benefits. The LCs are composed of 15% to 35% cellulose matrix and 65% to 85% lignocellulose reinforcement materials. The mechanical properties of the LCs are influenced by various factors, including reaction temperature, matrix concentration, solvent volume, and the type of alkaline solvent used.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Examples

#### Example 1: Preparation of LCs with Varying Reaction Temperatures

A series of experiments were conducted to determine the effect of reaction temperature on the mechanical properties of the LCs. A 25% cotton and 75% OOW formulation was prepared using a 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent solution at various temperatures (-5°C, -7.5°C, -10°C, -12.5°C, and -15°C). The cotton was immersed in the precooled solvent and stirred for approximately 15 minutes to form a cellulose gel matrix. OOW particles were then added and stirred for an additional 10 minutes. The mixture was transferred to a mold and subjected to solvent-water exchange treatments before drying under controlled conditions.

The mechanical properties of the LCs were evaluated, and it was found that LCs prepared at -12.5°C exhibited the highest ultimate tensile strength (σᵤ), Young's modulus (E), flexural strength (σfm), and flexural modulus (Eb) compared to LCs prepared at higher or lower temperatures. Specifically, LCs prepared at -12.5°C showed σᵤ, E, σfm, and Eb values that were 108%, 151%, 183%, and 203% higher, respectively, than those prepared at -5°C.

#### Example 2: Influence of Matrix Concentration on LC Properties

The effect of varying the concentration of the cotton matrix on the mechanical properties of the LCs was investigated. LCs were prepared with 15%, 25%, 30%, and 35% cotton matrix and a 50/50 OOW/AF reinforcement. The 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent solution was used at a reaction temperature of -12.5°C. The mechanical properties of the LCs were evaluated, and it was found that LCs with higher concentrations of cotton matrix exhibited better mechanical properties. For instance, LCs with 35% cotton matrix showed σᵤ, E, σfm, and Eb values that were 135%, 343%, 153%, and 109% higher, respectively, than those with 15% cotton matrix.

#### Example 3: Effect of Solvent Volume on LC Properties

The impact of solvent volume on the mechanical properties of the LCs was studied. LCs were prepared using a 25% cotton and 75% OOW/AF formulation with 45 ml, 60 ml, 75 ml, 90 ml, and 105 ml of the 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent solution per panel. The mechanical properties of the LCs were evaluated, and it was found that the optimal solvent volume was 60 ml/panel. LCs prepared with 60 ml/panel exhibited the best mechanical properties, with σᵤ, E, σfm, and Eb values that were 58%, 45%, 55%, and 58% higher, respectively, than those prepared with 105 ml/panel.

#### Example 4: Comparison of Alkaline Solvent Formulations

The effectiveness of different alkaline solvent formulations on the mechanical properties of the LCs was evaluated. LCs were prepared using a 25% cotton and 75% OOW/AF formulation with various solvent formulations, including 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea, 7% NaOH/12% urea, and 9.8% KOH/12% urea. The mechanical properties of the LCs were evaluated, and it was found that the 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea solvent produced the best results. LCs prepared with this solvent exhibited higher mechanical properties compared to those prepared with NaOH or KOH-based solvents.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Preparation of Cold Aqueous Alkaline Solvent Solution

The cold aqueous alkaline solvent solution is prepared by dissolving the appropriate amounts of lithium hydroxide monohydrate (LiOH·H₂O) and urea in distilled water. The solution is then cooled to the desired temperature using a refrigerated circulator or ice bath. The typical concentration of the solvent solution is 4.6% LiOH·H₂O and 15% urea, but other concentrations may be used depending on the specific requirements of the LCs being prepared.

### Formation of Cellulose Gel Matrix

The cellulose source, such as cotton, is immersed in the cold aqueous alkaline solvent solution and stirred for a sufficient period to form a cellulose gel matrix. The stirring time and temperature are critical parameters that affect the degree of dissolution of the cellulose and the properties of the resulting gel matrix. Typically, the cotton is stirred for 15 minutes at -12.5°C to form a viscous gel matrix.

### Addition of Lignocellulosic Reinforcement Materials

Lignocellulosic reinforcement materials, such as osage orange wood (OOW) particles and blue agave fibers (AF), are added to the cellulose gel matrix and stirred to ensure uniform distribution. The reinforcement materials provide structural support and improve the mechanical properties of the LCs. The ratio of cellulose matrix to reinforcement materials can vary, but a typical composition is 25% cellulose matrix and 75% reinforcement materials.

### Solvent-Water Exchange Treatments

The mixture is transferred to a mold and subjected to a series of solvent-water exchange treatments to remove excess solvent and improve the mechanical properties of the LCs. The solvent-water exchange treatments involve submerging the composite in tap water under vacuum for 15 minutes, followed by continuous submergence in water for 24 hours with multiple water replacements. This process helps to remove residual solvent and improve the adhesion between the matrix and reinforcement materials.

### Drying and Densification

After the solvent-water exchange treatments, the composite is dried under controlled conditions to remove the remaining solvent and form a solid LC panel. The drying process typically involves placing the composite between two steel plates under 0.5 psi pressure and drying in a vacuum oven at 60°C and 25 inches Hg for 24 hours. The panels are subsequently densified by subjecting them to 8 tons pressure for 10 minutes at 180°C.

### Characterization of LCs

The mechanical properties of the LCs are evaluated using standard testing methods, including tensile testing, flexural testing, and compressive testing. The physical properties of the LCs, such as density, porosity, and thermal stability, are also characterized using techniques such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA).

### Applications of LCs

The LCs prepared by the method of the present invention have a wide range of potential applications due to their unique properties. Some of the key applications include:

- **Packaging Materials**: The LCs can be used as sustainable and biodegradable alternatives to conventional plastic packaging materials.
- **Construction Materials**: The LCs can be used in the construction industry as lightweight and strong building materials.
- **Biodegradable Products**: The LCs can be used to produce a variety of biodegradable products, such as disposable tableware, agricultural mulch films, and biomedical implants.

### Environmental Benefits

The LCs prepared by the method of the present invention offer significant environmental benefits. Since they are composed entirely of biological materials, they are completely biodegradable and compostable. Additionally, the absence of adhesives or resins reduces the environmental impact of the LCs. The use of readily available and inexpensive biomass materials also makes the LCs a cost-effective alternative to traditional composites.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for preparing lignocellulosic composites (LCs) using cold aqueous alkaline solvents. The LCs are composed of a cellulose matrix and lignocellulose reinforcement materials and exhibit excellent mechanical properties and environmental benefits. The method allows for the optimization of various parameters, such as reaction temperature, matrix concentration, solvent volume, and solvent type, to tailor the properties of the LCs for specific applications. The LCs have the potential to replace conventional materials in a variety of industries, contributing to a more sustainable and environmentally friendly future.
