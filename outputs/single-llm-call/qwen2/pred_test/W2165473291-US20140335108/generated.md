# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and medicine, specifically to compositions and methods for treating autoimmune and immune-related diseases and disorders. More particularly, the invention provides insulin-like 6 (Insl6) agents, including Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, and their use in ameliorating the symptoms and progression of autoimmune myositis and other immune-related diseases and disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Idiopathic inflammatory myositis (IIM) is a category of autoimmune muscle disorders that includes polymyositis (PM), dermatomyositis (DM), and inclusion body myositis (IBM). Common features of these subtypes include muscle weakness, histological evidence of muscle inflammation, and elevated levels of creatine kinase. However, each subtype has unique clinical, immunopathologic, and histologic criteria. For instance, dermatomyositis is characterized by vascular endothelial lesions associated with perimysial inflammation and perifascicular muscle fiber atrophy, while polymyositis is associated with injury to muscle fibers by aggressive immune cells, primarily infiltrating the endomysium. Macrophages, dendritic cells, and T cells are prominently present in the muscle tissue of different myositis subgroups. In dermatomyositis, large numbers of helper T cells (CD4 T cells) are found within the perimysial, often perivascular areas. In polymyositis, activated cytotoxic T cells (CD8 T cells) surround and invade non-necrotic muscle fibers, while helper T cells are found at more distant parts of the infiltrates. These inflammatory muscle diseases are rare, and there are few effective treatment strategies available for patients suffering from these conditions.

Immune cells play a crucial role in regulating skeletal muscle regeneration after myofiber damage. Muscle damage stimulates massive infiltration of immune cells into the injury site, including neutrophils, macrophages, and T cells. These cells function in the phagocytosis of necrotic muscle fibers, express pro-inflammatory cytokines, and participate in satellite cell activation. Previous studies have demonstrated that Insl6, a member of the insulin-like/relaxin family of proteins, has muscle regenerative and anti-inflammatory activities in a cardiotoxin-induced model of severe muscle injury when expressed using an engineered adenovirus vector. Given these findings, the present invention seeks to extend these studies and explore the utility of Insl6 in the setting of inflammatory muscle injury using a rodent model of myositis.

### Insulin-Like 6 (Insl6) Agents

Insulin-like 6 (Insl6) is a member of the insulin-like/relaxin family of proteins. It has been shown to have muscle regenerative and anti-inflammatory properties, making it a promising therapeutic candidate for treating autoimmune and immune-related diseases and disorders, particularly idiopathic inflammatory myositis (IIM). Insl6 agents, including recombinant Insl6 and Insl6-Fc fusion proteins, can be used to reduce inflammation and improve muscle function in patients suffering from these conditions.

### Insl6-Fc Fusion Proteins

Insl6-Fc fusion proteins are recombinant proteins that comprise a human IgG1 Fc domain fused to the N-terminus of full-length Insl6. The Fc domain enhances the stability and bioavailability of Insl6 in vivo, thereby prolonging its therapeutic effects. The Insl6-Fc fusion protein can be produced using standard recombinant DNA techniques, such as cloning the Insl6 cDNA into a mammalian expression vector downstream of an in-frame human IgG1 Fc domain and a secretion signal peptide. The resulting plasmid can be used to perform hydrodynamic injection or other methods of gene delivery.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Objective

To evaluate the efficacy of Insl6-Fc fusion protein in a murine model of experimental autoimmune myositis (EAM).

#### Methods

 Female mice, eight to ten weeks old, were immunized intradermally with 600 μg of recombinant human skeletal muscle myosin-binding C protein emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA) containing 100 μg of heat-killed Mycobacterium butyricum. The immunogens were injected subcutaneously at multiple sites around the torso or on the feet, and 2 μg of pertussis toxin (PT) in PBS was injected intraperitoneally at the same time. Two weeks after immunization, the mice were divided into two groups: a treatment group receiving intravenous injections of Insl6-Fc fusion protein (1 mg/kg) and a control group receiving intravenous injections of a control vector. The treatment was repeated every three days for a total of four doses.

#### Results

Muscle function was evaluated using the Rotarod and treadmill tests. The results showed that mice treated with Insl6-Fc fusion protein had significantly improved motor function compared to the control group. Specifically, the running distance on the treadmill and the time spent on the Rotarod were significantly higher in the treatment group. Histological analysis of muscle tissue revealed reduced inflammation and fewer CD4 and CD8 T cell infiltrates in the treatment group compared to the control group. Additionally, qRT-PCR analysis of muscle tissue showed reduced expression of inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α and IFN-γ, in the treatment group.

#### Conclusion

These results demonstrate that Insl6-Fc fusion protein is effective in reducing inflammation and improving muscle function in a murine model of experimental autoimmune myositis. The findings support the potential therapeutic utility of Insl6 agents in treating autoimmune and immune-related diseases and disorders.
