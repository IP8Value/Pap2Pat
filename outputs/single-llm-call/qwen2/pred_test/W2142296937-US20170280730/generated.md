## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and specifically to the use of a novel bacterium, Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), for controlling the transmission of vector-borne diseases such as malaria and dengue fever. The invention encompasses compositions and methods for reducing the infection of vector mosquitoes with Plasmodium and dengue virus, as well as for exerting entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages of these mosquitoes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vector-borne diseases, such as malaria and dengue fever, pose significant global health challenges. These diseases are transmitted by mosquitoes, primarily Anopheles and Aedes species, which serve as vectors. Traditional control methods, including the use of insecticides and bed nets, have limitations and often result in the development of resistance in mosquito populations. Therefore, there is a critical need for novel approaches to control the transmission of these diseases.

Recent research has highlighted the role of the mosquito gut microbiota in influencing vector competence. Studies have shown that certain bacteria can reduce the infection of mosquitoes with pathogens such as Plasmodium and dengue virus. For instance, the presence of specific bacterial species in the mosquito midgut can activate the mosquito's immune system, leading to a reduction in pathogen infection. Additionally, some bacteria produce bioactive compounds that directly inhibit pathogen growth.

Chromobacterium sp. (Csp_P) is a bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. This bacterium has been shown to possess broad-spectrum anti-pathogen activity, including the ability to inhibit the growth of Plasmodium and dengue virus. Moreover, Csp_P can rapidly colonize the midgut of both Anopheles and Aedes mosquitoes, exerting entomopathogenic activity that results in high mosquito mortality. These properties make Csp_P a promising candidate for the development of novel control strategies for vector-borne diseases.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P):** A bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, characterized by its ability to rapidly colonize the midgut of Anopheles and Aedes mosquitoes and exert broad-spectrum anti-pathogen activity.
- **Entomopathogenic activity:** The ability of a substance or organism to cause mortality or reduce the fitness of insects.
- **Vector-borne diseases:** Diseases that are transmitted to humans or animals by arthropod vectors, such as mosquitoes.
- **Plasmodium:** A genus of protozoan parasites that cause malaria.
- **Dengue virus:** A flavivirus that causes dengue fever.
- **Midgut:** The middle section of the insect digestive tract, where many pathogen infections occur.
- **Biofilm:** A community of microorganisms that adhere to surfaces and produce a matrix of extracellular polymeric substances.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)

Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) is a Gram-negative bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. Csp_P can be cultured in Luria Bertani (LB) broth and on LB agar, forming flat colonies with a tan color that become darker with time and are opaque when exposed to light. Molecular characterization of its 16S rRNA gene sequence and phylogenetic analysis show a 98% similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, indicating that these are its closest relatives.

Csp_P is capable of forming biofilms, which are complex matrices of cells, proteins, polysaccharides, and DNA. Biofilm formation allows Csp_P to survive exposure to host-derived antimicrobial factors and other environmental stressors. The biofilm state of Csp_P is associated with the production of stable secondary metabolites that exhibit anti-pathogen activity.

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects

#### A. Colonization of the Mosquito Midgut

Csp_P displays an exceptional ability to rapidly colonize the midgut of Anopheles and Aedes mosquitoes. When administered through a sugar meal containing 10^6 colony-forming units (CFU)/ml for Aedes aegypti or 10^8 CFU/ml for Anopheles gambiae, Csp_P achieves a prevalence of 80% in An. gambiae and 97% in Ae. aegypti at 3 days post-exposure. The average bacterial load at this time point is approximately 10^5 and 10^4 CFU per midgut in Ae. aegypti and An. gambiae females, respectively.

In non-antibiotic treated mosquitoes, Csp_P colonizes the midgut of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti by day 1 post-exposure. However, the rapid mortality of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti after Csp_P exposure complicates the measurement of colonization efficiency beyond day 2 and 3, respectively.

#### B. Entomopathogenic Activity

Csp_P exerts strong entomopathogenic activity upon ingestion by mosquitoes. Exposure to Csp_P through sugar feeding or blood feeding results in a significant decrease in the longevity of both An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti. The entomopathogenic effect is more pronounced when Csp_P is introduced through the blood meal, likely due to the larger single bacterial dose received upon blood feeding.

Csp_P also exerts larvicidal activity. Exposure of mosquito larvae to Csp_P in the breeding water results in almost complete mortality of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti larvae over a 3- and 2-day period, respectively. The mechanism of Csp_P-mediated mortality may involve the production of mosquitocidal factors or systemic infection through dissemination into the hemolymph.

#### C. Reduction of Pathogen Infection

The presence of Csp_P in the mosquito midgut compromises the infection of An. gambiae with Plasmodium falciparum and of Ae. aegypti with dengue virus. Surviving mosquitoes exposed to Csp_P through sugar feeding prior to feeding on infectious blood display significantly increased resistance to Plasmodium and dengue infection. The inhibition of pathogen infection is even greater when Csp_P is introduced through the gametocyte-containing blood meal, likely due to the larger number of ingested bacteria.

#### D. Induction of Mosquito Innate Immune System Genes

Csp_P is a potent immune elicitor in the mosquito gut. Exposure of mosquito cells to Csp_P results in increased expression of immune genes, including antimicrobial peptides and antipathogen factors. This immune activation may contribute to the reduced pathogen infection observed in Csp_P-colonized mosquitoes.

#### E. In Vitro Anti-Pathogen Activity

Csp_P exerts potent anti-Plasmodium and anti-dengue activity in vitro, independent of the mosquito. Planktonic and biofilm preparations of Csp_P inhibit the development of both asexual and sexual stages of Plasmodium falciparum. Csp_P biofilm and biofilm supernatant also abolish dengue virus infectivity in vitro. The anti-pathogen activity of Csp_P is heat-sensitive and appears to be mediated by stable secondary metabolites produced by the bacterium.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes

#### Materials and Methods

- **Mosquito Rearing and Antibiotic Treatment:** Aedes aegypti and Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes were maintained on a 10% sugar solution at 27°C and 95% humidity with a 12-h light/dark cycle. Antibiotic treatment was performed to remove the native microbial flora.
- **Introduction of Bacteria via Sugar Meal:** Mosquitoes were fed a sugar meal containing Csp_P at a concentration of 10^6 CFU/ml for Ae. aegypti and 10^8 CFU/ml for An. gambiae.
- **Assaying Prevalence and Bacterial Load of Csp_P:** Midguts were dissected and homogenized in 1× PBS, and the presence of Csp_P was confirmed by plating on LB agar.
- **Introduction of Bacteria via Blood Meal:** Csp_P was added to the infectious blood meal at a concentration of 10^6 CFU/ml.
- **Mosquito Infections with Plasmodium and Dengue Virus:** Mosquitoes were infected with Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV-2) as described in the methods section of the research paper.
- **Oocyst Counts and Plaque Assays:** Oocyst counts were performed to assess Plasmodium infection, and plaque assays were conducted to assess dengue virus infection.

#### Results

Csp_P effectively colonized the midgut of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti mosquitoes, achieving a prevalence of 80% and 97%, respectively, at 3 days post-exposure. Surviving mosquitoes exposed to Csp_P through sugar feeding prior to feeding on infectious blood displayed significantly increased resistance to Plasmodium and dengue infection. The inhibition of pathogen infection was even greater when Csp_P was introduced through the gametocyte-containing blood meal.

### Conclusion

Csp_P reduces the infection of An. gambiae with Plasmodium falciparum and of Ae. aegypti with dengue virus, making it a promising candidate for the development of novel control strategies for vector-borne diseases.

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent

#### Materials and Methods

- **Mosquito Rearing and Antibiotic Treatment:** As described in Example 1.
- **Introduction of Bacteria via Sugar Meal and Blood Meal:** As described in Example 1.
- **Exposure of Larvae to Csp_P:** Mosquito larvae were exposed to Csp_P in the breeding water, and mortality was monitored over a 5-day period.
- **Cell Culture Maintenance and Mosquito Infections with Dengue Virus:** As described in Example 1.
- **P. Falciparum Cultivation and Mosquito Infections:** As described in Example 1.
- **Csp_P Culture Preparations for In Vitro Anti-Pathogen Activity Assays:** Csp_P was grown in planktonic and biofilm conditions, and the preparations were filtered through a 0.2-µm filter.
- **In Vitro Anti-Plasmodium and Anti-Dengue Activity Assays:** Planktonic and biofilm preparations of Csp_P were tested for their ability to inhibit the development of Plasmodium and dengue virus in vitro.

#### Results

Csp_P exerts strong entomopathogenic activity, causing high mortality in both larval and adult stages of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti. Exposure of larvae to Csp_P in the breeding water results in almost complete mortality over a 3- and 2-day period, respectively. In vitro assays show that Csp_P biofilm and biofilm supernatant inhibit the development of both asexual and sexual stages of Plasmodium falciparum and abolish dengue virus infectivity. The anti-pathogen activity of Csp_P is heat-sensitive and appears to be mediated by stable secondary metabolites produced by the bacterium.

### Conclusion

Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a general mosquitocidal agent and a potent transmission-blocking agent for malaria and dengue. Its broad-spectrum anti-pathogen activity and entomopathogenic properties make it a valuable tool for the control of vector-borne diseases.
