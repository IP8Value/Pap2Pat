# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medical and pharmaceutical sciences, specifically to the treatment of Human Papillomavirus (HPV)-related diseases. More particularly, the invention concerns the use of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) to inhibit the expression of HPV oncogenes E6 and E7, thereby reducing the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells and potentially treating HPV-related conditions such as cervical dysplasia and cervical carcinoma.

## BACKGROUND

Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a significant causative agent for cervical cancer, the second most common cancer in women and the third leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide. HPV has also been implicated in various other cancers, including anal, head and neck, and possibly breast cancers. Despite the widespread prevalence and severe health implications of HPV infections, there are currently no approved, effective nonsurgical interventions for cervical dysplasia or the underlying HPV infection. The standard treatments for localized and advanced localized cervical carcinoma, such as concurrent chemotherapy and radiation, are often toxic and not always curative. For metastatic cervical cancer, standard chemotherapy is primarily palliative, and the use of biologics in this context is limited. Therefore, there is a critical need for new treatment modalities that can effectively manage HPV-related diseases.

The expression of the viral gene products E6 and E7, regulated by the viral long control region (LCR), is predictive of the progression toward cancer. Higher levels of viral mRNA correlate with higher-grade lesions, and these oncoproteins contribute to the invasiveness and metastatic aggressiveness of transformed cells. Targeted inhibition of E6 and E7 expression has been shown to reactivate p53, induce cell-cycle exit, and promote apoptosis in HPV-positive cells. Both radiation and chemotherapy are more effective in killing cervical cancer cells when E6 and E7 expression is attenuated.

Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) is an endogenous human cytokine that has been identified as a potent inhibitor of HPV oncoprotein expression in cultured cervical squamous carcinoma cells. LIF has been tested in phase I clinical trials and has demonstrated minimal toxicity, making it a promising candidate for the treatment of HPV-related pathologies. The present invention leverages the ability of LIF to repress E6 and E7 expression and reduce the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells, offering a novel approach to managing HPV-related diseases.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for treating HPV-related diseases, such as cervical dysplasia and cervical carcinoma, by administering Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF). The method involves the use of LIF to inhibit the expression of the HPV oncogenes E6 and E7, which are regulated by the viral long control region (LCR). By reducing the expression of these oncoproteins, LIF can effectively halt the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells and potentially reverse the transformed phenotype.

In one embodiment, the invention includes a pharmaceutical composition comprising LIF and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition can be administered to a patient in need thereof to treat HPV-related conditions. The invention also encompasses methods for manufacturing such pharmaceutical compositions and methods for using them in the treatment of HPV-related diseases.

## DEFINITIONS

- **Cervical Dysplasia**: A precancerous condition characterized by abnormal changes in the cells of the cervix.
- **Cervical Carcinoma**: Cancer of the cervix, often caused by persistent HPV infection.
- **E6 and E7 Oncoproteins**: Viral proteins encoded by HPV that play a crucial role in the transformation of normal cells into cancerous cells.
- **Long Control Region (LCR)**: A regulatory region in the HPV genome that controls the expression of viral genes, including E6 and E7.
- **Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF)**: An endogenous human cytokine that has been shown to inhibit the expression of HPV oncoproteins and reduce the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Classification of Papillomaviruses

Papillomaviruses are a diverse group of DNA viruses that infect the epithelial cells of skin and mucous membranes. They are classified into different types based on their genetic sequences and the tissues they infect. High-risk HPV types, such as HPV-16 and HPV-18, are strongly associated with the development of cervical cancer and other anogenital and oropharyngeal cancers. These viruses integrate their DNA into the host genome, leading to the expression of viral oncoproteins E6 and E7, which disrupt normal cellular processes and contribute to the development of cancer.

### Papillomaviruses and Cancer

The expression of E6 and E7 oncoproteins is a key factor in the progression of HPV-infected cells to cancer. E6 promotes the degradation of the tumor suppressor protein p53, while E7 binds to and inactivates the retinoblastoma protein (pRb), leading to uncontrolled cell proliferation. The long control region (LCR) of the HPV genome regulates the expression of these oncoproteins. High levels of E6 and E7 mRNA are associated with higher-grade cervical lesions and are predictive of the progression to cancer. Therefore, strategies that can effectively inhibit the expression of E6 and E7 have the potential to prevent or treat HPV-related cancers.

### Diagnosis and Treatment of HPV

Diagnosis of HPV-related diseases typically involves cervical cytology (Pap smear), HPV DNA testing, and colposcopy. Current treatments for cervical dysplasia and cervical carcinoma include surgical interventions such as loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP), cryotherapy, and cone biopsy. For advanced cervical cancer, the standard treatment is a combination of chemotherapy and radiation therapy. However, these treatments are often associated with significant side effects and are not always curative. There is a clear need for noninvasive and less toxic treatment options.

### Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and its Utility in Treating HPV-Transformed Cells by Inhibiting LCR-Driven Transcription

Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) is a pleiotropic cytokine that belongs to the interleukin-6 (IL-6) family. It is known to play a role in various biological processes, including embryonic development, hematopoiesis, and immune regulation. Recent studies have shown that LIF can inhibit the expression of HPV oncoproteins E6 and E7 by repressing the viral long control region (LCR) in HPV-transformed cervical cancer cells. This inhibition leads to a reduction in the proliferation of these cells and may help in reversing the transformed phenotype.

In vitro studies have demonstrated that LIF treatment significantly reduces the expression of E6 and E7 mRNA in HPV-16 transformed cervical cancer cells. This reduction is accompanied by a decrease in cell proliferation, suggesting that LIF can effectively inhibit the growth of HPV-transformed cells. The mechanism of action of LIF involves the activation of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, particularly the phosphorylation of STAT3, which is known to play a role in HPV-16 pathogenesis.

### Manipulating Proteins, DNA, and RNA

The use of LIF to inhibit the expression of HPV oncoproteins involves manipulating the cellular machinery that regulates gene expression. LIF acts by binding to the LIF receptor (LIFR) and the gp130 co-receptor, which activates the JAK-STAT signaling pathway. This pathway leads to the phosphorylation of STAT3, which can then translocate to the nucleus and affect the transcription of target genes. In the context of HPV, LIF appears to repress the activity of the viral LCR, thereby reducing the expression of E6 and E7.

Other methods of manipulating proteins, DNA, and RNA to inhibit HPV oncoprotein expression include the use of small interfering RNA (siRNA), peptide aptamers, and the transfected E2 gene. These approaches have shown promise in vitro and in animal models but have not yet been widely adopted in clinical practice. The use of LIF offers a unique advantage as it is an endogenous human cytokine with minimal toxicity, making it a safer and more viable option for clinical use.

### Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

The pharmaceutical composition of the present invention comprises Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The LIF can be produced using recombinant DNA technology, and the pharmaceutical composition can be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and topical. The composition may also include additional components such as stabilizers, preservatives, and excipients to enhance the stability and efficacy of the formulation.

2. **Formulation**: The purified LIF is formulated with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier to create a stable and effective pharmaceutical composition. The carrier may include buffers, salts, and other excipients to maintain the stability and solubility of LIF.
3. **Sterilization**: The formulated composition is sterilized using appropriate methods such as filtration or autoclaving to ensure it is free from microbial contamination.
4. **Packaging**: The sterile composition is packaged in vials, syringes, or other appropriate containers for distribution and use.

### Experimental Procedures

#### Cell Culture

Human cervical cancer cell lines CaSki and SiHa were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and maintained in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 100 IU/mL penicillin, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, and 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) at 5% CO2. For certain experiments, the serum concentration was reduced or completely removed.

#### Drug Preparation

LIF (Millipore) was stored in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at 4°C and diluted in DMEM before use. Other cytokines, including TNFα, IL-6, and EGF (Biosource, Invitrogen), were dissolved in PBS with bovine serum albumin (BSA), stored at −20°C, and diluted in DMEM or RPMI before use.

#### Generation of an HPV-16 Reporter Cell Line

The cervical cancer cell line SiHa was chosen for its high native HPV-16 LCR activity and significant expression of the E6 and E7 genes. The cells were transfected with the plasmid pLCRGLuc, which contains a complete HPV-16 LCR cloned upstream of a secreted Gaussia luciferase gene and a neomycin resistance gene driven by an SV40 promoter. Cells were selected with 400 μg/mL G418 for seven days to generate a stable reporter cell line.

#### Cell Proliferation Assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using the CellTiter 96 (Promega) MTT assay. Cells were seeded at 5,000 per well in a 96-well plate and treated with the specified cytokines. After the indicated periods, the cells were incubated with MTT labeling solution for 4 hours, followed by the solubilization/stop solution overnight. The reduction of MTT to formazan, indicating cellular metabolic activity, was quantified by measuring absorbance at 570 nm on a SpectraMax microplate reader (Molecular Devices), with background absorbance at 650 nm subtracted.

#### Luciferase Assays

For firefly luciferase assays, cells were seeded in 12-well plates at a density of 50,000 per well and transfected with the indicated reporter plasmids using SuperFect (QIAGEN). After 18 hours, the medium was replaced, and the cells were treated with the specified stimuli for the indicated time periods. Luciferase activity was measured using the luciferase substrate (New England Biolabs).

For Gaussia luciferase assays, SiHa-LCR-gLuc cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 per well and treated as indicated. After incubation, 20 μL of supernatant from each well was transferred to a new plate, and Gaussia luciferase reagent (New England Biolabs) was added. Luminescence was measured for 5 seconds.

#### Quantitative Real-Time PCR

To measure the expression of E6 and E7 mRNA, primer sets and probes specific to the E6 and E7 genes were designed and synthesized by Eurofins MWG Operon. RNA was extracted using the RNeasy kit (Qiagen), quantified by spectrophotometry (NanoDrop 8000, Thermo Scientific), and equal quantities were amplified using the TaqMan kit (Applied Biosystems) on an ABI Prism 7700. The quantity of HPV mRNA was estimated relative to β-actin mRNA using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

#### Intracellular Phosphospecific Flow Cytometry

Trypsinized cells (10,000 for each timepoint) were fixed with 1.2% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 10 minutes, rinsed with PBS containing 1% BSA, permeabilized in PBS/90% ice-cold methanol, and stored at −20°C. Prior to staining, cells were washed twice in PBS containing 1% BSA, stained with the indicated antibodies for 30 minutes at 4°C, and analyzed on a FC500 flow cytometer (Beckman-Coulter). Further analysis was performed using cytobank (http://www.cytobank.org/).

#### Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis (unpaired t-test) was performed using GraphPad software (graphpad.com) as needed. P values indicating statistical significance are represented by a single (P < 0.1) or double asterisk (P < 0.05) on the figures.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: LIF Inhibits HPV-16 LCR-Driven Transcription

In this study, the transcriptional activity of the HPV-16 LCR was compared in untreated cervical SiHa cells and cells treated with various cytokines. The IL-6 superfamily member LIF reduced HPV-16 LCR expression by approximately 60% in a time- and dose-dependent manner. To verify that the observed decrease in LCR transcription was functionally significant, the effect of LIF on mRNA expression was examined by quantitative real-time PCR. CaSki cervical cancer cells were treated with 1 ng/mL or 10 ng/mL of LIF or with PBS for one, two, or three days. The expression of E7 mRNA was significantly reduced over this time course, initially in a dose-dependent manner, although at three days, cells treated with the low or high concentrations of LIF showed similar inhibition of E7. E6 inhibition followed a similar trend.

### Example 2: Induction of Phosphorylation of STAT3 in Cervical Cancer Cells

LIF is known to activate members of the JAK-STAT pathway, particularly JAK1 and STAT3, through binding to the heterodimeric LIFR-gp130 receptor. The effect of LIF on STAT3 activation in SiHa cells was evaluated. Following treatment with LIF, STAT3 was phosphorylated on tyrosine residue 705, indicative of activation, in a transient fashion. To determine if the observed phosphorylation was accompanied by transcriptional activation, a reporter plasmid 4xM67 pTATA TK-Luc was used. SiHa cells were transfected with the plasmid, treated with 10 ng/mL LIF or PBS, and analyzed after six hours. The activity of the reporter plasmid was 1.8-fold higher in LIF-treated cells than in untreated cells.

### Example 3: Proliferation of HPV-Transformed Cells

The effect of LIF on the proliferation of HPV-16 transformed cervical cancer cells was assessed. CaSki cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 per well in growth medium. After the cells attached, the medium was replaced with serum-free medium containing LIF, the LCR-inhibiting cytokines IL-6 or EGF, or PBS. After 40 hours, the proliferation of cells was measured using the MTT assay. As expected, EGF promoted the proliferation of the cells relative to untreated cells. The LIF-treated cells, however, proliferated much less rapidly, only reaching a final population density approximately half that of untreated cells. This reduction in proliferation was accompanied by a significant decrease in the expression of E6 and E7 mRNA, suggesting that LIF acts at least in part through an HPV-specific mechanism.

The present invention provides a novel and effective method for treating HPV-related diseases by leveraging the ability of LIF to inhibit the expression of HPV oncoproteins and reduce the proliferation of HPV-transformed cells. The use of LIF offers a promising noninvasive and less toxic alternative to current treatment options, with the potential to significantly improve outcomes for patients with HPV-related conditions.
