# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for the analysis of DNA methylation status using methylation-independent primers. More specifically, the invention provides a method for designing and using methylation-independent primers that can correct for PCR bias, thereby ensuring proportional amplification of both methylated and unmethylated DNA templates. The method is particularly useful in the context of bisulfite conversion-based methylation analysis, where the accurate determination of methylation status is crucial for understanding epigenetic regulation in various biological processes and diseases.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

### Importance of DNA Methylation

DNA methylation is a critical epigenetic modification that plays a pivotal role in gene regulation, genomic imprinting, and the development and progression of various diseases, including cancer. The addition of a methyl group to the cytosine residue in CpG dinucleotides can lead to changes in chromatin structure and transcriptional activity, influencing cellular function and phenotype. Understanding the methylation status of specific genomic regions is essential for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying these processes and for developing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

Many techniques have been developed for the analysis of DNA methylation, with the overwhelming majority involving sodium bisulfite modification of the DNA template followed by PCR amplification of the region of interest. Methylation-specific PCR (MSP) and methylation-independent PCR (MIP) are two prominent methods used in this context. MSP primers are designed to interrogate the methylation status of specific CpG sites within the primer binding site, while MIP primers are designed to amplify the region of interest regardless of its methylation status, allowing for post-PCR determination of methylation status.

Despite the widespread use of these techniques, they face significant challenges, particularly in achieving proportional amplification of both methylated and unmethylated templates. PCR bias, which favors the amplification of unmethylated sequences, is a major issue in MIP-based methods. This bias can lead to inaccurate representation of the true methylation status of the sample, compromising the reliability of the results.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing methylation analysis techniques by providing a method for designing and using methylation-independent primers that can correct for PCR bias. The method involves the inclusion of a limited number of CpG dinucleotides in the primer sequences and the optimization of PCR annealing temperatures to ensure proportional amplification of both methylated and unmethylated DNA templates. This approach enables more accurate and reliable determination of DNA methylation status, making it particularly useful for applications in epigenetic research, diagnostics, and therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:
- **DNA Methylation**: The addition of a methyl group to the cytosine residue in CpG dinucleotides, leading to changes in chromatin structure and transcriptional activity.
- **Bisulfite Conversion**: A chemical treatment that converts unmethylated cytosines to uracil, while leaving methylated cytosines unchanged, allowing for the differentiation of methylated and unmethylated DNA.
- **Methylation-Specific PCR (MSP)**: A PCR-based technique that uses primers designed to bind specifically to methylated or unmethylated DNA sequences.
- **Methylation-Independent PCR (MIP)**: A PCR-based technique that uses primers designed to amplify the region of interest regardless of its methylation status.
- **PCR Bias**: The phenomenon where PCR amplification disproportionately favors unmethylated DNA sequences over methylated ones.

### Samples

The method of the present invention can be applied to a wide range of biological samples, including but not limited to blood, tissue, cell cultures, and bodily fluids. The DNA is extracted from these samples using standard methods known in the art, such as phenol-chloroform extraction or commercial DNA extraction kits. The extracted DNA is then subjected to bisulfite conversion to differentiate methylated from unmethylated cytosines.

### Modification of DNA

The bisulfite conversion step is a critical component of the method. The DNA is treated with sodium bisulfite, which converts unmethylated cytosines to uracil, while methylated cytosines remain unchanged. This conversion allows for the subsequent PCR amplification to distinguish between methylated and unmethylated DNA. The bisulfite conversion can be performed using commercially available kits or by following established protocols.

### Methylation-Independent Primer

The key aspect of the present invention is the design of methylation-independent primers that can correct for PCR bias. These primers are designed to include a limited number of CpG dinucleotides, typically one or two, and to have a salt-adjusted melting temperature (Tm) of around 65°C. The inclusion of CpG residues in the primer sequences, particularly when positioned away from the 3' end, helps to balance the amplification of methylated and unmethylated templates. The primers should also include one or more thymine (T) residues originating from non-CpG cytosines at or near the 3' end to ensure amplification of only bisulfite-modified DNA.

### Amplifying Step

The PCR amplification step is performed using the methylation-independent primers designed as described above. The PCR conditions, including the annealing temperature, are optimized to achieve proportional amplification of both methylated and unmethylated templates. The annealing temperature can be adjusted to control the stringency of primer binding, with lower temperatures favoring the amplification of unmethylated templates and higher temperatures favoring the amplification of methylated templates. An intermediate annealing temperature is typically used to balance the amplification of both templates.

### Analysis of Amplified CpG-Containing Nucleic Acids

The amplified DNA can be analyzed using various techniques to determine the methylation status of the region of interest. Common methods include sequencing, restriction enzyme digestion, denaturing high-performance liquid chromatography (DHPLC), single-strand conformation analysis (SSCA), melting curve analysis, and high-resolution melting (HRM). Each method has its own advantages and can be chosen based on the specific requirements of the study.

### Nucleic Acid Sequencing

Sequencing of the amplified DNA provides a direct readout of the methylation status at individual CpG sites. Sanger sequencing or next-generation sequencing (NGS) can be used, depending on the scale of the analysis. Sequencing data can be analyzed using bioinformatics tools to quantify the methylation level at each CpG site and to identify differentially methylated regions.

### Primer Extension

Primer extension assays can be used to determine the methylation status of specific CpG sites within the amplified region. This method involves the hybridization of a labeled primer to the bisulfite-converted DNA, followed by extension using a DNA polymerase. The length of the extended product indicates the methylation status of the target CpG site.

### Restriction Enzyme Digestion

Restriction enzyme digestion can be used to analyze the methylation status of specific CpG sites. Certain restriction enzymes recognize and cut DNA sequences containing unmethylated CpG sites, while others recognize and cut methylated CpG sites. By comparing the digestion patterns of the amplified DNA, the methylation status can be inferred.

## Disorders

The method of the present invention is particularly useful for the diagnosis and monitoring of various disorders characterized by aberrant DNA methylation patterns. These disorders include but are not limited to cancer, neurological disorders, and developmental disorders. For example, hypermethylation of tumor suppressor genes is a common feature of many cancers, and the accurate determination of methylation status can aid in early detection, prognosis, and therapeutic decision-making.

## Kit

The present invention also encompasses a kit for performing the method of methylation analysis. The kit includes reagents for DNA extraction, bisulfite conversion, PCR amplification, and methylation analysis. The kit may also include pre-designed methylation-independent primers for commonly studied genomic regions, as well as detailed protocols and quality control standards.

## Use

The method and kit of the present invention have a wide range of applications in both research and clinical settings. In research, the method can be used to investigate the role of DNA methylation in various biological processes, such as gene regulation, cellular differentiation, and disease pathogenesis. In clinical settings, the method can be used for the diagnosis and monitoring of diseases characterized by aberrant DNA methylation, such as cancer. The method can also be integrated into high-throughput screening platforms for large-scale methylation analysis.
