# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer immunotherapy, specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the cytotoxicity of T cells against acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells. More particularly, the invention provides a method for blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis to augment the therapeutic efficacy of bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®) and other immunotherapeutic agents.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a promising approach for treating various malignancies, including hematological cancers such as acute myeloid leukemia (AML). One of the key challenges in cancer immunotherapy is overcoming the mechanisms by which cancer cells evade immune detection and destruction. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, which block inhibitory signals that dampen T-cell activity, have shown significant promise in this regard. However, the effectiveness of current checkpoint inhibitors is limited, and there is a need for identifying and targeting additional immune checkpoint pathways.

The TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis is a recently identified immune checkpoint pathway that plays a crucial role in regulating T-cell and natural killer (NK) cell function. TIGIT (T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains) is an inhibitory receptor expressed on activated T cells, regulatory T cells, NK cells, and NKT cells. Its ligands, poliovirus receptor (PVR, CD155) and poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2, CD112), are overexpressed on various tumor cells, including AML cells. Interaction between TIGIT and its ligands suppresses immune responses, leading to immune evasion by cancer cells.

Bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®) are a class of immunotherapeutic agents designed to bring T cells into close proximity with tumor cells, thereby enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity. AMG 330, a CD33/CD3 BiTE®, has shown preclinical and early clinical activity in AML. However, the therapeutic efficacy of BiTE® can be limited by the presence of inhibitory signals, such as those mediated by the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis.

The present invention addresses the need for improved immunotherapeutic strategies by providing a method for blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis to enhance the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cells, either as a monotherapy or in combination with BiTE® and other immunotherapeutic agents.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for enhancing the cytotoxicity of T cells against acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells by blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis. The method involves administering to a subject an effective amount of one or more agents that block the interaction between TIGIT and its ligands, PVR and PVRL2. The blocking agents can be antibodies, small molecules, or other compounds that inhibit the binding of TIGIT to PVR and PVRL2.

In one embodiment, the method includes administering a blocking agent that targets PVR and/or PVRL2. In another embodiment, the method includes administering a blocking agent that targets TIGIT. The blocking agents can be used alone or in combination with other immunotherapeutic agents, such as bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®), checkpoint inhibitors, or other immune modulators.

The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more blocking agents and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravenous, subcutaneous, and intramuscular.

The invention further provides a method for treating AML in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of a blocking agent that targets the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis, optionally in combination with a BiTE® antibody construct or other immunotherapeutic agents.

The invention is based on the discovery that blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis significantly enhances the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cells, both in vitro and in vivo. The combination of blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis with BiTE® therapy results in a synergistic effect, leading to improved therapeutic outcomes.

#### Overview

The present invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing the cytotoxicity of T cells against acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cells by blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis. #### Definitions

- **TIGIT**: T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains, an inhibitory receptor expressed on activated T cells, regulatory T cells, NK cells, and NKT cells.
- **PVR (CD155)**: Poliovirus receptor, a ligand for TIGIT overexpressed on various tumor cells, including AML cells.
- **PVRL2 (CD112)**: Poliovirus receptor-related 2, a ligand for TIGIT overexpressed on various tumor cells, including AML cells.
- **BiTE®**: Bispecific T-cell engager, an immunotherapeutic agent designed to bring T cells into close proximity with tumor cells, enhancing T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity.
- **AMG 330**: A CD33/CD3 BiTE® antibody construct with preclinical and early clinical activity in AML.
- **Checkpoint inhibitors**: Therapeutic agents that block inhibitory signals that dampen T-cell activity, such as anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4, and anti-TIGIT antibodies.

#### Methods for Enhancing Cytotoxicity

The invention provides a method for enhancing the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cells by blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis. The method involves administering to a subject an effective amount of one or more agents that block the interaction between TIGIT and its ligands, PVR and PVRL2.

1. **Administration of Blocking Agents**:
   - **PVR and PVRL2 Blocking Agents**: The method includes administering a blocking agent that targets PVR and/or PVRL2. Suitable blocking agents include monoclonal antibodies, small molecules, and other compounds that inhibit the binding of TIGIT to PVR and PVRL2. Examples of PVR and PVRL2 blocking agents include monoclonal antibodies such as anti-PVR (clone D171, NeoMarkers) and anti-PVRL2 (clone L14).
   - **TIGIT Blocking Agents**: The method also includes administering a blocking agent that targets TIGIT. Suitable TIGIT blocking agents include monoclonal antibodies and other compounds that inhibit the binding of TIGIT to its ligands. Examples of TIGIT blocking agents include monoclonal antibodies such as anti-TIGIT (Clone #A15153G, Biolegend).

2. **Combination Therapy**:
   - The blocking agents can be used alone or in combination with other immunotherapeutic agents, such as bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE®), checkpoint inhibitors, or other immune modulators. For example, the method can involve administering a blocking agent that targets the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis in combination with a BiTE® antibody construct such as AMG 330. The combination therapy can result in a synergistic effect, leading to improved therapeutic outcomes.

### Examples

#### Example 1: In Vitro Cytotoxicity Assay

**Objective**: To evaluate the effect of blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis on the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cell lines.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Lines and Culture**: AML cell lines (TF-1, Molm-13, Kasumi-1, MV4-11) were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin.
2. **Flow Cytometry**: Expression of PVR and PVRL2 on AML cell lines was measured using flow cytometry with anti-PVR (clone D171, NeoMarkers) and anti-PVRL2 (clone L14) antibodies.
3. **Cytotoxicity Assay**: AML target cells were stained with CellTracker™ Green CMFDA dye and mixed with peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from healthy donors at a ratio of 1:6. The cell mixture was incubated in the presence or absence of blocking antibodies against PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT, and the BiTE® antibody construct AMG 330 at 0.1 ng/mL. Specific lysis of the target cells was measured after 24 hours of incubation by flow cytometry using 7AAD staining.

**Results**:
- Blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis significantly enhanced the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cell lines. The specific lysis of AML cells was significantly increased in the presence of blocking antibodies against PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT, both alone and in combination with AMG 330.
- The combination of blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis with AMG 330 resulted in a synergistic effect, leading to a greater increase in cytotoxicity compared to either treatment alone.

#### Example 2: In Vivo Xenograft Model

**Objective**: To evaluate the effect of blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis on the survival of NSG mice reconstituted with human T cells and transplanted with AML cells.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Lines and Culture**: MV4-11 wildtype and PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout cells were generated using CRISPR/Cas9 technology and cultured as described in Example 1.
2. **Xenograft Model**: Female 8-week-old NSG mice were intravenously injected with 2 × 10^5 MV4-11 wildtype or PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout cells. Five days later, the mice were reconstituted with 2 × 10^7 enriched and activated T cells from a healthy donor.
3. **Treatment**: Starting from day 8, the mice were treated daily with PBS (placebo) or 15 µg/kg AMG 330 by intraperitoneal injection.
4. **Survival Analysis**: The survival of the mice was monitored over a 50-day observation period.

**Results**:
- Mice transplanted with MV4-11 PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout cells had a significant survival benefit compared to mice transplanted with MV4-11 wildtype cells, both in the placebo and AMG 330 treatment arms.
- The combination of PVR/PVRL2 double-knockout cells with AMG 330 treatment resulted in the greatest survival benefit, indicating a synergistic effect of blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis and BiTE® therapy.

#### Example 3: Clinical Significance

**Objective**: To evaluate the clinical significance of PVR and PVRL2 expression in AML patients.

**Methods**:
1. **Patient Cohorts**: Two independent patient cohorts were studied. Cohort A included 139 newly diagnosed AML patients treated within the AMLSG 07-04 study. Cohort B included 290 AML patients with microarray-based gene expression data.
2. **Gene Expression Analysis**: RNA was extracted from bone marrow and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) of AML patients, and PVR and PVRL2 expression was analyzed by RT-qPCR.
3. **Survival Analysis**: Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated for event-free survival (EFS), relapse-free survival (RFS), and overall survival (OS). Multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were used to identify independent prognostic markers.

**Results**:
- High expression of PVR and PVRL2 was associated with a negative prognosis in AML patients. High PVR expression was identified as an independent negative prognostic marker for EFS, RFS, and OS in both patient cohorts.
- High PVRL2 expression was also associated with a negative prognosis, particularly for RFS, and had a borderline significant negative impact on OS.
- These findings highlight the importance of the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis in the immune surveillance of AML and suggest that targeting this axis may be a promising therapeutic strategy.

The invention is based on the discovery that blocking the TIGIT-PVR/PVRL2 axis significantly enhances the cytotoxicity of T cells against AML cells, both in vitro and in vivo, and can lead to improved therapeutic outcomes in AML patients.
