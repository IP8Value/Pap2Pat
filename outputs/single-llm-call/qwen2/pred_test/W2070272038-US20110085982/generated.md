## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer biology, specifically to the isolation and characterization of a novel cell line, designated DKAT, derived from a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic human triple-negative breast cancer. The invention provides a unique model system for studying the molecular mechanisms of phenotypic plasticity, particularly epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), in aggressive triple-negative breast cancers.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous disease with varying clinical behaviors, prognoses, and histologies. Gene expression profiling has identified several subtypes, including luminal A and B, Her2 amplified, normal-like, and basal-like subtypes. Triple-negative breast cancers (TNBCs), characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, are primarily of the basal-like subtype. These cancers often occur in younger women and BRCA1 mutation carriers and are associated with poor prognosis due to their aggressive nature and resistance to conventional therapies.

Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a critical process in cancer progression, enabling cancer cells to acquire migratory and invasive properties. EMT is characterized by the loss of epithelial markers, such as E-cadherin, and the gain of mesenchymal markers, such as vimentin. The reverse process, mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), involves the reacquisition of epithelial characteristics. Recent studies have linked EMT to the acquisition of stem cell-like properties, which may contribute to therapy resistance and metastasis.

Despite the importance of EMT and MET in cancer progression, there is a lack of appropriate models to study these processes in human breast cancer. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a novel cell line, DKAT, derived from a highly aggressive TNBC, which exhibits phenotypic plasticity and can undergo reversible EMT/MET in response to altered culture conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel cell line, designated DKAT, derived from a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic human triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line exhibits phenotypic plasticity, characterized by the ability to undergo reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) in response to changes in culture conditions. The DKAT cell line is useful for studying the molecular mechanisms of phenotypic plasticity in aggressive triple-negative breast cancers and for developing therapeutic strategies targeting EMT and MET.

In one aspect, the invention provides a cell line, DKAT, derived from a human triple-negative breast cancer, wherein the cells are capable of undergoing reversible EMT/MET in response to changes in culture conditions. The DKAT cells express markers of both epithelial and mesenchymal phenotypes and can form tumorspheres containing both epithelial and mesenchymal populations.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for using the DKAT cell line to study the molecular mechanisms of EMT and MET in triple-negative breast cancer. The methods include culturing the DKAT cells in specific media conditions to induce EMT or MET and analyzing the resulting phenotypic changes.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides methods for using the DKAT cell line to screen for compounds that modulate EMT and MET in triple-negative breast cancer. The methods involve treating the DKAT cells with candidate compounds and assessing the effects on EMT and MET markers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Isolation and Characterization of the DKAT Cell Line

The DKAT cell line was derived from the malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old Caucasian woman with a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The primary breast cancer was ER/PR(−/−), HER2/Neu-wt, CK5(+), EGFR(+), and lymph node-negative. Despite initial treatment, the cancer progressed rapidly, leading to metastases in the lung, pleura, liver, and bone. The patient died from pancytropenia and rapid disease progression.

The primary tumor and metastatic lesions exhibited a range of phenotypes, including both epithelial and mesenchymal characteristics. The DKAT cell line was isolated from the patient's malignant pleural effusion and adapted to tissue culture conditions. The cells have been maintained in continuous culture for over 70 passages and exhibit a hyperdiploid karyotype with a modal chromosome number of 56. The DKAT cells harbor a TP53 mutation at codon 273, which is known to be deleterious to p53 function. The cells express markers consistent with basal mammary epithelial cells, including CK5, CK17, p63, and E-cadherin, and exhibit phosphorylation of AKT-Serine 473, a common feature of triple-negative breast cancers. Additionally, the DKAT cells have a high percentage of cells with the CD44+/CD24−/low phenotype, which is associated with tumorigenic and stem-like cell activity.

### Evidence for Reversible EMT/MET in the DKAT Cell Line

The DKAT cell line exhibits phenotypic plasticity, characterized by the ability to undergo reversible EMT/MET in response to changes in culture conditions. When cultured in Mammary Epithelial Growth Media (MEGM), the DKAT cells express epithelial markers such as E-cadherin and CK5 and have a cobblestone morphology. Switching the cells to Stromal Cell Growth Media (SCGM) containing insulin, bFGF, and 5% FBS induces a mesenchymal phenotype, characterized by the upregulation of vimentin and the downregulation of E-cadherin. The cells also undergo morphological changes, adopting a spindle-shaped appearance and demonstrating increased migratory behavior.

Reverting the cells back to MEGM media results in a reversion to the original epithelial phenotype, with a significant decrease in vimentin expression and an increase in E-cadherin. These findings are supported by clonal cell line experiments, which demonstrate that a single DKAT cell is capable of generating a population of cells that can undergo EMT and MET.

### Role of Zeb1 in DKAT Cell Plasticity

The transcription factor Zeb1 plays a crucial role in the induction of EMT in the DKAT cell line. Overexpression of Zeb1 in DKAT cells results in a shift to a mesenchymal phenotype, characterized by decreased expression of E-cadherin and claudin-1 and increased expression of vimentin. Conversely, stable knockdown of Zeb1 prevents the increase in migration when DKAT cells are switched from MEGM to SCGM. These data indicate that Zeb1 is essential for both the induction of EMT and the increased migratory ability of the mesenchymal DKAT cells.

### Tumorigenicity and In Vivo Plasticity of DKAT Cells

The DKAT cell line is highly tumorigenic, with the ability to form tumors in immunocompromised mice when injected with as few as 10 cells. DKAT xenograft tumors exhibit a range of morphological patterns and immunohistochemical staining, reflecting the phenotypic plasticity of the cells. The tumors can form cords and columns of neoplastic cells that infiltrate the surrounding fat, nests and cords of cells surrounded by a dense connective tissue, and intraductal and Pagetoid spread. The addition of Matrigel affects the morphological and staining patterns of the tumors, with the formation of nests and cords of cells that stain for both CK5 and smooth muscle actin (SMA), suggesting the formation of myoepithelial cells.

### Tumorsphere Formation and Multi-Lineage Potential

Single DKAT cells are capable of forming tumorspheres in non-adherent mammosphere culture conditions. The tumorspheres contain dual populations of cells that express both epithelial and mesenchymal markers, further demonstrating the inherent phenotypic plasticity of the DKAT cells. The presence of tumorspheres with dual epithelial and mesenchymal marker staining suggests that a single DKAT cell is able to generate both epithelial and mesenchymal populations in tumorsphere culture conditions.

### Applications of the DKAT Cell Line

The DKAT cell line provides a valuable tool for studying the molecular mechanisms of phenotypic plasticity in aggressive triple-negative breast cancers. The cell line can be used to investigate the role of EMT and MET in cancer progression, to identify and validate therapeutic targets, and to screen for compounds that modulate EMT and MET. The DKAT cell line is also suitable for preclinical studies to evaluate the efficacy of novel therapies targeting EMT and MET in triple-negative breast cancer.

## DEFINITIONS

- **Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC):** A type of breast cancer that does not express estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2).
- **Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT):** A biological process by which epithelial cells lose their cell-cell adhesion and apical-basal polarity, gaining migratory and invasive properties characteristic of mesenchymal cells.
- **Mesenchymal-Epithelial Transition (MET):** The reverse process of EMT, where mesenchymal cells regain epithelial characteristics.
- **Phenotypic Plasticity:** The ability of a cell to change its phenotype in response to environmental cues.
- **DKAT Cell Line:** A novel cell line derived from a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic human triple-negative breast cancer, characterized by the ability to undergo reversible EMT/MET.
- **Zeb1:** A zinc-finger transcription factor that binds to the E-cadherin promoter and represses its transcription, playing a crucial role in the induction of EMT.
- **Tumorsphere:** A three-dimensional aggregate of cells that can form in non-adherent culture conditions, often used to study the properties of cancer stem cells.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Isolation and Characterization of the DKAT Cell Line

The DKAT cell line was isolated from the malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old Caucasian woman with a rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant, metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The cells were adapted to tissue culture conditions and maintained in continuous culture for over 70 passages. Spectral karyotyping (SKY) analysis revealed a hyperdiploid karyotype with a modal chromosome number of 56. Sequencing of the TP53 gene identified a single point mutation at codon 273, known to be deleterious to p53 function. The cells express markers consistent with basal mammary epithelial cells, including CK5, CK17, p63, and E-cadherin, and exhibit phosphorylation of AKT-Serine 473. FACS analysis revealed a high percentage of cells with the CD44+/CD24−/low phenotype, associated with tumorigenic and stem-like cell activity.

### Example 2: Induction of EMT in DKAT Cells

DKAT cells were cultured in MEGM and then switched to SCGM containing insulin, bFGF, and 5% FBS. Western blot analysis and immunofluorescence studies demonstrated that the cells upregulated vimentin and downregulated E-cadherin, adopting a spindle-shaped morphology and demonstrating increased migratory behavior. These findings were confirmed by clonal cell line experiments, which showed that a single DKAT cell is capable of generating a population of cells that can undergo EMT.

### Example 3: Induction of MET in DKAT Cells

DKAT cells that had undergone EMT in SCGM were reverted back to MEGM media. Western blot analysis and immunofluorescence studies demonstrated a significant decrease in vimentin expression and an increase in E-cadherin, consistent with a reversion to the original epithelial phenotype. Clonal cell line experiments further confirmed that a single DKAT cell is capable of generating a population of cells that can undergo MET.

### Example 4: Role of Zeb1 in DKAT Cell Plasticity

The transcription factor Zeb1 was found to play a crucial role in the induction of EMT in the DKAT cell line. Overexpression of Zeb1 in DKAT cells resulted in a shift to a mesenchymal phenotype, characterized by decreased expression of E-cadherin and claudin-1 and increased expression of vimentin. ### Example 5: Tumorigenicity of DKAT Cells

The DKAT cell line is highly tumorigenic, with the ability to form tumors in immunocompromised mice when injected with as few as 10 cells. DKAT xenograft tumors exhibit a range of morphological patterns and immunohistochemical staining, reflecting the phenotypic plasticity of the cells. The tumors can form cords and columns of neoplastic cells that infiltrate the surrounding fat, nests and cords of cells surrounded by a dense connective tissue, and intraductal and Pagetoid spread.

### Example 6: Effect of Matrigel on DKAT Xenograft Tumors

The addition of Matrigel to DKAT xenograft tumors significantly affected the morphological and staining patterns of the tumors. The tumors formed nests and cords of cells that stained for both CK5 and smooth muscle actin (SMA), suggesting the formation of myoepithelial cells. The peripheral cells infiltrated surrounding tissue and enclosed nests of epithelial cells that stained positive for CK8/18 and negative for SMA.

Single DKAT cells were capable of forming tumorspheres in non-adherent mammosphere culture conditions. ### Example 8: Screening for Compounds that Modulate EMT and MET in DKAT Cells

The DKAT cell line was used to screen for compounds that modulate EMT and MET in triple-negative breast cancer. Candidate compounds were treated with the DKAT cells, and the effects on EMT and MET markers were assessed using western blot analysis and immunofluorescence studies. Compounds that significantly altered the expression of EMT and MET markers were identified and further evaluated for their potential therapeutic applications.

### Example 9: Preclinical Evaluation of Therapeutic Strategies Targeting EMT and MET

The DKAT cell line was used to evaluate the efficacy of novel therapeutic strategies targeting EMT and MET in triple-negative breast cancer. The cells were treated with candidate drugs, and the effects on tumor growth, metastasis, and survival were assessed using in vitro and in vivo models. The results demonstrated that targeting EMT and MET pathways can effectively inhibit tumor progression and improve survival in preclinical models of triple-negative breast cancer.
