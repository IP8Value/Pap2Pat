# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and specifically to the engineering of the Fc region of human IgG1 to selectively enhance the binding affinity to the inhibitory Fcγ receptor IIb (FcγRIIb) over the activating Fcγ receptor IIa (FcγRIIa). The invention provides novel Fc variants that exhibit significantly enhanced binding to FcγRIIb without increasing the binding to FcγRIIa, thereby reducing the risk of thromboembolism and rapid clearance from the bloodstream. These Fc variants can be used to improve the therapeutic efficacy of mAbs in various applications, including cancer immunotherapy and autoimmune disease treatment.

## BACKGROUND ART

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have emerged as crucial therapeutic agents for treating a wide range of diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The therapeutic efficacy of mAbs is often mediated through their interaction with Fcγ receptors (FcγRs), which are expressed on various immune cells. FcγRs can be broadly classified into activating receptors (e.g., FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) and inhibitory receptors (e.g., FcγRIIb). Activating FcγRs enhance immune responses, such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and phagocytosis, while inhibitory FcγRs modulate these responses and play a critical role in maintaining immune homeostasis.

Engineering the Fc region of mAbs to enhance their binding affinity to specific FcγRs has been a focus of extensive research. For instance, introducing substitutions such as S267E and L328F into the Fc region of human IgG1 has been shown to increase the binding affinity to FcγRIIb, thereby enhancing the therapeutic activity of mAbs. However, these substitutions also increase the binding to FcγRIIa, which can lead to unintended side effects, such as thromboembolism and rapid clearance from the bloodstream.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the need for Fc variants that selectively enhance the binding affinity to FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa. Current Fc variants, such as S267E/L328F, enhance binding to both FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa, leading to potential side effects such as thromboembolism and rapid clearance from the bloodstream. Therefore, there is a critical need for Fc variants that can selectively bind to FcγRIIb to maximize therapeutic benefits while minimizing adverse effects.

### Means for Solving the Problems

The present invention provides a novel Fc variant of human IgG1 that selectively enhances the binding affinity to FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa. The key substitution in this variant is P238D, which introduces a conformational change in the Fc region that allows for selective binding to FcγRIIb. Further optimization of this variant, designated as V12, involves additional substitutions (E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, and A330R) that significantly enhance the binding affinity to FcγRIIb without increasing the binding to FcγRIIa.

### Effects of the Invention

The Fc variant of the present invention, particularly the V12 variant, exhibits the following advantages:
1. **Selective Binding to FcγRIIb**: The V12 variant has a 217-fold higher affinity for FcγRIIb compared to wild-type IgG1, while maintaining a similar affinity for FcγRIIa.
2. **Reduced Risk of Thromboembolism**: The selective binding to FcγRIIb reduces the risk of platelet activation and aggregation, which are associated with thromboembolic complications.
3. **Improved Therapeutic Efficacy**: The V12 variant enhances the agonistic activity of anti-TNFR superfamily antibodies, such as anti-CD137, which is crucial for cancer immunotherapy.
4. **Comparable Pharmacokinetics and Stability**: The V12 variant maintains a similar in vivo half-life and storage stability compared to wild-type IgG1, making it suitable for pharmaceutical applications.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### <Substitution>

The present invention is based on the identification of a novel substitution, P238D, in the Fc region of human IgG1 that selectively enhances the binding affinity to FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa. This substitution introduces a conformational change in the Fc region, allowing for the formation of new interactions with FcγRIIb that are not possible with FcγRIIa. Specifically, the P238D substitution causes a shift in the position of Asp238, which forms novel hydrogen bonds and van der Waals contacts with residues in FcγRIIb.

### <Antibody>

The antibody of the present invention comprises the Fc region of human IgG1 with the P238D substitution. Further optimization of this variant, designated as V12, involves additional substitutions (E233D, G237D, H268D, P231G, Y296D, and A330R) that significantly enhance the binding affinity to FcγRIIb. The V12 variant is produced by expressing the heavy and light chains of the antibody in mammalian cells, such as FreeStyle™ 293F cells, and purifying the antibody using protein A.

### <Pharmaceutical Compositions>

The Fc variant of the present invention can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for various therapeutic applications. These compositions may include the Fc variant in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, and stabilizers. The compositions can be formulated for administration by various routes, including intravenous, subcutaneous, and intramuscular injection.

### Asparagine: Asn (N)

Asparagine (Asn, N) is an amino acid that plays a crucial role in the structure and function of proteins, including antibodies. In the context of the present invention, Asn is involved in the formation of N-linked glycosylation sites in the Fc region of human IgG1. The presence of N-linked glycans at Asn297 is essential for the proper folding and stability of the Fc region and its interaction with FcγRs.

### Glutamine: Gln (O)

Glutamine (Gln, O) is another amino acid that is important for the structure and function of proteins. In the context of the present invention, Gln is involved in the formation of hydrogen bonds and salt bridges that contribute to the stability and binding affinity of the Fc region to FcγRs.

### Glycine: Gly (G)

Glycine (Gly, G) is the smallest amino acid and is highly flexible. In the context of the present invention, the substitution of Pro271 to Gly (P271G) in the V12 variant allows for the release of conformational stress, which contributes to the enhanced binding affinity to FcγRIIb.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Screening and Characterization of Variants to Selectively Enhance FcγRIIb Binding by Comprehensive Mutagenesis**

A large set of over 500 variants of the Fc region of human IgG1 was generated by replacing each of about 30 residues in the lower hinge and CH2 region with every naturally occurring amino acid except cysteine. The binding affinity of these variants to human FcγRIIaH131, FcγRIIaR131, and FcγRIIb was evaluated using surface plasmon resonance (SPR). Two substitutions, P238D and L328E, were identified that selectively enhanced FcγRIIb binding while decreasing the binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes.

### Example 2

**Crystal Structure of the FcγRIIb Complex with IgG1 Fc Fragment with P238D**

The crystal structure of the complex between the Fc fragment of human IgG1 with the P238D substitution and FcγRIIb was determined using X-ray crystallography. The structure revealed that the P238D substitution caused a conformational change in the Fc region, allowing for the formation of new interactions with FcγRIIb. Specifically, Asp238 in the CH2-B domain formed a salt bridge with Arg131 of FcγRIIb, contributing to the enhanced binding affinity.

### Example 3

**Further Optimization of FcγRIIb Binding by Comprehensive Mutagenesis Using the P238D Variant as a Template**

Approximately 400 variants with an additional single substitution onto the P238D variant were generated, and the binding affinity to FcγRIIb was determined. Six effective substitutions (E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, and A330R) were identified. The V12 variant, which includes all six substitutions, showed a 217-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb compared to the P238D variant.

### Example 4

**Crystal Structure of the FcγRIIb Complex with Fc(V12)**

The crystal structure of the complex between the Fc fragment of the V12 variant and FcγRIIb was determined using X-ray crystallography. The structure revealed that the V12 variant retained the conformational changes induced by the P238D substitution and introduced additional interactions that further enhanced the binding affinity to FcγRIIb. Specifically, the P271G substitution allowed for the formation of a salt bridge between Asp270 and Arg131 of FcγRIIb.

### Example 5

**Pharmacokinetic and Biophysical Property Assessment of V12 Variant**

The pharmacokinetic properties of the V12 variant were assessed by measuring its binding affinity to human FcRn and its in vivo half-life in human FcRn transgenic mice. The V12 variant showed a binding affinity to hFcRn comparable to that of wild-type IgG1 and had a similar in vivo half-life. The thermal stability of the V12 variant was also evaluated, and it was found to have a melting temperature (TM) of 62°C, which is 8°C lower than that of wild-type IgG1. However, the V12 variant showed comparable stability to wild-type IgG1 in terms of high-molecular-weight (HMW) species formation after storage at 100 mg/ml for 4 weeks at 25°C.

### Example 6

**In Vitro Activation and Aggregation of Platelets by Immune Complexes Consisting of IgE and Anti-IgE Antibody with S267E/L328F Variant or V12 Variant**

Immune complexes (ICs) consisting of IgE and anti-IgE antibodies with the S267E/L328F variant or V12 variant were incubated with platelets obtained from donors homozygous for FcγRIIaR131 or FcγRIIaH131 genotypes. The S267E/L328F variant induced platelet activation and aggregation in platelets from donors with the FcγRIIaR131 genotype, while the V12 variant did not. This demonstrates that the V12 variant selectively enhances binding to FcγRIIb without increasing the risk of thromboembolism.

### Example 7

**Enhancement of Agonistic Activity of Anti-CD137 Antibody with Enhanced FcγRIIb Binding Fc**

The agonistic activity of anti-CD137 antibody was evaluated using a co-culture system of mouse T lymphoma cells (CTLL-2) and human B lymphoma cells (Raji). The V12 variant, as well as the S267E/L328F variant, significantly enhanced the production of mouse IFN-γ compared to wild-type IgG1. This demonstrates that the V12 variant can enhance the agonistic activity of anti-TNFR superfamily antibodies, which is crucial for cancer immunotherapy.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The Fc variant of the present invention, particularly the V12 variant, has significant industrial applicability in the development of therapeutic antibodies. The selective enhancement of binding to FcγRIIb over FcγRIIa reduces the risk of thromboembolism and rapid clearance from the bloodstream, making it suitable for a wide range of therapeutic applications, including cancer immunotherapy, autoimmune disease treatment, and the modulation of immune responses. The V12 variant can be incorporated into existing mAbs to improve their therapeutic efficacy and safety, thereby providing a valuable tool for the biopharmaceutical industry.
