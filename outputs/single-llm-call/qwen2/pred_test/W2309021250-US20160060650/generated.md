# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of plant biotechnology, specifically to the genetic modification of potato plants to express a cellulose binding domain (CBD) protein derived from the phytopathogen *Phytophthora infestans*. The invention addresses the problem of sterility in certain potato cultivars, particularly the Bintje cultivar, which typically fails to produce seed berries. The expression of the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein in transgenic Bintje potato plants has been found to restore female fertility, enabling the production of seed berries and viable seeds. This discovery has significant implications for breeding programs aimed at developing new potato varieties with enhanced traits, such as late blight resistance and diverse tuber colors.

Cellulose binding domains (CBDs) are a subset of carbohydrate binding modules (CBMs) commonly found in saprophyte-encoded cellulolytic enzymes. These domains facilitate the adherence of enzymes to carbohydrate substrates, and some CBDs can even dissociate cellulose microfibrils. The *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein is tightly associated with the cellulose-containing hyphal cell wall and plays a role in cell wall assembly and integrity. The expression of this protein in transgenic potato plants was hypothesized to alter host-pathogen interactions, potentially by shielding host cellulose molecules from pathogen endoglucanases or vice versa. However, the unexpected outcome was the restoration of female fertility in the Bintje cultivar, which had previously been sterile.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for restoring female fertility in sterile potato plants by expressing a cellulose binding domain (CBD) protein derived from *Phytophthora infestans* in the plants. The invention further encompasses transgenic potato plants expressing the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein, which are capable of forming seed berries and producing viable seeds. The restored fertility allows for the development of new potato varieties through breeding, including the introduction of late blight resistance and diverse tuber colors.

In one aspect, the invention provides a transgenic potato plant comprising a nucleic acid sequence encoding the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein operably linked to a promoter functional in plant cells. The expression of the CBD 1 protein in the transgenic plant results in the formation of seed berries and the production of viable seeds, thereby restoring female fertility.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for producing a transgenic potato plant with restored female fertility, comprising the steps of: (a) introducing a nucleic acid sequence encoding the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein into a potato plant cell; (b) regenerating a transgenic potato plant from the transformed plant cell; and (c) selecting a transgenic potato plant that expresses the CBD 1 protein and forms seed berries.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for breeding new potato varieties, comprising the steps of: (a) crossing a transgenic potato plant expressing the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein with a fertile donor plant; (b) selecting progeny plants that inherit the CBD 1 protein and form seed berries; and (c) further selecting progeny plants that exhibit desired traits, such as late blight resistance or diverse tuber colors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to the genetic modification of potato plants to express a cellulose binding domain (CBD) protein derived from *Phytophthora infestans*, specifically the CBD 1 protein. The expression of this protein in transgenic potato plants has been found to restore female fertility, enabling the production of seed berries and viable seeds. This discovery has significant implications for breeding programs aimed at developing new potato varieties with enhanced traits.

### Cellulose Binding Domain (CBD) Protein

The *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein is a member of the carbohydrate binding module (CBM) family and is known to bind to cellulose. In its native context, the CBD 1 protein is tightly associated with the cellulose-containing hyphal cell wall of *P. infestans* and plays a role in cell wall assembly and integrity. The amino acid sequence of the CBD 1 protein is provided in SEQ ID NO: 1, and the corresponding nucleic acid sequence is provided in SEQ ID NO: 2.

### Genetic Modification of Potato Plants

To restore female fertility in sterile potato plants, the nucleic acid sequence encoding the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein is introduced into potato plant cells. The nucleic acid sequence is operably linked to a promoter functional in plant cells, such as the cauliflower mosaic virus (CaMV) 35S promoter, to drive the expression of the CBD 1 protein. The construct is typically introduced into potato plant cells using standard transformation techniques, such as Agrobacterium-mediated transformation.

#### Transformation and Selection

Transgenic potato plants are generated using 4-week-old tissue culture-grown plantlets. Leaves are excised, cut laterally, and immersed for 15 minutes in an acetosyringone-induced (100 μM) culture of *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* LBA4404 containing the CBD 1 gene in the binary vector pBI121. The transformed plantlets are then selected and maintained on Murashige and Skoog (MS) media in a growth chamber. DNA samples from putative transformants are screened by PCR using 35S-specific primers, and the PCR products are sequenced to confirm the presence of the CBD 1 gene.

#### Integration Sites

The integration sites of the CBD 1 gene in the potato genome are determined using the APAgene GOLD Genome Walking Kit. Forward primers specific to the 3′ end of the CBD 1 gene and the T-DNA border of pBI121 are used in sequential PCR reactions. The final reactions are separated on an agarose gel, purified, cloned, and sequenced to identify the integration sites.

### Expression and Localization of CBD 1 Protein

The expression of the CBD 1 protein in transgenic potato plants is confirmed by immunolocalization. Developing seed berry cross-sections are fixed and rehydrated, and antibody localization is performed using peptide-specific CBD 1 antibodies. The secondary goat anti-rabbit antibody conjugated to alkaline phosphatase is used to visualize the CBD 1 protein. Control samples treated with rabbit pre-immune serum and the secondary antibody show no purple precipitant, confirming the specificity of the CBD 1 antibody.

### Phenotypic Characteristics of Transgenic Plants

Transgenic Bintje potato plants expressing the CBD 1 protein exhibit several phenotypic characteristics. Some transgenic lines show increased height compared to control plants. More importantly, the transgenic plants produce numerous seed berries, each containing viable seeds when out-crossed with donor pollen. The CBD 1 protein is abundantly localized in the wall and locule tissues of developing seed berries, as confirmed by immunolocalization.

### Breeding Studies

The transgenic Bintje plants expressing the CBD 1 protein are used as female parents in breeding studies. Pollen from late blight-resistant cultivars, such as Sarpo Mira, or from cultivars with desirable tuber colors, such as Peter Wilcox, is applied to the stigmas of the transgenic Bintje plants. The resulting seed berries are allowed to mature, and the seeds are harvested and dried. A subset of the seeds is planted to assess the progeny for desired traits, such as late blight resistance and tuber color.

### Late Blight Resistance Screening

The late blight resistance of the transgenic Bintje plants and their progeny is evaluated by inoculating leaflets with *P. infestans* sporangia and zoospores. The inoculated leaves are incubated under controlled conditions, and disease progress is scored relative to control Bintje leaflets. The transgenic Bintje plants themselves remain highly susceptible to late blight, but the resistance trait can be transferred to the progeny through breeding with late blight-resistant cultivars.

### Potential Applications

The restoration of female fertility in sterile potato cultivars through the expression of the *P. infestans* CBD 1 protein has several potential applications. It enables the development of new potato varieties with enhanced traits, such as late blight resistance and diverse tuber colors. Additionally, the CBD 1 protein can be used to produce seed berries in other sterile potato cultivars, expanding the genetic diversity of the potato gene pool. The method can also be applied to other Solanaceous crops, such as tomatoes, to modify fruit development and shape.
