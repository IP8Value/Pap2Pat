# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of bioluminescence imaging (BLI) and, more specifically, to novel near-infrared (NIR) bioluminescent systems and methods for their use in deep tissue imaging. The invention encompasses the design and characterization of two naphthyl-based luciferin analogs, amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH2-NpLH2) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH2), and the development of a mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) that can efficiently utilize these substrates to produce NIR bioluminescence. The invention also includes methods for using these substrates and the mutant luciferase in various imaging applications, particularly for deep tissue imaging in living organisms.

## BACKGROUND

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI) using firefly luciferase (Luc2) and D-luciferin (D-LH2) has become a standard method for gene expression analysis and preclinical evaluation of potential therapies in mouse models. The Luc2/D-LH2 system is widely adopted because the light it produces peaks near 600 nm at 37°C and can adequately penetrate shallow tissues such as skin. However, in deeper tissues such as the lung, brain, and bone, the sensitivity of Luc2/D-LH2 is limited due to absorption by hemoglobin, melanin, and other tissue components. Additionally, the biodistribution of D-LH2 is often insufficient for sustained imaging in challenging tissues, such as the brain.

To improve resolution for deep tissue imaging, attempts have been made to shift the wavelength of bioluminescence emission into the near-infrared (NIR) (650‒900 nm). Mutagenesis has been used successfully to red-shift the spectral properties of luciferases (utilizing D-LH2 as a substrate), but mutants with a significant NIR component to their emission have been elusive. Analogs of D-LH2 with extended π conjugation to support longer wavelength photon generation have been developed that produce NIR bioluminescence with Luc2, and aminoluciferin-NIR dye conjugates have been shown to produce NIR signals via energy transfer. Despite these efforts, the utility of these substrates is still limited due to the fact that bioluminescence signals are only enhanced over Luc2/D-LH2 at limited substrate concentrations.

The present invention addresses these challenges by creating improved substrates and luciferases. Specifically, the invention involves the design of two naphthyl-based luciferin analogs, NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2, and the engineering of a mutant luciferase, CBR2, that can efficiently utilize these substrates to produce NIR bioluminescence. The mutant enzyme, CBR2, is encoded by a codon-optimized gene sequence, CBR2opt, which enhances its expression and stability in live cells. The combination of CBR2opt with NH2-NpLH2 provides significant improvements in signal intensity and resolution for deep tissue imaging, particularly in the brain.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### 1. DEFINITIONS

- **Bioluminescence Imaging (BLI):** A non-invasive technique that uses bioluminescent reporters to monitor biological processes in living organisms.
- **Click Beetle Red Luciferase (CBR):** A luciferase enzyme from the click beetle that emits red light.
- **Mutant Click Beetle Red Luciferase (CBR2):** A variant of CBR with improved properties for producing NIR bioluminescence.
- **Codon-Optimized Gene Sequence (CBR2opt):** A gene sequence encoding CBR2 that has been optimized for expression in mammalian cells.
- **Naphthyl-Based Luciferin Analog:** A synthetic luciferin analog with an extended π conjugation system, such as NH2-NpLH2 or OH-NpLH2.
- **Near-Infrared (NIR):** Light with wavelengths in the range of 650-900 nm, which has improved tissue penetration compared to visible light.
- **Bioluminescence Tomography (BLT):** A technique that reconstructs the three-dimensional distribution of bioluminescent sources within a living organism.

### 2. CBR VARIANTS

The present invention involves the design and characterization of a mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) that can efficiently utilize naphthyl-based luciferin analogs to produce NIR bioluminescence. The mutant enzyme, CBR2, is encoded by a codon-optimized gene sequence, CBR2opt, which enhances its expression and stability in live cells.

#### Design of CBR2

To optimize CBR for enhanced luminescence with the NIR substrates, a homology model of the enzyme was built based on the X-ray structure of a related firefly luciferase with bound 5′-O-[N-(dehydroluciferyl)-sulfamoyl] adenosine (DLSA). The model was used to identify key residues in the active site that could be modified to improve enzyme efficiency. Specifically, residues R334 and G351 were targeted for mutagenesis. The mutant enzyme of highest interest, R334S+G351R (CBR2), was purified and further characterized.

#### Characterization of CBR2

CBR2 was found to produce luminescence comparable to CBR with OH-NpLH2, and the spectral emission peak was slightly blue-shifted to 743 nm. Surprisingly, the peak emission for CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 was shifted by more than 65 nm (to 730 nm). CBR2/D-LH2 produced about 2-fold less signal compared to CBR/D-LH2, and peak emission (614 nm) was unchanged.

### 3. NEAR-INFRARED BIOLUMINESCENCE SYSTEMS

The invention provides novel near-infrared (NIR) bioluminescent systems comprising the mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) and naphthyl-based luciferin analogs, specifically NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2. These systems are designed to produce bioluminescence with peak emissions in the NIR range, which improves tissue penetration and reduces absorption by hemoglobin and other tissue components.

#### NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2

NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 are naphthyl-based luciferin analogs with extended π conjugation systems. NH2-NpLH2 produces a peak emission at 730 nm, while OH-NpLH2 produces a peak emission at 743 nm. Both substrates were tested with several beetle luciferase enzymes, and CBR2 was found to be the most compatible enzyme for producing NIR bioluminescence.

### 4. METHODS OF USING THE CBR VARIANTS AND/OR BIOLUMINESCENT SYSTEMS

The invention provides methods for using the CBR2 variants and the NIR bioluminescent systems in various imaging applications, particularly for deep tissue imaging in living organisms.

#### Deep Tissue Imaging

The combination of CBR2opt and NH2-NpLH2 provides significant improvements in signal intensity and resolution for deep tissue imaging, particularly in the brain. The method involves administering a composition comprising a cell expressing CBR2 and NH2-NpLH2 to a living organism and detecting the bioluminescence emitted by the cell.

#### Bioluminescence Tomography

The invention also provides methods for performing bioluminescence tomography (BLT) using the CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system. BLT involves reconstructing the three-dimensional distribution of bioluminescent sources within a living organism. The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system facilitates the use of multispectral acquisitions required for BLT, enabling high-resolution imaging of deep tissue structures.

### 7. EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Characterization of NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2

NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 were synthesized using conventional luciferin chemistry with high enantiopurity. The monopotassium salt forms of NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 were formulated and found to have good aqueous solubility and stability at ambient temperature in PBS for at least 24 hours. Neither compound was cytotoxic in HEK-293 or HeLa cells, and no acute toxicity or adverse effects were observed in mice when administered intraperitoneally at high concentrations.

#### Example 2: Design of CBR2 Luciferase and CBR2opt Gene Sequence

A homology model of CBR was built using the X-ray structure of a related firefly luciferase with bound DLSA. Residues R334 and G351 were targeted for mutagenesis, and the mutant enzyme R334S+G351R (CBR2) was purified and characterized. The gene sequence for CBR2 was optimized (CBR2opt) to match codon frequencies as found in mice.

#### Example 3: Characterization of CBR2 with D-LH2 and Naphthyl-Luciferins

CBR2 was found to produce luminescence comparable to CBR with OH-NpLH2, and the spectral emission peak was slightly blue-shifted to 743 nm. The peak emission for CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 was shifted by more than 65 nm (to 730 nm). CBR2/D-LH2 produced about 2-fold less signal compared to CBR/D-LH2, and peak emission (614 nm) was unchanged.

#### Example 4: Characterization in Cells

Stable HEK-293 cell lines expressing either Luc2 or CBR2opt were created by lentiviral transduction. The cells were characterized for bioluminescence emission using four substrates: D-LH2, NH2-NpLH2, OH-NpLH2, and Aka-HCl. CBR2opt/D-LH2 produced the highest signal, and CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 and Luc2/Aka-HCl cells also gave strong signals.

#### Example 5: Imaging Skin of C57BL/6 Black Fur Mice

C57BL/6 black fur mice were implanted with 1 × 10^6 HEK-293 cells stably expressing either CBR2opt or Luc2. The combination of CBR2opt/D-LH2 produced nearly 8-fold higher signal compared to Luc2/D-LH2. The signal from both enzymes when paired with NH2-NpLH2 was only about 2-fold lower than Luc2/D-LH2, suggesting that the NIR photons produced by NH2-NpLH2 are less prone to absorption by the black fur.

#### Example 6: Deep Brain Imaging

Cells stably expressing CBR2opt or Luc2 were implanted at 3 mm depth in mouse brains. The bioluminescence signal from CBR2opt/D-LH2 was 2-fold higher than the signal from Luc2/D-LH2, and approximately 3-fold higher than the signal from CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2. The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system provided improved spatial resolution, allowing for the visualization of cell migration in the brain.

#### Example 7: Bioluminescence Tomography in Mouse Brain

The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system was used for bioluminescence tomography in mouse brain. The system facilitated the use of multispectral acquisitions required for BLT, enabling high-resolution imaging of deep tissue structures. The reconstructed images accurately localized the bioluminescent sources in the brain, confirming the accuracy of the CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system for deep tissue imaging.

The present invention provides a novel bioluminescent system comprising a mutant click beetle red luciferase (CBR2) and naphthyl-based luciferin analogs, specifically NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2. The invention also includes methods for using these substrates and the mutant luciferase in various imaging applications, particularly for deep tissue imaging in living organisms. The CBR2opt/NH2-NpLH2 system offers significant improvements in signal intensity and resolution, making it a valuable tool for bioluminescence imaging and bioluminescence tomography.
