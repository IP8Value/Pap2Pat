# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Intracellular pathogens, such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, pose significant challenges in medical diagnostics due to their ability to survive within host cells, such as macrophages and dendritic cells. This intracellular residence complicates bacterial detection, leading to delayed diagnosis and inappropriate treatment. Traditional serological immunoassays are ineffective for detecting intracellular pathogens in biological fluids like blood and lymphatic fluid, as the microorganisms are sequestered within immune cells. Consequently, more advanced methods, such as Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR), are required to isolate infected cells and extract their DNA for pathogen identification. While PCR is highly specific and sensitive, it is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and requires pure DNA samples, which are often difficult to obtain from complex biological samples. Additionally, culturing methods, although effective, can take weeks or even months, especially for slow-growing intracellular pathogens, and may not always yield viable bacteria.

Nanotechnology offers a promising alternative for the rapid and accurate detection of intracellular pathogens. Magnetic relaxation nanosensors (MRS) are particularly advantageous due to their unique size-dependent electronic, magnetic, and luminescence properties. These sensors consist of polymer-coated iron oxide nanoparticles conjugated with affinity ligands that facilitate binding and magnetic detection of specific targets. Upon target binding, changes in the sample's magnetic resonance signal, specifically the water proton relaxation time (T2), occur, correlating with the target concentration. This method allows for sensitive detection even in turbid and minimally processed samples, making it a valuable tool for point-of-care diagnostics.

This invention introduces hybridizing magnetic relaxation nanosensors (hMRS) designed to detect a specific genomic marker of Mycobacterium avium spp. paratuberculosis (MAP), an intracellular pathogen associated with Johne's disease in cattle and potentially linked to Crohn's disease in humans. The hMRS are engineered to bind to the IS900 genomic element of MAP, enabling rapid and sensitive detection in clinical samples.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and system for the rapid and sensitive detection of intracellular pathogens, specifically Mycobacterium avium spp. paratuberculosis (MAP), using hybridizing magnetic relaxation nanosensors (hMRS). The hMRS are designed to bind to the IS900 genomic element of MAP, a highly conserved DNA sequence unique to this pathogen. The invention includes the design, synthesis, and application of hMRS for detecting MAP in minimally processed clinical samples, such as peripheral blood and tissue homogenates. The hMRS method offers several advantages over traditional PCR and culturing techniques, including faster detection times, higher sensitivity, and reduced sample preparation requirements. The invention also encompasses the use of hMRS for the simultaneous detection of genomic and epitope markers of MAP, enhancing the accuracy and reliability of pathogen identification.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Design and Synthesis of hMRS

The hMRS are composed of polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles conjugated with an oligonucleotide sequence complementary to the IS900 genomic element of MAP. The nanoparticles are synthesized using propargylated polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles, which are functionalized with azide-modified oligonucleotides via "click" chemistry. The resulting hMRS have a diameter of approximately 78 ± 3 nm, a zeta potential of -35 mV, and an average of 55 oligonucleotides per nanoparticle. The relaxivities (R1 and R2) of the hMRS are 45 mM-1s-1 and 60 mM-1s-1, respectively, at 0.47T.

The synthesis process involves the following steps:
1. **Propargylation of Iron Oxide Nanoparticles**: Propargyl groups are incorporated onto polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles using carbodiimide chemistry, followed by magnetic separation.
2. **Conjugation of Azide-Modified Oligonucleotides**: The propargyl-functionalized nanoparticles are reacted with an azide-modified 15-bp oligonucleotide (5′-ATGTGGTTGCTGTGT-3′) complementary to the IS900 sequence using "click" chemistry.
3. **Dialysis and Purification**: The resulting hMRS are dialyzed against deionized water and magnetically separated to remove unreacted reagents.
4. **Characterization**: The size, zeta potential, and relaxivities of the hMRS are characterized using dynamic light scattering, zetasizer, and a compact relaxometer, respectively.

### Discussion

The hMRS method offers several advantages over traditional PCR and culturing techniques for the detection of intracellular pathogens, particularly MAP. The key benefits include:
1. **Rapid Detection**: hMRS can detect MAP in clinical samples within 30 minutes, significantly reducing the time required for diagnosis compared to PCR, which can take several hours, and culturing, which can take weeks.
2. **High Sensitivity**: hMRS can detect a single copy of the IS900 genomic element, even in minimally processed samples, outperforming PCR in terms of sensitivity.
3. **Minimal Sample Preparation**: The hMRS method requires only a 30-minute crude DNA extraction protocol, eliminating the need for complex and time-consuming sample purification steps.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness**: The hMRS are relatively inexpensive to produce and do not require multiple oligonucleotide primer pairs, expensive enzymes, or nucleotides, making the method more cost-effective.
5. **Point-of-Care Application**: The hMRS can be used with portable magnetic relaxometers, making it suitable for point-of-care screening in various settings.

### Methods

#### Preparation of IS900-Specific hMRS

1. **Propargylation of Iron Oxide Nanoparticles**:
   - Incorporate propargyl groups on polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles using carbodiimide chemistry.
   - Perform magnetic separation using an LS25 MACS column.

2. **Conjugation of Azide-Modified Oligonucleotides**:
   - Resuspend 400 µL propargylated iron oxide nanoparticles in 1,100 µL NaHCO3 buffer (0.1 M, pH 8.4).
   - Add 200 µL of 10 mM TCEP as a reducing agent.
   - Dilute 20 µL of 6.3 M oligonucleotide in 80 µL NaHCO3 buffer and add to the nanoparticle solution.
   - Initiate the reaction with the dropwise addition of 150 µL Cu(I)-TBTA complex.
   - Incubate at room temperature for 3 hours, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C.

3. **Dialysis and Purification**:
   - Dialyze the resulting hMRS against deionized water using a 6,000 – 8,000 MWCO membrane.
   - Perform magnetic separation using an LS25 MACS column.

4. **Characterization**:
   - Determine the size using dynamic light scattering.
   - Measure the zeta potential using a Malvern zetasizer.
   - Determine the relaxivities using a compact relaxometer.
   - Quantify the oligonucleotide concentration using a Nanodrop 1000 spectrophotometer.

#### Bacterial Cultures, Isolates, and Homogenized Tissue

- Grow MAP and other bacterial cultures in appropriate media.
- Heat-inactivate bacterial cultures by autoclaving for 10 minutes.
- Store inactivated bacterial stocks in a Fisher Isotemp freezer.
- Obtain clinical isolates and tissue specimens from tissue collections.

#### Extraction of Pure and Crude Bacterial DNA from Bacterial Cultures

1. **Crude DNA Extraction**:
   - Transfer 1 mL of bacterial culture to microcentrifuge tubes.
   - Centrifuge at 13,200 rpm.
   - Resuspend pellets in 120 µL sterile TE buffer and incubate at 100°C.

2. **Pure DNA Extraction**:
   - Place heated samples on ice for 15 minutes.
   - Centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C.
   - Transfer supernatants to Phase-lock gel tubes and supplement with phenol/chloroform/isoamyl alcohol.
   - Centrifuge for 5 minutes at 12,000 rpm at 4°C.
   - Precipitate DNA using 400 µL 100% ethanol cooled to -20°C.
   - Wash, dry, and reconstitute DNA in 50 µL TE buffer.

#### hMRS and Nested PCR Experiments

1. **Relaxation-Mediated Experiments**:
   - Prepare serial dilutions of bacterial DNA in TE buffer.
   - Incubate 200 µL of nanoparticle suspension (6 µg Fe/mL) with 1 µL of bacterial DNA or control.
   - Heat samples twice for 3 minutes at 95°C.
   - Cool samples at room temperature and measure relaxation times using a magnetic relaxometer.

2. **Specificity Assays**:
   - Add 1 µL of a complementary synthetic target (5′-TCCTTCGGCCATCCAACACAGCAACCACAT-3′) to hMRS mycobacterial samples.
   - Perform similar assays using other Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

3. **Sensitivity Experiments**:
   - Prepare serial dilutions of pure and crude MAP DNA from pure cultures.
   - Perform nested PCR (nPCR) as previously described.

#### Blood Samples, Extraction of Bacterial DNA from White Blood Cells, and MAP Culturing

1. **Blood Sample Collection**:
   - Collect blood samples from healthy individuals and patients with Crohn's disease.
   - Isolate white blood cells via centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes at room temperature.

2. **Crude DNA Extraction**:
   - Centrifuge isolated white blood cells at 13,200 rpm for 2 minutes.
   - Resuspend pellets in 120 µL sterile TE buffer and incubate at 100°C for 30 minutes.
   - Cool and centrifuge at 12,000 rpm to obtain supernatants rich in crude DNA.

3. **Pure DNA Extraction**:
   - Perform phenol/chloroform/isoamyl alcohol precipitation as described above.

4. **MAP Culturing**:
   - Prepare buffy coat samples in sterile phosphate-buffered saline.
   - Inoculate MGIT bottles and incubate at 37°C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere for 12 weeks.
   - Extract pure DNA from cultured isolates.

#### Measurement of Proton Relaxation Times

- Measure spin-spin relaxation times (T2) using a 0.47 T mq20 NMR analyzer.
- Perform T2 measurements using a CPMG pulse-echo train with a 1.5 ms interpulse spacing.
- Define the change in magnetic resonance signal (ΔΤ2 +) as the sample's T2 minus the magnetic signal of the corresponding negative control.

#### Statistical Analysis

- Perform clinical diagnosis and sample collection based on clinical and endoscopic criteria.
- Identify IS900-positive samples by the hMRS method with an average ΔΤ2 + higher than 1.5 ms.
- Perform nested PCR and mark the presence of a 298-bp nucleotide sequence as positive.
- Analyze data using the SPSS package with a 95% confidence level.
- Perform ROC analysis to correlate the ΔΤ2 + signal to bacterial genome copies.
