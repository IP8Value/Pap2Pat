# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the use of naltrexone hydrochloride as an antagonist of toll-like receptors (TLRs), particularly TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9, and its application in the treatment of inflammatory conditions and cancers. Specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for inhibiting the production of inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6 and TNF-α, by peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) following stimulation with TLR ligands.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Naltrexone hydrochloride is a well-known opioid antagonist used primarily in the treatment of opioid and alcohol dependence. It specifically inhibits the mu and, to a lesser extent, the delta opioid receptors, thereby preventing the euphoric effects of alcohol or opioids. Recent studies have suggested that low-dose naltrexone (LDN) may have broader therapeutic benefits, including the treatment of various inflammatory conditions and certain types of cancer. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying these effects remain incompletely understood.

Toll-like receptors (TLRs) are a family of pattern recognition receptors that play a crucial role in the innate immune response by recognizing conserved molecular patterns and nucleic acids. There are ten TLRs in humans, each with distinct cellular locations and expression profiles. TLR1, TLR2, TLR4, TLR5, TLR6, and TLR10 are expressed on the cell surface, while TLR3, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 are located within endosomes. TLRs are involved in the recognition of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), leading to the activation of immune responses. Inappropriate TLR activity and recognition of self-ligands are associated with inflammatory conditions and autoimmunity.

Previous studies have shown that naltrexone and related opioid antagonists can inhibit the activity of TLR4, suggesting a potential role for naltrexone in modulating TLR-mediated immune responses. However, the effects of naltrexone on other TLRs, particularly TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9, have not been fully explored.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Mechanism of Action

Naltrexone hydrochloride is a potent opioid antagonist that has been shown to inhibit the activity of toll-like receptors (TLRs), particularly TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9. The mechanism by which naltrexone exerts its inhibitory effects on TLRs is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve the modulation of signaling pathways downstream of TLR activation. TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 signal through the MyD88-dependent pathway, which leads to the activation of nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) and the subsequent production of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α. Naltrexone appears to interfere with this signaling pathway, thereby reducing cytokine production.

#### Inhibition of Cytokine Production

The present invention demonstrates that naltrexone hydrochloride can effectively inhibit the production of IL-6 and TNF-α in PBMCs following stimulation with TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 ligands. This inhibition is specific to these TLRs, as naltrexone does not affect cytokine production following stimulation with TLR4 ligands. The specificity of naltrexone's inhibitory effects suggests that it may interact with TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9 at a unique site, distinct from the binding site for TLR4 ligands.

#### Therapeutic Applications

The ability of naltrexone hydrochloride to inhibit TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9-mediated cytokine production has significant therapeutic implications. Inflammatory conditions characterized by excessive TLR activation, such as Crohn's disease, multiple sclerosis, and fibromyalgia, may benefit from treatment with naltrexone. Similarly, cancers that rely on TLR-mediated immune evasion, such as B cell lymphoma and pancreatic cancer, may be effectively treated with naltrexone.

#### Pharmaceutical Compositions

These compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and topical. The choice of formulation will depend on the specific condition being treated and the preferences of the healthcare provider.

#### Dosage Regimens

The effective dose of naltrexone hydrochloride for treating inflammatory conditions and cancers will depend on various factors, including the severity of the condition, the patient's age and weight, and the route of administration. Generally, low doses of naltrexone (LDN) are preferred, as they have been shown to exert immunomodulatory effects without the side effects associated with higher doses. Typical dosages may range from about 1 to 5 mg per day, administered once daily.

#### Safety and Efficacy

Naltrexone hydrochloride has a well-established safety profile, with minimal side effects at low doses. Common side effects include nausea, headache, and dizziness, which are generally mild and transient. The invention provides methods for monitoring the safety and efficacy of naltrexone treatment, including regular assessments of cytokine levels and clinical symptoms.

### Examples

#### Example 1—Identification of Naltrexone as an Antagonist of TLR9

In this example, we demonstrate that naltrexone hydrochloride can inhibit the production of IL-6 and TNF-α in PBMCs following stimulation with a TLR9 ligand, CpG-ODN 2395.

**Materials and Methods:**

- **Cell Culture:** Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from leukocyte cones (NHS Blood Donor Service) by density centrifugation over Histopaque (Sigma-Aldrich). PBMCs were resuspended at a concentration of 10^6 cells/ml in RPMI-1640 (Sigma-Aldrich) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Sigma-Aldrich), penicillin, and streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich).
- **Cell Stimulation:** PBMCs were stimulated with 1 µM CpG-ODN 2395 (Invivogen) in the presence or absence of naltrexone hydrochloride (1-200 µM) for 24 hours.
- **IL-6 ELISA:** Cell-free supernatants were collected, and IL-6 production was determined by ELISA using an IL-6 ELISA kit (BD Bioscience) according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Intracellular Cytokine Staining:** PBMCs were stimulated with 1 µM CpG-ODN 2395 and 200 µM naltrexone for 6 hours in the presence of brefeldin A (eBioscience) for the last 4 hours. Cells were then stained for cell surface markers and intracellular IL-6 and TNF-α using flow cytometry.

**Results:**

- **IL-6 Production:** Naltrexone hydrochloride significantly inhibited IL-6 production in PBMCs following stimulation with CpG-ODN 2395 (p < 0.05).
- **Intracellular Cytokine Staining:** Flow cytometric analysis revealed that naltrexone inhibited IL-6 production in B cells and TNF-α production in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) following TLR9 stimulation.

#### Example 2—LDN in the Treatment of Melanoma

In this example, we evaluate the efficacy of low-dose naltrexone (LDN) in the treatment of melanoma.

**Materials and Methods:**

- **Animal Model:** C57BL/6 mice were inoculated subcutaneously with B16F10 melanoma cells.
- **Treatment:** Mice were treated with either vehicle (control) or LDN (1-5 mg/kg/day) via oral gavage for 21 days.
- **Tumor Measurement:** Tumor size was measured every 3 days using calipers, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: (length × width^2)/2.
- **Cytokine Analysis:** Serum levels of IL-6 and TNF-α were measured by ELISA at the end of the treatment period.

**Results:**

- **Tumor Growth:** LDN treatment significantly reduced tumor growth compared to the control group (p < 0.05).
- **Cytokine Levels:** LDN treatment led to a significant decrease in serum levels of IL-6 and TNF-α (p < 0.05).

#### Example 3—LDN in the Treatment of Prostate Cancer

In this example, we assess the effectiveness of low-dose naltrexone (LDN) in the treatment of prostate cancer.

**Materials and Methods:**

- **Cell Lines:** Human prostate cancer cell lines (PC3 and LNCaP) were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Sigma-Aldrich).
- **Cell Stimulation:** Cells were treated with either vehicle (control) or LDN (1-5 µM) for 48 hours.
- **Cell Viability:** Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay.
- **Cytokine Analysis:** Supernatants were collected, and IL-6 and TNF-α levels were measured by ELISA.

**Results:**

- **Cell Viability:** LDN treatment did not significantly affect cell viability in PC3 and LNCaP cells.
- **Cytokine Levels:** LDN treatment led to a significant reduction in IL-6 and TNF-α production in both PC3 and LNCaP cells (p < 0.05).
