# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of electrochemical sensing, particularly to the development of mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) incorporating metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) for the potentiometric detection of molecular ions. More specifically, the invention provides a novel method for the fabrication of ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) using a specific MOSC, designated as 1-Co, which exhibits high selectivity and sensitivity towards methylene blue (MB+) and similar molecular ions.

## BACKGROUND

Electrochemical sensing plays a crucial role in various fields, including environmental monitoring, food quality control, and biomedical surveillance. Ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) are widely used for the detection of specific ions in solution due to their simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and portability. Traditional ISEs typically employ organic compounds known as ionophores, which selectively bind to target ions, thereby enabling the detection of these ions in solution. However, the selectivity and sensitivity of traditional ionophores are often limited, especially for larger molecular ions.

Metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) represent a new class of supramolecular structures that offer enhanced selectivity and sensitivity for the detection of molecular ions. These structures are characterized by their well-defined cavities, which can be tailored to fit specific molecular ions, thereby improving the binding affinity and selectivity of the ionophore. One such MOSC, designated as 1-Co, has been shown to possess unique binding properties for methylene blue (MB+), a common redox indicator and pollutant in water systems.

Despite the potential advantages of MOSCs, their integration into practical sensing devices has been challenging. The present invention addresses this challenge by providing a method for the fabrication of mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) incorporating 1-Co, which can be used to create highly sensitive and selective ISEs for the detection of MB+ and other molecular ions.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for the fabrication of mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) incorporating a metal-organic supercontainer (MOSC) designated as 1-Co for the potentiometric detection of molecular ions, particularly methylene blue (MB+). The invention includes the preparation of the MMM, the fabrication of both conventional and solid-contact ISEs, and the use of these ISEs for the detection of MB+ in various solutions, including simulated waste water.

The key features of the invention include:
1. The synthesis of 1-Co and its incorporation into a MMM.
2. The fabrication of conventional and solid-contact ISEs using the 1-Co MMM.
3. The demonstration of the ISEs' high selectivity and sensitivity towards MB+ and other molecular ions.
4. The stability and lack of leaching of 1-Co and MB+ in the MMM.
5. The applicability of the ISEs in practical settings, such as the detection of MB+ in simulated waste water.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Synthesis of 1-Co

The metal-organic supercontainer (MOSC) designated as 1-Co is synthesized following a published procedure. Briefly, 1-Co is obtained from the reaction of Co(II), p-tert-butylsulfonyl-calix[4]arene, and 1,4-benzenedicarboxylate. The resulting 1-Co molecule has an edge-directed octahedral geometry with an outer diameter of 3.3 nm, an inner diameter of 1.7 nm, and an internal volume of 1.2 nm³. It possesses a total of seven well-defined binding domains, including one endo-cavity and six exo-cavities. The 1-Co molecule has been shown to selectively bind to MB+ with a binding constant of 1.42 × 10⁴ M⁻¹.

### Preparation of Mixed-Matrix Membranes (MMMs)

The MMM is prepared by dissolving 330 mg of poly(vinylchloride) (PVC) and 722 μL of bis(2-ethylhexyl)sebacate (DOS) in approximately 5 mL of tetrahydrofuran (THF). Once the solution becomes homogeneous, 5.0 mg of potassium tetrakis(4-chlorophenyl)borate (KTpClPB) and 25.7 mg of 1-Co are added. The solution is mixed until it turns to a transparent light red color. Finally, 3.2 mg of MB+ is added, and the solution is mixed for an additional two hours, turning the solution to a dark blue color.

### Fabrication of Conventional ISEs

Conventional ISEs are fabricated by dipping a disposable pipette tip into the MMM solution, allowing the solution to fill the pipette tip by capillary force. The ISE is completed by inserting a silver/silver chloride (Ag/AgCl) pellet into the pipette body and filling the body with a saturated KCl/AgCl solution. The ISEs are conditioned in 10 μM MB+ overnight before use.

### Fabrication of Solid-Contact ISEs (SC-ISEs)

Solid-contact ISEs (SC-ISEs) are fabricated by spin-coating a 1.5 μm thick SU8 layer on a silicon dioxide (SiO₂)-covered silicon wafer. The SU8 film is pyrolyzed in a quartz tube flow-through furnace at 900°C in a reducing atmosphere of 95% N₂/5% H₂ for one hour. Polyethylene wells with inner diameters of approximately 6 mm are glued on top of the pyrolyzed SU8 using a quick-setting epoxy. The SC-ISE is finalized by drop-casting 40 μL of the MMM solution inside the fabricated well, on top of the pyrolyzed SU8. The electrodes are conditioned in 10 μM MB+ overnight before use.

### Impedance and Potentiometric Measurements

The impedance of the MMM is measured at open-circuit potential in the SC-ISE setup using a Bio-Logic VSP-300 potentiostat/galvanostat equipped with an impedance channel and a low-current option. A Ag/AgCl (saturated KCl) reference electrode and a platinum counter-electrode are used. The ISE potentials are measured in reference to the same reference electrode using a Burr-Brown INA116 instrumentation amplifier with an input impedance greater than 10¹⁵ Ω. The gain is set at 10, and the potential is digitized using a Keysight U2351A USB data acquisition device at 10 kS/s. The data is smoothed by averaging within a half-second window using National Instruments’ LabView programming environment. The ISE is rinsed thoroughly after each run to minimize hysteresis.

### Proof-Of-Concept Study with MB+ and TBA+

#### Example 1: Proof-Of-Concept Study with MB+ and TBA+

The 1-Co MMM ISEs were tested for their potentiometric response to methylene blue (MB+) and tetrabutylammonium (TBA+). The ISEs exhibited a near-Nernstian response to MB+ with a slope of 56.8 ± 2.5 mV/p[MB+] and a detection limit of approximately 300 nM. The response to TBA+ was also near-Nernstian with a slope of 60.0 ± 1.5 mV/p[TBA+] and a detection limit of approximately 1 μM. The response time for both MB+ and TBA+ was extremely short, on the order of 1 second for concentrations above 30 μM, with a slower response for lower concentrations due to slowed ion-exchange kinetics. The potential stability was excellent, with a drift of less than a few mV/h.

### Leeching Study

#### Example 2: Leeching Study

To evaluate the stability of the 1-Co MMM, a leaching test was performed. Two MMMs were prepared, one with 1-Co and one without, while keeping all other constituents the same. The MMMs were stored in deionized (DI) water for over one week. No change in the color of the water for the MOSC MMM was observed, indicating that MB+ was captured by 1-Co. However, the solution in contact with the MMM without 1-Co turned blue, indicating that MB+ had leached out. Both the SC-ISE with a MMM that did not contain 1-Co and the ISE without MMM altogether (i.e., plain carbon electrode) did not show a pronounced potentiometric response to MB+, confirming that the MMM ISE sensitivity resulted from the inclusion of the MOSC.

### Simulated Waste Water Sample

#### Example 3: Simulated Waste Water Sample

To demonstrate the practical applicability of the 1-Co MMM ISEs, a simulated waste water experiment was conducted using water collected from the Fyris River in Uppsala. Controlled amounts of MB+ were added to the river water, and the response curve was recorded. The response curve remained near-Nernstian, although an apparent shift in the detection limit was observed. This shift was attributed to a decrease in the oxygen content of the river water caused by microorganisms, which can reduce MB+ and cause a small shift in the response curve. Despite this reduction, the ISE still responded to the MB+ in the cationic form, demonstrating the potential of the 1-Co MMM ISEs for environmental monitoring.

### Molecular Ion Sensing

#### Example 4: Molecular Ion Sensing

The 1-Co MMM ISEs were further tested for their potentiometric response to a range of ions, including K⁺, Ag⁺, and NH₄⁺. The ISEs exhibited a substantially smaller response to these ions, with slopes of 10.4 ± 2.1 mV/p[K⁺], 5.0 ± 7.3 mV/p[Ag⁺], and −6.4 ± 1.4 mV/p[NH₄⁺], respectively. This indicates that the 1-Co MOSC is highly selective for larger molecular ions like MB+ and TBA+ due to the size and shape of its cavities, which do not effectively bind smaller ions.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel method for the fabrication of mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) incorporating a metal-organic supercontainer (MOSC) designated as 1-Co for the potentiometric detection of molecular ions, particularly methylene blue (MB+). The 1-Co MMM ISEs exhibit high selectivity and sensitivity towards MB+ and other molecular ions, making them suitable for various applications, including environmental monitoring, food quality control, and biomedical surveillance. The stability and lack of leaching of 1-Co and MB+ in the MMM, along with the practical applicability demonstrated in the simulated waste water experiment, highlight the potential of this technology for real-world use.
