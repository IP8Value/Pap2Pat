# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

The disclosed subject matter relates to the field of organ transplantation and, more specifically, to methods and systems for the recovery and regeneration of severely damaged lungs. The invention provides a novel approach to extend the viability and functionality of lungs injured by gastric aspiration, a condition that typically renders these organs unsuitable for transplantation. The disclosed methods and systems utilize an interventional cross-circulation platform to provide prolonged normothermic extracorporeal support and enable therapeutic interventions that facilitate the recovery and regeneration of severely damaged lungs.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

Organ transplantation has made significant strides in overcoming technical, immunological, and ethical hurdles, becoming a lifesaving treatment for many patients. However, the full potential of organ transplantation remains constrained by the global shortage of organs, particularly lungs. Up to 80% of donated lungs are not utilized due to injury at the time of death, such as trauma, pulmonary contusion, aspiration, and ventilator-associated lung injury. Efforts to expand the donor pool, including the use of ex vivo lung perfusion (EVLP), have shown promise in evaluating and improving marginal quality donor lungs. Despite these advancements, the annual number of lung transplants has not dramatically increased, highlighting the need for innovative solutions to extend organ recovery capabilities beyond marginal lungs to severely damaged lungs.

Gastric aspiration, a common cause of lung injury, results in damage to the pulmonary epithelium, leading to pneumonitis and respiratory insufficiency. Previous attempts to recover lungs injured by gastric aspiration using EVLP have been unsuccessful, often resulting in increased edema and failure to reduce levels of inflammatory cytokines or improve tissue morphology. The disclosed invention addresses this critical need by providing a method and system for the recovery and regeneration of severely damaged lungs using an interventional cross-circulation platform.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and system for the recovery and regeneration of severely damaged lungs, particularly those injured by gastric aspiration. The method involves the use of an interventional cross-circulation platform to provide prolonged normothermic extracorporeal support and enable therapeutic interventions that facilitate the recovery and regeneration of the injured lungs. The system includes a cross-circulation platform, therapeutic intervention modules, and non-invasive diagnostic tools.

1. Inducing lung injury in a donor animal by delivering standardized gastric contents into a single lung.
2. Explanting the injured lung and connecting it to the interventional cross-circulation platform.
3. Performing therapeutic interventions, including bronchoalveolar lavage, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment maneuvers.
4. Continuously monitoring the functional and molecular recovery of the injured lung using non-invasive diagnostic tools, such as surface thermography and exosome analysis.
5. Assessing the recovery and regeneration of the injured lung through histopathologic and functional analyses.

The system includes:
- A cross-circulation platform for providing normothermic extracorporeal support.
- Therapeutic intervention modules for performing bronchoalveolar lavage, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment maneuvers.
- Non-invasive diagnostic tools, such as a thermal imaging camera and exosome analysis equipment.
- A data acquisition and analysis system for monitoring and analyzing the recovery process.

The disclosed method and system enable the recovery and regeneration of severely damaged lungs, expanding the donor pool and potentially saving lives by making more organs available for transplantation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER

Organ transplantation has revolutionized the treatment of end-stage organ failure, but the availability of suitable organs remains a critical bottleneck. Up to 80% of donated lungs are not utilized due to various injuries, including those caused by gastric aspiration. Gastric aspiration results in severe damage to the pulmonary epithelium, leading to pneumonitis and respiratory insufficiency. Traditional methods, such as ex vivo lung perfusion (EVLP), have shown limited success in recovering lungs injured by gastric aspiration, often resulting in increased edema and failure to reduce inflammatory cytokines or improve tissue morphology.

### Detailed Description of the Method

#### Step 1: Induction of Lung Injury
1. **Animal Preparation**: Select a donor animal, such as a swine, and induce general anesthesia.
2. **Gastric Content Standardization**: Collect and standardize gastric contents from the donor animal to a pH of 2.00 ± 0.05.
3. **Induction of Injury**: Deliver the standardized gastric contents into a single lung of the anesthetized animal via bronchoscopy. Allow the injury to develop for 6 hours to mimic clinical scenarios.

#### Step 2: Explantation and Connection to Cross-Circulation Platform
1. **Explantation**: Perform a median sternotomy, heparinize the animal, and cannulate the main pulmonary artery. Flush the lungs with cold anterograde low-potassium dextran solution.
2. **Cannulation**: Cannulate the pulmonary artery and veins, and secure the endotracheal tube for ventilation.
3. **Connection to Cross-Circulation Platform**: Connect the explanted lungs to the cross-circulation platform, which provides normothermic extracorporeal support.

#### Step 3: Therapeutic Interventions
1. **Bronchoalveolar Lavage**: Perform bronchoalveolar lavage to clear gastric contents, secretions, and cellular debris from the injured lungs. Use sterile normal saline and aspirate the fluid.
2. **Surfactant Replacement**: Administer exogenous surfactant to replenish depleted and degraded endogenous surfactants and improve lung compliance.
3. **Alveolar Recruitment Maneuvers**: Perform alveolar recruitment maneuvers to transiently increase the airway positive pressure gradient, resulting in the opening of non-aerating lung units and increased vital capacity.

#### Step 4: Non-Invasive Diagnostic Monitoring
1. **Surface Thermography**: Use a thermal imaging camera to assess global and local changes in lung surface temperatures throughout the recovery process.
2. **Exosome Analysis**: Analyze exosome concentrations in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid to monitor the trajectory of lung injury and recovery.

#### Step 5: Assessment of Recovery and Regeneration
1. **Functional Assessment**: Monitor the functional recovery of the injured lungs using pressure-volume loops, dynamic compliance, and gas exchange parameters.
2. **Histopathologic Analysis**: Perform histopathologic and immunohistochemical analyses to assess the cellular and molecular recovery of the injured lungs.
3. **Non-Invasive Diagnostic Tests**: Conduct non-invasive diagnostic tests, such as the hypothermic probe test, to evaluate the recovery of lung function and structure.

### Detailed Description of the System

#### Cross-Circulation Platform
1. **Main Console**: A Jostra HL-20 pump console for controlling the flow and pressure of the extracorporeal circuit.
2. **Centrifugal Pump**: A Rotaflow centrifugal pump for maintaining blood flow through the lungs.
3. **Softshell Reservoir**: A softshell reservoir for collecting and storing blood during the cross-circulation process.
4. **Tubing**: Three-eighths inch Smart coated tubing for connecting the various components of the system.

#### Therapeutic Intervention Modules
1. **Bronchoscope**: A 3.8 mm flexible bronchoscope for performing bronchoalveolar lavage and surfactant delivery.
2. **Surfactant Delivery System**: A system for administering exogenous surfactant to the injured lungs.
3. **Ventilator**: A ventilator for performing alveolar recruitment maneuvers and maintaining lung ventilation.

#### Non-Invasive Diagnostic Tools
1. **Thermal Imaging Camera**: A FLIR T430sc infrared camera for capturing thermal images of the lungs.
2. **Exosome Analysis Equipment**: Equipment for analyzing exosome concentrations in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid.
3. **Data Acquisition and Analysis System**: A system for monitoring and analyzing the recovery process, including physiological parameters, pressure-volume loops, and exosome analysis data.

## EXAMPLES

### Methods

#### Animal Preparation and Induction of Lung Injury
1. **Animal Selection**: Sixteen miniature swine (eight donor-recipient pairs) from a partially inbred herd were used. The animals were 5-8 months old, with a median weight of 39.2 kg.
2. **Anesthesia**: General anesthesia was induced using Telazol and buprenorphine hydrochloride. The anesthetic regimen included continuous infusions of midazolam, fentanyl citrate, and inhaled isoflurane.
3. **Gastric Content Standardization**: Gastric contents were collected from six swine, admixed, and standardized to a pH of 2.00 ± 0.05. The concentration of total non-sulfated bile acids and the presence of pepsin were confirmed.
4. **Induction of Injury**: Standardized gastric contents (2 mL/kg; pH 2) were delivered bronchoscopically into a single lung of the anesthetized animal. The injury was allowed to develop for 6 hours.

#### Explantation and Connection to Cross-Circulation Platform
1. **Explantation**: A median sternotomy was performed, and the lungs were flushed with cold anterograde low-potassium dextran solution. The lungs were then explanted en-bloc.
2. **Cannulation**: The pulmonary artery and veins were cannulated, and the endotracheal tube was secured for ventilation.
3. **Connection to Cross-Circulation Platform**: The explanted lungs were connected to the cross-circulation platform, which provided normothermic extracorporeal support.

#### Therapeutic Interventions
1. **Bronchoalveolar Lavage**: Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed using sterile normal saline to clear gastric contents, secretions, and cellular debris.
2. **Surfactant Replacement**: Exogenous surfactant was administered to replenish depleted and degraded endogenous surfactants and improve lung compliance.
3. **Alveolar Recruitment Maneuvers**: Alveolar recruitment maneuvers were performed to transiently increase the airway positive pressure gradient, resulting in the opening of non-aerating lung units and increased vital capacity.

#### Non-Invasive Diagnostic Monitoring
1. **Surface Thermography**: Thermal images of the lungs were captured using a FLIR T430sc infrared camera to assess global and local changes in lung surface temperatures.
2. **Exosome Analysis**: Exosome concentrations in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid were analyzed to monitor the trajectory of lung injury and recovery.

#### Assessment of Recovery and Regeneration
1. **Functional Assessment**: The functional recovery of the injured lungs was monitored using pressure-volume loops, dynamic compliance, and gas exchange parameters.
2. **Histopathologic Analysis**: Histopathologic and immunohistochemical analyses were performed to assess the cellular and molecular recovery of the injured lungs.
3. **Non-Invasive Diagnostic Tests**: Non-invasive diagnostic tests, such as the hypothermic probe test, were conducted to evaluate the recovery of lung function and structure.

### Results

#### Functional Recovery
1. **Pressure-Volume Loops**: Significant improvements in pressure-volume loops were observed, with the injured lungs recovering normal hysteresis similar to that of control lungs.
2. **Dynamic Compliance**: Dynamic compliance of the injured lungs increased significantly, approaching the levels of control lungs.
3. **Gas Exchange**: The PaO2/FiO2 ratio of the injured lungs improved steadily, reaching levels comparable to those of control lungs.

#### Histopathologic and Molecular Recovery
1. **Histopathologic Analysis**: Histopathologic analyses showed a reduction in inflammation, edema, and cellular derangement, with the injured lungs exhibiting normal tissue histomorphology.
2. **Immunohistochemical Analysis**: Immunohistochemical analyses demonstrated the recovery of cell surface markers of the pulmonary epithelium and endothelium, as well as the regeneration of functional subcellular structures.

#### Non-Invasive Diagnostic Monitoring
1. **Surface Thermography**: Thermal images showed a reduction in heterogeneous and elevated surface temperatures, indicating improved lung function and structure.
2. **Exosome Analysis**: Exosome concentrations in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid decreased over time, approaching levels in control lungs, suggesting a reduction in lung injury and inflammation.
