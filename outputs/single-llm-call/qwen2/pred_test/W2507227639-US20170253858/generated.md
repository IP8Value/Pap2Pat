# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine, specifically to methods and compositions for promoting the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes (CMs). More particularly, the invention provides a method for enhancing the maturation of iPSC-derived CMs by culturing the cells in a three-dimensional (3D) extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from adult cardiac tissue.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in the United States and many developed countries. Myocardial infarction, a common manifestation of cardiovascular disease, results in the death of millions to billions of cardiomyocytes (CMs). The loss of CMs impairs heart function, and the limited regenerative capacity of endogenous CMs exacerbates the problem. Current treatments for myocardial infarction, such as reperfusion therapy and surgical interventions, do not address the fundamental issue of CM loss. Therefore, there is a critical need for novel therapeutic strategies to regenerate lost myocardium.

Recent advances in stem cell biology have focused on the potential of exogenous CMs, particularly those derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), to replace damaged heart tissue. iPSCs can be generated from a patient's own cells, reducing the risk of immune rejection. However, a significant challenge in this approach is the immaturity of iPSC-derived CMs. These cells often display characteristics of fetal CMs, including disorganized sarcomere structures, weak force contraction, spontaneous beating, improper calcium handling, and altered electrophysiological properties. Immature CMs are less effective for tissue replacement and drug screening, and they pose safety risks due to potential arrhythmias.

Several methods have been explored to promote the maturation of iPSC-derived CMs, including exposure to thyroid hormones, co-culture with non-CM cells, mechanical stress, microRNA overexpression, and long-term culture. However, these methods have limitations and do not consistently produce mature CMs. Recent studies have suggested that the extracellular matrix (ECM) plays a crucial role in cell and organ maturation. For instance, culturing CMs on porcine cardiac ECM has been shown to improve their development and maturation. However, the effects of different ECM sources and geometries on CM maturation remain poorly understood.

The present invention addresses these gaps by providing a method for enhancing the maturation of iPSC-derived CMs using a 3D adult cardiac ECM. This method not only improves the maturation of CMs but also enhances their functional properties, making them more suitable for therapeutic applications and drug screening.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a method for promoting the maturation of induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes (CMs) by culturing the cells in a three-dimensional (3D) extracellular matrix (ECM) derived from adult cardiac tissue. The method involves the following steps:

1. Isolating and decellularizing adult cardiac tissue to obtain a 3D adult cardiac ECM.
2. Preparing iPSC-derived CMs through a standardized differentiation protocol.
3. Seeding the iPSC-derived CMs into the 3D adult cardiac ECM.
4. Culturing the cells in the 3D ECM for a sufficient period to promote maturation.

The invention also encompasses the use of the 3D adult cardiac ECM for enhancing the expression of maturation-related genes and proteins in iPSC-derived CMs, improving their calcium handling, and increasing their responsiveness to pharmacological agents.

In one embodiment, the 3D adult cardiac ECM is prepared by decellularizing bovine adult cardiac tissue using a combination of detergents and enzymes. The decellularized ECM is then digested with pepsin to form a hydrogel. The iPSC-derived CMs are seeded into this hydrogel and cultured for at least 7 days to promote maturation.

In another embodiment, the method includes assessing the maturation of iPSC-derived CMs by evaluating the expression of maturation-related genes, such as Junctin (JCN), L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel (CaV1.2), and inward rectifier potassium ion channel (KCNJ2/Kir2.1), as well as the functional properties of the cells, including calcium handling and response to drugs.

The invention further provides a composition comprising iPSC-derived CMs cultured in a 3D adult cardiac ECM, which can be used for therapeutic applications, such as treating myocardial infarction, and for drug screening and toxicity testing.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Isolation and Decellularization of Adult Cardiac Tissue

The first step in the method of the present invention involves isolating and decellularizing adult cardiac tissue to obtain a 3D adult cardiac ECM. Bovine adult hearts are preferred for this purpose, although other mammalian sources, such as porcine or human, can also be used. The tissue is processed by removing excess fat and stored at -80°C for at least 16 hours. The tissue is then cut into small pieces (approximately 3 mm³) and subjected to a series of decellularization steps.

The decellularization process involves the following steps:
1. Washing the tissue with deionized water (ddH₂O) for 30 minutes.
2. Washing the tissue with 2× phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 15 minutes.
3. Treating the tissue with 1% sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) for 72 hours to remove cellular components.
4. Washing the tissue with 1% Triton X-100 for 90 minutes to further remove cellular debris.
5. Performing multiple washes with ddH₂O for 30 minutes each.
6. Washing the tissue with PBS containing antibiotic-antimycotic solution for 18 hours at 4°C to ensure sterility.

After decellularization, the tissue is lyophilized and milled to create a fine powder. The ECM powder is then digested with 2 mg/mL pepsin in 0.01 M cell culture grade hydrochloric acid (HCl) for 48-72 hours at room temperature. The digested ECM is divided into 1 mL aliquots and stored at -80°C until needed.

### Preparation of iPSC-Derived CMs

The next step involves preparing iPSC-derived CMs through a standardized differentiation protocol. The human WTC-11 iPSC line, which expresses the calcium flux reporter GCaMP6, is used in this embodiment. The iPSCs are maintained on Growth Factor-Reduced Matrigel and fed daily with mTeSR1 medium. To induce cardiac differentiation, the iPSCs are seeded as single cells into 12-well plates coated with Matrigel in the presence of 10 mM Y-27632. Cardiac induction is initiated 2-4 days later by adding glycogen synthase kinase 3 inhibitor (CHIR99021) in RPMI/B27 (without insulin). The medium is changed to fresh RPMI/B27 (without insulin) 24 hours later, and Wnt inhibitor (IWP2) is added on Day 3. The cells are fed fresh RPMI/B27 (with insulin) every 3 days starting at Day 7. By Day 12, the cells begin to spontaneously beat, indicating the successful differentiation into CMs.

### Seeding and Culturing iPSC-Derived CMs in 3D Adult Cardiac ECM

Once the iPSC-derived CMs are prepared, they are seeded into the 3D adult cardiac ECM hydrogel. The cells are suspended in the ECM hydrogel and allowed to gelate at 37°C. The cells are then cultured in the 3D ECM for at least 7 days to promote maturation. During this period, the cells are fed with RPMI/B27 (with insulin) every 3 days.

### Assessment of Maturation

The maturation of iPSC-derived CMs cultured in the 3D adult cardiac ECM is assessed by evaluating the expression of maturation-related genes and proteins, as well as the functional properties of the cells. Gene expression is analyzed using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) to measure the levels of maturation-related genes, such as Junctin (JCN), L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel (CaV1.2), inward rectifier potassium ion channel (KCNJ2/Kir2.1), Triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca²⁺ ATPase (SERCA2a), and potassium/sodium hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated channel 4 (HCN4).

Protein expression is evaluated using immunofluorescence and Western blotting to detect the presence and levels of key structural and calcium-handling proteins, such as cardiac troponin T (cTNT), CaV1.2, myosin light chain 2 (MYL2), and connexin 43 (Cx43).

Functional properties, including calcium handling and response to drugs, are assessed using the genetically encoded calcium flux reporter GCaMP6. Calcium transients are measured to evaluate the beat rate, calcium transient amplitude, maximum upslope, maximum downslope, and time to 50% and 75% decay. The cells are also exposed to pharmacological agents, such as isoproterenol and propranolol, to assess their responsiveness to β-adrenergic signaling, and to caffeine to evaluate the sensitivity of the ryanodine receptor (RyR).

### Examples

#### Example 1: Characterization of ECM from Decellularized Adult and Fetal Bovine Cardiac Tissue

To investigate the differences between adult and fetal cardiac ECMs, we isolated and decellularized bovine adult and fetal cardiac tissue using the methods described above. The protein compositions of the ECMs were characterized by nano-LC MS/MS. The results showed that both adult and fetal cardiac ECMs contained similar prominent proteins, including fibrinogen, collagen, periostin, fibrillin, fibulin, fibronectin, and other matrix proteins. However, the fetal cardiac ECM contained unique proteins, such as mimecan and versican, not found in the adult ECM.

The structure and distribution of collagen and elastin fibers within the ECM were examined using second-harmonic generation (SHG) microscopy and multiphoton microscopy. The elastin fibers of the fetal ECM were more evenly distributed, while the adult ECM displayed more defined organized bundles. The adult ECM also showed a higher collagen fiber signal intensity and larger bundles, indicating a higher density of collagen.

The mechanical properties of the ECMs were measured using parallel plate rheology. The adult cardiac ECM was approximately 10-fold stiffer than the fetal cardiac ECM, with a storage modulus (G') of 67.5 ± 12.6 Pa compared to 7.2 ± 3.2 Pa for the fetal ECM.

#### Example 2: 3D Adult Cardiac ECM Promotes Expression of CM Maturation Genes

To determine the effect of 3D adult cardiac ECM on iPSC-derived CM maturation, we cultured the cells in 2D and 3D fetal and adult cardiac ECMs for 7 days. Gene expression was analyzed using qRT-PCR. The results showed that iPSC-derived CMs cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM expressed higher levels of maturation-related genes, including Junctin (JCN), L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel (CaV1.2), inward rectifier potassium ion channel (KCNJ2/Kir2.1), Triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca²⁺ ATPase (SERCA2a), and potassium/sodium hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated channel 4 (HCN4), compared to cells cultured in 2D gels.

Protein expression was also evaluated using immunofluorescence and Western blotting. The results confirmed the increased expression of CaV1.2, MYL2, and connexin 43 (Cx43) in iPSC-derived CMs cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM compared to 2D.

#### Example 3: Cardiac ECM Geometry Affects iPSC-Derived CM Calcium Signaling

To assess the functional effects of ECM geometry on iPSC-derived CM calcium handling, we used the genetically encoded calcium flux reporter GCaMP6. Single cells were cultured in 2D and 3D gels for 7 days, and calcium transients were measured. The results showed that iPSC-derived CMs cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM had a significant decrease in beat rate, calcium transient amplitude, maximum upslope, and maximum downslope, and an increase in time to 50% decay compared to cells cultured in 2D.

When the cells were cultured as aggregates in 3D gels, the calcium-handling capabilities were different. The calcium transient amplitude, maximum upslope, and maximum downslope were all increased, and the time to peak, time to 50% decay, and time to 75% decay were decreased in 3D compared to 2D.

#### Example 4: 3D Adult Cardiac ECM Increases iPSC-Derived CM Response to Drugs

To further assess the functionality of iPSC-derived CMs in 3D adult cardiac ECM, the cells were exposed to isoproterenol and propranolol, known to modulate β-adrenergic signaling. The results showed that iPSC-derived CMs cultured in 3D adult cardiac ECM increased their beat rate upon the addition of 1 mM isoproterenol and returned to baseline levels following the addition of 10 mM propranolol.

Exposure to caffeine, which targets the ryanodine receptor (RyR), resulted in a more rapid release of calcium in 3D compared to 2D. The upslope was steeper, and the time to maximum calcium concentration was quicker in 3D.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for promoting the maturation of iPSC-derived CMs by culturing the cells in a 3D adult cardiac ECM. This method enhances the expression of maturation-related genes and proteins, improves calcium handling, and increases the responsiveness of the cells to pharmacological agents. The use of 3D adult cardiac ECM better recapitulates the in vivo environment, making the iPSC-derived CMs more suitable for therapeutic applications and drug screening. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of regenerative medicine and has the potential to revolutionize the treatment of myocardial infarction and other cardiovascular diseases.
