# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a device and method for repositioning an endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) catheter from the mouth to the nostril during endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP). More specifically, the invention provides a novel device, referred to as the "J-Loop," which simplifies and expedites the repositioning process, thereby reducing procedural time, enhancing safety, and improving patient comfort.

## BACKGROUND ART

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) is a critical procedure for diagnosing and treating biliary and pancreatic disorders. One common application of ERCP is the placement of an endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) catheter, which is essential for managing conditions such as cholangitis and biliary obstruction. The ENBD catheter facilitates the drainage of bile and allows for the collection of bile samples for cytological analysis or bacteriological culture.

Traditionally, the repositioning of the ENBD catheter from the mouth to the nostril involves a series of intricate steps. These steps typically include withdrawing the catheter from the mouth, removing the mouthpiece, inserting a nasal plastic tube, grasping the tip of the tube in the oral cavity, and finally pulling the tube and catheter toward the nose. This conventional technique has several drawbacks. For instance, it requires the patient to be awake and cooperative, which can be challenging in sedated patients. Additionally, the process is time-consuming and poses risks of injury to both the patient and the operator, such as oral cavity damage, tooth breakage, and finger injuries.

Several modified techniques have been proposed to address these issues, but they often require additional fluoroscopic or endoscopic observations, which can be cumbersome and prolong the procedure. Moreover, poor visualization of the oropharynx, particularly in patients with a high Mallampati score, can hinder the repositioning process.

Therefore, there is a need for a more efficient, safe, and user-friendly method for repositioning the ENBD catheter. The present invention addresses these needs by providing a novel device and method that significantly streamline the repositioning process.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inefficiency and safety concerns associated with the conventional methods for repositioning an ENBD catheter from the mouth to the nostril. Specifically, the conventional techniques are time-consuming, require the patient to be awake and cooperative, and pose risks of injury to both the patient and the operator. Additionally, these techniques often necessitate additional fluoroscopic or endoscopic observations, which can complicate the procedure.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a novel device and method for repositioning an ENBD catheter from the mouth to the nostril. The device, referred to as the "J-Loop," consists of a curved loop head and a bar-shaped handle. The method involves the following steps:

1. Withdrawing the ENBD catheter from the mouth after the ERCP procedure.
2. Inserting the J-Loop into the oral cavity.
3. Inserting a plastic tube into the nasal cavity up to specific markings that indicate the appropriate depth.
4. Catching the plastic tube in the loop of the J-Loop.
5. Pulling the J-Loop out of the oral cavity, thereby dragging the plastic tube along with it.
6. Connecting the plastic tube to the ENBD catheter and pulling the assembly toward the nose.

The J-Loop device is designed to simplify the repositioning process by eliminating the need for the patient to be awake and cooperative, reducing the risk of injury to both the patient and the operator, and minimizing the procedural time. The plastic tube includes markings that ensure proper insertion depth, further enhancing the safety and accuracy of the procedure.

### Advantageous Effects

The advantageous effects of the present invention are numerous and significant. Firstly, the use of the J-Loop device significantly reduces the time required for repositioning the ENBD catheter. Clinical trials have demonstrated that the new technique using the J-Loop reduces the repositioning time from an average of 194 seconds with the conventional method to approximately 44 seconds, representing a reduction of about 77%.

Secondly, the J-Loop method enhances patient safety by eliminating the need for the operator to touch the patient's oral cavity, thus reducing the risk of mucosal scratches, tooth breakage, and other injuries. Additionally, the procedure can be performed while the patient is still sedated, ensuring that the patient remains comfortable and does not experience any discomfort during the repositioning process.

Thirdly, the J-Loop method improves the safety of the operator by reducing the risk of finger injuries, which are common in the conventional technique. The operator can perform the repositioning without the need to grasp the tip of the plastic tube in the oral cavity, thereby avoiding potential trauma.

Lastly, the J-Loop method is effective across a range of patient anatomies, including those with a high Mallampati score. Unlike the conventional technique, which becomes more challenging in patients with poor visualization of the oropharynx, the J-Loop method maintains its efficiency and effectiveness regardless of the patient's anatomical characteristics.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention is embodied in a novel device and method for repositioning an ENBD catheter from the mouth to the nostril. The device, known as the J-Loop, comprises a curved loop head and a bar-shaped handle. The plastic tube used in conjunction with the J-Loop is marked with specific indicators to ensure proper insertion depth.

### Device Description

The J-Loop device is constructed from a biocompatible material that is flexible yet strong enough to maintain its shape during use. The curved loop head is designed to fit comfortably within the oral cavity and is sized to effectively catch the plastic tube. The bar-shaped handle provides a secure grip for the operator, allowing for precise control during the repositioning process.

The plastic tube is made from a flexible, biocompatible material and is marked with specific indicators to ensure proper insertion depth. These markings are clearly visible and are spaced at intervals that correspond to the typical insertion depths required for different patient anatomies.

### Method Description

The method for repositioning the ENBD catheter using the J-Loop device involves the following steps:

1. **Withdrawal of the ENBD Catheter**: After the completion of the ERCP procedure, the ENBD catheter is withdrawn from the mouth. The scope is removed, and the catheter is left in the oral cavity.

2. **Insertion of the J-Loop**: The J-Loop device is inserted into the oral cavity. The operator ensures that the curved loop head is positioned correctly to catch the plastic tube.

3. **Insertion of the Plastic Tube**: A plastic tube is inserted into the nasal cavity up to the appropriate marking. The markings on the plastic tube provide a visual guide to ensure that the tube is inserted to the correct depth.

4. **Catching the Plastic Tube**: The J-Loop is used to catch the plastic tube in the oral cavity. The operator gently maneuvers the J-Loop to ensure that the plastic tube is securely caught in the loop.

5. **Pulling the J-Loop**: The J-Loop is then pulled out of the oral cavity, dragging the plastic tube along with it. The operator ensures that the plastic tube is pulled smoothly and without excessive force.

6. **Connecting the Plastic Tube to the ENBD Catheter**: Once the plastic tube is pulled out, it is connected to the ENBD catheter. The operator then pulls the assembly toward the nose, completing the repositioning process.

### Clinical Trials and Outcomes

To validate the effectiveness of the J-Loop device and method, a comparative observational study was conducted. The study involved 145 patients who underwent ERCP at a tertiary medical center in Seoul, Korea, between January 2015 and December 2017. Patients were divided into two groups: the conventional technique group and the J-Loop technique group.

The results of the study demonstrated that the J-Loop technique significantly reduced the time required for ENBD catheter repositioning. The average repositioning time for the conventional technique was 194 seconds, while the J-Loop technique reduced this time to 44 seconds. The technical success rate for the J-Loop technique was 97.3%, with no significant complications reported.

Furthermore, the J-Loop technique was effective across a range of patient anatomies, including those with a high Mallampati score. The repositioning time using the J-Loop did not vary significantly based on the Mallampati score, indicating that the method is robust and reliable.

The J-Loop device simplifies the repositioning process, reduces procedural time, enhances patient and operator safety, and is effective across a range of patient anatomies. The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of endoscopic procedures and offers a practical solution to the challenges associated with the conventional techniques.
