# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel small molecule, specifically a tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid (SEGA (3a)), designed to protect gastric mucosa against nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced mitochondrial pathology, apoptosis, and gastropathy. The invention encompasses the synthesis, characterization, and biological evaluation of SEGA (3a), demonstrating its effectiveness in scavenging intramitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) and free iron, thereby preventing mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) and subsequent gastric mucosal injury.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are widely used for their analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory properties. However, their use is often associated with serious gastrointestinal side effects, including gastric mucosal injury or gastropathy. The primary mechanism underlying NSAID-induced gastropathy involves the generation of mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS), leading to the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and free iron. These ROS and free iron cause significant damage to mitochondrial components, disrupting mitochondrial function and ultimately leading to cell death via apoptosis.

Current treatments for NSAID-induced gastropathy, such as proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and histamine H2 receptor antagonists, primarily focus on reducing gastric acid secretion. While effective, these treatments have limitations and potential side effects, including diarrhea, linear mucosal defects, and increased risk of bacterial infections. Therefore, there is a pressing need for novel therapeutic strategies that directly address the underlying mechanisms of NSAID-induced gastropathy.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objectives of the present invention are to provide a novel small molecule, SEGA (3a), that:
1. Scavenges intramitochondrial ROS and free iron.
2. Prevents mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS).
3. Protects gastric mucosa against NSAID-induced injury.
4. Accelerates the healing of already damaged gastric mucosa.
5. Offers a safer and more effective alternative to existing treatments for NSAID-induced gastropathy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel small molecule, tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid (SEGA (3a)), which effectively protects gastric mucosa against NSAID-induced mitochondrial pathology, apoptosis, and gastropathy. SEGA (3a) is synthesized by conjugating 5-hydroxytryptamine (5HT) with gallic acid (GA) through an amide linkage. This hybrid molecule exhibits potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties, enabling it to scavenge intramitochondrial ROS and free iron, thereby preventing MOS and subsequent gastric mucosal injury.

Key features of SEGA (3a) include:
1. **Antioxidant and Iron-Chelating Properties**: SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior antioxidant and iron-chelating activities compared to other tryptamine-antioxidant conjugates and standard antioxidants.
2. **Gastroprotective Efficacy**: SEGA (3a) effectively protects against NSAID-induced gastric mucosal injury, as evidenced by a significant reduction in the gastric injury index and restoration of normal gastric mucosal architecture.
3. **Mitochondrial Protection**: SEGA (3a) prevents MOS by scavenging intramitochondrial ROS and free iron, maintaining mitochondrial function and integrity.
4. **Antiapoptotic Effects**: SEGA (3a) inhibits the activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, key markers of the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis, and modulates the expression of proapoptotic and antiapoptotic genes (bax and bcl-2).
5. **Accelerated Healing**: SEGA (3a) accelerates the healing of already damaged gastric mucosa, promoting the rapid restoration of normal mucosal architecture.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Biological Effects

#### Synthesis of Tryptamine-Gallic Acid Hybrid Molecule

The synthesis of SEGA (3a) involves the conjugation of 5-hydroxytryptamine (5HT) with gallic acid (GA) through an amide linkage. The general synthetic procedure is as follows:
1. **EDC Coupling Procedure for Formation of Amide**: To a solution of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (1 eq, 12 mmol), 5HT (1.2 eq, 14.4 mmol), 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (1 eq, 12 mmol), and triethylamine (6 eq, 72 mmol) in N,N-dimethylformamide, EDC hydrochloride (1.2 eq, 14.4 mmol) is added at 0°C. The reaction mixture is stirred at room temperature overnight. The mixture is then quenched with ice-cold water and extracted with ethyl acetate. The combined organic phase is washed with brine and dried over sodium sulfate. The organic phase is reduced in vacuo, and the concentrated ethyl acetate extracts are chromatographed over a silica gel column to obtain the desired product.
2. **Debenzylation (Hydrogenolysis)**: The benzyl-protected intermediate is hydrogenated using 10% palladium on carbon in a methanol/chloroform mixture (5:1) at 40 psi for 2 hours. The catalyst is removed by filtration, and the filtrate is evaporated in vacuo to obtain SEGA (3a).

#### In Vitro Antioxidant and Iron-Chelating Properties

1. **Ferric Reducing Antioxidant Power (FRAP) Assay**: SEGA (3a) shows a high reducing ability, as indicated by its FRAP value, which is significantly higher than that of other tryptamine-antioxidant conjugates.
2. **DPPH Radical-Scavenging Assay**: SEGA (3a) exhibits strong DPPH radical-scavenging activity, demonstrating its potent antioxidant properties.
3. **Iron-Chelating Activity**: SEGA (3a) effectively chelates free iron in vitro, as evidenced by the absence of the characteristic Fe(II)-TPTZ complex formation.

#### In Vivo Gastroprotective Effects

1. **Protection Against NSAID-Induced Gastric Mucosal Damage**: SEGA (3a) significantly reduces the gastric injury index in rats treated with indomethacin, a representative NSAID. Histological analysis reveals the restoration of normal gastric mucosal architecture.
2. **Mitochondrial Protection**: SEGA (3a) prevents the accumulation of intramitochondrial ROS and free iron, maintaining mitochondrial function and integrity. This is evidenced by the preservation of mitochondrial respiratory function, transmembrane potential (ΔΨm), and dehydrogenase activity.
3. **Antiapoptotic Effects**: SEGA (3a) inhibits the activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, key markers of the mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis. It also modulates the expression of proapoptotic (bax) and antiapoptotic (bcl-2) genes, further confirming its antiapoptotic properties.
4. **Accelerated Healing**: SEGA (3a) accelerates the healing of already damaged gastric mucosa, as demonstrated by a rapid reduction in the gastric injury index and restoration of normal mucosal architecture over time.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of SEGA (3a)

To a solution of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (1 eq, 12 mmol) and 5-hydroxytryptamine (1.2 eq, 14.4 mmol) in N,N-dimethylformamide, 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (1 eq, 12 mmol) and triethylamine (6 eq, 72 mmol) were added. EDC hydrochloride (1.2 eq, 14.4 mmol) was then added at 0°C, and the reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight. The mixture was quenched with ice-cold water and extracted with ethyl acetate. The combined organic phase was washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, and reduced in vacuo. The concentrated ethyl acetate extracts were chromatographed over a silica gel column to obtain the benzyl-protected intermediate. The intermediate was hydrogenated using 10% palladium on carbon in a methanol/chloroform mixture (5:1) at 40 psi for 2 hours. The catalyst was removed by filtration, and the filtrate was evaporated in vacuo to obtain SEGA (3a).

### Example 2: In Vitro Antioxidant Activity

The antioxidant activity of SEGA (3a) was evaluated using the FRAP and DPPH assays. In the FRAP assay, the absorbance change at 595 nm was measured, and the FRAP value was calculated. In the DPPH assay, the decrease in absorbance at 517 nm was measured. SEGA (3a) showed a high FRAP value and strong DPPH radical-scavenging activity, indicating its potent antioxidant properties.

### Example 3: In Vitro Iron-Chelating Activity

The iron-chelating activity of SEGA (3a) was assessed using the TPTZ assay. Fe(II) solution in the presence of TPTZ gave a broad peak at 595 nm. When SEGA (3a) was added to the Fe(II) solution, no such broad peak was observed, indicating that SEGA (3a) effectively chelates free iron.

### Example 4: In Vivo Gastroprotective Effects

Sprague-Dawley rats (180-220 g) were fasted for 24 hours and then treated with indomethacin (48 mg/kg b.w.) to induce gastric mucosal injury. Some rats were pretreated with SEGA (3a) (50, 20, 10, 5, 3, and 1 mg/kg b.w.) 30 minutes before indomethacin treatment. After 4 hours, the rats were sacrificed, and the severity of mucosal lesions was scored. SEGA (3a) significantly reduced the gastric injury index in a dose-dependent manner, with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg b.w.

### Example 5: Mitochondrial Protection

Mitochondria were isolated from the stomach tissue of control, indomethacin-treated, and SEGA (3a)-pretreated indomethacin-treated rats. Mitochondrial ROS and free iron were measured using MitoSOX and Phen Green SK, respectively. SEGA (3a) significantly reduced intramitochondrial ROS and free iron levels, preserving mitochondrial function and integrity.

### Example 6: Antiapoptotic Effects

The activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3 was measured in the stomach tissue of control, indomethacin-treated, and SEGA (3a)-pretreated indomethacin-treated rats using commercially available kits. SEGA (3a) significantly inhibited the activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, indicating its antiapoptotic properties. RT-PCR analysis showed that SEGA (3a) modulated the expression of proapoptotic (bax) and antiapoptotic (bcl-2) genes.

### Example 7: Accelerated Healing

Gastric mucosal injury was induced in rats with indomethacin (48 mg/kg b.w.). Some rats were treated with SEGA (3a) (50 mg/kg b.w.) 4 hours after injury induction. The rats were sacrificed at 2, 4, 8, and 24 hours, and the severity of mucosal lesions was scored. SEGA (3a) significantly accelerated the healing of already damaged gastric mucosa, as evidenced by a rapid reduction in the gastric injury index and restoration of normal mucosal architecture.

### Example 8: Quantitation of SEGA (3a) Entering Mitochondria

SEGA (3a) was radiolabeled with 99mTc and administered to rats. The radioactivity of 99mTc-chelate SEGA (3a) in isolated mitochondria was measured using a γ-scintillation counter. The data indicated that 0.05% of the administered dose of SEGA (3a) entered per mg of mitochondria of gastric mucosal tissue.

### Example 9: Stability Studies

The stability of 99mTc-labeled SEGA (3a) was determined in vitro using 0.9% sodium chloride and serum (from rat) by ascending TLC. The labeled complex was mixed with normal saline or rat serum and incubated at 37°C. The samples were withdrawn at regular intervals up to 24 hours and monitored by TLC. SEGA (3a) was found to be very stable in both physiological saline and serum.
