# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD AND BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of the present invention relates to methods and compositions for redifferentiating expanded human β-cells. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of HES1 inhibition to induce the redifferentiation of expanded human β-cells, thereby restoring their insulin-producing capabilities. This technology has significant implications for the treatment of diabetes, particularly in addressing the shortage of donor β-cells for cell replacement therapy.

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. Type 1 diabetes (T1D) is an autoimmune disease where the immune system destroys the insulin-producing β-cells in the pancreas, leading to a complete lack of insulin. Type 2 diabetes (T2D) is characterized by insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency. Current treatments for diabetes include insulin therapy, oral hypoglycemic agents, and lifestyle modifications. However, these treatments do not cure the disease and often fail to prevent long-term complications.

Cell replacement therapy, involving the transplantation of functional β-cells, offers a promising approach to treating diabetes. However, the limited availability of donor β-cells is a major bottleneck. Expanding β-cells in vitro and subsequently redifferentiating them to restore their insulin-producing capabilities could provide a solution to this problem. The NOTCH signaling pathway, particularly the HES1 gene, plays a crucial role in β-cell proliferation and differentiation. Inhibiting HES1 has been shown to promote the redifferentiation of expanded β-cells, making this an attractive target for therapeutic intervention.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for redifferentiating expanded human β-cells using HES1 inhibition. The invention is based on the discovery that inhibiting HES1 in expanded human islet cells, which have undergone dedifferentiation and lost their insulin-producing capabilities, can induce their redifferentiation and restore insulin production.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for redifferentiating expanded human β-cells, comprising the steps of:
1. Providing expanded human islet cells that have undergone dedifferentiation and lost insulin production.
2. Inhibiting HES1 in the expanded human islet cells.
3. Culturing the cells under conditions that promote redifferentiation, such as in serum-free medium (SFM) or redifferentiation medium (RM) containing soluble factors like nicotinamide, high glucose, activin A, and exendin-4.

In another aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising expanded human islet cells that have been treated with HES1 inhibition and are capable of redifferentiating into insulin-producing cells.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating diabetes, comprising the steps of:
1. Redifferentiating expanded human β-cells using the method described above.
2. Transplanting the redifferentiated cells into a subject in need thereof.

The invention also encompasses the use of HES1 inhibition in combination with other redifferentiation treatments, such as serum withdrawal and the addition of soluble factors, to enhance the redifferentiation efficiency of expanded human β-cells.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Examples

#### Example 1: Effect of HES1 Inhibition on Redifferentiation of Cultured Human Islet Cells

**Objective:** To determine the effect of HES1 inhibition on the redifferentiation of cultured human islet cells that have undergone dedifferentiation.

**Methods:**
1. **Cell Culture and Expansion:** Human islet cells were isolated and cultured in CMRL 1066 medium containing 5.6 mM glucose and supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), penicillin, streptomycin, gentamycin, and amphotericin B. The cells were expanded for 5-7 weeks (5-7 passages).
2. **HES1 Inhibition:** The expanded cells were infected with HES1 shRNA lentiviruses or nontarget shRNA lentiviruses and selected for puromycin resistance.
3. **Analysis:** Nine days post-infection, the cells were analyzed for HES1 and p57 protein levels, formation of cell clusters, nucleus size, and gene expression of insulin (INS), PDX1, NEUROD1, and other β-cell-specific genes.

**Results:**
- HES1 protein levels decreased by 40%, and p57 protein levels increased by 50% in the HES1 shRNA group.
- Cell clusters formed, and nucleus size was significantly reduced in the HES1 shRNA group.
- INS, PDX1, and NEUROD1 transcripts were significantly upregulated in the HES1 shRNA group.
- Transcripts for islet hormones GCG and SST were also upregulated, indicating the potential for differentiation into other islet cell types.

#### Example 2: Redifferentiation Effect of HES1 Inhibition in BCD Cells

**Objective:** To determine the redifferentiation effect of HES1 inhibition specifically in β-cell-derived (BCD) cells using a lineage tracing system.

**Methods:**
1. **Lineage Tracing:** Primary islet cells were infected with a mixture of two lentivirus vectors: one expressing a Cre recombinase-estrogen receptor fusion protein under the insulin promoter (RIP-Cre/ER) and the other a reporter cassette with an eGFP gene.
2. **HES1 Inhibition:** Labeled human islet cells at passage 4 were infected with HES1 shRNA or nontarget viruses.
3. **Analysis:** Nine days post-infection, the cells were co-stained for C-peptide and eGFP to identify redifferentiated cells.

**Results:**
- C-peptide staining was observed only in rare, mostly eGFP-positive, cells in the HES1 shRNA group.
- eGFP-positive cells in the HES1 shRNA group showed reduced nucleus size, confirming the redifferentiation of BCD cells.

#### Example 3: p57 Overexpression Insufficient for Redifferentiation of Expanded Islet Cells

**Objective:** To determine whether the redifferentiation effect of HES1 inhibition is mediated exclusively through the up-regulation of p57.

**Methods:**
1. **p57 Overexpression:** Human islet cells at passage 5 were infected with HES1 shRNA lentivirus, nontarget lentivirus, or CDKN1C adenovirus encoding p57.
2. **Analysis:** Seven days post-infection, the cells were analyzed for CDKN1C and INS transcript levels.

**Results:**
- CDKN1C transcript levels were up-regulated 4-fold following HES1 inhibition and 12,197-fold following CDKN1C adenovirus infection.
- Despite the much higher CDKN1C expression in the adenovirus-infected group, no elevation in INS transcripts was observed, suggesting that the redifferentiation effect of HES1 inhibition is not mediated exclusively through p57 up-regulation.

#### Example 4: HES1 Inhibition Enhanced BCD Cell Redifferentiation Induced by SFM

**Objective:** To evaluate the synergistic redifferentiation effect of HES1 shRNA and previously published redifferentiation treatments.

**Methods:**
1. **Cell Sorting and Expansion:** Labeled human islet cells were sorted into eGFP-positive (BCD cells) and eGFP-negative cells at passage 2 and further expanded in culture.
2. **HES1 Inhibition and SFM Treatment:** Dedifferentiated cells were infected with HES1 shRNA or nontarget virus and cultured in SFM for an additional 4-day period.
3. **Analysis:** The cells were analyzed for morphological changes, nucleus size, and gene expression of INS, PDX1, and other islet hormones.

**Results:**
- eGFP-positive cells aggregated more readily in SFM and showed more pronounced morphological changes in response to HES1 shRNA and SFM.
- INS transcripts were up-regulated 2.3-fold in eGFP-positive cells following HES1 inhibition and SFM treatment, compared to 1.5-fold with HES1 inhibition alone and 1.2-fold with SFM alone.

#### Example 5: HES1 Inhibition Enhanced BCD Cell Redifferentiation Induced by Soluble Factors

**Objective:** To combine HES1 inhibition with soluble factors that contribute to β-cell differentiation in vitro.

**Methods:**
1. **Redifferentiation Medium (RM):** SFM containing nicotinamide, high glucose, activin A, and exendin-4.
2. **HES1 Inhibition and RM Treatment:** Sorted eGFP-positive cells at passages 5 and 6 were infected with HES1 shRNA or nontarget shRNA viruses and cultured in RM for an additional 4-day period.
3. **Analysis:** The cells were analyzed for gene expression of INS, PDX1, and other islet hormones.

**Results:**
- RM together with nontarget shRNA virus increased INS transcript levels 23.5-fold, while the combined treatment of RM and HES1 shRNA led to a 144.2-fold increase.
- PDX1 transcripts were 5.5-fold higher in eGFP-positive cells treated with the combined treatment compared to RM alone.
- Transcripts for islet hormones GCG, SST, and PPY were also up-regulated following the combined treatment.

#### Example 6: Phenotype of Redifferentiated Cells following HES1 shRNA and RM Treatment

**Objective:** To characterize the phenotype of redifferentiated cells following HES1 shRNA and RM treatment.

**Methods:**
1. **Co-Staining:** Unlabeled expanded islet cells were co-stained for C-peptide, GCG, SST, and PPY.
2. **Transcription Factor Analysis:** The cells were co-stained for C-peptide and the transcription factors PDX1 and NKX2.2.
3. **Apoptosis Analysis:** A TUNEL assay was performed to evaluate cell mortality rates.

**Results:**
- 19.9% of the cells in the HES1 shRNA/RM group were positive for C-peptide, compared to 7.7% in the nontarget/RM group.
- All C-peptide-positive cells expressed PDX1 and NKX2.2 in their nucleus.
- The combined treatment of HES1 shRNA and RM increased apoptosis rates by 5% compared to the nontarget/RM group.

#### Example 7: Expanded Islet Cells Redifferentiated by HES1 Inhibition Corrected Hyperglycemia in Immunodeficient Mice

**Objective:** To evaluate the in vivo functionality of redifferentiated expanded islet cells.

**Methods:**
1. **Transplantation:** Human islet cells at passage 5 were infected with HES1 shRNA or nontarget viruses and transplanted under the kidney capsule of streptozotocin-treated hyperglycemic NOD-SCID mice.
2. **Blood Glucose and C-Peptide Analysis:** Blood glucose levels and human C-peptide in serum were measured over 56 days.
3. **Histological and Molecular Analysis:** The transplants were recovered for RNA and immunohistochemical analyses.

**Results:**
- Three of six experimental mice survived and showed decreased blood glucose levels and human C-peptide in their serum.
- qPCR analyses revealed a 433-fold increase in human INS transcripts in the transplanted cells.
- Immunohistochemical analyses confirmed the presence of insulin, C-peptide, and IAPP in the transplanted cells, demonstrating their functional capability.

These examples demonstrate the effectiveness of HES1 inhibition in redifferentiating expanded human β-cells and restoring their insulin-producing capabilities, both in vitro and in vivo. The invention provides a promising approach for overcoming the donor β-cell shortage and advancing cell replacement therapy for diabetes.
