# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and more specifically to methods and compositions for reducing corneal neovascularization (CNV) and associated inflammation. The invention involves the use of specific lipid mediators, including aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolving D1 (RvD1), and resolving E1 (RvE1), to inhibit angiogenesis and inflammation in the cornea.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Corneal neovascularization (CNV) is a pathological process characterized by the formation of new blood vessels in the normally avascular cornea. This condition can lead to visual impairment and is often associated with various ocular diseases and injuries, such as corneal ulcers, chemical burns, and surgical procedures. The development of CNV is driven by inflammatory processes and the upregulation of pro-angiogenic factors, particularly vascular endothelial growth factors (VEGFs).

Traditional treatments for CNV, such as corticosteroids and anti-VEGF agents, have limitations, including side effects and incomplete efficacy. There is a need for novel therapeutic approaches that can effectively reduce both inflammation and angiogenesis in the cornea.

Lipid mediators, such as lipoxins and resolvins, are endogenous molecules derived from essential polyunsaturated fatty acids. These mediators play a crucial role in the resolution of inflammation and have been shown to possess anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving properties. Recent studies have demonstrated that specific lipid mediators, including ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1, can modulate angiogenesis and inflammation in various tissues.

However, the specific effects of these lipid mediators on corneal neovascularization and the underlying mechanisms have not been fully elucidated. The present invention addresses this gap by providing methods and compositions for using ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 to reduce CNV and associated inflammation in the cornea.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for reducing corneal neovascularization (CNV) and associated inflammation using specific lipid mediators. The lipid mediators include aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolving D1 (RvD1), and resolving E1 (RvE1). These mediators are effective in reducing the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages, downregulating the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and inhibiting the expression of pro-angiogenic factors such as VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for reducing CNV in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1. The administration can be subconjunctival, topical, or via other suitable routes. The method is effective in reducing the number of infiltrating neutrophils and macrophages, decreasing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1α, and IL-1β, and inhibiting the expression of pro-angiogenic factors such as VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition can be formulated for subconjunctival, topical, or other suitable routes of administration.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating or preventing corneal inflammation and associated CNV in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1. The method is effective in reducing the severity of corneal inflammation and the development of CNV.

The invention also encompasses the use of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 in the preparation of a medicament for reducing CNV and associated inflammation in the cornea.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Results

The present invention is based on the discovery that specific lipid mediators, including ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1, are effective in reducing corneal neovascularization (CNV) and associated inflammation. The results of a series of experiments conducted to investigate the effects of these lipid mediators on CNV and inflammation in a murine model are summarized below.

#### LipoxinA4 and Resolvin E1 Receptor Expression in the Cornea

The expression of receptors for ATLa and RvE1 in corneal tissue was determined by RT-PCR. In normal corneas, Fpr-rs2 (one of the murine LXA4 receptors) and ChemR23 (the RvE1 receptor) were present in the epithelial and stromal-endothelial layers. The receptor for RvD1 has not been identified to date. Further investigation using an immortalized corneal keratocyte cell line (MK/T-1) and infiltrated CD11b+ cells (macrophages and dendritic cells) revealed that both Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 were expressed by these cells, irrespective of cytokine stimulation.

#### Reduction of Neutrophil and Macrophage Infiltration by ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1

Inflamed corneas were treated with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 to assess the action of these lipid mediators on the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages. The results showed that treatment with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 led to a significant reduction in the number of infiltrating neutrophils and macrophages compared to vehicle-treated controls. This reduction was observed at both 24 hours and 72 hours after the induction of corneal inflammation.

#### Reduction of Inflammatory Cytokine Expression by ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1

The mRNA levels of inflammatory cytokines IL-1α, IL-1β, and TNF-α were monitored by real-time PCR at 24 hours and 72 hours after suture placement. Treatment with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 led to a greater than 50% reduction in the increase of IL-1β expression levels compared to vehicle-treated controls. Increases in TNF-α expression were also significantly suppressed by these three mediators at 24 hours, but not at 72 hours, except for RvE1 application, which led to a significant lowering of TNF-α expression at 72 hours. The expression of IL-1α was not significantly altered by these lipid mediators.

#### Impact of ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 on the Expression of VEGFs and VEGFRs

To determine the effect of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 on angiogenesis, the mRNA expression for critical ligands (VEGF-A, -C, -D) and receptors (VEGFR-2, -3) involved in angiogenesis was measured. In contrast to the vehicle-treated group, the ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 application groups uniformly had lower mRNA expressions of the angiogenic growth factors VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and their receptor VEGFR-2 at 24 hours and 72 hours after suture placement. However, mRNA expression for the lymphangiogenic growth factor VEGF-D and its receptor VEGFR-3 were not significantly altered in the ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 groups compared to vehicle-treated controls.

#### Evaluation of Clinical CNV and Histologic Assessment of HA and LA

The growth of corneal neovessels over a 2-week period was measured by slit lamp biomicroscopy. Application with any of the three lipid mediators led to significant suppression of the angiogenic response compared to the vehicle control. By day 14 after suture placement, the density of blood vessels (BVs) was significantly suppressed with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 application compared to vehicle application. No significant changes in the density of lymphatic vessels (LVs) were observed among the lipid mediator groups compared to the vehicle controls.

### Discussion

The present invention demonstrates that the lipid mediators ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1, in addition to their resolving effects on innate immunity, regulate VEGF-A/-C and VEGFR2, and as a result, significantly reduce the development of neovascularization (NV) in the inflamed cornea. The neutrophil is the most prominent and earliest cell to migrate to the cornea in the early stages of inflammation, and anti-inflammatory lipid mediators can promote resolution by shortening the duration of neutrophil tissue infiltration. The administration of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 indeed blocked neutrophil infiltration of the cornea at 24 hours and 72 hours after insult, coinciding with the downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokine expression known to be secreted by innate immunocytes, particularly neutrophils. Moreover, macrophage infiltration is also reduced significantly after the local administration of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1.

Among all the angiogenic factors, the VEGF species play a pivotal role in vascular development. Ligation of VEGFR2 by VEGF-A is critical in vascular endothelial cell (EC) proliferation and differentiation in hemangiogenesis. Binding of VEGF-C/-D to VEGFR3 stimulates the development of lymphatic vessels. VEGF-C can also bind to and activate VEGFR2 and thereby contribute to hemangiogenesis, despite having weaker binding affinity than VEGF-A. Treatments with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 significantly reduced the gene expression levels of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and their receptor VEGFR2 but not of VEGF-D or VEGFR3. This indicates that such treatments selectively regulate hemangiogenesis rather than lymphangiogenesis, a finding further supported by immunohistochemistry.

The contribution of RvD1 and RvE1 to the regulation of angiogenesis through the suppression of TNF-α expression has been reported based on a hypoxia-induced pathologic retinal NV mouse model. Here, it is shown that RvD1 and RvE1 efficiently reduce IL-1β-induced, but not VEGF-A-induced, corneal angiogenesis. This suggests that the anti-angiogenesis function of RvD1 and RvE1 largely depends on their anti-inflammatory/pro-resolution function rather than on the direct regulation of VEGF-A function. This is further supported by the reduction of macrophage infiltration observed in IL-1β-induced corneal angiogenesis with RvE1 and RvD1 treatment.

Interestingly, ATLa treatment reduced not only IL-1β-induced corneal angiogenesis but also that induced by VEGF stimulation. The exclusive ability to suppress VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis by ATLa treatment could, in part, result from impairment in the early stage of EC migration. Compared with other treatments, ATLa is thought to be active for a longer duration because it is a stable analog of LXA4/ATL, which resists local inactivation. Moreover, ATLa exerts its inhibitory effects on multiple steps of VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, such as the inhibition of EC adhesion and the suppression of VEGF-A-induced EC proliferation. These factors explain the actions of ATLa and its demonstrably higher potency in suppressing angiogenesis compared with the synthetic forms of RvD1 and RvE1.

The anti-inflammation and pro-resolution functions of LXA4 are related to the receptor ALX/FPRL1, which has been identified on neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells, epithelial cells, and keratocytes in humans. This function is subserved by multiple receptors in the murine system, including Lxa4r/Fprl1 and Fpr-rs2, which share 89% and 83% homology at the nucleotide and protein levels, respectively. ChemR23, a receptor for RvE1 involved in the attenuation of TNF-α-activated NF-κB, is abundantly expressed in macrophages and dendritic cells but less so in neutrophils. The present findings demonstrate that Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23, but not Lxa4r/Fprl1, are expressed by the infiltrating CD11b+ cells (which include macrophages, monocytic dendritic cells, and a subset of neutrophils) in the cornea. These results suggest that ATLa activation of Fpr-rs2 and RvE1 activation of ChemR23 on these CD11b+ immunocytes stop their local migration and cytokine production into the cornea.

Finally, though the expression of ALX in the cornea has been implicated in epithelial cell proliferation in a wound-healing model, the location of ALX in the cornea has not been established to date. Here, for the first time, the distributions of Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 in the cornea are distinguished, which include epithelial cells and stromal keratocytes in normal and inflammatory conditions. In conclusion, ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 effectively resolve corneal inflammation and angiogenesis by controlling innate inflammation by marked reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion and inhibiting VEGF/VEGFR expression. These novel lipid mediators offer a potentially new therapeutic strategy for controlling corneal angiogenesis, a leading cause of blindness worldwide.

### Methods

#### Animals

Six-to 8-week-old male BALB/c mice were used in all experiments. All experimental protocols were approved by the Schepens Eye Research Institute Animal Care and Use Committee, and all animals were treated according to the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research.

#### Suture-Induced Inflammatory Corneal Angiogenesis

A standard model for the induction of inflammatory CNV was used, involving the development of intrastromal vessels in close association with a mixed-cell (primarily neutrophilic) infiltrate. Three interrupted sutures (11-0 nylon) were placed intrastromally with two stromal incursions extending over 120° of the corneal circumference to induce inflammatory CNV. The corneas were followed by slit lamp biomicroscopy for CNV development. NV was graded between 0 and 3, with increments of 0.5, using a grid system per each corneal quadrant based on the centripetal extent of the neovascular branches from the limbus. Scores for each quadrant were then summed to derive the NV index (range, 0 -12) for each eye.

#### Corneal Micropocket Assay

The corneal micropocket assay in mice and quantification of the resultant NV have been described previously. In brief, 0.3 μL hydron pellets containing 30 ng murine IL-1β or 200 ng VEGF-A were prepared and implanted into the corneal stroma of male BALB/c mice. After 7 days, the animals were euthanized, and the corneas were harvested for quantitative analysis of hemangiogenesis (HA) and lymphangiogenesis (LA).

#### Ocular Administration of Compounds

BALB/c mice were randomized to receive ATLa, RvD1, RvE1, or vehicle (normal saline) by subconjunctival injection in a masked fashion after suture or hydron pellet placement. The compounds were administered at a dose of 100 ng/10 μL per mouse every 48 hours after suture or pellet placement. RvD1 and RvE1 were prepared by total organic synthesis, and ATLa was synthesized as described previously. Physical properties were monitored routinely by liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry matching the reported biological and physical properties before analysis in the present experiments.

#### RNA Isolation and RT-PCR

Corneas were carefully dissected to ensure that the conjunctival and iris tissues were not included. Total RNA was isolated using a combined method with Trizol reagent and spin columns. Reverse transcription of total RNA was conducted using oligo(dT)20 primer and reverse transcriptase. PCR was conducted using primer pairs for Fprl1, Fpr-rs2, ChemR23, and GAPDH. PCR products were observed by agarose gel electrophoresis, and the mean density of each band was measured with ImageJ software.

#### Real-Time PCR

Total cDNA was synthesized from 400 ng total RNA with random hexamers using reverse transcriptase. Quantitative PCR was performed with TaqMan Universal PCR master mix and dye-labeled predesigned primers for IL-1α, TNF-α, IL-1β, VEGFR2, VEGFR3, VEGFA, and VEGFC. PCR conditions were 2 minutes at 50°C, 10 minutes at 95°C, followed by 35 cycles of 15 seconds at 95°C and 1 minute at 60°C. PCR amplification of the housekeeping gene encoding GAPDH was performed during each run for each sample to allow normalization between samples. Results were analyzed by the comparative threshold cycle (Ct) method.

#### Isolation of Cornea-Infiltrating Cells

Forty corneas were pooled, teased with scissors, and digested with collagenase D at 37°C for 1 hour in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. After incubation, corneas were disrupted by grinding with a syringe plunger. Total cells were then collected after passing through a steel mesh. On blockade by anti-FcR mAb, these cells were labeled with FITC-conjugated rat anti-mouse CD11b (granulocyte/monocyte/macrophage marker) at 4°C for 30 minutes. CD11b+ cells were sorted from total cells with a high-performance cell sorter.

#### MK/T-1 Cell Culture and Stimulation

MK/T-1 cells, immortalized keratocytes from the corneal stroma of C57BL/6 mouse, were used to identify the expression of lipid mediator receptors on the corneal keratocytes. MK/T-1 cells were grown in low-glucose Dulbecco minimum essential medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1 mM α-glutamine at 37°C in 5% CO2. To stimulate MK/T-1 cells, 10 ng/mL TNF-α and 10 ng/mL IL-1β were added to the culture medium.

#### Immunohistochemical Studies

Full-thickness corneal tissue or 8-μm frozen sections were fixed in acetone for 15 minutes at room temperature. To block nonspecific staining, anti-FcR mAb or 10% goat serum was used before primary antibodies or isotype-matched control antibodies were applied at 4°C overnight. Samples were incubated with secondary antibodies at room temperature. Each step was followed by three thorough washings in PBS for 5 to 10 minutes. Finally, the samples were covered with mounting medium and analyzed by epifluorescence microscopy. The areas covered by CD31 high/LYVE-1 low vessels (blood vessels) or CD31 low/LYVE-1 high vessels (lymphatics) were measured morphometrically with ImageJ software.

#### Statistical Analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± SEM. Statistical significance between the vehicle and each lipid mediator group was analyzed by the two-tailed t-test.

### Lipoxins

Lipoxins are a class of bioactive lipid mediators derived from arachidonic acid. They are known for their anti-inflammatory and pro-resolution properties. Aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa) is a stable analog of lipoxin A4 that is resistant to local inactivation. ATLa has been shown to reduce neutrophil infiltration, downregulate pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, and inhibit the expression of pro-angiogenic factors such as VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2. The mechanism of action of ATLa involves the activation of the Fpr-rs2 receptor, which is expressed by corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating CD11b+ cells.

In the context of corneal neovascularization, ATLa has been found to be particularly effective in reducing VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis. This is attributed to its ability to impair the early stage of endothelial cell migration and exert inhibitory effects on multiple steps of VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, such as the inhibition of endothelial cell adhesion and the suppression of VEGF-A-induced endothelial cell proliferation.

Overall, the use of ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 offers a promising therapeutic approach for reducing corneal neovascularization and associated inflammation, providing a new avenue for the treatment of corneal diseases and injuries.
