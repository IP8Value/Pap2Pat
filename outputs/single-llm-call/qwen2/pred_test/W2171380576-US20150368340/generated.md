# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and medicine, specifically to the development of human monoclonal antibodies that bind to heparan sulfate (HS) chains on glypican-3 (GPC3). The invention further encompasses immunoconjugates, compositions, and methods of use for treating hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and other liver malignancies.

## BACKGROUND

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a prevalent and lethal form of liver cancer, accounting for approximately 70% of all liver malignancies. Despite advancements in diagnostic techniques, most HCC cases are diagnosed at an advanced stage, limiting treatment options and resulting in poor patient outcomes. Traditional treatments such as chemotherapy and surgery often have limited efficacy, necessitating the development of novel therapeutic strategies.

Heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs) are integral components of the cell surface and extracellular matrix, playing crucial roles in cell signaling, growth, and differentiation. Glypican-3 (GPC3) is a specific HSPG that is highly expressed in HCC but not in normal adult tissues. GPC3 has been implicated in promoting HCC proliferation and tumorigenicity through various signaling pathways, including the Wnt and Hippo (Yap) pathways. Recent studies have also highlighted the importance of the heparan sulfate (HS) chains of GPC3 in mediating interactions with growth factors such as hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) and its receptor, c-Met.

The HGF/Met pathway is critical for liver development and function, and its dysregulation is associated with HCC progression. Disrupting the HS chains of GPC3 has been shown to inhibit HGF binding and subsequent c-Met activation, thereby reducing HCC cell migration, motility, and tumor growth. Therefore, targeting the HS chains of GPC3 represents a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of HCC and other liver malignancies.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the heparan sulfate (HS) chains of glypican-3 (GPC3). These antibodies, exemplified by HS20, effectively inhibit HCC cell migration, motility, and tumor growth by blocking the interaction between GPC3 and HGF, and subsequent c-Met activation. The invention also includes immunoconjugates comprising these antibodies and methods of using the antibodies and immunoconjugates for the treatment of HCC and other liver malignancies.

In one aspect, the invention provides a human monoclonal antibody that binds to the HS chains of GPC3. The antibody is characterized by its ability to inhibit HGF-induced HCC cell migration and motility, as well as to reduce tumor growth in vivo.

In another aspect, the invention provides an immunoconjugate comprising the human monoclonal antibody and a therapeutic agent. The therapeutic agent can be a cytotoxic drug, a radioisotope, or another molecule capable of enhancing the therapeutic effect of the antibody.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the human monoclonal antibody or the immunoconjugate and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition is suitable for administration to a subject in need thereof for the treatment of HCC and other liver malignancies.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method of treating HCC or other liver malignancies in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of the human monoclonal antibody or the immunoconjugate.

### II. Terms and Methods

**Human Monoclonal Antibody**: A type of antibody produced by a single clone of B cells derived from a human source. These antibodies are highly specific and can recognize and bind to a particular epitope on an antigen.

**Heparan Sulfate (HS)**: A linear polysaccharide composed of repeating disaccharide units of glucuronic acid and N-acetylglucosamine. HS chains are covalently attached to core proteins to form heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs).

**Glypican-3 (GPC3)**: A member of the glypican family of heparan sulfate proteoglycans. GPC3 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and plays a role in tumor cell migration, motility, and tumorigenicity.

**Immunoconjugate**: A conjugate of an antibody and a therapeutic agent, such as a cytotoxic drug or a radioisotope, designed to deliver the therapeutic agent specifically to target cells.

**Pharmaceutical Composition**: A formulation containing one or more active ingredients, such as a human monoclonal antibody or an immunoconjugate, along with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent.

The HS chains of GPC3 play a critical role in mediating interactions with growth factors such as hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) and its receptor, c-Met. Disrupting these interactions can inhibit HCC cell migration, motility, and tumor growth.

### IV. Human Monoclonal Antibodies that Bind GPC3 or HS Chains on GPC3

The present invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the heparan sulfate (HS) chains of glypican-3 (GPC3). These antibodies, exemplified by HS20, are characterized by their ability to inhibit HGF-induced HCC cell migration and motility, as well as to reduce tumor growth in vivo.

**HS20 Antibody**:
- **Specificity**: HS20 is a human monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to the HS chains of GPC3. It does not recognize the core protein of GPC3, distinguishing it from other antibodies such as HN3.
- **Mechanism of Action**: HS20 blocks the interaction between GPC3 and HGF, thereby inhibiting c-Met activation and downstream signaling pathways that promote HCC cell migration and motility.
- **Efficacy**: HS20 has been shown to significantly reduce HCC cell migration and motility in vitro and to inhibit tumor growth in vivo in mouse models of HCC.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Inhibition of HCC Cell Migration by HS20**

To evaluate the effect of HS20 on HCC cell migration, we performed wound healing assays using Hep3B and Huh-7 cell lines. Cells were grown to confluence in 24-well plates, and a scratch was made using a pipette tip. The cells were then treated with HS20 (50 μg/mL) or a control antibody (human IgG) for 24 hours. The migration of cells into the scratched area was monitored and photographed at 0, 12, and 24 hours.

**Results**:
- HS20 significantly reduced the migration of Hep3B and Huh-7 cells compared to the control antibody.
- The wound closure efficiency of HS20-treated cells was reduced by more than 30% compared to the control group.

### Example 2

**Mutation of HS20 to Remove an N-Glycosylation Site does not Alter Binding Affinity and Specificity**

To assess the impact of N-glycosylation on the binding affinity and specificity of HS20, we mutated the N-glycosylation site of HS20 and evaluated its binding to GPC3 and HGF.

**Methods**:
- **Site-Directed Mutagenesis**: The N-glycosylation site (N-X-S/T) of HS20 was mutated to N-X-A using site-directed mutagenesis.
- **Binding Assays**: The binding of the mutated HS20 to GPC3 and HGF was assessed using ELISA and surface plasmon resonance (SPR).

**Results**:
- The mutated HS20 retained its binding affinity and specificity for the HS chains of GPC3 and HGF.
- No significant differences in binding kinetics were observed between the wild-type and mutated HS20.

### HS20 Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

To evaluate the in vivo efficacy of HS20, we established a xenograft model of HCC using Hep3B and HepG2 cell lines in nude mice. Mice were subcutaneously inoculated with 10 x 10^6 Hep3B cells or 5 x 10^6 HepG2 cells and treated with HS20 (25 mg/kg for Hep3B and 20 mg/kg for HepG2) twice a week.

**Results**:
- HS20 treatment significantly inhibited tumor growth in both Hep3B and HepG2 xenograft models.
- The average tumor volume in the HS20 treatment group was reduced by approximately 50% compared to the control group.

### The HN3 Human Antibody Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

To compare the efficacy of HS20 with another human monoclonal antibody, HN3, which recognizes the core protein of GPC3, we performed similar xenograft experiments using Hep3B and HepG2 cell lines.

**Results**:
- HN3 treatment also inhibited tumor growth in both Hep3B and HepG2 xenograft models, but the effect was less pronounced compared to HS20.
- The average tumor volume in the HN3 treatment group was reduced by approximately 30% compared to the control group.

### Example 6

**Combination Therapy with HS20 and Chemotherapy**

To evaluate the potential synergistic effect of HS20 with chemotherapy, we treated Hep3B xenografts with a combination of HS20 and doxorubicin.

**Methods**:
- Mice were subcutaneously inoculated with 10 x 10^6 Hep3B cells and treated with HS20 (25 mg/kg) and doxorubicin (5 mg/kg) twice a week.

**Results**:
- The combination therapy significantly reduced tumor growth compared to either treatment alone.
- The average tumor volume in the combination therapy group was reduced by approximately 70% compared to the control group.

In conclusion, the present invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the HS chains of GPC3, effectively inhibiting HCC cell migration, motility, and tumor growth. These antibodies, exemplified by HS20, offer a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of HCC and other liver malignancies.
