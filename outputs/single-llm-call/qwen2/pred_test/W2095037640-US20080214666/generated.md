# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of fatty acid (FA) biosynthesis and metabolism in eukaryotic organisms, particularly in the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans*. Specifically, the invention concerns the identification and characterization of enzymes involved in the biosynthesis of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) and the regulatory mechanisms governing their production. The invention also encompasses methods for modulating the levels of mmBCFAs and their use in various biological and medical applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Fatty acids (FAs) are essential molecules involved in energy storage, membrane structure, and various signaling pathways. Different FAs have distinct physical properties that determine their unique functions. Monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) are a subclass of FAs characterized by a single methyl group appended to the carbon chain. These FAs are ubiquitous in nature, particularly in bacteria, where they play crucial roles in membrane function, fluidity, and proton permeability. Despite their prevalence and importance in prokaryotes, the biosynthesis and functions of mmBCFAs in eukaryotes have been less well understood.

Recent studies have shown that mmBCFAs are present in various eukaryotic organisms, including fungi, plants, and animals. In mammals, mmBCFAs have been detected in several tissues, such as skin, hair, brain, and blood, and have been implicated in various physiological processes and diseases. For instance, C21anteISO, a mmBCFA, is a major covalently bound FA in mammalian hair fibers, and its removal leads to a loss of hydrophobicity. Additionally, C15ISO has been shown to inhibit the growth of human cancer cells by inducing apoptosis.

The biosynthesis of long-chain mmBCFAs in bacteria involves a unique pathway that differs from the conventional straight-chain FA synthesis. This pathway requires branched-chain CoA primers derived from branched-chain amino acids and specific enzymes for their elongation. However, the enzymes and pathways responsible for mmBCFA biosynthesis in eukaryotes have not been fully characterized.

The present invention addresses this gap by identifying and characterizing key enzymes involved in the biosynthesis of mmBCFAs in *C. elegans*. The invention also provides insights into the regulatory mechanisms governing mmBCFA production and the physiological roles of these FAs in growth and development.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for modulating the levels of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) in eukaryotic organisms, particularly in *Caenorhabditis elegans*. The method involves the use of specific enzymes, such as ELO-5 and ELO-6, which are involved in the elongation of mmBCFAs. The invention also encompasses the identification of regulatory factors, such as the nematode homolog of SREBP-1c (lpd-1), that control the expression of these enzymes.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for increasing the levels of mmBCFAs in *C. elegans* by overexpressing ELO-5 and/or ELO-6. In another aspect, the invention provides a method for decreasing the levels of mmBCFAs by inhibiting the expression of ELO-5 and/or ELO-6, for example, through RNA interference (RNAi).

The invention further provides a method for rescuing the growth and developmental defects caused by a deficiency in mmBCFAs by supplementing the diet of *C. elegans* with exogenous mmBCFAs, such as C15ISO and C17ISO.

Additionally, the invention identifies and characterizes other genes that are differentially expressed in response to mmBCFA deficiency, including pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2. These genes may be involved in feedback regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis and may provide targets for further modulation of mmBCFA levels.

The invention also provides methods for using the identified enzymes and regulatory factors in various biological and medical applications, such as the treatment of diseases associated with mmBCFA deficiencies or imbalances.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Identification and Characterization of Enzymes Involved in mmBCFA Biosynthesis

#### ELO-5 and ELO-6: Key Enzymes in mmBCFA Elongation

The present invention identifies and characterizes two enzymes, ELO-5 and ELO-6, which are involved in the elongation of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) in *C. elegans*. ELO-5 is primarily responsible for the biosynthesis of C15ISO, while ELO-6 is involved in the elongation of C15ISO to C17ISO.

**ELO-5:**
- **Function:** ELO-5 is a fatty acid elongation enzyme that catalyzes the elongation of branched-chain fatty acids, specifically C15ISO.
- **Expression:** ELO-5 is expressed in the gut and certain head cells of *C. elegans*.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of ELO-5 results in a significant reduction in the levels of both C15ISO and C17ISO, leading to growth and developmental defects, including L1 arrest and egg-laying defects.

**ELO-6:**
- **Function:** ELO-6 is another fatty acid elongation enzyme that catalyzes the elongation of C15ISO to C17ISO.
- **Expression:** ELO-6 is expressed in the gut, neurons, pharynx, and vulva muscles of *C. elegans*.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of ELO-6 results in a significant reduction in the levels of C17ISO, but not C15ISO, suggesting that ELO-6 is specifically involved in the elongation step.

### Regulatory Mechanisms Governing mmBCFA Biosynthesis

#### Role of LPD-1 in mmBCFA Biosynthesis

The invention also identifies the nematode homolog of SREBP-1c, LPD-1, as a key regulator of mmBCFA biosynthesis. LPD-1 is a transcription factor that controls the expression of several lipogenic enzymes, including ELO-5 and ELO-6.

**LPD-1:**
- **Function:** LPD-1 promotes the expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6, thereby regulating the biosynthesis of mmBCFAs.
- **Expression:** LPD-1 is expressed in the gut and a subset of head neurons, including amphid neurons.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of LPD-1 results in a significant reduction in the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO, as well as changes in the levels of other fatty acids, such as C18:2 n6 and C16:0.

### Rescue of Growth and Developmental Defects

The invention provides methods for rescuing the growth and developmental defects caused by a deficiency in mmBCFAs. These methods involve supplementing the diet of *C. elegans* with exogenous mmBCFAs, such as C15ISO and C17ISO.

**Rescue with Exogenous mmBCFAs:**
- **C17ISO and C17anteISO:** Supplementation with C17ISO and C17anteISO can effectively rescue the L1 arrest phenotype caused by RNAi of ELO-5.
- **C15ISO and C15anteISO:** Partial rescue is observed with supplementation of C15ISO and C15anteISO, suggesting that C17ISO is the principal molecule conveying the ELO-5 function.

### Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes

The invention also identifies several genes that are differentially expressed in response to mmBCFA deficiency, including pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2. These genes may be involved in feedback regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis and may provide targets for further modulation of mmBCFA levels.

**pnk-1:**
- **Function:** pnk-1 is a pantothenate kinase homolog that is upregulated in response to mmBCFA deficiency.
- **Expression:** pnk-1 is expressed in the gut and certain head neurons.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of pnk-1 results in a significant reduction in the levels of mmBCFAs.

**nhr-49:**
- **Function:** nhr-49 is a nuclear hormone receptor that is upregulated in response to mmBCFA deficiency.
- **Expression:** nhr-49 is expressed in the gut and various tissues.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of nhr-49 results in an increase in fat accumulation and changes in the levels of saturated fatty acids.

**acs-1:**
- **Function:** acs-1 is a very-long-chain fatty acid CoA ligase homolog that is upregulated in response to mmBCFA deficiency.
- **Expression:** acs-1 is expressed in the gut and certain head neurons.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of acs-1 results in a significant reduction in the levels of mmBCFAs.

**C27H6.2:**
- **Function:** C27H6.2 is a RuvB-like DNA binding protein homolog that is downregulated in response to mmBCFA deficiency.
- **Expression:** C27H6.2 is expressed in the gut and various tissues.
- **RNAi Effects:** RNAi of C27H6.2 results in an increase in the levels of monounsaturated fatty acids.

### Biological and Medical Applications

The identified enzymes and regulatory factors have various biological and medical applications, including:

- **Treatment of Diseases:** Modulating the levels of mmBCFAs can be used to treat diseases associated with mmBCFA deficiencies or imbalances, such as certain types of cancer and metabolic disorders.
- **Nutritional Supplements:** Exogenous mmBCFAs can be used as nutritional supplements to support growth and development in organisms.
- **Research Tools:** The identified enzymes and regulatory factors can be used as research tools to study the biosynthesis and functions of mmBCFAs in various organisms.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Overexpression of ELO-5 and ELO-6

To increase the levels of mmBCFAs in *C. elegans*, a transgenic strain was created overexpressing ELO-5 and ELO-6. The transgenic animals exhibited a significant increase in the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO, as measured by gas chromatography (GC). The overexpression of ELO-5 and ELO-6 did not result in any observable morphological or growth defects, indicating that the increased levels of mmBCFAs are well tolerated by the organism.

#### Example 2: RNAi of ELO-5 and ELO-6

To decrease the levels of mmBCFAs in *C. elegans*, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6. RNAi of ELO-5 resulted in a significant reduction in the levels of both C15ISO and C17ISO, leading to growth and developmental defects, including L1 arrest and egg-laying defects. RNAi of ELO-6 resulted in a significant reduction in the levels of C17ISO, but not C15ISO, confirming the specific role of ELO-6 in the elongation step.

#### Example 3: Rescue of L1 Arrest with Exogenous mmBCFAs

To rescue the L1 arrest phenotype caused by RNAi of ELO-5, *C. elegans* were supplemented with exogenous mmBCFAs, including C17ISO and C17anteISO. Supplementation with C17ISO and C17anteISO effectively rescued the L1 arrest phenotype, allowing the animals to grow and develop normally. Partial rescue was observed with supplementation of C15ISO and C15anteISO, suggesting that C17ISO is the principal molecule conveying the ELO-5 function.

#### Example 4: RNAi of LPD-1

To study the role of LPD-1 in mmBCFA biosynthesis, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of LPD-1. RNAi of LPD-1 resulted in a significant reduction in the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO, as well as changes in the levels of other fatty acids, such as C18:2 n6 and C16:0. The RNAi-treated animals displayed slow growth, morphological abnormalities, and egg-laying defects, but no larval arrest.

#### Example 5: RNAi of pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2

To study the role of differentially expressed genes in mmBCFA biosynthesis, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2. RNAi of pnk-1 and acs-1 resulted in a significant reduction in the levels of mmBCFAs, confirming their involvement in mmBCFA biosynthesis. RNAi of nhr-49 resulted in an increase in fat accumulation and changes in the levels of saturated fatty acids. RNAi of C27H6.2 resulted in an increase in the levels of monounsaturated fatty acids.

#### Example 6: Overexpression of LPD-1

To increase the levels of mmBCFAs in *C. elegans*, a transgenic strain was created overexpressing LPD-1. The transgenic animals exhibited a significant increase in the levels of C15ISO and C17ISO, as measured by GC. The overexpression of LPD-1 did not result in any observable morphological or growth defects, indicating that the increased levels of mmBCFAs are well tolerated by the organism.

#### Example 7: RNAi of ELO-5 and Supplementation with Bacterial mmBCFAs

To study the ability of *C. elegans* to utilize exogenous mmBCFAs, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of ELO-5, and the animals were supplemented with mmBCFAs produced by the bacterium *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia*. The RNAi-treated animals accumulated bacterial C15ISO and C15anteISO and efficiently elongated these FA species to C17ISO and C17anteISO, indicating that ELO-6 function remains intact in the RNAi animals.

#### Example 8: RNAi of LPD-1 and Supplementation with C17ISO

To study the role of LPD-1 in mmBCFA biosynthesis, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of LPD-1, and the animals were supplemented with C17ISO. Supplementation with C17ISO did not significantly rescue the growth and developmental defects caused by RNAi of LPD-1, suggesting that LPD-1 has additional functions beyond regulating the expression of ELO-5 and ELO-6.

#### Example 9: RNAi of FAS

To study the role of fatty acid synthase (FAS) in mmBCFA biosynthesis, RNAi was used to suppress the expression of FAS. RNAi of FAS resulted in multiple defects and a lethal growth arrest, but the FA composition of the affected animals remained unchanged, indicating that disruption of FAS does not selectively alter FA biosynthesis and that neither FAS protein is specific for mmBCFA.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a comprehensive understanding of the biosynthesis and regulation of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) in *C. elegans*. The identification and characterization of key enzymes, such as ELO-5 and ELO-6, and regulatory factors, such as LPD-1, offer valuable insights into the physiological roles of mmBCFAs in growth and development. The invention also provides methods for modulating the levels of mmBCFAs and their use in various biological and medical applications.
