## BACKGROUND

Breast cancer metastasis is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women. The metastatic process involves a series of complex steps, including the invasion of cancer cells through the extracellular matrix (ECM), intravasation into the bloodstream, and colonization of distant organs. Key to this process is the ability of cancer cells to form elongated membrane protrusions driven by the polymerization of branched F-actin networks. This actin polymerization is orchestrated by various kinases and cytoskeletal regulatory proteins, such as c-Src and c-Abl kinases, Rac GTPase, the Scar/WAVE complex, and Ena/VASP proteins.

Ena/VASP proteins, including Mena, EVL, and VASP, play a crucial role in enhancing processive filament elongation, which is critical for cell migration and invasion. Mena, in particular, is upregulated in breast cancer and promotes invasive behavior. Lamellipodin (Lpd) has been identified as a binding partner of Ena/VASP proteins and is localized to lamellipodia, the thin membrane protrusions at the leading edge of migrating cells. The interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP is positively regulated by Abl kinase-mediated phosphorylation of Lpd, which facilitates the recruitment of Ena/VASP to lamellipodia.

While the role of Lpd in lamellipodium formation and random 2D cell migration has been established, the mechanisms by which Lpd coordinates the interplay between actin-elongation and actin-branching factors to promote cancer cell invasion in a 3D environment remain incompletely understood. This invention addresses this gap by elucidating the role of Lpd in mediating invasive 3D migration of cancer cells through selective, regulated interactions with Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE. The findings highlight the importance of increased Lpd levels in breast cancer invasion and metastasis, providing a novel target for therapeutic intervention.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and compositions for inhibiting the metastasis of breast cancer cells by modulating the expression and activity of Lamellipodin (Lpd). Specifically, the invention provides a method for reducing the metastatic potential of breast cancer cells by downregulating Lpd expression or interfering with its interactions with Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising agents that target Lpd, Ena/VASP, or Scar/WAVE, and methods for using these compositions to treat or prevent breast cancer metastasis.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting breast cancer cell invasion and metastasis, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an agent that reduces the expression or activity of Lpd. The agent may be an siRNA, shRNA, antisense oligonucleotide, small molecule inhibitor, or antibody specific for Lpd.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting breast cancer cell invasion and metastasis, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an agent that disrupts the interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP or Scar/WAVE. The agent may be a peptide, small molecule, or antibody that specifically binds to and blocks the interaction sites on Lpd, Ena/VASP, or Scar/WAVE.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an agent that reduces the expression or activity of Lpd, an agent that disrupts the interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP or Scar/WAVE, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

The invention further provides methods for diagnosing the risk of breast cancer metastasis by detecting the expression levels of Lpd in breast cancer tissues. The methods may involve analyzing Lpd mRNA or protein levels in biopsy samples and correlating these levels with the likelihood of metastasis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention is based on the discovery that Lamellipodin (Lpd) plays a critical role in mediating the invasive and metastatic behavior of breast cancer cells. Lpd is a key regulator of actin cytoskeletal dynamics, interacting with both Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex to coordinate actin filament elongation and branching. The invention provides methods and compositions for inhibiting breast cancer metastasis by targeting Lpd and its interactions with Ena/VASP and Scar/WAVE.

### Mechanism of Action

Lpd is upregulated in invasive and metastatic breast cancer cell lines and is associated with poor clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients. The invention demonstrates that Lpd promotes breast cancer cell invasion and metastasis by facilitating the formation and persistence of lamellipodial protrusions in response to growth factor stimulation, such as epidermal growth factor (EGF). Lpd achieves this by recruiting Ena/VASP proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex to the leading edge of migrating cells, where they drive actin polymerization and membrane protrusion.

The interaction between Lpd and Ena/VASP is positively regulated by Abl kinase-mediated phosphorylation of Lpd, while the interaction between Lpd and the Scar/WAVE complex is positively regulated by both Abl and Src kinase-mediated phosphorylation. This differential regulation allows Lpd to fine-tune actin cytoskeletal dynamics, optimizing protrusion morphology and dynamics during cellular responses to growth factors and extracellular matrix (ECM) composition.

### Methods for Inhibiting Breast Cancer Metastasis

#### Downregulation of Lpd Expression

One aspect of the invention involves reducing the expression of Lpd in breast cancer cells. This can be achieved using various molecular tools, including siRNAs, shRNAs, antisense oligonucleotides, and small molecule inhibitors. For example, shRNAs targeting Lpd can be delivered to breast cancer cells using viral vectors, such as lentiviruses or retroviruses. The reduction in Lpd expression impairs the ability of cancer cells to form lamellipodia and migrate, thereby reducing their invasive and metastatic potential.

#### Disruption of Lpd-Ena/VASP and Lpd-Scar/WAVE Interactions

Another aspect of the invention involves disrupting the interactions between Lpd and its binding partners, Ena/VASP and the Scar/WAVE complex. This can be achieved using peptides, small molecules, or antibodies that specifically bind to and block the interaction sites on Lpd, Ena/VASP, or Scar/WAVE. For example, peptides derived from the interaction domains of Lpd can be designed to compete with the natural binding partners and inhibit the formation of the Lpd-Ena/VASP and Lpd-Scar/WAVE complexes. Small molecules that interfere with these interactions can also be identified through high-throughput screening and optimized for therapeutic use.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising agents that target Lpd, Ena/VASP, or Scar/WAVE, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and topical. The agents can be encapsulated in nanoparticles, liposomes, or other delivery systems to enhance their stability and bioavailability.

### Methods for Diagnosing Breast Cancer Metastasis

The invention also provides methods for diagnosing the risk of breast cancer metastasis by detecting the expression levels of Lpd in breast cancer tissues. This can be achieved using quantitative PCR (qPCR) to measure Lpd mRNA levels or immunohistochemistry (IHC) to detect Lpd protein levels in biopsy samples. Elevated levels of Lpd, particularly at the plasma membrane, are indicative of a higher risk of metastasis.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Downregulation of Lpd Expression Reduces Breast Cancer Metastasis

In this example, MDA-MB-231-LM2 cells, a highly metastatic derivative of MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells, were stably transfected with two independent shRNAs targeting Lpd or a non-targeting control shRNA. The cells were then injected orthotopically into the mammary fat pad of immunodeficient mice. After 6 weeks, the primary tumors were harvested, and the lungs were examined for metastases. The results showed that Lpd knockdown significantly reduced the number and size of lung metastases compared to control cells, demonstrating the efficacy of Lpd downregulation in inhibiting breast cancer metastasis.

#### Example 2: Disruption of Lpd-Ena/VASP Interaction Impairs Breast Cancer Cell Invasion

In this example, MDA-MB-231 cells were transfected with a peptide derived from the interaction domain of Lpd that competes with Ena/VASP for binding. The cells were then subjected to a 3D invasion assay in matrigel. The results showed that disruption of the Lpd-Ena/VASP interaction significantly reduced the ability of breast cancer cells to invade through the matrigel, highlighting the importance of this interaction in promoting invasive behavior.

#### Example 3: Regulation of Lpd-Scar/WAVE Interaction by Src Kinase

In this example, MDA-MB-231 cells were transfected with wild-type c-Src or a kinase-inactive mutant of c-Src. The cells were then stimulated with EGF, and the interaction between Lpd and the Scar/WAVE complex was analyzed by co-immunoprecipitation. The results showed that c-Src kinase activity positively regulates the interaction between Lpd and the Scar/WAVE complex, providing insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying Lpd's pro-metastatic function.
