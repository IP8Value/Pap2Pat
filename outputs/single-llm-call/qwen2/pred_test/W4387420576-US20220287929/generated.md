# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel matrix of lipid particles, specifically designated as SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, for the topical treatment of skin conditions associated with the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). The invention provides a method for preparing these lipid particles and their use in cosmetic formulations to enhance skin hydration, improve skin barrier function, and reduce the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines, thereby addressing the aging process of the skin.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The skin undergoes a natural aging process, characterized by senescence, which is influenced by both intrinsic and extrinsic factors. Intrinsic factors include biological, biochemical, and molecular mechanisms that lead to a decreased proliferative capacity of cells, reduced extracellular matrix synthesis in the dermis, and increased expression of enzymes that degrade the dermal matrix. Extrinsic factors, such as pollutants and ultraviolet (UV) radiation, exacerbate the aging process by inducing cumulative oxidative damage, leading to the acceleration of skin senescence.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) play a critical role in the aging process. Under normal conditions, cells produce ROS during aerobic metabolism, which are essential for maintaining homeostasis. However, when ROS levels exceed the cellular antioxidant defenses, oxidative stress occurs, causing damage to macromolecules such as proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. The cell employs various substances, including vitamins A, C, and E, to counteract oxidative stress. Vitamin E protects polyunsaturated fatty acids in cell membranes, vitamin C safeguards macromolecules during cell metabolism and exposure to risk factors, and vitamin A preserves cell membranes and combats free radicals, making it a key ingredient in anti-wrinkle creams.

Despite the known benefits of these vitamins, their effectiveness can be limited by poor stability and skin irritation. Encapsulation of active substances in lipid matrices and nanoparticles has emerged as a promising approach to enhance the efficacy and stability of anti-aging treatments. Encapsulated active ingredients can provide better protection, reduce skin irritation, and enable controlled release, improving patient compliance and reducing the frequency of application. Additionally, lipid nanoparticles offer intrinsic benefits such as hydration, increased viscosity, and skin occlusion, which can further enhance the overall performance of the cosmetic formulation.

The present invention introduces SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, a novel matrix of lipid particles designed to address the limitations of free or encapsulated vitamins. This technology is based on a sustainable and solvent-free process, resulting in lipid particles that are stable, effective, and suitable for topical application. The invention aims to provide a robust solution for combating skin aging by effectively delivering antioxidants and other active ingredients to the skin.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel matrix of lipid particles, SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, for the topical treatment of skin conditions associated with the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). The lipid particles are prepared by a sustainable and solvent-free process, resulting in a stable and effective formulation. The invention includes a method for preparing the lipid particles and their use in cosmetic formulations to enhance skin hydration, improve skin barrier function, and reduce the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines.

The lipid particles are composed of a blend of waxes and natural polyunsaturated fatty acids, encapsulating vitamins A and C. The particles are designed to be highly sensitive to ROS, providing superior anti-aging properties compared to free or encapsulated vitamins. The invention also encompasses the use of the lipid particles in a gel base for topical application, demonstrating significant improvements in skin hydration, barrier function, and anti-aging effects in both in vitro and in vivo studies.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Preparation of SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™

The SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ system, hereinafter referred to as SIREN, is composed of lipid particles prepared by a modified emulsification method. The process involves the following steps:

1. **Melting of Waxes**: A blend of jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax is melted at a temperature of 55°C under magnetic stirring at 200 rpm for 10 minutes.
2. **Addition of Active Ingredients**: A mixture of natural polyunsaturated fatty acids obtained from safflower oil and other liposoluble active ingredients is added to the melted lipid mixture under magnetic stirring at 200 rpm while maintaining the temperature at 55°C until a homogeneous "fat" melted mixture is obtained. Just before emulsification, Retinyl palmitate (Vitamin A) is added under stirring.
3. **Preparation of Aqueous Dispersion**: An aqueous dispersion is prepared by adding decyl glucoside and sodium ascorbyl phosphate (Vitamin C) in water heated to 45-50°C, subsequently under stirring (500 rpm for 10 minutes).
4. **Emulsification**: The fat mixture prepared in step 2 is added to the aqueous dispersion prepared in step 3 by maintaining the temperature at 48-50°C under stirring at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes, obtaining an emulsion of lipid particles in an aqueous phase.
5. **Sieving and Cooling**: The emulsion obtained in step 4 is sieved through a net with pores with a diameter of 25 μm, obtaining an emulsion of lipid particles with a diameter of up to 25 μm in an aqueous phase. The emulsion is then cooled to 10°C by means of a water bath to accelerate the solidification of the lipid part. The microparticles are stored at 4°C until use.

The choice of the above-described mixture of waxes allows the particles to be prepared with a sustainable process that does not require high temperatures or organic solvents. The lipid particles are stable to oxidation, as evidenced by an iodine index of less than 60 g Iodine/100 g. The formulation containing Siren capsules was subjected to accelerated stability tests for 3 months at 40°C, room temperature (RT), and 4°C in its final packaging, with no significant changes observed in color, pH, viscosity, odor, aspect, or separation and aggregation.

#### In Vitro Evaluation of Protective Effects Against Menadione-Induced Oxidative Stress in Cell Cultures

The efficacy of SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ to counteract ROS production was tested using menadione, a well-characterized synthetic form of Vitamin K3, which generates mitochondrial ROS. The cells selected for this study were human keratinocytes (Hacat).

The cells were seeded in 96-well plates for 24 hours at 37°C and 5% CO2. The exhausted medium was replaced with fresh medium, and the samples were added at concentrations of 5 and 1.25 mg/mL diluted directly in the culture medium. The samples included the gel placebo, the gel containing free vitamins, the gel containing encapsulated vitamins, and the gel containing SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™. Four hours after the addition of the samples, 20 μM of menadione was added to stimulate ROS production.

The amount of hydrogen peroxide produced was measured using the ROS-Glo™ H₂O₂ Assay, a rapid and accurate in vitro test based on bioluminescence. The results showed that the SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ gel exhibited the highest protective activity against ROS production. At both tested concentrations, ROS production was significantly lower than in the control samples. Specifically, at a concentration of 5 mg/mL, the SIREN gel showed 44.5% ROS production, while at 1.25 mg/mL, the percentage produced was 78.59%. By comparison, the placebo gel, the gel containing free vitamins, and the gel containing encapsulated vitamins showed much higher ROS production.

#### In Vivo Evaluation

The in vivo studies were conducted on 30 healthy female volunteers aged 36-58, following a blinded clinical study design. The parameters evaluated included skin hydration, skin barrier function, and anti-aging effects. The products were applied daily for 4 weeks, and measurements were taken at the beginning (T0) and after 4 weeks of application (T1).

**Moisturizing Efficacy Evaluation**: The amount of water present in the stratum corneum was measured using a Corneometer. The SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ gel showed a significant increase in skin hydration, with a net effect of about +20.17% compared to the placebo gel and higher activity compared to gels containing free or encapsulated vitamins.

**Skin Barrier Function Efficacy Evaluation**: The integrity of the skin barrier was assessed by measuring transepidermal water loss (TEWL). The SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ gel produced a significant reduction in TEWL values, maintaining them within physiological limits. This indicates that the gel is well-tolerated and does not cause skin irritation.

**Anti-Aging Effect**: The anti-aging efficacy was evaluated by analyzing the micro-reliefs of the skin using 3D imaging software. The parameters considered were the average skin roughness (Ra) and the mean depth of roughness (Rz). The SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ gel demonstrated significant improvements in reducing the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines, with lower Ra and Rz values compared to the control gels.

The technology provides a robust solution for combating skin aging by effectively delivering antioxidants and other active ingredients to the skin, resulting in enhanced skin hydration, improved skin barrier function, and reduced appearance of wrinkles and fine lines. The sustainable and solvent-free process used to prepare the lipid particles ensures stability and effectiveness, making SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™ a promising innovation in the field of anti-aging cosmetics.
