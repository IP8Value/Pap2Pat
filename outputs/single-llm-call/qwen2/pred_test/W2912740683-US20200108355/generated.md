# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Fouling is a pervasive issue that impacts numerous industrial sectors, including cooling water towers, heat exchange systems, drinking water systems, wastewater treatment, and seawater desalination. Fouling is defined as the deposition of unwanted materials on surfaces, leading to reduced efficiency and increased operational costs. In membrane-based water treatment technologies, fouling can be categorized into four main types: mineral precipitation, colloidal or particulate matter fouling, organic fouling, and biofouling. Among these, biofouling is particularly problematic, especially in pressure-driven membrane processes. Biofouling occurs when microorganisms adhere to surfaces, grow, and secrete extracellular polymeric substances (EPS), forming a thin layer of microbial biofilm. This biofilm significantly reduces water flux and membrane permeability, necessitating higher operating pressures and frequent membrane cleaning or replacement, which increases energy consumption and maintenance costs.

Biofouling is a complex phenomenon influenced by various factors, including membrane surface characteristics, feed water composition, microbial characteristics, and operating conditions. Traditional methods to control biofouling, such as chemical and physical pretreatments, have limitations and can produce undesirable byproducts. Biological controls, such as the inhibition of quorum sensing (QS) and the use of bacteriophages, show promise but face challenges in industrial-scale application. Membrane cleaning and surface modification are also employed, but these methods often fail to provide long-term solutions.

Recent research has shown that arabic gum (AG) can improve the properties of polyethersulfone (PES) membranes, enhancing their hydrophilicity, porosity, and resistance to biofouling. However, the antifouling properties of polysulfone (PS)/AG membranes have been primarily tested with Escherichia coli (E. coli) bacteria. The present invention addresses this gap by fabricating and characterizing PS membranes with different AG loadings and testing their antifouling properties against a broader range of bacterial strains, including Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus), E. coli, Klebsiella pneumoniae (K. pneumoniae), and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa).

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel method for fabricating polysulfone (PS) membranes with varying loadings of arabic gum (AG) to enhance their antifouling properties. The invention provides a method for synthesizing PS/AG membranes with improved hydrophilicity, porosity, and negative surface charge, which collectively reduce bacterial colonization and biofouling. The membranes are characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), contact angle measurements, and zeta potential analysis. The antifouling properties of the PS/AG membranes are tested against a variety of bacterial strains, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

The key aspects of the invention include:
1. **Membrane Synthesis**: PS membranes are synthesized with different AG loadings (0%, 0.1%, 1%, 3%, 5%, and 7% by weight) using phase inversion techniques.
2. **Characterization**: The membranes are characterized for their surface morphology, porosity, pore size, hydrophilicity, and surface charge.
3. **Antifouling Properties**: The membranes are tested for their ability to inhibit bacterial colonization using SEM and colony-forming unit (CFU) counts.

The invention demonstrates that PS/AG membranes with 3% and 7% AG loadings exhibit the most significant reduction in bacterial colonization, making them suitable for various water treatment applications where biofouling is a critical concern.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Membrane Synthesis

The invention involves the synthesis of polysulfone (PS) membranes with varying loadings of arabic gum (AG) to enhance their antifouling properties. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Casting Solutions**:
   - PS with a molecular weight of 35 kDa is dissolved in N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMA) to form a 16 wt. % solution.
   - Different quantities of AG are added to the PS/DMA solution to achieve the desired AG loadings: 0% (control), 0.1%, 1%, 3%, 5%, and 7% by weight.
   - The casting solutions are subjected to sonication for 1 hour to ensure uniform dispersion of AG and stirred using a digital hotplate stirrer.
   - The solutions are degassed for 2 hours to eliminate air bubbles.

2. **Membrane Casting**:
   - The casting solutions are poured onto a glass plate using a Labcoat Master casting system.
   - The casting speed is set at 3 m/min, and a knife gap height of 200 μm is used.
   - The glass plate is immersed in a coagulation bath containing deionized water, where the solvent/non-solvent exchange occurs, causing the polymeric membrane to precipitate.
   - The cast membranes are washed and stored in deionized water.

### Characterization of Membrane Surface and Cross-Section

1. **Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**:
   - Field emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) is used to examine the surface and cross-sectional morphology of the synthesized membranes.
   - Samples are splintered using liquid nitrogen and sputtered with gold for high-resolution imaging.
   - The images reveal the presence of numerous pores and macrovoids in the membranes with higher AG loadings, indicating enhanced porosity and pore size.

2. **Membrane Porosity, Pore Size, and Flux**:
   - **Porosity**: The total porosity of the membranes is evaluated using the gravimetric method. The porosity increases with AG loading up to 3% and then slightly decreases at 7%.
   - **Pore Size**: The average pore size is calculated using the Guerout-Elford-Ferry equation. The pore size increases with AG loading up to 3% and then decreases at 7%.
   - **Flux**: The water flux of the membranes is measured using a dead-end stirred cell pressurized under nitrogen gas. The flux increases significantly with AG loading up to 3% and then decreases at 7%.

3. **Hydrophilicity and Membrane Surface Charge**:
   - **Contact Angle**: The hydrophilicity of the membranes is assessed using a drop shape analyzer. The contact angle decreases with increasing AG loading, indicating enhanced hydrophilicity.
   - **Zeta Potential**: The surface charge of the membranes is determined using a SurPASS™ 3 electrokinetic solid surface analyzer. The zeta potential becomes more negative with increasing AG loading, indicating a higher negative surface charge.

### Antifouling Properties of PS/AG Membranes

1. **Bacterial Strains**:
   - The antifouling properties of the PS/AG membranes are tested using a collection of Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria: S. aureus, E. coli, K. pneumoniae, and P. aeruginosa.

2. **Bacterial Suspension Preparation**:
   - Bacterial suspensions are prepared from 18-hour nutrient broth cultures, and the cell density is measured using a spectrophotometer.
   - The membranes are disinfected with 70% ethanol, rinsed with sterilized water, and dried in a laminar flow hood.
   - The membranes are then immersed in the bacterial suspensions for 10 minutes and placed on nutrient agar plates for 24 hours of incubation at 37°C.

3. **SEM Analysis**:
   - The bacterial cells on the membrane surfaces are fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and washed with PBS.
   - The samples are dried and sputter-coated with gold for SEM imaging.
   - The SEM images show a significant reduction in bacterial colonization on the PS/AG membranes, particularly those with 3% and 7% AG loadings.

4. **Colony-Forming Unit (CFU) Counts**:
   - The number of CFUs on the membrane surfaces is counted to quantify the extent of bacterial colonization.
   - The results confirm the SEM observations, showing a marked decrease in bacterial colonization on the PS/AG membranes compared to the control and commercial membranes.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for fabricating PS/AG membranes with enhanced antifouling properties. The incorporation of AG into the PS matrix significantly improves the hydrophilicity, porosity, and negative surface charge of the membranes, leading to a substantial reduction in bacterial colonization. The PS/AG membranes with 3% and 7% AG loadings exhibit the most significant antifouling performance, making them suitable for various water treatment applications where biofouling is a critical concern. The invention offers a promising solution to the persistent problem of biofouling in membrane-based water treatment systems.
