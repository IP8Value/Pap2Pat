# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of pharmacology and more specifically to the identification and use of macrocyclic inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). IDE is a zinc metalloprotease that plays a crucial role in the degradation of various bioactive peptides, including insulin, glucagon, and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP). The regulation of these peptides is essential for maintaining metabolic homeostasis, and dysregulation can lead to a variety of metabolic disorders, including diabetes and obesity.

Current therapeutic strategies for treating metabolic disorders often focus on modulating the activity of key enzymes and receptors involved in metabolic pathways. However, the development of specific and potent inhibitors for IDE has been challenging due to its complex structure and the difficulty in identifying small molecules that can effectively bind and inhibit the enzyme. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel macrocyclic compounds that selectively inhibit IDE, thereby offering new therapeutic options for the treatment of metabolic disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides macrocyclic inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) and methods for their use. These inhibitors are designed to selectively bind to IDE and inhibit its enzymatic activity, thereby modulating the levels of bioactive peptides such as insulin, glucagon, and CGRP. The invention encompasses the identification of IDE-binding compounds, methods for detecting IDE, labeled IDE inhibitor probes, clinical applications of the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, and kits for their use.

The macrocyclic inhibitors of the present invention are characterized by their high affinity for IDE and their ability to inhibit the enzyme's activity in vitro and in vivo. These inhibitors have been shown to improve glucose tolerance, enhance insulin sensitivity, and reduce the degradation of bioactive peptides, making them valuable tools for the treatment of metabolic disorders.

## DEFINITIONS

### Chemical Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the term "macrocyclic compound" refers to a cyclic molecule containing a large ring structure, typically with a molecular weight greater than 500 Da. The macrocyclic compounds of the present invention are designed to bind to IDE and inhibit its enzymatic activity.

The term "insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE)" refers to a zinc metalloprotease that degrades various bioactive peptides, including insulin, glucagon, and CGRP. IDE is a key regulator of metabolic homeostasis and is a target for the treatment of metabolic disorders.

The term "inhibitor" refers to a molecule that reduces or prevents the activity of an enzyme, such as IDE. The inhibitors of the present invention are macrocyclic compounds that selectively bind to IDE and inhibit its enzymatic activity.

The term "fluorogenic peptide" refers to a peptide that contains a fluorophore and a quencher, such that the peptide emits fluorescence upon cleavage by an enzyme. Fluorogenic peptides are commonly used to assay the activity of proteases, including IDE.

### Other Definitions

The term "in vitro" refers to processes or reactions that occur in a controlled laboratory environment, outside of a living organism. In vitro assays are used to evaluate the activity of IDE inhibitors in solution.

The term "in vivo" refers to processes or reactions that occur within a living organism. In vivo studies are used to evaluate the efficacy and safety of IDE inhibitors in animal models.

The term "therapeutically effective amount" refers to the amount of a compound that is sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic effect, such as improving glucose tolerance or enhancing insulin sensitivity.

The term "pharmaceutical composition" refers to a formulation containing one or more active ingredients, such as IDE inhibitors, along with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, carriers, or diluents. Pharmaceutical compositions are used to administer the active ingredients to a subject in need thereof.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

### Methods for Identifying IDE-Binding Compounds

The present invention provides methods for identifying compounds that bind to and inhibit IDE. These methods involve the use of a DNA-templated library of macrocyclic compounds, which are screened for their ability to bind to IDE and inhibit its enzymatic activity.

In one embodiment, the method involves the following steps:
1. Synthesis of a DNA-templated library of macrocyclic compounds. The library is designed to contain a diverse set of macrocyclic structures, each linked to a unique DNA barcode that encodes the identity of the compound.
2. Immobilization of IDE on a solid support, such as cobalt magnetic beads. The immobilized IDE is then incubated with the DNA-templated library under conditions that allow the compounds to bind to IDE.
3. Washing of the solid support to remove unbound compounds. The bound compounds are then eluted and their DNA barcodes are sequenced to identify the compounds that bind to IDE.
4. Synthesis and purification of the identified IDE-binding compounds. The compounds are then tested for their ability to inhibit IDE using various in vitro assays, such as fluorogenic peptide cleavage assays, time-resolved fluorescence (HTRF) assays, and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) assays.

The identified IDE-binding compounds are further characterized to determine their potency, selectivity, and mechanism of action. For example, the compounds may be tested for their ability to inhibit the cleavage of insulin, glucagon, and CGRP by IDE. Additionally, the compounds may be evaluated for their binding affinity to IDE using techniques such as surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC).

### Methods for Detecting IDE

The present invention also provides methods for detecting the presence and activity of IDE in biological samples. These methods are useful for diagnosing and monitoring metabolic disorders, as well as for evaluating the efficacy of IDE inhibitors in preclinical and clinical settings.

In one embodiment, the method involves the use of a fluorogenic peptide substrate, such as Mca-RPPGFSAFK(Dnp)-OH, which is cleaved by IDE to produce a fluorescent signal. The method includes the following steps:
1. Incubation of a biological sample, such as plasma or tissue homogenate, with the fluorogenic peptide substrate in the presence or absence of an IDE inhibitor.
2. Measurement of the fluorescent signal using a fluorometer or other suitable detection device. The intensity of the fluorescent signal is proportional to the activity of IDE in the sample.
3. Comparison of the fluorescent signal in the presence and absence of the IDE inhibitor to determine the level of IDE activity in the sample.

In another embodiment, the method involves the use of an anti-insulin antibody time-resolved fluorescence (HTRF) assay. This assay measures the degradation of insulin by IDE and can be used to quantify the activity of IDE in a sample. The method includes the following steps:
1. Incubation of a biological sample with insulin and an anti-insulin antibody labeled with a donor fluorophore and an acceptor fluorophore.
2. Measurement of the time-resolved fluorescence signal using a HTRF reader. The intensity of the fluorescence signal is proportional to the amount of intact insulin in the sample.
3. Comparison of the fluorescence signal in the presence and absence of the IDE inhibitor to determine the level of IDE activity in the sample.

### Labeled IDE Inhibitor Probes

The present invention further provides labeled IDE inhibitor probes for use in various diagnostic and research applications. These probes are designed to bind to IDE and can be detected using various imaging techniques, such as fluorescence microscopy, positron emission tomography (PET), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

In one embodiment, the labeled IDE inhibitor probe is a macrocyclic compound that is conjugated to a fluorescent dye, such as fluorescein or rhodamine. The probe is synthesized by coupling the macrocyclic inhibitor to the fluorescent dye using standard chemical methods. The labeled probe can be used to visualize the distribution of IDE in tissues and cells, as well as to monitor the binding of the inhibitor to IDE in real-time.

In another embodiment, the labeled IDE inhibitor probe is a macrocyclic compound that is conjugated to a radiolabel, such as 18F or 125I. The probe is synthesized by radiolabeling the macrocyclic inhibitor using standard radiolabeling techniques. The labeled probe can be used for PET imaging to visualize the distribution of IDE in the body and to monitor the pharmacokinetics of the inhibitor in vivo.

### Clinical Applications of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors

The macrocyclic IDE inhibitors of the present invention have a wide range of clinical applications, particularly in the treatment of metabolic disorders such as diabetes and obesity. These inhibitors can be used to modulate the levels of bioactive peptides, such as insulin and glucagon, thereby improving glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity.

In one embodiment, the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors are administered to subjects with type 2 diabetes to improve glucose tolerance and reduce hyperglycemia. The inhibitors can be administered orally, intravenously, or by other suitable routes. The therapeutically effective amount of the inhibitor is determined based on the severity of the condition and the response of the subject to the treatment.

In another embodiment, the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors are used to treat obesity by reducing the degradation of appetite-regulating peptides, such as CGRP. The inhibitors can be administered in combination with other anti-obesity agents, such as appetite suppressants and metabolic enhancers, to achieve a synergistic effect.

In yet another embodiment, the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors are used to treat neurodegenerative diseases, such as Alzheimer's disease, by reducing the degradation of amyloid-beta peptides. The inhibitors can be administered in combination with other neuroprotective agents to slow the progression of the disease.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Identification of IDE-Binding Macrocyclic Compounds

A DNA-templated library of 13,824 macrocyclic compounds was synthesized and screened for IDE-binding activity. The library was incubated with 20 µg of His6-tagged mouse IDE immobilized on cobalt magnetic beads. After washing to remove unbound compounds, the bound compounds were eluted and their DNA barcodes were sequenced to identify the IDE-binding compounds.

Six macrocyclic compounds (1-6) were identified as IDE binders. These compounds were synthesized and purified by HPLC. The compounds were then tested for their ability to inhibit IDE using a fluorogenic peptide cleavage assay. The results showed that the trans isomers (1b-6b) were more potent inhibitors of IDE compared to the cis isomers (1a-6a).

To further characterize the identified IDE inhibitors, the compounds were tested in additional assays, including an anti-insulin antibody time-resolved fluorescence (HTRF) assay and a LC-MS assay for CGRP cleavage fragments in plasma. The results confirmed that the macrocyclic inhibitors effectively inhibited IDE activity in multiple assays.

### Example 2

#### In Vivo Efficacy of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors

The in vivo efficacy of the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors was evaluated in mouse models of diabetes and obesity. Wild-type lean and diet-induced obese (DIO) C57BL/6J male mice were used in the studies. The mice were fasted overnight for all experiments, except for the insulin tolerance test, which required 5 hours of fasting during the morning.

In one study, lean mice were treated with a single intraperitoneal (i.p.) injection of the macrocyclic IDE inhibitor 6bK (80 mg/kg) or vehicle alone. Blood glucose levels were measured using AccuCheck Aviva meters. The results showed that treatment with 6bK significantly improved glucose tolerance and enhanced the hypoglycemic response to insulin injection.

In another study, DIO mice were treated with 6bK (60 mg/kg i.p.) or vehicle alone. The mice were then subjected to an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT). The results showed that treatment with 6bK improved glucose tolerance and reduced hyperglycemia in the DIO mice.

To further investigate the mechanism of action of the macrocyclic IDE inhibitors, the levels of bioactive peptides, such as insulin and glucagon, were measured in the plasma of treated mice. The results showed that 6bK treatment increased the levels of insulin and reduced the levels of glucagon, indicating that the inhibitor modulates the levels of these peptides by inhibiting IDE.
