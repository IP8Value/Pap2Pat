# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for producing graphene from petroleum coke using an electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of needle coke as a feedstock for the production of high-quality graphene nanosheets, which can be utilized in various applications such as batteries, supercapacitors, structural materials, transparent electronics, and flexible wearable devices.

## BACKGROUND

The petrochemical industry faces significant challenges in managing its product streams sustainably. By-products of oil refining, such as petroleum coke, pose environmental concerns due to their high carbon dioxide emissions when used as fuel. Additionally, the use of petroleum coke in the steel and aluminum industries as an anode for smelting also contributes to greenhouse gas emissions. These issues highlight the global need to repurpose existing petroleum streams toward sustainable end-uses.

Graphene, a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice, has gained considerable attention due to its exceptional mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties. It is widely used in various applications, including batteries, supercapacitors, structural materials, transparent electronics, and flexible wearable devices. The primary sources of graphene are natural graphite and synthetic methods, but these sources have limitations. Natural graphite is a finite resource, and only a small fraction of it is suitable for graphene production. Synthetic methods often require complex and expensive processes.

Petroleum coke, particularly needle coke, offers a promising alternative feedstock for graphene production. Needle coke is highly crystalline and has a long needle-like structure, making it an ideal precursor for graphene. The economic benefits of converting needle coke into graphene are significant, as the value of graphene is orders of magnitude higher than that of needle coke. Furthermore, the use of petroleum coke for graphene production can help divert this by-product from high-emission end-uses, contributing to more sustainable industrial practices.

Despite the potential, the production of graphene from petroleum coke has not been extensively explored. Previous attempts have focused on the production of graphene oxide (GO) and have yielded limited success. The present invention addresses these gaps by providing a method for the high-yield and scalable production of high-quality graphene nanosheets from needle coke using an electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process in a compressed expandable chamber.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for producing graphene from petroleum coke, specifically needle coke, using an electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Pre-treatment**: Washing the needle coke with dichloromethane and deionized water to remove impurities, followed by treating the washed material with nitric acid to enhance its hydrophilicity.
2. **Pre-reaction Separation**: Centrifuging the pre-treated material to remove smaller particles, leaving behind a more suitable feedstock for the ECE process.
3. **Electrochemical Exfoliation**: Compacting the separated material in a dialysis bag and applying a voltage across the material to exfoliate it into graphene-like sheets.
4. **Post-reaction Centrifugation**: Separating the exfoliated material from unreacted parent material and unwanted small particles through a two-step centrifugation process.
5. **Annealing**: Optionally, annealing the separated graphene-like sheets to improve their electrical conductivity and structural integrity.

The method of the present invention enables the production of high-quality graphene nanosheets from needle coke, which can be used in various applications, including batteries, supercapacitors, structural materials, transparent electronics, and flexible wearable devices. The invention also provides a sustainable and economically viable approach to repurposing petroleum coke, thereby reducing its environmental impact.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to a method for producing graphene from petroleum coke, specifically needle coke, using an electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. The method involves several steps to ensure the high yield and quality of the graphene nanosheets produced.

### Pre-treatment

The first step in the method is the pre-treatment of the needle coke. The needle coke is washed with dichloromethane and deionized water to remove any impurities that may interfere with the ECE process. The washed material is then treated with 6 M nitric acid to enhance its hydrophilicity, allowing for better exposure to the electrolyte solution during the ECE process. The mixture of nitric acid and needle coke is placed in a stainless-steel autoclave container and heated at 120°C for 4 hours. After the treatment, the material is washed with deionized water to neutralize the acid and dried overnight at 100°C on a hotplate.

### Pre-reaction Separation

The pre-treated needle coke is then subjected to a pre-reaction separation process to remove smaller particles that are less suitable for the ECE process. The pre-treated material is mixed with water in a ratio of 5 mg/mL and centrifuged for 10 minutes at 4000 rpm. The supernatant containing the smaller particles is discarded, and the sediment is collected. This separated material, designated as CK-b, is used as the starting material for the ECE process.

### Electrochemical Exfoliation

The next step is the electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. The separated material (CK-b) is compacted in a dialysis bag (length ~16 cm and width ~2.5 cm) and clipped on both ends. A platinum wire is inserted into the material as the working electrode, and copper mesh is wrapped around the dialysis bag as the counter electrode. A known weight (~0.75 kg) is placed on top of the dialysis bag and copper mesh to maintain electrical contact throughout the mixture. The apparatus is submerged in 2 liters of 0.1 M ammonium sulfate solution, and a potential of 12 V is applied across the platinum electrode and copper mesh. The reaction is run for 2 hours. After the reaction, the product is washed with deionized water to remove the electrolyte. The unseparated reaction products are designated as CK-c.

### Post-reaction Centrifugation

The post-reaction separation of the final product is carried out in a two-step centrifugation method. First, the unseparated material (CK-c) is centrifuged for 10 minutes at low speed (2000 rpm). In this step, larger particles are sedimented and discarded, and the supernatant is collected. Second, the supernatant is centrifuged again for 20 minutes at high speed (5000 rpm). In this step, unreacted, non-graphitic smaller particles are removed in the supernatant. The sediment of the second step is collected as the final product, designated as CK-d.

### Annealing

Optionally, the separated graphene-like sheets (CK-d) can be annealed to improve their electrical conductivity and structural integrity. The annealing process is performed in a tube furnace under an argon atmosphere. The sample is heated at a rate of 10°C/min and annealed at various temperatures (500°C, 900°C, and 1100°C) for 12 hours. The annealed samples are designated as CK-d A1, CK-d A2, and CK-d A3, respectively. After cooling, the powder sample is collected and dispersed in water by bath sonication. Buckypaper of the annealed sample is prepared through a vacuum filtration process.

### Characterization Techniques

The quality and properties of the graphene-like sheets produced by the method of the present invention are characterized using various techniques:

- **Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**: Cold emission SEM is used to image the parent coke materials and the final product. The accelerating voltage is set to 15 keV, and a secondary electron detector is used. The coke is conductive enough that no beam charging is observed.
- **Raman Spectroscopy**: Raman spectra are measured using a Horiba Jobin-Yvon LabRam HR with a laser wavelength of 633 nm, laser power of 1.91 mW, exposure time of 10 seconds, and three accumulations. The "Auto" baseline correction method in LabSpec is used for baseline removal.
- **Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA)**: TGA and derivative thermogravimetric (DTG) traces are recorded using a TA instrument. The heating rate is 10°C/min, and the sample size is 3 ± 1 mg under an N2 atmosphere (flow rate 60 mL/min).
- **Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)**: TEM is performed on a JEOL F200 cold emission transmission electron microscope. The coke is ground for imaging and suspended in alcohol. The suspensions are sonicated, and a droplet of the solution is deposited on a TEM grid with a lacey carbon support film. Imaging is done at room temperature using a 200 keV acceleration voltage.
- **Energy Loss Spectroscopy (ELS)**: ELS is performed with a JEOL F200 cold emission transmission electron microscope operated in scanning transmission electron microscopy mode. An annular dark field detector is used to search the sample and identify the appropriate spot for data collection. The spectrum image is recorded from a uniformly flat part of the particle.
- **X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)**: XPS is used to probe the surface chemistry of the final product. An Omicron X-ray photoelectron spectrometer employing an Mg-sourced X-ray beam is used to irradiate the sample surface. The emitted photoelectrons are collected by a 180° hemispherical electron energy analyzer. Charge neutralization by a dual-beam charge neutralizer is performed to eliminate binding energy shifts in the recorded spectra.
- **Electrical Conductivity Measurement**: Electrical conductivity is measured on buckypapers produced by vacuum filtration using a four-point resistivity probe powered by Keithley 2000, 6221, and two 6514.

### Yield Calculation

The yield of the coke-derived graphene (CK-d) is measured using UV-vis absorbance spectroscopy. The absorbance measurement at 666 nm is used to calculate the concentration of graphene in the product dispersion. An extinction coefficient of 1293 L/g/m is used for the calculation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Preparation of Needle Coke Fines (CK-1)**

Needle coke fines (CK-1) were washed with dichloromethane and deionized water to remove impurities. The washed material was then treated with 6 M nitric acid by mixing it with the material, using 1 mL of acid per gram of material. The mixture was kept in a stainless-steel autoclave container and placed inside an oven at 120°C for 4 hours. The pre-treated material was washed with deionized water to neutralize the acid and dried overnight at 100°C on a hotplate. The pre-treated material was designated as CK-1a.

**Pre-reaction Separation**

The pre-treated material (CK-1a) was mixed with water in a ratio of 5 mg/mL and centrifuged for 10 minutes at 4000 rpm. The supernatant containing the smaller particles was discarded, and the sediment was collected. The separated material was designated as CK-1b.

**Electrochemical Exfoliation**

The separated material (CK-1b) was compacted in a dialysis bag and clipped on both ends. A platinum wire was inserted into the material as the working electrode, and copper mesh was wrapped around the dialysis bag as the counter electrode. A known weight (~0.75 kg) was placed on top of the dialysis bag and copper mesh. The apparatus was submerged in 2 liters of 0.1 M ammonium sulfate solution, and a potential of 12 V was applied across the platinum electrode and copper mesh. The reaction was run for 2 hours. After the reaction, the product was washed with deionized water to remove the electrolyte. The unseparated reaction products were designated as CK-1c.

**Post-reaction Centrifugation**

The unseparated material (CK-1c) was centrifuged for 10 minutes at low speed (2000 rpm). The larger particles were sedimented and discarded, and the supernatant was collected. The supernatant was centrifuged again for 20 minutes at high speed (5000 rpm). The unreacted, non-graphitic smaller particles were removed in the supernatant, and the sediment was collected as the final product, designated as CK-1d.

**Characterization**

The final product (CK-1d) was characterized using SEM, Raman spectroscopy, TGA, TEM, ELS, XPS, and electrical conductivity measurements. The results confirmed the formation of high-quality graphene nanosheets with a lateral dimension in the 1-3 µm range and a hexagonally packed crystalline lattice. The electrical conductivity of CK-1d was measured to be ~56.9 S/m, which increased to 250 S/m after annealing at 500°C for 12 hours.

### Example 2

**Preparation of Needle Coke Large Particles (CK-2)**

Needle coke large particles (CK-2) were washed and pre-treated as described in Example 1. The pre-treated material was designated as CK-2a.

**Pre-reaction Separation**

The pre-treated material (CK-2a) was mixed with water in a ratio of 5 mg/mL and centrifuged for 10 minutes at 4000 rpm. The supernatant containing the smaller particles was discarded, and the sediment was collected. The separated material was designated as CK-2b.

**Electrochemical Exfoliation**

The separated material (CK-2b) was compacted in a dialysis bag and subjected to the ECE process as described in Example 1. The unseparated reaction products were designated as CK-2c.

**Post-reaction Centrifugation**

The unseparated material (CK-2c) was centrifuged for 10 minutes at low speed (2000 rpm) and 20 minutes at high speed (5000 rpm). The sediment was collected as the final product, designated as CK-2d.

**Characterization**

The final product (CK-2d) was characterized using SEM, Raman spectroscopy, TGA, TEM, ELS, XPS, and electrical conductivity measurements. The results showed that the ECE process did not produce significant amounts of well-defined, graphene-like sheets. The final product contained mostly large plate-like and needle-like structures, indicating that the ECE process was not successful in exfoliating CK-2.

### Conclusion

The method of the present invention successfully produces high-quality graphene nanosheets from needle coke fines (CK-1) using an electrochemical exfoliation (ECE) process. The pre-treatment, pre-reaction separation, ECE, and post-reaction centrifugation steps are critical for achieving the desired graphene product. The method is not as effective for larger and medium-sized needle coke particles (CK-2 and CK-3), highlighting the importance of the feedstock's morphology and structure. The final graphene product can be further improved through annealing, making it suitable for various high-value applications.
