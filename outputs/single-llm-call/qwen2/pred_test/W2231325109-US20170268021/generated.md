# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cell therapy, specifically to methods for converting somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) using a combination of transcription factors. The invention further encompasses the use of these iDPs for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases, particularly Parkinson's disease (PD).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) of the midbrain. The depletion of these neurons leads to a reduction in dopamine levels, resulting in motor symptoms such as tremors, rigidity, bradykinesia, and postural instability. Current treatments for PD, including pharmacological and surgical interventions, aim to alleviate symptoms but do not address the underlying neuronal loss.

Stem cell-based therapies offer a promising approach to replace the lost dopaminergic neurons and restore normal function. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and embryonic stem cells (ESCs) have been extensively studied for their potential to differentiate into dopaminergic neurons. However, these approaches face challenges such as ethical concerns, tumorigenic risk, and the complexity of differentiation protocols.

Direct lineage conversion, or transdifferentiation, involves the reprogramming of somatic cells directly into a desired cell type without passing through a pluripotent state. This method has the potential to overcome some of the limitations associated with iPSCs and ESCs. Recent studies have demonstrated the successful conversion of fibroblasts into induced neural progenitors (iNPCs) using various combinations of transcription factors. However, the efficiency and specificity of dopaminergic neuron differentiation from these iNPCs remain suboptimal.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel method for the direct conversion of somatic cells, specifically fibroblasts, into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) using a combination of transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The resulting iDPs exhibit dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential and can be used for cell therapy in PD.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for converting somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) by ectopically expressing the transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The iDPs generated by this method possess dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential and can be used for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases, particularly Parkinson's disease (PD).

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for generating iDPs, comprising the steps of:
1. Isolating somatic cells, such as fibroblasts, from a subject.
2. Introducing nucleic acids encoding the transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 into the somatic cells.
3. Culturing the cells under conditions suitable for the expression of the transcription factors and the formation of iDPs.

In another aspect, the invention provides the iDPs generated by the method described above. These iDPs express specific dopaminergic progenitor markers and possess dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating Parkinson's disease (PD) comprising the step of administering the iDPs to a subject in need thereof. The iDPs can be administered through intracranial injection into the striatum of the brain.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the iDPs and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for use in the treatment of PD.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Somatic Cells**: Any cell of a multicellular organism other than a gamete, germ cell, or undifferentiated stem cell.
- **Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)**: The process of converting one cell type directly into another cell type without passing through a pluripotent state.
- **Induced Dopaminergic Progenitors (iDPs)**: Somatic cells that have been reprogrammed to express dopaminergic progenitor markers and possess dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential.
- **Transcription Factors**: Proteins that bind to specific DNA sequences and control the rate of transcription of genetic information from DNA to messenger RNA (mRNA).

### Somatic Cells

Somatic cells, such as fibroblasts, are widely available and can be easily isolated from various tissues, including skin. Fibroblasts are connective tissue cells that play a crucial role in wound healing and the production of collagen. In the context of the present invention, fibroblasts are used as the starting material for the generation of iDPs.

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)

The reprogramming of somatic cells into iDPs involves the introduction of specific transcription factors that can induce the expression of neural progenitor markers and confer dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential. The transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 have been identified as key factors in this process.

- **Brn2**: A member of the POU family of transcription factors, Brn2 is involved in the regulation of neural differentiation and the maintenance of neural stem cell properties.
- **Sox2**: A member of the SRY-box transcription factor family, Sox2 is essential for the maintenance of pluripotency and the induction of neural fate.
- **Foxa2**: A member of the forkhead box (FOX) family of transcription factors, Foxa2 is critical for the specification of midbrain dopaminergic neurons and the regulation of their development.

### iDP Production

The production of iDPs involves the following steps:

1. **Isolation of Somatic Cells**: Fibroblasts are isolated from a subject, typically from a skin biopsy. The cells are cultured in a suitable medium, such as Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS), non-essential amino acids, penicillin, and streptomycin.

2. **Introduction of Transcription Factors**: Nucleic acids encoding the transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 are introduced into the fibroblasts using viral vectors, such as retroviral or lentiviral vectors. The vectors are designed to express the transcription factors under the control of appropriate promoters.

3. **Culture Conditions**: The cells are cultured under conditions that promote the expression of the transcription factors and the formation of iDPs. The culture medium is typically NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult® NSC Proliferation Supplements, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and epidermal growth factor (EGF).

4. **Selection and Expansion**: The iDPs are selected based on the expression of specific neural progenitor markers, such as Nestin, Sox1, and Pax6. The cells are expanded in culture to obtain sufficient numbers for further characterization and application.

### Human iDP Upon Ectopic Expression of Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2

The ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 in fibroblasts results in the formation of iDPs that exhibit dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential. The iDPs express high levels of dopaminergic progenitor markers, including Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Msx1, Ngn2, Otx2, Mash1, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1. These markers are indicative of the midbrain identity and dopaminergic lineage of the iDPs.

The iDPs can be further differentiated into mature dopaminergic neurons using a neuronal differentiation protocol. The differentiated neurons express mature dopaminergic neuron markers, such as tyrosine hydroxylase (TH), aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC), dopamine transporter (DAT), and vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2). The neurons also exhibit functional membrane properties, including the ability to generate action potentials and express voltage-gated ion channels.

## DISCUSSION

The present invention provides a novel method for the direct conversion of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic progenitors (iDPs) using a combination of transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The iDPs generated by this method possess dopaminergic neuronal-restricted differentiation potential and can be used for the treatment of neurodegenerative diseases, particularly Parkinson's disease (PD).

The use of iDPs for cell therapy offers several advantages over traditional approaches. First, the direct conversion of somatic cells into iDPs bypasses the pluripotent state, reducing the risk of tumorigenesis. Second, the iDPs can be generated from autologous cells, minimizing the risk of immune rejection. Third, the iDPs can be efficiently expanded in culture, providing a scalable source of cells for transplantation.

The iDPs have been shown to survive and differentiate into functional dopaminergic neurons in vivo when transplanted into the striatum of a mouse model of PD. The transplantation of iDPs led to the partial recovery of motor function, as measured by the rotarod test. These results suggest that iDPs can serve as a safe and effective cell source for the treatment of PD.

## Methods

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming

Mouse skin fibroblasts were isolated from adult Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice aged 5.5–7.0 weeks. The fibroblasts were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1× Non-Essential Amino Acids, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 100 μg/ml streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified atmosphere. Fibroblasts were used within passages 2–5 to avoid replicative senescence.

Mouse Brn2 open reading frame (ORF) (EcoRI + NotI) and Foxa2 ORF (BamHI + XhoI) were cloned into pMXs-retroviral vectors. pMXs-Sox2 was purchased from Addgene (Plasmid #13367). Retroviruses (pMXs) were generated with Plat-E packaging cells. Plat-E cells were seeded at 3.6 × 10^6 cells per 100-mm dish. Twenty-four hours after seeding, 15 μg DNA of pMXs-based retroviral vectors encoding Sox2, Brn2, and Foxa2 were introduced into Plat-E cells using 15 μl of Lipofectamine™ LTX transfection reagent. The medium was replaced with 5 ml of DMEM containing 5% FBS 24 hours after transfection.

Fibroblasts from Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice were seeded at 2 × 10^5 cells per 35-mm dish. At 48 hours and 72 hours post-transfection, virus-containing supernatants from these Plat-E cultures were recovered and filtered through a 0.45-μm cellulose acetate filter. Equal volumes of the supernatants were mixed and supplemented with 10 μg/ml polybrene. Cells were incubated in virus/polybrene-containing supernatants overnight. The medium was changed 3 days after infection to NeuroCult® NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult® NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 ng/ml basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and 20 ng/ml epidermal growth factor (EGF). After 9–14 days, the predicted iDP colonies were monitored by fluorescence microscopy.

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR

Total mRNA was isolated using TRIzol Reagent and RNeasy Mini Kit. The reverse transcription was performed using Transcription 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. RT-PCR analyses for the detection of neural stem cell-specific mRNAs were performed using SYBR® Select Master Mix with 0.5 μl of cDNA, corresponding to 1 μg of total RNA in a 15 μl final volume, 1.5 μl H2O, 7.5 μl SYBR Green, and 5.5 μl oligonucleotide primer pairs. PCR program: 1. 50°C for 2 min, 2. 95°C for 2 min; 3. 95°C for 15 sec, 4. specific annealing temperature for 15 sec, 5. 72°C for 1 min. Steps 2–4 were repeated 40 times. All samples were amplified in triplicate, and the mean was used for further analysis.

### Immunocytochemistry

The cultured cells were fixed in 4% formaldehyde for 20 min at room temperature and then washed with PBS three times. The fixed cells were permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 min and blocked with 2% BSA in PBS for 1 h at room temperature. Cells were incubated with primary antibodies overnight and then washed with PBS three times and incubated for 2 h at room temperature with secondary antibodies. Fluorescent images were obtained using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope.

### Differentiation

For astrocyte differentiation, the iDP culture medium was replaced by DMEM/F-12 with 10% FBS and cultured for 7 days, with the medium changed every other day. For oligodendrocyte differentiation, iDPs were cultivated in DMEM/F12 with 1× N2, 10 ng/ml PDGF, 10 ng/ml FGF-2, and 10 μM forskolin for 4 days. Afterwards, PDGF and forskolin were replaced by 30 ng/ml 3, 3, 5-triiodothyronine (T3) hormone and 200 mM ascorbic acid for another 7 days. For neuronal differentiation, SHH/FGF8 independent protocol: iDPs were plated on poly-L-Ornithine/laminin-coated coverslips in 24-well plates with DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1× N2, 1× B27, 1.0 mM Glutamax, 0.11 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1.0 mM dibutyrylcAMP, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, 10 ng/mL brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and 10 ng/mL glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) for 4 weeks. The medium was changed every 3-4 days. SHH/FGF8 dependent protocol: Cells were plated on poly-L-Ornithine/laminin-coated coverslips in 24-well plates with DMEM/F12 containing 1× N2, 10 ng/ml bFGF, 100 ng/ml SHH, and 100 ng/ml FGF8 for 6 days, and then switched to DMEM/F12 containing 1× N2, 1× B27, 1.0 mM Glutamax, 0.11 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1.0 mM dibutyrylcAMP, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, 10 ng/mL BDNF, and 10 ng/mL GDNF for another 4 weeks, with the medium changed every 3-4 days.

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents

Action potentials and total currents were recorded using the whole-cell patch-clamp technique with an Axonpatch 200B patch-clamp amplifier. pClamp 10.2 program was used for data acquisition. The patch-pipette solution contained (in mM): 105 K-aspartate, 20 KCl, 1 CaCl2, 5 MgATP, 10 HEPES, 10 EGTA, and 25 Glucose (pH 7.2; 320 mOsm/L). The bath solution consisted of (in mM): 140 NaCl, 5.4 KCl, 0.5 MgCl2, 2.5 CaCl2, 5.5 HEPES, 11 Glucose, and 10 Sucrose (pH 7.4; 330 mOsm/L). The same pipette and bath solutions were used for action potentials and total currents measurement. Resistance of the patch pipette was 3–5 MΩ. Series resistance of 6–13 MΩ was electronically compensated 80–90%. Action potentials were elicited by a series of 1-s depolarizing currents injection from 20 pA to 100 pA with 20-pA increments. Total currents were evoked from a holding potential of −80 mV by stepping to voltages between −70 and +70 mV in 10 mV increments for 500 ms. Recorded traces were sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz. All experiments were done at room temperature.

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests

Rotarod test was used to evaluate the recovery of MPTP-induced motor deficits by the engrafted iDPs. The apparatus was fitted with a 7-cm diameter rod and interfaced with automatic timing instrumentation. Mice were habituated and trained to perform on the accelerating rotarod at 2–12 rpm for 5 min for four daily sessions in three consecutive days prior to the administration of MPTP. Twice before MPTP-intoxication, all mice were evaluated at 10 rpm for a maximum of 90 s per trial to obtain a pre-treatment baseline performance. After iDPs engraftment, mice were re-evaluated on the rotarod twice a week for 3 weeks. Weekly post-treatment rotarod performance was calculated as the average of two trials and as a ratio relative to the animal group’s baseline performance. Scores from MPTP mice with or without iDPs engraftment were normalized to the mean performance of the PBS control group.

### Statistical Analysis

Data were expressed as means ± SD and statistically evaluated by analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Tukey’s test for paired observations unless specified. Significance was considered as a p value of < 0.05. All assays were performed at least twice, with triplicate samples in each experiment.

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology

L-Myc was cloned into the Tet-All retroviral vector, which contains both reverse tetracycline-controlled transactivator (rtTA) and tetracycline response element (TRE) promoter. The retrovirus encoding L-Myc was packaged in Plat-E cells, and iDPs were infected with the virus and screened with neomycin (neo).

For iDPs engraftment, at 7 days after MPTP intoxication, mice were anesthetized with Ketamine (120 mg/kg) and xylazine (16 mg/kg) by i.p, placed in a stereotaxic apparatus for intracranial injection. A linear skin incision was made over the bregma, and two 1-mm burr holes were drilled in the skull 0.2 mm posterior and 3.5 mm lateral to the bregma using a hand-held driller. iDPs (0.25 million per injection) were labeled with GFP-expressing retrovirus and injected into the striatum of both hemispheres. Saline was used as a control for iDPs injection. Coordinates for inoculation were set as: 0.22 mm posterior to bregma, 3.25 mm lateral from the Sagittal midline, and a depth of 2.9 mm in vertical line. A Hamilton 10-μl syringe was used for cell injection. Mice were sacrificed 21 days post-injection after intracardiac perfusion, and brains were quickly removed. Immunohistochemistry for TH+ nerve terminals and quantification were performed in a blinded fashion.
