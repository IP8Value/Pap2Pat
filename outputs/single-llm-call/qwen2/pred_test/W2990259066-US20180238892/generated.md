# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The field of the invention pertains to the identification and modulation of the BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) pathway in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) to enhance the sensitivity of TNBC cells to nonspecific chemotherapeutic agents. Specifically, the invention involves methods and compositions for assessing the expression of the BAD pathway and modulating BAD phosphorylation to improve the efficacy of chemotherapy in TNBC.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a particularly aggressive subtype of breast cancer characterized by the lack of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression. TNBC disproportionately affects premenopausal women and is more prevalent among women of African and Hispanic descent. Despite initial sensitivity to standard chemotherapy, TNBC patients often experience rapid recurrence and poor survival rates. The lack of targeted therapies for TNBC highlights the critical need for new therapeutic strategies.

The BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) is a member of the BCL2 family of proteins, which regulate apoptosis. BAD function is controlled by phosphorylation, which determines its ability to interact with pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins. Phosphorylation of BAD by kinases such as protein kinase B (PKB/AKT) and protein kinase A (PKA) inhibits its pro-apoptotic activity, while dephosphorylation by phosphatases such as PP1, PP2A, and PPM1 promotes apoptosis.

Recent studies have shown that the expression and phosphorylation status of BAD are associated with chemoresistance in various cancers, including TNBC. There is a need for methods to identify and modulate the BAD pathway to enhance the sensitivity of TNBC cells to chemotherapy, thereby improving patient outcomes.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for assessing the expression of the BAD pathway and modulating BAD phosphorylation to enhance the sensitivity of TNBC cells to nonspecific chemotherapeutic agents. The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Assessment of BAD Pathway Expression**: Methods for determining the expression of the BAD pathway in TNBC samples using principal component analysis (PCA) to generate a BAD pathway gene expression signature (BPGES).
2. **Modulation of BAD Phosphorylation**: Methods for modulating BAD phosphorylation using inhibitors of BAD protein kinases, such as AKT and PKA inhibitors, to enhance the sensitivity of TNBC cells to chemotherapy.
3. **Therapeutic Compositions**: Compositions comprising BAD pathway inhibitors and chemotherapeutic agents for the treatment of TNBC.
4. **Diagnostic Kits**: Diagnostic kits for assessing the expression of the BAD pathway and the phosphorylation status of BAD protein in TNBC samples.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definitions

- **BAD Pathway**: A biological pathway involving the BCL2 antagonist of cell death (BAD) protein and its regulatory kinases and phosphatases.
- **BPGES (BAD Pathway Gene Expression Signature)**: A gene expression signature derived from a subset of genes that comprise the BAD pathway, used to assess the expression of the BAD pathway in cancer samples.
- **Phosphorylation**: The addition of a phosphate group to a protein, which can alter its activity, localization, or interactions with other proteins.
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: A statistical method used to reduce the dimensionality of large datasets while retaining trends and patterns.
- **TNBC (Triple-Negative Breast Cancer)**: A subtype of breast cancer that lacks expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2).

### Methods

#### Assessment of BAD Pathway Expression

1. **Sample Collection**: Obtain breast cancer tissue samples or cell lines from patients or cell repositories.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Extract total RNA from the samples using standard methods, such as the Qiagen RNeasy Mini kit.
3. **Microarray Expression Analysis**: Perform microarray analysis using Affymetrix gene chips to measure the expression levels of genes involved in the BAD pathway.
4. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: Use PCA to derive a BAD pathway gene expression signature (BPGES) score from the expression data of 16 key genes in the BAD pathway, including BAX, BCL-2, EGFR, PDK1, PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PPP1CA, PPP2CA, PPP3CA, PPM1A, YWHAB, YWHAE, YWHAG, YWHAH, YWHAQ, and YWHAZ.
5. **Statistical Analysis**: Compare the BPGES scores between TNBC and non-TNBC samples using t-tests and log-rank tests to assess the association with triple-negative status and overall survival.

#### Modulation of BAD Phosphorylation

1. **Cell Culture**: Cultivate TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines in appropriate media supplemented with fetal bovine serum and other necessary components.
2. **Kinase Inhibitors**: Treat the cells with inhibitors of BAD protein kinases, such as the AKT inhibitor perifosine and the PKA inhibitor H89 dihydrochloride hydrate.
3. **Western Blot Analysis**: Assess the expression and phosphorylation status of BAD protein and its regulatory kinases using Western blotting techniques.
4. **Cell Viability Assays**: Evaluate the sensitivity of the cells to chemotherapeutic agents, such as cisplatin, using MTS cell proliferation assays.
5. **siRNA Transfection**: Deplete BAD protein in TNBC cells using siRNA transfection to determine the role of BAD in the sensitization effects of kinase inhibitors.

### BAD Pathway Expression

The BAD pathway gene expression signature (BPGES) was derived using principal component analysis (PCA) of a subset of 16 genes known to influence the phosphorylation status and apoptotic function of BAD protein. The BPGES was capable of distinguishing TNBC from non-TNBC samples in multiple datasets, including the Total Cancer Care (TCC) dataset (n = 106) and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset (n = 408). The BPGES was also associated with decreased overall survival in both datasets, indicating its potential as a prognostic biomarker.

### Analysis of Genomic and Chemo-Sensitivity Data for NCI60 Cancer Cell Lines

The BPGES was evaluated in the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) dataset, which includes 42 breast cancer cell lines (28 TNBC, 14 non-TNBC). The BPGES showed differential expression between TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines, with TNBC cell lines expressing higher levels of the BPGES. This suggests that the BAD pathway is activated in TNBC cell lines, which may contribute to their resistance to chemotherapy.

### Modulating BAD Phosphorylation by siRNA Transfection

TNBC cell lines were transfected with siRNA to deplete BAD protein and assess the impact on cell sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents. Depletion of BAD protein did not change the sensitivity of TNBC cells to cisplatin but reduced the sensitivity to AKT and PKA inhibitors, suggesting that BAD protein is required for the sensitization effects of these inhibitors.

### MTS Cell Proliferation Assays

MTS cell proliferation assays were used to evaluate the sensitivity of TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines to cisplatin in the presence and absence of BAD protein kinase inhibitors. Most TNBC cells treated with perifosine or H89 were more sensitive to cisplatin than cells treated with cisplatin alone. In contrast, the sensitivity of non-TNBC cells to cisplatin did not significantly change or was lessened in the presence of perifosine and H89.

### Western Blot Analysis

Western blot analysis was performed to assess the expression and phosphorylation status of BAD protein and its regulatory kinases in TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines. The results showed that TNBC cell lines expressed higher levels of phosphorylated BAD protein, particularly at the AKT phosphorylation site (Ser136) and the PKA phosphorylation site (Ser155).

### Immunofluorescence Microscopy

Immunofluorescence microscopy was used to visualize the localization of BAD protein and its phosphorylated forms in TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines. The results confirmed the higher expression of phosphorylated BAD protein in TNBC cells, particularly in the cytoplasm, where it is known to interact with anti-apoptotic proteins.

## Results

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with Cancer Development, Progression, Relapse, and Survival

The BAD pathway gene expression signature (BPGES) was associated with the triple-negative phenotype in breast cancer and was predictive of decreased overall survival in both the TCC and TCGA datasets. The BPGES was capable of distinguishing TNBC from non-TNBC samples and was associated with a higher risk of death in patients with breast cancer, particularly those with TNBC.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In-Vitro Sensitivity to a Broad Range of Cytotoxic Drugs

The BPGES was associated with in-vitro sensitivity to a broad range of cytotoxic drugs in TNBC cell lines. TNBC cells expressing higher levels of the BPGES were more resistant to chemotherapy, suggesting that the BAD pathway contributes to chemoresistance in TNBC.

### BAD Pathway Expression is Associated with In Vitro Levels of Phosphorylated BAD Protein

The expression of the BPGES was correlated with the levels of phosphorylated BAD protein in TNBC samples. TNBC cases expressed higher mean levels of phospho-BAD-Ser136, which is phosphorylated by AKT, compared to non-TNBC cases. This suggests that the activation of the BAD pathway, particularly through AKT-mediated phosphorylation, is a mechanism of chemoresistance in TNBC.

### Modulation of BAD Phosphorylation Affects Proliferation of Cancer Cell Lines

Modulation of BAD phosphorylation using inhibitors of BAD protein kinases, such as the AKT inhibitor perifosine and the PKA inhibitor H89, enhanced the sensitivity of TNBC cells to cisplatin. Depletion of BAD protein using siRNA reduced the sensitization effects of these inhibitors, indicating that BAD protein is required for the enhanced cytotoxicity of cisplatin in TNBC cells.

### BAD Phosphorylation Status and Cancer Development

The phosphorylation status of BAD protein, particularly at the AKT and PKA phosphorylation sites, was associated with the development and progression of TNBC. TNBC cases expressing higher levels of phospho-BAD-Ser136 and phospho-BAD-Ser155 were at a higher risk of death, suggesting that the activation of the BAD pathway contributes to the aggressive nature of TNBC.

## Conclusion

The present invention provides methods and compositions for assessing the expression of the BAD pathway and modulating BAD phosphorylation to enhance the sensitivity of TNBC cells to nonspecific chemotherapeutic agents. The BAD pathway gene expression signature (BPGES) is a valuable biomarker for identifying TNBC patients who may benefit from targeted therapies. Modulation of BAD phosphorylation using inhibitors of BAD protein kinases, such as AKT and PKA inhibitors, can enhance the efficacy of chemotherapy in TNBC, potentially leading to improved patient outcomes. The invention also includes diagnostic kits for assessing the expression of the BAD pathway and the phosphorylation status of BAD protein in TNBC samples, which can aid in the development of personalized treatment strategies.
