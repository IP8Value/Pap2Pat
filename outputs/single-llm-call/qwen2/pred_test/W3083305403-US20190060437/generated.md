# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of vaccine technology, specifically to the development of stabilized synthetic carbohydrate-based vaccines. More particularly, the invention pertains to the synthesis and use of stabilized carbaMenA oligomers as antigens in glycoconjugate vaccines for the prevention of meningococcal disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A (MenA).

## BACKGROUND ART

Vaccination is one of the most significant advancements in modern medicine, providing a cost-effective strategy to protect populations from infectious diseases. Traditional vaccines, such as those based on live-attenuated or whole-cell killed microorganisms, have been widely used and are generally effective. However, the increasing prevalence of antimicrobial resistance has necessitated the development of new and more efficient vaccines. Subunit vaccines, which contain only purified microbial components, have gained attention due to their improved safety and efficacy profiles. Among subunit vaccines, carbohydrate-based vaccines have been particularly successful in preventing bacterial infections, including those caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Neisseria meningitidis.

Neisseria meningitidis, a Gram-negative encapsulated bacterium, is a leading cause of bacterial meningitis. Six serotypes (A, C, B, W, Y, and X) are responsible for the majority of reported infections. Serogroup A (MenA) is especially prevalent in developing countries, particularly in the African meningitis belt, affecting over 250 million people at risk. The MenA capsular polysaccharide (MenA CPS) is a critical virulence factor, consisting of (1→6)-linked 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-mannopyranosyl phosphate repeating units. While MenA CPS-based vaccines have shown promise, they suffer from limited clinical efficacy, particularly in infants, young children, and immunocompromised patients. This is due to the T cell-independent immune response they elicit, characterized by the production of low-affinity IgM antibodies and a lack of immunological memory.

To address these limitations, MenA CPS has been conjugated to carrier proteins, such as tetanus toxoid (TT), diphtheria toxoid (DT), or the nontoxic mutant of diphtheria toxin (CRM197). These glycoconjugates exhibit T cell-dependent properties, enabling the induction of memory B cell proliferation and long-lasting protection. Despite these advancements, the development of a fully liquid, shelf-stable glycoconjugate vaccine remains a significant challenge. The intrinsic instability of MenA CPS, particularly its susceptibility to hydrolysis in aqueous environments, complicates the formulation and storage of such vaccines.

Recent efforts have focused on the generation of synthetic carbohydrate vaccines, where chemically synthesized oligosaccharides serve as antigens in conjugate vaccines. Synthetic vaccines offer several advantages, including well-defined size, minimal batch-to-batch variation, and controlled conjugation chemistry. However, the development of synthetic glycomimetics that can effectively mimic the natural polysaccharide and elicit a robust immune response remains a grand challenge.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a stabilized synthetic carbohydrate-based vaccine for the prevention of meningococcal disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A (MenA). The vaccine comprises a glycoconjugate wherein the carbohydrate antigen is a carbaMenA oligomer, specifically a carbaMenA oligomer with a degree of polymerization (DP) of 8, optionally acetylated at the C3 and C4 positions. The carbaMenA oligomer is conjugated to a carrier protein, such as CRM197, to enhance its immunogenicity.

### Particular Embodiments of the Invention

1. **CarbaMenA Oligomers**: The invention encompasses carbaMenA oligomers with a degree of polymerization (DP) ranging from 1 to 8. These oligomers are synthesized using phosphoramidite chemistry, which allows for the precise control of the oligomer length and the introduction of various functional groups, including acetyl esters.

2. **Stabilized CarbaMenA Oligomers**: The carbaMenA oligomers of the invention are stabilized by replacing the ring oxygen of the N-acetyl mannosamines with a methylene group. This modification significantly enhances the stability of the oligomers, particularly in aqueous environments, compared to the natural MenA CPS.

3. **Acetylated CarbaMenA Oligomers**: The carbaMenA DP8 oligomer can be further optimized by introducing acetyl esters at the C3 and C4 positions. The acetylation level is adjusted to match that of the natural MenA CPS, thereby improving the mimicry of the natural polysaccharide and enhancing the immunogenicity of the vaccine.

4. **Glycoconjugate Vaccines**: The carbaMenA oligomers are conjugated to a carrier protein, such as CRM197, to form a glycoconjugate vaccine. The conjugation process involves the use of a di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate linker, which ensures the efficient and stable attachment of the carbohydrate antigen to the carrier protein.

5. **Immunogenicity and Efficacy**: The glycoconjugate vaccines of the invention elicit a strong immune response, characterized by the production of high levels of anti-MenA CPS antibodies with bactericidal activity. The Ac-carbaMenA DP8-CRM197 conjugate, in particular, demonstrates immunogenicity and efficacy comparable to the benchmark vaccine based on the natural MenA CPS.

6. **Stability and Shelf Life**: The carbaMenA oligomers and their glycoconjugate vaccines exhibit superior stability compared to the natural MenA CPS. This stability allows for the formulation of fully liquid, shelf-stable vaccines, which can be stored at room temperature without the need for a cold chain, making them highly attractive for use in resource-limited settings.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Chemical Synthesis of CarbaMenA Oligomers

The carbaMenA oligomers are synthesized using phosphoramidite chemistry, which allows for the precise control of the oligomer length and the introduction of various functional groups. The synthesis starts with the preparation of the building block, which is then used to elongate the oligomer through iterative coupling reactions.

1. **Building Block Preparation**:
   - The carbasugar building block is prepared from 3,4,6-tri-O-acetyl-D-glucal through a series of reactions, including the removal of the primary silyl ether and acetyl ester, the introduction of the dimethoxytrityl (DMTr) group, and the conversion to the phosphoramidite building block.
   - The phosphoramidite building block is used in the elongation cycles to form the desired carbaMenA oligomers.

2. **Elongation and Purification**:
   - The elongation cycles involve the coupling of the phosphoramidite with the growing chain alcohol, oxidation of the intermediate phosphite to the corresponding phosphotriester, and unmasking of the primary hydroxyl on the (n+1) oligomer.
   - The crude products are purified by size-exclusion chromatography or flash chromatography to obtain the target carbaMenA oligomers.

3. **Acetylation**:
   - The carbaMenA DP8 oligomer can be acetylated by treatment with acetic anhydride and imidazole to introduce acetyl esters at the C3 and C4 positions.
   - The acetylation level is adjusted to match that of the natural MenA CPS, ensuring optimal mimicry and immunogenicity.

### Conjugation to Carrier Protein

The carbaMenA oligomers are conjugated to a carrier protein, such as CRM197, using a di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate linker. The conjugation process involves the following steps:

1. **Activation of the Oligomer**:
   - The amino-oligosaccharides are solubilized in a 1:9 H2O:DMSO solution and reacted with a 12-fold molar excess of di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate linker in the presence of triethylamine.
   - The activated oligosaccharides are purified by precipitation with ethyl acetate and washed to remove excess reagents.

2. **Conjugation to CRM197**:
   - The activated oligosaccharides are conjugated to CRM197 in a buffered solution at a specific active ester:protein molar ratio.
   - The conjugates are purified by tangential flow filtration using a 30 kDa cutoff and characterized by micro BCA for total protein content and by MALDI analysis for total saccharide content.

### Immunization Studies

The immunogenicity and efficacy of the carbaMenA-CRM197 glycoconjugate vaccines are evaluated in animal models, such as BALB/c mice. The following steps are involved:

1. **Immunization Protocol**:
   - Groups of mice are immunized with the glycoconjugate vaccines on days 1, 14, and 28.
   - Blood samples are collected on days 0, 27, and 42 to assess the antibody response.

2. **ELISA Analysis**:
   - The antibody response is measured by ELISA, where plates are coated with MenA CPS or de-O-acetylated MenA CPS.
   - Serial dilutions of the sera are added to the plates, and the bound antibodies are detected using alkaline phosphatase-conjugated secondary antibodies.

3. **Bactericidal Assay**:
   - The functional activity of the induced antibodies is assessed by measuring the complement-mediated lysis of N. meningitidis using an in vitro bactericidal assay.
   - The serum bactericidal titers are defined as the serum dilution resulting in a 50% decrease in colony-forming units (CFU) per mL after 60 minutes of incubation.

### Stability Studies

The stability of the carbaMenA oligomers and their glycoconjugate vaccines is evaluated under accelerated conditions to predict their behavior at recommended storage temperatures. The following steps are involved:

1. **Accelerated Stability Testing**:
   - The carbaMenA DP8 oligomer is compared to the natural MenA CPS and its de-O-acetylated form in buffered 5 mM sodium acetate pH 7 at 37°C.
   - The change in chain length is monitored over time using 1H and 31P NMR spectroscopy.

2. **Storage Stability**:
   - The glycoconjugate vaccines are stored at room temperature and periodically tested for immunogenicity and stability.
   - The results demonstrate that the carbaMenA-based vaccines maintain their immunogenicity and stability over extended periods, making them suitable for use in resource-limited settings.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a stabilized synthetic carbohydrate-based vaccine for the prevention of meningococcal disease caused by Neisseria meningitidis serogroup A (MenA). The vaccine comprises a glycoconjugate wherein the carbohydrate antigen is a carbaMenA oligomer, specifically a carbaMenA oligomer with a degree of polymerization (DP) of 8, optionally acetylated at the C3 and C4 positions. The carbaMenA oligomers are synthesized using phosphoramidite chemistry, conjugated to a carrier protein, and evaluated for immunogenicity and stability. The resulting vaccines exhibit superior stability and immunogenicity, making them highly attractive for use in resource-limited settings.
