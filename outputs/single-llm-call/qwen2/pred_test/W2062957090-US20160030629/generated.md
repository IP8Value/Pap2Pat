# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of in vitro cell culture has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the development of three-dimensional (3D) cell culture systems. Traditional two-dimensional (2D) cell culture platforms, such as tissue culture polystyrene, have been widely used for decades. However, these 2D platforms fail to accurately mimic the complex 3D architecture of the extracellular matrix (ECM) where native cells reside. In 2D culture, cells experience a homogeneous concentration of nutrients and growth factors, leading to unnatural cell environments and altered cell-cell interactions. This results in a flat and stretched morphology, which can significantly affect cellular processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, gene expression, migration, and drug sensitivities.

On the other hand, biological in vivo 3D systems, such as animal models, are expensive and time-consuming. Therefore, there is a critical need for advanced in vitro 3D model systems that can bridge the gap between the inaccuracies of 2D systems and the complexities of animal models. These systems should mimic the ECM and the physiological relevance of an in vivo biological system.

Hydrogel scaffolds, which are cross-linked networks with high water content, have gained attention for their potential to mimic in vivo conditions. These hydrogels provide a 3D spatial support for cell growth, porosities for cell migration, and facilitate the transportation of oxygen, nutrients, waste, and soluble factors. Hydrogels can be derived from natural sources, such as collagen, fibrin, hyaluronic acid, chitosan, and alginate, or from synthetic materials. While natural hydrogels are biocompatible and bioactive, they often have varying biochemical presentations and material properties that are difficult to control. Synthetic hydrogels, while highly reproducible, often lack the structural and mechanical similarity to natural ECM.

Peptide-based hydrogels have emerged as a promising alternative due to their amino acid composition and structural and mechanical similarity to natural ECM. These hydrogels offer a balance between the advantages of natural and synthetic materials, providing a versatile platform for 3D cell culture.

However, the process of cell encapsulation and isolation in hydrogels remains challenging. Current peptide/protein hydrogels require specific pH or temperature adjustments for gel formation, which can be detrimental to cell viability. Additionally, isolating cells from the hydrogel matrix often requires harsh conditions that can threaten cell survival and compromise downstream studies.

This invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel peptide hydrogel system that offers a convenient, effective, and safe process for cell encapsulation and isolation. The hydrogel forms at neutral pH and room temperature, allowing cells to be surrounded by their culture medium during encapsulation. Moreover, the hydrogel exhibits unique deformability and reassembly capabilities, enabling easy and safe cell isolation through simple mechanical shearing and dilution.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel peptide hydrogel system for 3D cell culture. Specifically, the invention provides a solution of a newly identified peptide, referred to as h9e, which can be mixed with a cell culture medium, such as Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), at neutral pH and room temperature. Upon mixing, the h9e peptides self-assemble into a hydrogel matrix with a final peptide concentration as low as 1 mM (0.17%).

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Convenient and Mild Hydrogel Formation**: The hydrogel forms without the need for additional gel-forming buffers or adjustments to pH or temperature. This ensures that cells remain viable and comfortable during the gel formation process.
2. **Unique Deformability and Reassembly Capabilities**: The hydrogel exhibits special deformability and reassembly properties, allowing it to be converted to a liquid form through mechanical shearing and dilution. This property enables easy and safe cell isolation from the hydrogel matrix.
3. **Biocompatibility and Support for 3D Cell Growth**: The hydrogel provides a 3D scaffold that supports the growth of cells in a clustered, tumor-like structure, mimicking the in vivo environment. Cells exhibit high viability and bioactivity, making the system suitable for various biological and medical applications.
4. **Drug Testing Applications**: The hydrogel allows for the diffusion of nutrients and drugs throughout the matrix, making it an ideal platform for drug testing. The system can be used to evaluate the efficacy of drugs, such as cisplatin, on 3D cultured cells.

The invention is particularly useful for culturing and studying various cell types, including cancer cells, and for developing and testing new therapeutic agents. The h9e peptide hydrogel system offers a promising tool for advancing in vitro 3D cell culture and drug discovery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Composition and Preparation of the Hydrogel

The h9e peptide is synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques. The peptide is then dissolved in 100 mM sodium bicarbonate to a final concentration of 10 mM. For hydrogel formation, the peptide solution is mixed with MEM containing 10% FBS. The mixture is hand-shaken for about 10 seconds, and the hydrogel forms within 15 minutes at room temperature. The final peptide concentration in the hydrogel can be adjusted to 1, 2, or 3 mM, depending on the desired properties of the hydrogel.

### Rheological Properties of the Hydrogel

The rheological properties of the h9e hydrogel were characterized using a rheometer. The storage and loss moduli (G' and G", respectively) of the hydrogel were measured at 37°C. The hydrogel formation rate was determined by monitoring the storage modulus over time. The results show that the hydrogel forms rapidly, reaching a self-supporting strength within 15 minutes. The storage modulus increases with peptide concentration, with values of approximately 100, 400, and 700 Pa for 1, 2, and 3 mM hydrogels, respectively.

The hydrogel exhibits unique deformability and reassembly capabilities. When subjected to high shear stress, the hydrogel can be converted to a liquid form. Upon removal of the shear stress, the hydrogel quickly reassembles, recovering its original strength. This property is crucial for cell isolation, as it allows the hydrogel to be broken down and reformed multiple times without permanent damage.

### Cell Encapsulation and Culture

Cells, such as the MCF-7 human breast cancer cell line, can be encapsulated in the h9e hydrogel by mixing the cell suspension with the peptide solution and MEM. The mixture is seeded into culture plates and incubated at 37°C. The hydrogel forms within 30 minutes, and the cells are uniformly distributed within the matrix. The hydrogel provides a 3D scaffold that supports cell growth and proliferation.

### Cell Isolation from the Hydrogel

To isolate cells from the hydrogel matrix, the hydrogel is diluted with MEM and mechanically sheared using a pipette. The diluted solution is then centrifuged at 4°C, and the cells are collected as a pellet. This process is gentle and does not compromise cell viability. The isolated cells can be used for further analysis, such as immunofluorescence staining, Western blotting, and drug testing.

### Cell Morphology and Viability

Cells cultured in the h9e hydrogel exhibit a 3D clustered morphology, distinct from the flat, stretched morphology observed in 2D cultures. The cells form tumor-like structures, which are indicative of a more physiologically relevant environment. The viability of cells in the hydrogel is high, and no significant apoptosis is induced by the 3D culture. The proliferation rate of cells in the hydrogel is lower compared to 2D cultures, which is consistent with the behavior of cells in a more complex 3D environment.

### Drug Testing

The h9e hydrogel system is suitable for drug testing. The hydrogel allows for the diffusion of drugs, such as cisplatin, throughout the matrix. The efficacy of cisplatin on MCF-7 cells in the hydrogel was evaluated using different delivery methods, including top surface, top-bottom transwell, and pre-mixed with the hydrogel. The results show that cisplatin can cause a dose-dependent decrease in cell viability, with the pre-mixed method being the most effective. The IC50 value of cisplatin in the hydrogel is consistent with literature values, confirming the suitability of the system for drug testing.

### Immunofluorescence and Confocal Microscopy

Immunofluorescence staining and confocal microscopy were used to examine the effects of cisplatin on cells in the h9e hydrogel. The results show that cisplatin causes nuclear fragmentation, disruption of the cytoskeleton, and induction of apoptosis, as indicated by the expression of cleaved caspase-3. These findings provide evidence that the h9e hydrogel system is a reliable platform for studying drug-induced cellular responses.

### Western Blot Analysis

Western blot analysis was performed to further validate the effects of cisplatin on cells in the h9e hydrogel. The results show that cisplatin induces the expression of apoptotic markers, such as cleaved caspase-3, in the treated cells. The ability to recover and analyze cells from the hydrogel matrix is a significant advantage of the h9e hydrogel system.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Hydrogel Formation and Rheological Properties

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Peptide Synthesis:** The h9e peptide was synthesized using an automated CEM Liberty microwave peptide synthesizer according to the base-labile 9-fluorenylmethoxycarbonyl (Fmoc) strategy with Rink amide resin and Fmoc-protected amino acids.
- **Hydrogel Preparation:** Lyophilized h9e peptide was dissolved in 100 mM sodium bicarbonate to a concentration of 10 mM. The peptide solution was mixed with MEM containing 10% FBS to achieve final peptide concentrations of 1, 2, and 3 mM.
- **Rheological Testing:** The storage and loss moduli of the hydrogels were measured using a C-VOR 150 rheometer system at 37°C.

**Results:**
- The h9e hydrogel formed rapidly, reaching a self-supporting strength within 15 minutes.
- The storage modulus increased with peptide concentration, with values of approximately 100, 400, and 700 Pa for 1, 2, and 3 mM hydrogels, respectively.
- The hydrogel exhibited unique deformability and reassembly capabilities, allowing it to be converted to a liquid form and reformed multiple times without permanent damage.

#### Example 2: Cell Encapsulation and Culture

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cell Culture:** MCF-7 cells were grown in MEM with 10% FBS and passaged regularly.
- **Encapsulation:** MCF-7 cells were mixed with the h9e peptide solution and MEM, and the mixture was seeded into 12-well culture plates.
- **Culture Conditions:** The plates were incubated at 37°C, and the medium was changed every 2 days.

**Results:**
- Cells were uniformly distributed within the hydrogel matrix and formed 3D clustered structures.
- The cells exhibited high viability and bioactivity, indicating that the hydrogel provides a supportive 3D environment.

#### Example 3: Cell Isolation from the Hydrogel

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Isolation:** The hydrogel was diluted with MEM and mechanically sheared using a pipette. The solution was centrifuged at 4°C, and the cells were collected as a pellet.
- **Viability Testing:** The viability of isolated cells was determined using the trypan blue exclusion method.

**Results:**
- The cell isolation process was gentle and did not compromise cell viability.
- The isolated cells were viable and could be used for further analysis.

#### Example 4: Drug Testing with Cisplatin

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Drug Treatment:** MCF-7 cells in the h9e hydrogel were treated with 40 µM cisplatin using different delivery methods: top surface, top-bottom transwell, and pre-mixed with the hydrogel.
- **Viability Testing:** Cell viability was determined using the trypan blue exclusion method at various time points.

**Results:**
- Cisplatin caused a dose-dependent decrease in cell viability, with the pre-mixed method being the most effective.
- The IC50 value of cisplatin in the hydrogel was consistent with literature values, confirming the suitability of the system for drug testing.

#### Example 5: Immunofluorescence and Confocal Microscopy

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Staining:** MCF-7 cells in the h9e hydrogel were fixed and stained with antibodies against actin, Ki67, survivin, and cleaved caspase-3. Nuclei were stained with DAPI.
- **Microscopy:** Images were captured using a confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss LSM 700 META).

**Results:**
- Cisplatin caused nuclear fragmentation, disruption of the cytoskeleton, and induction of apoptosis, as indicated by the expression of cleaved caspase-3.
- The results provide evidence that the h9e hydrogel system is a reliable platform for studying drug-induced cellular responses.

#### Example 6: Western Blot Analysis

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Sample Preparation:** MCF-7 cells were cultured in 2D monolayer and 3D hydrogel, treated with 30 µM cisplatin, and harvested.
- **Western Blotting:** Whole cell extracts were prepared, and the expressions of apoptotic markers were detected using Western blot analysis.

**Results:**
- Cisplatin induced the expression of apoptotic markers, such as cleaved caspase-3, in the treated cells.
- The ability to recover and analyze cells from the hydrogel matrix is a significant advantage of the h9e hydrogel system.

The h9e peptide hydrogel system provides a novel and advanced platform for 3D cell culture. The hydrogel forms at neutral pH and room temperature, allowing cells to be encapsulated safely and effectively. The unique deformability and reassembly capabilities of the hydrogel enable easy and safe cell isolation, making it an ideal system for various biological and medical applications, including drug testing. The h9e hydrogel system offers a promising tool for advancing in vitro 3D cell culture and drug discovery.
