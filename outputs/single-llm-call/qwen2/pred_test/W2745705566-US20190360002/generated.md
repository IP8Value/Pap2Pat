# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and biotechnology, specifically to the identification and characterization of a novel Cas9 endonuclease from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12. The invention encompasses the use of this Cas9 endonuclease, referred to herein as ThermoCas9, for genome editing and silencing in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. The invention further includes methods for the isolation, purification, and use of ThermoCas9, as well as the design and use of RNA guides for directing ThermoCas9 to specific target sequences.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats (CRISPR) and CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins provide adaptive and heritable immunity in prokaryotes against invading genetic elements. CRISPR-Cas systems are classified into two main classes and six types based on their complexity and signature proteins. Class 2 systems, including type-II CRISPR-Cas9 and type V CRISPR-Cas12a, have been extensively utilized for genome engineering in both eukaryotes and prokaryotes. These systems introduce targeted double-stranded DNA breaks (DSBs) using a ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex formed by a single Cas endonuclease and an RNA guide.

The guide for Cas9 consists of a crRNA (CRISPR RNA) and a tracrRNA (trans-activating CRISPR RNA) that can be engineered into a single guide RNA (sgRNA). The sgRNA directs Cas9 to a specific target sequence, which requires a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) for cleavage. The PAM is a short sequence adjacent to the target sequence and is highly variable among different Cas9 proteins.

Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9) is the most widely used Cas9 for genome engineering. However, SpCas9 is not active at temperatures above 42°C, limiting its utility for engineering thermophilic organisms. There is a need for a Cas9 endonuclease that remains active at elevated temperatures, enabling genome editing and silencing in thermophilic bacteria and other high-temperature applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel Cas9 endonuclease, ThermoCas9, from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12. ThermoCas9 is active in vitro and in vivo over a wide temperature range, from 20°C to 70°C, making it suitable for genome editing and silencing in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. The invention includes methods for isolating, purifying, and using ThermoCas9, as well as designing and using RNA guides for directing ThermoCas9 to specific target sequences.

The invention further encompasses the use of ThermoCas9 for in vivo genome editing and silencing in the thermophile Bacillus smithii ET138 and the mesophile Pseudomonas putida KT2440. The invention also includes the use of a catalytically inactive variant of ThermoCas9 (ThermodCas9) for transcriptional silencing.

## RNA Guides and Target Sequences

### Binding, Cleavage, Marking and Modifying Temperatures

ThermoCas9 binds to and cleaves target DNA sequences in the presence of a specific RNA guide (sgRNA). The sgRNA is designed to complement the target sequence and includes a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). ThermoCas9 is active over a wide temperature range, from 20°C to 70°C, with optimal activity at temperatures between 37°C and 65°C. The cleavage efficiency of ThermoCas9 is influenced by the temperature, with higher temperatures generally increasing the cleavage rate. However, at lower temperatures (≤30°C), ThermoCas9 is highly specific and only cleaves targets with preferred PAM sequences, reducing off-target effects.

### Functional Moieties

The sgRNA used with ThermoCas9 includes a crRNA and a tracrRNA, which can be engineered into a single chimeric RNA. The tracrRNA contains three stem-loop structures that are essential for optimal ThermoCas9 activity. The length of the spacer sequence in the sgRNA is also critical, with spacer lengths of 23, 21, 20, and 19 nucleotides showing the highest cleavage efficiency. The sgRNA can be modified with functional moieties such as fluorescent labels or affinity tags for detection and purification purposes.

### Nuclease Activity

ThermoCas9 is a dsDNA nuclease that introduces double-stranded breaks at specific target sequences. The cleavage activity of ThermoCas9 is dependent on the presence of divalent cations, with Mg2+ and Mn2+ being the most effective. ThermoCas9 does not cleave single-stranded DNA, indicating its specificity for dsDNA targets. The catalytic activity of ThermoCas9 can be modulated by introducing point mutations in the RuvC and HNH domains, resulting in nickase or catalytically inactive variants.

## Expression Vectors

Expression vectors for ThermoCas9 and ThermodCas9 have been constructed for use in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. The vectors include the thermocas9 gene under the control of a constitutive or inducible promoter, a homologous recombination template for repairing Cas9-induced double-stranded DNA breaks, and a sgRNA expressing module. The vectors are designed for single-plasmid approaches, minimizing the number of genetic parts required for genome editing and silencing.

## Host Cells

ThermoCas9 and ThermodCas9 have been successfully expressed and used in various host cells, including the thermophile Bacillus smithii ET138 and the mesophile Pseudomonas putida KT2440. The expression of ThermoCas9 in these hosts has been optimized for high activity and stability at the respective growth temperatures. The use of ThermoCas9 in these hosts has enabled efficient genome editing and silencing, demonstrating the broad applicability of ThermoCas9 across a wide range of temperatures and organisms.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, a Gram-positive, moderately thermophilic bacterium with an optimal growth temperature of 65°C, was isolated and sequenced. The genome sequencing revealed the presence of a type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system, including the Cas9 endonuclease (ThermoCas9). ThermoCas9 was predicted to be relatively small (1082 amino acids) compared to other Cas9 orthologues, such as SpCas9 (1368 amino acids). The size difference is primarily due to a truncated REC lobe. ThermoCas9 was expected to be active at temperatures around the optimal growth temperature of G. thermodenitrificans T12.

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

The essential consensus sequences for ThermoCas9 were defined using in silico predictions and in vitro assays. The crRNA and tracrRNA modules were predicted, and a 190-nucleotide sgRNA chimera was designed. ThermoCas9 was heterologously expressed in E. coli and purified to homogeneity. The thermostability of ThermoCas9 was assessed by incubating purified apo-ThermoCas9 and ThermoCas9 loaded with in vitro transcribed sgRNA at 60°C and 65°C. SDS-PAGE analysis showed that ThermoCas9 denatures at 65°C but not at 60°C, while the denaturation temperature of the ThermoCas9-sgRNA complex is above 65°C.

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs which are Essential for the Function of CAS9 and Those which Confer Thermostability in Thermophilic Cas9 Nucleases

The core amino acid motifs essential for the function of ThermoCas9 were identified through sequence analysis and mutagenesis studies. Key residues in the RuvC and HNH domains were identified as critical for nuclease activity. Mutations in these domains, such as D8A and H582A, resulted in a catalytically inactive variant (ThermodCas9). The thermostability of ThermoCas9 was attributed to the association with an appropriate sgRNA guide, which stabilizes the protein at elevated temperatures.

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence of G. thermodenitrificans gtCas9

The PAM sequence recognized by ThermoCas9 was determined using in vitro cleavage assays. A library of DNA targets with randomized 7-base pair (bp) sequences flanking the protospacer was generated. ThermoCas9-based cleavage assays at 55°C revealed that ThermoCas9 recognizes a 5′-NNNNCNR-3′ PAM, with a preference for cytosine at the first, third, fourth, and sixth PAM positions. Additional assays revealed that an adenine at the eighth PAM position optimizes targeting efficiency.

### Example 5: Target Generation with Randomized PAM

A set of 16 different target DNA fragments with 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′ PAMs was generated to further clarify the PAM requirements. Cleavage assays at various temperatures (20°C, 30°C, 37°C, 45°C, 55°C, and 60°C) showed that ThermoCas9 cleaves all targets at temperatures between 37°C and 60°C. At 30°C, only targets with optimal PAM sequences (5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′) were cleaved, and at 20°C, only targets with 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′ PAM sequences were cleaved.

### Example 6: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences for gtCas9

In vitro cleavage assays were performed to determine the PAM sequences recognized by ThermoCas9. Plasmid targets with distinct 5′-CCCCCCAN-3′ PAMs were incubated with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA at 55°C. The results showed that ThermoCas9 cleaves targets with an adenine at the eighth PAM position most efficiently. Cleavage assays with 16 different 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′ PAMs at various temperatures confirmed that ThermoCas9 is highly specific for certain PAM sequences at lower temperatures.

### Example 7: In Silico PAM Prediction for gtCas9

In silico PAM prediction was performed using the 10 spacers of the G. thermodenitrificans T12 CRISPR locus to search for potential protospacers in viral and plasmid sequences. The results suggested the significance of a cytosine at the fifth and an adenine at the eighth PAM positions, which was confirmed by in vitro cleavage assays.

### Example 8: Determination of 8 Nucleotide Long PAM Sequences for gtCas9

Additional PAM determination assays were performed to extend the PAM identity to the eighth position. Four linearized plasmid targets with distinct 5′-CCCCCCAN-3′ PAMs were incubated with ThermoCas9 and sgRNA at 55°C. The results showed that an adenine at the eighth PAM position is optimal for targeting efficiency.

### Example 9: In Vivo Genome Editing of Bacillus smithii ET138 with gtCas9 and 8 Nucleotide Length PAM Sequences

ThermoCas9 was used for in vivo genome editing of the thermophile Bacillus smithii ET138. A single plasmid approach was used, combining homologous recombination and ThermoCas9-based counter-selection. The pNW33n-based pThermoCas9 plasmids contained the thermocas9 gene under the control of the native xylL promoter, a homologous recombination template for repairing Cas9-induced double-stranded DNA breaks within a gene of interest, and a sgRNA expressing module. The deletion of the pyrF gene was achieved, demonstrating the effectiveness of ThermoCas9 for genome editing in thermophiles.
