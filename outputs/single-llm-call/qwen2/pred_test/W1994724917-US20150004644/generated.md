# DESCRIPTION

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for detecting the functionality of the accessory gene regulator (agr) system in Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) using Whole Cell Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization Time-Of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (WC-MALDI-TOF MS). Specifically, the invention provides a method for identifying the presence or absence of the delta-toxin, a marker of agr functionality, in S. aureus isolates. The method involves analyzing the MALDI-TOF MS spectrum of S. aureus isolates to detect a specific peak corresponding to the delta-toxin. The invention also includes the use of this method to correlate agr functionality with clinical outcomes, particularly in chronic infections and glycopeptide resistance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Staphylococcus aureus is a common opportunistic pathogen responsible for a wide range of infections, from minor skin infections to life-threatening systemic diseases. The virulence of S. aureus is regulated by the accessory gene regulator (agr) system, which controls the expression of numerous virulence factors. The agr system is crucial for the pathogenesis of S. aureus, and its dysfunction has been associated with chronic infections and increased resistance to antibiotics, particularly glycopeptides.

Whole Cell Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization Time-Of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (WC-MALDI-TOF MS) is a rapid and accurate method for bacterial identification and characterization. It has been widely adopted in clinical microbiology laboratories due to its efficiency and reliability. The present invention leverages the capabilities of WC-MALDI-TOF MS to detect the delta-toxin, a key marker of agr functionality, in S. aureus isolates. This method provides a rapid and reliable way to assess agr functionality, which can have significant implications for clinical management and patient outcomes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for detecting the functionality of the accessory gene regulator (agr) system in Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) using Whole Cell Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization Time-Of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (WC-MALDI-TOF MS). The method involves the following steps:

1. Culturing S. aureus isolates on appropriate agar media.
2. Preparing the S. aureus isolates for WC-MALDI-TOF MS analysis.
3. Analyzing the MALDI-TOF MS spectrum of the S. aureus isolates to detect a specific peak corresponding to the delta-toxin.
4. Interpreting the presence or absence of the delta-toxin peak to determine agr functionality.

The invention also includes the use of this method to correlate agr functionality with clinical outcomes, particularly in chronic infections and glycopeptide resistance. The method can be applied in clinical settings to guide treatment decisions and improve patient outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Equipment

The method of the present invention utilizes Whole Cell Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization Time-Of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (WC-MALDI-TOF MS) for the detection of the delta-toxin in S. aureus isolates. The primary equipment required for this method includes:

- **MALDI-TOF Mass Spectrometer**: An Axima Assurance® mass spectrometer (Shimadzu Biotech) or a similar instrument capable of performing WC-MALDI-TOF MS analysis.
- **Software**: LaunchPad® software (version 2.8.4.20081127, Shimadzu Biotech) or equivalent software for controlling the mass spectrometer and analyzing the data.
- **Database**: SARAMIS® (bioMérieux) or a similar database for bacterial identification.
- **Calibration Standards**: E. coli ATCC 8739 for general calibration and PEG3000 dissolved in matrix solution for calibration in the 2800 - 3200 Th range.
- **Matrix Solution**: α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid (CHCA) matrix for preparing the bacterial samples.

### 2. Methodology

#### 2.1 Sample Preparation

1. **Culturing S. aureus Isolates**: S. aureus isolates are cultured on trypticase soy agar with horse blood (TSH, bioMérieux) for 24 hours at 35±2°C under aerobic conditions. Alternative media such as Columbia agar with 5% sheep blood (COS), chocolate agar with Polyvitex® mixture (PVX), S. aureus chromogenic medium (CHROM ID SA®), and GP agar plate (Difco®) can also be used.
2. **Sample Processing**: One colony of the cultured S. aureus isolate is transferred to a disposable target plate (Fleximass DS®, bioMérieux) using the "smear method" of deposition. One microliter of the CHCA matrix solution is then added to the sample and allowed to dry for 15 minutes.

#### 2.2 MALDI-TOF MS Analysis

1. **Mass Spectrometer Settings**: The mass spectrometer is set to conventional settings determined for bacterial identification. Additional calibration is performed in the 2800 - 3200 Th range using PEG3000 dissolved in the matrix solution.
2. **Data Acquisition**: The mass spectral profiles are obtained using LaunchPad® software (Shimadzu Biotech) or SirWeb MaldiTof® software (I2A).

#### 2.3 Data Interpretation

1. **Peak Detection**: The MALDI-TOF MS spectrum is analyzed to detect a specific peak at 3005±5 Th, corresponding to the wild-type delta-toxin, or at 3035±5 Th, corresponding to a G10S mutant form of delta-toxin.
2. **Agr Functionality Assessment**: The presence of the 3005±5 Th or 3035±5 Th peak indicates the production of delta-toxin and, consequently, a functional agr system. The absence of both peaks indicates a lack of delta-toxin production and suggests agr dysfunction.

### 3. Results

#### 3.1 Validation of the Method

The method was validated using purified delta-toxin and isogenic strain pairs. Purified delta-toxin from S. aureus was analyzed by MALDI-TOF MS and produced a peak at 3005±1 Th, consistent with the expected molecular weight of the formylated delta-toxin. Isogenic strains for agr-rnaIII (and thus for hld) were tested, and the agr-negative derivatives lacked the 3005±5 Th peak, confirming the specificity of the method.

#### 3.2 Robustness and Repeatability

The method was tested for robustness and repeatability under routine calibration conditions. The 3005±5 Th peak was consistently detected in delta-toxin-positive strains and absent in delta-toxin-negative strains across different incubation times (18, 24, and 48 hours) and various agar media. The relative intensity of the 3005±5 Th peak slightly decreased at 48 hours, but the presence or absence of the peak remained consistent.

#### 3.3 Analysis of Clinical Strains

A collection of 168 consecutive clinical isolates was analyzed using the method. The results showed that 139 isolates presented the 3005±5 Th peak, 12 isolates presented a peak at 3035±5 Th, and 17 isolates did not present any peak in this range. The 3035±5 Th peak was confirmed to correspond to a G10S mutant form of delta-toxin. Northern blot analysis confirmed that the lack of delta-toxin production in the 17 isolates corresponded to a lack of RNAIII expression, indicating agr dysfunction.

#### 3.4 Clinical Correlations

The lack of delta-toxin production was significantly associated with chronic infections (p = 0.005) but not with colonization (p = 0.541) or biomaterial infections (p = 0.470). Methicillin resistance was also significantly associated with the delta-toxin minus status (p = 0.035). Glycopeptide resistance was independently and significantly associated with delta-toxin deficiency (p = 0.023).

#### 3.5 Within-Host Shift of agr Function

For several patients with delta-toxin-negative isolates, consecutive isolates were available. Genotyping of these isolates showed that the delta-toxin-negative isolates were selected from a previous delta-toxin-positive population, confirming that agr dysfunction can occur during the course of infection.

## CONCLUSION

The present invention provides a rapid and reliable method for detecting the functionality of the accessory gene regulator (agr) system in Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) using Whole Cell Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization Time-Of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (WC-MALDI-TOF MS). The method involves analyzing the MALDI-TOF MS spectrum of S. aureus isolates to detect a specific peak corresponding to the delta-toxin. The presence or absence of the delta-toxin peak can be used to determine agr functionality, which has significant implications for clinical outcomes, particularly in chronic infections and glycopeptide resistance. This method can be applied in clinical settings to guide treatment decisions and improve patient outcomes.
