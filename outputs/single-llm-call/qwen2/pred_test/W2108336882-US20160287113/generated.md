# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of neural interfaces and, more particularly, to microelectrode arrays (MEAs) for neural recording and stimulation. The invention addresses the need for improved biocompatibility, low impedance, high charge injection capacity, and robustness in neural interfaces, which are essential for advanced clinical rehabilitation solutions and the exploration of neural networks.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Neural interfaces, such as microelectrode arrays (MEAs), are crucial for exploring neural networks and developing advanced clinical rehabilitation solutions. These devices are used to record the electrical activity from neural structures and deliver electrical microstimulation to restore previously lost functions. Applications include addressing pathologies such as hearing loss, pathological tremors, visual impairments, and paralysis. Increasing the number and density of electrodes in MEAs can significantly enhance the efficiency of brain-machine interfaces and neural prostheses. However, this increase in density leads to smaller electrode dimensions, which can result in high intrinsic noise levels and reduced charge injection capacity, thereby limiting the performance of the neural interface.

Boron-doped diamond (BDD) is a promising material for long-term neural implants due to its biocompatibility, chemical inertness, and structural stability. Despite these advantages, BDD suffers from low double-layer capacitance and high impedance, which can hinder its effectiveness in neural recording and stimulation. Recent advancements have shown that nanostructuration strategies, such as the use of vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs), can significantly enhance the performance of BDD electrodes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel microelectrode array (MEA) for neural recording and stimulation, comprising microelectrodes made of 3D-nanostructured boron-doped diamond (BDD). The 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes are designed to overcome the limitations of conventional BDD microelectrodes by significantly reducing impedance and increasing charge injection capacity. The invention further includes methods for fabricating the 3D-nanostructured BDD MEAs and evaluating their performance in neural recording and stimulation.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### 1. Fabrication of 3D-Nanostructured BDD Substrates

The fabrication process of 3D-nanostructured BDD substrates involves several steps. Initially, boron-doped diamond films are produced on silicon substrates using microwave plasma chemical vapor deposition (MPCVD). The diamond films are then oxygenated to create a hydrophilic surface. Vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) are grown on the BDD films and subsequently coated with a second BDD film. This process results in a 3D-nanostructured BDD material with enhanced electrochemical properties.

#### 2. Fabrication of 3D-Nanostructured BDD MEAs

The fabrication of 3D-nanostructured BDD MEAs involves the following steps:
1. **Diamond Seed Deposition**: Detonation diamond nanoparticles are spread onto a fused silica substrate.
2. **Aluminum Masking**: An aluminum layer is deposited to define the electrode patterns.
3. **Diamond Growth**: Diamond electrodes are grown using MPECVD.
4. **Electrode Contacting**: Metal tracks are deposited to connect the electrodes.
5. **Isolation Layer**: A SU8 layer is deposited to isolate the metal tracks from the electrolyte solution.

#### 3. Performance Evaluation

The performance of the 3D-nanostructured BDD MEAs is evaluated through various electrochemical and biological tests:
1. **Cyclic Voltammetry**: Measures the potential window and double-layer capacitance of the electrodes.
2. **Impedance Spectroscopy**: Evaluates the impedance of the electrodes.
3. **Intrinsic Noise Measurement**: Determines the noise level of the electrodes.
4. **Biocompatibility Testing**: Assesses the ability of the substrates to support neural cell cultures.
5. **Neural Recording and Stimulation**: Tests the ability of the MEAs to record and stimulate neural activity in both ex vivo and in vitro settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Fabrication of 3D-Nanostructured BDD Substrates

#### 1.1. Diamond Film Production

Boron-doped diamond (BDD) films are produced on silicon substrates using a microwave plasma chemical vapor deposition (MPCVD) system. The process involves the following steps:
1. **Substrate Preparation**: Silicon substrates are cleaned and then coated with 6-nm detonation diamond nanoparticles dispersed in water.
2. **Diamond Growth**: A 600-nm-thick nano-crystalline diamond film is grown at a temperature of 650°C under a pressure of 26 mbar, with a gas mixture of 1% methane in 99% hydrogen and trimethylboron as a dopant.
3. **Surface Treatment**: The surfaces are oxygenated using an ozone treatment to create a hydrophilic surface.

#### 1.2. VACNT Growth and Coating

Vertically aligned carbon nanotubes (VACNTs) are grown on the BDD films using a plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) reactor. The process involves:
1. **Nickel Catalyst Deposition**: A 7-nm nickel layer is deposited on the diamond surface and heated to form 50-nm nanoparticles.
2. **VACNT Growth**: The nickel particles catalyze the growth of 3-mm-long VACNTs.
3. **Diamond Coating**: The VACNTs are coated with a layer of 25-nm diamond particles using an electrostatic grafting method. The diamond seeds are then grown to form a 50-nm BDD thin film.

### 2. Fabrication of 3D-Nanostructured BDD MEAs

#### 2.1. Diamond Seed Deposition

Detonation diamond nanoparticles are spread onto a 4-inch fused silica substrate using a process described in the literature. The substrate is chosen to avoid electrical cross-talk between the electrodes.

#### 2.2. Aluminum Masking

An aluminum layer, composed of 80-nm disks, is deposited to define the electrode patterns using photolithography. The diamond nanoparticles outside these protected areas are etched away using reactive ion etching (RIE) under a pure oxygen plasma.

#### 2.3. Diamond Growth

Diamond electrodes are grown using MPECVD in a diamond growth reactor housing a gas mixture of methane, hydrogen, and trimethylboron. The fabricated diamond electrodes exhibit a thickness of 500 nm.

#### 2.4. Electrode Contacting

Metal tracks are deposited to connect the electrodes. This involves:
1. **Metal Track Deposition**: 10/150-nm Cr/Au metal tracks are deposited using lift-off with AZ nLof 2020 photoresist material.
2. **Contact Formation**: A metal ring is deposited across the edges of the electrodes with a 5-mm overlap.

#### 2.5. Isolation Layer

A 2-mm-thick SU8 layer is deposited onto the substrate to isolate the metal tracks from the electrolyte solution. The SU8 layer is opened with SU8 developer to define the 20-μm-diameter of the microelectrode.

### 3. Performance Evaluation

#### 3.1. Cyclic Voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) is performed to measure the potential window and double-layer capacitance of the electrodes. The CV is conducted in solutions such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), artificial cerebrospinal fluid (aCSF), and lithium perchlorate (LiClO4) using a three-electrode setup.

#### 3.2. Impedance Spectroscopy

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is performed to evaluate the impedance of the electrodes. The EIS is conducted in PBS and LiClO4 over a frequency range from 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz.

#### 3.3. Intrinsic Noise Measurement

The intrinsic noise level of the electrodes is measured by recording the electrical potential in aCSF between each microelectrode and an Ag/AgCl ground electrode. The noise level is calculated as the standard deviation of the signal.

#### 3.4. Biocompatibility Testing

The biocompatibility of the 3D-nanostructured BDD substrates is assessed by culturing hippocampal and spinal cord cells on the substrates. The cell attachment, distribution, and expression of neuron-specific markers are evaluated using immunocytochemistry and fluorescence microscopy.

#### 3.5. Neural Recording and Stimulation

The performance of the 3D-nanostructured BDD MEAs in neural recording and stimulation is evaluated using:
1. **Acute Embryonic Spinal Cord Recording**: The MEAs are used to record rhythmic activity from whole embryonic hindbrain-spinal cord preparations.
2. **Long-Term Hippocampal Cell Culture Recording**: The MEAs are used to record spiking activity from long-term hippocampal cell cultures.

The present invention provides a microelectrode array (MEA) for neural recording and stimulation, comprising microelectrodes made of 3D-nanostructured boron-doped diamond (BDD). The 3D-nanostructured BDD microelectrodes exhibit significantly reduced impedance and increased charge injection capacity compared to conventional BDD microelectrodes.
