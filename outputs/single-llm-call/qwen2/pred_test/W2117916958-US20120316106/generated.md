# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of antibiotics and, more specifically, to a novel combination of antibiotics, lankacidin and lankamycin, which act synergistically to inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding to neighboring sites on the ribosome. The invention also encompasses methods for producing and using these antibiotics, as well as chemical derivatives and conjugates thereof.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Antibiotics are essential tools in the fight against bacterial infections. They function by targeting various cellular processes in bacteria, including protein synthesis, cell wall synthesis, and DNA replication. Many natural and semi-synthetic antibiotics inhibit protein synthesis by binding to the ribosome, a critical organelle responsible for translating mRNA into proteins. The ribosome consists of two subunits: the large subunit (50S in prokaryotes) and the small subunit (30S in prokaryotes). The peptidyl transferase center (PTC) in the large ribosomal subunit is a key target for many antibiotics, including phenicols, lincosamides, pleuromutilins, and oxazolidinones. These compounds inhibit cell growth by interfering with peptide bond formation.

Another major antibiotic binding site in the large ribosomal subunit is located at the upper segment of the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET), adjacent to the PTC. Compounds binding to this site, such as macrolides and streptogramins B, impede the progression of nascent proteins towards the tunnel exit. Simultaneous inhibition of successive steps in protein synthesis often results in a synergistic action of the inhibitors. Streptogramin antibiotics, produced by several Streptomyces species, exemplify this synergistic effect. These antibiotics consist of two structurally distinct compounds: streptogramin A (SA) and streptogramin B (SB). SA compounds bind to the PTC, while SB compounds bind to the NPET. Each component is a weak antibiotic on its own, but in combination, they exhibit a strong inhibitory effect.

Lankacidin and lankamycin are two inhibitory compounds produced by *Streptomyces rochei*. Lankacidin (LC) is a macrocyclic compound with a 17-membered carbocyclic ring bridged by a 6-membered lactone. Lankamycin (LM) is a 14-member ring macrolide decorated with 4-acetyl-L-arcanose and D-chalcose sugars. LC exhibits both antibiotic and antitumor activities and is used in veterinary medicine. LM exhibits a weak antibiotic activity against several Gram-positive bacteria. Despite their co-production by *Streptomyces rochei*, the binding sites and functional interactions of LC and LM have not been fully elucidated.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel combination of antibiotics, lankacidin (LC) and lankamycin (LM), which act synergistically to inhibit bacterial protein synthesis. The invention is based on the discovery that LC and LM bind to neighboring sites on the ribosome, specifically the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) and the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET), respectively. The simultaneous binding of LC and LM to these sites results in a synergistic inhibitory effect on bacterial growth.

The invention includes:
1. A method for inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis comprising administering a combination of lankacidin and lankamycin to a bacterial cell.
2. A pharmaceutical composition comprising a combination of lankacidin and lankamycin and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.
3. Chemical derivatives and conjugates of lankacidin and lankamycin that enhance their synergistic inhibitory effect.
4. Methods for producing lankacidin and lankamycin, including synthetic and semi-synthetic methods.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Lankacidin Binding Site

Lankacidin (LC) binds to the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) of the large ribosomal subunit. The binding site of LC is characterized by a unique network of interactions with five re-oriented nucleotides: U2506, G2505, G2581, C2610, and G2576. Within this network, G2576 stacks upon G2505, C2610 stacks upon G2581, and the exocyclic amine of G2505 reaches hydrogen bonding distance from the O2 of C2610, forming a CG rS* base pairing. The amino group of G2502 is within hydrogen bond reach of the O4 of U2506, which shifts towards the lankacidin 1,3-dicarbonyl system. These newly established contacts stabilize the placement of G2505 and U2506 in a conformation that favors binding of LC.

Additionally, LC interacts with second-shell nucleotides, specifically G2576, A2062, C2530, U2531, C2507, U2584, G2581, C2610, and A2059. The macrolactone ring of LC fits into a shallow depression in the wall of the PTC A-site, forming van der Waals interactions with U2504, G2505, and U2506. The 2-methyl group at the lactone edge of the macrocyclic ring inserts into the opening of the hydrophobic crevice formed by the splayed-out bases of A2451 and C2452. This cleft also hosts the aminoacyl moiety of A-site-bound aminoacyl-tRNA and is involved in binding other PTC-targeting antibiotics, such as phenicols, lincosamides, pleuromutilins, oxazolidinones, and streptogramin A (SA) antibiotic compounds like dalfopristin.

The binding of LC to the PTC inhibits bacterial cell-free transcription-translation systems and interferes with the peptide bond formation, as evidenced by the inhibition of the puromycin reaction catalyzed by ribosomes. The IC50 values for LC in these assays are 1.5 ± 0.1 μM for E. coli and 0.32 ± 0.02 μM for Staphylococcus aureus.

### Lankamycin Binding Site

Lankamycin (LM) binds to the nascent peptide exit tunnel (NPET) of the large ribosomal subunit. The binding site of LM is similar to that of erythromycin (ERY), a 14-member ring macrolide. LM binds to the NPET in immediate proximity to the PTC, the LC binding site. The desosamine sugar of ERY approaches the macrocyclic ring of LC somewhat too close for simultaneous binding of both drugs, as indicated by competition experiments showing that LC displaces 14C-ERY from the ribosome.

RNA probing experiments using D. radiodurans large ribosomal subunits confirmed that LM binds to the NPET. Binding of LM results in the protection of U2609 from CMCT modification and of A2058 and A2059 from DMS modification. These protections are similar to those observed for ERY and other 14-member ring macrolides. However, the exact positioning of LM in the NPET is slightly different from that of ERY, likely due to the structural differences between the two compounds.

### Lankacidin and Lankamycin Can Simultaneously Bind to the Ribosome

Despite the partial overlap of their binding sites, LC and LM can simultaneously bind to the ribosome. This was demonstrated using RNA probing experiments. At 50 μM, LC shields U2506 and U2585 from CMCT modification, while LM or ERY (at 50 μM) strongly protect U2609. When LC and LM are present together, all three residues (U2506, U2585, and U2609) are protected, indicating simultaneous binding of LC and LM to the ribosome. In contrast, the LC-mediated protection of U2506 and U2585 is partially relieved upon addition of ERY, and ERY-dependent protection of U2609 is partly reversed when LC is present. This indicates that while LC and ERY compete for binding to the ribosome, LC and LM can bind simultaneously to their respective targets in the PTC and NPET.

### LC and LM Act Synergistically Upon Bacterial Cells

The synergistic action of LC and LM was confirmed through in vivo and in vitro experiments. In a checkerboard assay using a susceptible strain of Gram-positive S. aureus, the minimal inhibitory concentrations (MIC) of LC and LM were determined. The results showed a concave-shaped curve, indicating a synergistic interaction between the two compounds. The synergistic effect was further verified in an E. coli cell-free transcription-translation system, where the combination of LC and LM inhibited protein synthesis more effectively than either compound alone.

## Materials and Methods

### Crystallography

Crystals of the large ribosomal subunit (50S) from Deinococcus radiodurans (D50S) were grown as described in the literature. Crystals were soaked in solutions containing 0.025 mM of lankacidin for 20 hours at 20°C, transferred into cryo-buffer, and shock-frozen in liquid nitrogen. X-ray data were collected at 85-100 K from shock-frozen crystals using synchrotron radiation at 19ID at the Advanced Photon Source/Argonne National Laboratory and at ID23-2 at the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF). Data were recorded on charge-coupled devices and processed with HKL2000. The structure of D50S was refined against the structure factor amplitudes of the antibiotic complex D50S-LC using rigid body refinement as implemented in CNS. For free R-factor calculation, random 5% of the data were omitted during refinement. An unbiased electron density map was calculated, and further refinement was carried out using CNS 1.2 minimization combined with various programs, exploiting the available crystal structure of lankacidin (29). The ribosome-antibiotic interactions were determined with LigPlot and LPC, and images were generated using PyMol.

### Antibiotic Binding

D. radiodurans 50S subunits (0.1 μM) were preincubated for 15 minutes at 37°C with varying concentrations of lankacidin. The ribosome-antibiotic complexes were purified by gel filtration in BioGel P30 spin columns, and the amount of ribosome-bound radioactivity was measured in a scintillation counter. The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism.

### Inhibition of Cell-Free Protein Synthesis by LC and LM

The E. coli cell-free transcription-translation system for circular DNA (Promega) was preincubated with varying concentrations of LC and LM for 5 minutes at 20°C. The reactions (10 μL final volume) were initiated by adding 0.64 μg of pBestLuc plasmid DNA (Promega). Reactions were incubated for 40 minutes at 20°C and stopped by chilling on ice. The activity of firefly luciferase synthesized in the reaction was determined using the Bright-Glo Luciferase Assay System (Promega). The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism.

### Inhibition of the Peptidyl Transferase Reaction by LC

The inhibition of the peptidyl transferase reaction by LC was performed and analyzed as described in the literature. The data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism.

### RNA Probing

RNA probing was carried out using D. radiodurans 50S ribosomal subunits at 200 nM concentration and antibiotics at the following concentrations: LC - 50 μM, LM - 500 μM, ERY - 50 μM. Prior to addition of the modifying reagents, ribosomal subunits were preincubated with the drug for 10 minutes at 37°C and then 10 minutes at 20°C.

### Cell-Free Translation System Used for Detecting Synergism In Vitro

Inhibition of cell-free protein synthesis by LC and LM was measured using the E. coli cell-free transcription-translation system and the firefly luciferase activity assay. Cell-free extract was prepared from Escherichia coli (Strain BL21-DE3) as previously reported. An aqueous solution containing amino acid mix (1.3 mM for each amino acid), magnesium acetate (20 mM), NTP mix (0.9 mM), ATP (0.4 mM), potassium glutamate (150 mM), E. coli tRNA mixture (0.17 mg/mL), DTT (1.8 mM), folinic acid (35 μg/mL), cAMP (0.65 mM), NH4OAc (28 mM), creatine phosphate (80 mM), HEPES (pH 7.5 at 37°C, 140 mM), 9.5% w/v PEG-8000, tyrosine (0.4 mg/mL), creatine kinase (0.35 mg/mL), and S12 cell-free extract (12% v/v) was prepared. To this solution, PIVEX.6D plasmid encoding wild-type GFP (1 ng/μL) was added, commencing the transcription-translation reaction. This solution was immediately added to a 96-well plate containing serial dilutions of lankamycin and lankacidin. After incubation for 1 hour at 37°C, 50 μL of erythromycin (8 μM) was added to each well to completely stop the translation reaction. The fluorescence data were collected on a TECAN SpectraFluor Plus 96-well plate reader (excite = 485 nm, emit = 535 nm). The data were fitted using the least-squared regression analysis package in Igor Pro.

## Pharmacology

### Chemical Derivatives and Conjugates

Chemical derivatives and conjugates of lankacidin and lankamycin can be synthesized to enhance their synergistic inhibitory effect. These derivatives can be designed to increase the affinity of the compounds for their respective binding sites on the ribosome or to introduce additional chemical moieties that can form stable interactions with the ribosome. For example, the introduction of a bulky substituent at the 2-methyl group of LC can enhance its binding to the hydrophobic cleft formed by A2451 and C2452. Similarly, modifications to the desosamine sugar of LM can improve its binding to the NPET.

### Synthetic and Semi-Synthetic Methods for Producing Antibiotics

Lankacidin and lankamycin can be produced using both synthetic and semi-synthetic methods. Synthetic methods involve the step-by-step construction of the molecules from smaller building blocks, while semi-synthetic methods involve the modification of naturally occurring compounds. For example, lankacidin can be synthesized by coupling a 17-membered carbocyclic ring with a 6-membered lactone, followed by the introduction of the 2-methyl group. Lankamycin can be synthesized by constructing a 14-member ring macrolide and decorating it with 4-acetyl-L-arcanose and D-chalcose sugars.

Semi-synthetic methods can be used to produce derivatives of lankacidin and lankamycin. For example, the natural product lankacidin can be isolated from *Streptomyces rochei* and modified to introduce additional functional groups. Similarly, lankamycin can be isolated and modified to enhance its binding affinity and synergistic effect with lankacidin.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel combination of lankacidin and lankamycin, which act synergistically to inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding to neighboring sites on the ribosome. The invention also encompasses methods for producing and using these antibiotics, as well as chemical derivatives and conjugates thereof. This combination has the potential to be developed into a new class of antibiotics with improved efficacy against bacterial infections.
