## BACKGROUND

Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a severe and often fatal disease primarily affecting premature infants. Characterized by acute inflammation and necrosis of the intestine, NEC leads to overwhelming sepsis and has a mortality rate of approximately 30%. The disease typically develops in the context of premature birth, the administration of formula feeds, and bacterial colonization of the newborn gastrointestinal tract. Current understanding posits that NEC arises from exaggerated signaling of the toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) on the intestinal epithelium, which is more prevalent in premature infants. Despite extensive research, all preventive strategies have focused on postnatal interventions. However, recent evidence suggests that NEC may also have an in utero component, raising the possibility that prenatal interventions could prevent the disease.

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) is a ligand-activated transcription factor that recognizes environmental and dietary ligands, including those found in green leafy vegetables. AHR has been shown to induce immune protection and modulate inflammatory responses. Based on these observations, the present invention explores the hypothesis that maternal-fetal signaling, particularly through AHR activation, can modulate the pathogenesis of NEC and provide a window of opportunity for prenatal prevention.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for preventing and treating necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in premature infants. The invention is based on the discovery that maternal administration of aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonists during pregnancy can prevent NEC in the offspring. The AHR agonists, such as indole-3-carbinol (I3C) and a newly identified compound "A18," activate AHR in the maternal and fetal tissues, leading to reduced TLR4 signaling and inflammation in the neonatal intestinal epithelium. The invention also includes the use of AHR agonists in infant nutritional formulas and breast milk to prevent NEC.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### A. Compounds

The present invention encompasses the use of aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) agonists for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in premature infants. The AHR agonists can be naturally occurring or synthetic compounds. Examples of naturally occurring AHR agonists include indole-3-carbinol (I3C), which is found in cruciferous vegetables such as broccoli and cabbage. Synthetic AHR agonists, such as the compound "A18" (C16H14F3N3O2S), have also been identified and are effective in preventing NEC.

### B. Methods of Treatment

The methods of treatment according to the present invention involve the administration of AHR agonists to the mother during pregnancy and to the neonate after birth. The AHR agonists can be administered orally, intravenously, or through other suitable routes. The dosage and frequency of administration will depend on the specific AHR agonist used and the clinical condition of the mother and infant.

1. **Maternal Administration During Pregnancy:**
   - **Indole-3-Carbinol (I3C):** I3C can be administered to the mother in the form of dietary supplements or enriched foods. The recommended dose is 25 mg per kg body weight per day.
   - **Compound A18:** A18 can be administered to the mother in the form of a pharmaceutical composition. The recommended dose is 300 mg per kg body weight per day.

2. **Neonatal Administration:**
   - **Infant Nutritional Formulas:** AHR agonists can be added to infant formulas to provide a continuous source of AHR activation. The recommended concentration of I3C in the formula is 5%.
   - **Breast Milk:** AHR agonists can be administered to the mother to ensure that the breast milk contains sufficient levels of AHR ligands. The mother can be given I3C or A18 as described above.

### C. Infant Nutritional Formula Comprising Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonists for the Prevention and Treatment of Inflammatory Disorders

The present invention provides infant nutritional formulas that include AHR agonists to prevent and treat inflammatory disorders, particularly necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC). The formulas are designed to be safe and effective for premature infants and can be used as a supplement to or replacement for breast milk.

1. **Composition:**
   - **Base Formula:** The base formula can be a commercially available infant formula, such as Similac Advance or Enfamil.
   - **AHR Agonists:** The formula is enriched with AHR agonists, such as I3C or A18. The recommended concentration of I3C is 5% by weight.
   - **Additional Ingredients:** The formula may also include other nutrients and supplements to support the overall health and development of the infant, such as vitamins, minerals, and probiotics.

2. **Preparation:**
   - The base formula is mixed with the AHR agonist in the specified concentration.
   - The mixture is thoroughly blended to ensure uniform distribution of the AHR agonist.
   - The final product is sterilized and packaged in a suitable container for storage and distribution.

### D. Maternal Administration of an Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonist

The present invention also includes methods for maternal administration of AHR agonists to prevent NEC in the offspring. The AHR agonists can be administered to the mother during pregnancy and lactation to ensure that the fetus and neonate receive sufficient levels of AHR ligands.

1. **Oral Administration:**
   - **I3C:** The mother can be given I3C in the form of dietary supplements or enriched foods. The recommended dose is 25 mg per kg body weight per day.
   - **A18:** The mother can be given A18 in the form of a pharmaceutical composition. The recommended dose is 300 mg per kg body weight per day.

2. **Intravenous Administration:**
   - **A18:** A18 can be administered intravenously to the mother during pregnancy. The recommended dose is 300 mg per kg body weight per day.

### E. Pharmaceutical Compositions and Administration

The present invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AHR agonists for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC). The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, and topical.

1. **Oral Formulations:**
   - **Tablets/Capsules:** The AHR agonist can be formulated into tablets or capsules for oral administration. The tablets or capsules can be coated to improve stability and palatability.
   - **Solutions/Suspensions:** The AHR agonist can be dissolved or suspended in a suitable carrier, such as water or a buffered solution, for oral administration.

2. **Intravenous Formulations:**
   - **Injectable Solutions:** The AHR agonist can be dissolved in a suitable solvent, such as saline or dextrose, to prepare an injectable solution. The solution can be administered intravenously to the mother during pregnancy.

3. **Topical Formulations:**
   - **Creams/Gels:** The AHR agonist can be formulated into creams or gels for topical application. The formulations can be applied to the skin to ensure systemic absorption.

### F. Definitions

- **Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR):** A ligand-activated transcription factor that recognizes environmental and dietary ligands and regulates gene expression.
- **AHR Agonist:** A compound that binds to and activates the aryl hydrocarbon receptor, leading to the induction of downstream gene expression.
- **Indole-3-Carbinol (I3C):** A naturally occurring AHR agonist found in cruciferous vegetables.
- **Compound A18 (C16H14F3N3O2S):** A synthetic AHR agonist identified through a screening process for its ability to activate AHR and reduce TLR4 signaling.
- **Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC):** A severe inflammatory disease of the intestine that primarily affects premature infants.
- **Toll-Like Receptor 4 (TLR4):** A pattern recognition receptor that plays a critical role in the innate immune response and is implicated in the pathogenesis of NEC.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Maternal Administration of I3C During Pregnancy Prevents NEC in Mice**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Animals:** Pregnant C57BL/6 mice were used for the study.
- **Diets:** The control diet was a standard rodent chow. The I3C-enriched diet was supplemented with 25 mg/kg body weight of I3C.
- **Experimental Design:** Mice were fed the control or I3C-enriched diet throughout pregnancy and lactation. At postnatal day 11 (p11), the pups were induced to develop NEC using a well-validated model involving formula feeding and hypoxia exposure.
- **Assessments:** The severity of NEC was assessed by histological examination of the ileum, measurement of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α), and expression of AHR and TLR4.

**Results:**
- **AHR Activation:** I3C administration induced the expression of the AHR response gene Cyp1a1 in the maternal and fetal intestines.
- **NEC Severity:** Pups from I3C-fed mothers showed significantly reduced histological injury, lower NEC severity scores, and decreased expression of IL-6 and TNF-α in the intestinal epithelium compared to pups from control-fed mothers.
- **TLR4 Signaling:** I3C administration significantly reduced the induction of IL-6 and TNF-α in response to LPS in the neonatal mouse intestine.

### Example 2

**AHR Activation on the Intestinal Epithelium Protects Against NEC**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Animals:** Wild-type (WT), Ahr-/-, AhrΔIEC, and AhrΔlys mice were used for the study.
- **Diets:** Pups were fed a standard infant formula or a formula supplemented with I3C (25 mg/kg body weight).
- **Experimental Design:** NEC was induced in the pups using the same model as described in Example 1.
- **Assessments:** The severity of NEC was assessed by histological examination, measurement of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and expression of AHR and TLR4.

**Results:**
- **AHR Expression:** AHR expression was significantly reduced in the small intestine of NEC-affected infants, mice, and piglets compared to controls.
- **NEC Severity:** Ahr-/- and AhrΔIEC mice developed more severe NEC compared to WT mice, while AhrΔlys mice showed no significant difference in NEC severity compared to WT mice.
- **Mechanism:** AHR activation in the intestinal epithelium, but not in leukocytes, was required for the protection against NEC. I3C supplementation significantly reduced NEC severity in WT and AhrΔlys mice but had little effect in Ahr-/- and AhrΔIEC mice.

### Example 3

**Identification of the AHR Ligand A18 and Its Effect on NEC Prevention**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Screening:** A clinical compound library containing FDA-approved drugs was screened for AHR agonists using an AHR-luciferase reporter intestinal epithelial cell line.
- **Lead Compound:** The lead compound, A18 (C16H14F3N3O2S), was identified and characterized for its ability to activate AHR and reduce TLR4 signaling.
- **Animal Studies:** Wild-type and Ahr-/- mice were fed A18 (300 mg/kg body weight) during pregnancy and lactation. NEC was induced in the pups using the same model as described in Example 1.
- **Human Tissue Studies:** Freshly resected human ileal samples from infants undergoing surgery for NEC were treated with A18 and LPS to assess AHR activation and TLR4 signaling.

**Results:**
- **AHR Activation:** A18 induced the expression of Cyp1a1 in wild-type but not Ahr-/- enterocytes and in the intestinal epithelium of wild-type but not Ahr-/- mice.
- **NEC Severity:** A18 administration significantly reduced NEC severity in wild-type mice, as manifest by improved histology, reduced NEC severity scores, and decreased expression of IL-6, TNF-α, and TLR4.
- **Human Tissue:** A18 treatment of human intestinal cultures induced the expression of CYP1A1 and reduced LPS-induced TNF-α and TLR4 expression, confirming its ability to activate AHR and reduce TLR4 signaling in human tissue.

These examples demonstrate the effectiveness of AHR agonists, particularly I3C and A18, in preventing and treating necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in premature infants. The methods and compositions provided by the present invention offer a novel and promising approach to addressing this devastating disease.
