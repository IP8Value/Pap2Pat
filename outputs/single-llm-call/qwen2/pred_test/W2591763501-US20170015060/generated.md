## BACKGROUND

The field of additive manufacturing (AM) has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the realm of 3D printing of composite materials. Traditional composite manufacturing methods often involve labor-intensive processes and can be limited in the complexity of structures they can produce. Direct ink writing (DIW) 3D printing, a form of extrusion-based AM, offers a promising solution by enabling the precise placement and alignment of reinforcing fibers within a polymer matrix. This technology has the potential to revolutionize the production of high-performance composite materials, especially those containing high aspect ratio carbon fibers.

Carbon fiber-reinforced composites (CFRCs) are widely used in industries such as aerospace, automotive, and sports equipment due to their superior mechanical properties, lightweight nature, and durability. However, the alignment and distribution of carbon fibers within these composites are critical factors that influence their performance. Conventional methods for producing CFRCs often result in random fiber orientations, which can limit their mechanical and functional properties. The ability to precisely control fiber alignment and distribution through 3D printing can lead to composites with enhanced mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a method for 3D printing meso-structurally ordered carbon fiber/polymer composites with unprecedented orthotropic physical properties. This method leverages the unique capabilities of DIW 3D printing to create high-resolution structures with controlled fiber alignment, offering a new paradigm in composite material design and fabrication.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for 3D printing meso-structurally ordered carbon fiber/polymer composites with controlled fiber alignment and enhanced physical properties. The method involves the use of a direct ink writing (DIW) 3D printing process to extrude a carbon fiber-loaded ink, which is formulated to exhibit the necessary rheological properties for precise extrusion and rapid solidification. The ink is composed of a bisphenol-F epoxy resin oligomer (BPFE) modified with colloidal silica and dispersed high aspect ratio carbon fibers.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Ink Composition**: The ink comprises a bisphenol-F epoxy resin oligomer (BPFE) modified with colloidal silica and dispersed high aspect ratio carbon fibers. The ink is designed to be extrudable at room temperature and to rapidly solidify upon extrusion, forming self-supporting structures.
2. **Shear Alignment**: During the extrusion process, the high aspect ratio carbon fibers are aligned in the direction of flow, resulting in meso-structurally ordered composite structures.
3. **Controlled Fiber Placement**: The DIW process allows for the precise placement of aligned carbon fiber bundles in the x, y, and z planes, enabling the creation of complex and tailored composite architectures.
4. **Tailored Chemical Crosslinking**: The ink is formulated with a latent thermal cure catalyst, allowing for controlled homopolymerization of the epoxy resin. This ensures that the printed structures achieve full network density and optimal mechanical properties.
5. **Enhanced Physical Properties**: The resulting 3D printed composites exhibit improved mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties compared to conventional random pressed CFRCs. The controlled fiber alignment and distribution enable the composites to have orthotropic properties, making them suitable for a wide range of high-performance applications.

The invention provides a method for producing 3D printed carbon fiber/polymer composites with unprecedented orthotropic physical properties, opening new possibilities for the design and fabrication of advanced composite materials.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF SPECIFIC EMBODIMENTS

### Ink Composition

The ink used in the present invention is a critical component that enables the precise 3D printing of meso-structurally ordered carbon fiber/polymer composites. The ink is formulated to have the necessary rheological properties for extrusion and rapid solidification, ensuring the formation of self-supporting structures during the printing process.

#### Base Resin

The base resin of the ink is a bisphenol-F epoxy resin oligomer (BPFE). This oligomer is chosen for its excellent mechanical and thermal properties, which are essential for high-performance composite applications. The BPFE resin is modified with colloidal silica to impart the desired rheological characteristics. The addition of silica nanoparticles increases the viscosity of the resin, making it suitable for extrusion while maintaining the ability to flow under shear.

#### Carbon Fibers

The ink contains high aspect ratio carbon fibers, which are crucial for achieving the desired mechanical and functional properties of the composite. The carbon fibers used in this invention are grade HTS40, obtained from Toho Tenax (Japan), with a fiber tensile modulus of 240 GPa, 1.8% elongation at break, and a mean density of 1.77 g/cm³. The carbon fibers are dispersed in the resin at a volume fraction of up to 8%, although higher loadings are possible with advanced nozzle geometries.

#### Rheological Properties

The rheological properties of the ink are carefully controlled to ensure that it can be extruded through a micro nozzle and rapidly solidify upon extrusion. The ink exhibits thixotropic behavior, meaning it has a high viscosity at rest but becomes more fluid under shear. This property is essential for the controlled extrusion and placement of the ink during the printing process.

The rheological behavior of the ink is modeled using a Carreau fluid model, which describes the shear rate-dependent response of the ink. The viscosity of the ink at zero shear rate (μ0) is approximately 8000 Pa·s, while the viscosity at infinite shear rate (μinf) is 2.2 Pa·s. The coefficients a and n in the Carreau model are determined to be 45 and 0.1, respectively. These parameters ensure that the ink flows smoothly through the nozzle while maintaining sufficient viscosity to form self-supporting structures.

### Shear Alignment

One of the key advantages of the present invention is the ability to achieve high levels of fiber alignment during the printing process. As the ink is extruded through the nozzle, the high aspect ratio carbon fibers are subjected to shear forces, which cause them to align in the direction of flow. This alignment is critical for enhancing the mechanical and functional properties of the composite.

The alignment of the carbon fibers is influenced by several factors, including the rheological properties of the ink, the geometry of the nozzle, and the extrusion parameters. Computational modeling and simulation have been used to optimize these factors and achieve the desired degree of fiber alignment. The simulations show that the fibers align preferentially in the direction of extrusion, with the alignment becoming more pronounced in the bulk of the fluid domain as the simulation progresses.

### Controlled Fiber Placement

The DIW process allows for the precise placement of aligned carbon fiber bundles in the x, y, and z planes. This capability enables the creation of complex and tailored composite architectures with controlled fiber orientation and distribution. By varying the loading of carbon fibers in different areas of the build and controlling the local orientation, it is possible to introduce orthotropy into the physical properties of the final composite structure.

For example, the method can be used to print structures with controlled orthotropy in electrical conductivity, thermal channeling, and mechanical response. This level of control over the microstructure of the composite opens new possibilities for optimizing the performance of the material for specific applications.

### Tailored Chemical Crosslinking

The ink is formulated with a latent thermal cure catalyst, which allows for controlled homopolymerization of the epoxy resin. The latent cure catalyst is a strong Lewis acid generator, such as boron trifluoride salts, which initiates the ring-opening polymerization of the epoxy groups at elevated temperatures. This approach eliminates the need to combine reactive polymer components immediately before or during the printing process, simplifying the manufacturing process.

The curing process is initiated by heating the printed structure to a temperature of 80°C for 12 hours, followed by a post-cure at 120°C for 24 hours. The latent cure catalyst ensures that the resin remains stable at ambient temperatures, allowing for flexible printing schedules. The rapid curing response at elevated temperatures ensures that the printed structures achieve full network density and optimal mechanical properties.

### Enhanced Physical Properties

The 3D printed carbon fiber/polymer composites produced using the present invention exhibit enhanced mechanical, thermal, and electrical properties compared to conventional random pressed CFRCs. The controlled fiber alignment and distribution enable the composites to have orthotropic properties, which can be tailored for specific applications.

#### Mechanical Properties

Mechanical testing of the 3D printed composites has shown significant improvements in tensile and compressive strength compared to random pressed CFRCs. For example, a 3 vol% 610 μm AMCFRC exhibits a 6% increase in Young's modulus compared to an 8 vol% random pressed part. An 8 vol% AMCFRC has a Young's modulus that is 37% higher than the equivalent volume fraction random pressed part. These improvements are attributed to the preferential alignment of carbon fibers gained through the DIW process, which maximizes the number of fibers contributing to the stress distribution in the desired axis.

#### Thermal and Electrical Properties

The 3D printed composites also exhibit controlled and tunable orthotropic thermal and electrical properties. For instance, a 3 vol% CF loading AMCFRC resin printed in a uniaxial layup at a filamentary print resolution of 250 μm shows a surface conductivity of 1.1 S/cm in the printing direction, which is orders of magnitude higher than the conductivity measured perpendicular to the printing direction (3.7 × 10^-5 S/cm). This directional disparity in conductivity is a result of the highly orthotropic nature of the AMCFRC parts, demonstrating the potential for more complex multi-dimensional control of electrical properties within such composites.
