## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the development of ionic liquid (IL)-based non-aqueous electrolytes for use in nickel/metal hydride (Ni/MH) batteries. These electrolytes are designed to replace conventional 30 wt% KOH aqueous electrolytes, thereby enhancing the energy density and electrochemical performance of the batteries. The invention specifically involves the creation of mixtures of ILs and anhydrous acids, such as acetic acid, to achieve high ionic conductivity and proton conductivity. The resulting electrolytes exhibit a wide electrochemical stability window, making them suitable for use with high-potential electrode materials. The invention also includes methods for preparing and using these electrolytes in Ni/MH batteries, as well as the batteries themselves.

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE

### x is B or Al and

The present invention provides a novel class of electrolytes for use in nickel/metal hydride (Ni/MH) batteries. These electrolytes are based on ionic liquids (ILs) mixed with anhydrous acids, particularly acetic acid. The ILs used in the invention can be represented by the general formula [Cation][Anion], where the cation is selected from the group consisting of 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium (EMIM), 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium (BMIM), 1-ethylimidazolium (EIM), and diethylmethylammonium (DEMA). The anion is selected from the group consisting of trifluoromethanesulfonate (TfO), bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (TFSI), and acetate (Ac).

### X is B or Al and

The anhydrous acid used in the invention is acetic acid (CH3COOH). The acid is added to the IL to enhance the ionic conductivity and proton conductivity of the electrolyte. The concentration of acetic acid in the electrolyte can range from 0.5 M to 6 M, with a preferred concentration of 2 M. The addition of acetic acid reduces the viscosity of the IL, thereby increasing the ionic conductivity and facilitating the diffusion of protons through the electrolyte.

The metal hydride (MH) anode used in the Ni/MH batteries of the invention can be composed of various alloys, including but not limited to AB5, AR3, and BCC-B08. The AB5 alloy has a composition of La10.5Ce4.3Pr0.5Nd1.4Ni60.0Co12.7Mn5.9Al4.7. The AR3 alloy is an MgNi-based AB type MH alloy with a composition of Mg52Ni39Co3Mn6. The BCC-B08 alloy has a bcc crystal structure and a composition of Ti40V30Cr15Mn13Mo2. These alloys are chosen for their ability to store and release hydrogen efficiently, which is essential for the operation of the battery.

### X is B or Al and

The positive electrode in the Ni/MH batteries of the invention is composed of sintered Ni(OH)2. The Ni(OH)2 is fabricated in-house and is used as a standard positive electrode for Ni/MH battery research. The positive electrode is cut into 1.25 cm (diameter) circular disks and is baked at 115°C in a vacuum oven for 12 hours before cell assembly to ensure the removal of any residual moisture.

### X is B or Al and

The electrolyte is prepared by mixing the selected IL with anhydrous acetic acid. The IL is first baked at 115°C in a vacuum oven for 48 hours to remove any residual water. The water content of the IL is then determined to be less than 1000 ppm using a Metrohm 831 KF coulometer. The acetic acid is added to the IL in the desired concentration, and the mixture is stirred until homogeneous. The ionic conductivity of the resulting electrolyte is measured using a YSI Model 3200 conductivity meter at room temperature (25°C).

### X is B or Al and

The Ni/MH battery cells are assembled in an Ar-filled glove box. The negative electrode (anode) is composed of the selected MH alloy, which is pressed onto a 1.25 cm (diameter) circular Ni mesh current collector without any binder or additive. The positive electrode is the sintered Ni(OH)2 disk. A standard non-woven separator (0.2 mm thick) is placed between the electrodes, and 1 mL of the prepared electrolyte is injected into the cell before sealing. The cell is then subjected to electrochemical testing.

The electrochemical performance of the Ni/MH batteries using the IL-based electrolytes is evaluated using a combination of cyclic voltammetry (CV) and charge/discharge tests. The CV measurements are conducted using a Gamry's Interface 1000 potentiostat/galvanostat in a three-electrode Swagelok cell. The working electrode is the MH alloy anode, the counter electrode is a sintered Ni(OH)2 disk, and a leak-free microminiature reference electrode (Ag/AgCl, 0.242 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode) is used. The charge/discharge tests are performed using an Arbin BT-2143 battery test station with a constant charge/discharge current.

## EXAMPLES

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Acetic Acid

In one embodiment, the electrolyte is prepared by mixing 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate ([EMIM][Ac]) with 2 M acetic acid. The [EMIM][Ac] is baked at 115°C in a vacuum oven for 48 hours to remove residual water. The water content is determined to be less than 1000 ppm using a Metrohm 831 KF coulometer. The acetic acid is then added to the [EMIM][Ac] and the mixture is stirred until homogeneous. The ionic conductivity of the resulting electrolyte is measured to be approximately 10^-2 S•cm^-1 at room temperature (25°C).

### Example 5

In a further example, the Ni/MH battery cell is assembled using the 2 M acetic acid/[EMIM][Ac] electrolyte. The negative electrode is composed of an AB5 alloy, which is pressed onto a 1.25 cm (diameter) circular Ni mesh current collector without any binder or additive. The positive electrode is a sintered Ni(OH)2 disk. A standard non-woven separator (0.2 mm thick) is placed between the electrodes, and 1 mL of the 2 M acetic acid/[EMIM][Ac] electrolyte is injected into the cell before sealing. The cell is then subjected to electrochemical testing.

The electrochemical performance of the cell is evaluated using a combination of cyclic voltammetry (CV) and charge/discharge tests. The CV measurements are conducted using a Gamry's Interface 1000 potentiostat/galvanostat in a three-electrode Swagelok cell. The working electrode is the AB5 alloy anode, the counter electrode is a sintered Ni(OH)2 disk, and a leak-free microminiature reference electrode (Ag/AgCl, 0.242 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode) is used. The charge/discharge tests are performed using an Arbin BT-2143 battery test station with a constant charge/discharge current of 4 mA•g^-1. The cell is charged to a capacity of 40 mAh•g^-1 and discharged at the same current density until a cut-off voltage of 0.7 V is reached.

The initial specific capacity is low, and several cycles are required to activate the cell. After activation, the capacity increases gradually until a maximum of 28.6 mAh•g^-1 is attained at the 25th cycle, after which the capacity slightly degrades to reach 23.9 mAh•g^-1 at the 100th cycle. The charge/discharge curves show that there is a very high overpotential for the first charge process, and the discharge capacity is close to zero. At the 10th cycle, the charge/discharge overpotentials are dramatically decreased, with charge and discharge plateaus at 1.8 V and 1.1 V, respectively. The lowest charge/discharge overpotentials and highest discharge capacities are observed at the 20th and 30th cycles, with charge and discharge plateaus at 1.6 V and 1.2 V, respectively. At the 100th cycle, the charge/discharge overpotentials increase slightly, and the discharge capacity decreases as a consequence of cell degradation, typically as a result of MH anode pulverization/disintegration or corrosion.

The invention thus provides a novel class of IL-based non-aqueous electrolytes for use in Ni/MH batteries, which offer enhanced ionic conductivity, proton conductivity, and electrochemical performance. The use of these electrolytes enables the utilization of high-potential electrode materials, leading to a significant increase in the energy density of the batteries.
