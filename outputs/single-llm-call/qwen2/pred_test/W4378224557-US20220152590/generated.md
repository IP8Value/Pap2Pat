## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of catalytic materials and, more specifically, to a novel catalyst for the oxidation of methane. The invention addresses the challenges of water inhibition and sulfur poisoning, which are common issues in the operation of lean burn natural gas engines. The catalyst comprises palladium (Pd) and platinum (Pt) supported on a lanthanum-doped alumina support, which exhibits enhanced hydrothermal stability and sulfur resistance.

## BACKGROUND

Natural gas (NG) is increasingly being used as a fuel in the transition toward a zero-carbon economy, particularly in the heavy-duty (lean burn engine) segment. It is abundant, inexpensive, and produces approximately 20-25% less greenhouse gas (GHG) on a lifecycle basis compared to diesel and gasoline engines. However, lean burn NG engines produce high levels of unburned methane (methane slip) in the exhaust, which is a potent GHG with a global warming potential of about 86 times that of CO2 over a 20-year period. Methane is difficult to oxidize due to its strong C-H bonds, requiring temperatures above 1000°C for complete combustion. Catalytic combustion is an effective method to reduce methane emissions at lower temperatures, and Pd-based catalysts are known for their high activity in this regard. However, these catalysts are prone to water inhibition and sulfur poisoning, which significantly reduces their effectiveness over time.

Water inhibition is caused by the formation of stable Pd(OH)2 groups and the blocking of O interchange between the support and Pd active sites by OH groups. Sulfur poisoning occurs when SO2 reacts with Pd to form inactive palladium sulfur oxide species (Pd(SOx)), which decreases the overall catalytic activity. To address these issues, various researchers have explored the use of hydrophobic supports, such as mesoporous silica (SBA-15), and the addition of Pt or Zr to Pd-containing catalysts to improve sulfur tolerance and hydrothermal stability.

Despite these advancements, there remains a need for a catalyst that can effectively oxidize methane under real-world conditions, which include the presence of water vapor and sulfur compounds in the exhaust of lean burn NG vehicles (NGVs). The present invention aims to provide such a catalyst by combining Pd and Pt on a lanthanum-doped alumina support, which offers enhanced hydrothermal stability and sulfur resistance.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel catalyst for the oxidation of methane, which is particularly suitable for use in lean burn natural gas engines. The catalyst comprises palladium (Pd) and platinum (Pt) supported on a lanthanum-doped alumina support. The lanthanum-doped alumina support enhances the hydrothermal stability and sulfur resistance of the catalyst, making it highly effective in reducing methane slip under real-world conditions.

In one embodiment, the catalyst comprises:
- Palladium (Pd) in an amount ranging from about 1 wt% to about 5 wt%;
- Platinum (Pt) in an amount ranging from about 2 wt% to about 6 wt%; and
- Lanthanum-doped alumina support, wherein the lanthanum content ranges from about 1 wt% to about 10 wt%.

The catalyst exhibits enhanced activity and stability, as demonstrated by its ability to maintain high methane conversion rates even after prolonged exposure to water vapor and sulfur compounds. The lanthanum-doped alumina support effectively traps sulfur species, preventing them from deactivating the Pd and Pt active sites. Additionally, the presence of Pt improves the reducibility of Pd, further enhancing the overall catalytic performance.

The invention also includes methods for preparing the catalyst and using it in the oxidation of methane. The catalyst can be prepared by impregnating a lanthanum-doped alumina support with aqueous solutions of palladium and platinum precursors, followed by drying and calcination steps. The catalyst is then tested for its activity and stability under conditions simulating the exhaust of lean burn NG engines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Catalyst Composition

The catalyst of the present invention comprises palladium (Pd) and platinum (Pt) supported on a lanthanum-doped alumina support. The lanthanum-doped alumina support is prepared by impregnating alumina with an aqueous solution of lanthanum nitrate, followed by drying and calcination. The lanthanum content in the support ranges from about 1 wt% to about 10 wt%, preferably from about 3 wt% to about 7 wt%.

The palladium (Pd) content in the catalyst ranges from about 1 wt% to about 5 wt%, preferably from about 2 wt% to about 4 wt%. The platinum (Pt) content ranges from about 2 wt% to about 6 wt%, preferably from about 3 wt% to about 5 wt%. The total metal content (Pd + Pt) in the catalyst is typically about 6 wt%.

### Catalyst Preparation

The catalyst is prepared through a multi-step process involving the preparation of the lanthanum-doped alumina support, followed by the impregnation of the support with palladium and platinum precursors, and finally, drying and calcination steps.

#### Preparation of Lanthanum-Doped Alumina Support

1. **Impregnation**: Alumina is impregnated with an aqueous solution of lanthanum nitrate to achieve the desired lanthanum content. The impregnation is performed using the incipient wetness method, where the support is slowly added to the solution until it is fully saturated.
2. **Drying**: The impregnated support is dried at a temperature of about 120°C for about 16 hours to remove excess water.
3. **Calcination**: The dried support is calcined at a temperature of about 550°C for about 6 hours to decompose the lanthanum nitrate and form lanthanum-doped alumina.

#### Impregnation with Palladium and Platinum Precursors

1. **Impregnation**: The lanthanum-doped alumina support is impregnated with aqueous solutions of palladium nitrate and tetraammineplatinum nitrate to achieve the desired Pd and Pt contents. The impregnation is performed using the incipient wetness method.
2. **Drying**: The impregnated support is dried at a temperature of about 120°C for about 16 hours to remove excess water.
3. **Calcination**: The dried support is calcined at a temperature of about 550°C for about 6 hours to decompose the metal precursors and form the active catalyst.

### Catalyst Characterization

The catalyst is characterized using various techniques to determine its physico-chemical properties and performance in the oxidation of methane.

#### Textural Properties

The textural properties of the catalyst, including specific surface area, pore volume, and pore size distribution, are measured using nitrogen adsorption-desorption isotherms. The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method is used to determine the specific surface area, while the Barrett-Joyner-Halenda (BJH) method is used to calculate the pore volume and pore size distribution.

#### X-ray Diffraction (XRD)

XRD analysis is performed to identify the crystalline phases present in the catalyst. The XRD patterns are recorded using a Rigaku Ultima IV XRD spectrometer over the angular range of 5-95° 2θ. The average crystallite size is calculated using the Scherrer equation.

#### X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)

XPS analysis is performed to determine the surface composition and oxidation states of the metals in the catalyst. The XPS spectra are recorded using a Kratos Axis Ultra XPS device equipped with a monochromated Al X-ray source. The high-resolution spectra are analyzed using CasaXPS data processing software.

#### Temperature-Programmed Reduction (TPR)

TPR analysis is performed to study the reducibility of the catalyst. The TPR profiles are obtained using a Micromeritics AutoChem II 2920 Analyzer equipped with a thermal conductivity detector. The catalyst is pretreated in a helium flow at 300°C for 30 minutes, cooled to room temperature, and then heated to 900°C at a rate of 10°C/min in a 10 vol% H2 and 90 vol% Ar gas mixture.

### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalytic activity of the catalyst is evaluated using a fixed-bed flow reactor system. The reactor consists of a vertical quartz tube enclosed in a temperature-controlled furnace. The catalyst is pelletized and sieved to a particle size of 125-180 µm before testing. The feed gas composition simulates the exhaust of a lean burn NG engine and consists of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, 10 ppm SO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min.

#### Light-Off Curves

The light-off curves are obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs, where the furnace temperature is raised from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min. The methane conversion is monitored as a function of temperature to determine the T50 (temperature at which 50% methane conversion is achieved).

#### Aging Tests

The catalyst is aged for 40 hours at 500°C using the same feed gas composition. The aged catalyst is then tested for its activity and stability using light-off curves. The T50 values of the fresh and aged catalysts are compared to evaluate the catalyst's long-term performance.

### Advantages of the Catalyst

The catalyst of the present invention offers several advantages over existing catalysts for the oxidation of methane:

1. **Enhanced Hydrothermal Stability**: The lanthanum-doped alumina support provides excellent hydrothermal stability, allowing the catalyst to maintain high activity even in the presence of water vapor.
2. **Improved Sulfur Resistance**: The lanthanum-doped alumina support effectively traps sulfur species, preventing them from deactivating the Pd and Pt active sites. This results in sustained catalytic activity under real-world conditions.
3. **High Activity and Stability**: The combination of Pd and Pt on the lanthanum-doped alumina support enhances the reducibility of Pd, leading to higher catalytic activity and stability.
4. **Practical Application**: The catalyst is suitable for practical application in reducing methane slip from lean burn natural gas engines, contributing to the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Catalysts with Pd and Pt on a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Exhibit Enhanced Methane Conversion after Aging in the Presence of Sulfur and Water

#### Catalyst Preparation

Catalysts were prepared by impregnating lanthanum-doped alumina with aqueous solutions of palladium nitrate and tetraammineplatinum nitrate. The lanthanum content in the support was 5 wt%, and the Pd and Pt contents in the catalyst were 2 wt% and 4 wt%, respectively. The impregnated support was dried at 120°C for 16 hours and calcined at 550°C for 6 hours.

#### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalyst was tested in a fixed-bed flow reactor system using a feed gas composition of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, 10 ppm SO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min. The light-off curves were obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min. The T50 value of the fresh catalyst was 450°C.

The catalyst was then aged for 40 hours at 500°C using the same feed gas composition. The aged catalyst was tested for its activity and stability using light-off curves. The T50 value of the aged catalyst was 481°C, indicating minimal loss of activity after aging.

### Example 2: A Catalyst with a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Support Exhibits Higher Activity in the Presence of Excess Water Vapour than a Catalyst with an Alumina Support not Doped with Lanthanum

#### Catalyst Preparation

Two catalysts were prepared: one with a lanthanum-doped alumina support and one with an undoped alumina support. Both catalysts had Pd and Pt contents of 2 wt% and 4 wt%, respectively. The lanthanum content in the doped support was 5 wt%. The impregnation, drying, and calcination steps were the same as described in Example 1.

#### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalysts were tested in a fixed-bed flow reactor system using a feed gas composition of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min. The light-off curves were obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min.

The T50 value of the catalyst with the lanthanum-doped alumina support was 450°C, while the T50 value of the catalyst with the undoped alumina support was 500°C. This result demonstrates the enhanced hydrothermal stability of the catalyst with the lanthanum-doped alumina support.

### Example 3: Sulfur Resistance and Hydrothermal Stability of Catalysts Having an Alumina Support Doped with Lanthanum at Different Weight Ratios of Platinum and Palladium

#### Catalyst Preparation

Three catalysts were prepared with different weight ratios of platinum and palladium on a lanthanum-doped alumina support. The lanthanum content in the support was 5 wt%. The catalysts had the following compositions:
- Catalyst A: 2 wt% Pd and 4 wt% Pt
- Catalyst B: 3 wt% Pd and 3 wt% Pt
- Catalyst C: 4 wt% Pd and 2 wt% Pt

The impregnation, drying, and calcination steps were the same as described in Example 1.

#### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalysts were tested in a fixed-bed flow reactor system using a feed gas composition of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, 10 ppm SO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min. The light-off curves were obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min.

The T50 values of the fresh catalysts were as follows:
- Catalyst A: 450°C
- Catalyst B: 460°C
- Catalyst C: 470°C

The catalysts were then aged for 40 hours at 500°C using the same feed gas composition. The aged catalysts were tested for their activity and stability using light-off curves. The T50 values of the aged catalysts were as follows:
- Catalyst A: 481°C
- Catalyst B: 490°C
- Catalyst C: 500°C

These results demonstrate that the catalyst with a 2 wt% Pd and 4 wt% Pt composition (Catalyst A) exhibited the highest sulfur resistance and hydrothermal stability.

### Example 4: Effect of Calcination Temperature on Catalyst Activity

#### Catalyst Preparation

Catalysts were prepared by impregnating lanthanum-doped alumina with aqueous solutions of palladium nitrate and tetraammineplatinum nitrate. The lanthanum content in the support was 5 wt%, and the Pd and Pt contents in the catalyst were 2 wt% and 4 wt%, respectively. The impregnated support was dried at 120°C for 16 hours and calcined at different temperatures: 450°C, 550°C, and 650°C for 6 hours.

#### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalysts were tested in a fixed-bed flow reactor system using a feed gas composition of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, 10 ppm SO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min. The light-off curves were obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min.

The T50 values of the catalysts calcined at different temperatures were as follows:
- 450°C: 470°C
- 550°C: 450°C
- 650°C: 460°C

These results demonstrate that the catalyst calcined at 550°C exhibited the highest activity, likely due to the optimal decomposition of the metal precursors and the formation of active metal species.

### Example 5: Effect of Method Preparation on Catalyst Activity

#### Catalyst Preparation

Catalysts were prepared using two different methods: co-impregnation and sequential impregnation. In the co-impregnation method, the lanthanum-doped alumina support was impregnated with a single solution containing both palladium nitrate and tetraammineplatinum nitrate. In the sequential impregnation method, the support was first impregnated with palladium nitrate, dried, and calcined, followed by impregnation with tetraammineplatinum nitrate, drying, and calcination. The lanthanum content in the support was 5 wt%, and the Pd and Pt contents in the catalyst were 2 wt% and 4 wt%, respectively.

#### Catalytic Activity Tests

The catalysts were tested in a fixed-bed flow reactor system using a feed gas composition of 1 vol% CH4, 10 vol% O2, 6 vol% CO2, 10 ppm SO2, and 10 vol% H2O in a balance of N2 supplied at 500 mL/min. The light-off curves were obtained by conducting temperature gradient runs from 75°C to 600°C at a rate of 3°C/min.

The T50 values of the catalysts prepared by the two methods were as follows:
- Co-impregnation: 450°C
- Sequential impregnation: 460°C

These results demonstrate that the co-impregnation method resulted in a catalyst with higher activity, likely due to better dispersion and interaction of the Pd and Pt species on the support.
