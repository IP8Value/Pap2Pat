# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The present invention relates to a method for enhancing the efficiency of multicrystalline silicon (mc-Si) solar cells through a combination of reactive ion etching (RIE) texturing and damage recovery etching (DRE). Solar cells are critical components in renewable energy systems, and their efficiency directly impacts the overall performance and economic viability of these systems. One of the primary challenges in improving solar cell efficiency is reducing the reflection and transmission losses of incident light. Traditional methods such as isotropic wet acidic etching have been widely used but suffer from limitations in achieving extremely low reflectance and optimal light coupling.

Reactive Ion Etching (RIE) has emerged as a promising technique for texturing mc-Si wafers due to its ability to create highly textured surfaces with low reflectance. However, RIE can introduce surface damage, leading to a high surface recombination velocity and a reduction in cell efficiency. To address this issue, the present invention incorporates a damage recovery etching (DRE) step using a mixture of nitric acid (HNO₃) and hydrofluoric acid (HF) to remove the plasma-induced damage while maintaining the low reflectance properties of the RIE-textured surface.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for fabricating high-efficiency multicrystalline silicon (mc-Si) solar cells. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Initial Texturing**: Isotropic texturing of mc-Si wafers using acidic solutions to create a preliminary textured surface.
2. **Reactive Ion Etching (RIE)**: Further texturing of the acid-treated wafers using RIE to create a highly textured surface with low reflectance.
3. **Damage Recovery Etching (DRE)**: Treating the RIE-textured wafers with a mixture of HNO₃ and HF to remove plasma-induced damage and optimize the surface morphology.
4. **Selective Emitter Formation**: Depositing a SiO₂ layer as a diffusion barrier mask, patterning it with etch paste, and performing a two-step diffusion process using POCl₃ to create a selective emitter structure.
5. **Antireflective Coating**: Depositing a silicon nitride (SiNx) layer on the emitter to enhance light trapping and reduce reflection.
6. **Contact Formation**: Screen-printing silver (Ag) and aluminum (Al) pastes onto the front and back of the wafers, respectively, and cofiring them in a lamp-heated belt furnace.
7. **Final Processing**: Isolating the cells using a laser and testing them with a solar simulator to evaluate their performance.

The method significantly improves the short-circuit current density (I_sc), open-circuit voltage (V_oc), fill factor (FF), and overall conversion efficiency of the mc-Si solar cells. Specifically, the invention achieves a conversion efficiency of 17.6% using an industrial screen printing process with a selective emitter structure, demonstrating a substantial improvement over traditional acidic-textured solar cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EXEMPLARY EMBODIMENTS

### Initial Texturing

The process begins with the isotropic texturing of multicrystalline silicon (mc-Si) wafers. The wafers, typically measuring 156 × 156 mm², are boron-doped p-type with a resistivity of 1.9 ohm•cm and a thickness of 200 μm. The wafers are treated with acidic solutions to create a preliminary textured surface. This step is crucial for preparing the wafers for subsequent RIE texturing and ensures a uniform base texture.

### Reactive Ion Etching (RIE)

After the initial texturing, the acid-treated wafers are subjected to reactive ion etching (RIE). RIE is performed in a plasma reactor, where the wafers are exposed to a plasma containing reactive species that etch the silicon surface. The RIE process creates a highly textured surface with deep and dense features, resulting in a significant reduction in reflectance. The textured surface appears very dark due to the presence of numerous nanosized features, which enhance light trapping and absorption.

### Damage Recovery Etching (DRE)

One of the key challenges with RIE texturing is the introduction of surface damage, which can lead to a high surface recombination velocity and a reduction in cell efficiency. To mitigate this issue, the RIE-textured wafers undergo damage recovery etching (DRE). DRE involves treating the wafers with a mixture of nitric acid (HNO₃) and hydrofluoric acid (HF). The etching time is carefully controlled to remove the plasma-induced damage while preserving the low reflectance properties of the RIE-textured surface. Optimal DRE times of 20 and 30 seconds have been found to yield the best results, as they balance the removal of surface damage with the maintenance of the textured morphology.

### Selective Emitter Formation

To further enhance the performance of the mc-Si solar cells, a selective emitter structure is formed. A SiO₂ layer is deposited on the textured surface as a diffusion barrier mask. The mask is then patterned using etch paste to create areas for heavy doping. The wafers are doped in a conventional tube furnace using POCl₃ as the dopant source. This two-step diffusion process creates a shallow diffused region with a sheet resistance of 20 Ω/□ under the contact fingers and a higher sheet resistance of 80 Ω/□ between the fingers. The phosphorous silicate glass (PSG) and remaining SiO₂ are removed using a 10% HF solution.

### Antireflective Coating

An antireflective coating is applied to the emitter to further reduce reflection and enhance light trapping. A silicon nitride (SiNx) layer is deposited using plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD). The refractive index and thickness of the SiNx layer are optimized to 2.06 and 85 nm, respectively, to achieve the best antireflective properties.

### Contact Formation

Silver (Ag) and aluminum (Al) pastes are screen-printed onto the front and back of the wafers, respectively. The pastes are then cofired in a lamp-heated belt furnace to form the contacts. The Ag paste forms the front contacts, while the Al paste forms the back contacts. The cofiring process ensures good adhesion and electrical conductivity of the contacts.

### Final Processing

The completed solar cells are isolated using a laser to ensure proper electrical isolation. The cells are then tested using a solar simulator to evaluate their performance. The results show a significant improvement in the short-circuit current density (I_sc), open-circuit voltage (V_oc), fill factor (FF), and overall conversion efficiency. Specifically, the optimized mc-Si solar cells with RIE texturing and selective emitter structure achieve a conversion efficiency of 17.6%, which is a substantial improvement over traditional acidic-textured solar cells.

### Performance Evaluation

The performance of the mc-Si solar cells fabricated using the method of the present invention is evaluated through various tests. The external quantum efficiency (EQE) measurements demonstrate that the RIE-textured cells have improved quantum efficiency, particularly for wavelengths below 600 nm. This improvement is attributed to the low reflectance and the absence of plasma-induced damage, which enhances the light absorption and carrier collection efficiency.

### Conclusion

The method of the present invention provides a robust and effective approach for fabricating high-efficiency multicrystalline silicon (mc-Si) solar cells. By combining RIE texturing with damage recovery etching (DRE) and incorporating a selective emitter structure, the invention achieves a significant improvement in cell performance. The optimized mc-Si solar cells exhibit a conversion efficiency of 17.6%, making them highly suitable for industrial-scale production and deployment in renewable energy systems.
