# DESCRIPTION

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The present invention relates to a microfluidic device and method for performing precise operations on microdroplets without the need for precise control over the rate of flow or the timing of switching the flow on and off. The device comprises a series of geometric modules, each designed to perform a specific function on the microdroplets, such as metering, merging, locking, and delaying. These modules are integrated into a network that can execute a sequence of operations in a passive manner, making the device suitable for point-of-care diagnostics, screening, and in-field testing.

The metering trap is a key component of the device. It is designed to precisely measure and lock a specific volume of liquid. The trap consists of a main channel with two side bypasses that are milled to 1/4 of the depth of the main channel. At the terminus of the trap, a barrier extends upwards to 3/4 of the height of the main channel. When a droplet shorter than the length of the trap flows into the module, it is locked in place due to the increased Laplace back-pressure. The continuous liquid bypasses the droplet through the side channels, allowing the droplet to remain stationary. When a droplet longer than the trap arrives, the rear of the droplet blocks the entrance to the bypasses, causing the continuous liquid to push the droplet forward until the rear aligns with the entrance to the trap. At this point, the droplet stops, and the Rayleigh-Plateau instability breaks the liquid neck at the front barrier, leaving a precisely metered volume locked in the trap.

### Example 2

The merging trap is another essential module in the microfluidic device. It is designed to merge two droplets and release the merged droplet for further processing. The merging trap includes the same bypasses as the metering trap and a set of side intrusions at both termini. These intrusions create an obstacle that increases the Laplace back-pressure, similar to the barrier in the metering trap. However, the neck of the droplet squeezed through the intrusions is less deformed and more stable against Rayleigh-Plateau instability. This stability prevents the droplet from breaking when pushed out of the trap.

The operation of the merging trap can be described as follows: a droplet longer than the trap passes through it because it continues to block the inlets to the bypasses when its front meets the intrusions at the outlet of the trap. A droplet shorter than the trap is locked once its front approaches the side intrusions at the outlet of the trap because the continuous liquid flows by via the bypasses. When a second droplet flows into the trap, it flows all the way down to the first drop because the continuous liquid can be expelled from between the two drops via the bypasses. This allows the drops to merge. If the merged droplet is shorter than the trap, it remains trapped. If it is longer than the trap, its rear blocks the entrance to the bypasses, leading to a build-up of pressure drop along the droplet and pushing it through the front barrier. This, in turn, blocks the outlet of the bypasses, causing a complete release of the merged droplet from the trap.

### Example 3

The "lock&shift" trap is designed to hold droplets in well-specified positions and to allow the replacement of trapped droplets without the risk of cross-contamination. The main difference between the metering trap and the lock&shift trap is that in the latter, the bypasses are connected to the main channel only at the entrance and exit of the trap, while being separated from the main channel along the length of the trap. The lock&shift module can be designed with two different types of obstacles at the end of the trap: one with a barrier similar to the one used in the metering trap or second, with two side intrusions, as in the merging trap.

The trap locks short droplets: when a droplet flows into the trap, it is pushed towards the barrier and is stopped there by the increased back-acting Laplace pressure. The continuous liquid bypasses the droplet through the parallel channels, decreasing the pressure drop along the droplet. This functionality allows the trap to hold a single droplet and release it upon the approach of a consecutive drop, ensuring that consecutive droplets never touch each other.

### Example 4

The delay trap is designed to reduce the speed of the droplet while it is in the trap. The geometry of the delay trap is similar to the metering trap but does not have any barriers at its termini. The parallel slits facilitate the bypassing of the droplet by the continuous liquid, decreasing the pressure drop along the droplet and slowing down its forward motion. We tested traps that allowed up to a 2-fold reduction in the speed of the droplet while maintaining the same rate of flow of the continuous liquid.

### Example 5

An integrated serial dilutor I is a system that uses a combination of metering, delay, and lock&shift traps to generate an array of droplets presenting serial dilutions of an input solution. The device consists of three main parts: (1) a part that transforms a continuous stream of buffer into 10 precisely metered drops locked in the metering traps, (2) a metering trap for trapping the sample and later to release dilutions, and (3) an array of 10 lock&shift traps to array the dilutions.

The execution of the protocol comprises three steps: First, the continuous liquid is flowed from inlet in1 to outlet out1 together with an amount of solvent from inlet inSol. The continuous phase pushes a portion of the solvent through the array of metering traps, leaving precisely metered aliquots of volume V in them. Then, the continuous liquid is flowed from inlet in1 to outlet outSam, and a portion of the sample is introduced from inSam to meter and lock a precisely metered volume of sample in the metering trap. Finally, the flow of the continuous liquid from inlet in2 to outlet out2 pushes the drops of buffer into the metering trap containing the sample. The drops, in sequence, merge with the droplet in the sample trap and release an equal volume of the current concentration. While the content of the droplet homogenizes, the released droplet travels to the array of lock&shift traps. Running the whole sequence of droplets through the system results in the generation of an array of well-positioned, metered droplets presenting an exponential decay of the concentration of the sample.

### Example 6

The integrated serial dilutor II, or the DOMINO system, is designed to generate a sequence of serial dilutions without any stringent requirements on the speed of flow of the liquids. The system consists of a series of metering and merging traps. The merging trap is first filled with a droplet of solvent of volume V by passing this droplet from right to left (from a metering trap outside the micrograph). Then, a droplet of the sample (e.g., a solution of dye) of volume 2V is passed from left to right through the metering trap. The metering trap locks a droplet of volume V of the sample and passes the remaining volume (also V) into the merging trap. The droplet coalesces with the drop of solvent and is released to mix by convection inside the droplet. The concentration of the output droplet is decreased after mixing with the droplet of solvent, and the resulting concentration depends only on the ratio of volumes of both mixed droplets, which is fixed by the design of the device.

The device is operated in three steps: In the first step, the flow of the carrier liquid is introduced from inlet in1 to outlet out1, and a sufficiently large aliquot of solvent is injected from inlet inSol. The continuous liquid pushes the droplet of solvent through the array of traps, leaving precisely metered aliquots of volume V in the metering traps. In the second step, the flow of the continuous liquid is switched on from inlet in2 to outlet out2, pushing the drops of solvent from the metering traps to the merging traps where they are locked. During this phase, the sample is introduced from inlet inSam, and the flow of the continuous liquid locks the precisely metered volume (2V) of sample (of concentration C0) in the metering trap. In the last step, the flow is started from inlet in1 to outlet out1, driving the droplet of the sample through the array of traps. In the first metering trap, a drop (of volume V) of sample is trapped, passing the remaining (V) to the merging trap where it coalesces with the drop (V) of solvent. The merged droplet is carried (and simultaneously mixed to a homogeneous concentration C1 = C0 * V / (V + V) = C0 / 2) to the next metering trap. Passing through each subsequent set of a metering and merging trap creates a two-fold dilution locked in the metering trap. After the whole protocol is completed, the device presents an array of precisely metered dilutions.

### Example 7

The precision of the operations performed by the microfluidic device is confirmed by measurements of the standard deviation of the volume of the droplets locked in the metering traps. At a fixed rate of flow, the normalized standard deviation of the trapped volumes does not exceed 3%. Within an approximately 3-fold range of rates of flow (e.g., between 100 and 300 μL/h or 300 and 750 μL/h), the same parameter does not exceed 5%. These results demonstrate that even a very loose control of the rate of inflow of liquids results in precise execution of the protocols hard-wired into the geometry of the chip.

### Example 8

The microfluidic device and method of the present invention offer several advantages over existing microfluidic systems. The device can perform complex liquid handling and chemical protocols in a small and user-friendly format, making it suitable for point-of-care diagnostics, screening, and in-field testing. The device is designed to be operated as simply as possible, requiring only the deposition of samples onto the chip and the use of gravity to drive the liquids through the traps. The modules can be freely arranged into functional networks, allowing for the construction of a broad range of systems that can be operated in a passive manner.

### Example 9

The fabrication of the polycarbonate microfluidic chips is straightforward and can be achieved using a CNC milling machine. The milled pieces of polycarbonate are thermally bonded to neat 2-mm plates by compressing them together. The world-to-device interface is created using 21-gauge needles, and the device inlets are connected to syringes driven by syringe pumps via PE tubing. Hexadecane is used as the continuous phase, and clear distilled water or distilled water dyed by ink is used as the droplet phase. The device can be easily modified and expanded for a combinatorial number of functions, making it a versatile platform for various applications in diagnostics, microbiology, and molecular biology.
