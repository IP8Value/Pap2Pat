# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and, more specifically, to the production of high-quality virus-like particles (VLPs) using a yeast expression system. The invention particularly focuses on optimizing the carbon source concentration in the culture medium to enhance the antigenicity and immunogenicity of human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV16) L1 VLPs.

## BACKGROUND ART

The rapid advancements in genetic engineering and recombinant protein production have led to the development of subunit vaccines that utilize monomeric proteins derived from pathogens. Additionally, peptide vaccines using synthetic pathogen epitopes have been explored, although they pose significant challenges. Despite these advancements, both subunit and peptide vaccines often exhibit low immunogenicity and limited ability to elicit neutralizing antibodies. This is primarily attributed to the low levels of pathogen-specific complexes and tertiary structures in these vaccines.

Virus-like particles (VLPs) offer a promising alternative. VLPs are multimeric protein complexes that resemble naturally occurring virions but lack viral genetic material, making them non-infectious and safer than conventional inactivated or attenuated vaccines. VLPs possess capsid-specific neutralizing epitopes and a highly ordered structure, which results from the assembly of their subunit proteins. These repetitive conformational epitopes on the surface of VLPs stimulate antigen-presenting cells (APCs) more effectively than monomeric or disassembled antigens. Furthermore, their shape and size (20 – 100 nm) facilitate preferential uptake by APCs, enhancing their ability to stimulate the immune system and evoke protective immunity.

Saccharomyces cerevisiae (S. cerevisiae) is a preferred expression system for producing VLPs due to its low production costs and the ability to produce VLPs of the human hepatitis B virus (HBV) and human papillomavirus (HPV). However, VLPs produced in this system often exhibit low structural stability, which correlates with decreased antigenicity and immunogenicity. Various in vitro strategies, such as redox refolding, VLP maturation, and salt treatment, have been employed to improve VLP quality. Nevertheless, insufficient attention has been given to optimizing the quality of VLPs during cell culture, despite the critical role of intracellular assembly in VLP bioprocessing.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the optimization of the carbon source concentration in the culture medium to enhance the antigenicity and immunogenicity of HPV16 L1 VLPs produced in the S. cerevisiae expression system. Conventional fermentation strategies typically use carbon source concentrations of 2 – 4%, which can cause osmotic stress and lead to higher alcohol production, potentially detrimental to the cultures. The invention aims to overcome these limitations by exploring higher carbon source concentrations and longer culture durations to produce superior-quality VLPs.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a method for producing high-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs using a yeast expression system. The method involves culturing S. cerevisiae cells expressing the HPV16 L1 gene in a medium containing a carbon source concentration of 6 – 8%. The cells are cultured for an extended period of 6 days to ensure optimal protein production and assembly. The HPV16 L1 protein is then purified using a series of steps, including cell disruption, ammonium sulfate precipitation, dialysis, and cation-exchange chromatography.

The optimized carbon source concentration and extended culture duration result in higher yields of HPV16 L1 protein, which facilitates the formation of VLPs with improved antigenicity and immunogenicity. The VLPs produced using this method exhibit smaller hydrodynamic sizes and higher reactivity against neutralizing monoclonal antibodies (Mabs) compared to VLPs produced with lower carbon source concentrations.

### Advantageous Effects

The advantageous effects of the present invention include:

1. **Enhanced Antigenicity and Immunogenicity**: The VLPs produced using the optimized carbon source concentration and extended culture duration exhibit higher reactivity against neutralizing Mabs and induce stronger antibody responses in immunized mice.
2. **Improved Structural Integrity**: The VLPs have smaller hydrodynamic sizes, indicating better structural integrity and stability.
3. **Increased Yield**: The method results in higher yields of HPV16 L1 protein, which is crucial for large-scale production of VLPs.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness**: The use of S. cerevisiae as the expression system and the optimization of culture conditions contribute to the overall cost-effectiveness of the production process.

## MODES OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a method for producing high-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs using a yeast expression system. The method comprises the following steps:

1. **Expression Vector Construction**: The codon-optimized HPV16 L1 gene is ligated into the YEGα-MCS vector to create the plasmid YEGα-MCS-HP16 L1 gene-opt.
2. **Transformation of S. cerevisiae**: The plasmid YEGα-MCS-HP16 L1 gene-opt is introduced into S. cerevisiae strain Y2805. Transformants are selected on SD-ura medium, a synthetic medium without uracil.
3. **Culture Conditions**: The transformants are inoculated into 150 mL of YPDG medium containing 1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, and various concentrations of carbon source (6 – 8%). The glucose to galactose ratio is set at 1:1, resulting in percentages of 3% – 4% each. The cells are cultured for 6 days at 30°C with shaking at 230 rpm.
4. **Cell Disruption and Protein Recovery**: The cells are disrupted using glass beads, and the cell lysate is separated by centrifugation. The L1 protein is recovered from the cell lysate by precipitation with 40% saturated ammonium sulfate.
5. **Dialysis and Purification**: The precipitated L1 protein is resuspended in a buffer and dialyzed against the same buffer. Contaminating proteins are removed by precipitation, and the supernatant containing L1 protein is further purified using cation-exchange chromatography.
6. **Characterization of VLPs**: The purity and quantity of the L1 protein are determined by SDS-PAGE and Western blot analysis. The VLPs are characterized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and dynamic light scattering (DLS).
7. **Immunization and Antibody Response**: Mice are immunized with the purified HPV16 L1 VLPs, and the antibody response is measured using ELISA and neutralization assays.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Optimization of Carbon Source Concentration

#### Materials and Methods

**Ethics**: All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Chung-Ang University. The protocol was approved by the IACUC, and the conditions of the mice were monitored twice daily. Mice were anesthetized intraperitoneally with a 4:1 mixture of Zoletil 50 and Rompun before blood collection.

**Production of HPV16 L1 VLPs**: The codon-optimized HPV16 L1 gene was ligated into the YEGα-MCS vector to create the plasmid YEGα-MCS-HP16 L1 gene-opt. S. cerevisiae strain Y2805 was transformed with the plasmid and selected on SD-ura medium. Transformants were inoculated into 150 mL of YPDG medium containing 1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, and various concentrations of carbon source (2%, 4%, 6%, and 8%). The glucose to galactose ratio was set at 1:1, resulting in percentages of 1%, 2%, 3%, and 4% each. The cells were cultured for 6 days at 30°C with shaking at 230 rpm.

**Purification of HPV16 L1 VLPs**: The cells were disrupted using glass beads, and the cell lysate was separated by centrifugation. The L1 protein was recovered by precipitation with 40% saturated ammonium sulfate and resuspended in a buffer. The solution was dialyzed against the same buffer, and contaminating proteins were removed by precipitation. The supernatant containing L1 protein was further purified using cation-exchange chromatography.

**Measuring Protein Concentrations**: Protein concentrations were measured using the Bradford protein assay with bovine serum albumin (BSA) as a standard. The purity and quantity of L1 were determined by SDS-PAGE and Western blot analysis.

**Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)**: The purified HPV16 L1 VLPs were absorbed onto carbon-coated grids, stained with 2% phosphotungstic acid, and analyzed under a TEM200CX microscope at a final magnification of 234,000×.

**Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)**: A 96-well ELISA plate was coated overnight with 400 ng of purified HPV16 L1 VLPs per well and blocked with 5% skim milk in PBS containing 0.05% Tween 20 (PBST). The plate was incubated with 250 ng/mL of anti-HPV16 neutralizing monoclonal antibodies (Mabs) H16.V5 or H16.E70 for 1 h at 37°C. Bound Mabs were detected using HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG antibody, and color reactions were developed with o-phenylenediamine. Optical density was measured at an absorbance of 492 nm.

**Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS)**: DLS was performed using an ELSZ-2 system to measure the hydrodynamic size of the VLPs.

**Mice Immunization**: Six-week-old female BALB/c mice were immunized three times subcutaneously with purified HPV16 L1 VLPs at two-weekly intervals. The mice were divided into five groups of 8, 9, or 10 mice each. The control group received PBS, and the remaining groups received HPV16 L1 VLPs purified from cultures containing 2%, 4%, 6%, or 8% carbon source. The mice received 10 or 1000 ng purified HPV16 L1 VLPs per dose in combination with aluminum hydroxide (200 μg per dose).

**Titration of Anti-HPV16 L1 IgG**: The anti-HPV16 L1 IgG titers of mouse sera were measured using ELISA. The plates were coated overnight with 100 ng of purified HPV16 L1 VLPs per well and blocked with 5% skim milk in PBST. The plates were reacted with three- or four-fold serial dilutions of mouse sera for 1 h at 37°C. Bound anti-HPV16 L1 IgG was detected using HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG antibody, and color reactions were developed as described above.

**Neutralization Assays Using HPV16 Pseudovirus (HPV16 PsV)**: HPV16 PsV was prepared according to a published method. Neutralizing activity in mouse sera was determined using a neutralization assay. The neutralizing antibody titer of a mouse serum was defined as the reciprocal of the highest dilution that caused a reduction of at least 50% in secreted embryonic alkaline phosphatase (SEAP) activity.

#### Results

**Final Quantities and Purities of HPV16 L1 VLPs**: The specific L1 protein production increased with increasing carbon source concentration. The amount of L1 protein finally recovered after purification was higher in cultures containing 6% or 8% carbon source. The amount of L1 protein obtained from the culture containing 8% carbon source was 7.6-, 2.5-, and 1.2-fold higher than the amounts obtained from the 2%, 4%, and 6% cultures, respectively.

**Comparison of In Vitro Antigenicity of the HPV16 L1 VLPs**: ELISAs using neutralizing Mabs H16.V5 and H16.E70 showed that HPV16 L1 VLPs-6% and -8% had higher reactivity against these Mabs compared to HPV16 L1 VLPs-2% and -4%. DLS analysis revealed that the hydrodynamic sizes of VLPs from cultures with 6% and 8% carbon source were smaller, supporting the improved antigenicity of these VLPs.

**Antibody Response Following Immunization with HPV16 L1 VLPs**: Mice immunized with 10 or 1000 ng of HPV16 L1 VLPs per dose showed higher anti-HPV16 L1 IgG titers and neutralizing activities against HPV16 PsVs when immunized with HPV16 L1 VLPs-6% and -8% compared to HPV16 L1 VLPs-2% and -4%.

### Example 2: Intracellular Redox Status and VLP Quality

#### Materials and Methods

**Measurement of Glutathione Levels**: The levels of reduced glutathione (GSH) and oxidized glutathione (GSSG) were measured in prolonged cultures of S. cerevisiae producing HPV L1. The GSSG and GSH were measured using a previously described method.

#### Results

**Intracellular Redox Status**: The levels of GSSG decreased in favor of GSH in prolonged cultures of S. cerevisiae producing HPV L1, indicating a shift in the intracellular redox state. This suggests a correlation between the folding and assembly of HPV L1 and the intracellular redox status.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention has significant industrial applicability in the field of vaccine development. The method for producing high-quality HPV16 L1 VLPs using optimized carbon source concentrations and extended culture durations can be applied to the large-scale production of VLPs for use in vaccines. The enhanced antigenicity and immunogenicity of the VLPs produced using this method make them suitable for developing high-efficacy and low-cost VLP vaccines. The use of S. cerevisiae as the expression system and the optimization of culture conditions contribute to the overall cost-effectiveness of the production process, making the invention particularly valuable for the biotechnology and pharmaceutical industries.
