# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Glaucoma, a leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide, primarily affects the optic nerve and is often associated with elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). Controlling IOP remains the primary therapeutic approach to managing glaucoma. When pharmacological treatments and laser therapies fail to adequately lower IOP, surgical interventions become necessary. Common surgical options include trabeculectomy, glaucoma drainage implants (GDIs), and minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS).

Trabeculectomy, the standard surgical approach for adult primary glaucoma, involves creating a new drainage pathway for aqueous humor (AH) to lower IOP. However, its success is limited in certain populations, such as children and those with specific types of glaucoma. GDIs are designed to treat secondary glaucoma, pediatric glaucoma, and refractory cases following failed trabeculectomy. Despite their utility, GDIs often face long-term failure due to fibrotic encapsulation, which obstructs fluid drainage. MIGS, a newer approach, offers a safer profile but is generally effective only for mild to moderate glaucoma, aiming to reduce the need for glaucoma medications.

The primary challenge in glaucoma surgery is the body's natural healing response, which leads to fibrosis and closure of the newly created drainage pathways. This issue highlights the critical need for an implant that can maintain effective AH outflow while minimizing fibrotic tissue formation. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel glaucoma drainage device featuring a microfluidic meshwork designed to reduce fibrosis and enhance long-term functionality.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a modified glaucoma drainage implant (GDI) designed to reduce fibrotic tissue formation and improve long-term success rates in glaucoma surgery. The device comprises a microfluidic meshwork that replaces the traditional solid plate of conventional GDIs. The meshwork is characterized by interconnected, cellular-dimensioned microfluidic channels and ultra-flexibility, allowing it to conform to the curvature and movement of the eye tissue.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Microfluidic Channels**: The meshwork consists of interconnected microfluidic channels with outer diameters of 20 μm and inner diameters of 8 μm, designed to ensure sufficient AH outflow.
2. **Ultra-Flexibility**: The meshwork is ultra-flexible, conforming to the eye's curvature and minimizing mechanical mismatch with the surrounding tissue.
3. **Biocompatibility**: The design of the meshwork aims to reduce the foreign body reaction and minimize fibrotic tissue formation, thereby enhancing the long-term effectiveness of the implant.

The invention also encompasses methods for fabricating the microfluidic meshwork using photolithography techniques and methods for surgically implanting the device in the eye. Experimental evidence from animal models demonstrates that the microfluidic meshwork significantly reduces fibrotic capsule formation compared to conventional GDI plates.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

- **Glaucoma**: A group of eye conditions that damage the optic nerve, often associated with elevated intraocular pressure (IOP).
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP)**: The pressure inside the eye, primarily determined by the balance between the production and drainage of aqueous humor.
- **Aqueous Humor (AH)**: A clear, watery fluid produced by the ciliary body and drained through the trabecular meshwork, maintaining IOP.
- **Glaucoma Drainage Implant (GDI)**: A surgical device used to create an additional drainage pathway for AH to lower IOP.
- **Microfluidic Meshwork**: A flexible, interconnected network of microfluidic channels designed to conduct AH and minimize fibrotic tissue formation.
- **Fibrosis**: The formation of excess fibrous connective tissue, often leading to the failure of glaucoma surgical implants.

### Glaucoma Drainage Meshwork

The glaucoma drainage meshwork is a novel component of a modified GDI designed to reduce fibrotic tissue formation and enhance long-term success rates in glaucoma surgery. The meshwork is fabricated using advanced photolithography techniques and consists of interconnected, cellular-dimensioned microfluidic channels. These channels are designed to conduct AH efficiently while minimizing the mechanical mismatch between the implant and the surrounding eye tissue.

#### Design and Fabrication

The microfluidic meshwork is fabricated on silicon wafers with a nickel-releasing layer. Microchannel walls are patterned using negative photoresist SU-8, and the microchannels are formed by sacrificial photoresist (LOR 5A and AZ1505). The meshwork has an overall area of 7 mm × 7 mm and a grid period of 100 μm. The thickness of the meshwork is 4 μm, and the microfluidic channels have outer diameters of 20 μm and inner diameters of 8 μm. These dimensions are optimized to provide sufficient AH outflow (2 μL/min at 10 mmHg) while ensuring ultra-flexibility and biocompatibility.

#### Biocompatibility and Flexibility

The ultra-flexibility of the microfluidic meshwork allows it to conform to the curvature and movement of the eye tissue, minimizing interfacial forces that can elicit tissue reactions. The interconnected microfluidic channels are designed to maintain fluidic conductance while introducing minimal perturbation to the cellular and vascular processes at the implant site. This design significantly reduces the foreign body reaction and fibrotic tissue formation, enhancing the long-term functionality of the implant.

### Methods of Making

The microfluidic meshwork is fabricated using a multi-step photolithography process. Silicon wafers with a nickel-releasing layer are used as the substrate. Microchannel walls are patterned using negative photoresist SU-8, and the microchannels are formed by sacrificial photoresist (LOR 5A and AZ1505). The meshwork is then released from the substrate, washed, and stored in a buffer solution prior to autoclaving and implantation.

#### Photolithography Process

1. **Substrate Preparation**: Silicon wafers with a nickel-releasing layer are cleaned and prepared for photolithography.
2. **Photoresist Application**: Negative photoresist SU-8 is spin-coated onto the silicon wafer to form the microchannel walls.
3. **Pattern Exposure**: The photoresist is exposed to ultraviolet (UV) light through a photomask to define the microchannel walls.
4. **Development**: The exposed photoresist is developed to remove the unexposed areas, leaving the microchannel walls intact.
5. **Sacrificial Layer Formation**: Sacrificial photoresist (LOR 5A and AZ1505) is applied and patterned to form the microchannels.
6. **Release and Washing**: The meshwork is released from the substrate and washed to remove any residual photoresist.
7. **Storage and Sterilization**: The meshwork is stored in a buffer solution and sterilized by autoclaving before implantation.

### Methods of Use

The microfluidic meshwork is surgically implanted in the eye to create an additional drainage pathway for AH, thereby lowering IOP. The surgical procedure involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation**: The eye is prepared with povidone-iodine, and the rabbit is anesthetized using a combination of ketamine hydrochloride and xylazine hydrochloride, followed by isoflurane mask anesthesia.
2. **Conjunctival Peritomy**: Conjunctival peritomy is performed at the limbus in the supratemporal quadrant, followed by posterior dissection in the same plane.
3. **Implant Placement**: The microfluidic meshwork is placed on the episcleral surface without suturing. The conventional AGV PF7 plate is sutured with 9-0 nylon sutures onto the episcleral surface approximately 6 mm from the limbus.
4. **Conjunctival Closure**: The conjunctiva is closed with interrupted 8-0 vicryl sutures. A limbal 10-0 nylon suture is placed at the middle of the microfluidic meshwork to facilitate visualization.
5. **Postoperative Care**: The rabbit eyes are treated with Polymyxin antibiotic drops for one week and prednisolone acetate 1% drops, starting with 3 times a day and tapering every 3 days.

### Experimental Examples

#### Example 1: A Novel Flexible Microfluidic Meshwork to Reduce Fibrosis in Glaucoma Surgery

**Objective**: To evaluate the chronic tissue reactions to the implanted microfluidic meshwork in a rabbit model and compare them with those of the conventional AGV PF7 plate.

**Materials and Methods**:
- **Animals**: Three healthy Albino New Zealand rabbits (12–14 weeks old, weighing 2–3 kg) were used.
- **Surgical Procedure**: Each rabbit received one eye with the AGV PF7 plate and the other with the microfluidic meshwork. The surgery was performed in a standard fashion under an operating microscope.
- **Postoperative Monitoring**: The animals were monitored for signs of infection, inflammation, or plate erosion on days 1, 3, 7, and 14 and monthly thereafter for up to 3 months.
- **Histology Preparation**: Three months after surgery, the rabbits were euthanized, and the entire orbital contents were removed and fixed with 10% formalin. Histology slides were prepared and stained with hematoxylin-eosin (HE) for microscopic examination.

**Results**:
- **Capsule Thickness**: Histological examination revealed a thick fibrotic capsule around the AGV PF7 plate, with an average thickness of 89 ± 6.6 μm. In contrast, nearly no capsule formed around the microfluidic meshwork, with an average thickness of 1.3 ± 0.6 μm. The difference was statistically significant (P = 0.002).
- **Inflammatory Cell Activation**: Some inflammatory cells accumulated in the region where the meshwork had stacked into multiple layers during surgery, but this was observed in less than 10% of the total area.

**Conclusion**:
The microfluidic meshwork demonstrated excellent biocompatibility, significantly reducing fibrotic tissue formation compared to the conventional AGV PF7 plate. These results suggest that the microfluidic meshwork has the potential to improve the long-term success rates of glaucoma surgery by minimizing the foreign body reaction and fibrosis. Further development and clinical testing are warranted to fully realize the benefits of this novel GDI design.
