# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunostimulatory oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) and, more specifically, to nonaggregating A/D type CpG ODNs. These ODNs are designed to overcome the limitations associated with the aggregation and precipitation of traditional A/D type ODNs, thereby enhancing their stability, solubility, and clinical applicability. The invention is particularly useful in the context of vaccine adjuvants and immunomodulators.

## BACKGROUND ART

Immunostimulatory CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) have been widely studied and utilized as potent activators of the innate immune system and as adjuvants in vaccine formulations. Based on their backbone and sequence characteristics, these ODNs can be classified into four main types: A/D, B/K, C, and P-type ODNs. A/D type ODNs, characterized by their phosphodiester backbone and poly-G tail at the 3′ end, are known for their ability to stimulate interferon-α (IFN-α) production from plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs). B/K type ODNs, with a phosphorothioate backbone, primarily activate B cells and interleukin-6 (IL-6) production. C type ODNs can stimulate both IFN-α and IL-6, albeit with lower IFN-α induction compared to A/D type ODNs. Recently, P-type ODNs, also with a phosphorothioate backbone, have been described, which contain two palindromic sequences and exhibit a cytokine profile similar to C type ODNs but with higher IFN-α production.

Despite the extensive development of various immunostimulatory ODNs, the most significant difference among them lies in their IFN-α induction profile. A/D type ODNs, known for their high IFN-α production, have been closely linked to the formation of G-quadruplex DNA structures and nanoparticle/aggregates in salt solutions. This aggregation is essential for their high IFN-α production but poses significant challenges for clinical applications due to uncontrolled aggregation and precipitation, leading to high product variability and administration difficulties.

To address these issues, various strategies have been explored, including the introduction of thermolytic protective groups to prevent aggregate formation before administration and allow G-quadruplex formation post-administration. However, the feasibility of these methods for clinical application remains to be fully evaluated, and no human clinical trials have been reported.

In this context, the present invention provides nonaggregating A/D type ODNs by modifying the original D35 ODN with the addition of a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide at the 3′ end. This modification prevents aggregate formation in saline while maintaining the high IFN-α inducing property, thereby offering a promising solution for the clinical application of A/D type ODNs.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides nonaggregating A/D type CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) that overcome the limitations of traditional A/D type ODNs. Specifically, the invention addresses the problem of uncontrolled aggregation and precipitation of A/D type ODNs, which hampers their clinical application. The nonaggregating A/D type ODNs of the invention are designed to maintain high IFN-α inducing properties while ensuring solubility and stability in saline solutions.

The key features of the invention include:
1. **Nonaggregating Property**: The ODNs do not form large aggregates or precipitate in saline, ensuring consistent and predictable behavior in solution.
2. **High IFN-α Induction**: The ODNs retain the ability to induce high levels of IFN-α, making them effective as immunostimulatory agents.
3. **Solubility**: The ODNs can be easily solubilized in saline, facilitating their preparation and administration for clinical use.
4. **Enhanced Adjuvanticity**: The ODNs demonstrate superior adjuvanticity in vivo, particularly when used as vaccine adjuvants.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The nonaggregating A/D type ODNs of the invention offer several advantageous effects:
1. **Improved Stability and Solubility**: The ODNs remain stable and soluble in saline, reducing the risk of aggregation and precipitation, which are common issues with traditional A/D type ODNs.
2. **Consistent Performance**: The lack of aggregation ensures consistent performance across different batches and preparations, enhancing the reliability of the ODNs in clinical settings.
3. **Enhanced Clinical Application**: The ODNs can be easily formulated and administered, making them suitable for a wide range of clinical applications, including vaccine adjuvants and immunomodulators.
4. **Superior Immunostimulatory Properties**: The ODNs maintain high IFN-α inducing properties, which are crucial for their effectiveness as immunostimulatory agents.
5. **Better Adjuvanticity**: The ODNs demonstrate superior adjuvanticity in vivo, particularly in animal models, indicating their potential for enhancing vaccine efficacy.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Synthesis and Characterization of Nonaggregating A/D Type ODNs

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Human PBMC Preparation**: PBMCs were isolated from healthy adult volunteers using Ficoll-Paque PLUS and LeucoSep. The cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin.
- **CpG ODNs Stimulation**: The ODNs, including D35, D35-dAs40, and D35core-dAs40, were synthesized by GeneDesign. PBMCs were stimulated with the ODNs for 24 hours, and cytokine production was measured using ELISA kits.
- **Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS)**: DLS measurements were performed to analyze the size and polydispersity of the ODNs in PBS.
- **Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)**: TEM was used to visualize the physical properties of the ODNs, including the presence of aggregates.

**Results:**
- **Cytokine Production**: D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 induced high levels of IFN-α and IL-6 in human PBMCs, similar to the original D35. The addition of the dAs40 tail did not significantly alter the cytokine profile.
- **Physical Properties**: D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 did not form visible aggregates in PBS, as confirmed by DLS and TEM. The size of the ODNs was less than 20 nm, indicating excellent solubility and stability.

#### Example 2: In Vivo Adjuvanticity of Nonaggregating A/D Type ODNs

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cynomolgus Monkey Immunization**: Cynomolgus monkeys were subcutaneously administered influenza split vaccine (SV) with or without K3, D35, D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, or D35-SPG. Serum samples were collected at 4 and 8 weeks post-immunization, and anti-SV total IgG levels were measured by ELISA.

**Results:**
- **Adjuvanticity**: D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 showed better and more consistent adjuvanticity compared to K3 and original D35. The anti-SV total IgG levels were significantly higher in the groups receiving D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40, indicating their superior ability to enhance vaccine-induced immune responses.

#### Example 3: Effect of Phosphorothioate Polynucleotide Tail Length on Immunostimulatory Activity

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cytokine Production**: Human PBMCs were stimulated with D35core-dAs40 with varying lengths of the dAs40 tail (dAs6, dAs10, dAs20, dAs40, dAs60, and dAs100). Cytokine production was measured using ELISA kits.

**Results:**
- **Cytokine Production**: The length of the dAs40 tail positively correlated with the induction of IFN-α and IL-6. D35core-dAs40 with a dAs40 tail of 60 or more showed the highest cytokine production, with a slight decrease in activity observed at dAs100.

#### Example 4: Effect of Phosphorothioate Polynucleotide Tail Composition on Immunostimulatory Activity

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cytokine Production**: Human PBMCs were stimulated with D35core-dNs40 with varying compositions of the dNs40 tail (random, 10% G, 30% G, 50% G, 70% G, and 90% G). Cytokine production was measured using ELISA kits.

**Results:**
- **Cytokine Production**: The composition of the dNs40 tail affected the immunostimulatory activity. D35core-dNs40 with a higher guanosine content (70% G and 90% G) showed increased IFN-α production, while random and lower guanosine content (10% G and 30% G) showed reduced activity.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The nonaggregating A/D type ODNs of the invention have significant industrial applicability in the fields of immunotherapy and vaccine development. These ODNs can be used as potent adjuvants to enhance the efficacy of vaccines, particularly in the context of infectious diseases and cancer. The improved stability and solubility of the ODNs make them suitable for various formulations, including injectable solutions and lyophilized powders, facilitating their clinical application. Additionally, the high IFN-α inducing properties of the ODNs make them valuable as immunomodulators for treating conditions that benefit from enhanced innate immune responses, such as viral infections and autoimmune disorders. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in the development of clinically applicable immunostimulatory ODNs.
