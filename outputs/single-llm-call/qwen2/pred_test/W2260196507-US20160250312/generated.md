# DESCRIPTION

## 1 MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present invention relates to a method for assessing the immunogenicity and efficacy of candidate vaccines for Plasmodium falciparum, a causative agent of malaria. The method involves the use of a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost intramuscular (i.m.) regimen with an eight-week interval. The candidate vaccines express various P. falciparum antigens, including liver-stage antigen 1 (PfLSA1), liver-stage associated protein 2 (PfLSAP2), upregulated in sporozoite 3 (PfUIS3), and others. The antigens were chosen based on their expression at the liver stage, their ability to elicit T cell responses in natural infections, and evidence of protection in non-human primate models.

The antigens were expressed in recombinant adenoviruses (ChAd63) and modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) vectors. The ChAd63 vectors were generated by sub-cloning the antigen constructs into an entry vector comprising a modified human cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter with two tetracycline repressor-operator complexes. The modified entry vectors were directionally inserted into the E1 and E3 deleted adenoviral genome at the E1 locus by site-specific recombination. The recombinant adenoviral plasmids were transfected into T-REx™ 293 cells and the adenoviruses purified and titred. The MVA vectors were generated by cloning the antigen constructs into a shuttle plasmid with the antigen under control of the p7.5 promoter, enabling markerless insertion at the thymidine kinase locus by recombination and transient-dominant selection in infected cells. The recombinant viruses were purified by flow sorting and plaque-picking, and titred.

Mice were immunized i.m. into the musculus tibialis with a total volume of 50 μl vaccine administered in endotoxin-free D-PBS, with a dose of 1 × 10^8 infectious units (ifu) for ChAd63 and 1 × 10^7 plaque-forming units (pfu) for MVA in BALB/c mice, and 1 × 10^6 pfu MVA in C57BL/6 mice. The identity and purity of the viruses were determined by PCR.

## 2 ASSESSING IMMUNOGENICITY AND EFFICACY OF EIGHT CANDIDATE P. FALCIPARUM VACCINES IN MICE

The immunogenicity of the candidate vaccines was assessed in BALB/c and C57BL/6 mice using a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost i.m. regimen with an eight-week interval. T cell responses were evaluated one week after the MVA boost using intracellular cytokine staining (ICS) and ex vivo IFNγ enzyme-linked immunosorbent spot (ELISpot) assays. Humoral immunity was assessed through the luminescence immunoprecipitation system (LIPS).

In BALB/c mice, T cell responses, predominantly CD8+ IFNγ+ cells, were detected for all antigens, with the highest frequencies observed for PfLSA1, PfLSA3, and PfUIS3 (medians of 6.3%, 4.8%, and 4.2% of CD8+ cells producing IFNγ, respectively). CD4+ IFNγ+ cells were also detected, but at a frequency of less than 0.5% of total CD4+ cells for most antigens. The splenic IFNγ ELISpot results confirmed the highest immunogenicity levels for PfUIS3 and PfLSA1 (medians of 1200 and 868 spot-forming units (SFU) per million splenocytes, respectively). Humoral responses were detected against all antigens except PfLSAP1, with the greatest magnitude responses observed for PfFalstatin and PfLSAP2 (fold changes of 1.6 and 1.5, respectively).

In C57BL/6 mice, CD8+ IFNγ+ T cell responses were detected for all antigens except PfLSAP1 and PfLSA1. The highest frequencies were observed for PfUIS3 and PfLSA3 (medians of 13.7% and 12.8% of CD8+ cells producing IFNγ, respectively). CD4+ IFNγ+ responses were detected but at a lower frequency (less than 0.8% of total CD4+ cells). The splenic IFNγ ELISpot results confirmed the absence of detectable cellular immune responses to PfLSA1 and PfLSAP1. No antibodies were detectable against PfLSAP1, PfLSAP2, and PfLSA1, with a borderline response to PfETRAMP5. The greatest magnitude response was measured for PfFalstatin (fold change of 1.5).

## 3 ASSESSMENT OF PfUIS3, PfLSA1 AND PfLSAP2 AS CANDIDATES FOR A LIVER-STAGE MALARIA VACCINE

The protective efficacy of the candidate vaccines was assessed in BALB/c and CD-1 outbred mice using chimeric P. berghei sporozoites expressing the cognate P. falciparum antigen. Mice were challenged eight days after the MVA boost with 1000 chimeric sporozoites injected intravenously (i.v.) to focus the challenge on the liver stage. PfCSP and PfTRAP were used as control antigens. Statistically significant efficacy (sterile protection or delay) was assessed using the Log-Rank (Mantel-Cox) Test.

Vaccination with PfTRAP did not induce a statistically significant level of sterile protection in BALB/c mice but protected 3/10 (30%) CD-1 mice. Vaccination with PfCSP protected 3/8 (37.5%) BALB/c mice and 3/9 (33.3%) CD-1 mice. The remaining mice had a significant delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia, with a median delay of 1.47 (p = 0.0008) and 0.48 days (p = 0.001) in BALB/c and CD-1 mice, respectively. In comparison, vaccination with PfLSA1 protected 7/8 (87.5%) of both BALB/c and CD-1 mice, a statistically significantly higher proportion than unvaccinated mice (p < 0.0001). Similarly, vaccination with PfLSAP2 protected 7/8 (87.5%) BALB/c mice and 7/10 (70%) CD-1 mice (p < 0.0001 and p = 0.0009, respectively).

Vaccination with PfUIS3 induced sterile protection in 1/8 BALB/c mice (12.5%) and a significant delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia compared to naïve controls (1.42 days, p = 0.004, overall p = 0.0001). However, PfUIS3 vaccination was unable to confer protection in CD-1 mice despite a non-significant trend towards delayed parasitaemia (median delay of 1.10 days compared with naïve controls). PfFalstatin was also able to induce a statistically significant but small delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in both BALB/c and CD-1 mice (median delay of 0.59 days, p = 0.007, and 0.97 days, p < 0.0001, respectively). PfLSA3 was able to induce a statistically significant but small delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in BALB/c mice (median 0.35 days, p = 0.03), but not in CD-1 mice. PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, or PfETRAMP5 vaccination was unable to provide sterile protection or a delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in BALB/c or CD-1 mice.

## 4. PROTECTIVE EFFICACY OF THE CANDIDATES VACCINES IN CD-1 OUTBRED MICE

To further validate the protective efficacy of the candidate vaccines, the same experiments were conducted in an outbred strain of mice, CD-1, to limit the effect of MHC restriction and immunodominance that can be observed in inbred strains of mice. Following an eight-week interval i.m. vaccination regimen, mice were challenged eight days after the MVA boost with 1000 chimeric sporozoites injected i.v. to focus the challenge on the liver stage.

Vaccination with PfTRAP did not induce a statistically significant level of sterile protection in CD-1 mice, but it did protect 3/10 (30%) of the mice. Vaccination with PfCSP protected 3/9 (33.3%) of the CD-1 mice, with the remaining mice showing a significant delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia (median delay of 0.48 days, p = 0.001). Vaccination with PfLSA1 protected 7/10 (70%) of the CD-1 mice, a statistically significantly higher proportion than unvaccinated mice (p = 0.0009). Similarly, vaccination with PfLSAP2 protected 7/10 (70%) of the CD-1 mice (p = 0.0009).

Vaccination with PfUIS3 induced a significant delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in CD-1 mice (median delay of 1.10 days, p = 0.0001), although it did not confer sterile protection. PfFalstatin was also able to induce a statistically significant but small delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in CD-1 mice (median delay of 0.97 days, p < 0.0001). PfLSA3 was able to induce a statistically significant but small delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in CD-1 mice (median delay of 0.35 days, p = 0.03). PfCelTOS, PfLSAP1, or PfETRAMP5 vaccination was unable to provide sterile protection or a delay in the time to 1% parasitaemia in CD-1 mice.

## 5. SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for assessing the immunogenicity and efficacy of candidate vaccines for Plasmodium falciparum, a causative agent of malaria. The method involves the use of a heterologous ChAd63-MVA prime-boost i.m. regimen with an eight-week interval. The candidate vaccines express various P. falciparum antigens, including PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, and PfUIS3, among others. The antigens were chosen based on their expression at the liver stage, their ability to elicit T cell responses in natural infections, and evidence of protection in non-human primate models.

The immunogenicity of the candidate vaccines was assessed in BALB/c and C57BL/6 mice, and the protective efficacy was evaluated in both inbred and outbred (CD-1) mice using chimeric P. berghei sporozoites expressing the cognate P. falciparum antigen. The results demonstrate that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are highly effective in inducing strong CD8+ T cell responses and providing significant protection against liver-stage malaria. The method provides a robust platform for identifying and validating new candidate antigens for malaria vaccines.

## 5. SPECT-1

The present invention also includes a method for evaluating the mechanism of protective efficacy in BALB/c mice. The P. falciparum antigen expression in the chimeric parasites was under the control of the P. berghei upregulated in sporozoites 4 (UIS4) promoter, leading to expression both in sporozoites and during the liver stage. To determine the reliance on T cells and the relative contribution of each subset to protection, CD8+ and CD4+ T cells were depleted before challenge using monoclonal antibodies.

For PfLSAP2 vaccination, protection was dependent on the presence of CD8+ T cells. CD8+ depletion reduced sterile protection from 6/8 (75%) in the IgG control group to 1/8 (12.5%) (p = 0.03), and there was no statistically significant difference between the CD8+ depleted group and naïve mice. CD4+ T cells did not appear to play a protective role following vaccination with PfLSAP2, as no significant difference compared to the IgG control group was observed, with 5/8 (62.5%) mice remaining sterilely protected. Furthermore, there was a statistically significant difference between the CD4+ depleted group and naïve mice (p = 0.01).

For PfLSA1 vaccination, the contribution of CD8+ T cells was not as clear as for PfLSAP2. CD8+ depletion reduced sterile protection from 4/7 (57.1%) in the IgG control group to 1/8 (12.5%), but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.07). 2/8 (25%) mice were sterilely protected after CD4+ depletion, a statistically greater level than compared to the naïve control group (p = 0.0003), suggesting these mice are still afforded some protection.

While depletion experiments were not conducted in CD-1 mice, PfLSA1 vaccination was immunogenic in CD-1 mice, with a median CD8+ IFNγ+ response of 1.1% of total CD8+ cells, CD8+ TNFα+ of 1.2%, and CD8+ CD107a+ of 5.5%. A detectable antibody response was observed in 6/8 (75%) CD-1 mice. PfLSAP2 was able to induce a low cellular response in CD-1 mice, with 6/10 PfLSAP2-vaccinated CD-1 mice exhibiting a detectable level of antibodies, but there was no difference between protected and unprotected mice.

## 6. RESULTS

The results of the present invention demonstrate that the candidate vaccines expressing PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 are highly effective in inducing strong CD8+ T cell responses and providing significant protection against liver-stage malaria. The method provides a robust platform for identifying and validating new candidate antigens for malaria vaccines. The use of chimeric P. berghei parasites expressing the cognate P. falciparum antigen allows for the assessment of efficacy in a murine model, which is crucial for preclinical evaluation. The depletion experiments highlight the importance of CD8+ T cells in mediating protection, particularly for PfLSAP2. The method also demonstrates the potential for PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 to be included in future malaria vaccine formulations, potentially leading to a new generation of more effective malaria vaccines.
