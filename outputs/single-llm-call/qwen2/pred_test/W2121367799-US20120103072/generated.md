## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and systems for predicting mortality in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients using novel lipoprotein particle measurements. More particularly, the invention involves the use of ion mobility techniques to determine lipoprotein particle diameter and subfraction concentrations, which are then used to assess the risk of mortality in MHD patients.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) is a life-sustaining treatment for patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Despite advancements in dialysis technology, the mortality rate among MHD patients remains alarmingly high, with approximately two-thirds of patients dying within five years of initiating dialysis treatment. Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is a leading cause of death in this population. Traditional lipid profiles, such as total cholesterol (TC), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), have been widely used to assess cardiovascular risk in the general population. However, in MHD patients, these conventional lipid measures often fail to predict mortality, a phenomenon known as the "lipid paradox" or "reverse epidemiology."

Recent studies have suggested that novel lipoprotein measures, including lipoprotein particle concentration (Pc) and particle diameter (Pd), may provide better prognostic information in MHD patients. Each lipoprotein class consists of a continuous spectrum of particles of different size, density, metabolism, and atherogenic effect. Ion mobility techniques offer a high-resolution method to measure these lipoprotein subfractions, providing insights into the atherogenic potential of different lipoprotein particles.

There is a critical need for reliable biomarkers to risk-stratify MHD patients and guide therapeutic interventions. The present invention addresses this need by providing methods and systems for determining the prognosis of MHD patients using novel lipoprotein particle measurements.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Sample Collection, Preparation, and Purification

The methods of the present invention involve collecting a blood sample from an MHD patient. The blood sample is typically collected in a fasting state to ensure accurate and consistent results. The sample is then prepared for analysis using standard laboratory techniques. For example, the blood sample may be centrifuged to separate plasma or serum from cellular components. The plasma or serum is then stored at appropriate temperatures to maintain the integrity of the lipoprotein particles.

To purify the lipoprotein particles, the plasma or serum sample is subjected to an ion mobility method. Ion mobility techniques, such as those described in the literature (14,15), are used to fractionate lipoprotein particles based on their size and charge. The ion mobility method directly counts each lipoprotein particle, allowing for the determination of lipoprotein particle concentration (Pc) and particle diameter (Pd).

### Determining Prognosis

Once the lipoprotein particle diameter (Pd) and subfraction concentrations are determined, they are analyzed to predict the risk of mortality in the MHD patient. The analysis involves comparing the measured values to reference ranges or thresholds that have been established through clinical studies.

For example, a smaller LDL particle diameter (Pd) is associated with an increased risk of mortality, while a larger LDL particle diameter is associated with a decreased risk of mortality. Similarly, higher concentrations of very small LDL particles are associated with higher mortality, whereas higher concentrations of large LDL particles are associated with greater survival.

The data processing module of the system can use statistical models, such as Cox proportional hazard models, to calculate the death hazard ratio (HR) based on the lipoprotein particle measurements. The HR can be adjusted for various covariates, including age, gender, race/ethnicity, diabetes, dialysis vintage, comorbidities, and inflammatory markers.

### Examples

#### Example 1

**Objective:** To demonstrate the use of ion mobility techniques to determine lipoprotein particle diameter (Pd) and subfraction concentrations in MHD patients.

**Methods:** Blood samples were collected from 235 MHD patients. The samples were prepared by centrifugation to obtain plasma. The plasma was then analyzed using an ion mobility method to determine the lipoprotein particle diameter and subfraction concentrations.

**Results:** The ion mobility method successfully fractionated the lipoprotein particles into very small, small, medium, and large LDL subfractions. The particle diameters and concentrations were measured with high precision and accuracy. The data showed that smaller LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of very small LDL particles were associated with increased mortality, while larger LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of large LDL particles were associated with decreased mortality.

#### Example 2

**Objective:** To validate the prognostic value of lipoprotein particle measurements in MHD patients using a Cox proportional hazard model.

**Methods:** The lipoprotein particle diameter and subfraction concentrations were determined for 235 MHD patients using the ion mobility method. The patients were followed for up to 6 years to record mortality outcomes. Cox proportional hazard models were used to calculate the death hazard ratios (HRs) for different lipoprotein particle measurements, adjusting for various covariates.

**Results:** The Cox proportional hazard models confirmed that smaller LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of very small LDL particles were associated with increased mortality, while larger LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of large LDL particles were associated with decreased mortality. The HRs were statistically significant after adjusting for age, gender, race/ethnicity, diabetes, dialysis vintage, comorbidities, and inflammatory markers.

#### Example 3

**Objective:** To compare the prognostic value of novel lipoprotein particle measurements with conventional lipid profiles in MHD patients.

**Methods:** The lipoprotein particle diameter and subfraction concentrations were determined for 235 MHD patients using the ion mobility method. Conventional lipid profiles, including total cholesterol (TC), LDL-C, and HDL-C, were also measured. The patients were followed for up to 6 years to record mortality outcomes. Cox proportional hazard models were used to compare the death hazard ratios (HRs) for novel lipoprotein particle measurements and conventional lipid profiles.

**Results:** The Cox proportional hazard models showed that conventional lipid profiles, such as LDL-C and HDL-C, were not associated with mortality in MHD patients. In contrast, novel lipoprotein particle measurements, including LDL particle diameter and subfraction concentrations, were strongly associated with mortality. The HRs for novel lipoprotein particle measurements were statistically significant and remained significant after adjusting for conventional lipid profiles and other covariates.

#### Example 4

**Objective:** To evaluate the clinical utility of lipoprotein particle measurements in risk-stratifying MHD patients.

**Methods:** The lipoprotein particle diameter and subfraction concentrations were determined for 235 MHD patients using the ion mobility method. The patients were stratified into four categories based on the combination of LDL particle diameter and LDL-C concentration. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to compare the survival outcomes of the different categories.

**Results:** The Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed that MHD patients with above-median LDL particle diameter and above-median LDL-C concentration had the lowest risk of mortality. In contrast, patients with below-median LDL particle diameter and above-median LDL-C concentration had the highest risk of mortality. These findings suggest that lipoprotein particle measurements can effectively risk-stratify MHD patients and guide therapeutic interventions.

## SUMMARY OF RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The present invention demonstrates the utility of novel lipoprotein particle measurements, including lipoprotein particle diameter (Pd) and subfraction concentrations, in predicting mortality in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients. The ion mobility method provides high-resolution measurements of lipoprotein particles, which are more accurate and reliable than conventional lipid profiles in assessing the risk of mortality in MHD patients.

The results of the clinical studies show that smaller LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of very small LDL particles are associated with increased mortality, while larger LDL particle diameters and higher concentrations of large LDL particles are associated with decreased mortality. These findings are consistent across different statistical models and remain significant after adjusting for various covariates.

The invention also highlights the limitations of conventional lipid profiles in MHD patients and emphasizes the need for more sophisticated lipid measurements. The clinical utility of lipoprotein particle measurements in risk-stratifying MHD patients and guiding therapeutic interventions is evident from the Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox proportional hazard models.

Further studies, including randomized controlled trials, are needed to examine the impact of modulating lipoprotein subfractions on outcomes in MHD patients. The present invention provides a foundation for developing new diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies to improve the prognosis of MHD patients.
