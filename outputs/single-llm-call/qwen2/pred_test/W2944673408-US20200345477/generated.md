# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention pertains to the field of medical devices, specifically to breast implants. More particularly, the invention relates to a method and apparatus for evaluating the mechanical properties of breast implants, including their shape stability and response to compressive loads. The invention provides a new methodology for assessing the multi-dimensional mechanical properties of breast implants, which can be used to compare the potential shape stability and durability of different implant designs.

### Description of Related Art

Breast implants have undergone extensive mechanical testing to ensure their strength and durability. Current testing methods, as recommended by the FDA and based on ASTM and ISO standards, primarily focus on tensile strength, ultimate elongation, tear resistance, joint testing, cyclic fatigue testing, ultimate strength tests, and valve competency for saline implants. These tests, while rigorous and repeatable, do not fully simulate the in vivo environment. As a result, there is a need for a more comprehensive method to evaluate the mechanical properties of breast implants, particularly their shape stability and response to compressive loads.

Previous studies have focused on the mechanical properties of breast implants, but none have provided a detailed methodology for evaluating the multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli of implants under compressive loads. Additionally, there is a lack of standardized testing methods that consider the effects of frictional forces between the implant and the compressing surfaces. The present invention addresses these gaps by providing a new methodology for evaluating the mechanical properties of breast implants, including their shape stability and response to compressive loads.

It is an object of the present invention to provide a method and apparatus for evaluating the mechanical properties of breast implants, including their shape stability and response to compressive loads.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for evaluating the mechanical properties of breast implants, including their shape stability and response to compressive loads. The method involves compressing the implant between two parallel plates and analyzing the resulting changes in the implant's geometry and mechanical properties. The apparatus includes a load frame, such as an Instron 5500R, equipped with a load cell and an automated data acquisition system. The method and apparatus can be used to evaluate the multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli of breast implants, which provide a mathematical description of the implant's shape stability and response to compressive loads.

The method includes the following steps:
1. Placing the breast implant on a lower platen of the load frame.
2. Applying a compressive load to the implant using an upper platen.
3. Measuring the load and the displacement of the upper platen.
4. Calculating the implant's diameter and surface area using a geometric model.
5. Repeating the compression test for different loads and recording the data.
6. Analyzing the data to determine the multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli of the implant.

The method can be performed using both dry and lubricated platens to evaluate the effects of frictional forces on the mechanical properties of the implant. Additionally, a simple "pinch" test can be performed to assess the tactile properties of the implant at very low and localized compressive loads.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### First Invention Embodiment

The first embodiment of the present invention involves a method for evaluating the mechanical properties of breast implants, including their shape stability and response to compressive loads. The method utilizes a load frame, such as an Instron 5500R, equipped with a load cell and an automated data acquisition system. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Implant**: An unimplanted commercial breast implant is prepared for testing. The implant is placed on a lower platen of the load frame, and the upper platen is positioned to apply a minimal initial load of 0.445 N. The initial implant diameter (Do) and initial implant projection (Ho) are measured using calipers.

2. **Static Compression Test**: The implant is compressed by manually dialing the position of the upper platen to apply a series of loads (F) ranging from 22.2 N to 534 N. At each load, the implant projection (H) and diameter (D) are measured and recorded. The contact diameter (d) is not required for the analysis.

3. **Dynamic Compression Test**: The implant is then tested dynamically by continuously applying an increasing load (F) from 0.445 N to 534 N at a cross-head speed of 25.4 cm/minute. The load (F) and implant projection (H) are continuously measured and recorded by the computer system.

4. **Lubricated Platen Test**: The upper and lower platens are lubricated with a thin layer of silicone oil, and the dynamic compression test is repeated using the same procedure as the dry platen test.

5. **Data Analysis**: The data from the static and dynamic tests are analyzed to determine the multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli of the implant. The multi-dimensional strains, including projection strain (ϵH), diametric strain (ϵD), and areal strain (ϵA), are calculated using the following equations:
   - \( \epsilon_H = - \frac{H - H_o}{H_o} \times 100\% \)
   - \( \epsilon_D = - \frac{D - D_o}{D_o} \times 100\% \)
   - \( \epsilon_A = - \frac{A - A_o}{A_o} \times 100\% \)

   The tangent moduli, including projection tangent modulus (E_SH), diametric tangent modulus (E_SD), and areal tangent modulus (E_SA), are calculated using the following equations:
   - \( E_{SH} = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta \epsilon_H} \)
   - \( E_{SD} = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta \epsilon_D} \)
   - \( E_{SA} = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta \epsilon_A} \)

   The compressive stress (S) is defined as:
   - \( S = \frac{F}{A_{po}} \)
   where \( A_{po} \) is the initial implant planform area.

### Second Invention Embodiment

The second embodiment of the present invention involves a method for evaluating the effects of frictional forces on the mechanical properties of breast implants. The method utilizes the same load frame and data acquisition system as described in the first embodiment. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Implant**: An unimplanted commercial breast implant is prepared for testing. The implant is placed on a lower platen of the load frame, and the upper platen is positioned to apply a minimal initial load of 0.445 N. The initial implant diameter (Do) and initial implant projection (Ho) are measured using calipers.

2. **Dry Platen Test**: The implant is tested dynamically by continuously applying an increasing load (F) from 0.445 N to 534 N at a cross-head speed of 25.4 cm/minute. The load (F) and implant projection (H) are continuously measured and recorded by the computer system.

3. **Lubricated Platen Test**: The upper and lower platens are lubricated with a thin layer of silicone oil, and the dynamic compression test is repeated using the same procedure as the dry platen test.

4. **Data Analysis**: The data from the dry and lubricated platen tests are analyzed to determine the effects of frictional forces on the mechanical properties of the implant. The multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli are calculated as described in the first embodiment. The differences in the mechanical properties between the dry and lubricated platen tests are compared to evaluate the effects of frictional forces.

### Third Invention Embodiment

The third embodiment of the present invention involves a method for assessing the tactile properties of breast implants at very low and localized compressive loads. The method utilizes a simple "pinch" test setup, including a Torbal FC10 10 N push-pull digital force gauge configured with simulated silicone fingers. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Implant**: An unimplanted commercial breast implant is prepared for testing. The implant is placed between two silicone probes simulating fingertips, with the center of the probes approximately 17 mm from the horizontal surface.

2. **Pinch Test**: The implant is pinched or squeezed by moving one of the probes towards the other. The final pinch distance (Xf) is controlled by an adjustable stop plate, and the pinch force (F) is recorded using the force gauge. The test is repeated 30 times for each implant and each pinch distance (Xf).

3. **Data Analysis**: The data from the pinch tests are analyzed to determine the localized diametric strain (ϵLD) using the following equation:
   - \( \epsilon_{LD} = - \frac{X_f - D_o}{D_o} \times 100\% \)

   The pinch force (F) is plotted as a function of the localized diametric strain (ϵLD) to provide a mathematical description of the implant's tactile properties.

### Fourth Invention Embodiment

The fourth embodiment of the present invention involves a method for comparing the mechanical properties of different types of breast implants. The method utilizes the same load frame and data acquisition system as described in the first embodiment. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Selection of Implants**: Three different types of breast implants are selected for testing: an IDEAL IMPLANT, an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA cohesive gel implant, and an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA gel implant. The implants are chosen to have similar sizes, volumes, and initial dimensions.

2. **Testing of Implants**: Each implant is tested using the static and dynamic compression tests as described in the first embodiment. The tests are performed using both dry and lubricated platens.

3. **Data Analysis**: The data from the tests are analyzed to compare the multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli of the different types of implants. The differences in the mechanical properties between the IDEAL IMPLANT and the gel implants are evaluated to assess the shape stability and durability of the implants.

### Fifth Invention Embodiment

The fifth embodiment of the present invention involves a method for evaluating the clinical relevance of the mechanical properties of breast implants. The method utilizes the same load frame and data acquisition system as described in the first embodiment. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Selection of Implants**: Three different types of breast implants are selected for testing: an IDEAL IMPLANT, an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA cohesive gel implant, and an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA gel implant. The implants are chosen to have similar sizes, volumes, and initial dimensions.

2. **Testing of Implants**: Each implant is tested using the static and dynamic compression tests as described in the first embodiment. The tests are performed using both dry and lubricated platens.

3. **Clinical Relevance Analysis**: The data from the tests are analyzed to evaluate the clinical relevance of the mechanical properties of the implants. The multi-dimensional strains and tangent moduli are correlated with clinical outcomes, such as rupture rates, capsular contracture rates, and fold flaw failures. The results are used to provide insights into the potential links between the mechanical properties of the implants and their clinical performance.
