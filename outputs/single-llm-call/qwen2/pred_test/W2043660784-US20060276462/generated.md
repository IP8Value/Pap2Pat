# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION
The present invention relates to the field of pharmacology and neuroscience, specifically to the use of ampakines for enhancing cognitive performance and mitigating the detrimental effects of sleep deprivation in non-human primates. More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of the ampakine CX717 to improve performance in a delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) task and to restore cognitive function impaired by prolonged sleep deprivation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation
Sleep is a fundamental physiological process essential for maintaining cognitive function and overall health. Prolonged sleep deprivation has been shown to have significant detrimental effects on cognitive performance, including impairments in memory, attention, and decision-making. Electrophysiological studies have revealed that sleep deprivation alters brain activity patterns, particularly in the prefrontal cortex and other regions involved in cognitive processing. These changes are often reflected in alterations in electroencephalogram (EEG) patterns, such as increased delta and beta band activity, which are associated with microsleep episodes and reduced vigilance.

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation
The neuroanatomical substrates of sleep deprivation involve multiple brain regions, including the prefrontal cortex (PFC), dorsal striatum (DStr), medial temporal lobe (MTL), and thalamus. These regions are critical for various cognitive functions, and their dysfunction during sleep deprivation contributes to the observed cognitive impairments. For instance, the PFC is involved in executive functions such as working memory and attention, while the MTL, including the hippocampus, plays a crucial role in declarative memory. Sleep deprivation has been shown to decrease metabolic activity in these regions, leading to impaired cognitive performance.

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance
Stress is a significant factor that exacerbates the effects of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance. Chronic stress can lead to dysregulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, resulting in elevated levels of cortisol and other stress hormones. These hormonal changes can further impair cognitive function by affecting synaptic plasticity and neurotransmitter balance in the brain. Additionally, stress can disrupt sleep architecture, leading to fragmented and non-restorative sleep, which exacerbates the cognitive deficits associated with sleep deprivation.

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory
Sleep deprivation has profound effects on memory, particularly on working memory and short-term memory. Studies have shown that sleep-deprived individuals exhibit significant impairments in tasks requiring the maintenance and manipulation of information over short periods. These deficits are thought to be mediated by changes in the neural circuits involved in memory consolidation and retrieval, including the PFC and MTL. Furthermore, sleep deprivation can also affect long-term memory formation by disrupting the consolidation processes that occur during sleep.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions
For the purposes of this invention, the following definitions apply:
- **Ampakine**: A class of drugs that act as positive modulators of AMPA receptors, enhancing glutamatergic neurotransmission.
- **Delayed-Match-to-Sample (DMS) Task**: A cognitive task used to assess working memory and attention in non-human primates. The task involves presenting a sample stimulus and requiring the animal to select a matching stimulus from a set of options after a delay.
- **Sleep Deprivation**: A condition characterized by a lack of adequate sleep, leading to cognitive impairments and other physiological effects.
- **Metabolic Rate of Glucose (CMRglc)**: A measure of brain activity based on the rate of glucose metabolism, typically assessed using positron emission tomography (PET) imaging.

### II. Non-Human Primate Model for Testing Effects of Sleep Deprivation on a Delayed-Match-to-Sample Task (DMTS).
The invention utilizes a non-human primate model to investigate the effects of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance and the potential for ampakines to mitigate these effects. Specifically, the study employs a delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) task to assess working memory and attention in rhesus monkeys. The DMS task involves presenting a sample image and requiring the animal to select a matching image from a set of options after a delay. The task parameters, including the duration of the delay and the number of non-matching images, are varied to assess performance under different conditions.

### Salts of Compounds
The ampakine CX717, a key compound of the invention, is administered in the form of a salt. The salt form of CX717 is prepared by dissolving the compound in a 10% w/v hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin and saline (0.45%) vehicle. This formulation ensures optimal solubility and bioavailability of the drug when administered intravenously.

### Pharmaceutical Formulations
The pharmaceutical formulations of CX717 for use in the invention include a sterile solution suitable for intravenous administration. The solution is prepared by dissolving CX717 in a 10% w/v hydroxypropyl-beta-cyclodextrin and saline (0.45%) vehicle. The final concentration of CX717 in the solution is 1.5 mg/ml. The solution is administered in a dose range of 0.3–1.5 mg/kg, depending on the specific experimental design.

### Dosage
The dosage of CX717 is determined based on the weight of the animal and the desired effect. In the experiments described herein, CX717 is administered at a dose of 0.8 mg/kg intravenously, 10 minutes prior to the start of the DMS task. This dose has been shown to produce significant improvements in cognitive performance under both normal alert conditions and sleep-deprived conditions. The dose-response relationship of CX717 is also investigated, with doses ranging from 0.3 to 1.5 mg/kg, to determine the optimal dose for maximal cognitive enhancement.

The invention provides a method for enhancing cognitive performance and mitigating the detrimental effects of sleep deprivation in non-human primates using the ampakine CX717. The method involves administering CX717 to the animals prior to performing a DMS task, which assesses working memory and attention. The results demonstrate that CX717 significantly improves performance in the DMS task under both normal and sleep-deprived conditions, suggesting its potential for use in combating cognitive impairments associated with sleep deprivation.
