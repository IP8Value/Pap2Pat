# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of diagnostic assays for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria, particularly those harboring the plasmid-borne colistin-resistant genes mcr-1 and mcr-2. More specifically, the invention provides novel polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies against the MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, as well as a highly sensitive and specific sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for detecting these proteins in various biological and environmental samples.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Colistin, also known as polymyxin E, is a bactericidal antibiotic with a broad spectrum of activity against Gram-negative bacteria. It functions by binding to the anionic lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and phospholipids in the outer cell membrane of bacteria, displacing divalent cations from the phosphate groups of membrane lipids. This binding disrupts the outer cell membrane, leading to the leakage of intracellular contents and ultimately bacterial death. Historically, colistin resistance was primarily attributed to chromosomal mutations affecting the synthesis or modification of lipid A, the anchor molecule of LPS. However, the discovery of the plasmid-borne mcr-1 gene in 2015 marked a significant shift in understanding colistin resistance. The mcr-1 gene encodes a phosphoethanolamine (PEA) transferase that modifies the lipid A component of LPS, reducing the negative charge of the cell surface and preventing colistin from binding and initiating membrane lysis. Since then, additional plasmid-mediated colistin-resistant genes, such as mcr-2 to mcr-8, have been identified, further complicating efforts to control the spread of colistin resistance.

The emergence of mobile colistin resistance is of great concern, particularly in clinical settings where colistin is often a last-resort antibiotic for treating multidrug-resistant infections. Timely and accurate detection of colistin-resistant bacteria is crucial for preventing the spread of resistance and implementing appropriate infection control measures. Current detection methods, including phenotypic antibiotic sensitivity testing and genotypic methods like PCR and whole genome sequencing (WGS), have limitations such as long turnaround times, high discrepancies in minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values, and the need for specialized equipment and expertise. Therefore, there is a pressing need for rapid, sensitive, and specific diagnostic tools that can directly detect the presence of MCR proteins in various samples.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the need for rapid and accurate detection of colistin-resistant bacteria by providing novel polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies against the MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, as well as a highly sensitive and specific sandwich ELISA for detecting these proteins. The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Recombinant MCR-1 and MCR-2 Proteins**: The invention provides full-length and catalytic domain forms of the MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, which serve as standards and immunogens for producing antibodies.

2. **Polyclonal and Monoclonal Antibodies**: The invention discloses the production of polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against the MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins. These antibodies exhibit high specificity and affinity for their respective antigens and can be used in various immunoassays.

3. **Sandwich ELISA**: The invention describes a sandwich ELISA for detecting MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins in biological and environmental samples. The ELISA uses a combination of polyclonal antibodies as capture antibodies and monoclonal antibodies as detection antibodies, providing high sensitivity and specificity. The assay is capable of detecting MCR-1 at concentrations as low as 0.01 ng/mL in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and 0.4 × 10^6 cfu/mL in bacterial culture. The ELISA also demonstrates excellent tolerance to complex food matrices, making it suitable for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria in meat samples.

4. **Applications**: The antibodies and ELISA described in the invention have numerous applications, including:
   - Surveillance of colistin-resistant bacteria in clinical and environmental settings.
   - Source tracking of colistin-resistant bacteria in food animals and agricultural environments.
   - Quality control in the production and distribution of food products.
   - Research and development of new diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for combating colistin resistance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Materials and Methods

#### Construction, Expression, and Purification of MCR-1 and MCR-2 Recombinant Proteins

The genes coding for the full-length MCR-1 (residues 1–541 amino acids) and the catalytic domain of MCR-1 (cMCR-1: residues 214–541) were amplified by PCR using the plasmid pBSKSII-KanR-mcr1 as a template. Similarly, the genes coding for the full-length MCR-2 (residues 1–538 amino acids) and the catalytic domain of MCR-2 (cMCR-2: residues 212–538) were amplified using the mcr-2 gene synthesized by IDT. The PCR products were inserted into the pET His6 TEV LIC cloning vector and expressed in E. coli BL21DE3 cells. Protein expression was induced by adding IPTG, and the cells were harvested by centrifugation. The cell pellet was resuspended in buffer A (300 mM NaCl, 20 mM Tris, 20 mM imidazole, pH 8, 10% glycerol) and lysed by sonication. The supernatant was loaded onto a HisTrap FF Crude column, and the proteins were eluted with buffer A supplemented with 500 mM imidazole. The cMCR-1 and cMCR-2 proteins were further purified by size-exclusion chromatography using a Superdex 200 XK 26/70 column. The N-terminal His-tag was removed from cMCR-1 using tobacco etch virus (TEV) protease before gel filtration. Protein concentration was determined by absorbance measurements at 280 nm using the ProtParam tool.

#### Bacterial Strains

E. coli strains AR-Bank #0346 and AR-Bank #0349, harboring the mcr-1 gene on a multicopy plasmid, were obtained from the FDA-CDC Antimicrobial Resistance Bank. E. coli strains ATCC25922 and ATCC29425 were used as negative controls for MCR-1.

#### Polyclonal Antibody Production

Polyclonal antibodies against MCR-1 were produced by Pacific Immunology Corp. Two rabbits were immunized with 300 μg of cMCR-1 at 3-week intervals for a total of four injections. Bleeds were collected, and sera were evaluated for binding to cMCR-1 by direct ELISA. Antibodies were purified from pooled sera using a Protein-A affinity column, and the concentration was determined by absorbance measurement at 280 nm.

#### Monoclonal Antibody Production

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were prepared by immunizing female Balb/cJ mice with 100 μL of recombinant cMCR-1 (50 μg/mL) in Sigma Adjuvant System. Following the third injection, sera were evaluated for anti-MCR-1 titers. Mice with strong antibody titers were boosted with a fourth injection, and splenocytes were fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells. Hybridomas were screened by direct ELISA, and positive clones were expanded and antibodies were purified using a Protein-G conjugated Sepharose column. Antibody isotype was determined by ELISA using isotype-specific antibodies.

### Results and Discussion

#### Generation of Recombinant MCR-1 and MCR-2 Proteins

Full-length and catalytic domain forms of the MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins were successfully expressed and purified from E. coli. The full-length proteins appeared smaller than expected on SDS-PAGE and Western blots due to their high insolubility. Mass spectrometry confirmed the integrity of the full-length MCR-1 protein, and the catalytic domain proteins were further purified by size-exclusion chromatography. These proteins served as standards and immunogens for producing antibodies.

#### Development and Characterization of Polyclonal and Monoclonal Antibodies

Polyclonal antibodies (pAbs) against MCR-1 exhibited high titers and specificity, as demonstrated by ELISA and Western blot analysis. Nine monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were produced and characterized. The mAbs were predominantly IgG1, with one mAb (MCR-1-7) being IgG2b. Western blot analysis showed that mAb MCR-1-7 binds to both MCR-1 and MCR-2, while mAb MCR-1-9 binds only to MCR-1. The binding affinity of the mAbs was evaluated by direct ELISA, with mAb MCR-1-7 showing the highest ELISA value.

#### Sensitivity, Specificity, and Linearity of the Sandwich ELISA

A sandwich ELISA was developed using the pAb as the capture antibody and mAb MCR-1-7 as the detection antibody. The ELISA demonstrated good linearity for detecting cMCR-1 from 0.01 to 4 ng/mL (R² = 0.98) and cMCR-2 from 0.1 to 6 ng/mL (R² = 0.99). The limit of detection (LOD) for cMCR-1 was 0.01 ng/mL, and for cMCR-2, it was 0.1 ng/mL. The ELISA showed high precision, with intra-assay precision (% CV) less than 5% and inter-assay precision (% CV) less than 15% at concentrations within the linear ranges.

#### Detection of MCR-1 Positive Bacteria in Ground Chicken, Pork, and Beef

The ELISA was validated for detecting MCR-1 in ground chicken, pork, and beef samples spiked with mcr-1 positive and negative strains. The ELISA results were positive when the cell density was ≥0.4 × 10^6 cfu/mL for the AR-Bank #346 strain and ≥4 × 10^6 cfu/mL for the AR-Bank #349 strain. The ELISA was specific, with no false positive results for the mcr-1 negative strain, even at high cell densities. The ELISA demonstrated excellent tolerance to complex food matrices, allowing for the unambiguous detection of colistin-resistant bacteria in meat samples.

The development of standards, high-affinity polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies, and a sensitive and specific sandwich ELISA for detecting MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins represents a significant advancement in the diagnosis and surveillance of colistin-resistant bacteria. The simplicity and reliability of this ELISA make it a valuable tool for identifying colistin-resistant bacteria in real-life samples, contributing to the prevention and control of the spread of mobile colistin resistance.
