# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Microbial lipid production has garnered significant attention for the synthesis of specialty chemicals and the production of fuels and bulk chemicals from low-cost carbon feedstocks. The high demand for lipid molecules has driven research into augmenting current plant- and animal-based production methods through microbial conversion of various substrates, including starch, sugar crops, agricultural residues, and lignocellulosic materials. Among natively oleaginous organisms, which can produce lipids at levels exceeding 20% of their dry cell weight, the well-characterized yeast *Yarrowia lipolytica* has received particular interest. Wild-type *Y. lipolytica* can accumulate lipids up to 36% of its dry cell weight from glucose and 50–60% of its dry cell weight when fed exogenous fatty acid substrates. Researchers have metabolically engineered *Y. lipolytica* strains to produce high-value lipids. In addition to lipid production and bio-modification, *Y. lipolytica* strains can secrete low-molecular-weight secondary metabolites such as citric acid and polyols, and extracellular enzymes under specific fermentation conditions. Extensive genetic systems have been developed to manipulate this yeast, making it a versatile platform for biotechnological applications.

Lipids serve as energy storage molecules and building blocks for cellular membranes, primarily composed of fatty acids, isoprenoids, and other water-insoluble hydrocarbon molecules. In many oleaginous yeasts, fatty acids constitute 90–95% of total lipid material. To avoid the potentially toxic and membrane-disturbing effects of free fatty acids, they are incorporated into nonpolar lipids such as triacylglycerols (TAGs) and sterol esters, which are stored in subcellular compartments called lipid droplets or lipid bodies.

Several applied studies have focused on increasing TAG production through genetic engineering of TAG synthesis and degradation pathways. Key enzymes in this pathway include diacylglycerol acyltransferase type 2 (DGA1) and diacylglycerol acyltransferase type 1 (DGA2), which perform the final step of TAG synthesis by incorporating the third acyl-CoA onto the diacylglycerol backbone and transporting it into the lipid droplet. Upregulation of these enzymes has significantly increased lipid yield and productivity in *Y. lipolytica*. Additionally, the deletion of DGA2 impairs TAG synthesis and lipid production, highlighting its critical role in the lipid pathway.

This invention describes a systematic lipid biosynthesis engineering approach centered on the overexpression of diacylglycerol acyltransferase genes (DGA1 and DGA2) in *Y. lipolytica*. The resulting strain reaches a 77% lipid content and a 0.21 g lipid per g glucose yield in batch glucose fermentation, while in fed-batch glucose fermentation, it produces 85 g/L lipid at a 0.73 g/L/h productivity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to genetically modified *Yarrowia lipolytica* strains and methods for increasing lipid production. Specifically, the invention involves the overexpression of diacylglycerol acyltransferase genes (DGA1 and DGA2) and the deletion of triacylglycerol lipase genes (TGL3) to enhance lipid accumulation. The engineered strains exhibit significantly higher lipid content and productivity compared to wild-type *Y. lipolytica*.

The invention provides a method for increasing the activity of a DGA1 protein (DGAT2 gene) in *Y. lipolytica* by overexpressing the DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides* and the DGA2 gene from *Claviceps purpurea*. Additionally, the invention includes a method for deleting the TGL3 gene to reduce TAG degradation during the late phase of the bioprocess when nutrients are limited.

The engineered *Y. lipolytica* strains are useful for the production of high-value lipids, which can be used in various applications, including biofuels, specialty chemicals, and pharmaceuticals. The methods and strains described herein offer a cost-effective and efficient approach to microbial lipid production.

### Definitions

- **Diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGA1 and DGA2):** Enzymes that catalyze the final step of triacylglycerol (TAG) synthesis by incorporating the third acyl-CoA onto the diacylglycerol backbone and transporting it into the lipid droplet.
- **Triacylglycerol (TAG):** A nonpolar lipid composed of three fatty acid molecules attached to a glycerol backbone.
- **Lipid droplets:** Subcellular compartments where TAGs are stored.
- **Triacylglycerol lipase (TGL3):** An enzyme responsible for the first step of lipid body-localized TAG degradation.
- **Overexpression:** The process of increasing the expression level of a gene in a cell.
- **Deletion:** The process of removing a gene from the genome of a cell.

### Microbe Engineering

The invention involves the genetic modification of *Yarrowia lipolytica* to enhance lipid production. The key strategies employed include the overexpression of DGA1 and DGA2 genes and the deletion of TGL3 genes.

### Exemplary Nucleic Acids, Cells, and Methods

The invention utilizes *Yarrowia lipolytica* as the host organism. The DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides* and the DGA2 gene from *Claviceps purpurea* are overexpressed in *Y. lipolytica* to increase lipid production. The TGL3 gene is deleted to reduce TAG degradation during the late phase of the bioprocess.

### Species of Transformed Cell

The transformed cell species is *Yarrowia lipolytica*. The specific strain used in this invention is designated NS18, which was obtained from NRRL # YB-392. The engineered strains are designated NS297, NS281, NS377, and NS432, each with specific genetic modifications as described below.

### Products

The products of the invention include high-value lipids, such as triacylglycerols (TAGs), which can be used in various applications, including biofuels, specialty chemicals, and pharmaceuticals.

### Methods Related to DGA1 and DGA2

The methods related to DGA1 and DGA2 involve the overexpression of these genes in *Y. lipolytica* to enhance lipid production. The DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides* and the DGA2 gene from *Claviceps purpurea* are overexpressed using strong constitutive promoters, such as the Y. lipolytica GPD1 promoter.

### Methods Related to DGA2

The methods related to DGA2 involve the overexpression of the DGA2 gene from *Claviceps purpurea* in *Y. lipolytica* to further enhance lipid production. The DGA2 gene is overexpressed using the Y. lipolytica TEF1 promoter.

### Methods Related to DGA3

The methods related to DGA3 involve the overexpression of the DGA3 gene in *Y. lipolytica* to explore its potential in enhancing lipid production. The DGA3 gene is overexpressed using the Y. lipolytica GPD1 promoter.

## EXEMPLIFICATION

### Example 1: Method to Increase the Activity of a DGA1 Protein (DGAT2 Gene)

To increase the activity of the DGA1 protein in *Y. lipolytica*, the DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides* was overexpressed. The DGA1 gene was cloned into a plasmid under the control of the strong constitutive Y. lipolytica GPD1 promoter. The plasmid was then introduced into the *Y. lipolytica* strain NS18 using standard transformation protocols. Transformants were screened for lipid content using a fluorescence-based lipid assay. The top isolates were further tested in 50 mL shake flasks and 1 L bioreactors. The strain NS281, which overexpresses the R. toruloides DGA1 gene, exhibited a threefold increase in lipid content compared to the wild-type strain NS18.

### Example 2: Lipid Assay

Lipid content was measured using a fluorescence-based lipid assay. Strains were grown in 96-well or 48-well plates in nitrogen-limited media. After 72 hours of growth, 20 μL of cells were mixed with 20 μL of 100% ethanol and incubated at 4°C for 30 minutes. A master mix containing 1 M potassium iodide, 1 mM bodipy 493/503, 0.5 µL dimethyl sulfoxide, 1.5 µL of 60% PEG 4000, and 27 µL water was added to Costar black well/clear bottom plates. Fluorescence was measured at excitation 484 nm and emission 510 nm using a SpectraMax M2 spectrophotometer. The optical density (OD) at 600 nm was also measured. Fluorescence correlated with lipid content measured by GC.

### Example 3: Analysis and Screening of Y. lipolytica Strains that Express DGA1

To analyze and screen *Y. lipolytica* strains that express DGA1, the DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides* was overexpressed in the *Y. lipolytica* strain NS18. Transformants were screened for lipid content using a fluorescence-based lipid assay. The top isolates were further tested in 50 mL shake flasks and 1 L bioreactors. The strain NS281, which overexpresses the R. toruloides DGA1 gene, exhibited a threefold increase in lipid content compared to the wild-type strain NS18.

### Example 4: Method to Knockout Triacylglycerol Lipase Knockout Gene in Y. lipolytica

To knockout the triacylglycerol lipase gene (TGL3) in *Y. lipolytica*, a two-fragment deletion cassette was amplified by PCR from a plasmid containing the hygromycin resistance gene hph. The hph gene was split into two fragments that overlapped and were flanked by ~50 bp of homology to the upstream and downstream regions of the TGL3 coding sequence. The resulting PCR fragments were co-transformed into *Y. lipolytica* grown overnight on solid YPD media. Hygromycin-resistant colonies were screened by PCR to confirm the absence of the TGL3 gene and the presence of a specific product.

### Example 5: Cells that Overexpress Both DGA1 and DGA2 and That Contain a TGl3 Deletion Accumulate More TAGs Than Cells That Do Not Overexpress DGA2

To create a strain that overexpresses both DGA1 and DGA2 and contains a TGL3 deletion, the C. purpurea DGA2 gene was overexpressed in the strain NS377, which already overexpresses the R. toruloides DGA1 gene and has a TGL3 deletion. The resulting strain, NS432, exhibited significantly higher lipid content and productivity compared to the strain NS450, which only overexpresses the R. toruloides DGA1 gene and C. purpurea DGA2 gene without the TGL3 deletion.

### Example 6: Increasing the Activity of DGA1, DGA2, or DGA3 in Arxula adeninivorans

To increase the activity of DGA1, DGA2, or DGA3 in *Arxula adeninivorans*, the respective genes were overexpressed using strong constitutive promoters. The DGA1 gene from *Rhodosporidium toruloides*, the DGA2 gene from *Claviceps purpurea*, and the DGA3 gene from *Yarrowia lipolytica* were cloned into plasmids and introduced into *A. adeninivorans* using standard transformation protocols. Transformants were screened for lipid content using a fluorescence-based lipid assay. The top isolates were further tested in 50 mL shake flasks and 1 L bioreactors. The strains that overexpressed the respective genes exhibited increased lipid content compared to the wild-type strain.

### Example 7: Increasing the Activity of DGA1, DGA2, or DGA3 in Yarrowia lipolytica

To increase the activity of DGA1, DGA2, or DGA3 in *Yarrowia lipolytica*, the respective genes were overexpressed using strong constitutive promoters.
