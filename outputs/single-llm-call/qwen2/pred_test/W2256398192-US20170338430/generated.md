# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of photovoltaic devices, particularly to perovskite solar cells. More specifically, the invention pertains to a method and apparatus for enhancing the stability and efficiency of perovskite solar cells through the use of a tri-layer hole transport layer (HTL) comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD. The invention addresses the challenges associated with the degradation of perovskite materials in air and the optimization of energy level alignment for efficient charge carrier extraction.

## BACKGROUND ART

Perovskite solar cells have garnered significant attention due to their high power conversion efficiency (PCE) and low manufacturing costs. However, one of the primary challenges in the commercialization of these devices is their poor long-term stability, especially when exposed to environmental factors such as moisture and oxygen. The degradation of perovskite materials in air is often attributed to the presence of pinholes in the hole transport layer (HTL), which allow air molecules to interact with the perovskite layer, leading to a decline in device performance.

Traditional HTLs, such as spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD, are known to have pinholes that facilitate the diffusion of air molecules and mobile ions, thereby accelerating the degradation process. To mitigate this issue, various strategies have been explored, including the use of alternative HTL materials and the optimization of deposition techniques. However, these approaches often come with trade-offs, such as increased complexity and cost.

Recent studies have shown that the use of vacuum-evaporated HTLs can significantly improve the stability of perovskite solar cells. Vacuum evaporation allows for the formation of pinhole-free films, which act as effective barriers against environmental degradation. Additionally, the ability to control the doping concentration of the HTL through vacuum evaporation offers the potential to optimize the energy level alignment between the perovskite and the HTL, thereby enhancing the overall device performance.

Despite these advancements, there remains a need for a comprehensive approach that combines the benefits of pinhole-free HTLs with precise control over the energy levels to achieve both high efficiency and long-term stability in perovskite solar cells. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel tri-layer HTL structure that optimizes the doping concentration and energy level alignment, resulting in substantial improvements in device performance and stability.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for fabricating perovskite solar cells with enhanced stability and efficiency. The invention involves the use of a tri-layer hole transport layer (HTL) comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD. The n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited under optimized DMC vapor pressure to match the HOMO level of the HTL with the valence band maximum of the perovskite layer, ensuring efficient hole extraction. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer minimizes the inter-diffusion of n- and p-type dopants, while the p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer facilitates hole transport and enhances the overall device performance.

The method of the invention includes the following steps:
1. Depositing a compact layer of TiO2 on a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) substrate.
2. Forming a perovskite layer on the TiO2 layer by spin coating and thermal annealing.
3. Depositing an n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer on the perovskite layer using vacuum evaporation under optimized DMC vapor pressure.
4. Depositing an intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer on the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer using vacuum evaporation.
5. Depositing a p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer on the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer using co-evaporation of F4-TCNQ and spiro-OMeTAD.
6. Depositing a metal contact, such as gold, on the p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer to complete the solar cell structure.

The apparatus of the invention comprises a vacuum evaporation system equipped with a DMC vapor source, a spiro-OMeTAD source, and an F4-TCNQ source. The system is configured to deposit the n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers sequentially on the perovskite layer under controlled conditions to achieve the desired doping concentrations and energy level alignment.

The invention provides several advantages over existing technologies:
- **Enhanced Stability:** The pinhole-free tri-layer HTL acts as an effective barrier against environmental degradation, significantly improving the long-term stability of the perovskite solar cells.
- **Optimized Energy Level Alignment:** The precise control over the doping concentration ensures that the HOMO level of the HTL matches the valence band maximum of the perovskite layer, leading to efficient hole extraction and high open circuit voltage.
- **Improved Device Performance:** The combination of optimized energy level alignment and enhanced stability results in higher power conversion efficiency and longer operational lifetimes.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### Embodiment 1: Fabrication of Perovskite Solar Cells with Tri-Layer HTL

#### Step 1: Deposition of Compact TiO2 Layer
The fabrication process begins with the preparation of a fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrate. The FTO substrate is cleaned using a solution of hydrochloric acid (HCl) and zinc powder to remove any contaminants. A compact layer of TiO2 is then deposited on the FTO substrate using spray pyrolysis. The precursor solution for the TiO2 layer consists of acetylacetone, titanium (IV) isopropoxide, and anhydrous ethanol in a 3:3:2 molar ratio. The solution is sprayed onto the FTO substrate, and the layer is subsequently annealed at 480 °C on a hotplate to ensure a uniform and dense TiO2 layer.

#### Step 2: Formation of Perovskite Layer
Next, a perovskite layer is formed on the TiO2 layer. The perovskite solution is prepared by dissolving methylammonium iodide (MAI) and lead chloride (PbCl2) in N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) at a 2.5:1 molar ratio. The concentration of MAI in the solution is 2.2 M, and the concentration of PbCl2 is 0.88 M. The perovskite solution is spin-coated onto the TiO2 layer at 2000 rpm for 45 seconds, followed by thermal annealing at 100 °C for 45 minutes in a nitrogen (N2) glove box to ensure the formation of a high-quality perovskite film.

#### Step 3: Deposition of n-Type Doped Spiro-OMeTAD Layer
The n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited on the perovskite layer using vacuum evaporation. A glass ampule containing dimethyl carbonate (DMC) powder is placed in a vacuum chamber, and the ampule is heated to approximately 100 °C to evaporate the DMC powder. The base pressure of the vacuum chamber is maintained at 1.0 × 10^-8 Torr. The DMC vapor pressure is varied from 1.0 × 10^-6 to 5.0 × 10^-5 Torr to find the optimal doping concentration that matches the HOMO level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer with the valence band maximum of the perovskite layer. Under the optimal DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10^-5 Torr, the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited at a rate of 0.5 Å/s, as measured by a quartz crystal microbalance.

#### Step 4: Deposition of Intrinsic Spiro-OMeTAD Layer
Following the deposition of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer, an intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited in the same vacuum chamber. The intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer serves to minimize the inter-diffusion of n- and p-type dopants and is deposited at a rate of 0.5 Å/s to achieve a thickness of 20 nm.

#### Step 5: Deposition of p-Type Doped Spiro-OMeTAD Layer
The p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is deposited using co-evaporation of F4-TCNQ and spiro-OMeTAD. The sample is moved to a second vacuum chamber, and the F4-TCNQ and spiro-OMeTAD are co-evaporated at rates of 0.1 Å/s and 0.5 Å/s, respectively, to achieve a thickness of 30 nm. The F4-TCNQ concentration is set to 2 wt.% to ensure effective p-type doping.

#### Step 6: Deposition of Metal Contact
Finally, a 60 nm-thick gold contact is deposited on the p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer using thermal evaporation. The gold contact serves as the top electrode of the solar cell, completing the device structure.

### Embodiment 2: Optimization of DMC Vapor Pressure

The optimization of the DMC vapor pressure is crucial for achieving the desired energy level alignment between the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer and the perovskite layer. To determine the optimal DMC vapor pressure, a series of experiments are conducted where the DMC vapor pressure is varied from 1.0 × 10^-6 to 5.0 × 10^-5 Torr. The work function and HOMO level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer are measured using ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) for each vapor pressure condition.

The UPS measurements reveal that the work function decreases from 3.5 eV to 1.8 eV as the DMC vapor pressure increases from 1.0 × 10^-6 to 5.0 × 10^-5 Torr. Similarly, the HOMO level shifts from 1.5 eV to 4.6 eV below the Fermi level (EF) as the DMC vapor pressure increases. The optimal DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10^-5 Torr is identified, as it enables the HOMO level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer to match the valence band maximum of the perovskite layer, which is measured to be 2.6 eV below EF.

### Embodiment 3: Characterization of Device Performance and Stability

The performance and stability of the fabricated perovskite solar cells are characterized using a variety of techniques. The thicknesses of the n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers are measured using atomic force microscopy (AFM) and found to be 30 nm, 20 nm, and 30 nm, respectively. The crystalline structure of the perovskite layer is confirmed using X-ray diffraction (XRD), which shows characteristic perovskite (110) and (220) peaks at 14.2° and 28.5°, respectively.

The energy level alignment of the tri-layer HTL is further verified using UPS measurements. The UPS spectra of the perovskite, n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers are recorded, and the HOMO levels are determined. The HOMO level of the n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is found to be 2.5 eV below EF, which matches the valence band maximum of the perovskite layer. The HOMO level of the intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD layer is 1.5 eV below EF, and the HOMO level of the p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is 0.3 eV below EF, confirming the p-type doping effect.

The current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of the solar cells are measured under standard 1 sun AM1.5 simulated solar irradiation (100 mW/cm²). The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of the devices is calculated based on the short-circuit current density (Jsc), open circuit voltage (Voc), and fill factor (FF). The PCE of the solar cells with the tri-layer HTL is found to be significantly higher compared to reference devices with a standard spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD HTL.

To evaluate the long-term stability of the devices, the solar cell performance is monitored over a period of 890 hours in air and in high vacuum (10^-6 Torr). The PCE of the reference devices with a spin-coated spiro-OMeTAD HTL decreases and stabilizes to a value of approximately 50% in air and 60% in vacuum after 300 hours. In contrast, the PCE of the devices with the tri-layer HTL increases during the first 300 hours and remains stable thereafter, with the air-stored devices showing a slight improvement in PCE after 840 hours. The improvement in PCE is attributed to the reduced series resistance and enhanced hole extraction efficiency facilitated by the optimized energy level alignment and pinhole-free HTL.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for fabricating perovskite solar cells with a tri-layer hole transport layer (HTL) comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD. The invention addresses the challenges of environmental degradation and energy level alignment, resulting in substantial improvements in device performance and stability. The optimized doping concentration and energy level alignment achieved through the use of vacuum evaporation offer a promising route for the development of high-efficiency, high-stability perovskite solar cells.
