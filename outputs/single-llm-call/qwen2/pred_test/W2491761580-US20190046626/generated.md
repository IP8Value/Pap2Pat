# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and pharmaceuticals, specifically to the recombinant expression and purification of the Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) in Escherichia coli (E. coli). The invention further encompasses the use of the recombinant MOMP (rMOMP) in pharmaceutical compositions and methods for preventing or treating Chlamydia trachomatis infections.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular Gram-negative bacterium responsible for a range of pathologies, including ocular trachoma, lymphogranuloma venereum, and oculogenital diseases. These infections impose a significant medical and economic burden globally, making the development of a prophylactic vaccine highly desirable. Traditional vaccine candidates, such as formalin-inactivated Chlamydia trachomatis, have shown limited efficacy, leading researchers to explore subunit vaccines.

The Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) is a key component of the bacterium's outer membrane, accounting for about 60% of the mass of the elementary body (EB). MOMP is highly immunogenic and has been identified as a promising subunit vaccine candidate. Native MOMP (nMOMP) isolated from EBs has been shown to induce protective immune responses in animal models. However, the purification of nMOMP from infected cells is challenging and not scalable for commercial production.

Recombinant expression of MOMP in E. coli offers a viable alternative. However, previous attempts to express rMOMP in the cytoplasm of E. coli have resulted in the formation of inclusion bodies, leading to reduced immunogenicity. To address this issue, the present invention optimizes the recombinant expression of MOMP in the outer membrane of E. coli, ensuring the correct conformation and enhanced immunogenicity of the protein.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for the recombinant expression and purification of the Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) in the outer membrane of Escherichia coli (E. coli). The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified rMOMP and methods of using these compositions to prevent or treat Chlamydia trachomatis infections.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions may also include adjuvants to enhance the immunogenicity of the rMOMP. The pharmaceutical compositions are suitable for administration to a subject to prevent or treat Chlamydia trachomatis infections.

### Methods of Use

The invention provides methods for preventing or treating Chlamydia trachomatis infections in a subject. The methods involve administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition comprising the purified rMOMP. The administration can be via various routes, including subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intranasal, depending on the formulation and the desired therapeutic effect.

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences

The invention involves the use of codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding the Chlamydia MOMP. Codon harmonization is a technique that matches the codon usage in the recombinant host (E. coli) closely to the usage in the gene's native organism (Chlamydia trachomatis). This approach improves the yield and solubility of the recombinant protein by incorporating pauses in the translation process, which leads to improved protein folding and efficient biosynthesis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention comprise the purified recombinant Chlamydia MOMP (rMOMP) and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. Suitable adjuvants for use in the compositions of the present invention include, but are not limited to, aluminum salts (e.g., aluminum hydroxide, aluminum phosphate), oil emulsions (e.g., Montanide ISA 720 VG), and immunostimulatory oligonucleotides (e.g., CpG motifs). The pharmaceutical compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including subcutaneous, intramuscular, and intranasal. The compositions are prepared using standard techniques known in the art, ensuring stability, sterility, and appropriate dosing.

The methods of the present invention involve administering to a subject a therapeutically effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition comprising the purified rMOMP. The administration can be prophylactic or therapeutic, depending on the clinical context.

#### Prophylactic Use

For prophylactic use, the compositions are administered to subjects at risk of Chlamydia trachomatis infection. The administration schedule may involve multiple doses to ensure a robust immune response. The compositions can be used in vaccination programs to prevent the spread of Chlamydia trachomatis infections in populations.

#### Therapeutic Use

For therapeutic use, the compositions are administered to subjects already infected with Chlamydia trachomatis. The administration schedule may vary depending on the severity of the infection and the response to treatment. The compositions can be used in conjunction with other treatments, such as antibiotics, to enhance the overall therapeutic effect.

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences

The invention utilizes codon-harmonized nucleotide sequences encoding the Chlamydia MOMP. Codon harmonization is a technique that matches the codon usage in the recombinant host (E. coli) closely to the usage in the gene's native organism (Chlamydia trachomatis). This approach improves the yield and solubility of the recombinant protein by incorporating pauses in the translation process, which leads to improved protein folding and efficient biosynthesis.

#### Codon Harmonization Process

The codon harmonization process involves the following steps:
1. Retrieval of the native MOMP gene sequence from Chlamydia trachomatis.
2. Calculation of the codon usage frequency for both the native host (Chlamydia trachomatis) and the expression host (E. coli).
3. Selection of codons in the expression host that have the closest frequency to the corresponding codons in the native host.
4. Generation of the harmonized gene sequence for Chlamydia MOMP.

The harmonized gene sequence is then synthesized and cloned into an appropriate expression vector for transformation into E. coli.

## EXAMPLE 1

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP

#### Codon Harmonization

Nucleotide sequences of the gene encoding MOMP were retrieved from the Comprehensive Microbial Resources (CMR) database for the following strains: C. muridarum Nigg (strain MoPn) ORF TC0052, C. trachomatis strain D/UW-3/CX CT ORF TC_681 Serovar D, and C. trachomatis strain E/12–94 ORF O175_03780 Serovar E. The amino acid sequences were codon harmonized using the Codon Usage Database. The harmonized gene sequences were synthesized and cloned into the pUC57 cloning vector.

#### Cloning and Expression

The harmonized gene sequences were excised from the pUC57 vector and ligated into the pAVE029 expression vector. The recombinant plasmids were transformed into E. coli K12 W25113. The transformed cells were grown in Cinnabar media and induced with 0.4 mM IPTG for 4 hours at 30°C. The cell cultures were characterized by whole cell flow cytometry antibody binding, SDS-PAGE, and western blot analyses.

## EXAMPLE 2

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP

#### Cell Disruption and Membrane Fractionation

E. coli cells expressing rMOMP were harvested by centrifugation and resuspended in 50 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0 buffer with EDTA-free protease inhibitor. The cells were disrupted by microfluidization, and the undisrupted cells were removed by centrifugation. The membrane fraction was pelleted and washed with high salt buffer (1 M NaCl, 0.05% Tween-20) followed by another centrifugation.

#### Extraction and Purification

The washed membrane fraction was resuspended in buffer A (20 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0, 1 mM EDTA) with 1% Triton X-100 to remove the bacterial inner membrane. The pellets were resuspended in buffer A with 3% β-octyl-glucoside to remove other outer membrane proteins. rMOMP was extracted with buffer A containing 1% sarkosyl and 5 mM DTT. The extracted rMOMP was subjected to size exclusion chromatography (Sephacryl S300) and ion exchange chromatography (Hitrap Q FF). The purified rMOMP fractions were pooled and stored at 4°C.

## EXAMPLE 3

### Mouse Immunogenicity and Challenge Study

#### Immunization

Female C57BL/6 mice were immunized subcutaneously with 10 μg of purified rMOMP or nMOMP in combination with an adjuvant containing 50 μg CpG and 70% (w/v) Montanide ISA 720 VG. The immunizations were administered on days 0, 20, and 30.

#### Challenge

Two weeks after the last immunization, the mice were challenged intravaginally with 1 × 10^5 C. trachomatis serovar D EBs. Vaginal swabs were collected on days 7, 11, 14, 18, and 21 post-challenge and analyzed by real-time PCR to quantify Chlamydia shedding.

#### Results

The rMOMP immunized mice showed comparable IgG Fcγ titers and antibody profiles to the nMOMP immunized group. The rMOMP immunized mice also exhibited a significant reduction in Chlamydial shedding compared to the adjuvant-only control group, with similar bacterial loads post-challenge as the nMOMP immunized group.

## EXAMPLE 4

### Circular Dichroism Spectroscopy

Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy was performed to analyze the secondary structure of the purified rMOMP. The CD spectra of rMOMP and nMOMP both showed a peak at ~215 nm, indicating the presence of β-strand structure. The rMOMP appeared to have a higher mean molar ellipticity compared to nMOMP, possibly due to differences in the extraction and purification methods.

## EXAMPLE 5

### Evaluation of Expression Vectors

A panel of E. coli expression vectors was evaluated to optimize the surface expression of rMOMP. The key elements affecting the outer membrane expression included promoter strength and vector copy number. Vectors with moderate promoters and low copy numbers, such as pAVE029, resulted in the highest surface expression levels of rMOMP.

## EXAMPLE 6

### Optimization of Secretion Leader Sequences

Different secretion leader sequences were evaluated to improve the outer membrane localization of rMOMP. Leader sequences from E. coli OmpA and OmpP, as well as omptins (SopA, PgtE, and Pla), and the PelB leader, all resulted in detectable surface expression of rMOMP. The native leader sequences of C. trachomatis serovars D and E did not result in surface expression.

## EXAMPLE 7

### Optimization of Expression Conditions

The surface expression of rMOMP was optimized by evaluating induction time, temperature, cell density, and cell culture medium. The optimal conditions for rMOMP expression were found to be induction for 4 hours at 30°C in cells grown in Cinnabar medium when the cell density reached ~0.5 OD590.

## EXAMPLE 8

### Purification Yield and Purity

The total yield of purified rMOMP was about 6 mg per liter of cell culture. The purity of the rMOMP preparation was estimated to be about 70% as determined by mass spectrometry. The endotoxin level in the final purified protein sample was undetectable with an anti-E. coli lipo-oligosaccharide (LPS) antibody on western blot.

## EXAMPLE 9

### SDS-PAGE and Western Blot Analysis

The purified rMOMP migrated similarly to the native MOMP on SDS-PAGE. The rMOMP showed a slightly higher amount of dimeric and oligomeric forms that were SDS-resistant. Western blot analysis confirmed the complete processing of the PelB leader sequence and the absence of detectable endotoxin in the final purified protein sample.

## EXAMPLE 10

### Evaluation of Co-Expressed Chaperones and Oxidoreductases

Co-expression of peptidyl-prolyl cis/trans-isomerases (SurA, PpiD, FkpA, PpiA) and disulfide bond-forming enzymes (DsbA, DsbC) was evaluated to improve the efficiency of periplasmic folding and outer membrane expression of rMOMP. The results showed that co-expression of these enzymes significantly enhanced the surface expression level of rMOMP.

## EXAMPLE 11

### Comparison with Other Challenge Models

The vaccine efficacy of rMOMP was evaluated in a more stringent C. muridarum mouse challenge model. The results showed that immunization with rMOMP resulted in similar levels of post-challenge bacterial burden as the nMOMP immunized group, confirming the role of adaptive immune response in the protection against Chlamydia trachomatis infection.

## EXAMPLE 12

### Application in Vaccine Research

The methodology for recombinant expression of MOMP in the outer membrane of E. coli can serve as a platform to produce other outer membrane proteins for vaccine research. The platform can be used to develop vaccines against other pathogens and to produce useful reagents for research.
