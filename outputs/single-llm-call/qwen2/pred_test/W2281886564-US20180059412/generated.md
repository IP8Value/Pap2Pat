# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of nanophotonics and, more specifically, to a method and device for tuning the optical resonances of photonic resonators. The invention provides a scalable, high-precision technique for tuning the resonant frequencies of photonic resonators, particularly GaAs disk resonators, using resonant cavity-enhanced photoelectrochemical (PEC) etching. This method enables the precise and selective adjustment of the resonant wavelengths of multiple resonators simultaneously, thereby facilitating the alignment of their optical properties.

## PRIOR ART

In the realm of nanophotonics, the ability to precisely control the optical properties of photonic resonators is crucial for a wide range of applications, including optical communication, sensing, and quantum information processing. Conventional methods for tuning the resonant frequencies of photonic resonators often involve mechanical, thermal, or chemical processes. However, these methods suffer from limitations such as low precision, lack of spatial selectivity, and difficulty in scaling to multiple resonators.

One known method for tuning photonic resonators is the use of photoelectrochemical (PEC) etching. In standard PEC etching, a semiconductor is optically pumped above its bandgap in the presence of an electrically conductive liquid, leading to the generation of ionic species that can be dissolved in the electrolyte. This process can be used to etch the semiconductor and adjust its dimensions, thereby tuning its optical resonances. However, standard PEC etching lacks the spatial selectivity and precision required for advanced nanophotonic applications.

Recent advancements have explored the use of resonant PEC etching, where the semiconductor is optically pumped at the resonance frequency of a photonic cavity. This approach leverages the enhanced light-matter interaction within the cavity to achieve higher spatial selectivity and precision. However, the scalability of resonant PEC etching to multiple resonators has been a significant challenge.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the aforementioned challenges by providing a method and device for high-precision, scalable tuning of photonic resonators using resonant cavity-enhanced photoelectrochemical (PEC) etching. The invention enables the simultaneous and precise adjustment of the resonant frequencies of multiple photonic resonators, making it particularly suitable for applications requiring the alignment of optical properties across an ensemble of resonators.

### The Method

The method involves the following steps:
1. **Preparation of the Resonators**: Fabricate photonic resonators, such as GaAs disk resonators, with specific dimensions and optical properties. Isolate the resonators from the substrate using a suitable material, such as an aluminum gallium arsenide (AlGaAs) pedestal.
2. **Immersion in a Conductive Liquid**: Immerse the resonators in a conductive liquid, such as water, to enable the PEC etching process. The liquid provides the necessary ionic species for the etching reaction.
3. **Optical Pumping**: Use a laser to optically pump the resonators at their resonant frequencies. The laser light is coupled into the resonators via waveguides, and the resonant enhancement of the light within the cavities facilitates the generation of ionic species.
4. **Tuning Process**: Continuously monitor the optical resonances of the resonators by sweeping the laser wavelength over the resonances. Adjust the laser power and the number of tuning cycles to achieve the desired amount of tuning. The resonant PEC etching process preferentially etches the resonators at their resonant frequencies, leading to a blue shift in their resonant wavelengths.
5. **Scalability**: For multiple resonators, the method can be scaled by sequentially tuning the resonators from the largest to the smallest. The laser wavelength is adjusted to follow the resonant wavelengths of the resonators as they are tuned, ensuring that all resonators are brought to the same resonant frequency.
6. **Finalization**: Once the desired tuning is achieved, switch off the laser and dry the sample. The tuned resonators exhibit improved optical properties, such as higher quality factors (Q-factors) and reduced optical losses.

### The Device

The device for implementing the method includes:
1. **Photonic Resonators**: High-Q photonic resonators, such as GaAs disk resonators, with specific dimensions and optical properties.
2. **Waveguides**: Evanescently coupled waveguides for delivering the laser light to the resonators.
3. **Laser Source**: A tunable laser source capable of generating light at the resonant frequencies of the resonators.
4. **Conductive Liquid**: A conductive liquid, such as water, for enabling the PEC etching process.
5. **Control System**: A control system for monitoring and adjusting the laser wavelength and power, as well as for managing the tuning cycles.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### The Photonic Device

The photonic device of the present invention comprises high-Q photonic resonators, such as GaAs disk resonators, with typical dimensions of 320 nm in thickness and 1 μm in radius. These resonators can support whispering gallery modes (WGMs) with mode volumes less than 1 μm³, making them ideal for various nanophotonic applications. The resonators are isolated from the substrate by an AlGaAs pedestal, which helps to minimize substrate-induced losses and improve the Q-factors of the resonators.

The resonators are fabricated from a GaAs/AlGaAs/GaAs epitaxial wafer using electron beam lithography, non-selective inductively-coupled plasma reactive ion etching, and selective under-etching in a dilute hydrofluoric acid solution. Waveguides with nanoscale transverse dimensions are integrated into the GaAs layer to allow evanescent optical coupling of light into the disk's WGMs. The large refractive index of GaAs ensures that the chip can be immersed in a transparent liquid while preserving the confinement and guiding of light in the resonators and waveguides.

### Installation Set-Up

The installation set-up for the resonant PEC etching process involves the following components:
1. **Resonators and Waveguides**: The fabricated GaAs disk resonators are placed in close proximity to the waveguides, which are designed to couple light into the resonators.
2. **Conductive Liquid**: A microlitre droplet of a conductive liquid, such as water, is applied to cover the resonators and waveguides. The liquid provides the necessary ionic species for the PEC etching process.
3. **Laser Source**: A tunable laser source is used to generate light at the resonant frequencies of the resonators. The laser is coupled into the waveguides using appropriate optical components.
4. **Control System**: A control system is used to monitor the optical resonances of the resonators and adjust the laser wavelength and power. The control system can also manage the tuning cycles, ensuring that the resonators are tuned to the desired resonant frequencies.

### Method for Tuning an Optical Resonator (FIG. 7)

The method for tuning an optical resonator using resonant PEC etching involves the following steps:
1. **Initial Setup**: Place the resonators and waveguides in a conductive liquid and couple the laser light into the waveguides.
2. **Optical Pumping**: Set the laser wavelength to the resonant frequency of the target resonator and apply a continuous or pulsed laser light to optically pump the resonator.
3. **Monitoring and Tuning**: Continuously monitor the optical resonances of the resonator by sweeping the laser wavelength over the resonances. Adjust the laser power and the number of tuning cycles to achieve the desired amount of tuning. The resonant PEC etching process preferentially etches the resonator at its resonant frequency, leading to a blue shift in its resonant wavelength.
4. **Finalization**: Once the desired tuning is achieved, switch off the laser and dry the sample. The tuned resonator exhibits improved optical properties, such as higher Q-factors and reduced optical losses.

### Method for Tuning Several Resonators at a Targeted Value (FIG. 8)

The method for tuning several resonators to a targeted value using resonant PEC etching involves the following steps:
1. **Initial Setup**: Place the resonators and waveguides in a conductive liquid and couple the laser light into the waveguides.
2. **Sequential Tuning**: Start with the largest resonator and set the laser wavelength to its resonant frequency. Apply a continuous or pulsed laser light to optically pump the resonator and monitor its resonant wavelength. Adjust the laser power and the number of tuning cycles to bring the resonator to the targeted resonant frequency.
3. **Progressive Alignment**: As the largest resonator is tuned, adjust the laser wavelength to follow the resonant wavelengths of the next largest resonator. Continue this process until all resonators are brought to the same resonant frequency.
4. **Finalization**: Once all resonators are tuned to the targeted value, switch off the laser and dry the sample. The tuned resonators exhibit improved optical properties and are spectrally aligned.

### Different Embodiments for Varying the Laser Wavelength in Order to Tune Optical Resonators

The invention can be implemented using different embodiments to vary the laser wavelength and achieve the desired tuning of the optical resonators:
1. **Continuous Laser Sweep**: In this embodiment, the laser wavelength is continuously swept over the resonant frequencies of the resonators. The laser power and the sweep rate are adjusted to achieve the desired amount of tuning. This method is particularly useful for tuning multiple resonators to a targeted value.
2. **Pulsed Laser Sweeps**: In this embodiment, the laser wavelength is rapidly swept back and forth over the resonant frequencies of the resonators in a series of pulses. Each pulse corresponds to a single tuning cycle, and the number of cycles is adjusted to achieve the desired amount of tuning. This method provides high precision and control over the tuning process.
3. **Step-by-Step Tuning**: In this embodiment, the laser wavelength is adjusted in discrete steps to tune the resonators. The laser power and the duration of each step are adjusted to achieve the desired amount of tuning. This method is particularly useful for fine-tuning the resonators to specific resonant frequencies.

### Parameters and Experimental Data

The parameters and experimental data for the resonant PEC etching process are as follows:
1. **Resonator Dimensions**: The GaAs disk resonators have typical dimensions of 320 nm in thickness and 1 μm in radius.
2. **Resonant Frequencies**: The resonant frequencies of the resonators are determined by their dimensions and the refractive index of GaAs. The resonant frequencies can be tuned by adjusting the size of the resonators through the resonant PEC etching process.
3. **Laser Power**: The laser power is typically in the range of 1 μW to 1 mW, depending on the desired amount of tuning and the precision required.
4. **Tuning Precision**: The tuning precision of the resonant PEC etching process is on the order of picometers (pm), with a blue shift of 7.2 pm per tuning cycle. The precision can be further improved by using a more accurate laser and control system.
5. **Tuning Speed**: The tuning speed is on the order of 0.5 to 1 nm of radius change per second for 1 μW of continuous optical power injected into the cavity WGM. The tuning speed can be adjusted by varying the laser power and the number of tuning cycles.
6. **Experimental Results**: Experiments have shown that the resonant PEC etching process can improve the Q-factors of the resonators by up to 70,000 and reduce optical losses. The method has been successfully used to tune multiple resonators to a targeted value, achieving spectral alignment with high precision.

### Characteristics of the Tuned Optical Resonator

The characteristics of the tuned optical resonator are as follows:
1. **Resonant Wavelength**: The resonant wavelength of the tuned resonator is shifted to the desired value, with a blue shift of up to several tens of nanometers.
2. **Quality Factor (Q-factor)**: The Q-factor of the tuned resonator is significantly improved, with values ranging from 52,000 to 70,000, depending on the initial conditions and the tuning process.
3. **Optical Losses**: The tuned resonator exhibits reduced optical losses, indicating that the resonant PEC etching process has smoothed out geometric irregularities and improved the optical properties of the resonator.
4. **Stability**: The tuned resonator is stable and retains its optical properties over time, making it suitable for long-term applications.
5. **Scalability**: The method is scalable and can be used to tune multiple resonators simultaneously, achieving spectral alignment with high precision.

By providing a method and device for high-precision, scalable tuning of photonic resonators using resonant cavity-enhanced photoelectrochemical etching, the present invention opens new avenues for the development of advanced nanophotonic devices and systems. The invention is particularly useful for applications requiring the precise control and alignment of optical properties across multiple resonators, such as in optical communication, sensing, and quantum information processing.
