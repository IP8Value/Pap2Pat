# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Thermotoga, a group of bacteria capable of thriving at temperatures up to 90°C, have been isolated from diverse environments such as heated sea floors, continental hot springs, and oil fields. These strict anaerobes represent some of the earliest forms of life on Earth, as indicated by their deep branching in the 16S rRNA phylogenetic tree. Their unique ability to hydrolyze polysaccharides and produce hydrogen gas through fermentative catabolism has sparked significant interest in their potential for biomass-based clean energy production. However, the lack of genetic tools has hindered comprehensive genetic manipulation and functional genomics studies of Thermotoga. This invention addresses the challenge by providing an improved method for the cultivation and genetic transformation of Thermotoga, enabling the stable expression and maintenance of heterologous genes in these hyperthermophilic bacteria.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to an improved method for the cultivation and genetic transformation of Thermotoga bacteria. Specifically, the invention provides a novel Thermotoga-E. coli shuttle vector, designated pDH10, which carries a thermostable kanamycin adenyl transferase gene (kan) for antibiotic selection and the replication origin of the cryptic plasmid pRQ7 for autonomous replication in Thermotoga. The invention also includes methods for preparing competent Thermotoga cells, transforming them with pDH10 via electroporation or liposome-mediated transformation, and maintaining the transformed DNA stably in the host cells. Additionally, the invention describes an embedded growth method for handling Thermotoga cultures in an aerobic environment, enhancing plating efficiency and facilitating the isolation of transformants.

### Improved Method for Cultivation of Thermotoga

The success of genetic manipulation in Thermotoga is contingent upon efficient methods for handling and cultivating these bacteria. Traditional methods often require anaerobic chambers, which are expensive and cumbersome. The present invention introduces an embedded growth method that simplifies the process and enhances plating efficiency.

#### Embedded Growth Method

The embedded growth method involves suspending diluted Thermotoga cultures in a hot SVO medium containing 0.3% Gelrite. This mixture is then poured into Petri dishes and allowed to solidify, embedding the cells in the medium matrix. This approach reduces the exposure of Thermotoga to oxygen, thereby increasing plating efficiency. For example, a 10^4 dilution of an overnight culture of T. sp. RQ7 can form approximately 1.26 × 10^9 colony-forming units (CFU) per ml, achieving a plating efficiency close to 50%.

#### Soft SVO Medium

To facilitate the transfer of single colonies from solid to liquid media under aerobic conditions, a soft SVO medium is introduced. This medium contains 0.075% Gelrite, which creates a local anaerobic environment at the bottom of test tubes. Single colonies are picked from plates and pushed down to the bottom of the test tubes containing soft SVO. After 12-24 hours of incubation, the cultures are transferred to liquid SVO using a syringe. This method ensures maximum viability of Thermotoga cells during the transfer process and can also serve as an excellent storage medium for long-term culture maintenance.

### Strains and Cultivation Conditions

#### Bacterial Strains and Vectors

- **E. coli DH5α**: Used for the construction of vectors and grown at 37°C in Luria Broth (LB) with 1.5% agar for plates. Ampicillin (100 μg/ml) is added when necessary.
- **Thermotoga neapolitana ATCC 49049**: Purchased from ATCC and grown at 77°C in SVO medium.
- **T. sp. RQ7 and T. maritima MSB8**: Provided by Dr. Harald Huber, University of Regensburg, Germany, and grown at 77°C in SVO medium.

#### SVO Medium Preparation

SVO medium is prepared by dispensing 50 ml of the medium into 100 ml serum bottles, sparging with nitrogen gas to remove oxygen, sealing with rubber stoppers and aluminum caps, and sterilizing. For soft SVO, 0.075% Gelrite is added to liquid SVO. For solid SVO, double-strength liquid SVO and various concentrations of agar or Gelrite are mixed and poured into Petri dishes. The medium is incubated in an anaerobic jar containing a palladium catalyst to exchange the atmosphere to 96:4 N2-H2.

### Purification and Analyses of CTN 0339 and CTN 0340 Gene Products

The purification and analysis of the gene products of CTN 0339 and CTN 0340 are crucial for understanding their functions and interactions within Thermotoga. These genes are involved in the restriction and modification systems, which play a significant role in the defense against foreign DNA.

#### Purification

The gene products of CTN 0339 and CTN 0340 are purified using standard chromatographic techniques, including ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. The purified proteins are then analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting to confirm their identity and purity.

#### Functional Analysis

The functional properties of the purified proteins are assessed through restriction and modification assays. These assays involve incubating the proteins with various DNA substrates and analyzing the products by agarose gel electrophoresis. The results provide insights into the specific activities and substrate preferences of the gene products.

### Determination of the Cleavage Site of the REase

The determination of the cleavage site of the restriction enzyme (REase) is essential for understanding its mechanism of action and for optimizing its use in genetic engineering applications.

#### Cleavage Site Analysis

The cleavage site of the REase is determined by digesting a known DNA sequence with the enzyme and analyzing the products by sequencing. The cleavage site is identified by comparing the sequence of the digested products with the original sequence. This information is used to design specific recognition sequences for the REase, which can be incorporated into plasmids and other genetic constructs.

### Restriction and Modification Assays of the Thermotoga Proteins

Restriction and modification assays are performed to evaluate the activity and specificity of the Thermotoga proteins involved in the restriction and modification systems.

#### Assay Procedures

- **Restriction Assay**: The purified REase is incubated with various DNA substrates, and the products are analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. The presence of cleaved DNA fragments indicates the activity of the REase.
- **Modification Assay**: The purified methyltransferase (MTase) is incubated with DNA substrates in the presence of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM). The modified DNA is then tested for resistance to the REase. The presence of uncut DNA indicates the activity of the MTase.

### Overexpression of R.TneDI

Overexpression of the restriction endonuclease R.TneDI is achieved by introducing a plasmid carrying the R.TneDI gene into a suitable host organism, such as E. coli.

#### Plasmid Construction

A plasmid carrying the R.TneDI gene is constructed using standard molecular biology techniques. The gene is amplified by PCR and cloned into a high-copy-number plasmid vector. The plasmid is then transformed into E. coli, and the expression of R.TneDI is induced by adding an appropriate inducer, such as IPTG.

#### Protein Purification

The overexpressed R.TneDI protein is purified using affinity chromatography, followed by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. The purified protein is analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting to confirm its identity and purity.

### Determination of the Cleavage Site of R.TneDI

The cleavage site of R.TneDI is determined by digesting a known DNA sequence with the enzyme and analyzing the products by sequencing.

#### Cleavage Site Analysis

The DNA sequence is digested with R.TneDI, and the products are analyzed by sequencing. The cleavage site is identified by comparing the sequence of the digested products with the original sequence. This information is used to design specific recognition sequences for R.TneDI, which can be incorporated into plasmids and other genetic constructs.
