# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of *Pinus sylvestris* and its use in enhancing the immune response. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of PPC to modulate the primary and secondary T cell responses, particularly by enhancing the proliferation and survival of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells during the primary T cell response.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The therapeutic potential of natural products has been recognized for centuries. Ancient texts, such as De Materia Medica by Pedanius Dioscorides, document the use of pinecone extracts for treating various ailments, including kidney and digestive issues and skin conditions like psoriasis. Similarly, the inhabitants of the island of Kyushu in Japan have traditionally used aqueous extracts from pinecones to treat a wide range of illnesses, from infectious diseases to cancer. These anecdotal reports suggest that the effects of pinecone extracts are likely mediated through the immune system.

Recent scientific investigations have focused on a specific extract, polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC), derived from the cones of *Pinus sylvestris*. Studies have shown that PPC can bias the immune response towards a T helper type 1 (Th1) response and significantly enhance the generation of antigen-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T cells during a secondary T cell response. Additionally, PPC has been found to induce the rapid production of dendritic-like cells in vitro, suggesting its potential to modulate immune cell function.

Despite these promising findings, the mechanisms by which PPC enhances the immune response, particularly during the primary T cell response, remain incompletely understood. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a detailed description of how PPC affects the primary T cell response, specifically by enhancing the expansion of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells during the early phase of the primary response.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for enhancing the immune response using a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of *Pinus sylvestris*. The method involves administering PPC to a subject to enhance the primary T cell response, particularly by increasing the number of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells. The invention also encompasses the use of PPC in combination with other immunostimulatory agents or therapeutic interventions to improve the overall immune response.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for enhancing the expansion of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells during the primary T cell response. The method includes the step of administering PPC to a subject prior to or concurrently with an immunogenic stimulus, such as an antigen or superantigen. The administration of PPC results in a significant increase in the number of CD8+ T cells specific to the antigen, thereby enhancing the immune response.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a composition comprising PPC and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition can be formulated for oral, intravenous, or intraperitoneal administration and is suitable for use in enhancing the immune response in subjects with compromised immune systems or in the context of vaccine development.

The invention further provides methods for preparing PPC and its use in various therapeutic applications, including the treatment of infectious diseases, cancer, and autoimmune disorders. The invention also includes methods for evaluating the efficacy of PPC in enhancing the immune response, such as through the measurement of cytokine levels, T cell proliferation, and the expression of apoptosis-related genes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Preparation of PPC

The polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) used in the present invention is derived from the cones of *Pinus sylvestris*. The cones are washed extensively in deionized water and then shredded. The shredded pinecones are loaded into a stainless steel reaction vessel and extracted with water at an elevated pH (12.0) and temperature (121°C) for 30 minutes. The resulting extract is standardized by UV spectroscopy at a wavelength of 280 nm and by a series of biological assays to ensure the absence of contaminants such as herbicides, pesticides, heavy metals, endotoxin, or microorganisms. The liquid extract is then spray-dried to form a dark brown, odorless, stable powder.

The powdered extract is suspended in sterile water to a stock concentration of 25 mg/mL (w/v), centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 20 minutes to remove particulates, and filtered through a 0.2 μm nylon filter. The concentration of active ingredients is defined by the optical density (OD) at 280 nm using the following equation:

\[ \text{PPC conc (mg/mL)} = \text{OD}_{280} \times \left( \frac{1}{22.8 \, \text{L} \cdot \text{g}^{-1} \cdot \text{cm}^{-1}} \right) \]

where 22.8 L·g^-1·cm^-1 is the extinction coefficient for polyphenylpropanoids. A working stock containing 200 μg/mL of polyphenylpropanoids diluted in sterile distilled water is prepared fresh each week.

### In Vivo Immune Stimulation

To evaluate the immune-stimulating properties of PPC, an in vivo model using Balb/c mice is employed. On Day 0, three mice in the SEB group are injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) with 100 μg of Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) dissolved in 100 μL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), pH 7.4. Three mice in the Naive group are injected i.p. with 100 μL PBS, pH 7.4. The mice in the SEB + PPC group are gavaged with 100 μL of a 200 μg/mL solution of PPC three times daily on Day 0 and Day 1 for a total of 6 doses. At 48 or 96 hours post-SEB immunization, the mice are euthanized by CO2 inhalation. The set of inguinal lymph nodes from each mouse is pooled and used in various assays.

### ELISA Assays

Standard ELISA development kits specific for murine IL-2, IFNγ, and IL-12p70 are purchased from Peprotech. The detection of serum IL-2, IFNγ, and IL-12p70 is performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The absorbance of the final product is measured using BioTek’s μQuant plate spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 450 nm. Standard curves are prepared using GraphPad Prism software to quantify the cytokine levels.

### Lymph Node and Spleen Cell Isolation

Isolated inguinal lymph nodes and spleens are homogenized in a sterile plastic bag containing 4 mL Hanks balanced salt solution (HBSS), pH 7.4, using a Seward Stomacher®80 with settings of medium speed for 60 seconds. The resulting cell suspensions are pelleted and then suspended in 2 mL HBSS (for lymph node cells) or 4 mL HBSS (for spleen cells). The yield of cells is determined using the Millipore Muse cell counter. The cells are pelleted and then suspended in complete media (RPMI 1640 containing 10% fetal calf serum and 1x penicillin/streptomycin) to provide a final concentration of 4 × 10^6 cells/mL.

### Flow Cytometry

For each mouse, 1 × 10^6 lymph node cells are added to a 1.5 mL microfuge tube and pelleted by centrifugation for 1 minute at 1000 × g. The pelleted cells are placed on ice and suspended in 100 μL of FACS buffer (PBS containing 10% fetal bovine serum and 0.1% sodium azide) containing 1 μL anti-CD16/32 Fc blocking antibody (clone 93). After 10 minutes, 1–5 μL of fluorescent antibodies specific for cell surface antigens are added. The fluorescent anti-mouse antibodies used to detect the SEB-activated T lymphocytes are as follows: CD3 (clone 17A2) labeled with allophycocyanin (APC), CD4 (clone GK1.5) labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC), CD8 (clone SK1) labeled with phycoerythrin (PE), and Vβ8 (clone KJ16-133) labeled with a proprietary peridinin chlorophyll protein conjugated with eFluor710 (PerCP-eFluor®710). The cells are incubated on ice for 30 minutes, washed with 500 μL FACS buffer, and analyzed by flow cytometry using a BD FACSCalibur equipped with CellQuest Pro software. The FACS data is further analyzed using FloJo software.

### Detection of Apoptosis

Annexin V staining is performed according to the protocol provided with eBioscience’s Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit. Isolated lymph node cells (1 × 10^6/mouse) are suspended in Annexin V staining buffer containing 1 μL APC-labeled anti-CD3, 5 μL PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8, and 1 μL FITC-labeled Annexin V to identify T cells undergoing apoptosis. The cells are incubated at room temperature for 10 minutes, washed in binding buffer, and analyzed by flow cytometry.

### Detection of Reactive Oxygen Species

To detect the presence of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in activated T cells, isolated lymph node cells from each mouse are exposed to Invitrogen’s CM-H2DCFDA. CM-H2DCFDA passively diffuses into cells where its acetate groups are cleaved by intracellular esterases and its thiol-reactive chloromethyl group reacts with intracellular glutathione and other thiols. Oxidation then produces a fluorescent adduct that cannot escape the cell. The resulting fluorescence is measured by flow cytometry. Isolated lymph node cells (1 × 10^6/mouse) are exposed to 100 μL FACS buffer containing 1 μL APC-labeled anti-CD3 antibody, 5 μL PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8 antibody, and 2 μL of a 50 μM solution of CM-H2DCFDA for 15 minutes at 37°C. The cells are washed with 500 μL FACS buffer, pelleted, and suspended in 200 μL FACS buffer. The levels of fluorescence within the various cell samples are measured by flow cytometry.

### Detection of Intracellular Bcl-2

To detect the intracellular levels of the anti-apoptosis related protein, Bcl-2, cells previously stained with APC-labeled anti-CD3 and PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8 antibodies are washed in 0.03% saponin dissolved in HBSS (HBSS-SAP) and pelleted by centrifugation at 1000 × g for 2 minutes. The cell pellets are suspended in 100 μL HBSS-SAP containing 1 μL FITC-labeled anti-Bcl-2 antibody (clone 10C4, eBioscience) and incubated on ice for 60 minutes. The cells are washed twice with 500 μL HBSS-SAP buffer and once with HBSS only. The cells are then suspended in 200 μL HBSS and analyzed by flow cytometry.

### Analysis of Gene Expression

Total RNA is isolated from lymph node cells using Qiagen’s RNeasy Plus Mini Kit following the manufacturer’s instructions. The resulting RNA is eluted in 30 μL sterile RNase-free water, and the yield of RNA is determined by spectrophotometry at an optical density of 260 nm. One microgram of total RNA is reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the QuantiTect Reverse Transcription kit from Qiagen. SABiosciences’ RT2 Profiler qPCR SYBR green master mix kit is used for real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR). One microliter of the cDNA per reaction is added to a 1.5 mL microfuge tube containing 12.5 μL 2x SYBR green/fluorescein master mix and 10.5 μL H2O per reaction needed. Validated primer pairs from SABiosciences' RT2 qPCR Primer Assays specific for murine Bcl-2, Bcl-3, Bcl-xL, Bax, and Bim are utilized. These primers are pre-mixed at a concentration of 10 μM. One microliter of each primer pair is placed in duplicate wells of a 96-well PCR plate, and 24 μL of the cDNA/SYBR green master mix is added to the appropriate wells. Once all the samples have been added to the plate, the plate is covered with clear plastic film and centrifuged at 200 × g for 1 minute to mix and collect all the solutions in the bottom of the wells. The plate is then placed in a BioRad iCycler programmed to run one cycle of 10 minutes at 95°C to activate the polymerase and then 40 cycles of [95°C × 15 sec → 60°C × 1 min] to amplify the gene-specific cDNA. The relative levels of mRNA expression are normalized to the mouse β-actin housekeeping gene expressed in lymph node cells from naive (untreated) mice and determined by the ∆∆-Ct method.

The preparation of PPC involves the following steps:
1. **Washing and Shredding**: Pinecones from *Pinus sylvestris* are washed extensively in deionized water and then shredded.

3. **Standardization**: The resulting extract is standardized by UV spectroscopy at a wavelength of 280 nm and by a series of biological assays to ensure the absence of contaminants.
4. **Spray-Drying**: The liquid extract is spray-dried to form a dark brown, odorless, stable powder.
5. **Suspension and Filtration**: The powdered extract is suspended in sterile water to a stock concentration of 25 mg/mL (w/v), centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 20 minutes to remove particulates, and filtered through a 0.2 μm nylon filter.
6. **Concentration Determination**: The concentration of active ingredients is defined by the optical density (OD) at 280 nm using the equation provided in the detailed description.

To evaluate the immune-stimulating properties of PPC, the following in vivo experiment is conducted:
1. **Animal Groups**: Three groups of Balb/c mice are used: Naive, SEB, and SEB + PPC.
