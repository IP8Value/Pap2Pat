# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method and apparatus for controlling bubble nucleation during boiling processes. More specifically, the invention pertains to the active control of bubble nucleation by electrostatically adsorbing and desorbing charged surfactants using small electric potentials. This technique allows for the rapid and precise modulation of boiling behavior, including the ability to turn bubble formation "on" and "off" with sub-second precision. The invention has broad applications in various industries, including heat transfer enhancement, electronics cooling, and flow boiling systems.

## BACKGROUND ART

Boiling is a critical process in many industrial and technological applications, such as heat exchangers, cooling systems, and power generation. The efficiency and control of boiling processes are essential for optimizing performance and reliability. Traditional methods for controlling boiling often involve modifying the surface properties of the boiling surface or altering the fluid composition. However, these methods are static and do not allow for dynamic control of the boiling process.

Recent advancements in the field have explored the use of electric fields to influence boiling behavior. For example, electrowetting and electrohydrodynamic (EHD) effects have been studied for their potential to modulate wettability and heat transfer. However, these techniques often require high voltages and complex setups, limiting their practicality in real-world applications.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a method and apparatus for dynamically controlling bubble nucleation using charged surfactants and small electric potentials. This approach offers a simple, efficient, and scalable solution for enhancing and modulating boiling performance.

## SUMMARY OF EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for controlling bubble nucleation during boiling processes. The method involves the use of charged surfactants and small electric potentials to electrostatically adsorb and desorb the surfactants on the boiling surface. By adjusting the electric potential, the wettability of the surface can be modulated, thereby controlling the nucleation of bubbles.

In one embodiment, the method comprises the steps of:
1. Providing a boiling surface and a counter electrode immersed in a fluid containing charged surfactants.
2. Applying a constant heat input to the boiling surface.
3. Adjusting the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode to control the adsorption and desorption of the charged surfactants.
4. Monitoring and adjusting the electric potential to achieve the desired level of bubble nucleation.

The apparatus for implementing the method includes:
1. A boiling surface capable of being heated.
2. A counter electrode immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants.
3. A power supply for applying a constant heat input to the boiling surface.
4. A voltage source for adjusting the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode.
5. A control system for monitoring and adjusting the electric potential to control bubble nucleation.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for spatially and temporally controlling boiling. The method involves the use of multiple independently addressable electrodes on the boiling surface, each capable of being switched between different electric potentials. By selectively activating and deactivating specific electrodes, the method allows for precise control of bubble nucleation in localized areas of the boiling surface.

The apparatus for spatially and temporally controlling boiling includes:
1. A boiling surface with multiple independently addressable electrodes.
2. A counter electrode immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants.
3. A power supply for applying a constant heat input to the boiling surface.
4. Multiple voltage sources for adjusting the electric potential of each electrode.
5. A control system for monitoring and adjusting the electric potential of each electrode to control bubble nucleation in localized areas.

The invention further provides a method for optimizing the performance of boiling processes by modulating the heat transfer coefficient (HTC) and critical heat flux (CHF). By adjusting the electric potential, the method allows for the tuning of boiling curves, enabling the optimization of performance for a variety of conditions.

The apparatus for optimizing boiling performance includes:
1. A boiling surface capable of being heated.
2. A counter electrode immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants.
3. A power supply for applying a constant heat input to the boiling surface.
4. A voltage source for adjusting the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode.
5. A control system for monitoring and adjusting the electric potential to optimize the HTC and CHF.

## DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

### Embodiment 1: Basic Method for Controlling Bubble Nucleation

#### Apparatus
The apparatus for controlling bubble nucleation includes a boiling surface, a counter electrode, a power supply, a voltage source, and a control system. The boiling surface is made of a conductive material, such as silver, and is roughened to provide nucleation sites. The counter electrode is a titanium mesh or rod immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants. The power supply applies a constant heat input to the boiling surface, while the voltage source adjusts the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode. The control system monitors and adjusts the electric potential to control bubble nucleation.

#### Method
1. **Preparation**: Prepare a fluid containing charged surfactants, such as sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) or dodecyl trimethylammonium bromide (DTAB), at a concentration of 2.6 mM. Place the fluid in a container with the boiling surface and the counter electrode.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input of 60 W to the boiling surface using the power supply.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Adjust the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode using the voltage source. For example, apply a potential of -0.1 V to promote bubble nucleation and a potential of -2.0 V to suppress bubble nucleation.
4. **Monitoring and Control**: Monitor the boiling behavior using a high-speed camera and adjust the electric potential as needed to achieve the desired level of bubble nucleation.

### Embodiment 2: Spatial and Temporal Control of Boiling

#### Apparatus
The apparatus for spatial and temporal control of boiling includes a boiling surface with multiple independently addressable electrodes, a counter electrode, a power supply, multiple voltage sources, and a control system. The boiling surface is fabricated on a silicon wafer with gold electrodes insulated from each other. The counter electrode is a titanium mesh or rod immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants. The power supply applies a constant heat input to the boiling surface, while the multiple voltage sources adjust the electric potential of each electrode. The control system monitors and adjusts the electric potential of each electrode to control bubble nucleation in localized areas.

#### Method
1. **Preparation**: Prepare a fluid containing charged surfactants, such as DTAB, at a concentration of 2.6 mM. Place the fluid in a container with the boiling surface and the counter electrode.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input of approximately 1.5 W/cm² to the boiling surface using the power supply.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Adjust the electric potential of each electrode using the multiple voltage sources. For example, apply a potential of -2.0 V to the electrodes to be activated (promote bubble nucleation) and a potential of -0.1 V to the electrodes to be deactivated (suppress bubble nucleation).
4. **Monitoring and Control**: Monitor the boiling behavior using a high-speed camera and adjust the electric potential of each electrode as needed to achieve the desired level of bubble nucleation in localized areas.

### Embodiment 3: Optimization of Boiling Performance

#### Apparatus
The apparatus for optimizing boiling performance includes a boiling surface, a counter electrode, a power supply, a voltage source, and a control system. The boiling surface is made of a conductive material, such as silver, and is roughened to provide nucleation sites. The counter electrode is a titanium mesh or rod immersed in the fluid containing charged surfactants. The power supply applies a constant heat input to the boiling surface, while the voltage source adjusts the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode. The control system monitors and adjusts the electric potential to optimize the HTC and CHF.

#### Method
1. **Preparation**: Prepare a fluid containing charged surfactants, such as SDS or DTAB, at a concentration of 2.6 mM. Place the fluid in a container with the boiling surface and the counter electrode.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input to the boiling surface using the power supply. Vary the heat input in a quasi-static manner to obtain boiling curves.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Adjust the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode using the voltage source. For example, apply a potential of -0.1 V and -2.0 V to obtain different boiling curves.
4. **Monitoring and Control**: Monitor the boiling behavior using a high-speed camera and thermocouples. Analyze the boiling curves to determine the optimal electric potential for achieving the desired HTC and CHF.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Basic Control of Bubble Nucleation

#### Experimental Setup
A boiling surface made of silver was roughened with 240 grit sandpaper and soldered to a copper heating block. The heating block was heated from the bottom by five cartridge heaters, and the top section of the block was insulated to provide one-dimensional conduction. A glass enclosure was placed on top of the silver foil, sealed by an EPDM rubber gasket. The fluid side of the condenser was connected to a chiller to maintain a closed-loop system. The counter electrode was a titanium mesh around a titanium rod.

#### Procedure
1. **Fluid Preparation**: Prepare a solution of 2.6 mM SDS in deionized water.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input of 60 W to the boiling surface.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Adjust the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode from -0.1 V to -2.0 V and back to -0.1 V.
4. **Observation**: Use a high-speed camera to observe the boiling behavior. Record the time taken for bubble nucleation to subside and reappear.

#### Results
When the electric potential was adjusted from -0.1 V to -2.0 V, bubble nucleation subsided within 600 ms. When the potential was returned to -0.1 V, bubble nucleation reappeared within 300 ms. These results demonstrate the ability to turn bubble formation "on" and "off" with sub-second precision.

### Example 2: Spatial and Temporal Control of Boiling

#### Experimental Setup
A boiling surface was fabricated on a silicon wafer with eight independently addressable gold electrodes insulated from each other. The electrodes were heated by platinum resistive heaters underneath. The counter electrode was a titanium mesh around a titanium rod.

#### Procedure
1. **Fluid Preparation**: Prepare a solution of 2.6 mM DTAB in deionized water.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input of approximately 1.5 W/cm² to the boiling surface.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Switch the electric potential of the electrodes between -0.1 V and -2.0 V. Apply -2.0 V to the electrodes to be activated and -0.1 V to the electrodes to be deactivated.
4. **Observation**: Use a high-speed camera to observe the boiling behavior. Record the time taken for bubble nucleation to appear and disappear in the localized areas.

#### Results
Bubble nucleation was selectively activated in the areas limited to the electrodes with -2.0 V potential and completely suppressed in the areas with -0.1 V potential. The switching between the "on" and "off" states occurred with sub-second precision, demonstrating the ability to spatially and temporally control boiling.

### Example 3: Optimization of Boiling Performance

#### Experimental Setup
A boiling surface made of silver was roughened with 240 grit sandpaper and soldered to a copper heating block. The heating block was heated from the bottom by five cartridge heaters, and the top section of the block was insulated to provide one-dimensional conduction. A glass enclosure was placed on top of the silver foil, sealed by an EPDM rubber gasket. The fluid side of the condenser was connected to a chiller to maintain a closed-loop system. The counter electrode was a titanium mesh around a titanium rod.

#### Procedure
1. **Fluid Preparation**: Prepare solutions of 2.6 mM SDS and 2.6 mM DTAB in deionized water.
2. **Heat Input**: Apply a constant heat input to the boiling surface and vary the heat input in a quasi-static manner to obtain boiling curves.
3. **Electric Potential Adjustment**: Adjust the electric potential between the boiling surface and the counter electrode from -0.1 V to -2.0 V.
4. **Observation**: Use a high-speed camera and thermocouples to monitor the boiling behavior. Analyze the boiling curves to determine the optimal electric potential for achieving the desired HTC and CHF.

#### Results
For SDS, the boiling curve at -2.0 V was shifted to the right compared to the baseline -0.1 V curve, indicating increased wettability and decreased nucleation. The HTC for SDS could be increased up to 1,000% over its minimum value at -2.0 V for a given superheat. For DTAB, the boiling curve at -2.0 V was shifted to the left, indicating decreased wettability and increased nucleation. The HTC for DTAB could be increased up to 1,100% over its minimum value at -0.1 V for a given superheat. These results demonstrate the ability to optimize boiling performance by modulating the electric potential.
