# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to genetically encoded death indicators (GEDIs) for detecting neuronal death in live-imaging studies. More specifically, the invention provides a class of genetically encoded calcium indicators (GECIs) that are specifically engineered to detect the loss of calcium homeostasis in neurons, thereby indicating the moment of neuronal death with high sensitivity and specificity. The GEDIs are particularly useful for longitudinal single-cell analysis in neurodegenerative disease models and can be applied to various cell types and organisms, including zebrafish larvae.

## BACKGROUND

Neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson’s disease (PD), Huntington’s disease (HD), frontotemporal dementia (FTD), Alzheimer’s disease (AD), and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) are characterized by progressive neuronal dysfunction and death, leading to a deterioration of cognitive, behavioral, or motor functions. The precise determination of whether a particular neuron is alive, dead, or dying can be challenging, particularly in live-imaging studies. Traditional methods, such as vital dyes and stains, can be toxic and may not provide a clear and early indication of cell death. Additionally, many assays are pathway-specific and may not capture the full spectrum of neuronal death mechanisms.

Genetically encoded calcium indicators (GECIs) have been widely used to monitor intracellular calcium dynamics in neurons. However, these indicators are typically optimized to detect physiological calcium transients and may not be suitable for detecting the catastrophic loss of calcium homeostasis associated with neuronal death. There is a need for a robust, early, and pathway-agnostic marker of neuronal death that can be used in live-imaging studies to provide a clear and unbiased readout of cell death.

## SUMMARY

The present invention addresses the need for a sensitive and specific marker of neuronal death by providing genetically encoded death indicators (GEDIs). The GEDIs are engineered GECIs that are designed to detect the loss of calcium homeostasis in neurons, which is a critical and irreversible event in the process of neuronal death. The GEDIs can be used in live-imaging studies to monitor the time course of neurodegeneration and to identify subpopulations of neurons with differing sensitivities to toxins or disease-related stressors.

The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Genetically Encoded Death Indicators (GEDIs)**: The GEDIs are genetically encoded calcium indicators that are specifically engineered to detect the loss of calcium homeostasis in neurons. The GEDIs are designed to increase in fluorescence intensity when cytosolic calcium levels approach those of intracellular organelles or the extracellular milieu, indicating the moment of neuronal death.

2. **Pseudo-Ratiometric GEDIs**: The GEDIs can be designed as pseudo-ratiometric indicators by fusing a GEDI with a self-cleaving P2a peptide and a reference fluorescent protein (e.g., EGFP). This facilitates simple detection of the moment of neuronal death based on a cell’s color change when the green and red channels are overlaid.

3. **Applications in Neurodegenerative Disease Models**: The GEDIs can be used to monitor neuronal death in various neurodegenerative disease models, including models of HD, PD, ALS, and FTD. The GEDIs can be used to perform high-throughput survival analysis and to identify subpopulations of neurons with differing sensitivities to toxins or disease-related stressors.

4. **In Vivo Applications**: The GEDIs can be used to detect neuronal death in vivo in zebrafish larvae. The GEDIs can be expressed in specific neuronal subtypes and can be used to perform longitudinal single-cell analysis in live zebrafish larvae.

5. **Kits**: The invention also provides kits for using the GEDIs in live-imaging studies. The kits can include plasmids encoding the GEDIs, transfection reagents, and imaging protocols.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

- **Neuronal Death**: The irreversible loss of neuronal function and structural integrity, leading to the cessation of all cellular activities.
- **Calcium Homeostasis**: The maintenance of intracellular calcium levels within a narrow physiological range, which is essential for neuronal function.
- **Genetically Encoded Calcium Indicator (GECI)**: A fluorescent protein that is genetically encoded and designed to detect changes in intracellular calcium levels.
- **Genetically Encoded Death Indicator (GEDI)**: A genetically encoded calcium indicator that is specifically engineered to detect the loss of calcium homeostasis in neurons, indicating the moment of neuronal death.
- **Pseudo-Ratiometric Indicator**: A fluorescent indicator that is designed to provide a ratiometric signal by fusing a GEDI with a self-cleaving P2a peptide and a reference fluorescent protein.
- **Longitudinal Single-Cell Analysis**: A method for monitoring the behavior and fate of individual cells over time using live-imaging techniques.

### Vectors

The vectors used to express the GEDIs in neurons can be plasmids, viral vectors, or other gene delivery systems. The vectors can include regulatory elements such as promoters and enhancers to drive the expression of the GEDIs in specific neuronal subtypes. For example, the hSyn1 promoter can be used to drive expression in neurons, and the neuroD promoter can be used to drive expression in zebrafish larvae.

### Cells

The GEDIs can be expressed in various types of neurons, including primary cortical neurons, motor neurons, and neurons derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). The GEDIs can also be expressed in other cell types, such as HEK293 cells, to validate their performance in non-neuronal cells.

### Animals

The GEDIs can be used in various animal models, including rats, mice, and zebrafish larvae. In zebrafish larvae, the GEDIs can be used to perform in vivo imaging of neuronal death in specific neuronal subtypes, such as motor neurons.

### Methods of Use

#### Development of Genetically Encoded Death Indicators (GEDIs)

The GEDIs are developed by engineering GECIs to detect the loss of calcium homeostasis in neurons. For example, the red GEDI variant (RGEDI) is derived from the CEPIA GECI by removing the ER retention signals and allowing the GECI to localize to the cytosol. The RGEDI is designed to increase in fluorescence intensity when cytosolic calcium levels approach those of intracellular organelles or the extracellular milieu, indicating the moment of neuronal death.

#### Pseudo-Ratiometric GEDIs

The RGEDI can be designed as a pseudo-ratiometric indicator by fusing it with a self-cleaving P2a peptide and a reference fluorescent protein, such as EGFP. This facilitates simple detection of the moment of neuronal death based on a cell’s color change when the green and red channels are overlaid. The GEDI ratio is calculated as the ratio of RGEDI fluorescence to EGFP fluorescence, and a threshold value is established to define the moment of neuronal death.

#### Longitudinal Single-Cell Analysis

The GEDIs can be used to perform longitudinal single-cell analysis in live-imaging studies. Neurons expressing the GEDIs can be imaged at regular intervals to monitor the time course of neurodegeneration. The GEDI ratio can be used to quantify the amount of cell death in high-throughput studies and to identify subpopulations of neurons with differing sensitivities to toxins or disease-related stressors.

#### In Vivo Applications

The GEDIs can be used to detect neuronal death in vivo in zebrafish larvae. The GEDIs can be expressed in specific neuronal subtypes, such as motor neurons, and can be used to perform longitudinal single-cell analysis in live zebrafish larvae. The GEDIs can be used to monitor the spread of neuronal death and to identify subpopulations of neurons with differing sensitivities to toxins or disease-related stressors.

### Kits

The invention also provides kits for using the GEDIs in live-imaging studies. The kits can include:

- **Plasmids**: Plasmids encoding the GEDIs, such as phSyn1:RGEDI-P2a-EGFP, phSyn1:RGEDI-P2a-3xTagBFP2, phSyn1:RGEDI-NLS-P2a-EGFP-NLS, and phSyn1:mRuby-P2a-GCaMP6f.
- **Transfection Reagents**: Reagents for transfecting the plasmids into neurons, such as Lipofectamine 2000.
- **Imaging Protocols**: Detailed protocols for performing live-imaging studies using the GEDIs, including guidelines for setting up the imaging system, acquiring images, and analyzing the data.
- **Control Reagents**: Reagents for validating the performance of the GEDIs, such as positive and negative control plasmids.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Development of RGEDI-P2a-EGFP**

Rat cortical primary neurons were transfected with plasmids encoding RGEDI-P2a-EGFP. The neurons were imaged at baseline, during 30 Hz field stimulation, and after treatment with NaN3, a known cytotoxin. The RGEDI-P2a-EGFP construct was designed to increase in fluorescence intensity when cytosolic calcium levels approached those of intracellular organelles or the extracellular milieu, indicating the moment of neuronal death. The results showed that the RGEDI-P2a-EGFP construct responded specifically to neuronal death and not to physiological calcium transients.

### Example 2

**Longitudinal Single-Cell Analysis of Neuronal Death**

Automated longitudinal microscopy was used to image individual neurons transfected with RGEDI-P2a-EGFP at 3-hour intervals. Neurons that died over the course of imaging were marked by a clear increase in the GEDI ratio, followed by the disappearance of the debris. The GEDI ratio was used to quantify the amount of cell death in high-throughput studies and to identify subpopulations of neurons with differing sensitivities to toxins or disease-related stressors.

### Example 3

**Detection of Neuronal Death in Neurodegenerative Disease Models**

The RGEDI-P2a-EGFP construct was co-transfected into rat cortical primary neurons with disease-causing proteins such as HttEx1Q97 (HD), α-synuclein (PD), and TDP43 (ALS/FTD). The GEDI ratio was used to monitor the time course of neuronal death in these disease models. The results showed that the GEDIs could be used to report toxicity over time across a variety of neurodegenerative disease models.

### Example 4

**In Vivo Detection of Neuronal Death in Zebrafish Larvae**

The GC150-P2a-mApple construct was used to detect neuronal death in vivo in zebrafish larvae. The GC150-P2a-mApple construct was co-injected with NTR-BFP into the 1-cell stage zebrafish embryos. The larvae were immobilized and imaged at regular intervals after treatment with MTZ, a prodrug that is activated by NTR. The results showed that the GC150-P2a-mApple construct could be used to detect neuronal death in vivo in zebrafish larvae.

### Example 6

**Application of GEDIs in iPSC-Derived Motor Neurons**

iPSC-derived motor neurons were transfected with the RGEDI-P2a-EGFP construct. The neurons were imaged at regular intervals to monitor the time course of neuronal death. The results showed that the GEDIs could be used to detect neuronal death in iPSC-derived motor neurons and to derive cumulative risk-of-death (CRD) plots, which showed that SOD1-D90A–containing neurons exhibited an increased risk of death compared to controls.
