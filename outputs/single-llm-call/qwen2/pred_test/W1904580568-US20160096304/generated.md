# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of electrospinning, particularly to the fabrication of core-sheath micro and nanofibers. More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel slit-surface electrospinning technology that significantly enhances the throughput and versatility of core-sheath fiber production, thereby addressing the limitations of traditional needle-based electrospinning methods.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method and apparatus for producing core-sheath micro and nanofibers using a slit-surface electrospinning technology. This technology involves the use of a slit fixture that aligns two triangular-shaped nozzles along a single vertical plane, allowing for the co-localization of core and sheath materials. When an electric field is applied, multiple core-sheath cone-jets form, leading to the production of high-quality core-sheath fibers at significantly higher throughputs compared to conventional needle-based electrospinning methods. The invention also includes the optimization of various parameters such as core and sheath flow rates, slit fixture geometry, and solution viscosities to control the formation and morphology of the core-sheath cone-jets.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Core Flow Rate

The core flow rate is a critical parameter in the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The core flow rate determines the amount of core material that is entrained by the sheath solution to form the core-sheath structure. In the present invention, the core flow rate is manipulated to ensure that the core solution exits the core slit at a velocity that is lower than the sheath solution velocity. This condition facilitates the formation of distinct core-sheath cone-jets. For example, in experiments using a sheath flow rate of 200 mL/h, distinct core-sheath cone-jets were observed when the core flow rate was set to 20 or 40 mL/h. Conversely, when the core flow rate was increased to 60 mL/h, the core-sheath structure was not maintained. This indicates that the ratio of sheath to core flow rates (vsheath/vcore) should be greater than 1 to ensure proper core-sheath formation.

### Slit Fixture Geometry Core Slit Width

The core slit width is another crucial parameter that influences the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The core slit width affects the flow velocity of the core solution at the slit exit. A narrower core slit width results in a higher core flow velocity, which can disrupt the formation of distinct core-sheath cone-jets. In the present invention, the core slit width is optimized to ensure that the core flow velocity is lower than the sheath flow velocity. For instance, when the core slit width was reduced from 2.2 mm to 1.5 mm, distinct core-sheath cone-jets were observed. However, increasing the core slit width to 3.0 mm resulted in the loss of core-sheath structure. By adjusting the core slit width, the invention ensures that the core solution is properly entrained by the sheath solution, leading to the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers.

### Sheath Slit Width

The sheath slit width is equally important in the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The sheath slit width affects the flow velocity of the sheath solution at the slit exit. A wider sheath slit width results in a lower sheath flow velocity, which can hinder the formation of distinct core-sheath cone-jets. In the present invention, the sheath slit width is optimized to ensure that the sheath flow velocity is greater than the core flow velocity. For example, when the sheath slit width was increased from 2.2 mm to 3.0 mm, distinct core-sheath cone-jets were not observed. However, reducing the core flow rate from 20 mL/h to 10 mL/h restored the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. By optimizing the sheath slit width, the invention ensures that the sheath solution provides sufficient shear force to entrain the core solution, leading to the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers.

### Sheath Slit Width and Core Slit Width

The relationship between the sheath slit width and the core slit width is critical in the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The invention optimizes both the sheath and core slit widths to ensure that the sheath flow velocity is greater than the core flow velocity. This condition is necessary to facilitate the formation of distinct core-sheath cone-jets. For example, when the sheath slit width was 2.2 mm and the core slit width was 1.5 mm, distinct core-sheath cone-jets were observed. However, increasing the sheath slit width to 3.0 mm and maintaining the core slit width at 1.5 mm resulted in the loss of core-sheath structure. By carefully balancing the sheath and core slit widths, the invention ensures that the core solution is properly entrained by the sheath solution, leading to the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers.

### Core Slit Height

The core slit height is another important parameter that influences the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The core slit height affects the flow path of the core solution and its interaction with the sheath solution. In the present invention, the core slit height is optimized to ensure that the core solution exits the core slit in a manner that promotes the formation of distinct core-sheath cone-jets. For example, a taller core slit height may result in a more laminar flow of the core solution, which can enhance the entrainment of the core solution by the sheath solution. Conversely, a shorter core slit height may result in turbulent flow, which can disrupt the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. By optimizing the core slit height, the invention ensures that the core solution is properly entrained by the sheath solution, leading to the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers.

### Viscosities of the Solutions

The viscosities of the core and sheath solutions play a crucial role in the formation of core-sheath cone-jets. The viscosity of the sheath solution must be sufficient to provide the necessary shear force to entrain the core solution. In the present invention, the viscosities of the core and sheath solutions are optimized to ensure that the sheath solution provides sufficient shear force to entrain the core solution. For example, when the sheath solution viscosity was 760 cP (16 wt% PCL solution), distinct core-sheath cone-jets were observed. However, when the sheath solution viscosity was 280 cP (12 wt% PCL solution), the core-sheath structure was not maintained. By optimizing the viscosities of the core and sheath solutions, the invention ensures that the sheath solution provides sufficient shear force to entrain the core solution, leading to the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers.

### Core Sheath Fiber Applications

The core-sheath fibers produced by the present invention have a wide range of applications across multiple industries. For example, core-sheath fibers can be used for controlled drug delivery and tissue engineering applications. The core-sheath structure allows for the encapsulation of compounds such as small molecule drugs, proteins, and growth factors, providing controlled release profiles. Additionally, core-sheath fibers can be used to create bicomponent fibers with novel properties such as self-cleaning, self-healing, and superhydrophobic properties, which are valuable in the textile and filtration industries. Hollow core-sheath fibers with high surface-to-volume ratios are suitable for use in microfluidics, photonics, energy storage, and sensor applications. Furthermore, the invention enables the processing of low molecular weight, flowable materials that are otherwise difficult to electrospin, expanding the range of materials that can be used in fiber applications.

### Advantages of the Invention as it Relates to High Throughput Open-Bath Monofiber Fabrication Systems

The present invention offers several advantages over traditional needle-based electrospinning methods, particularly in terms of high throughput and versatility. The slit-surface electrospinning technology allows for the production of core-sheath fibers at throughputs exceeding 1 L/h, which is orders of magnitude higher than traditional methods. This high throughput is achieved by the formation of multiple core-sheath cone-jets along the length of the slit-surface, enabling the production of quality fibers at significantly higher flow rates. Additionally, the invention is less prone to maintenance issues and clogging, providing a more stable and reliable process. The versatility of the invention is demonstrated by its ability to fabricate a wide range of core-sheath fibers, including particle-encapsulating, bicomponent, hollow, and unelectrospinnable fibers. By optimizing various parameters such as core and sheath flow rates, slit fixture geometry, and solution viscosities, the invention ensures the formation of high-quality core-sheath fibers, making it a valuable tool for manufacturing and commercialization.
