# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and pharmacology, specifically to the use of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP) to enhance energy metabolism and contractile kinetics in skeletal muscle cells. The invention encompasses the use of TCAP to increase glucose uptake and ATP production, thereby improving muscle function and performance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Skeletal muscle is a critical tissue for voluntary movement and is responsible for a significant portion of glucose metabolism in the body. Efficient energy metabolism and contractile strength are essential for optimal muscle function. Disorders affecting muscle metabolism, such as type-II diabetes, often result in reduced muscle function and degradation. The teneurins and their receptors, the latrophilins (LPHNs), are part of an ancient regulatory system that modulates cell adhesion and metabolism. Recent studies have shown that the teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP) can regulate glucose uptake and energy metabolism in neurons. However, the role of TCAP in skeletal muscle has not been extensively explored.

The present invention addresses this gap by demonstrating that TCAP-1, a specific isoform of TCAP, can enhance glucose uptake and energy metabolism in skeletal muscle cells. This enhancement leads to improved contractile kinetics and muscle performance, making TCAP-1 a promising therapeutic agent for conditions affecting muscle function.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for enhancing energy metabolism and contractile kinetics in skeletal muscle cells using teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP-1). The method involves administering an effective amount of TCAP-1 to a subject in need thereof. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP-1 and methods for their preparation and administration.

The invention is based on the discovery that TCAP-1 can increase glucose uptake and ATP production in skeletal muscle cells, thereby improving muscle function and performance. Specifically, the invention includes:

1. **Method for Enhancing Energy Metabolism and Contractile Kinetics**: Administering an effective amount of TCAP-1 to a subject to increase glucose uptake and ATP production in skeletal muscle cells, thereby enhancing contractile kinetics and muscle performance.

2. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: Compositions comprising TCAP-1 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier or excipient, suitable for various modes of administration, including but not limited to subcutaneous, intramuscular, and oral routes.

3. **Therapeutic Uses**: The use of TCAP-1 to treat or prevent conditions associated with impaired muscle function, such as type-II diabetes, muscle wasting, and fatigue.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

- **TCAP-1**: Teneurin C-terminal associated peptide-1, a specific isoform of TCAP that is highly conserved among vertebrates.
- **Skeletal Muscle Cells**: Cells that form the skeletal muscle tissue, responsible for voluntary movement and a significant portion of glucose metabolism.
- **Energy Metabolism**: The processes by which cells generate and utilize energy, including glucose uptake, glycolysis, and the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle.
- **Contractile Kinetics**: The properties of muscle contraction, including force generation, relaxation rate, and fatigue resistance.
- **Pharmaceutically Acceptable Carrier**: A substance that is compatible with the active ingredient and suitable for administration to a subject without causing undue toxicity or adverse effects.

### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells

The present invention demonstrates that TCAP-1 can significantly enhance energy metabolism and contractile kinetics in skeletal muscle cells. Specifically, TCAP-1 increases glucose uptake and ATP production, leading to improved muscle function and performance. This effect is mediated through the activation of the phospholipase C (PLC)-inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3)-diacylglycerol (DAG) pathway, which results in increased intracellular calcium (Ca²⁺) levels and subsequent mitochondrial activation.

#### In Vivo Studies

In vivo studies using rat models have shown that TCAP-1 administration increases the expression of myosin heavy chain (MHC) I fibers, which are associated with oxidative metabolism. Additionally, functional positron emission tomography (fPET) studies have demonstrated that TCAP-1 treatment increases glucose uptake in the hind-limb muscles. These findings suggest that TCAP-1 can enhance glucose metabolism and energy availability in skeletal muscle.

#### In Vitro Studies

In vitro studies using the C2C12 mouse skeletal muscle cell line have further confirmed the role of TCAP-1 in regulating energy metabolism. TCAP-1 treatment increases intracellular Ca²⁺ levels, glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4) expression, and glucose uptake. Furthermore, TCAP-1 enhances ATP and NADH production, as well as the expression of succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), a key enzyme in the TCA cycle. These effects are consistent with the in vivo observations and provide a mechanistic understanding of how TCAP-1 improves muscle function.

The dosage and frequency of administration will be adjusted to ensure optimal absorption and efficacy.

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis

TCAP-1 and a scrambled (sc) control peptide were synthesized commercially by AmbioPharm, Inc. The peptides were prepared as acetylated salts at 95% purity. The primary structure of rat/mouse TCAP-1 is identical, and the peptide was used for both rat and mouse preparations. The synthetic peptide was solubilized in saline after alkalization and diluted into the required media for in vitro or in vivo studies.

### Example 2—In-Vivo Experiments

Male adult Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats were used for short-term and long-term muscle function studies. For the short-term study, rats were treated with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) by subcutaneous injection for 5 days. Muscle function was tested 3 days after the last treatment. For the long-term study, rats were treated with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (25 nmoles/kg) for 3 months (1 injection/week). Muscle function was tested 2 weeks after the last treatment. The results showed that TCAP-1 treatment increased the expression of MHC I fibers and maintained contraction cycling efficiency during fatigue.

### Example 3—Effect of TCAP on Blood Glucose Levels

Male Wistar rats were treated with either vehicle or TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) by subcutaneous injection. Three days after treatment, blood glucose levels were measured. The results showed that TCAP-1 treatment significantly decreased blood glucose levels, indicating an increase in glucose uptake by skeletal muscle.

### Example 4—Glucose Insulin and Glucagon Changes in Blood

Blood samples were collected from the rats treated with TCAP-1 to measure changes in glucose, insulin, and glucagon levels. The results showed that TCAP-1 treatment decreased serum glucose and insulin levels, consistent with increased glucose uptake and utilization by skeletal muscle.

### Example 5—Liver and Muscle Glycogen Content in Extracted Liver of TCAP-1 Treated Wistar Rats

The liver and muscle glycogen content were measured in Wistar rats treated with TCAP-1. The results showed that TCAP-1 treatment increased glycogen content in both liver and muscle, indicating enhanced glucose storage and utilization.

### Example 6—Haematology Assays

Haematology assays were performed to evaluate the safety and tolerability of TCAP-1 treatment. The results showed no significant changes in haematological parameters, indicating that TCAP-1 is safe for use in vivo.

### Example 7—Localization of TCAP in Muscle Tissue

Immunohistochemical (IHC) studies were performed to localize TCAP-1 in rat skeletal muscle tissue. The results showed that TCAP-1 co-localizes with β-dystroglycan (DG) along the sarcolemma, indicating its presence and potential role in muscle function.

### Example 8—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to β-Dystroglycan in Skeletal Cells

Further IHC studies were conducted to determine the localization of TCAP-1 relative to β-dystroglycan (DG) in skeletal cells. The results showed that TCAP-1 co-localizes with DG, suggesting a functional relationship between TCAP-1 and the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex.

### Example 9—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to Rapsyn in Skeletal Cells

IHC studies were also performed to determine the localization of TCAP-1 relative to rapsyn, a protein involved in the clustering of acetylcholine receptors. The results showed that TCAP-1 co-localizes with rapsyn, indicating a potential role in neuromuscular junction formation and function.
