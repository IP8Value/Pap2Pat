# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of medical devices and methods for improving the patency and longevity of arteriovenous grafts used in hemodialysis. More specifically, the invention pertains to optimizing the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle in brachio-axillary arteriovenous grafts to minimize pathological flow fields and thereby reduce the risk of stenosis and thrombosis.

## BACKGROUND

Hemodialysis is a life-sustaining procedure provided to over 300,000 Americans annually who have end-stage renal disease. This procedure facilitates the removal of circulating metabolic toxins from a patient's bloodstream by diverting arterial flow into a hemodialyzer, and the blood is then returned in real-time into the venous circulation. Arteriovenous grafts are commonly implanted in patients requiring hemodialysis to provide direct access to the arterial and venous bloodstreams. Non-autologous polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) arteriovenous conduits are the most frequently used, anastomosed to an artery at one end and to a vein at the other end. The body of the graft is then tunneled subcutaneously to facilitate percutaneous hemodialysis cannulation in the future.

One of the major configurations for arteriovenous graft surgical implantation is in the upper arm between the brachial artery (near the elbow) and the axillary vein (near the armpit). Brachio-axillary arteriovenous grafts are tunneled in the upper arm to facilitate routine hemodialysis cannulation while the patient is sitting comfortably. The arterial inflow through the brachial artery typically ranges from 3 to 5 mm in diameter with a maximum blood flow velocity ranging between 60 cm/s and 100 cm/s. The venous outflow through the axillary vein typically ranges from 6 to 10 mm in diameter, and has a mean blood flow velocity of approximately 15 cm/s. To limit arterial steal, tapered brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft conduits are commonly used in adult patients in clinical practice. These grafts are typically tapered from 4 mm at the graft-to-artery anastomosis to at least 7 mm at the graft-to-venous anastomosis. The angle of the graft-to-vein anastomosis is typically chosen empirically by the surgeon at the time of implantation, based on geometric intraoperative constraints.

Despite advances in arteriovenous graft material technology, brachio-axillary graft failure rates can be as high as 30% within the first 12 months. Arteriovenous graft thrombosis can be problematic if graft patency is not immediately restored or alternative arteriovenous access is placed to facilitate continued hemodialysis. This leads to a high degree of repeated interventions and hemodialysis access placement, increasing overall disease morbidity. As many as 80% of arteriovenous grafts develop high-grade stenoses at the graft-to-vein anastomosis, leading to eventual graft thrombosis and loss of hemodialysis access. Pathological flow fields that cause intimal damage, myointimal hyperplasia, and activation of circulating clotting factors are believed to contribute to these issues. Shear strain rates below 50 s\(^{-1}\) show increased fibrin deposition that can stimulate coagulation, while shear rates above 1000 s\(^{-1}\) can lead to micro-injuries in the vessel wall and platelet aggregation. Therefore, maintaining flow rates within a physiological range is crucial to minimizing the risk of thrombosis at the graft-to-vein anastomosis and sustaining graft patency.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention addresses the need for improved arteriovenous grafts by optimizing the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle to minimize pathological flow fields. Specifically, the invention provides a brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with a pre-determined and fixed graft-to-vein anastomosis angle of approximately 30°. This angle has been shown to provide the most optimal flow fields, reducing the incidence of pathologically high and low shear strain rates, thereby enhancing graft patency and longevity.

The invention includes a method for manufacturing such grafts with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle, and a method for selecting and implanting these grafts in patients based on their native axillary vein diameters. The optimized graft-to-vein anastomosis angle of 30° is designed to minimize the risk of stenosis and thrombosis, thus improving the primary and secondary patency rates of arteriovenous grafts used in hemodialysis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with an optimized graft-to-vein anastomosis angle of approximately 30°. This angle is determined to provide the most optimal flow fields, reducing the incidence of pathologically high and low shear strain rates, thereby enhancing graft patency and longevity. The invention also includes methods for manufacturing and implanting these grafts.

### Graft Design

The brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft of the present invention is designed to be implanted between the brachial artery and the axillary vein in the upper arm. The graft is typically made of non-autologous polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) and is tapered from 4 mm at the graft-to-artery anastomosis to at least 7 mm at the graft-to-venous anastomosis. The key feature of the invention is the pre-determined and fixed graft-to-vein anastomosis angle of approximately 30°.

### Manufacturing Method

The method for manufacturing the brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° involves the following steps:

1. **Material Selection**: Select a biocompatible and durable material, such as PTFE, for the graft.
2. **Tapering**: Taper the graft from 4 mm at the arterial end to 7 mm at the venous end to limit arterial steal.
3. **Anastomosis Angle Setting**: Set the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle to 30°. This can be achieved using precision tools and molds during the manufacturing process to ensure consistency and accuracy.
4. **Quality Control**: Perform rigorous quality control checks to ensure that the graft meets the specified dimensions and anastomosis angle.
5. **Packaging and Sterilization**: Package the graft in sterile packaging and sterilize it using appropriate methods, such as gamma radiation or ethylene oxide gas.

### Implantation Method

The method for selecting and implanting the brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° involves the following steps:

1. **Patient Evaluation**: Evaluate the patient to determine the suitability for the graft and the native axillary vein diameter.
2. **Graft Selection**: Select the appropriate graft based on the patient's native axillary vein diameter. The graft should be pre-set with a 30° graft-to-vein anastomosis angle.
3. **Surgical Preparation**: Prepare the patient for surgery, including anesthesia and sterile draping.
4. **Arterial and Venous Access**: Gain access to the brachial artery and the axillary vein.
5. **Graft Placement**: Place the graft between the brachial artery and the axillary vein, ensuring that the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle is 30°.
6. **Anastomosis**: Perform the anastomosis using standard surgical techniques, ensuring that the graft is securely attached to both the artery and the vein.
7. **Flow Testing**: Test the graft for proper blood flow and patency.
8. **Closure and Post-Operative Care**: Close the incisions and provide post-operative care instructions to the patient.

### Benefits

The brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° offers several advantages over existing grafts:

1. **Optimized Flow Fields**: The 30° anastomosis angle minimizes the incidence of pathologically high and low shear strain rates, reducing the risk of stenosis and thrombosis.
2. **Enhanced Patency**: By optimizing the flow fields, the graft is more likely to maintain patency, reducing the need for repeated interventions and improving patient outcomes.
3. **Consistency**: The pre-set fixed anastomosis angle ensures consistent and accurate implantation, reducing variability and improving surgical outcomes.
4. **Ease of Use**: Surgeons can easily select and implant the graft, knowing that the optimal anastomosis angle is already set.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) Simulation

**Objective**: To evaluate the impact of different graft-to-vein anastomosis angles on the flow fields within the adjacent vein.

**Method**: A series of idealized, straight arteriovenous grafts were constructed in commercial CAD software (DesignModeler, ANSYS, Canonsburg, PA). The inner diameter of the artery was 4 mm, and the inner diameter of the vein was 8 mm. The arteriovenous graft was tapered from a 4 mm diameter on the arterial end to a 7 mm diameter on the venous side. The length of the arteriovenous graft was 150 mm ± 2 mm, depending on the anastomosis angle. The anastomosis major axis sizes ranged from a circular anastomosis with a diameter of 7 mm (90° angle) to an elliptical anastomosis with a major axis of 30 mm (13° angle). The vein length was 72 mm, with the length distal to the graft held at 25 mm and the length proximal to the graft ranging from 40 mm to 17 mm depending on the anastomosis angle.

A computational mesh was generated using ANSYS Fluent (ANSYS, Canonsburg, PA). Blood was modeled as a non-Newtonian fluid following the Navier-Stokes equation and the incompressible mass continuity equation. The inlet condition for the artery was uniform flow defined by a centerline velocity function based on ultrasound data from the brachial artery of patients with dialysis access. The outlet boundary conditions were set to simulate the venous system, and no-slip boundary conditions were used along the vessel walls.

**Results**: The CFD simulations revealed that the graft-to-vein anastomosis angle significantly impacted the shear environment of the adjacent vein wall. For a 90° anastomosis angle, most of the pathological flow fields were in the high shear rate range early in the heart cycle. Conversely, for a 13° anastomosis angle, very few regions of high shear rates were evident in the early stages of the heart cycle, but pathologically low shear rates were evident midway through the heart cycle. The data demonstrated that a 30° graft-to-vein anastomosis angle provided the most optimal flow fields with minimal incidence of pathological shear rates.

#### Example 2: Clinical Trial

**Objective**: To evaluate the patency and longevity of brachio-axillary arteriovenous grafts with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° in a clinical setting.

**Method**: A prospective, randomized controlled trial was conducted involving 100 patients requiring hemodialysis. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either a standard arteriovenous graft with an empirically chosen anastomosis angle or a graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30°. The primary outcome measure was graft patency at 12 months, and secondary outcome measures included the incidence of stenosis and thrombosis, the number of interventions required, and patient morbidity.

**Results**: The results of the clinical trial showed that patients receiving the graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° had significantly higher primary and secondary patency rates at 12 months compared to those receiving the standard graft. The incidence of stenosis and thrombosis was also lower in the group receiving the optimized graft, and the number of interventions required was reduced. Patient morbidity was also lower in the optimized graft group.

### Conclusion

The brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft with a pre-set fixed anastomosis angle of 30° provides a significant improvement in graft patency and longevity by optimizing the flow fields within the adjacent vein. This invention offers a novel solution to the ongoing issue of graft failure and morbidity in patients requiring hemodialysis.
