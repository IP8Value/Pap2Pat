# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of chromatin biology has seen significant advancements in understanding the intricate interplay between chromatin-modifying enzymes and the regulation of gene expression. The COMplex of Proteins Associated with Set1 (COMPASS) family members, particularly MLL3 and MLL4, play crucial roles in the regulation of enhancer activity and gene expression through their catalytic-dependent function of H3K4 mono-methylation and their catalytic-independent cofunctional association with UTX and p300. These enzymes are essential for various developmental processes, including myogenesis, adipogenesis, cardiogenesis, macrophage activation, and germinal center B cell development. 

Mutations in the genes encoding MLL3 (KMT2C) and MLL4 (KMT2D) are frequently found in a broad spectrum of cancers and developmental disorders, such as Kabuki syndrome. These mutations often result in loss of function, leading to impaired cellular processes and contributing to disease progression. Despite the extensive cataloging of these mutations, the molecular mechanisms underlying their oncogenic function remain poorly understood.

Recent studies have highlighted the importance of metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells and the potential for targeting metabolic vulnerabilities as a therapeutic strategy. This invention identifies a novel cellular vulnerability in MLL3/4-impaired cells, specifically an increased dependence on de novo nucleotide synthesis. This discovery opens new avenues for the development of targeted therapies for diseases associated with MLL3/4 loss-of-function mutations.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for treating diseases associated with MLL3/4 loss-of-function mutations by targeting the increased dependence on de novo nucleotide synthesis in affected cells. Specifically, the invention provides a method for inhibiting de novo purine synthesis in MLL3/4-impaired cells, thereby inducing synthetic lethality and selectively reducing the viability of these cells.

The invention is based on the discovery that cells depleted of MLL3 and MLL4 exhibit increased flux through de novo purine synthesis pathways and are more sensitive to inhibitors of purine synthesis. This sensitivity is due to the compensatory upregulation of purine metabolism genes and the increased demand for purine nucleotides in MLL3/4-impaired cells. The invention further demonstrates that this metabolic vulnerability can be exploited to selectively target and eliminate MLL3/4-deficient cells, including cancer cells harboring MLL4 mutations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Identification of Cellular Vulnerabilities in MLL3/4-Impaired Cells

To identify cellular vulnerabilities arising due to MLL3/4 depletion, a genome-wide CRISPR knockout screen was conducted in wild-type (WT) and MLL3/4 double-knockout (KO) mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs). The screen revealed that MLL3/4-KO cells exhibit increased dependence on de novo nucleotide synthesis pathways, particularly purine synthesis. This was evidenced by the significant enrichment of metabolic genes, including those involved in purine and pyrimidine synthesis, among the top negatively selected genes in the KO cells.

### Metabolic Profiling and Flux Analysis

Metabolomic profiling using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) was performed to assess the global metabolome of WT and MLL3/4-KO cells. The results showed altered steady-state relative abundance of metabolites involved in methionine metabolism, urea cycle, and nucleotide metabolism in MLL3/4-deficient cells. Heavy isotope-labeled metabolite tracing experiments further confirmed that MLL3/4-KO cells exhibit increased flux through de novo purine synthesis pathways.

### Sensitivity to Purine Synthesis Inhibition

The increased dependence on de novo purine synthesis in MLL3/4-KO cells was validated by treating WT and KO cells with lometrexol (LTX), a de novo purine synthesis inhibitor. MLL3/4-KO cells showed enhanced sensitivity to LTX treatment, with increased inhibition of cell growth and viability. This sensitivity was rescued by the addition of hypoxanthine, a purine salvage pathway nucleobase, confirming the specific targeting of de novo purine synthesis.

### Mechanistic Insights

To understand the mechanisms underlying the increased sensitivity to purine synthesis inhibition, RNA-Seq and TMT proteomics were employed to identify changes in gene and protein expression in MLL3/4-KO cells. The data revealed upregulation of purine metabolism genes and pathways, consistent with the increased flux through de novo purine synthesis. Further analysis suggested that the loss of MLL3/4 leads to a redistribution of COMPASS subunits, particularly increased MLL1 chromatin binding, which compensates for the loss of MLL3/4 and drives the upregulation of purine metabolism genes.

### Application in Cancer Therapy

The invention extends the findings from mESCs to cancer cells, demonstrating that colorectal cancer cells harboring MLL4 mutations are selectively sensitive to LTX treatment. In vivo xenograft studies in mice confirmed that LTX treatment significantly inhibits the growth of MLL4-mutant tumors, extending the survival of treated animals. These results suggest that targeting de novo purine synthesis could be a viable therapeutic strategy for cancers with MLL4 mutations.

### Therapeutic Agents and Methods

The invention provides methods for treating diseases associated with MLL3/4 loss-of-function mutations by administering agents that inhibit de novo purine synthesis. Suitable agents include, but are not limited to, lometrexol, methotrexate, and other inhibitors of purine synthesis pathways. The methods involve administering an effective amount of the inhibitor to a subject in need thereof, thereby selectively reducing the viability of MLL3/4-impaired cells.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: CRISPR Dropout Screen Identifies Nucleotide Synthesis Pathways as Essential for Viability in MLL3/4-KO mESCs

A genome-wide CRISPR knockout screen was performed in WT and MLL3/4-KO mESCs using the Brie CRISPR knockout library. The screen identified 12 metabolic enzymes critical for purine and pyrimidine synthesis as essential in KO but not WT cells. The top 300 negatively selected genes were enriched in pathways related to nucleobase-containing small molecule metabolic processes, confirming the increased dependence on de novo nucleotide synthesis in MLL3/4-KO cells.

#### Example 2: Increased Flux Through Purine Synthesis in MLL3/4-KO mESCs

Metabolomic profiling and heavy isotope-labeled metabolite tracing experiments were conducted to assess the global metabolome and flux through metabolic pathways in WT and MLL3/4-KO mESCs. The results showed significantly altered steady-state relative abundance of metabolites involved in nucleotide metabolism and increased flux through de novo purine synthesis pathways in MLL3/4-KO cells.

#### Example 3: Enhanced Sensitivity to Purine Synthesis Inhibition in MLL3/4-KO mESCs

WT and MLL3/4-KO mESCs were treated with lometrexol (LTX) to inhibit de novo purine synthesis. MLL3/4-KO cells exhibited enhanced sensitivity to LTX treatment, with increased inhibition of cell growth and viability. The sensitivity was rescued by the addition of hypoxanthine, confirming the specific targeting of de novo purine synthesis.

#### Example 4: Mechanistic Insights into Purine Metabolism Upregulation in MLL3/4-KO Cells

RNA-Seq and TMT proteomics were used to identify changes in gene and protein expression in MLL3/4-KO cells. The data revealed upregulation of purine metabolism genes and pathways, consistent with the increased flux through de novo purine synthesis. Further analysis suggested that the loss of MLL3/4 leads to a redistribution of COMPASS subunits, particularly increased MLL1 chromatin binding, which compensates for the loss of MLL3/4 and drives the upregulation of purine metabolism genes.

#### Example 5: Selective Sensitivity to LTX Treatment in MLL4-Mutant Colorectal Cancer Cells

Colorectal cancer cell lines harboring MLL4 mutations were treated with lometrexol (LTX) to assess their sensitivity to de novo purine synthesis inhibition. MLL4-mutant cell lines exhibited enhanced sensitivity to LTX treatment, with increased inhibition of cell growth and viability. In vivo xenograft studies in mice confirmed that LTX treatment significantly inhibits the growth of MLL4-mutant tumors, extending the survival of treated animals.

These examples demonstrate the utility of targeting de novo purine synthesis as a therapeutic strategy for diseases associated with MLL3/4 loss-of-function mutations, particularly in cancer. The methods and compositions provided by this invention offer a promising approach to selectively target and eliminate MLL3/4-impaired cells, thereby improving treatment outcomes for affected individuals.
