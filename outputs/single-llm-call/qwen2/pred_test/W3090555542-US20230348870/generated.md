# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of gene therapy and, more specifically, to the use of adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors for the delivery of CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing systems to satellite cells in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) patients. The invention provides methods and compositions for correcting mutations in the dystrophin gene in satellite cells, thereby enabling sustained therapeutic effects for DMD.

## INTRODUCTION

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe genetic disorder affecting approximately 1 in 5,000 live male births. It is characterized by the absence of functional dystrophin protein, leading to progressive and ultimately fatal skeletal muscle degeneration. Current treatments for DMD are palliative and do not address the underlying genetic defect. Gene therapy, particularly using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, offers a promising approach to correct the dystrophin gene mutations and potentially provide a long-term cure.

Satellite cells, which are muscle stem cells, play a crucial role in muscle regeneration. They are activated in response to muscle damage and can differentiate into new muscle fibers. In DMD, the continuous demand for muscle regeneration leads to the exhaustion of satellite cells, exacerbating muscle degeneration. Therefore, correcting the dystrophin gene in satellite cells could provide a sustainable source of therapeutic gene production.

Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) are widely used as gene delivery vectors due to their safety and efficiency. However, the transduction efficiency of AAVs in satellite cells has been a subject of debate. Recent studies have demonstrated that certain AAV serotypes, such as AAV8 and AAV9, can effectively transduce satellite cells in vivo. This invention builds upon these findings to develop a robust method for gene editing in satellite cells using CRISPR/Cas9 technology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for correcting mutations in the dystrophin gene in satellite cells using CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing systems delivered by adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors. Specifically, the invention involves the use of AAV8 and AAV9 serotypes to transduce satellite cells and deliver the necessary components for CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing.

The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AAV vectors encoding CRISPR/Cas9 components, such as the Cas9 enzyme and guide RNAs (gRNAs) specific to the dystrophin gene.

2. **Methods of Treatment**: The invention provides methods for treating DMD by administering the pharmaceutical compositions to patients. The methods involve intramuscular or systemic administration of the AAV vectors to achieve gene editing in satellite cells.

3. **Kits**: The invention also provides kits for performing the gene editing methods, including the necessary reagents and instructions for use.

4. **Gene Editing Outcomes**: The invention includes methods for quantifying the level of gene editing in satellite cells, such as using unbiased sequencing approaches to detect various gene editing outcomes, including exon deletions, indels, inversions, and integration of AAV inverted terminal repeats (ITRs).

5. **Sustained Therapeutic Effects**: The invention demonstrates the long-term contribution of dystrophin-corrected satellite cells to muscle regeneration, even after the loss of the episomal AAV vector.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### 1. Definitions

**Adeno-associated virus (AAV)**: A small, non-enveloped virus that is a member of the Parvoviridae family. AAVs are commonly used as vectors for gene therapy due to their safety and ability to transduce a wide range of cell types.

**CRISPR/Cas9**: A gene editing technology that uses a Cas9 enzyme and guide RNA (gRNA) to make precise changes to the DNA sequence. The gRNA directs the Cas9 enzyme to a specific location in the genome, where it can introduce double-strand breaks, leading to gene editing.

**Dystrophin**: A large protein that helps stabilize and protect muscle fibers. Mutations in the dystrophin gene are responsible for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD).

**Satellite cells**: Muscle stem cells that are located beneath the basal lamina of muscle fibers. They are activated in response to muscle damage and can differentiate into new muscle fibers.

### 2. Dystrophin Gene

The dystrophin gene is one of the largest known human genes, spanning approximately 2.5 million base pairs on the X chromosome. It encodes a protein that is essential for maintaining the structural integrity of muscle fibers. Mutations in the dystrophin gene, such as deletions, duplications, and point mutations, result in the production of a truncated or non-functional dystrophin protein, leading to DMD.

### 3. CRISPR/Cas9-Based Gene Editing System

The CRISPR/Cas9 system consists of two main components: the Cas9 enzyme and the guide RNA (gRNA). The gRNA is designed to bind to a specific sequence in the target gene, guiding the Cas9 enzyme to that location. Once at the target site, the Cas9 enzyme introduces a double-strand break in the DNA, which can be repaired by cellular mechanisms, leading to gene editing.

In the context of DMD, the CRISPR/Cas9 system can be used to correct mutations in the dystrophin gene. For example, gRNAs can be designed to target specific exons, such as exon 23, which is commonly deleted in DMD patients. By excising the mutated exon, the reading frame of the dystrophin gene can be restored, allowing for the production of a functional dystrophin protein.

### 4. Genetic Constructs

The invention utilizes AAV vectors to deliver the CRISPR/Cas9 components to satellite cells. The AAV vectors are designed to encode the Cas9 enzyme and the gRNAs. Specifically, the invention employs AAV8 and AAV9 serotypes, which have been shown to have high transduction efficiency in satellite cells.

The genetic constructs include:

- **AAV8 and AAV9 vectors**: These vectors are used to deliver the CRISPR/Cas9 components to satellite cells. The vectors can be packaged with a variety of promoters, including constitutive promoters like CMV and muscle-specific promoters like CK8e, SPc5-12, and MHCK7.

- **Cas9 enzyme**: The Cas9 enzyme is encoded by the AAV vector and is responsible for introducing double-strand breaks in the DNA at the target site.

- **Guide RNAs (gRNAs)**: The gRNAs are designed to target specific sequences in the dystrophin gene. For example, gRNAs can be designed to target intronic sequences flanking exon 23, allowing for the excision of the mutated exon.

### 5. Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AAV vectors encoding the CRISPR/Cas9 components. The compositions can be formulated for intramuscular or systemic administration. The AAV vectors are prepared in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as saline or a buffered solution, and can be administered in a single dose or multiple doses.

### 6. Administration

The pharmaceutical compositions can be administered to DMD patients using various routes, including intramuscular and systemic administration. Intramuscular administration involves injecting the AAV vectors directly into the affected muscles, such as the tibialis anterior (TA) muscle. Systemic administration involves injecting the AAV vectors into the bloodstream, typically via the tail vein in animal models or intravenously in humans.

### 7. Kits

The invention also provides kits for performing the gene editing methods. The kits include the necessary reagents, such as the AAV vectors encoding the CRISPR/Cas9 components, and instructions for use. The kits may also include reagents for quantifying the level of gene editing in satellite cells, such as primers and probes for PCR and sequencing.

### 8. Methods

#### 8.1. Profiling AAV Serotypes for Targeting Efficiency of Satellite Cells

To determine the most effective AAV serotypes for transducing satellite cells, a series of experiments were conducted using a dual reporter mouse model. The Ai9 mouse allele harbors a CAG-loxP-STOP-loxP-tdTomato expression cassette at the Rosa26 locus. Excision of the stop cassette by the Cre recombinase leads to permanent labeling of target cells with the tdTomato fluorescent protein. The Pax7nGFP mouse model, in which a nuclear-localized GFP (nGFP) is knocked into the first exon of Pax7, was used to specifically label satellite cells.

AAV1, AAV2, AAV5, AAV6.2, AAV8, and AAV9 serotypes encoding CMV-Cre were injected into the tibialis anterior (TA) muscles of Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice at equivalent doses. Muscles were harvested 8 weeks after injection, and the tissue was dissociated to single cells for immediate analysis by flow cytometry. The results showed that AAV9, AAV6.2, and AAV8 marked the Pax7nGFP+ cells most efficiently, leading to tdTomato expression in ~60% of nGFP+ cells. Systemic injection of AAV8 and AAV9 via tail-vein injection also demonstrated significant targeting of Pax7-GFP+ cells, ranging from 20% to 30% for various muscle types.

#### 8.2. AAV9-CMV-CRISPR Constructs Target Satellite Cells for Gene Editing In Vivo

To assess the level of gene editing in satellite cells, a dual AAV9-CRISPR strategy was employed. One AAV9 vector encoded CMV-driven SaCas9, and the other AAV9 vector encoded two gRNAs designed to excise exon 23 from the Dmd gene in mdx mice. The AAV vectors were premixed in equivalent viral titers and injected into the TA muscle. At 8 weeks after injection, muscle was harvested for enzymatic dissociation and satellite cell sorting. Genomic DNA was isolated from sorted cells, and a nested PCR reaction was performed across exon 23 to visualize the rare and smaller deletion band in satellite cells isolated from CRISPR-treated muscle. Systemic delivery of AAV9-CMV-CRISPR at 5E+12 vg/vector was also performed, and satellite cells were isolated from hind-limb muscles and the diaphragm 8 weeks after treatment. A deletion band could also be detected from satellite cells after systemic intravenous delivery in the majority of samples. Sanger sequencing of the gel-extracted deletion band confirmed exon 23 deletion.

To quantify the level of gene editing in satellite cells, an unbiased Tn5 transposon-based DNA tagmentation protocol was used for sequencing. The various editing outcomes ranged from ~0.01% to 1% in satellite cells, with indels at single gRNA sites being the most common outcome. The level of exon 23 deletion in dystrophin transcripts ranged from ~0.4% to 1%.

#### 8.3. Muscle-Specific Promoters Are Active in Satellite Cells

To determine the efficiency of gene expression in satellite cells with muscle-specific promoters, AAV9 vectors encoding the ubiquitous CMV promoter or the muscle-specific CK8e, SPc5-12, or MHCK7 promoters driving Cre recombinase expression were delivered to the TA muscle of Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice. The efficiency of recombination was highest in CMV-driven Cre (33%), followed by MHCK7 (15.6%), CK8e (15.6%), and SPc5-12 (11.5%). These results indicate that muscle-specific promoters are active in satellite cells, albeit to a lesser degree than CMV.

To compare gene-editing efficiencies in ubiquitous versus muscle-specific promoters, SaCas9 expression was driven with CMV, CK8e, SPc5-12, or MHCK7 promoters, and AAV9-CRISPR constructs were delivered intramuscularly at equivalent viral doses. Dystrophin restoration at the bulk muscle level was higher in muscles treated with AAV-CRISPR harboring MHCK7 (73%), SPc5-12 (53%), and CK8e (48.3%) promoters compared to CMV (35%). The highest occurrence of deletions was observed in the MHCK7-SaCas9-treated mice, followed by SPc5-12. Exon 23 deletion bands were detected in all conditions in sorted satellite cells.

#### 8.4. Sustained Dystrophin Expression in AAV-CMV-CRISPR-Treated Muscle After Serial Injuries

To investigate the long-term contribution of dystrophin-corrected satellite cells, a serial injury strategy was implemented. TA muscles of mdx mice were injected with AAV9-CMV-CRISPR constructs and subjected to muscle injury with 1.2% barium chloride (BaCl2) every 2 weeks for a maximum of 6 weeks. After 2 or 3 injuries, the loss of the AAV9 vectors was confirmed by western blot. Despite the loss of vector, dystrophin expression was maintained over three rounds of regeneration. The quantification of dystrophin+ fibers indicated an initial restoration of dystrophin in 28.3% of fibers, which decreased to ~10% after either 2 or 3 injuries. This suggests that edited satellite cells may serve as a long-term reservoir for dystrophin production.

#### 8.5. Serial Transplantation of CRISPR-Corrected Satellite Cells Contributes to Regeneration of Dystrophin+ Myofibers

To demonstrate that gene-edited satellite cells can give rise to dystrophin+ myofibers, a serial transplantation study was performed. Pax7nGFP;mdx mice were injected with AAV9-CMV-CRISPR constructs or PBS. Eight weeks later, the injected muscles were harvested, and ~30,000 GFP+ satellite cells were sorted by flow cytometry and immediately transplanted into the TA muscle of an otherwise untreated mdx host mouse. The host mice received 18 Gy irradiation to incapacitate host satellite cells and were immunosuppressed with daily intraperitoneal injections of tacrolimus. Four weeks post-engraftment, the TA muscles were harvested and analyzed for dystrophin expression. Patches of dystrophin+ fibers were observed in the host TA muscles injected with CRISPR-corrected satellite cells. The number of dystrophin+ fibers per square millimeter was significantly higher in host muscles injected with CRISPR-treated satellite cells compared to those injected with PBS-treated satellite cells. Deletion PCR across the Dmd locus confirmed the presence of the deletion band only in genomic DNA from the host TA that was injected with CRISPR-treated satellite cells.
