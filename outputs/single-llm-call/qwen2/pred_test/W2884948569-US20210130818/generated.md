# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in genome editing using CRISPR-Cas9 technology. Specifically, the invention involves the use of catalytically dead guide RNAs (dgRNAs) in conjunction with CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) and CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) modules to modulate the expression of key genes involved in DNA damage repair pathways. This modulation shifts the balance from non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) to HDR, thereby increasing the precision and efficiency of genome editing.

Genome editing technologies, particularly those utilizing CRISPR-Cas9, have revolutionized the field of molecular biology. CRISPR-Cas9 systems induce site-specific double-strand breaks (DSBs) in DNA, which can be repaired by either NHEJ or HDR. NHEJ is an error-prone process that often introduces insertions or deletions (indels) at the break site, leading to gene disruption. In contrast, HDR is a more accurate process that can be used to introduce precise modifications, such as gene corrections or insertions, using a donor template. However, HDR is generally less efficient than NHEJ, especially in mammalian cells, limiting its utility in many applications.

To address this limitation, various strategies have been explored to enhance HDR efficiency. These include the use of small molecules, cell cycle synchronization, and genetic manipulation of DNA repair pathways. While these approaches have shown some promise, they often come with limitations such as off-target effects, toxicity, and limited applicability in vivo.

The present invention provides a novel and effective method for enhancing HDR efficiency by modulating the expression of key genes involved in DNA repair pathways using CRISPRa and CRISPRi. This approach leverages the power of CRISPR-Cas9 to simultaneously perform genome editing and regulate gene expression, offering a versatile and precise tool for genome engineering.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

- **CRISPR-Cas9**: A genome editing technology that uses a guide RNA (gRNA) to direct the Cas9 nuclease to a specific DNA sequence, where it induces a double-strand break (DSB).
- **Homology-Directed Repair (HDR)**: A DNA repair mechanism that uses a homologous DNA template to repair double-strand breaks, allowing for precise genome modifications.
- **Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ)**: An error-prone DNA repair mechanism that directly ligates broken DNA ends, often introducing insertions or deletions (indels).
- **Catalytically Dead Guide RNA (dgRNA)**: A guide RNA that is catalytically inactive but retains the ability to bind to a target DNA sequence.
- **CRISPR Activation (CRISPRa)**: A technique that uses CRISPR-Cas9 to upregulate gene expression by recruiting transcriptional activators.
- **CRISPR Interference (CRISPRi)**: A technique that uses CRISPR-Cas9 to downregulate gene expression by recruiting transcriptional repressors.
- **Expression Vector**: A DNA molecule used to introduce and express genetic material in cells.

### Description

The present invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in genome editing using CRISPR-Cas9 technology. The invention involves the use of catalytically dead guide RNAs (dgRNAs) in conjunction with CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) and CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) modules to modulate the expression of key genes involved in DNA repair pathways. This modulation shifts the balance from non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) to HDR, thereby increasing the precision and efficiency of genome editing.

### Compositions

The compositions of the present invention include:

1. **Catalytically Dead Guide RNAs (dgRNAs)**: These are guide RNAs that are catalytically inactive but retain the ability to bind to a target DNA sequence. The dgRNAs are designed to target key genes involved in DNA repair pathways, such as CDK1, CtIP, LIG4, KU70, and KU80.

2. **CRISPRa and CRISPRi Effector Domains**:
   - **CRISPRa Effector Domain (e.g., MCP-P65-HSF1)**: This domain is used to upregulate the expression of target genes. The MCP-P65-HSF1 complex is recruited to the target gene by the dgRNA, leading to increased transcription.
   - **CRISPRi Effector Domain (e.g., COM-KRAB)**: This domain is used to downregulate the expression of target genes. The COM-KRAB complex is recruited to the target gene by the dgRNA, leading to decreased transcription.

3. **Expression Vectors**: These vectors are used to deliver the dgRNAs and effector domains into cells. The vectors can be plasmids, lentiviruses, or other suitable delivery systems.

### Methods

The methods of the present invention involve the following steps:

1. **Transfection of Cells**:
   - Cells are transfected with expression vectors encoding the dgRNAs and CRISPRa/CRISPRi effector domains. The cells can be any suitable cell type, including mammalian cells, yeast, and plant cells.

2. **Induction of DSBs**:
   - CRISPR-Cas9 is used to induce double-strand breaks (DSBs) at a target locus. The Cas9 nuclease is directed to the target locus by a guide RNA (gRNA).

3. **Provision of Donor Template**:
   - A donor template is provided to facilitate HDR. The donor template is a DNA sequence that is homologous to the region surrounding the DSB and contains the desired modification.

4. **Measurement of HDR Efficiency**:
   - The efficiency of HDR at the target locus is measured using various techniques, such as flow cytometry, PCR, and sequencing. The percentage of cells with the desired modification is quantified to assess the enhancement of HDR efficiency.

## Experimental Examples

### Example 1

#### Objective

To demonstrate the effectiveness of the invention in enhancing HDR efficiency in mammalian cells.

#### Materials and Methods

1. **Cell Culture**:
   - HEK293, HEK293T, and HeLa cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, penicillin, and streptomycin.

2. **Plasmid Construction**:
   - Expression vectors were constructed to encode dgRNAs targeting CDK1, CtIP, LIG4, KU70, and KU80, along with CRISPRa (MCP-P65-HSF1) and CRISPRi (COM-KRAB) effector domains.

3. **Transfection**:
   - Cells were transfected with the expression vectors using Lipofectamine 3000. After 24 hours, the cells were co-transfected with a Cas9/sgRNA plasmid targeting the AAVS1 locus and a donor template for HDR.

4. **Flow Cytometry**:
   - The cells were analyzed by flow cytometry to measure the percentage of cells with the desired modification (EGFP-positive cells).

5. **Sequencing**:
   - Genomic DNA was isolated from the transfected cells, and PCR was performed to amplify the target locus. The PCR products were sequenced to confirm the precise integration of the donor template.

#### Results

- **Enhanced HDR Efficiency**:
  - The percentage of EGFP-positive cells was significantly increased in cells transfected with the expression vectors encoding the dgRNAs and CRISPRa/CRISPRi effector domains compared to control cells.
  - The enhancement of HDR efficiency was most dramatic in the group where CDK1 was activated and KU80 was repressed, with a 4-8 fold increase in HDR efficiency.

- **Sequencing Confirmation**:
  - Sequencing of the PCR products confirmed the precise integration of the donor template into the AAVS1 locus, validating the enhancement of HDR efficiency.
