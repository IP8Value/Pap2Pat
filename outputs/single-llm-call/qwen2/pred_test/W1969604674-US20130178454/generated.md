# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for increasing testosterone levels in a subject while minimizing adverse effects on the prostate. More specifically, the invention involves the use of testosterone or analogues or derivatives thereof in combination with ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitors to achieve selective anabolic effects on skeletal muscle without promoting prostatic growth.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Testosterone is a critical hormone that plays a pivotal role in the development and maintenance of male sexual characteristics, muscle mass, and bone density. It is well-established that testosterone levels in men are positively associated with skeletal muscle mass, strength, and physical function. Testosterone administration has been shown to increase lean body mass and maximal voluntary strength in men, making it a promising anabolic therapy for various conditions, including age-related muscle loss and chronic illness.

However, the use of testosterone as a therapeutic agent is often limited by its potential to cause adverse effects on the prostate, such as prostatic hyperplasia and increased risk of prostate cancer. These concerns have hindered the widespread adoption of testosterone therapy, particularly in older men and those with a history of prostate issues. Therefore, there is a significant unmet need for novel therapeutics that can selectively exert anabolic effects on skeletal muscle while minimizing or eliminating adverse effects on the prostate.

Recent research has identified a potential solution to this problem. It has been discovered that testosterone promotes myogenic differentiation of muscle progenitor cells through the activation of the androgen receptor (AR)/β-catenin pathway, leading to the expression of Wnt-target genes, including follistatin. Follistatin is essential for mediating testosterone's effects on myogenesis, and its administration has been shown to increase muscle mass without affecting prostate size. This observation suggests that follistatin and testosterone may activate different signaling pathways in the prostate and muscle.

Further investigation using microarray analysis revealed that polyamine biosynthesis, particularly the expression of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), is a key androgen-sensitive pathway in the prostate. ODC is the rate-limiting enzyme in polyamine biosynthesis, which is known to regulate prostate growth. In contrast, follistatin does not activate this pathway in the prostate. Based on these findings, it was hypothesized that combining testosterone with an ODC inhibitor could block testosterone's effects on the prostate while retaining its anabolic effects on muscle.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following definitions apply:

- **Testosterone**: A steroid hormone from the androgen group and is found in mammals, reptiles, birds, and other vertebrates. It is the principal male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid.
- **Analogue or Derivative**: A chemical compound that is structurally similar to another but differs slightly in composition (e.g., substitution of one atom or group of atoms for another).
- **ODC Inhibitor**: A compound that inhibits the activity of ornithine decarboxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme in polyamine biosynthesis.
- **Subject**: Any individual, including humans and animals, who may benefit from the compositions and methods described herein.
- **Low Testosterone Disorder**: A condition characterized by low levels of testosterone in the body, which can lead to symptoms such as decreased muscle mass, reduced bone density, and diminished sexual function.

### Testosterone

Testosterone is a steroid hormone that plays a crucial role in the development and maintenance of male sexual characteristics, muscle mass, and bone density. It is synthesized primarily in the testes and, to a lesser extent, in the adrenal glands and ovaries. Testosterone exerts its effects by binding to the androgen receptor (AR), which then translocates to the nucleus and modulates the expression of target genes. In skeletal muscle, testosterone promotes myogenic differentiation and hypertrophy through the activation of the AR/β-catenin pathway and the induction of Wnt-target genes, including follistatin.

### Compositions Comprising Testosterone or Analogues or Derivatives Thereof and ODC Inhibitors

The compositions of the present invention comprise testosterone or an analogue or derivative thereof in combination with an ODC inhibitor. The testosterone component can be in the form of testosterone itself or any of its analogues or derivatives, such as testosterone propionate, testosterone enanthate, or testosterone undecanoate. The ODC inhibitor component can be any compound that effectively inhibits the activity of ornithine decarboxylase, such as α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO).

The compositions can be formulated in various forms suitable for administration to a subject, including oral formulations, injectable solutions, and topical preparations. The exact formulation will depend on the specific needs of the subject and the desired route of administration.

### Method for Increasing Testosterone Levels in a Subject

The method of the present invention involves administering to a subject a composition comprising testosterone or an analogue or derivative thereof and an ODC inhibitor. The administration can be via any suitable route, including oral, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intravenous. The dosage and frequency of administration will depend on the specific condition being treated and the individual characteristics of the subject.

The compositions of the present invention can be formulated for oral administration. Oral formulations can include tablets, capsules, powders, granules, and liquids. The formulations can contain excipients, binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and other pharmaceutically acceptable additives to enhance stability, bioavailability, and patient compliance.

The dosages of testosterone and the ODC inhibitor in the compositions of the present invention can vary depending on the specific condition being treated and the individual characteristics of the subject. Generally, the testosterone component can be administered at a dose of about 15 μg to about 100 μg per day, while the ODC inhibitor component can be administered at a dose of about 7.5 mg to about 15 mg per day. The administration regimen can be adjusted based on the response of the subject and the severity of the condition.

The compositions and methods of the present invention are particularly useful for treating low testosterone disorders, such as age-related muscle loss, chronic illness, and hypogonadism. Subjects amenable to treatment include older men with low testosterone levels, hypogonadal men at increased risk of prostate cancer, and men with prostate cancer who have undergone radical prostatectomy and are at low risk of disease recurrence.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Objective

To evaluate the effectiveness of a composition comprising testosterone and an ODC inhibitor in increasing muscle mass while minimizing adverse effects on the prostate.

#### Methods

**Animal Model**: Eight-week-old C57BL6 male mice were randomly assigned to one of four groups (n = 6 per group):
1. Intact (control)
2. Castrated (vehicle-treated)
3. Castrated + testosterone (15 μg/day)
4. Castrated + testosterone (15 μg/day) + DFMO (7.5 mg/day)

**Administration**: Testosterone and DFMO were administered subcutaneously and intraperitoneally, respectively, for 14 days. Body weights and lean and fat mass were measured weekly using EchoMRI-700. At the end of the treatment period, the mice were euthanized, and the wet weights of the levator ani muscle and dorsal prostate were measured.

**Microarray Analysis**: Total RNA was extracted from the prostate and levator ani muscle of the mice. Biotin-labeled cDNA was hybridized to Affymetrix Mouse 430 2.0 chips. Differential expression calculations were made using linear models to calculate a moderated t-statistic. Gene Ontology term enrichment was used to identify biological processes over-represented in sets of genes identified through gene expression analysis.

**In Vitro Studies**: LNCaP cells were treated with vehicle, testosterone (100 nm), or rFst (125 ng/mL) for 24 hours. Cell cycle analysis was performed using FITC BrdU kit, and Odc1 expression was measured by Western blotting.

#### Results

**Body Weight and Lean Mass**: Castrated mice had significantly lower body weights and lean mass compared to intact controls. Testosterone administration alone in castrated mice restored body weight and lean mass to levels observed in intact controls. Combined administration of testosterone and DFMO also restored body weight and lean mass to levels comparable to those of intact controls.

**Prostate Weight**: Castrated mice had significantly lower prostate weights compared to intact controls. Testosterone administration alone in castrated mice restored prostate weight to levels observed in intact controls. However, combined administration of testosterone and DFMO did not restore prostate weight, indicating that DFMO effectively blocked testosterone's effects on the prostate.

**Microarray Analysis**: Microarray analysis revealed that Odc1 expression was significantly upregulated in the prostate of testosterone-treated mice but not in rFst-treated mice. In the levator ani muscle, Odc1 expression showed little or no difference under any treatment.

**In Vitro Studies**: In LNCaP cells, testosterone treatment increased the expression of Odc1 and promoted cell proliferation, as evidenced by increased BrdU incorporation and the fraction of cells in S phase. In contrast, rFst treatment had no significant effect on Odc1 expression or cell proliferation.

#### Conclusion

The results of this study demonstrate that the combination of testosterone and an ODC inhibitor, such as DFMO, effectively increases muscle mass while minimizing adverse effects on the prostate. This combination therapy represents a promising approach for treating low testosterone disorders and enhancing muscle health without compromising prostate health.
