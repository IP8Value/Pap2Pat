# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the accumulation of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides in the brain. These peptides form various aggregation states, including monomers, oligomers, and fibrils, which contribute to the pathogenesis of AD. Among these, oligomeric forms of Aβ, particularly those with N-terminal modifications such as pyroglutamate (AβpE), have been implicated as key mediators of neurotoxicity and cognitive decline. The identification and characterization of specific antibodies that can selectively detect and target these oligomeric forms are crucial for developing diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for AD.

The present invention relates to novel monoclonal antibodies, specifically 9D5 and 8C4, which are highly selective for low molecular weight (LMW) oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42. These antibodies exhibit unique binding characteristics and have been shown to inhibit the aggregation and toxicity of AβpE3-42 in vitro. Furthermore, these antibodies have demonstrated therapeutic potential in transgenic mouse models of AD, reducing plaque load and improving cognitive function.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies, 9D5 and 8C4, that are highly selective for low molecular weight (LMW) oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42. These antibodies are useful for detecting and targeting AβpE3-42 oligomers, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease (AD). The antibodies can be used in various applications, including diagnostic assays, therapeutic interventions, and research tools.

The invention includes the following aspects:
1. Monoclonal antibodies, 9D5 and 8C4, that specifically bind to low molecular weight (LMW) oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42.
2. Methods for using these antibodies to detect AβpE3-42 oligomers in biological samples, such as plasma and brain tissue.
3. Therapeutic methods involving the administration of these antibodies to reduce AβpE3-42 oligomer levels and improve cognitive function in subjects with AD.
4. Diagnostic kits and methods for detecting AβpE3-42 oligomers using the antibodies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Generation and Characterization of Antibodies Selectively Detecting AβpE3 Oligomers

Two mouse monoclonal antibodies, 9D5 and 8C4, were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with AβpE3-38. These antibodies were selected based on their binding characteristics to different N-terminal Aβ epitopes. Both 9D5 and 8C4 were found to compete for the same epitope in dot blot analysis and exhibited an indistinguishable staining pattern using immunohistochemistry. Therefore, 9D5 was chosen for further detailed study.

To analyze the binding properties of the oligomeric AβpE3 antibody (9D5), size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) was performed under native conditions with N-terminally truncated and modified AβpE3-42 and wildtype Aβ1-42 peptides, followed by dot blot analysis. SEC of Aβ1-42 showed dominant peaks of low-n oligomers (4-6×) with some higher (16-20×) and few smaller (1-2×) aggregates. In contrast, SEC of AβpE3-42 yielded high levels of smaller forms (1-2×), low-n oligomers (4-6×), and lower levels of higher oligomeric aggregates (10-20×), indicating differential aggregation characteristics of Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42.

All Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 SEC fractions were recognized by the generic Aβ antibody W0-2 in a dot blot analysis. However, the 9D5 antibody detected only low-n oligomeric fractions (4-10×) of AβpE3-42, while no signal was obtained using the Aβ1-42 fractions. Under denaturing conditions, 9D5 detected one single band of low molecular weight (LMW) AβpE3-42 without any cross-reactivity for Aβ1-42. As expected, W0-2 detected a range of aggregation states of Aβ1-42 peptides as well as monomeric Aβ1-42. These data demonstrate that 9D5 is highly selective for lower oligomeric variants of AβpE3-42.

### Antibody 9D5 Inhibited Aggregation and Toxicity in Vitro

The aggregation of monomeric Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 peptides (55 μM) was investigated using a Thioflavin T (ThT) fluorescence assay. Aβ1-42 showed the expected aggregation profile with a pronounced lag phase before fibril growth, while AβpE3-42 showed very rapid formation of intermediate oligomeric assemblies. Interestingly, elongation rates of AβpE3-42 were much slower than those of Aβ1-42. These data indicate that AβpE3-42 rapidly forms intermediate oligomeric assemblies but has a decreased propensity to form larger fibrils, a behavior that clearly differs from that of Aβ1-42.

Notably, the presence of antibody 9D5 efficiently decreased the formation of higher aggregates of the AβpE3-42 peptide at a 1:76 (9D5:Aβ) ratio, but not the rapid formation of lower oligomers. This observation suggests that 9D5 inhibits the formation of higher Aβ aggregates by binding to LMW oligomers, as indicated in SEC and Western blot experiments.

The toxicity of Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 peptides was studied in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells. To determine whether the toxic effect of AβpE3-42 could be influenced by 9D5 antibody, neuroblastoma cells were incubated with Aβ1-42, AβpE3-42, or peptides and 9D5. Application of both AβpE3-42 and 9D5 completely abolished the toxic effect of AβpE3-42. This effect is highly specific, as application of Aβ1-42 together with 9D5 caused the same effect on cell viability as Aβ1-42 incubation alone.

### Antibody 9D5 Shows a Specific Staining Profile in Alzheimer Brain

The 9D5 antibody was used to characterize the distribution of oligomeric AβpE in human post-mortem brain tissue (frontal cortex and hippocampus from sporadic AD, familial AD (FAD), and non-demented individuals). While none of the non-demented controls showed plaque staining with 9D5, some specimens showed plaque staining using 4G8 (against Aβ17-24). Occasionally, weak 9D5 blood vessel immunostaining was observed. This observation demonstrated that plaques in healthy controls do not harbor the 9D5 epitope and indicates that plaques in non-demented controls do not contain oligomeric AβpE3.

In contrast, most of the sporadic AD and FAD cases demonstrated high abundance of intraneuronal and cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) staining with 9D5, clearly differing from the 4G8 pattern. FAD cases having mutations in the APP gene (Swedish or Arctic mutation) revealed abundant 9D5 immunoreactivity. Of interest, all analyzed FAD cases harboring mutations in the presenilin-1 gene (P264L, L418F, PS1Δexon9) showed prominent intraneuronal 9D5 immunoreactivity. Oligomeric AβpE3-42 antibody 9D5 showed a specific staining pattern different from the staining pattern with an antibody against the N-terminus of AβpE3-42.

### Lower Levels of Oligomeric AβpE3 in Plasma of Alzheimer Patients

To assess the diagnostic potential of oligomeric AβpE3 variants and antibody 9D5, a novel ELISA was established and tested on plasma from AD patients and healthy controls (HC). Levels of AβpE3 oligomers were significantly reduced in AD patients by 46% compared with healthy controls. This observation is consistent with previous findings that the level of IgM autoantibodies in plasma directed against AβpE3 was significantly decreased in AD patients compared with healthy controls. The signal of AβpE3 oligomers detected by 9D5 was significantly lower in plasma of AD patients, again pointing out that 9D5 can be used as a biomarker tool for AD diagnosis.

### Antibody 9D5 Shows a Specific Staining Profile in Transgenic Alzheimer Mouse Models

The presence of oligomeric AβpE peptides was also detected in transgenic mouse models for AD. Staining of 3-month-old 5XFAD mice did not show any immunoreactivity, while considerable staining was detected in the subiculum of 6-month-old 5XFAD mice, showing a dramatic increase at the age of 12 months. 9D5 detected only intracellular and no plaque-associated staining, corroborating the staining pattern in AD patients. Other brain areas like the cortex, pons, or brainstem nuclei also stained strongly positive at that time point.

Double-staining using 9D5 (red) and the astrocytic marker GFAP (green) in the subiculum of 12-month-old 5XFAD mice revealed almost no co-localization in astrocytes. On the other hand, double-staining using 9D5 (red) and the microglia/macrophage marker Iba-1 (green) showed a strong co-localization, suggesting that oligomeric AβpE3 variants are internalized by microglia. Strong intraneuronal 9D5-immunoreactivity was also demonstrated.

### Therapeutic Effect of Passive Immunization with 9D5 in 5XFAD Mice

The therapeutic potential of 9D5 was studied using a passive immunization approach. 4.5-month-old 5XFAD (female) mice were weekly injected with 250 μg of 9D5 intraperitoneally for 6 weeks. 9D5 treatment significantly reduced generic Aβ, Aβ42, Aβ40, and AβpE3 plaque load in the hippocampus (HC) and cortex (Ctx). In good agreement, 9D5 treatment significantly stabilized the performance in the elevated plus maze. Confirming the plaque load data, a significant reduction of AβpE3 levels was observed in both the TBS and SDS fraction of brain lysates after 9D5 immunization of 5XFAD mice. Passive immunization of 5XFAD mice with 9D5 showed a reduction of intracellular AβpE3-42 oligomers.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Generation and Characterization of 9D5 and 8C4 Antibodies

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Antibodies:** The AβpE3 oligomer-specific antibodies 9D5 (IgG2b) and 8C4 (IgG1) were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with AβpE3-38. Hybridoma supernatants were screened by ELISA and subcloned to isolate clones producing signals with AβpE3-38 and AβpE3-42 but no signal with AβpE1-42.
- **Size-Exclusion Chromatography (SEC):** Freshly dissolved synthetic Aβ peptides were loaded onto a Superdex 75 column and eluted with PBS. Fractions were spotted on nitrocellulose and detected by dot blot using W0-2 or 9D5 antibodies.
- **Western Blotting:** Peptides were loaded on 4-12% vario gels, transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, and detected using W0-2 and 9D5 antibodies.

**Results:**
- 9D5 and 8C4 were highly selective for low-n oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42, as shown by SEC and Western blot analysis.

#### Example 2: Inhibition of AβpE3-42 Aggregation and Toxicity by 9D5

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Aggregation Assay:** Aβ1-42 and AβpE3-42 peptides were solubilized in 10 mM NaOH and incubated at 37°C with stirring. ThT fluorescence was measured over time.
- **Toxicity Assay:** SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells were incubated with Aβ1-42, AβpE3-42, or peptides and 9D5. Cell viability was determined using an MTS assay.

**Results:**
- 9D5 efficiently inhibited the formation of higher aggregates of AβpE3-42 and reduced its toxicity in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells.

#### Example 3: Detection of AβpE3 Oligomers in Human Brain Tissue

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Immunohistochemistry:** Post-mortem brain tissue from AD patients and healthy controls was processed and stained with 9D5 and 4G8 antibodies.
- **Quantification:** Plaque load was evaluated using an Olympus BX-51 microscope and ImageJ software.

**Results:**
- 9D5 showed specific intraneuronal and CAA staining in AD brains, while no staining was observed in healthy controls.

#### Example 4: Diagnostic Potential of 9D5 in Plasma

**Materials and Methods:**
- **ELISA:** Plasma samples from AD patients and healthy controls were analyzed using 9D5 as the capture antibody and 2-48 as the detection antibody.

**Results:**
- Levels of AβpE3 oligomers were significantly lower in AD patients compared to healthy controls, indicating the diagnostic potential of 9D5.

#### Example 5: Therapeutic Effect of 9D5 in 5XFAD Mice

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Passive Immunization:** 4.5-month-old 5XFAD mice were weekly injected with 250 μg of 9D5 for 6 weeks.
- **Behavioral Testing:** Anxiety levels were assessed using an elevated plus maze.
- **Plaque Load and Aβ Levels:** Brain lysates were analyzed for Aβ levels using ELISA and Western blot.

**Results:**
- 9D5 treatment significantly reduced plaque load and improved cognitive function in 5XFAD mice.

In conclusion, the novel monoclonal antibodies 9D5 and 8C4 provide a powerful tool for detecting and targeting low molecular weight oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42, which are implicated in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's disease. These antibodies have significant potential for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications.
