# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of immunotherapy has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the context of cancer and other complex diseases. However, a persistent challenge has been the lack of consistent correlates between changes in the immune status of a patient and clinical outcomes. Traditional methods of describing immunity, such as measuring regulatory T cells (Tregs) as a percentage of CD4+ cells or lymphocytes, often fail to capture the full complexity of the immune system. These methods do not account for the patient's overall immune status, including the interactions between different immune cell types and the potential impact of conditions like lymphopenia.

To address these limitations, a more comprehensive and systemic approach is necessary. This invention introduces a novel methodology for characterizing human immunity using quantitative flow cytometry of whole blood. By determining the frequency of defined immune markers per unit volume of blood, this approach allows for direct quantification of immune markers and eliminates the need for processing steps that can affect the analysis. More importantly, it enables the determination of relationships between all members of the immune system, providing a more accurate and holistic view of the patient's immune status.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for comprehensively characterizing human immunity using quantitative flow cytometry of whole blood. The method involves determining the frequency of defined immune markers per unit volume of blood, which allows for direct quantification of immune markers and the elimination of processing steps that can affect the analysis. By analyzing the absolute and relative distribution of leukocytes, the method identifies immune profiles that are shared across different diseases and are prognostic of patient outcomes. The invention also includes the identification of novel relationships between immune cell populations and the development of a prognostic biomarker using the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte ratio.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview of the Method

The invention provides a method for comprehensively characterizing human immunity using quantitative flow cytometry of whole blood. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Sample Collection**: Peripheral blood samples are collected from patients and healthy volunteers under appropriate institutional review board approval. The samples are processed immediately to ensure the integrity of the immune cells.

2. **Flow Cytometry Analysis**: Immune markers are identified and quantified using flow cytometry. The markers include granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, CD3+ T cells, CD19+ B cells, (CD56+CD16+) NK cells, CD4+ T cells, regulatory T cells (CD4+CD25+CD127lo), CD86+ total monocytes, and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg immunosuppressive monocytes. The cell counts are measured directly in cells per microliter (cells/μl) or converted into cells/μl using the T, B, and NK counts.

3. **Data Normalization**: The immune marker values for each individual are normalized by dividing the individual value by the mean value of healthy volunteers for that marker. This normalization step ensures that the data is comparable across different individuals and disease states.

4. **Hierarchical Clustering**: The normalized immune marker values are analyzed using hierarchical clustering and principal component analysis. This step identifies immune profiles, which are groups of immune phenotypes with similar immune characteristics. The clustering is performed using unsupervised agglomerative Euclidean average linkage clustering.

5. **Statistical Analysis**: The immune profiles are statistically analyzed to determine their distribution across different disease groups and to identify prognostic factors. The analysis includes the use of non-parametric tests, correlation tests, and Cox models to evaluate the relationship between immune profiles and patient outcomes.

### Identification of Immune Profiles

The method identifies immune profiles by clustering immune phenotypes within individual diseases and across multiple diseases. The immune profiles are characterized by unique combinations of immune markers and are prognostic of patient outcomes. For example, in patients with glioblastoma multiforma (GBM), non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL), renal cell carcinoma (RCC), and ovarian cancer (OVA), the method identified distinct immune profiles that were associated with different survival rates. In acute lung injury (ALI) patients, the method identified immune profiles that correlated with the severity of the condition and patient survival.

### Novel Relationships Between Immune Markers

The hierarchical clustering analysis also identifies novel relationships between immune cell populations. For instance, the method revealed that granulocytes cluster independently of CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes and regulatory T cells, suggesting independent regulation. Additionally, the method identified an inverse relationship between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes, which has not been previously described. This relationship is important for understanding the dynamics of the immune system and developing targeted therapies.

### Prognostic Biomarker

One of the key findings of the invention is the identification of the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte ratio as a prognostic biomarker. The ratio is calculated by dividing the number of CD4+ T cells by the number of CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocytes (cells/μl). In a cohort of patients with GBM, NHL, and RCC, the median overall survival for patients with a high ratio (above 2.0) was significantly longer compared to patients with a low ratio (below 2.0). This biomarker has the potential to be used for risk stratification and prognosis in cancer patients.

### Applications and Future Directions

The method has several potential applications, including:

1. **Patient Stratification**: The method can be used to stratify patients based on their immune profiles, which can inform treatment decisions and improve patient outcomes.
2. **Drug Development**: The identification of immune profiles and novel relationships between immune markers can guide the development of immune modulating drugs.
3. **Longitudinal Studies**: The method can be applied in longitudinal studies to monitor changes in the immune system over time and to understand the dynamics of immune responses to different stimuli.
4. **Multi-Institutional Databases**: The method can be used to establish large multi-institutional databases to provide a comprehensive resource for understanding human immunology.

Future directions for the invention include expanding the sampling to include more disease states and healthy volunteers, characterizing additional immune markers, and integrating other global measures of immunity such as chemokines and cytokines.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Identification of Immune Profiles in GBM Patients

In this example, the method was applied to a cohort of 27 GBM patients and 40 healthy volunteers. The hierarchical clustering analysis identified three distinct immune profiles. Profile 1 contained 32 healthy volunteers, 5 dexamethasone (DEX) treated patients, and 5 untreated GBM patients. Profile 2 contained 8 healthy volunteers and 4 untreated patients. Profile 3 contained only 13 DEX treated GBM patients. The segregation of patients based on DEX treatment agreed with previously known informative subgroups, confirming the validity of the method.

#### Example 2: Identification of Immune Profiles in ALI Patients

In this example, the method was applied to a cohort of ALI patients. The hierarchical clustering analysis identified three distinct immune profiles. Profile 1 contained all healthy volunteers and 2 ALI patients. Profile 2 contained 7 ALI patients (5 septic), and Profile 3 contained 16 ALI patients (11 septic). Patients in Profile 2 exhibited a lower survival rate compared to patients in Profiles 1 and 3, indicating that the immune profiles are prognostic of patient outcomes.

#### Example 3: Identification of the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg Monocyte Ratio as a Prognostic Biomarker

In this example, the method was used to calculate the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte ratio in a cohort of patients with GBM, NHL, and RCC. The median overall survival for patients with a high ratio (above 2.0) was 30 months, compared to 9 months for patients with a low ratio (below 2.0). This finding confirms the potential of the CD4+/CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg monocyte ratio as a prognostic biomarker in cancer patients.

These examples demonstrate the utility and potential of the invention in characterizing human immunity and improving patient outcomes in various disease states.
