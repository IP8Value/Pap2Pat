# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD
The present invention relates to the field of virology and immunology, specifically to methods and compositions for inhibiting dengue virus replication. More particularly, the invention provides assays and pharmaceutical compositions for screening and treating dengue virus infections by targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and specific host response genes.

## BACKGROUND
Dengue virus (DENV) is a significant global health concern, causing millions of infections annually, including severe forms such as dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and dengue shock syndrome (DSS). Despite extensive research, the pathogenesis of dengue remains incompletely understood, and there are no specific antiviral treatments available. The virus infects various cell types, including hepatocytes, and triggers a complex host response involving multiple signaling pathways.

Recent studies have identified the upregulation of specific host genes and pathways during dengue infection, including the NF-κB-mediated cytokine/chemokine responses, type I interferon (IFN) response, and the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Notably, the upregulation of chemokines like IP-10 and I-TAC, and the antiviral protein viperin, has been observed in both cell lines and dengue patients. Additionally, proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN have been shown to reduce dengue virus replication.

There is a critical need for effective methods to screen for and treat dengue virus infections. The present invention addresses this need by providing assays and pharmaceutical compositions that target the identified host response pathways and specific genes, thereby offering new avenues for therapeutic intervention.

## SUMMARY
The present invention provides methods and compositions for inhibiting dengue virus replication by targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and specific host response genes. The invention includes:

1. **Assays for Identifying Compounds that Inhibit Dengue Virus Replication**: The assays involve measuring the effect of candidate compounds on the expression and activity of host response genes, particularly those involved in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, NF-κB-mediated cytokine/chemokine responses, and the type I IFN response.

2. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: The compositions comprise proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN, and other compounds that modulate the expression or activity of host response genes, including viperin, IP-10, and I-TAC.

3. **Methods for Treating Dengue Virus Infections**: The methods involve administering the pharmaceutical compositions to subjects infected with dengue virus, thereby reducing viral replication and ameliorating the symptoms of dengue fever, DHF, and DSS.

4. **Other Uses**: The invention also encompasses the use of the identified host response genes and pathways for diagnostic purposes, such as detecting the presence of dengue virus infection and monitoring the progression of the disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Dengue
Dengue virus (DENV) is a member of the Flaviviridae family and is transmitted primarily by the mosquitoes *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus*. There are four serotypes of dengue virus (DENV1-4), and infection with one serotype confers lifelong immunity to that serotype but not to others. Dengue infection can manifest as dengue fever (DF), a mild illness characterized by fever, headache, and muscle pain, or as more severe forms such as dengue hemorrhagic fever (DHF) and dengue shock syndrome (DSS), which can be life-threatening.

### Assays for Dengue
The invention provides assays for identifying compounds that inhibit dengue virus replication. These assays involve measuring the effect of candidate compounds on the expression and activity of host response genes, particularly those involved in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, NF-κB-mediated cytokine/chemokine responses, and the type I IFN response. The assays can be performed using cell lines that are susceptible to dengue virus infection, such as A549 and HepG2 cells, and can include the following steps:

1. **Infection of Cell Lines**: Cell lines are infected with dengue virus at a specified multiplicity of infection (MOI) and incubated for a predetermined period.
2. **Treatment with Candidate Compounds**: Candidate compounds are added to the infected cell cultures at various concentrations.
3. **Measurement of Viral Replication**: Viral replication is measured using methods such as plaque assays, real-time RT-PCR, and flow cytometry to quantify the production of viral particles.
4. **Analysis of Host Response Genes**: The expression and activity of host response genes, including viperin, IP-10, I-TAC, and genes involved in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, are analyzed using techniques such as quantitative PCR (qPCR) and Western blotting.
5. **Identification of Effective Compounds**: Compounds that significantly reduce viral replication and modulate the expression or activity of host response genes are identified as potential antiviral agents.

### Ubiquitin-Proteasome Pathway
The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is a critical component of the cellular machinery involved in protein degradation and trafficking. During dengue virus infection, the expression of genes involved in this pathway is upregulated, suggesting a role in viral replication. The invention provides methods for inhibiting dengue virus replication by targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. Specifically, the use of proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN has been shown to significantly reduce viral replication in cell lines.

### Screening Assays
Screening assays for identifying compounds that inhibit dengue virus replication by targeting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway can be designed as follows:

1. **Cell Culture and Infection**: Cultivate A549 or HepG2 cells and infect them with dengue virus at an appropriate MOI.
2. **Compound Treatment**: Treat the infected cells with a library of candidate compounds at varying concentrations.
3. **Viral Replication Assay**: Measure viral replication using plaque assays or real-time RT-PCR.
4. **Proteasome Activity Assay**: Assess the activity of the proteasome using fluorogenic substrates or Western blotting to detect the accumulation of polyubiquitinated proteins.
5. **Host Response Gene Expression**: Analyze the expression of host response genes, such as those involved in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, using qPCR or Western blotting.
6. **Data Analysis**: Identify compounds that significantly reduce viral replication and modulate the activity of the proteasome and the expression of host response genes.

### Variants and Functional Equivalents of ALLN and MG-132
The invention also encompasses variants and functional equivalents of the proteasome inhibitors ALLN and MG-132. These variants and equivalents include structurally similar compounds that exhibit similar or enhanced inhibitory activity against the proteasome and can effectively reduce dengue virus replication. The variants and equivalents can be identified through chemical synthesis and screening using the assays described above.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions
The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN, and other compounds that modulate the expression or activity of host response genes, including viperin, IP-10, and I-TAC. The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, and topical. The compositions may also include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, and adjuvants.

### Other Uses
The identified host response genes and pathways can be used for diagnostic purposes, such as detecting the presence of dengue virus infection and monitoring the progression of the disease. For example, the levels of IP-10 and I-TAC in patient serum can be measured using ELISA to diagnose dengue infection and predict the severity of the disease. Additionally, the expression of host response genes in patient samples can be analyzed using qPCR to monitor the effectiveness of antiviral treatments.

### Examples
#### Example 1: Inhibition of Dengue Virus Replication by Proteasome Inhibitors
A549 cells were infected with dengue virus (TSV01 strain) at an MOI of 1 and incubated for 48 hours. The cells were then treated with varying concentrations of MG-132 and ALLN for 24 hours. Viral replication was measured using a plaque assay, and the activity of the proteasome was assessed using a fluorogenic substrate. The results showed that both MG-132 and ALLN significantly reduced viral replication in a dose-dependent manner, with no cytotoxic effects at lower concentrations.

#### Example 2: Overexpression of Viperin Reduces Dengue Virus Replication
A549 cells stably overexpressing viperin were infected with dengue virus (TSV01 strain) at an MOI of 1 and incubated for 48 hours. Viral replication was measured using a plaque assay, and the expression of viperin was confirmed by Western blotting. The results showed that overexpression of viperin significantly reduced viral replication compared to wild-type A549 cells, both with and without pre-treatment with IFNβ.

#### Example 3: Upregulation of IP-10 and I-TAC in Dengue Patients
Serum samples from dengue patients and non-dengue fever patients were collected at different time points during the course of the disease. The levels of IP-10 and I-TAC were measured using ELISA. The results showed that the levels of IP-10 and I-TAC were significantly higher in dengue patients during the acute phase of the disease compared to the convalescent phase and non-dengue fever patients.

These examples demonstrate the effectiveness of the methods and compositions of the present invention in inhibiting dengue virus replication and provide a basis for the development of new therapeutic strategies for treating dengue virus infections.
