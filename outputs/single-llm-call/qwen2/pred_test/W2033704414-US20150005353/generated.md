# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) is a lipid metabolite that plays a crucial role in various cellular and physiological processes, including cell motility, invasion, proliferation, angiogenesis, and apoptosis. S1P is synthesized from sphingosine through the action of two isoenzymes, sphingosine kinase-1 (SphK1) and sphingosine kinase-2 (SphK2). Upon synthesis, S1P interacts with a family of G protein-coupled receptors (S1PR1–5) on the cell surface and/or intracellular targets, such as histone deacetylase (HDAC) and TRAF2, to mediate a wide range of functions in diverse pathophysiological conditions, including inflammation, immunity, and cancer.

Ceramide and sphingosine, the precursors of S1P, are associated with growth arrest and apoptosis. In contrast, S1P exerts pro-survival roles. The regulation of the levels of these metabolites, often referred to as the "sphingolipid rheostat," is complex and involves multiple enzymes, with SphK1 and SphK2 being central players. While SphK1 and SphK2 share a high degree of homology, they differ significantly in size, tissue distribution, and subcellular localization. SphK1 primarily resides in the cytosol, whereas SphK2 is present in several intracellular compartments, including the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria.

Evidence has shown that SphK1 promotes cell growth and survival and is frequently upregulated in tumor tissues, contributing to cancer development and progression. In contrast, the role of SphK2 is less understood. Initially, SphK2 was thought to be pro-apoptotic, but recent studies have demonstrated that downregulation of SphK2 inhibits the proliferation and migration of tumor cells, such as glioblastoma and breast cancer cells. Additionally, SphK2 has been implicated in histone acetylation, gene expression, and mitochondrial function, highlighting its potential importance in cancer pathology.

Despite the significant role of SphK2 in cancer, the development of selective SphK2 inhibitors has been limited. Currently, only a few SphK2 inhibitors with moderate potency, such as ABC 294640, SG-12, R-FTY720-OMe, and trans-12, have been reported. Therefore, the development of new and adaptable chemical scaffolds as selective SphK2 inhibitors is of great value for both pharmacological and therapeutic purposes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel compound, K145, which is a selective inhibitor of sphingosine kinase-2 (SphK2). K145, chemically named 3-(amino-ethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyl-phenyl)-propylidene]-thiazolidine-2,4-dione, is designed as a thiazolidine-2,4-dione (TZD) analogue. This compound selectively inhibits SphK2 with an IC50 of 4.30 ± 0.06 µM, without affecting SphK1 or ceramide kinase (CERK). K145 also demonstrates anti-proliferative and apoptotic effects in human leukemia U937 cells by inhibiting the phosphorylation of FTY720, a SphK2-specific substrate, and reducing the levels of S1P. Furthermore, K145 inhibits the activation of the ERK and Akt signaling pathways, which are known to be downstream of SphK2.

In vivo studies show that K145 significantly inhibits the growth of U937 tumors in nude mice through both intraperitoneal and oral administration without apparent toxicity. Additionally, K145 demonstrates anti-tumor activity in a syngeneic breast cancer model using BALB/c mice, further confirming its potential as a lead compound for the development of more potent and selective SphK2 inhibitors and new anticancer agents.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### In Formula I:

The compound of the present invention, K145, is represented by the following formula:

\[ \text{3-(amino-ethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyl-phenyl)-propylidene]-thiazolidine-2,4-dione} \]

This compound is a thiazolidine-2,4-dione (TZD) analogue designed to mimic the amino-hydroxyl sphingoid base of sphingosine. The 4-butoxy-phenyl ring and the propylidene linker are critical structural features that contribute to the selectivity and potency of K145 as a SphK2 inhibitor.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Synthesis of K145**

The synthesis of K145 is outlined in the following steps:

1. **Preparation of 3-(4-butoxy-phenyl)-propionaldehyde (10)**:
   - 4-Butoxybenzaldehyde (891.0 mg, 5.0 mmol) and Meldrum’s acid (720.0 mg, 5.0 mmol) were dissolved in ethanol (5.0 mL) and piperidine (two drops) was added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight. The resulting solid was filtered and washed with ethanol to yield 731.0 mg of a light yellow solid.
   - The solid was then reduced using sodium borohydride (314.0 mg, 8.4 mmol) in dichloromethane (20.0 mL) and acetic acid (3.0 mL). The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature for 1 hour, diluted with dichloromethane, and washed with brine and water. The organic layer was dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, filtered, and concentrated under vacuum. The residue was purified by flash column chromatography (hexane/acetone = 5/2) to yield 10 as a light yellow solid (28% yield).

2. **Preparation of 4-butoxyphenyl-3-propanal (11)**:
   - Compound 10 (368.0 mg, 1.2 mmol) was dissolved in tetrahydrofuran (THF, 7.0 mL) and triethylamine (0.3 mL, 2.4 mmol) followed by phenylsilane (0.4 mL, 3.6 mmol) was added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 2 hours, diluted with diethyl ether, and washed with water and brine. The organic layer was dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, filtered, and concentrated under vacuum. The residue was purified by flash column chromatography (hexane/acetone = 10/1) to yield 11 as a light colorless oil (81% yield).

3. **Preparation of t-butyl 2-bromoethylcarbamate (13)**:
   - Bromoethylamine hydrobromide (20.5 g, 100.0 mmol) and (Boc)2O (21.8 g, 100.0 mmol) were suspended in dichloromethane (200.0 mL) and triethylamine (13.9 mL, 100.0 mmol) was added dropwise at 0°C. The mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight, diluted with water, and the organic layer was washed with brine and dried over anhydrous Na2SO4. The solvents were removed under vacuum to yield 13 as a colorless oil (89% yield).

4. **Preparation of t-butyl 2-(2,4-dioxothiazolidin-3-yl)-ethylcarbamate (14)**:
   - Thiazolidine-2,4-dione (7.9 g, 68.0 mmol), 13 (17.9 g, 80.0 mmol), K2CO3 (11.1 g, 92.0 mmol), and TBAI (2.5 g, 6.8 mmol) were mixed in acetone (100.0 mL) and stirred at 40°C for 10 hours. The reaction mixture was cooled to room temperature, filtered, and concentrated under vacuum. The residue was purified by flash chromatography (hexane/ethyl acetate = 4/1 to 2/1) to yield 14 as a white solid (70% yield).

5. **Preparation of 3-(2-aminoethyl)-5-(3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene)thiazolidine-2,4-dione (7)**:
   - Compound 14 (2.0 g, 7.7 mmol) and 4-butoxyphenyl-3-propanal (1.9 g, 7.7 mmol) were dissolved in ethanol (60.0 mL) and piperidine (0.2 mL, 3.6 mmol) was added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight, concentrated under vacuum, and purified by flash column chromatography (hexane/ethyl acetate = 8/1) to yield t-butyl 2-(5-(3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene)-2,4-dioxothiazolidin-3-yl)ethylcarbamate as a white solid (60% yield).
   - The t-butyl 2-(5-(3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene)-2,4-dioxothiazolidin-3-yl)ethylcarbamate (2.3 g, 4.7 mmol) was dissolved in ethyl acetate (20.0 mL) and 4 M HCl in dioxane (6.0 mL) was added. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 5 hours, and the precipitate was collected by filtration to yield the HCl salt of 7 as a white solid (75% yield).

### Example 2

**In Vitro Inhibition of SphK2 by K145**

K145 was tested for its ability to inhibit recombinant SphK1 and SphK2. The results showed that K145 selectively inhibited SphK2 with an IC50 of 4.30 ± 0.06 µM, while no inhibition of SphK1 was observed at concentrations up to 10 µM. Lineweaver-Burk analysis revealed a Ki of 6.4 ± 0.7 µM for SphK2, indicating that K145 is a substrate-competitive inhibitor of SphK2. K145 also did not inhibit ceramide kinase (CERK) at concentrations up to 10 µM, further confirming its selectivity for SphK2.

### Example 3

**Effect of K145 on Cellular Levels of S1P**

Human leukemia U937 cells were treated with K145 at various concentrations. The results showed that K145 was readily taken up by U937 cells in a concentration-dependent manner. Treatment with K145 (10 µM) caused a decrease in total cellular S1P levels without significantly affecting ceramide levels. The level of ceramide-1-phosphate (C1P) was also not significantly affected, indicating that K145 does not interfere with ceramide kinase or ceramide synthase.

### Example 4

**Phosphorylation of FTY720 by K145**

K145 was tested for its ability to inhibit the phosphorylation of FTY720, a specific substrate of SphK2. The results showed that K145 inhibited the phosphorylation of FTY720, further confirming its specificity for SphK2.

### Example 5

**Anti-Proliferative and Apoptotic Effects of K145**

K145 was tested for its anti-proliferative and apoptotic effects in U937 cells. The results showed that K145 significantly inhibited the growth of U937 cells in a concentration-dependent manner. K145 also induced apoptosis in U937 cells, primarily late apoptosis with minimal necrosis. The inhibition of the ERK and Akt signaling pathways by K145 was also observed, consistent with its anti-proliferative and apoptotic effects.

### Example 6

**Molecular Modeling of K145 Binding to SphK2**

Molecular modeling studies were conducted to understand the selectivity of K145 for SphK2. The results showed that K145 binds preferentially to SphK2, forming strong hydrogen bond interactions with the carboxylate group of Asp344 and favorable π-stacking interactions with Phe350. The 4-butoxy-phenyl ring of K145 fits into a hydrophobic pocket in SphK2, further contributing to its selectivity.

### Example 7

**In Vivo Anti-Tumor Activity of K145 in U937 Xenografts**

K145 was tested for its in vivo anti-tumor activity in a U937 xenograft model in nude mice. The results showed that K145 significantly inhibited the growth of U937 tumors in nude mice with a TGI of 44.2%, slightly less potent than tamibarotene (TGI = 50.4%). No significant changes in body weight were observed in K145-treated mice, indicating a lack of toxicity.

### Example 8

**In Vivo Anti-Tumor Activity of K145 in Syngeneic Breast Cancer Model**

K145 was tested for its in vivo anti-tumor activity in a syngeneic breast cancer model using BALB/c mice. The results showed that K145 significantly inhibited the growth of JC tumors at both 20 mg/kg and 35 mg/kg doses, with the higher dose being more potent. No significant changes in body weight or major organs were observed, further confirming the safety and efficacy of K145.

### Compounds 28-36 39-40 and 42 of Schemes 2 and 3

Additional compounds 28-36, 39-40, and 42, derived from Schemes 2 and 3, were synthesized and tested for their ability to inhibit SphK2. The results showed that these compounds exhibited varying degrees of selectivity and potency, with some compounds showing improved selectivity and potency compared to K145. These findings suggest that further optimization of the chemical scaffold can lead to the development of more potent and selective SphK2 inhibitors.

## CONCLUSIONS

K145, a thiazolidine-2,4-dione (TZD) analogue, has been identified as a selective inhibitor of sphingosine kinase-2 (SphK2). Biochemical and molecular modeling studies have confirmed the selectivity and potency of K145 for SphK2. Biological characterization using human leukemia U937 cells has demonstrated that K145 accumulates in cells, inhibits the phosphorylation of FTY720, and reduces the levels of S1P. K145 also exhibits anti-proliferative and apoptotic effects by inhibiting the ERK and Akt signaling pathways. In vivo studies have shown that K145 significantly inhibits the growth of U937 tumors in nude mice and JC tumors in BALB/c mice, demonstrating its potential as a lead compound for the development of more potent and selective SphK2 inhibitors and new anticancer agents.
