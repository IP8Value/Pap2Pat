# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine, specifically to the use of human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) for the treatment of diabetes. More particularly, the invention concerns the genetic modification of hBMSCs to express vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) or pancreatic and duodenal homeobox 1 (PDX1) and their subsequent transplantation into diabetic animals to ameliorate hyperglycemia and promote β-cell regeneration.

## BACKGROUND

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin production, insulin action, or both. Despite advances in insulin therapy, achieving euglycemia remains challenging, and complications such as cardiovascular disease, neuropathy, and nephropathy continue to affect patients. Current treatments, including insulin replacement and pancreas/islet transplantation, have limitations such as the need for lifelong immunosuppression and the scarcity of donor organs.

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) derived from bone marrow (BMSCs) have emerged as a promising alternative for the treatment of diabetes. These cells possess multilineage differentiation potential, immunomodulatory properties, and the ability to secrete trophic factors that can promote tissue repair and regeneration. Previous studies have shown that BMSCs can differentiate into insulin-producing cells and improve glucose control in diabetic animals. However, the mechanisms underlying these effects, particularly the role of specific genes in enhancing β-cell regeneration, remain to be fully elucidated.

The present invention addresses these issues by providing a method for the genetic modification of hBMSCs to express VEGF or PDX1, followed by their transplantation into diabetic animals. The invention demonstrates that hBMSCs expressing VEGF can effectively ameliorate hyperglycemia and promote β-cell regeneration, primarily through the activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT signaling pathway and the modulation of intra-islet angiogenesis.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating diabetes using genetically modified human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs). Specifically, the invention involves the following steps:

1. **Isolation and Expansion of hBMSCs**: hBMSCs are isolated from human bone marrow and expanded in culture to obtain a sufficient number of cells for transplantation.

2. **Genetic Modification**: The hBMSCs are genetically modified to express vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) or pancreatic and duodenal homeobox 1 (PDX1) using viral vectors.

3. **Transplantation**: The genetically modified hBMSCs are transplanted into diabetic animals, preferably via intracardiac injection.

4. **Assessment of Efficacy**: The efficacy of the treatment is assessed by monitoring blood glucose levels, serum insulin levels, and histological analysis of pancreatic islets.

The invention further provides a composition comprising hBMSCs genetically modified to express VEGF or PDX1, and a method for preparing such a composition.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Isolation and Expansion of hBMSCs

Human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hBMSCs) are isolated from bone marrow aspirates obtained from a normal adult donor. The bone marrow aspirates are processed to isolate nucleated cells, which are then plated into a basal medium consisting of Alpha-MEM, 17% fetal bovine serum, 2 mM glutamine, 50 U/L penicillin, and 25 µg/L streptomycin. Non-adherent cells are removed after 24 hours, and the adherent cells are cultured for 5-7 days. At 80% confluence, the cells are harvested with trypsin/EDTA and passaged into new dishes. The cells undergo further expansions until they reach passage 6-7, at which point they are cryopreserved in 90% fetal bovine serum and 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at -80°C.

### Genetic Modification of hBMSCs

The hBMSCs are genetically modified to express VEGF or PDX1 using adenoviral vectors. The cDNAs encoding human VEGF165 or human PDX1 are subcloned into an Adeno-X viral DNA vector (BD Biosciences Clontech) under the control of a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter. The recombinant viruses are produced by homologous recombination in HEK293 cells and expanded according to the ViraPower Adenoviral Expression System manual (Invitrogen). The hBMSCs are transfected with the adenovirus carrying VEGF (Ad-VEGF) or PDX1 (Ad-PDX1) 2 days before transplantation. The successful expression of VEGF and PDX1 in the transfected cells is confirmed by quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) and Western blotting.

### Transplantation of Genetically Modified hBMSCs

Diabetes is induced in NOD/SCID mice by intraperitoneal injections of streptozotocin (STZ) at a dose of 50 mg/kg on days 1-3. Mice showing hyperglycemia (blood glucose >250 mg/dL) 7 days after STZ treatment are selected for transplantation. The genetically modified hBMSCs (hBMSCs-VEGF or hBMSCs-PDX1) are suspended in 150 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and injected into the left cardiac ventricle of the diabetic mice using a 30-gauge needle. Control groups include mice receiving sham injections, hBMSCs without genetic modification, and fibroblasts transfected with Ad-VEGF or Ad-PDX1.

### Assessment of Efficacy

#### Blood Glucose and Serum Insulin Measurements

Blood glucose levels are measured daily for the first week and twice weekly thereafter using a One Touch Ultra Meter and Test Strips (Lifescan Inc.). Serum insulin levels are determined by ultrasensitive mouse insulin enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (Alpco Diagnostics) and human insulin ELISA (Linco Research, Millipore Corporation) at 6 weeks post-transplantation.

#### Histological and Immunohistochemical Analyses

At 6 weeks post-transplantation, the mice are sacrificed, and pancreatic tissues are harvested and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The tissues are embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histological analysis. Immunohistochemical staining is performed to detect insulin, glucagon, VEGF, PDX1, and human β2-microglobulin. Double staining is used to identify human cells that have differentiated into β-cells or vascular structures.

#### Real-Time PCR and DNA Extraction

Total RNA is extracted from pancreatic tissues using the RNeasy Minikit (Qiagen), and cDNA is synthesized using the RT2 First Strand Kit (SABiosciences). Real-time PCR is performed to analyze the expression of genes related to the insulin signaling pathway using the Roche Light Cycler 480. Genomic DNA is extracted from mouse organs and human BMSCs using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen). Real-time PCR is performed to detect human DNA in the mouse tissues using human-specific primers targeting the β-actin gene.

### Mechanisms of β-Cell Regeneration

The activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT signaling pathway is a key mechanism underlying the β-cell regeneration observed in mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF. This pathway is known to promote β-cell survival, proliferation, and differentiation. The upregulation of insulin receptor-associated genes, such as Ins1, Igf2, and Igfbp1, and the downregulation of pro-apoptotic genes, such as Jun and p27Kip1, are indicative of the activation of this pathway. Additionally, the increased expression of VEGF in the β-cells of rescued mice suggests a role for VEGF in modulating intra-islet angiogenesis, which is essential for β-cell recovery.

Total RNA was extracted from pancreatic tissues using the RNeasy Minikit (Qiagen), and cDNA was synthesized using the RT2 First Strand Kit (SABiosciences). Real-time PCR was performed to analyze the expression of genes related to the insulin signaling pathway using the Roche Light Cycler 480. The expression levels of insulin receptor-associated genes, such as Ins1, Igf2, and Igfbp1, were significantly upregulated in the pancreas of mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF compared to diabetic control mice. Additionally, the expression of pro-apoptotic genes, such as Jun and p27Kip1, was downregulated in the rescued mice, indicating the activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT signaling pathway.

### Immunocytochemistry

Pancreatic tissues were harvested 6 weeks after stem cell injection and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The tissues were embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histological analysis. Immunohistochemical staining was performed to detect insulin, glucagon, VEGF, PDX1, and human β2-microglobulin. Double staining was used to identify human cells that had differentiated into β-cells or vascular structures. The results showed that hBMSCs-VEGF were robustly engrafted and diffusely survived in the pancreas, with some cells differentiating into vessels and β-cells. The pancreatic islets of mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF showed a significant increase in the number of insulin-expressing cells and a reduction in apoptosis, as indicated by the decreased expression of caspase 3 cleaved.

### Example 5

To further validate the role of VEGF in β-cell regeneration, the expression of VEGF in the β-cells of rescued mice was analyzed. The results showed a dramatic increase in VEGF expression in the β-cells of rescued mice compared to diabetic control mice, suggesting a possible activation of VEGF expression via the PI-3K/AKT pathway. Additionally, the activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT pathway in the β-cells of rescued mice was confirmed by the increased expression and nuclear localization of PDX1 and the reduced expression of p27Kip1, a cell cycle inhibitor known to be negatively regulated by the PI-3K/AKT pathway.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel method for the treatment of diabetes using genetically modified hBMSCs expressing VEGF or PDX1. The invention demonstrates that hBMSCs-VEGF can effectively ameliorate hyperglycemia and promote β-cell regeneration, primarily through the activation of the insulin/IGF1 receptor/PI-3K/AKT signaling pathway and the modulation of intra-islet angiogenesis. This approach offers a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes.
