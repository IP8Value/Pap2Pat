## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular biology and, more specifically, to the identification and use of a single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamer that selectively binds to the activated form of the RelA protein, a key component of the NF-κB signaling pathway. The invention further encompasses methods for the isolation, enrichment, and quantification of activated RelA using the aptamer, as well as applications of the aptamer in therapeutic and diagnostic contexts.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The NF-κB signaling pathway plays a crucial role in regulating various cellular processes, including inflammation, immune responses, and cell survival. Central to this pathway is the RelA protein, which, when activated, translocates from the cytoplasm to the nucleus and binds to specific DNA sequences to modulate gene expression. Activation of RelA involves its release from the inhibitory protein IκBα, followed by its phosphorylation and subsequent nuclear translocation. However, the precise quantification and isolation of activated RelA in cellular environments have been challenging due to the transient nature of its activation and the low abundance of the activated form.

Traditional methods for detecting and quantifying RelA, such as Western blotting and immunoprecipitation, often lack the specificity and sensitivity required to distinguish between the activated and inactive forms of the protein. This limitation has hindered the development of targeted therapies and diagnostics for diseases involving dysregulation of the NF-κB pathway.

Aptamers, which are short, single-stranded DNA or RNA molecules that can bind to specific target molecules with high affinity and specificity, offer a promising solution to these challenges. The present invention describes the selection and characterization of a novel ssDNA aptamer, designated P028F4, that selectively binds to the activated form of RelA. This aptamer has been shown to compete with the natural DNA binding sites of RelA, indicating its potential to interfere with RelA's transcriptional activity. Furthermore, the aptamer can be used to enrich and quantify activated RelA using mass spectrometry techniques, providing a powerful tool for studying the dynamics of RelA activation in various biological contexts.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamer, P028F4, that selectively binds to the activated form of the RelA protein. The aptamer has the sequence 5'-GGTAACCTTGAGTCA CGAATTCAA-3' and includes a 30-base variable region that is essential for binding to RelA. The aptamer binds to RelA with a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 6.4 × 10^-10 M, demonstrating high affinity and specificity.

The invention further encompasses methods for using the aptamer to isolate, enrich, and quantify activated RelA in cellular extracts. The methods involve incubating a sample containing RelA with the aptamer, capturing the aptamer-bound RelA using streptavidin-coated magnetic beads, and quantifying the captured RelA using selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SRM-MS). The aptamer can also be used to compete with natural DNA binding sites of RelA, thereby inhibiting RelA's transcriptional activity in cells.

Additionally, the invention provides compositions and kits comprising the aptamer and reagents necessary for its use in isolating, enriching, and quantifying activated RelA. The aptamer and methods of the invention have applications in therapeutic and diagnostic contexts, particularly in the treatment and monitoring of diseases involving dysregulation of the NF-κB pathway.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Selection and Characterization of the Aptamer

The ssDNA aptamer P028F4 was selected through a process of genetic enrichment using a library of DNA sequences. The library consisted of a 30-base random sequence flanked by 25-base common primer binding sites. The selection process involved multiple rounds of incubation with the target protein, GST-RelA, followed by separation of bound and unbound DNA using nitrocellulose filters. The bound DNA was eluted, amplified, and subjected to further rounds of selection until a pool of DNA sequences with high binding affinity to RelA was obtained.

Sequencing of the enriched pool revealed several clones, and the aptamer P028F4 was selected for further characterization based on its high binding affinity and specificity. The sequence of P028F4 is 5'-GGTAACCTTGAGTCA CGAATTCAA-3', and the 30-base variable region contains the sequence GGGAC, which matches half of the double-strand NF-κB natural binding site GGGACTTTCC.

### Binding Studies

#### Nitrocellulose Filter Binding Assay

The binding affinity of P028F4 to RelA was confirmed using a nitrocellulose filter binding assay. The 5'-end-labeled aptamer was incubated with GST-RelA in aptamer binding buffer, and the bound material was separated from the free DNA by filtration through nitrocellulose filters. The bound material was quantified using a scintillation counter, and the dissociation constant (Kd) was determined to be approximately 6.4 × 10^-10 M.

#### Surface Plasmon Resonance

The binding kinetics of P028F4 to RelA were further characterized using surface plasmon resonance (SPR). The 5'-biotinylated aptamer was immobilized on a CM5-SA chip, and GST-RelA was injected at various concentrations. The binding curves were analyzed using Biacore software, and the kinetic constants (ka and kd) were determined to be 1.8 × 10^6 1/Ms and 0.0018 1/s, respectively, yielding a Kd of 6.4 × 10^-10 M.

### Competition Experiments

Competition experiments were performed to determine whether P028F4 competes with natural double-strand NF-κB binding sites for RelA binding. Radiolabeled P028F4 was incubated with nuclear extracts (NE) from control or TNFα-stimulated A549 cells in the presence of increasing concentrations of unlabeled P028F4 or a control aptamer, P028A1. The bound complexes were fractionated by native gel electrophoresis, and the results showed that P028F4 effectively competed for RelA binding, while P028A1 did not.

### Cellular Localization and Inhibition of Gene Expression

#### Confocal Microscopy

To determine the cellular localization of P028F4, A549 cells expressing EGFP-RelA were stimulated with TNFα and stained with TEX 615-conjugated P028F4. Confocal microscopy revealed that P028F4 colocalized with activated EGFP-RelA in the nuclei of TNFα-stimulated cells, indicating that the aptamer binds to the activated form of RelA in vivo.

#### Inhibition of NF-κB Dependent Gene Expression

The ability of P028F4 to inhibit TNFα-induced NF-κB dependent gene expression was assessed using quantitative reverse transcriptase PCR (Q-RT-PCR). A549 cells were transfected with P028F4 or a control aptamer, P028A1, and stimulated with TNFα. The expression of NF-κB target genes, Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20, was significantly reduced in cells transfected with P028F4 compared to cells transfected with P028A1 or untransfected cells.

### Aptamer Tandem Affinity Purification (ATAP)

#### Affinity Isolation of RelA

The aptamer P028F4 was used to selectively enrich for activated RelA complexes in cellular extracts. Whole-cell extracts (WCE) from control or TNFα-stimulated A549 cells were incubated with biotinylated P028F4, and the aptamer-bound RelA was captured using streptavidin-coated magnetic beads. Western blot analysis of the captured material showed that P028F4 selectively enriched for activated RelA, as evidenced by the presence of phosphorylated Ser 536 RelA and the absence of IκBα.

#### Quantification Using Selected Reaction Monitoring Mass Spectrometry (SRM-MS)

The enriched RelA complexes were quantified using selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SRM-MS). The proteins were digested with trypsin, and the resulting peptides were analyzed by LC-SRM-MS. The quantification of RelA and its associated proteins, such as p300, was performed using stable isotope dilution (SID) methods. The results showed that P028F4 enrichment significantly increased the signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio for RelA quantification, allowing for the precise determination of the fraction of activated RelA in unstimulated and TNFα-stimulated cells.

### Applications

The aptamer P028F4 and the methods of the invention have numerous applications in both therapeutic and diagnostic contexts. In therapeutic applications, the aptamer can be used to inhibit RelA's transcriptional activity, thereby modulating the NF-κB signaling pathway and treating diseases involving chronic inflammation, such as rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease. In diagnostic applications, the aptamer can be used to quantify the levels of activated RelA in patient samples, providing valuable information for disease diagnosis and monitoring.

### Compositions and Kits

The invention also provides compositions and kits comprising the aptamer P028F4 and reagents necessary for its use in isolating, enriching, and quantifying activated RelA. The kits may include, but are not limited to, the aptamer, streptavidin-coated magnetic beads, trypsin, and stable isotope-labeled peptides for SRM-MS quantification.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel ssDNA aptamer, P028F4, that selectively binds to the activated form of the RelA protein with high affinity and specificity. The aptamer and methods of the invention offer a powerful tool for studying the dynamics of RelA activation and have significant applications in therapeutic and diagnostic contexts. The ability to precisely quantify and isolate activated RelA using the aptamer and SRM-MS opens new avenues for understanding the regulation of the NF-κB signaling pathway and developing targeted therapies for related diseases.
