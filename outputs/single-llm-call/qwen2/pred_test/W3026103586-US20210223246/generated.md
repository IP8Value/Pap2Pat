# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a device and method for the real-time diagnosis of cancer-involved lymph nodes. More specifically, the invention provides a simple, non-invasive, and reliable method to detect metastatic lymph nodes using a pH sensing device embedded in a syringe needle. The device, termed the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD), utilizes the acidification of lymph nodes due to fatty acid oxidation metabolism of cancer cells to accurately identify metastatic lymph nodes within seconds.

## BACKGROUND

Lymph nodes play a critical role in the immune system by filtering lymphatic fluid and containing white blood cells called lymphocytes. In various types of cancers, particularly breast carcinoma, lymph nodes are often the first site of metastasis. The sentinel lymph node (SLN) is the first lymph node to receive drainage from a primary tumor and is a key indicator of disease progression and metastasis. Accurate detection of metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes is essential for determining the appropriate treatment protocol.

Currently, the standard method for detecting metastatic cancer cells in lymph nodes during surgery is the frozen pathology technique. This method involves dissecting the lymph nodes and examining a portion of the tissue under a microscope. However, this process is highly dependent on the experience and skills of the pathologist and is limited by the time constraints of the frozen section. As a result, only a part of the lymph node is typically examined, leading to potential errors in diagnosis. Additionally, the dissection of lymph nodes, even if they are free of cancer, can cause significant side effects such as lymphedema, loss of sensation, and stiffness.

To address these limitations, several alternative methods have been explored. These include the use of fluorescent substances, microwave waves, and tissue impedance imaging techniques. While these methods have shown promise, they still lack the simplicity, speed, and reliability needed for real-time, intraoperative diagnosis.

The present invention provides a novel and improved method for the real-time diagnosis of cancer-involved lymph nodes. By leveraging the acidification of lymph nodes due to the fatty acid oxidation metabolism of cancer cells, the invention offers a non-invasive, rapid, and accurate diagnostic tool that can significantly enhance the surgical process and improve patient outcomes.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a device and method for the real-time diagnosis of cancer-involved lymph nodes. The device, referred to as the Metabolism-based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser (MMLD), consists of a micro-syringe with a gauge 18 needle embedded with a thin litmus paper. The litmus paper is used to measure the pH of the lymph node fluid, which changes due to the metabolic activity of cancer cells. The MMLD can detect metastatic lymph nodes within seconds, providing a quick and reliable diagnostic tool for surgeons.

The MMLD operates by injecting a small volume of sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) into the lymph node, which mixes with the lymph node fluid. The litmus paper embedded in the needle then changes color based on the pH of the mixture. If the pH is below 6, the lymph node is identified as metastatic. This method has been validated through extensive in vitro testing and has demonstrated high selectivity and sensitivity in detecting metastatic lymph nodes.

The invention also includes a method for using the MMLD to diagnose metastatic lymph nodes during surgery. The method involves inserting the MMLD into the lymph node, injecting PBS, and observing the color change of the litmus paper. The results can be used to guide surgical decisions, preventing the unnecessary removal of healthy lymph nodes and reducing the need for secondary surgeries.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Fabrication of MMLD for In Vitro Assays

The MMLD is fabricated using a micro-syringe with a gauge 18 needle. The inner diameter of the needle is 838 µm, and the syringe is filled with 100 µl of injectable sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). A thin litmus paper strip, such as ColorpHast® pH Test Strips (Merck, Germany), is embedded into the needle. The litmus paper is sensitive to pH changes and changes color based on the acidity or basicity of the fluid it comes into contact with.

The mechanism of the MMLD involves inserting the needle into the lymph node, injecting the PBS, and allowing the PBS to mix with the lymph node fluid. The litmus paper then changes color based on the pH of the mixture. If the pH is below 6, the lymph node is identified as metastatic. The test can be repeated with multiple needles to ensure accuracy, and if any one of the needles shows an acidic pH, the lymph node is declared as involved.

### In Vitro Sample Collection from Patients

The MMLD was tested on in vitro fresh lymph node samples collected from 25 breast cancer patients. The samples were collected from 2018-10-30 to 2019-11-30 at breast cancer central clinics and assistant hospitals. The patients provided consent according to an ethically approved protocol (IR.TUMS.VCR.REC.1397.532).

### Staining Process Using Hematoxylin and Eosin

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining is a routine histopathological technique used to demonstrate the nucleus and cytoplasmic inclusions in clinical specimens. The process involves deparaffinizing the tissue section, hydrating it through a series of alcohol baths, and staining it with hematoxylin and eosin. Hematoxylin stains the nucleus in light blue, and eosin stains the cytoplasm in pink. The detailed steps of the H&E staining process are as follows:

1. Deparaffinize the tissue section by placing it in xylene and repeating the process.
2. Hydrate the tissue section by passing it through decreasing concentrations of alcohol baths (100%, 90%, 80%, and 70%) and water.
3. Stain the tissue section in hematoxylin for 3-5 minutes.
4. Wash the tissue section in running tap water until it "blues" for 5 minutes or less.
5. Place the tissue section in 1% acid alcohol (1% HCl in 70% alcohol) for 5 minutes.
6. Wash the tissue section in running tap water until it turns blue again by dipping it in an alkaline solution (e.g., ammonia water) and then washing it in tap water.
7. Stain the tissue section in 1% eosin Y for 10 minutes.
8. Wash the tissue section in tap water for 1-5 minutes.
9. Dehydrate the tissue section by passing it through increasing concentrations of alcohol and clearing it in xylene.

### Result and Discussion

The metabolism of cancer cells that metastasize to lymph nodes is complex. Recent studies have shown that cancer cells in metastatic lymph nodes shift their metabolism from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO). This shift is characterized by the expression of Yes-Associated Protein (YAP), which activates FAO-related genes. The oxidation of fatty acids occurs in three stages: β-oxidation, participation in the Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. During these processes, CO2 is released and dissolves in the lymphatic fluid, resulting in the production of HCO3− (bicarbonate) and H+ ions. This leads to an acidic environment in metastatic lymph nodes.

By measuring the pH of the lymph node fluid in real-time, the MMLD can distinguish between normal and metastatic lymph nodes. The device was tested on 65 lymph nodes from 25 breast cancer patients. The pH of the lymph node fluid was measured using the MMLD, and the results were compared with the gold standard of frozen and permanent pathology. The MMLD showed approximately 90% selectivity and 92% sensitivity in detecting metastatic lymph nodes, outperforming the conventional frozen pathology method, which had a selectivity and sensitivity of 83%.

The pH of the lymph node fluid in cancerous lymph nodes was consistently below 6, while the pH in healthy lymph nodes ranged from 7.0 to 10.0. The MMLD's ability to detect metastatic lymph nodes in real-time can significantly enhance the surgical process by preventing the unnecessary removal of healthy lymph nodes and reducing the need for secondary surgeries.

### Example 1: Real-Time Diagnosis of Cancer-Involved Lymph Nodes

In a clinical setting, the MMLD was used to diagnose metastatic lymph nodes in real-time during surgery. The device was inserted into the lymph node, and 100 µl of PBS was injected. The litmus paper embedded in the needle changed color based on the pH of the lymph node fluid. If the pH was below 6, the lymph node was identified as metastatic.

For example, in one case, the MMLD detected a metastatic lymph node that was missed by the frozen pathology method. The patient's lymph node was initially declared negative by the frozen pathology, but the MMLD indicated an acidic pH, suggesting the presence of cancer cells. The final diagnosis, confirmed by permanent pathology, revealed that the lymph node was indeed metastatic. This case highlights the importance of the MMLD in providing accurate and real-time diagnosis, which can significantly impact surgical decisions and patient outcomes.

In conclusion, the MMLD offers a simple, non-invasive, and reliable method for the real-time diagnosis of cancer-involved lymph nodes. By leveraging the acidification of lymph nodes due to the fatty acid oxidation metabolism of cancer cells, the MMLD can provide immediate and accurate results, enhancing the surgical process and improving patient care.
