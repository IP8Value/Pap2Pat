# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a novel system for the sustained expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) in the cytoplasm of cells. Specifically, the invention provides a T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system capable of inducing long-term gene silencing effects both in vitro and in vivo. The system includes a T7 polymerase (T7pol) mRNA, a pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, and a T7 autogene plasmid. The invention further encompasses the use of neutral DOPC liposomes for the efficient delivery of this hybrid system to target cells and tissues.

## BACKGROUND ART

RNA interference (RNAi) has emerged as a powerful tool for gene silencing in therapeutic applications and gene functional studies. Chemically synthesized small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) have been widely used to trigger RNAi in eukaryotic cells. However, the transient nature of siRNA-mediated gene silencing, typically lasting only 2-4 days, has limited its clinical utility. While viral vectors have been used to achieve long-term gene silencing, their use is often constrained by safety concerns such as unwanted immune responses and insertion mutagenesis, as well as difficulties in large-scale manufacturing.

Nonviral approaches, such as plasmid-based expression systems, have been developed to overcome these limitations. However, these systems often suffer from low transfection efficiency and the requirement for nuclear entry, which limits their effectiveness in post-mitotic or non-dividing cells. Cytoplasmic expression systems, which can transcribe shRNA directly in the cytoplasm, have been proposed as an alternative. However, existing cytoplasmic systems still require nuclear entry for the initial expression of T7 polymerase (T7pol), which compromises their efficiency and sustainability.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system that enables sustained cytoplasmic expression of shRNA without the need for nuclear entry. This system utilizes a T7 autogene plasmid, T7pol mRNA, and a pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment to achieve high levels of T7pol expression and subsequent shRNA production. The invention further includes the use of neutral DOPC liposomes for efficient delivery of the hybrid system to target cells and tissues, ensuring long-term gene silencing effects both in vitro and in vivo.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the need for a nonviral system capable of inducing long-term gene silencing effects in cells and tissues. Existing siRNA-based systems are limited by their transient nature, while viral vectors pose safety concerns. Nonviral plasmid-based systems often require nuclear entry, which limits their effectiveness in certain cell types. The present invention aims to provide a solution to these problems by developing a T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system that can sustainably express shRNA in the cytoplasm without the need for nuclear entry.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system for sustained cytoplasmic expression of shRNA. The system comprises:
1. A T7 autogene plasmid, which includes a T7 promoter, an EMCV (Encephalomyocarditis virus) IRES (Internal Ribosomal Entry Site) element, a T7pol-encoding gene, a polyA sequence, and a T7 terminator.
2. T7pol mRNA, which is 5′-capped and designed to initiate the expression of T7pol in the cytoplasm.
3. A pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, which specifies the sequence of the shRNA to be expressed under the control of the T7 promoter.

The T7 autogene plasmid is designed to produce T7pol mRNA in the cytoplasm, which is then translated into T7pol protein. The T7pol protein binds to the T7 promoter on the T7 autogene plasmid, initiating a positive feedback loop that results in the production of large quantities of T7pol protein. These T7pol proteins then bind to the T7 promoter on the pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, inducing the expression of shRNA in the cytoplasm. The shRNA is processed into mature siRNA by the cellular RNAi machinery, leading to the long-term silencing of the target gene.

The invention further provides a method for delivering the hybrid mRNA/DNA system to target cells and tissues using neutral DOPC liposomes. The liposomes encapsulate the T7pol mRNA, pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, and T7 autogene plasmid, ensuring efficient cellular uptake and sustained gene silencing effects.

### Advantageous Effects of the Invention

The present invention offers several advantages over existing gene silencing systems:
1. **Long-term Gene Silencing**: The T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system enables sustained cytoplasmic expression of shRNA, leading to long-term gene silencing effects both in vitro and in vivo.
2. **No Nuclear Entry Required**: The system does not require nuclear entry for the initial expression of T7pol, making it effective in post-mitotic or non-dividing cells.
3. **High Efficiency**: The positive feedback loop of T7pol expression ensures high levels of T7pol protein, leading to efficient shRNA production.
4. **Nonviral Delivery**: The use of neutral DOPC liposomes for delivery avoids the safety concerns associated with viral vectors.
5. **Low Immunogenicity**: The system does not induce significant innate immune responses, reducing the risk of adverse effects.
6. **Versatility**: The system can be adapted to target various genes and cell types, making it suitable for a wide range of therapeutic applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1. Design and Preparation of pT7/T7 Polymerase System Capable of Sustained Expression of shRNA in Cytoplasm

To develop the T7 autogene-based hybrid mRNA/DNA system, we first constructed a T7 autogene plasmid. The plasmid was designed to include the T7 promoter, an EMCV IRES element, a T7pol-encoding gene, a polyA sequence, and a T7 terminator. The T7pol-encoding gene was amplified from the chromosomal DNA of E. coli BL21 (DE3) using PCR and cloned into the pT7CFE1-NHis plasmid. The resulting T7 autogene plasmid was verified by DNA sequencing.

Next, we synthesized the pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment using an assembly PCR method. The DNA fragment was designed to specify a 19-nt sequence directed against the target mRNA, separated by a 9-nt loop from the reverse complementary sequence. The first nucleotide of the DNA fragment was designed to be guanosine to ensure efficient T7pol initiation.

Finally, we synthesized 5′-capped T7pol mRNA using in vitro transcription. The mRNA was synthesized from chemically synthesized dsDNA templates using the HiScribe T7 High Yield RNA Synthesis Kit, with the incorporation of anti-reverse cap analog (ARCA) to ensure 5′-capping.

### Example 2. Preparation of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

To prepare the liposome-formulated lipoplexes (auto_shRNA@LS), we mixed neutral lipid DOPC with the T7pol mRNA, pT7/shRNA-encoding DNA fragment, and T7 autogene plasmid in the presence of excess t-butanol. The mixture was lyophilized to remove the residual organic solvent, and then hydrated with normal 0.9% saline solution. Free oligonucleotides not taken up by the liposomes were removed using an Amicon ultracentrifugal filter. The complexing efficiency of the oligonucleotides with the liposomes was determined to be more than 90%. The final concentration of the lipoplexes was adjusted to 150 μg/mL.

### Example 3. Examination of Biophysicochemical Properties of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

The hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential of the lipoplexes were measured using a Zetasizer Nano ZS. The lipoplexes had a nano-sized hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm and a zeta potential of 1.1 mV. Gel retardation assays were performed to evaluate the complexing ratio between the oligonucleotides and liposomes. The results showed that the lipoplexes were fully complexed at a weight ratio of 1:10 (oligonucleotides:DOPC).

### Example 4. Experiment on Sustained Expression of T7 Polymerase Using Modified pT7/T7 Polymerase System

To evaluate the sustained expression of T7pol, B16F10/RFP cells were transfected with lipoplexes containing the T7 autogene plasmid and either T7pol mRNA or pCMV_T7pol plasmid. The expression levels of T7pol were monitored over time using western blotting. The results showed that the co-delivery of T7pol mRNA and T7 autogene plasmid led to a significant increase in T7pol protein levels, which persisted for at least 9 days. In contrast, the pCMV_T7pol plasmid system showed a much lower and less sustained increase in T7pol expression.

### Example 5. Examination of Long-Term Cytoplasmic Expression of shRNAs by Auto_shRFP@LS and their Conversion to siRNAs

To assess the long-term cytoplasmic expression of shRNAs, B16F10/RFP cells were transfected with auto_shRFP@LS or auto(−)_shRFP@LS. The levels of mature siRNA were quantified using a TaqMan assay. The results showed that the levels of mature siRNA in the auto_shRFP@LS-transfected cells increased exponentially over time, reaching maximum values 10 days post-transfection. In contrast, the auto(−)_shRFP@LS-transfected cells showed much lower levels of mature siRNA, indicating the importance of the T7 autogene plasmid in sustaining shRNA expression.

### Example 6. Examination of In Vitro Gene Silencing by Auto shRNA @LS Nanoparticles

To evaluate the gene silencing effects of the hybrid system, B16F10/RFP cells were transfected with auto_shRFP@LS or synthetic siRFP@LS. The levels of RFP mRNA and protein were quantified using qRT-PCR and western blotting, respectively. The results showed that the auto_shRFP@LS system led to a significant and sustained decrease in RFP mRNA and protein levels, lasting for at least 13 days. In contrast, the synthetic siRFP@LS system showed a transient decrease in RFP expression, lasting only 3-4 days.

### Example 7. Examination of Cancer Accumulation Property of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

To evaluate the biodistribution of the lipoplexes in vivo, B16F10 xenograft mice were intravenously administered with Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRFP@LS or free Cy5.5-siRFP. The ex vivo fluorescence images showed that the auto_shRFP@LS lipoplexes preferentially accumulated in the tumor sites, while the free Cy5.5-siRFP was primarily cleared by the kidneys. These results indicate that the DOPC liposomes effectively delivered the hybrid system to the tumor sites.

### Example 8. Examination of Gene Silencing of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

To assess the in vivo gene silencing effects of the hybrid system, B16F10/RFP xenograft mice were intravenously administered with auto_shRFP@LS, scrambled auto_shRFP@LS, or synthetic siRFP@LS. The RFP fluorescence intensities at the tumor sites were monitored using a fluorescence animal imaging system. The results showed that the auto_shRFP@LS system led to a significant and sustained decrease in RFP fluorescence, lasting for at least 6 days. In contrast, the scrambled auto_shRFP@LS and synthetic siRFP@LS systems showed much weaker and transient silencing effects. The ex vivo fluorescence images and qRT-PCR analysis of the excised tumors confirmed the long-term gene silencing effects of the auto_shRFP@LS system.
