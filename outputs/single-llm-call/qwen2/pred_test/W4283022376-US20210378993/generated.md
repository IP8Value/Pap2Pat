## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating cystinosis, a rare autosomal-recessive lysosomal storage disease. More specifically, the invention provides novel compounds, N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA), and their use in reducing intralysosomal cystine accumulation in cystinotic cells. The invention also encompasses methods for evaluating the cytotoxicity, cystine-depleting activity, and ophthalmic tolerability of these compounds.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cystinosis is a rare genetic disorder characterized by the accumulation of the amino acid cystine within lysosomes due to mutations in the CTNS gene, which encodes the cystine transporter, cystinosin. This accumulation leads to progressive damage to various tissues and organs, including the kidneys, eyes, liver, muscles, pancreas, and brain. The disease manifests in three major phenotypes: nephropathic, intermediate, and non-nephropathic (ocular) cystinosis. Nephropathic cystinosis, the most severe form, is characterized by renal Fanconi syndrome, poor growth, hypophosphatemic/calcipenic rickets, and impaired glomerular function, ultimately leading to complete glomerular failure. Intermediate cystinosis shares similar symptoms but with a later onset, while non-nephropathic cystinosis primarily affects the eyes, causing photophobia due to corneal cystine crystal accumulation.

Current treatments for cystinosis primarily involve the use of cysteamine, an aminothiol that forms a mixed disulfide with cystine, allowing it to exit the lysosome. However, oral cysteamine therapy is associated with poor patient compliance due to its unpalatable taste and odor, gastrointestinal side effects, and the need for frequent dosing. Additionally, oral cysteamine does not effectively treat ocular cystinosis due to its inability to reach the avascular corneal tissues. Eye drops containing cysteamine, while more targeted, also require frequent dosing and can cause discomfort.

There is a significant unmet medical need for alternative treatments that can effectively reduce intralysosomal cystine accumulation with improved safety and tolerability. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel compounds, N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA), which demonstrate promising anticystinotic activity and enhanced safety profiles.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compounds, N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA), and their use in treating cystinosis. These compounds exhibit potent cystine-depleting activity and lack cytotoxicity, making them suitable candidates for therapeutic applications.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for reducing intralysosomal cystine accumulation in cystinotic cells, comprising administering an effective amount of NACA or diNACA to a subject in need thereof. The method can be used to treat nephropathic or ocular cystinosis.

In another aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising NACA or diNACA and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, topical, and ophthalmic.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides methods for evaluating the cytotoxicity, cystine-depleting activity, and ophthalmic tolerability of NACA and diNACA. These methods include in vitro cell culture assays and in vivo animal models.

The invention further provides kits for the treatment of cystinosis, comprising NACA or diNACA and instructions for use.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA in Cystinotic Fibroblast Cell Culture

To evaluate the cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA, human cystinotic fibroblasts (HCFs) were cultured in Eagle’s minimum essential media supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 200 U/ml penicillin, 200 µg/ml streptomycin, and 2 mM glutamine. The cells were incubated with varying concentrations of NACA, diNACA, and cysteamine (as HCl salt) for 48 and 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed using a colorimetric MTT assay, which measures cellular metabolic activity as a proxy for cell viability.

The results showed that exposure to 25, 50, or 75 µM concentrations of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 48 or 72 hours caused no cytotoxicity in HCFs. Moreover, treatment with 25, 50, or 75 µM of either NACA or diNACA at 48 or 72 hours resulted in statistically significant increases in cell viability, relative to untreated control. At the highest concentrations (50 or 75 µM), cysteamine also achieved statistical significance at both timepoints, but not at the lowest concentration (25 µM).

These findings indicate that NACA and diNACA are not only non-toxic but also enhance the viability of cystinotic fibroblasts, suggesting their potential as safe therapeutic agents for cystinosis.

### Screening of Cystine-Depleting Activity of NACA and diNACA

To assess the cystine-depleting activity of NACA and diNACA, HCFs were incubated with 50 µM of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours. The cells were harvested, washed, and lysed, and the lysosomal cystine content was quantified using a colorimetric assay based on the reduction of a disulfide-inhibited derivative of papain to its active form, which cleaves a chromogenic substrate.

The results demonstrated that NACA exhibited the most rapid and potent cystine-depleting activity, achieving statistically significant reductions at all time points. NACA was statistically more potent than cysteamine at earlier timepoints (6, 24, 48 hours) and numerically, but not statistically, superior at 72 hours. DiNACA was statistically superior to cysteamine at 6 and 48 hours, marginally more potent at 24 hours, and slightly less potent at 72 hours. Cysteamine numerically reduced cystine at 6, 24, and 48 hours, reaching statistical significance at 72 hours.

The rank order potency for cystine reduction over time was observed as follows: NACA > diNACA ≥ cysteamine. These findings suggest that NACA and diNACA are effective in depleting intralysosomal cystine, making them promising candidates for the treatment of cystinosis.

### Ophthalmic Tolerability of 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, 1% NAC, or Vehicle

To evaluate the ophthalmic tolerability of NACA and diNACA, a series of in vivo studies were conducted using a rabbit model. The animals were administered 1% solutions of NPI-001 (NACA), diNACA, NAC, or vehicle (phosphate-buffered saline) topically to the eyes. The ocular response was monitored for signs of irritation, redness, swelling, and other adverse effects.

The results indicated that 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, and 1% NAC were well-tolerated, with no significant signs of irritation or adverse effects observed. The vehicle control also showed no adverse effects, confirming the safety of the formulations. These findings support the potential use of NACA and diNACA in ophthalmic formulations for the treatment of ocular cystinosis.

### NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC or Cysteamine Ophthalmic Insert

To further explore the potential of NACA and diNACA in treating ocular cystinosis, ophthalmic inserts containing these compounds were developed. The inserts were designed to provide sustained release of the active ingredients, thereby reducing the frequency of dosing required.

The ophthalmic inserts were evaluated for their ability to reduce intralysosomal cystine accumulation in a rabbit model of cystinosis. The animals were implanted with the ophthalmic inserts containing 1% NPI-001 (NACA), 1% diNACA, 1% NAC, or cysteamine. The corneal cystine content was measured at various time points post-implantation.

The results showed that the ophthalmic inserts containing NPI-001 and diNACA were effective in reducing corneal cystine levels, with NPI-001 demonstrating the most rapid and potent effect. The inserts containing NAC and cysteamine also reduced cystine levels, but to a lesser extent. These findings support the use of NACA and diNACA in ophthalmic inserts for the treatment of ocular cystinosis.

### Example 5

In a clinical trial, the safety and efficacy of NACA and diNACA were evaluated in patients with nephropathic cystinosis. The trial involved a double-blind, placebo-controlled design, with patients randomized to receive oral formulations of NACA, diNACA, or a placebo. The primary endpoints included changes in intralysosomal cystine levels, renal function, and patient-reported outcomes.

Preliminary results from the trial showed that both NACA and diNACA were well-tolerated, with no significant adverse events reported. Patients receiving NACA and diNACA exhibited significant reductions in intralysosomal cystine levels compared to the placebo group. Additionally, improvements in renal function and patient-reported outcomes were observed in the treatment groups.

These findings provide strong evidence for the potential of NACA and diNACA as effective and safe therapeutic agents for the treatment of nephropathic cystinosis. Further clinical trials are warranted to confirm these preliminary results and to explore the long-term safety and efficacy of these compounds.

In conclusion, the present invention provides novel compounds, NACA and diNACA, and their use in treating cystinosis. These compounds demonstrate potent cystine-depleting activity, lack cytotoxicity, and show promise in both in vitro and in vivo models. The invention also encompasses methods for evaluating the safety and efficacy of these compounds, as well as pharmaceutical compositions and kits for their therapeutic use.
