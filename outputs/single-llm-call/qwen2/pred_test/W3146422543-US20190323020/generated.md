## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, specifically to the production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) using recombinant fungi. More particularly, the invention provides methods and compositions for producing 3HP in recombinant ΔcadA fungi, which are capable of efficiently converting sugars to 3HP under acidic conditions.

## BACKGROUND

Since the industrial revolution, petroleum-based feedstocks have been the primary source for the production of fuels and chemicals. However, the non-renewable nature of these resources and the environmental impacts associated with their extraction and use have driven efforts to develop sustainable alternatives. A bioeconomy, based on the production of fuels and chemicals from renewable biological feedstocks, has been proposed as a means to improve the sustainability of industrial processes. To support this, extensive metabolic engineering of various microorganisms, including bacteria, plants, and fungi, has been undertaken to enable the production of a wide range of chemicals.

One such chemical of interest is 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP), which can be used to produce biobased and biodegradable plastics, such as poly-3HP, or more complex structures incorporating other monomers like 3-hydroxybutyric acid, 4-hydroxybutyric acid, and lactic acid. 3HP can also be converted to acrylic acid, a key component in the production of acrylates, and to acrylonitrile for the production of carbon fibers. 

Several microorganisms, including bacteria and yeasts, have been engineered to produce 3HP. However, these organisms may not be ideal hosts for the bioconversion of lignocellulosic and low-cost renewable feedstocks, which often contain a complex mixture of organic compounds. Additionally, the production of 3HP as a free acid, rather than its conjugate base, can facilitate downstream separation processes.

Filamentous fungi, particularly those from the genus Aspergillus, are known for their ability to produce organic acids at industrial scale and at low pH. This characteristic makes them attractive hosts for the production of 3HP. Aspergillus pseudoterreus, in particular, is notable for its acid tolerance, high glycolytic flux, and ability to utilize a broad range of carbon and nitrogen sources. Therefore, the present invention focuses on the use of recombinant Aspergillus pseudoterreus for the efficient production of 3HP.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) using recombinant ΔcadA fungi. Specifically, the invention involves the use of Aspergillus pseudoterreus strains engineered to overexpress a synthetic pathway for 3HP production, while simultaneously deleting the cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) gene to redirect metabolic flux away from itaconic acid production.

In one aspect, the invention provides recombinant ΔcadA fungi capable of producing 3HP. These fungi are engineered to express a synthetic pathway comprising the genes for aspartate-1-decarboxylase (pand), beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase (bapat), and 3-hydroxypropionic acid dehydrogenase (hpdh). The pathway is integrated into the genome at the cadA locus, and in some embodiments, additional copies of the pathway are integrated at other genomic loci to enhance 3HP production.

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for producing 3HP using the recombinant ΔcadA fungi. The methods involve culturing the fungi in a suitable medium under conditions that promote the production of 3HP, and subsequently harvesting the 3HP from the culture medium.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides compositions and kits for producing 3HP. These compositions include the recombinant ΔcadA fungi, as well as any necessary growth media, enzymes, and reagents required for the production and purification of 3HP.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides methods and compositions for producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3HP) using recombinant ΔcadA fungi, specifically Aspergillus pseudoterreus. The invention involves the genetic engineering of A. pseudoterreus to overexpress a synthetic pathway for 3HP production while deleting the cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) gene to redirect metabolic flux away from itaconic acid production. The engineered strains are capable of producing 3HP under acidic conditions, which facilitates downstream separation processes.

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi

The recombinant ΔcadA fungi of the present invention are engineered to produce 3HP by overexpressing a synthetic pathway comprising the genes for aspartate-1-decarboxylase (pand), beta-alanine pyruvate transaminase (bapat), and 3-hydroxypropionic acid dehydrogenase (hpdh). The pathway is integrated into the genome at the cadA locus, and in some embodiments, additional copies of the pathway are integrated at other genomic loci to enhance 3HP production.

The deletion of the cadA gene is crucial for redirecting metabolic flux away from itaconic acid production, which is a major by-product in wild-type A. pseudoterreus. By eliminating itaconic acid production, the engineered strains can more efficiently convert sugars to 3HP.

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid

The methods of producing 3HP using the recombinant ΔcadA fungi involve culturing the fungi in a suitable medium under conditions that promote the production of 3HP. The medium typically contains a carbon source, such as glucose, and a nitrogen source, such as ammonium sulfate. The pH of the medium is maintained at a low level (e.g., pH 2.8) to favor the production of 3HP in its free acid form.

The culture is typically performed in a bioreactor, such as a stirred-tank bioreactor, to ensure optimal aeration and mixing. The temperature is maintained at around 30°C, and the agitation rate is adjusted to maintain the desired pH and dissolved oxygen levels. The culture is monitored for the production of 3HP, and the 3HP is harvested from the culture medium once the desired titer is achieved.

### Methods of Producing 3-HP

The methods of producing 3HP using the recombinant ΔcadA fungi involve several steps:

1. **Strain Construction**: The A. pseudoterreus strain is genetically engineered to delete the cadA gene and to overexpress the synthetic pathway for 3HP production. The pathway genes (pand, bapat, and hpdh) are codon-optimized for expression in A. pseudoterreus and integrated into the genome at the cadA locus. Additional copies of the pathway may be integrated at other genomic loci to enhance 3HP production.

2. **Culture Conditions**: The engineered strains are cultured in a suitable medium containing a carbon source (e.g., glucose) and a nitrogen source (e.g., ammonium sulfate). The pH of the medium is maintained at a low level (e.g., pH 2.8) to favor the production of 3HP in its free acid form. The culture is typically performed in a bioreactor to ensure optimal aeration and mixing.

3. **Monitoring and Harvesting**: The culture is monitored for the production of 3HP using techniques such as high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) or gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Once the desired titer of 3HP is achieved, the 3HP is harvested from the culture medium.

### Compositions and Kits

The compositions and kits of the present invention include the recombinant ΔcadA fungi, as well as any necessary growth media, enzymes, and reagents required for the production and purification of 3HP. The kits may also include instructions for the use of the fungi and the production of 3HP.

### Example 1

**Strain Construction and Characterization**

The A. pseudoterreus strain ATCC® 32359TM was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection. The strain was maintained on complete medium agar (CM) and spore suspensions were stored in 15% glycerol at -80°C. Spore inoculum was grown on CM agar plates for 5 days to produce conidia, which were harvested by washing the plates with sterile 0.4% Tween 80 solution.

The coding sequences for TcPAND, BcBAPAT, and EcHPDH were codon-optimized for A. pseudoterreus and synthesized. The genes were assembled into the pBlueScript SK(–) vector using Gibson Assembly and linearized with restriction enzymes HindIII and PstI to form pZD4025. The plasmid was then linearized with EcoRV and transformed into A. pseudoterreus using chemical-mediated protoplast transformation to construct the strains ABF_004528_2 (cad:3HP+) and ABF_004528_6 (cad:3HP+, 3HP+).

The strains were characterized by multi-omics analysis, including proteomics and metabolomics, to evaluate the impact of the engineered pathway on the metabolism of A. pseudoterreus. The results showed that the engineered strains were capable of producing 3HP, with the strain ABF_004528_6 producing nearly twice the yield of 3HP compared to the strain ABF_004528_2.

### Example 2

**Scale-Up of 3HP Production in Bioreactors**

The highest-producing 3HP strain from shake-flask experiments (ABF_004528_6) was scaled up to a 30 L bioreactor. The culture was monitored for off-gas, biomass, and targeted extracellular metabolite concentrations to provide boundary constraints for parsimonious flux balance analysis (pFBA).

During bioreactor cultivation, biomass and CO2 were the main products during the first 2 days of cultivation, with CO2 production peaking at hour 44. Production of 3HP and glycerol began early during the exponential growth phase of the culture and prior to peak CO2 production. In contrast, production of organic acids (aconitic acid, isocitric acid, citric acid, alpha-ketoglutaric acid, and malic acid) began later when biomass production had slowed and become a near-linear process.

The production of extracellular 3HP in bioreactor conditions reached a maximum titer of 0.69 g/L at hour 67 and then declined steadily to a final titer of 0.17 g/L. Follow-up experiments demonstrated that 3HP is stable in acidic culture conditions and degraded biotically. The decline in 3HP production suggested the activation of a competing degradation pathway at the onset of organic acid production.

### Example 3

**Deletion of Malonate Semialdehyde Dehydrogenase (Apald6)**

To improve 3HP yield, a putative malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6) was identified and deleted in the highest-producing 3HP strain (ABF_004528_6). The resulting strain, ABF_008339 (cad:3HP+, 3HP+; ΔApald6), was evaluated in shake-flask experiments.

The yield of 3HP was improved by 3.4-fold in the Apald6 mutant compared to the parent strain. The strain was then scaled up to 0.5 L bioreactors to re-evaluate 3HP production. By day seven, the parent strain had accumulated 0.27 ± 0.10 g/L 3HP, while the yield was improved by 3.3-fold to 0.88 ± 0.11 g/L 3HP in the Apald6 mutant.

### Example 4

**Optimization of 3HP Production in Bioreactors**

The ΔApald6 and parent strains were further optimized for 3HP production in 0.5 L bioreactors. The culture conditions were adjusted to improve aeration and pH control. The rate of CO2 evolution was slower, and higher levels of glycerol and erythritol were accumulated. The major product during cultivation in the 20 L bioreactor, aconitic acid, was not observed in the 0.5 L bioreactors, supporting the hypothesis that a shift to organic acid production coincides with the loss of 3HP.

The results demonstrated that the deletion of Apald6 significantly improved 3HP yield, and the optimized culture conditions further enhanced the production of 3HP. The ΔApald6 strain produced 0.88 ± 0.11 g/L 3HP by day seven, indicating the potential for large-scale production of 3HP using the recombinant ΔcadA fungi.
