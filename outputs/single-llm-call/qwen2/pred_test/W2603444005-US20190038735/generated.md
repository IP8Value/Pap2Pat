## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to a virus-like particle (VLP) vaccine designed to target the accessory gene regulator (agr) type I quorum sensing system of Staphylococcus aureus. The vaccine is intended to elicit antibodies that neutralize the autoinducing peptide (AIP1) and thereby inhibit the expression of agr-regulated virulence factors, reducing the pathogenicity of S. aureus infections.

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION

Staphylococcus aureus is a leading cause of skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs), with a significant proportion of these infections being caused by methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA). The increasing prevalence of antibiotic-resistant strains of S. aureus has highlighted the need for alternative approaches to combat these infections, particularly those involving the development of vaccines. The accessory gene regulator (agr) system plays a crucial role in the virulence of S. aureus by regulating the expression of numerous virulence factors through a quorum sensing mechanism. The agr system is activated by the accumulation of autoinducing peptides (AIPs), which bind to the AgrC receptor, leading to the production of RNAIII and the subsequent upregulation of virulence genes.

Despite the importance of the agr system in S. aureus pathogenesis, there has been limited success in developing vaccines that target this pathway. Traditional approaches, such as the use of chemically cross-linked AIPs, have failed to provide adequate protection against S. aureus infections. The present invention addresses this gap by utilizing a virus-like particle (VLP) platform to present a modified AIP1 sequence (AIP1S) in a conformationally restricted manner. This approach aims to elicit a robust immune response that can neutralize native AIP1 and inhibit agr signaling, thereby reducing the virulence of S. aureus infections.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a virus-like particle (VLP) vaccine that targets the accessory gene regulator (agr) type I quorum sensing system of Staphylococcus aureus. The vaccine is designed to elicit antibodies against the autoinducing peptide (AIP1) by presenting a modified AIP1 sequence (AIP1S) on the surface of VLPs derived from the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage PP7. The modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM) is inserted into the AB-loop of the PP7 coat protein, ensuring conformational restriction and strong immunogenicity. The resulting VLPs, designated PP7-AIP1S, are capable of inducing high-titer, high-affinity antibodies that recognize and neutralize native AIP1, leading to the inhibition of agr signaling and the reduction of S. aureus virulence.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Production of Virus-Like Particles

The present invention involves the production of virus-like particles (VLPs) that present a modified AIP1 sequence (AIP1S) on their surface. The VLPs are derived from the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage PP7, which self-assembles from 180 coat protein monomers arranged as 90 dimers. The AB-loop of the PP7 coat protein is an immuno-prominent surface loop that can be genetically modified to display foreign epitopes.

To produce the PP7-AIP1S VLPs, the modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM) is inserted into the second AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain dimer. The modified sequence includes a cysteine-to-serine mutation at position 4 to avoid potential intermolecular disulfide bond formation, which could negatively impact VLP purification and immune presentation. The resulting construct is cloned into a plasmid under the control of the T7 promoter and expressed in Escherichia coli. The expressed PP7-AIP1S proteins self-assemble into VLPs, which are purified using size exclusion chromatography and verified for purity and homogeneity by SDS-PAGE, agarose gel electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering analysis.

### Efficacy of PP7-AIP1S Vaccination

The efficacy of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine was evaluated in a murine model of S. aureus skin infection using the highly virulent USA300 LAC strain. Mice were vaccinated twice with 10 µg of PP7-AIP1S VLPs, and the immune response was assessed by measuring the ability of serum antibodies to recognize AIP1. Competitive ELISA assays demonstrated that the antibodies induced by PP7-AIP1S vaccination specifically recognized native AIP1 and not AIP2, indicating the specificity of the immune response.

In the challenge studies, PP7-AIP1S vaccinated mice showed reduced abscess formation, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden compared to control groups. Additionally, the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, and IL-17) were significantly lower in the vaccinated mice, suggesting a reduction in agr-mediated virulence. The efficacy of the vaccine was further confirmed by measuring the expression of RNAIII and the production of alpha-hemolysin (Hla) at the site of infection. Both RNAIII transcription and Hla levels were significantly reduced in PP7-AIP1S vaccinated mice, indicating the successful inhibition of agr signaling.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Production and Characterization of PP7-AIP1S VLPs

The modified AIP1 sequence (YSTSDFIM) was inserted into the second AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain dimer using standard molecular biology techniques. The resulting construct was cloned into the pDSP7K plasmid and expressed in E. coli C41 cells. The expressed PP7-AIP1S proteins were purified by size exclusion chromatography and characterized by SDS-PAGE, agarose gel electrophoresis, and dynamic light scattering analysis. The purified PP7-AIP1S VLPs were homogeneous and displayed the AIP1S sequence on their surface.

### Example 2: Induction of Anti-AIP1 Antibodies by PP7-AIP1S Vaccination

BALB/c mice were vaccinated twice with 10 µg of PP7-AIP1S VLPs, and serum samples were collected at various time points post-vaccination. ELISA assays were performed to measure the binding of serum antibodies to PP7-AIP1S VLPs and to assess the ability of these antibodies to recognize native AIP1. Competitive ELISA assays were conducted to confirm the specificity of the immune response. The results demonstrated that PP7-AIP1S vaccination induced high-titer, high-affinity antibodies that specifically recognized native AIP1.

### Example 3: Efficacy of PP7-AIP1S Vaccination in a Murine Model of S. aureus Skin Infection

BALB/c mice were vaccinated twice with 10 µg of PP7-AIP1S VLPs and challenged with the USA300 LAC strain of S. aureus. The severity of infection was assessed by measuring abscess formation, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden. The levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, and IL-17) were also measured in the abscess tissue homogenate. The results showed that PP7-AIP1S vaccinated mice had significantly reduced abscess formation, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden compared to control groups. Additionally, the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines were significantly lower in the vaccinated mice, indicating a reduction in agr-mediated virulence.

### Additional Examples

### Example 4: Inhibition of Agr Signaling by PP7-AIP1S Vaccination

RNA was isolated from the abscess tissue of vaccinated and control mice, and the expression of RNAIII was measured by quantitative PCR (qPCR). The results showed that RNAIII transcription was significantly reduced in PP7-AIP1S vaccinated mice compared to control groups, confirming the inhibition of agr signaling.

### Example 5: Reduction of Alpha-Hemolysin Production by PP7-AIP1S Vaccination

The levels of alpha-hemolysin (Hla) in the abscess tissue homogenate were measured by Western blot analysis. The results showed that Hla production was significantly reduced in PP7-AIP1S vaccinated mice compared to control groups, further supporting the inhibition of agr-mediated virulence.

## DISCUSSION

The present invention demonstrates the potential of a VLP-based vaccine to target the agr type I quorum sensing system of Staphylococcus aureus. The PP7-AIP1S vaccine elicits a robust immune response that neutralizes native AIP1 and inhibits agr signaling, leading to a reduction in S. aureus virulence. The efficacy of the vaccine was confirmed in a murine model of S. aureus skin infection, where vaccinated mice showed reduced abscess formation, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden. Additionally, the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the production of alpha-hemolysin were significantly lower in vaccinated mice, indicating the successful inhibition of agr-mediated virulence.

The use of VLPs as a vaccine platform offers several advantages, including strong immunogenicity, the ability to present conformationally restricted epitopes, and the potential for multivalent presentation. These properties make VLPs a promising tool for the development of vaccines against bacterial pathogens, particularly those that utilize quorum sensing systems for virulence regulation. The success of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine in targeting agr type I S. aureus suggests that similar approaches could be applied to other pathogens that use similar mechanisms for virulence regulation.

The methods described herein include the production and characterization of PP7-AIP1S VLPs, the evaluation of the immune response induced by PP7-AIP1S vaccination, and the assessment of the efficacy of the vaccine in a murine model of S. aureus skin infection. The methods involve molecular biology techniques for the construction of the PP7-AIP1S plasmid, expression and purification of the VLPs, and immunological assays to measure the immune response and the inhibition of agr signaling. The results demonstrate the potential of the PP7-AIP1S vaccine to reduce the virulence of S. aureus infections and highlight the broader applicability of VLP-based vaccines in targeting bacterial virulence regulation.
