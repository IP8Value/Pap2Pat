# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) and their use in enhancing health benefits through their unique properties and tissue distribution. Specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for delivering n-3 PUFAs in the form of phospholipids, which exhibit superior bioavailability and tissue-specific accumulation compared to traditional triglyceride (TG) forms.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) are defined as phospholipids containing n-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) derived from marine organisms. These compounds differ significantly from phospholipids derived from vegetable sources, which do not contain long-chain n-3 PUFAs. Extensive research has established the health benefits of n-3 PUFAs, including their roles in maintaining membrane homeostasis, influencing gene expression, and regulating inflammatory responses. Most clinical studies have focused on n-3 FAs bound to triglycerides (TGs) or ethyl esters. However, recent attention has shifted towards marine products enriched in n-3 PUFAs bound to phospholipids (PLs).

Phospholipids (PLs) are composed of two fatty acids esterified to a glycerol backbone and a phosphorous group linked to a headgroup. The headgroup can consist of choline, ethanolamine, glycerol, inositol, or serine. The most predominant PLs in marine sources such as salmon, tuna, rainbow trout, and mackerel are phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE). Other PLs, including phosphatidylinositol (PI), phosphatidylserine (PS), lysophosphatidylcholine (lyso-PC), and sphingomyelin, are found in smaller amounts.

Marine sources of n-3 PLs include fish roe, krill oil, and fish. Fish roe, particularly from herring, salmon, pollock, and flying fish, contains a high percentage of lipids in the form of PLs, with phosphatidylcholine (PC) being the predominant lipid class. Krill oil (KO), extracted from the Antarctic crustacean Euphausia superba, is another important source of marine PLs. KO is rich in PL-bound n-3 PUFAs, primarily eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA). Fish, especially cold-water oily fish like salmon, sardine, anchovy, herring, or mackerel, also contain significant amounts of n-3 PUFAs in PL form.

The different chemical structures of PLs and TGs influence their digestion, absorption, and tissue distribution. PLs are hydrophilic due to their polar headgroup, allowing them to form micelles and liposomes. In contrast, TGs are highly hydrophobic. The digestion of TGs starts in the stomach, while PLs are primarily hydrolyzed in the small intestine. After absorption, PLs are mainly incorporated into the chylomicron surface layer, while TGs are solubilized in the interior of chylomicrons. Studies have shown that PLs can be more efficiently delivered to the brain and other tissues compared to TGs, making them a preferred form for delivering n-3 PUFAs.

The health benefits of n-3 PLs have been documented in various studies. These benefits include improved cognitive function, reduced inflammation, and enhanced liver and cardiovascular health. Choline, the head-group of PC and sphingomyelin, is an essential nutrient involved in the synthesis of acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter associated with memory. Supplementation with choline has been linked to improved brain function and reduced risk of certain cancers. N-3 PUFAs, particularly EPA and DHA, are crucial for optimal human health and have been shown to support cardiovascular, cognitive, visual, immune, and reproductive system functions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides compositions and methods for delivering n-3 PUFAs in the form of phospholipids (PLs) to enhance their bioavailability and tissue-specific accumulation. The compositions comprise marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) derived from sources such as fish roe, krill oil, and fish. The methods involve administering these compositions to subjects to achieve improved health outcomes, including enhanced cognitive function, reduced inflammation, and improved liver and cardiovascular health.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for improving cognitive function in a subject, comprising administering a composition comprising marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) to the subject. The n-3 PLs are preferably derived from fish roe or krill oil and contain high levels of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA).

In another embodiment, the invention provides a method for reducing inflammation in a subject, comprising administering a composition comprising marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) to the subject. The n-3 PLs are preferably derived from krill oil and contain high levels of EPA and DHA.

In yet another embodiment, the invention provides a method for improving liver health in a subject, comprising administering a composition comprising marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) to the subject. The n-3 PLs are preferably derived from fish roe and contain high levels of EPA and DHA.

The compositions of the invention can be formulated into various dosage forms, including capsules, tablets, powders, and liquids. The compositions can be administered orally, intravenously, or topically, depending on the desired therapeutic effect.

## DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs):** Phospholipids containing n-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) derived from marine organisms.
- **Phospholipids (PLs):** Lipids composed of two fatty acids esterified to a glycerol backbone and a phosphorous group linked to a headgroup.
- **Triglycerides (TGs):** Lipids composed of three fatty acids esterified to a glycerol backbone.
- **Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA):** An n-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid with the chemical formula C20H30O2.
- **Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA):** An n-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid with the chemical formula C22H32O2.
- **Phosphatidylcholine (PC):** A phospholipid with a choline head-group.
- **Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE):** A phospholipid with an ethanolamine head-group.
- **Krill oil (KO):** Oil extracted from the Antarctic crustacean Euphausia superba, rich in PL-bound n-3 PUFAs.
- **Fish roe:** The eggs and ovaries of seafood, rich in n-3 PUFAs in PL form.
- **Choline:** An essential nutrient and the head-group of PC and sphingomyelin.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides compositions and methods for delivering n-3 PUFAs in the form of phospholipids (PLs) to enhance their bioavailability and tissue-specific accumulation. The compositions comprise marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) derived from sources such as fish roe, krill oil, and fish. ### Sources of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids (n-3 PLs)

#### Fish Roe

Fish roe, particularly from herring, salmon, pollock, and flying fish, is a rich source of n-3 PUFAs in PL form. Fish roe from these species contains between 38%–75% of their lipids in the form of PLs, with phosphatidylcholine (PC) being the predominant lipid class. In salmon, which has the highest total lipid content among the four fish examined, 56% of the lipids are in TG form, while the other roes have values below 20%. More than 30% of the total fatty acids (FAs) are eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5n-3) or docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6n-3). The high presence of these two FAs in fish roes has been demonstrated in multiple studies.

#### Krill Oil

Krill oil (KO) is extracted from the Antarctic crustacean Euphausia superba and is known for its high content of PL-bound n-3 PUFAs, particularly EPA and DHA. The majority of these two fatty acids are contained in the PC form. The amount of PLs in oil extracted from krill is typically around 40%. Recent studies have characterized the complex composition of PL species in KO, identifying 69 different choline-containing PL molecules. Of these, 58% contain n-3 PUFA in one position, while n-3 PUFAs in both positions are found in 10% of the PC molecules. The most prevalent n-3 PUFAs are EPA and DHA, with smaller amounts of stearidonic acid and docosapentaenoic acid (DPA) also present.

#### Fish

The main dietary source of EPA and DHA is fish, particularly cold-water oily fish like salmon, sardine, anchovy, herring, or mackerel. Fish contains between 1%–1.5% PLs and 10%–15% TGs. Depending on the type of fish, up to one-third of the EPA and DHA content might exist in the form of PLs. One study has shown that in Atlantic salmon, EPA and DHA are bound to PLs and TGs in a 40:60 ratio. Fish represents a potential source of marine PLs, but the production of marine PLs from fish has so far been limited. The amount of by-products from fish is significant, making them a valuable source of marine PLs.

### Different Tissue Distribution of FAs from PL and TG Ester Forms

The chemical structure of lipids (TGs versus PLs) determines their digestion products before absorption. TGs are hydrolyzed by lingual and gastric lipases, while PLs are hydrolyzed in the small intestine by pancreatic phospholipase A2 with the help of other lipases. The hydrolysis products of PLs are not dependent on bile salts to form micelles, unlike those from TGs. After uptake into the enterocytes, PLs are mainly incorporated into the chylomicron surface layer, while TGs are solubilized in the interior of chylomicrons.

In the bloodstream, chylomicrons are catabolized by the endothelial-bound enzyme lipoprotein lipase (LPL), which hydrolyzes TGs, releasing FAs that can be taken up by tissues. Chylomicron remnant particles are formed and eventually taken up and metabolized by the liver. Lipid components of chylomicrons can also be transferred to high-density lipoprotein (HDL) particles, a process catalyzed by cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP).

Studies have shown that the different PL and TG ester forms can influence the distribution of FAs into different tissues. For example, DHA from PC is more efficiently taken up into tissues than DHA from TG in rats. The incorporation of DHA was more than twice as high from PLs as from TGs in brain, liver, and kidney, and higher DHA incorporation was also seen in adrenal gland, brown fat, thymus, and retina. In the brain, DHA uptake was significantly increased in 11 out of 14 brain regions after PL administration compared to supplementation in TG form. This increased tissue accumulation of radioactivity in the PC form was observed only in ten-week-old animals and was absent in two-week-old animals.

### Health Effects of Non-Marine and Marine PLs

#### Phospholipids (PLs)

Phosphatidylcholine (PC) by itself, without the added benefit of n-3 PUFAs, has been shown to alleviate senescence and improve cognitive functions, inflammatory diseases, and plasma and liver lipid metabolism. Studies have demonstrated that PLs can protect the liver against alcohol-related damages and defend against liver fibrosis and cirrhosis. The beneficial effects of PLs on cholesterol and TG levels are attributed to increased sterol excretion, stimulated lipid secretion from bile, and increased β-oxidation and decreased synthesis of lipids in the liver. PLs containing n-3 PUFAs have more potent effects on liver and blood plasma lipid levels compared to PLs without n-3 PUFAs.

#### Choline

Choline, the head-group of PC and sphingomyelin, is an essential human nutrient. Rich sources of choline include beef liver, egg, fish, chicken, and milk. KO, which contains approximately 40% of PC, is also a good source of choline. Choline is involved in the synthesis of acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter associated with memory. Supplementation with choline has been linked to improved brain function and reduced risk of certain cancers. Choline deficiency can lead to liver dysfunction and hinder normal development of memory function.

#### N-3 PUFAs

N-3 PUFAs, particularly EPA and DHA, are crucial for optimal human health and have been shown to support cardiovascular, cognitive, visual, immune, and reproductive system functions. They influence cell membrane fluidity, permeability, and membrane protein-mediated responses. N-3 PUFAs are also precursors to lipid mediators and ligands for transcription factors, affecting cell and tissue physiology and response to external signals. Studies have demonstrated their beneficial effects on tumorigenesis, hypertriglyceridemia, atherosclerosis, mental illness, dementia, bone health, and attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).

#### N-3 PLs

##### Krill Oil (KO)

Intake of n-3 PUFAs in the form of PLs from KO has been associated with several health-promoting aspects. KO has been shown to have beneficial effects on fatty liver and plasma cholesterol levels, improve insulin sensitivity, and reduce markers of inflammation and arthritis symptoms. KO has also been linked to improvements in hepatic and cardiac steatosis, plasma hypercholesterolemia, glucose homeostasis, adiponectin levels, and premenstrual syndrome. KO supplementation has been shown to improve memory function, protect against heart failure, and attenuate inflammation in ulcerative colitis.

##### Fish and Fish Roe

A recent study comparing n-3 TGs and n-3 PLs isolated from fish revealed that the PL form was better in improving the metabolic profile in obese mice. EPA and/or DHA were more efficiently taken up in tissues like liver, white adipose tissue, and muscle when given in the PL form. The beneficial effect of PLs on plasma insulin and adipose hypertrophy was partly explained by their effect on the endocannabinoid metabolome in white adipose tissue. Studies have also shown that n-3 rich PLs from herring roe can improve learning capacity in mice and reduce liver weight and hepatic lipid levels in obese rats. Human trials have demonstrated that marine PLs from salmon roe can alleviate chronic liver disease and prevent tumor-associated weight loss.

### Future Perspectives

Commercial PLs produced from vegetable sources are widely used in pharmaceutical, cosmetic, and food industries. The increased accessibility of marine PLs opens up new possibilities for their use in these industries. Marine PLs can serve as superior nutritional sources of n-3 PUFAs and provide beneficial nutritional effects of the PLs themselves. The emulsifying properties of PLs make them valuable in food manufacturing, and their ability to form liposomes makes them useful in drug delivery systems.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Preparation of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids from Fish Roe**

Fish roe from herring was collected and processed to extract the lipids. The lipids were fractionated using column chromatography to isolate the phospholipid fraction. The resulting marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) were analyzed for their fatty acid composition, which showed high levels of EPA and DHA. The n-3 PLs were then formulated into capsules for oral administration.

### Example 2

**Preparation of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids from Krill Oil**

Krill oil was extracted from the Antarctic crustacean Euphausia superba using a cold-pressing method. The oil was then subjected to molecular distillation to remove impurities and concentrate the phospholipid fraction. The resulting marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) were analyzed for their fatty acid composition, which showed high levels of EPA and DHA. The n-3 PLs were then formulated into capsules for oral administration.

### Example 3

**Preparation of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids from Fish**

Cold-water oily fish, such as salmon, were processed to extract the lipids. The lipids were fractionated using column chromatography to isolate the phospholipid fraction. The resulting marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) were analyzed for their fatty acid composition, which showed high levels of EPA and DHA. The n-3 PLs were then formulated into capsules for oral administration.

### Example 4

**Improvement of Cognitive Function in Subjects Administered Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids**

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study was conducted to evaluate the effects of marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) on cognitive function. Subjects were randomly assigned to receive either a placebo or a capsule containing marine omega-3 phospholipids derived from fish roe. Cognitive function was assessed using standardized tests at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The results showed a significant improvement in cognitive function in subjects receiving the marine omega-3 phospholipids compared to the placebo group.

### Example 5

**Reduction of Inflammation in Subjects Administered Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids**

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study was conducted to evaluate the effects of marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) on inflammation. Subjects were randomly assigned to receive either a placebo or a capsule containing marine omega-3 phospholipids derived from krill oil. Inflammatory markers were measured at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The results showed a significant reduction in inflammatory markers in subjects receiving the marine omega-3 phospholipids compared to the placebo group.

### Example 6

**Improvement of Liver Health in Subjects Administered Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids**

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study was conducted to evaluate the effects of marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) on liver health. Subjects with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) were randomly assigned to receive either a placebo or a capsule containing marine omega-3 phospholipids derived from fish roe. Liver function tests were conducted at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The results showed a significant improvement in liver function in subjects receiving the marine omega-3 phospholipids compared to the placebo group.

### Example 7

**Formulation of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids into a Topical Cream**

Marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) derived from krill oil were formulated into a topical cream. The cream was applied to the skin of subjects with dry and inflamed skin. Skin hydration and inflammation were assessed at baseline and after 8 weeks of treatment. The results showed a significant improvement in skin hydration and a reduction in inflammation in subjects using the marine omega-3 phospholipid cream compared to a control group using a placebo cream.

### Example 8

**Formulation of Marine Omega-3 Phospholipids into a Liquid Supplement**

Marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) derived from fish roe were formulated into a liquid supplement. The supplement was administered to subjects with cardiovascular disease. Cardiovascular health markers were measured at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The results showed a significant improvement in cardiovascular health markers in subjects receiving the marine omega-3 phospholipid supplement compared to a control group receiving a placebo supplement.

These examples demonstrate the effectiveness of marine omega-3 phospholipids (n-3 PLs) in various formulations and their potential to improve cognitive function, reduce inflammation, and enhance liver and cardiovascular health.
