# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of flexible and stretchable electronics, particularly to methods and systems for fabricating adhesive-integrated flexible and stretchable electrical and mechanical sensors. More specifically, the invention provides a simplified microfabrication approach that reduces the number of processing steps required to produce such sensors, thereby enhancing yield and enabling large-scale production.

## BACKGROUND

Flexible and stretchable electronics systems, comprised of sensors and circuitry, are increasingly relevant to the future of industrial and consumer electronics devices. The evolution from bulky and rigid electronics to thin and unobtrusive counterparts has necessitated innovative techniques beyond standard CMOS microfabrication. Screen-printing techniques have achieved low-cost and scalable processing of flexible sensors and systems, but some devices lack the potential for fully integrated electronics with ultra-thin profiles. Engineering of thin film nanocomposites is another example of this trend in miniaturizing everyday electronic devices. Within this class, electronics systems utilizing sacrificial layers (e.g., poly(acrylic acid) (PAA), poly(sodium 4-styrene sulfonate) (PSSNa), poly(n-vinylpyrrolidone) (PVP), poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA), water soluble tape, Silicon (Si), SiO2) and intermediate substrates (e.g., polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), water soluble tapes) for transfer printing have allowed for nanomembranes with mechanically-tuned properties. For example, epidermal electronics systems boast ultra-thin, high-resolution, "skin-like" sensors and circuitry designed for conformal lamination onto the skin. These systems excel in intimate integration with contoured and elastic real estates, as compared to traditionally rigid or thin film sheet devices that are otherwise too inflexible and/or unstretchable. The result is robust skin-electrode contact yielding prolonged biosignal acquisition with reduced motion artifact.

However, the fabrication of these sensors often involves complex and time-consuming processes, including the use of sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer printing steps. These methods require precise control and can lead to low yields and high costs, which are barriers to widespread adoption, especially in the context of the Internet of Things (IoT) and wearable devices. There is a growing demand for simpler, more efficient fabrication methods that can reduce the number of steps, enhance yield, and enable large-scale production of flexible and stretchable sensors.

## SUMMARY

The present invention addresses the aforementioned challenges by providing a novel microfabrication approach that significantly reduces the number of processing steps required to produce adhesive-integrated flexible and stretchable electrical and mechanical sensors. This approach, referred to as the Adhesive-Peeling (AP) method, eliminates the need for sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer printing steps, thereby simplifying the fabrication process and enhancing yield.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for fabricating an adhesive-integrated flexible and stretchable sensor. The method includes the following steps:
1. Providing a weakly-adhering donor substrate.
2. Depositing a conductive layer on the weakly-adhering donor substrate.
3. Forming a polymer backing on the conductive layer.
4. Etching the conductive layer to form a patterned sensor.
5. Peeling the patterned sensor from the weakly-adhering donor substrate using an adhesive.

The weakly-adhering donor substrate is typically a silicon wafer coated with a thin silicone layer, which facilitates the easy removal of the patterned sensor. The conductive layer is typically a thin film of gold (Au) and chromium (Cr), with the Au layer interfacing with the weakly-adhering donor and the Cr layer exposed at the top. The polymer backing is formed using a photodefinable polyimide (PPI) for simultaneous formation and patterning of the sensor.

The AP method allows for the direct integration of the sensor from the donor substrate to a target receiving adhesive, bypassing the need for solvents and intermediate stamps. This results in a significant reduction in processing time and complexity, making the method suitable for large-scale production.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a flexible and stretchable sensor fabricated using the AP method. The sensor includes a patterned conductive layer embedded within an adhesive substrate, wherein the conductive layer is formed by a thin film of gold and chromium, and the adhesive substrate is capable of conforming to various surfaces.

The invention also encompasses the use of the AP method for fabricating non-electrical sensors, such as mechanical strain sensors, and the adaptation of the method for use with flexible donor substrates, enabling roll-to-roll post-processing.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Exemplary Embodiments

The present invention provides a novel microfabrication approach for producing adhesive-integrated flexible and stretchable electrical and mechanical sensors. The approach, referred to as the Adhesive-Peeling (AP) method, significantly reduces the number of processing steps required to fabricate these sensors, thereby enhancing yield and enabling large-scale production.

#### Fabrication Process

1. **Providing a Weakly-Adhering Donor Substrate:**
   The process begins with the provision of a weakly-adhering donor substrate. In one embodiment, a standard silicon (Si) wafer is coated with a thin silicone layer to create a weakly-adhering interface. This interface facilitates the easy removal of the patterned sensor during the final peel-off step.

2. **Depositing a Conductive Layer:**
   A conductive layer is deposited on the weakly-adhering donor substrate. In one embodiment, the conductive layer is a thin film of gold (Au) and chromium (Cr). The Au thin film interfaces with the weakly-adhering donor substrate, while the Cr layer is exposed at the top. The deposition of the conductive layer is typically achieved using standard cleanroom techniques, such as sputtering or e-beam evaporation.

3. **Forming a Polymer Backing:**
   A polymer backing is formed on the conductive layer. In one embodiment, a photodefinable polyimide (PPI) is used for simultaneous formation and patterning of the sensor's polymer backing. The PPI is applied and patterned using standard photolithography techniques, resulting in a polymer backing that supports the conductive layer and forms the desired sensor shape.

4. **Etching the Conductive Layer:**
   The conductive layer is etched to form a patterned sensor. In one embodiment, an etch-back process is used to remove the exposed Cr-Au regions, leaving behind the desired pattern. The etching process is carefully controlled to ensure that the pattern is accurately formed and that the conductive layer remains intact.

5. **Peeling the Patterned Sensor:**
   The patterned sensor is peeled from the weakly-adhering donor substrate using an adhesive. In one embodiment, the adhesive is a pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA) that can be applied to the donor substrate and then peeled off, carrying the patterned sensor with it. The peel-off process is simple and straightforward, requiring minimal force and no additional processing steps.

#### Advantages of the AP Method

The AP method offers several advantages over traditional transfer printing (TP) methods:

- **Reduced Processing Steps:** The AP method eliminates the need for sacrificial layers and intermediate transfer printing steps, reducing the number of processing steps by approximately 50%. This simplification of the process enhances yield and reduces the risk of defects.
- **Higher Yield:** The AP method is less prone to deformation and damage during the fabrication process, leading to higher yields. The use of a weakly-adhering donor substrate and the direct peel-off process minimize the risk of sensor failure.
- **Ease of Use:** The AP method is straightforward and can be easily adapted for large-scale production. The peel-off process is simple and can be automated, making it suitable for roll-to-roll post-processing.
- **Versatility:** The AP method can be used to fabricate a wide range of sensors, including electrical and mechanical sensors. The method is also adaptable to different donor substrates, including flexible substrates, enabling the production of sensors for various applications.

### Additional Exemplary Embodiments

#### Non-Electrical Sensors

The AP method is not limited to the fabrication of electrical sensors. In one embodiment, the method is used to fabricate mechanical strain sensors. The process is similar to that described for electrical sensors, with the pattern used during photolithography being replaced by the desired pattern to produce a variety of functional strain sensors with tailored gauge factors. The axial strain-resistance performance of these sensors can be characterized, and their gauge factors calculated.

#### Flexible Donor Substrates

The AP method can also be adapted for use with flexible donor substrates, enabling roll-to-roll post-processing. In one embodiment, a flexible Kapton film is used as the donor substrate. The fabrication process is the same as described for rigid donor substrates, with the added advantage of flexibility. The peel-off process from the flexible donor substrate is demonstrated to be effective, and the resulting sensors show no apparent differences compared to their analogs from rigid donor substrates. The functionality of the sensors is confirmed through the acquisition of biopotential signals, such as EKG, using wired AP sensors.

#### Integration with Miniaturized Circuits

The AP method can be extended to integrate flexible and stretchable sensors with miniaturized back-end transmission and processing circuits. In one embodiment, the method is used to fabricate integrated systems of AP sensors with miniaturized rigid electronic circuits. The integration of active components and passive interconnections can be achieved using the present approach, enabling the production of more complex and versatile sensor systems.

#### Characterization of Interfacial Adhesion

The interfacial adhesion between the PDMS elastomeric substrate and the thin Au films is a critical aspect of the AP method. In one embodiment, the adhesion force required for successful peel-off is characterized, and the range of forces necessary for successful peel-off is determined. This information can be used to optimize the fabrication process and tailor the adhesion properties of the PDMS-sensor interface for specific applications.

#### Use of Different Metals

The AP method can be adapted for use with different metals, such as silver (Ag) and platinum (Pt). In one embodiment, the method is used to fabricate AP sensors comprising Ag or Pt as the interfacing material. The layer designations are changed during reverse metallization to accommodate the different metals, and the resulting sensors are characterized for their performance and adhesion properties.

#### Roll-to-Roll Post-Processing

The AP method is particularly well-suited for roll-to-roll post-processing, which is a ubiquitous industrial process. In one embodiment, the method is integrated into an existing tape manufacturer's production line, where AP sensors are directly peeled onto the target adhesive and sent out to the end user. This approach enables the large-scale production of flexible and stretchable sensors, meeting the growing demand for wearable and industrial sensing applications.

#### Single-Use Sensors

The AP method produces sensors that are immediately suitable as single-use, peel-and-stick sensors. In one embodiment, the sensors are used in clinical patient monitoring, where disposable systems are preferred due to concerns of sterility and contamination. The sensors are also suitable for consumer and industrial sensing applications that benefit from single-use flexible and stretchable form factors.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel and efficient microfabrication approach for producing adhesive-integrated flexible and stretchable electrical and mechanical sensors. The AP method significantly reduces the number of processing steps, enhances yield, and enables large-scale production, making it a valuable tool for advancing the field of flexible and stretchable electronics.
