# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and specifically to the structural and functional aspects of the interaction between immunoglobulin E (IgE) and the monoclonal antibody omalizumab. More particularly, the invention concerns the detailed structural analysis of the IgE-omalizumab complex and the implications of this interaction for the therapeutic use of omalizumab in the treatment of allergic disorders.

## BACKGROUND

Immunoglobulin E (IgE) plays a central role in allergic reactions and is involved in the pathogenesis of various allergic diseases such as asthma, atopic dermatitis, and allergic rhinitis. IgE binds to high-affinity receptors (FcεRI) on the surface of mast cells and basophils, leading to the release of mediators that cause allergic symptoms. Omalizumab, a monoclonal antibody, is clinically used to treat severe allergic asthma by binding to free IgE, thereby preventing its interaction with FcεRI and reducing the activation of mast cells and basophils.

Despite the clinical success of omalizumab, the detailed structural and mechanistic understanding of its interaction with IgE has been limited. Understanding the precise binding mode and the structural basis of omalizumab's inhibitory effects on IgE-receptor interactions is crucial for optimizing current therapies and developing new, more effective treatments.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a detailed structural analysis of the interaction between IgE and omalizumab, revealing the specific binding sites and the mechanisms by which omalizumab inhibits IgE interactions with its receptors. The invention includes the following key aspects:

1. **Crystal Structure of the IgE-Omalizumab Complex**: The invention describes the crystal structure of the IgE-omalizumab complex, which reveals that omalizumab binds perpendicularly to the IgE Cε3 domain, contacting symmetric binding sites on the face of the two Cε3 domains of the IgE dimer. This binding mode blocks interactions with both FcεRI and CD23 receptors.

2. **Impact of IgE Conformation on Omalizumab Binding**: The invention elucidates how the conformation of the IgE Cε3 domain affects omalizumab binding. It demonstrates that omalizumab can bind efficiently to both closed and open conformations of the IgE Cε3 domain, and that the Cε2 domain of full-length IgE can mask one of the omalizumab binding sites.

3. **Omalizumab Epitope**: The invention identifies the specific residues on IgE that form the omalizumab epitope. These residues are primarily located on the Cε3 domain and are distinct from those that engage FcεRIα. The invention also describes the interactions between omalizumab complementarity-determining region (CDR) loops and the IgE epitope.

4. **Mechanism of FcεRI and CD23 Competition**: The invention explains the structural basis for omalizumab's inhibition of FcεRI and CD23 binding. It shows that omalizumab's mechanism involves substantial steric conflicts with the receptors, particularly at the FcεRIα binding site 2 and the CD23 binding site.

5. **Steric Overlap and Inhibitor-Induced FcεRIα Complex Dissociation**: The invention compares the steric overlap between omalizumab and FcεRIα with that of a designed ankyrin repeat protein (DARPin) inhibitor, E2_79. It demonstrates that omalizumab has a significantly larger region of steric conflicts with FcεRIα, which correlates with its lower activity in disrupting preformed IgE:FcεRIα complexes.

6. **Single IgE Mutation Prevents Omalizumab Binding**: The invention describes a novel IgE mutant, IgE-R419N, which introduces a glycosylation site at the core of the omalizumab epitope, thereby abolishing omalizumab binding. This mutant retains binding to FcεRIα and can be used in combination with omalizumab to exchange native IgE on human cells.

7. **Exchange of IgE on Human Basophils**: The invention demonstrates that the co-administration of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 can effectively exchange the receptor-bound IgE on human basophils for IgE-R419N-Fc3–4, maintaining the occupancy of both high- and low-affinity IgE receptors.

8. **Synergistic Effect of IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 and Omalizumab**: The invention shows that the combination of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 acts synergistically to inhibit basophil activation at very low doses, suggesting a potential adjunct therapy with omalizumab.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### I. DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **IgE (Immunoglobulin E)**: A type of antibody that plays a crucial role in allergic reactions and is involved in the pathogenesis of various allergic diseases.
- **Omalizumab**: A monoclonal antibody used to treat severe allergic asthma by binding to free IgE and preventing its interaction with FcεRI.
- **FcεRI (High-Affinity Receptor for IgE)**: A receptor found on the surface of mast cells and basophils that binds to IgE and triggers the release of mediators causing allergic symptoms.
- **CD23 (Low-Affinity Receptor for IgE)**: A receptor that binds to IgE and is involved in the regulation of IgE production.
- **Cε3 Domain**: One of the constant regions of the IgE heavy chain, which is involved in the binding of IgE to its receptors.
- **Cε2 Domain**: Another constant region of the IgE heavy chain, which can mask one of the omalizumab binding sites.
- **DARPin (Designed Ankyrin Repeat Protein)**: A class of engineered proteins that can bind to specific targets with high affinity and specificity.
- **Glycosylation Site**: A site on a protein where a carbohydrate moiety is attached, often affecting the protein's structure and function.
- **Basophil Activation Test**: A method used to assess the activation of basophils, typically by measuring the expression of activation markers such as CD63.

### II. MODES OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

#### Crystal Structure of the IgE-Omalizumab Complex

The invention provides a detailed crystal structure of the IgE-omalizumab complex, which reveals the specific binding mode of omalizumab to IgE. The structure shows that omalizumab binds perpendicularly to the IgE Cε3 domain, contacting symmetric binding sites on the face of the two Cε3 domains of the IgE dimer. This binding mode blocks interactions with both FcεRI and CD23 receptors. The crystal structure was solved using molecular replacement techniques and refined to a resolution of 2.5 Å. The asymmetric unit contains two copies of IgE-G335C-Fc3–4 and four copies of the omalizumab–Fab, providing four copies of the IgE:omalizumab interface related by non-crystallographic symmetry (NCS).

#### Impact of IgE Conformation on Omalizumab Binding

The invention elucidates how the conformation of the IgE Cε3 domain affects omalizumab binding. It demonstrates that omalizumab can bind efficiently to both closed and open conformations of the IgE Cε3 domain. The Cε2 domain of full-length IgE can mask one of the omalizumab binding sites, which explains why omalizumab binds more efficiently to IgE-Fc3–4 fragments than to full-length IgE. The invention also shows that the association rate constant (ka) of full-length IgE is 15–30-fold lower than that of IgE-Fc3–4 fragments, while the dissociation rate constant (kd) is 4.5–10.9-fold slower, resulting in only minor differences in the overall equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd).

#### Omalizumab Epitope

The invention identifies the specific residues on IgE that form the omalizumab epitope. These residues are primarily located on the Cε3 domain and are distinct from those that engage FcεRIα. The epitope includes 23 IgE residues that bury approximately 725 Å² of surface area on IgE. The omalizumab complementarity-determining region (CDR) loops, with the exception of the light chain CDR3 loop, contact IgE. The heavy chain CDR3 shows the most extensive conformational change from the unbound omalizumab:Fab structure and contains three aromatic side chains that contact IgE. The invention also describes the hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions that facilitate omalizumab:IgE interactions.

#### Mechanism of FcεRI and CD23 Competition

The invention explains the structural basis for omalizumab's inhibition of FcεRI and CD23 binding. It shows that omalizumab's mechanism involves substantial steric conflicts with the receptors, particularly at the FcεRIα binding site 2 and the CD23 binding site. The invention calculates the theoretical volumes of atomic overlap between omalizumab and its two IgE receptors, demonstrating that the degree of steric overlap of omalizumab with CD23 is significantly greater than that observed for FcεRIα.

#### Steric Overlap and Inhibitor-Induced FcεRIα Complex Dissociation

The invention compares the steric overlap between omalizumab and FcεRIα with that of a designed ankyrin repeat protein (DARPin) inhibitor, E2_79. It demonstrates that omalizumab has a significantly larger region of steric conflicts with FcεRIα, which correlates with its lower activity in disrupting preformed IgE:FcεRIα complexes. The invention also shows that the steric conflicts with both inhibitors occur between the protein backbone of omalizumab or E2_79 and carbohydrate groups on FcεRIα, which may help facilitate the association of these inhibitors to preformed IgE:FcεRIα complexes.

#### Single IgE Mutation Prevents Omalizumab Binding

The invention describes a novel IgE mutant, IgE-R419N, which introduces a glycosylation site at the core of the omalizumab epitope, thereby abolishing omalizumab binding. This mutant retains binding to FcεRIα and can be used in combination with omalizumab to exchange native IgE on human cells. The R419N mutation induces a shift in the mass of recombinant IgE-Fc3–4, consistent with the introduction of an additional N-linked glycan. The invention demonstrates that the R419N mutation completely abolishes omalizumab binding but only slightly perturbs IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 binding to FcεRIα.

#### Exchange of IgE on Human Basophils

The invention demonstrates that the co-administration of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 can effectively exchange the receptor-bound IgE on human basophils for IgE-R419N-Fc3–4, maintaining the occupancy of both high- and low-affinity IgE receptors. The invention shows that co-administration of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 effectively exchanges the receptor-bound IgE on human basophils for IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 ex vivo. The invention also demonstrates that the combination of omalizumab and IgE-R419N-Fc3–4 can act synergistically to inhibit basophil activation at very low doses, suggesting a potential adjunct therapy with omalizumab.

### III. EXPERIMENTAL

#### Preparation of Omalizumab and Recombinant Proteins

Fab fragments of omalizumab were prepared by digestion over an immobilized ficin agarose resin in 10 mM citrate buffer with 25 mM cysteine and 5 mM EDTA at pH 6.0 for 5 h. The Fab fragments were purified in two steps with protein G and gel filtration to yield homogenous omalizumab–Fab. Protein G columns were washed with phosphate buffer over a pH gradient (8.0, 7.0, 6.0, and 5.0) and Fab was eluted with glycine buffer at pH 2.5. Insect-cell-derived IgE-Fc3–4, IgE-G335C-Fc3–4, and FcεRIα were expressed in High Five insect cells and purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography and further purified using gel filtration on a Superdex 200 10/300 GL column. Mammalian-derived IgE-Fc3–4 and the R419N mutant, used in all cell-based assays, were cloned into the pYD7 vector with a vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signal sequence and transiently expressed in suspension HEK-6E cells. The DARPins E2_79 and bi53_79 were cloned into the pQE-30 expression vector and expressed in XL-1 blue E. coli.

#### Crystallization Conditions

Crystals were grown in hanging drops in 0.2 M Lithium Sulfate, 0.1 M Tris pH 8.5, and 41% PEG 400. A volume of 0.1 μl of complex at 9 mg/ml was added to 0.1 μl of well solution. Crystals were harvested and frozen in the same crystallization buffer for data collection.

#### Structure Determination of the IgE-Omalizumab Complex

Diffraction data were collected in two 360° sweeps of one crystal on the microfocus beamline 12-2 at the Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource. Data were indexed, integrated, and scaled using the HKL2000 suite. The omalizumab-C335 crystals grew in the P21 space group with unit cell dimensions a=100.10 Å, b=107.14 Å, c=151.04 Å, and β=95.18°. A molecular replacement solution was obtained using Phaser-MR in the Phenix package using the models 4GT7 (for C335-IgE-Fc) and 4X7S (for omalizumab–Fab). Automated model building and refinement were performed using Phenix AutoBuild and Phenix Refine, respectively. NCS restraints were identified automatically in phenix.refine and applied in early rounds of refinement. The final model was refined to 2.5 Å and validated using MolProbity and Phenix comprehensive validation.

#### Calculation of Volume Overlaps

Structural alignments with omalizumab:IgE and E2_79:IgE complexes were made with the Cε3 domain at site 2 of the FcεRIα complex. The coordinates of each IgE-binding molecule (FcεRIα, omalizumab, E2_79, and CD23) from these aligned complexes were then loaded into pdbset (CCP4) and centered within an arbitrarily defined unit cell. The coordinates of the aligned and transformed structures were then input into sfall (CCP4) to generate an atom map from the polypeptide chain, and input into mapmsk (CCP4) to make a map mask. The resulting map masks were input into overlapmap (CCP4) using the MAP INCLUDE function, keeping only density of overlapping regions of each inhibitor with the receptor complex in question. The volume of the resulting overlap maps was then calculated with the volumes tool in Chimera.

#### Biotinylation of Proteins

IgE-Fc3–4 or R419N-IgE-Fc3–4 was biotinylated using EZ-Link Sulfo-NHS-LC-biotin with a 30-fold molar excess of sulfo-NHS-LC-biotin for 30 min at room temperature. The reaction was stopped using 1.0 M Tris pH 7.4, and proteins were dialysed overnight into PBS pH 7.4 and sterile-filtered with a 0.22-μm filter.

#### Basophil IgE Exchange Experiments

Blood was drawn from healthy volunteers and a volunteer with a history of food allergy. Blood was collected in heparin vacutainer tubes and washed in BF buffer. Washed blood cells were suspended in their original blood volume in BF buffer and treated with or without E2_79 at 25 μM for 2 h at 37°C to remove surface IgE. Stripped cells were then reloaded with IgE-JW8 at 120–300 ng/ml or were left untreated. These cells were then treated overnight at 37°C with omalizumab, IgE-Fc variants, or vehicle controls. The following day, cells were washed three times with BF buffer at 4°C and stained for analysis.

#### Flow Cytometry for Basophil IgE Exchange

After washing, treated cells and controls were incubated with human Fc-block and stained with the following antibodies: IgE-FITC, FcεRIα-APC, anti-biotin AF-488, anti-mouse-lambda-light-chain PE, CD123 PE-Cy5, HLA-DR PE-Cy7, CD203c BV421, CD19-PE, CD23-BV421, and Aqua Live Dead stain. Stained cells were lysed with RBS lysis buffer and washed with FACS buffer. Data were collected on a DxP FACSCAN and analysed using FlowJo.

#### SPR Assays

SPR measurements were conducted on a BIAcore X100 device. For kinetic analysis of different IgE fragments on omalizumab and FcεRIα, 1,000 response units of omalizumab or rhFcεRIα were immobilized on a CM5 chip. The different IgE fragments or full-length Sus11 IgE were diluted in HBS-EP+ running buffer and injected for 120 s at a constant flow rate of 10 μl/min. Dissociation was assessed for 240 s under constant buffer flow. The chip surface was regenerated with 50 mM NaOH. Individual sensorgram curves were exported to Excel, and graphs were prepared with GraphPad Prism 5.0 software.

#### Functional Assay with Primary Human Basophils

Human primary basophils were isolated from whole blood of volunteers and treated with 50 μM disruptive anti-IgE inhibitor bi53_79 for 8 h in the presence of 10 ng/ml rhIL-3. Cells were washed and resensitized with 100 nM JW8-IgE for 2 h in the presence of 10 ng/ml rhIL-3. Subsequently, cells were washed and treated with omalizumab, IgE-R419N-Fc3–4, or a combination of these molecules for 3 or 6 days at the indicated concentrations. For determination of basophil activation, cells were stimulated with 1–1,000 ng/ml NIP(7)BSA in the presence of 10 ng/ml rhIL-3 for 30 min at 37°C, 5% CO2. Cells were stained with anti-CD63 FITC and anti-CCR3-PE for 20 min at room temperature, and at least 3 × 10³ basophils were acquired on a FACSCalibur device. Data were analysed with FlowJo V10 software.

# Conclusion

The present invention provides a comprehensive understanding of the structural and functional aspects of the interaction between IgE and omalizumab. The detailed crystal structure of the IgE-omalizumab complex, the impact of IgE conformation on omalizumab binding, the identification of the omalizumab epitope, and the mechanisms of FcεRI and CD23 competition are key findings that advance the field of immunology. The invention also introduces a novel IgE mutant, IgE-R419N, which can be used in combination with omalizumab to exchange native IgE on human cells, offering a potential adjunct therapy for allergic disorders. The experimental data supporting these findings are robust and provide a solid foundation for further research and development in this area.
