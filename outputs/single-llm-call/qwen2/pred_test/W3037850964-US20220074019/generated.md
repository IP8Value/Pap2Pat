# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for the recovery of rare earth elements (REE) from clay-rich sedimentary rocks, particularly underclay associated with coal seams. More specifically, the invention involves the use of citric acid-based leaching solutions to efficiently extract REE from these materials while minimizing the release of gangue elements and radioactive contaminants.

## BACKGROUND

Rare earth elements (REE) are critical for the development of modern technologies, including renewable energy systems, electronics, and advanced manufacturing processes. Despite their importance, the United States (U.S.) has limited domestic production of REE, leading to reliance on foreign sources, particularly China. To address this issue, there is a growing need to identify and develop new domestic sources of REE.

Coal and coal utilization byproducts (CUB) are promising sources of REE due to their high concentrations of these elements. Underclay, a clay-rich sedimentary rock found adjacent to coal seams, is particularly rich in REE. Traditional methods for extracting REE from such materials often involve harsh chemical treatments that can release significant amounts of gangue elements and radioactive contaminants, making them environmentally and economically challenging.

Recent studies have shown that organic acids, such as citric acid, can effectively leach REE from clay-rich materials. Citric acid, with its multiple carboxylic acid groups, can form stable complexes with REE, facilitating their extraction. Moreover, citric acid-based leaching solutions can be pH-buffered to optimize the recovery of REE while minimizing the release of unwanted elements.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for the recovery of rare earth elements (REE) from clay-rich sedimentary rocks, particularly underclay associated with coal seams. The method involves the use of a citric acid-based leaching solution to extract REE from the material while minimizing the release of gangue elements and radioactive contaminants. The method comprises the following steps:

1. **Sample Preparation**: Collecting and preparing underclay samples from coal seams.
2. **Leaching Solution Preparation**: Preparing a citric acid-based leaching solution, optionally amended with sodium chloride and pH-buffered to a range of 3-6.
3. **Leaching Process**: Mixing the underclay samples with the leaching solution and allowing the mixture to react for a specified period.
4. **Separation and Filtration**: Separating the leachate from the solid residue and filtering the leachate to remove any suspended particles.
5. **REE Recovery**: Using a sorbent material to selectively capture and concentrate the REE from the leachate.

The method is environmentally friendly and cost-effective, providing a viable alternative to traditional leaching methods that often release significant amounts of gangue elements and radioactive contaminants.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Experimental Studies

#### Sample Preparation

The method begins with the collection and preparation of underclay samples from coal seams. The underclay samples are typically obtained from strata associated with production coal seams, such as the Lower Freeport, Middle Kittanning, and Pittsburgh No.5 in West Virginia, Pennsylvania, and Ohio. The samples are first crushed and ground to a fine powder using a jaw crusher and a Zr-Ti lined shatter box. The powdered samples are then sieved to ensure a consistent particle size, typically less than 149 µm. The homogenized samples are stored in a nitrogen-purged desiccator to prevent contamination.

#### Leaching Solution Preparation

The citric acid-based leaching solution is prepared by dissolving citric acid in water to achieve a concentration of 0.1 mol/L. Optionally, sodium chloride (NaCl) can be added to the solution to enhance the leaching process. The solution can be pH-buffered using a mixture of citric acid and sodium citrate to maintain a pH range of 3-6. This pH range is optimal for the complexation of REE with the citric acid anion, promoting their efficient extraction from the underclay.

#### Leaching Process

The underclay samples are mixed with the citric acid-based leaching solution in a 50 mL polypropylene centrifuge tube. The mixture is then rotated on a rotational mixer for a specified period, typically ranging from 4 to 24 hours. The duration of the leaching process can be adjusted based on the desired recovery rate of REE. After the leaching period, the mixture is centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 25 minutes to separate the leachate from the solid residue. The leachate is collected and filtered through a 0.45-micron nylon filter to remove any suspended particles.

#### REE Recovery

To recover the REE from the leachate, a sorbent material is used to selectively capture and concentrate the REE. The leachate is passed over a bed of sorbent material, which binds the REE while allowing other elements to pass through. The sorbent material can be an amine-based sorbent, which has a high affinity for REE. After the REE are bound to the sorbent, they can be selectively eluted using a suitable eluent, such as a dilute acid solution, to produce a high-purity REE fraction.

#### Optimization and Scale-Up

The method can be optimized by adjusting various parameters, such as the concentration of citric acid, the addition of sodium chloride, the pH of the leaching solution, and the duration of the leaching process. Flow-through experiments can be conducted to evaluate the efficiency of the method at a larger scale. These experiments involve passing the leaching solution through a column packed with underclay samples and monitoring the concentration of REE in the effluent solutions. The results from these experiments can be used to guide the development of a scalable process for the recovery of REE from underclay.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for the recovery of rare earth elements (REE) from clay-rich sedimentary rocks, particularly underclay associated with coal seams. The method uses a citric acid-based leaching solution to efficiently extract REE while minimizing the release of gangue elements and radioactive contaminants. The method is environmentally friendly and cost-effective, offering a promising alternative to traditional leaching methods. Future work will focus on optimizing the method and scaling it up for industrial applications.
