# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating esophageal cancer. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of Armillaridin (AM), a sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester compound isolated from the mycelium of the medicinal fungus Armillaria mellea, for inhibiting the growth of human esophageal cancer cells and enhancing their radiosensitivity. Esophageal cancer is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis, characterized by a 5-year survival rate of less than 25%. Current treatment modalities, including surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy, often fail to provide satisfactory outcomes due to the refractory nature of the disease. Therefore, there is an urgent need for novel and effective therapeutic agents to improve patient outcomes.

Armillaria mellea is a medicinal and edible fungus known for its symbiotic relationship with the Chinese medicinal herb Gastrodia elata (Tien-Ma). Extracts from Armillaria mellea have been reported to exhibit various biological activities, including antioxidation and lymphocyte proliferation. Armillaridin, a key compound isolated from Armillaria mellea, has been identified as a potent antibacterial agent. The chemical structure of Armillaridin is a sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester, characterized by its unique molecular composition and properties.

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing treatments by providing a novel method for using Armillaridin to inhibit the growth of human esophageal cancer cells and enhance their radiosensitivity. This approach offers a promising strategy for improving the efficacy of radiotherapy and reducing the overall burden of the disease.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for treating esophageal cancer using Armillaridin (AM). The method involves administering a therapeutically effective amount of AM to a subject suffering from esophageal cancer. The invention further encompasses the use of AM in combination with radiation therapy to enhance the radiosensitivity of esophageal cancer cells, thereby improving the effectiveness of the treatment.

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising Armillaridin and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition can be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, and intraperitoneal.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting the growth of human esophageal cancer cells. The method involves contacting the cells with a therapeutically effective amount of Armillaridin. The invention demonstrates that Armillaridin induces apoptosis in esophageal cancer cells through a mitochondrial pathway, leading to cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for enhancing the radiosensitivity of human esophageal cancer cells. The method involves pre-treating the cells with Armillaridin before exposing them to radiation therapy. The invention shows that Armillaridin significantly enhances the radiosensitivity of esophageal cancer cells, as evidenced by a sensitizer enhancement ratio (SER) of up to 1.6.

The invention also includes in vivo studies demonstrating the therapeutic potential of Armillaridin in inhibiting the growth of esophageal cancer xenografts in animal models. These studies show that Armillaridin effectively reduces tumor size without causing significant adverse effects on body weight or white blood cell counts.

Overall, the present invention provides a novel and effective approach for treating esophageal cancer, offering improved outcomes and reduced toxicity compared to existing therapies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Preparation of Armillaridin and Determination of Purity

Armillaridin (AM), a sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester compound, is isolated from the mycelium of the medicinal fungus Armillaria mellea. The isolation process involves standard extraction techniques, such as solvent extraction and chromatography, to obtain a pure form of AM. The compound is then dissolved in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) and stored as a stock solution at −20°C. The working solution is freshly prepared prior to each experiment to ensure optimal stability and activity. The final concentration of DMSO in the cell culture experiments is maintained at levels not exceeding 0.1% (v/v), which has been demonstrated to have no influence on cell growth.

### Cell Culture

The human esophageal cancer cell lines used in the present invention include squamous cell carcinoma (CE81T/VGH and TE-2) and adenocarcinoma (BE-3 and SKGT-4). These cell lines are cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) supplemented with sodium bicarbonate (10 mmol/L), HEPES (20 mmol/L), and 10% heat-inactivated fetal calf serum (FCS) in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator to maintain exponential growth. The cell lines are obtained from reputable sources, such as the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC), and are authenticated to ensure their identity and purity.

### Cell Viability

To determine the effect of Armillaridin on cell viability, the cells are treated with various concentrations of AM. Cell viability is assessed using the MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl-tetrazolium bromide) assay. Briefly, 1 mg/mL MTT is added to the culture medium, and the cells are incubated at 37°C for 4 hours. An equal volume of acid isopropanol (0.04 M HCl in isopropanol) is then added to dissolve the formazan inside the viable cells. The absorbance is measured at 570 nm using an ELISA reader. The concentration of 50% inhibition (IC50) values is calculated using GraphPad Prism 4 software. The results demonstrate that AM inhibits the viability of human esophageal cancer cell lines in a time- and concentration-dependent manner, with IC50 values ranging from 3.4 to 6.9 μM.

### Cell Morphology

The morphological changes in CE81T/VGH cells treated with 0 and 10 μM AM for 3 days are observed after Liu's staining. Micrographs are taken using a 1000x light microscope (Olympus). The results show that AM induces characteristic features of apoptosis, including chromatin condensation, membrane blebbing, and apoptotic body formation.

### Cell Cycle Analysis

To investigate the effect of Armillaridin on the cell cycle, the cells are treated with AM and then harvested and washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The cells are fixed and permeated at 4°C with ethanol, stained with propidium iodide (PI) solution, and analyzed using a FACScalibur flow cytometer. The data from 10^4 cells are collected and analyzed using ModFit software to calculate the proportion of cells at the G2/M phase. The results indicate that AM treatment causes a significant accumulation of cells at the G2/M phase, with the percentage increasing from 5.91 ± 0.43% in control cells to 14.04 ± 3.17% in AM-treated cells. Additionally, the percentage of sub-G1 phase cells, indicative of apoptosis, increases from 4.44 ± 0.01% in control cells to 8.72 ± 2.47% in AM-treated cells.

### Mitochondrial Membrane Potential

The effect of Armillaridin on mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) is assessed using the lipophilic fluorescence dye, 3,3′-dihexyloxacarbocyanine iodide (DiOC6(3)). The cells are stained with 40 nM DiOC6(3) and analyzed immediately using a FACS Calibur flow cytometer. The results show a significant reduction in MMP in AM-treated cells, indicating the involvement of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway.

### Annexin-V and PI Staining for Apoptosis Assessment

To further confirm the induction of apoptosis by Armillaridin, the cells are stained with Annexin V-fluorescent isothiocyanate (FITC) conjugate and PI. After treatment with AM, the cells are washed with PBS, resuspended in Annexin-V-binding buffer, and incubated with FITC-conjugated Annexin V and PI. The cells are then analyzed using a flow cytometer, and the data are collected and analyzed using CellQuest Pro Software. The results show a significant increase in the proportion of early apoptotic cells (FITC+/PI−) in AM-treated cells, confirming the pro-apoptotic activity of Armillaridin.

### Caspase Substrate Activity Assay

The activation of caspases, key mediators of apoptosis, is measured using a caspase fluorometric substrate set. The cells are pretreated with 20 μM pan caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK for one hour, followed by treatment with AM for 24 and 48 hours. The cells are harvested, washed, and lysed, and the protein concentrations are determined using the Pierce BCA protein assay kit. Fifty μg of cell lysates are mixed with 50 μL of 2× reaction buffer containing DTT and 50 μM of AFC-conjugated caspase substrates. Caspase activity is assayed using a spectrofluorometer, and the results show a significant increase in caspase 3 activity in AM-treated cells, which is blocked by pretreatment with the pan caspase inhibitor.

### Armillaridin Treatment and Radiation Delivery

To evaluate the radiosensitizing activity of Armillaridin, the cells are plated onto culture dishes and allowed to grow in DMEM medium containing 10% FCS mixed with various concentrations of AM (0, 0.125, 0.25, and 0.5 μM) for 24 hours. The drug is then washed out, and the cells are irradiated using a 6 MeV electron beam delivered by a linear accelerator. The radiation doses range from 0 to 4 Gy, with a dose rate of 2.4 Gy/min. The selection of radiation doses ensures adequate coverage from 100% to less than 37% survival (D0 in radiobiology) for further estimation of the surviving fraction. For clinical relevance, a radiation dose of 2 Gy is also selected to match the daily fraction size commonly used in clinical practice.

### Clonogenic Assay and Estimation of SER

After radiation, the cells are plated for a clonogenic assay. Viable tumor cells (10^3) are plated into each 35 mm culture dish and allowed to grow in DMEM containing 10% FCS. After 10-14 days, the culture dishes are stained with 3% crystal violet, and the number of colonies (more than 50 cells) is counted. The mean control plating efficiency for untreated CE81T/VGH cells is around 37%. The surviving fraction is calculated as the mean number of colonies divided by the number of cells inoculated. Survival curves are fitted using a linear-quadratic model, and the sensitizer enhancement ratio (SER) is calculated as the radiation dose needed for radiation alone divided by the dose needed for various concentrations of AM plus radiation at a survival fraction of 37% (D0 in radiobiology). The results show that AM significantly enhances the radiosensitivity of CE81T/VGH cells, with a SER of up to 1.6.

### Animal Model of Esophageal Tumor Xenograft

All experimental protocols involving animals are reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Experimentation Committee of Mackay Memorial Hospital. The animals are housed in a rodent facility at 22 ± 1°C with a 12-hour light-dark cycle and have free access to food and water. Male nude mice aged 4-5 weeks are obtained from the National Laboratory Animal Center and housed in the facility for acclimatization for about 7 days. 10^6 CE81T/VGH cancer cells in 0.1 mL PBS are subcutaneously implanted in the right gluteal region. After 21 days, the tumors grow to approximately 0.5 cm in diameter, and the animals are subjected to further experiments.

### In Vivo Therapeutic Studies

The animals are arranged in groups of 5 to 7 mice. AM at a dose of 80 mg/kg is intraperitoneally (I.P.) injected three times per week for 12 doses. Mice treated with equal amounts of vehicle and 2.5 mg/kg cisplatin are used as vehicle and positive controls. The size of the implanted tumor is measured using calipers, and the tumor volumes are estimated using the formula 0.5ab^2. The total body weight of each mouse is determined every other day, and the leukocyte count is estimated by retro-orbital blood sampling every other day during the study period. The results show that AM effectively inhibits the growth of CE81T/VGH xenografts without causing significant adverse effects on body weight or white blood cell counts.

### Statistics

Data are presented as mean ± standard error from triplicated experiments. Statistical comparisons are made using Student's t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) as indicated. The difference is considered significant for P < 0.05. Data analysis is performed using SPSS software (version 10.0, Chicago, IL, USA). Survival curves are fitted using Sigma Plot software (version 8.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) with syntax to fit a linear-quadratic model.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Inhibition of Cell Viability by Armillaridin

Human esophageal cancer cell lines (CE81T/VGH, TE-2, BE-3, and SKGT-4) were treated with various concentrations of Armillaridin (0, 1, 5, 10, 20 μM) for 24, 48, and 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. The results showed that Armillaridin inhibited the viability of all four cell lines in a time- and concentration-dependent manner, with IC50 values ranging from 3.4 to 6.9 μM.

#### Example 2: Induction of Apoptosis by Armillaridin

CE81T/VGH and BE-3 cells were treated with 10 μM Armillaridin for 3 days. Morphological changes were observed using Liu's staining and a 1000x light microscope. The results showed chromatin condensation, membrane blebbing, and apoptotic body formation, characteristic of apoptosis. Additionally, the cells were stained with Annexin V-FITC and PI, and the proportion of early apoptotic cells (FITC+/PI−) was measured using flow cytometry. The results confirmed a significant increase in the proportion of early apoptotic cells in Armillaridin-treated cells.

#### Example 3: Cell Cycle Arrest by Armillaridin

CE81T/VGH cells were treated with 10 μM Armillaridin for 24 hours. The cells were harvested, fixed, and stained with propidium iodide (PI) solution. Flow cytometry analysis showed a significant accumulation of cells at the G2/M phase, with the percentage increasing from 5.91 ± 0.43% in control cells to 14.04 ± 3.17% in Armillaridin-treated cells. The sub-G1 phase, indicative of apoptosis, also increased from 4.44 ± 0.01% in control cells to 8.72 ± 2.47% in Armillaridin-treated cells.

#### Example 4: Reduction in Mitochondrial Membrane Potential by Armillaridin

CE81T/VGH cells were treated with 10 μM Armillaridin for 24 hours. The cells were stained with 40 nM DiOC6(3) and analyzed using flow cytometry. The results showed a significant reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential in Armillaridin-treated cells, indicating the involvement of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway.

#### Example 5: Activation of Caspase 3 by Armillaridin

CE81T/VGH cells were pretreated with 20 μM pan caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK for one hour, followed by treatment with 10 μM Armillaridin for 24 hours. The cells were harvested, lysed, and the protein concentrations were determined. Caspase 3 activity was measured using a caspase fluorometric substrate set. The results showed a significant increase in caspase 3 activity in Armillaridin-treated cells, which was blocked by pretreatment with the pan caspase inhibitor.

#### Example 6: Radiosensitizing Activity of Armillaridin

CE81T/VGH cells were treated with various concentrations of Armillaridin (0, 0.125, 0.25, and 0.5 μM) for 24 hours, followed by irradiation with 2 Gy. The cells were then plated for a clonogenic assay, and the surviving fraction was calculated. The results showed that Armillaridin significantly enhanced the radiosensitivity of CE81T/VGH cells, with a sensitizer enhancement ratio (SER) of up to 1.6.

#### Example 7: Inhibition of Tumor Growth in Vivo

Male nude mice were subcutaneously implanted with 10^6 CE81T/VGH cancer cells. After 21 days, the tumors grew to approximately 0.5 cm in diameter. The mice were then treated with 80 mg/kg Armillaridin intraperitoneally (I.P.) three times per week for 12 doses. The tumor size was measured using calipers, and the tumor volumes were estimated using the formula 0.5ab^2. The results showed that Armillaridin effectively inhibited the growth of CE81T/VGH xenografts without causing significant adverse effects on body weight or white blood cell counts.
