# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel strain of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* designated as Liulou 1, which exhibits exceptional capabilities in degrading atrazine, a widely used herbicide. The invention further encompasses methods for isolating, identifying, and utilizing this strain for the bioremediation of atrazine-contaminated environments. Atrazine, a s-triazine herbicide, is extensively used in agriculture due to its effectiveness in controlling broadleaf and grassy weeds. However, its persistence in the environment and potential toxic effects on non-target organisms have raised significant environmental concerns. Bioremediation, particularly through the use of atrazine-degrading bacteria, offers a sustainable and environmentally friendly approach to mitigate these issues.

Strains of the genus *Arthrobacter* are well-known for their ability to degrade various xenobiotic compounds, including atrazine. These bacteria are typically found in soil and are characterized by their robustness and adaptability to diverse environmental conditions. The present invention focuses on a specific strain, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1, which demonstrates superior atrazine degradation capabilities and root-colonizing abilities, making it a valuable resource for bioremediation and phytoremediation applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel strain of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* designated as Liulou 1, which is capable of efficiently degrading atrazine. The strain is characterized by its ability to colonize plant roots and enhance the degradation of atrazine in the rhizosphere. The invention further includes methods for isolating, identifying, and utilizing this strain for the bioremediation of atrazine-contaminated soils and water bodies.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Isolation and Identification**: The strain *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 is isolated from the rhizosphere of maize plants and identified through phylogenetic and molecular techniques.
2. **Atrazine Degradation**: The strain exhibits high efficiency in degrading atrazine, converting it into less harmful metabolites such as hydroxyatrazine (HA), deethylatrazine (DEA), and deisopropylatrazine (DIA).
3. **Root-Colonizing Ability**: The strain can effectively colonize the roots of various plants, including maize, wheat, and alfalfa, thereby enhancing the degradation of atrazine in the rhizosphere.
4. **Bioremediation Applications**: The strain can be used to treat atrazine-contaminated soils and water bodies, reducing the environmental impact of atrazine and improving soil health.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1

The strain *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize plants grown in atrazine-contaminated soil. The isolation process involved the following steps:
1. **Sample Collection**: Soil samples were collected from the rhizosphere of maize plants grown in fields with a history of atrazine application.
2. **Enrichment Culture**: Soil samples were enriched in mineral medium (SM) supplemented with atrazine as the sole carbon and nitrogen source.
3. **Plating and Isolation**: Enriched cultures were plated on solid SM medium and incubated at 28°C. Colonies were isolated and purified through repeated streaking.
4. **Screening for Atrazine Degradation**: Isolated colonies were screened for their ability to degrade atrazine using a colorimetric assay and high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS/MS).

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1

The phylogenetic identification of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 was conducted using 16S rRNA gene sequencing and analysis. The steps involved are as follows:
1. **DNA Extraction**: Genomic DNA was extracted from the isolated strain using standard protocols.
2. **PCR Amplification**: The 16S rRNA gene was amplified using universal bacterial primers.
3. **Sequencing**: The amplified 16S rRNA gene was sequenced using Sanger sequencing.
4. **Sequence Analysis**: The obtained sequence was compared with sequences in public databases (e.g., NCBI) to determine the closest relatives and construct a phylogenetic tree.

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification of Strain Liulou 1 by Repetitive Elements Sequence-Based PCR (Rep-PCR)

Repetitive elements sequence-based PCR (Rep-PCR) was used to confirm the taxonomic identity and individuality of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1. The process involved the following steps:
1. **DNA Preparation**: Genomic DNA was prepared from the isolated strain.
2. **Rep-PCR Amplification**: Rep-PCR was performed using primers targeting repetitive DNA elements.
3. **Gel Electrophoresis**: The amplified products were separated by gel electrophoresis and visualized.
4. **Band Pattern Analysis**: The banding patterns were compared with those of known *Arthrobacter* strains to confirm the identity of the isolated strain.

### Identification of the Genes for Atrazine Degradation in Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1

The genes responsible for atrazine degradation in *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 were identified and characterized. The steps involved are as follows:
1. **Genome Sequencing**: The genome of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 was sequenced using next-generation sequencing technologies.
2. **Bioinformatics Analysis**: The genomic data were analyzed to identify genes encoding enzymes involved in atrazine degradation, such as trzN and atzBC.
3. **Gene Expression Analysis**: The expression of identified genes was confirmed using reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR).
4. **Functional Validation**: The functional roles of the identified genes were validated through gene knockout and complementation studies.

### Example 5

#### Application of Strain Liulou 1 in Bioremediation of Atrazine-Contaminated Soil

The effectiveness of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 in degrading atrazine in contaminated soil was demonstrated through a series of experiments. The steps involved are as follows:
1. **Soil Preparation**: Atrazine-contaminated soil was prepared by spiking clean soil with known concentrations of atrazine.
2. **Inoculation**: The soil was inoculated with *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 at various concentrations.
3. **Incubation**: The inoculated soil was incubated under controlled conditions for a specified period.
4. **Atrazine Degradation Analysis**: The degradation of atrazine was monitored over time using HPLC-MS/MS.
5. **Root Colonization Analysis**: The ability of the strain to colonize plant roots was assessed by planting seeds in the inoculated soil and analyzing root-associated bacterial populations.

The results showed that *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 efficiently degraded atrazine in the soil, with significant reductions in atrazine levels observed. Additionally, the strain successfully colonized the roots of various plants, enhancing the degradation of atrazine in the rhizosphere. These findings highlight the potential of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 as a valuable bioremediation agent for managing atrazine contamination in agricultural and environmental settings.
