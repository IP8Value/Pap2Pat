# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for preventing and treating ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury, particularly in the context of myocardial infarction and other cardiovascular diseases. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of an acidic polysaccharide fraction (AP1) derived from ginseng (Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer) for protecting cardiomyocytes from hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) injury.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury is a significant clinical problem, particularly in the context of myocardial infarction (MI). According to the World Health Organization (WHO), ischemic heart disease (IHD) is the leading cause of death globally, accounting for approximately 7.4 million deaths annually. The primary cause of myocardial damage in IHD is the deprivation of nutrients and oxygen due to coronary occlusion. While timely reperfusion can reduce short-term mortality, it can also trigger cardiac remodeling and additional oxidative stress, leading to long-term complications.

Functional foods and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) offer promising alternatives for the prevention and treatment of I/R injury due to their minimal side effects and regular consumption benefits. Ginseng, an herbal root with a rich history in TCM, has been extensively studied for its cardiovascular activities, including reducing myocardial infarct size, regulating blood circulation, and improving lipid profiles. These effects are primarily attributed to ginsenosides, the active glycosides in ginseng. However, the role of ginseng polysaccharides, particularly acidic polysaccharides, in cardioprotection has been underexplored.

This invention addresses the gap by isolating and characterizing an acidic polysaccharide fraction (AP1) from ginseng and demonstrating its cardioprotective effects against H/R injury in cardiomyocytes. The invention provides a novel approach to leveraging natural compounds for the prevention and treatment of I/R injury, offering a safer and more effective alternative to conventional therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel acidic polysaccharide fraction (AP1) derived from ginseng (Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer) and methods for its use in preventing and treating ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury, particularly in the context of myocardial infarction. The invention is based on the discovery that AP1 exhibits significant cardioprotective effects by modulating mitochondrial function and activating the RISK/eNOS/NO pathway.

The key features of the invention include:
1. **Isolation and Characterization of AP1**: The invention describes a method for isolating AP1 from ginseng using high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC) and stepwise ethanol precipitation. AP1 is characterized as an acidic heteroglycan with a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10^4 Da, primarily composed of glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), and small amounts of galacturonic acid (Gal A), arabinose (Ara), and glucuronic acid (Glc A).
2. **Cardioprotective Effects of AP1**: AP1 demonstrates significant cardioprotective effects against H/R injury in H9c2 cardiomyocytes. It reduces cell death, inhibits the release of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and creatine kinase (CK), and decreases the formation of malondialdehyde (MDA) and reactive oxygen species (ROS). AP1 also maintains mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), increases ATP generation, and prevents the release of cytochrome c, thereby inhibiting the activation of caspase-3 and caspase-9.
3. **Mechanism of Action**: The cardioprotective effects of AP1 are mediated through the activation of the RISK pathway, involving the phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, and eNOS, and the subsequent production of nitric oxide (NO). AP1 also upregulates the expression of glucocorticoid receptors (GR) and estrogen receptors (ER), which play crucial roles in the activation of the RISK pathway.
4. **Pharmaceutical Compositions and Methods of Use**: The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AP1 and methods for their use in preventing and treating I/R injury. These compositions can be formulated into various dosage forms, including tablets, capsules, and injectable solutions, for oral or parenteral administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Isolation and Characterization of AP1

#### Raw Materials and Extraction of Ginseng
The root of *Panax ginseng* C.A. Meyer was obtained from a certified supplier in Jinlin Province, China. The quality of the ginseng was verified according to the Hong Kong Standard of Chinese Materia Medica and the Chinese Pharmacopeia. The ginseng extract (RSE) was prepared by ethanol extraction, a well-established method for preparing ginseng extracts.

#### High-Speed Counter-Current Chromatography (HSCCC) Separation
The ginseng extract was fractionated using HSCCC. The HSCCC system, equipped with a three-polytetrafluoroethylene coil with a total capacity of 1000 mL, was operated at a β value ranging from 0.59 to 0.75. The diphase solvent system was prepared by mixing solvents in a specific volume ratio and equilibrated by shaking at room temperature. After separation and degassing, the upper and lower phases were collected and used for the HSCCC separation. The ginseng extract was dissolved in the two-phase solvents and centrifuged to obtain the supernatant. The stationary phase (upper phase of butanol-water, 1:1) was filled into the HSCCC column, which was then rotated at 800 rpm and injected with the sample solution. The mobile phase (lower phase of butanol-water, 1:1) was injected at a flow rate of 8 mL/min. After 100 minutes, the mobile phase was changed to the lower phase of ethyl acetate-water (1:1). Fractions were collected at 5-minute intervals and combined based on silica gel TLC examination results.

#### Stepwise Ethanol Precipitation
The HSCCC fractions with biological activity were further fractionated using stepwise ethanol precipitation. The fractions were dissolved in water and precipitated using 90%, 80%, and 70% ethanol solutions. The supernatants and precipitates were separated by centrifugation and lyophilized. The bioactivity of the fractions was evaluated using H9c2 cardiomyocytes exposed to H/R.

#### Ion-Exchange Chromatography
The final precipitate from the stepwise ethanol precipitation was separated by anion-exchange chromatography on Toyopearl DEAE 650 M. The column was eluted stepwise with water, 0.5 M NaCl, 1.0 M NaCl, 2.0 M NaCl, and 0.2 M NaOH to yield five fractions: neutral polysaccharides (NP), acidic polysaccharides (AP1, AP2, AP3, and AP4). These fractions were dialyzed, centrifuged, and lyophilized. The bioactivity of the fractions was evaluated using H9c2 cardiomyocytes exposed to H/R.

#### Characterization of Polysaccharides
The molecular weight of the active polysaccharide fractions was determined by high-performance gel permeation chromatography (HPGPC). The monosaccharide composition was analyzed by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The total sugar content was determined using the phenol-H2SO4 colorimetric method, and the uronic acid content was determined using the m-hydroxydiphenyl method. Protein content was determined using the Bradford assay.

### Cardioprotective Effects of AP1

#### Cell Viability
The cell viability of H9c2 cardiomyocytes was evaluated using the mitochondrial viability stain (MVS) assay. H9c2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and antibiotics. The cells were exposed to H/R, and the effect of AP1 on cell viability was assessed by measuring the fluorescence intensity at excitation 550 nm and emission 590 nm using a microplate reader.

#### Hypoxia/Reoxygenation and Drug Treatment
Hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) was induced by placing the cells in a hypoxic chamber and flushing with pure nitrogen for 5 minutes at a flow rate of 20 mL/min. The cells were then incubated in a hypoxic environment for 3 hours, followed by reoxygenation in a normoxic environment for 4 hours. AP1 was added to the cells 1 hour before hypoxia treatment and remained throughout the H/R process. The normal control cells were cultured in DMEM under normoxic conditions for 8 hours.

#### Measurement of Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) and Creatine Kinase (CK) Activities
The activities of LDH and CK in the cell culture medium were measured using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer's instructions. The absorbance was read at 450 nm and 660 nm, respectively, using a spectrophotometer.

#### Measurement of Intracellular Malondialdehyde (MDA) Level and Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) Activity
The intracellular MDA level and SOD activity were measured using commercial assay kits. The cellular protein was extracted and quantified using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method. The MDA level was measured at 532 nm, and SOD activity was measured at 450 nm using a spectrophotometer.

#### Measurement of Intracellular and Mitochondrial Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)
The intracellular and mitochondrial ROS levels were measured using DCFH-DA and mitoSOX probes, respectively. The cells were incubated with the probes and analyzed by flow cytometry. The fluorescence intensity was recorded at 488 nm and 510 nm.

#### Western Blot Analysis
The expression of various proteins involved in the RISK pathway and mitochondrial function was analyzed by western blotting. The cells were lysed, and the protein concentration was determined using a Bradford Protein Assay Kit. Equal amounts of protein were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane. The membranes were probed with specific primary antibodies and detected using secondary antibodies conjugated with IRDye. The bands were visualized using an Odyssey CLx Imaging System, and the band intensity was analyzed using Odyssey v3.0 software.

#### Measurement of Nitric Oxide (NO)
The level of NO in the cell culture medium was measured using the Griess reaction according to the manufacturer's instructions. The absorbance was read at 540 nm using a spectrophotometer.

#### Apoptosis Analysis with Flow Cytometer
Apoptosis was measured using a FITC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit. The cells were stained with FITC-labeled Annexin V and propidium iodide and analyzed by flow cytometry. The percentage of apoptotic cells was determined using Flow J software.

#### Determination of Caspase Activity
The activities of caspase-3/7 and caspase-9 were measured using a Caspase-Glo assay kit. The cells were incubated with the caspase reagents, and the luminescence was measured using a microplate reader.

#### Measurement of Mitochondrial Membrane Potential (MMP)
The MMP was measured using rhodamine 123. The cells were incubated with rhodamine 123 and analyzed using a DeltaVision Elite Cell Imaging System or a microplate reader.

#### Determination of ATP Level
The ATP level was measured using an ATP measurement detection assay kit. The cells were incubated with the ATP substrate solution, and the luminescence was measured using a microplate reader.

#### Evaluation of Oxygen Consumption Rate (OCR)
The OCR was measured using an XFp Extracellular Flux Analyzer. The cells were seeded in XFp cell culture miniplates and subjected to H/R treatment. The OCR was measured at baseline and after the addition of oligomycin, FCCP, and antimycin A/rotenone. The OCR was normalized for total protein per well and expressed as pmol/min/μg protein.

### Mechanism of Action

#### Activation of RISK Pathway
The cardioprotective effects of AP1 are mediated through the activation of the RISK pathway, which consists of the PI3K/Akt and ERK1/2 signaling pathways. AP1 upregulates the expression of glucocorticoid receptors (GR) and estrogen receptors (ER), leading to the phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, and eNOS. This results in the production of nitric oxide (NO), which plays a crucial role in cardioprotection by neutralizing ROS and maintaining mitochondrial function.

#### Modulation of Mitochondrial Function
AP1 stabilizes the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) and maintains ATP generation, thereby preventing the release of cytochrome c and the activation of caspase-3 and caspase-9. This inhibition of the mitochondrial-dependent apoptotic pathway contributes to the cardioprotective effects of AP1.

#### Pharmaceutical Compositions
The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AP1 and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, or excipients. The compositions can be formulated into various dosage forms, including tablets, capsules, and injectable solutions, for oral or parenteral administration.

#### Methods of Use
The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention can be used for preventing and treating ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury, particularly in the context of myocardial infarction. The compositions can be administered to subjects at risk of I/R injury or to subjects who have experienced an I/R event. The dosage and frequency of administration will depend on the specific condition and the individual patient.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Isolation and Characterization of AP1
Ginseng extract was prepared by ethanol extraction and fractionated using HSCCC. The HSCCC fractions were further fractionated using stepwise ethanol precipitation and ion-exchange chromatography. The final precipitate, designated as AP1, was characterized as an acidic heteroglycan with a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10^4 Da, primarily composed of glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), and small amounts of galacturonic acid (Gal A), arabinose (Ara), and glucuronic acid (Glc A).

### Example 2

#### Cardioprotective Effects of AP1
H9c2 cardiomyocytes were exposed to hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) and treated with AP1. The cell viability was significantly increased, and the release of LDH and CK was reduced. The formation of MDA and ROS was inhibited, and the mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) was maintained. The release of cytochrome c and the activation of caspase-3 and caspase-9 were prevented. Western blot analysis showed that AP1 upregulated the expression of GR and ER and activated the RISK pathway, leading to the phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, and eNOS and the production of nitric oxide (NO).

These examples demonstrate the isolation, characterization, and cardioprotective effects of AP1, providing a strong foundation for the development of novel pharmaceutical compositions and methods for preventing and treating ischemia-reperfusion injury.
