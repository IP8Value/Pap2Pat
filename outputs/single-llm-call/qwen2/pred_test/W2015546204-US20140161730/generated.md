# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) contrast agents. More specifically, it pertains to novel manganese (Mn²⁺)-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures that exhibit significantly enhanced relaxivity compared to conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes. These structures are particularly useful for enhancing the diagnostic capabilities of MRI by providing superior contrast enhancement.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a non-invasive diagnostic technique widely used in radiology to provide detailed images of various organs and tissues. Contrast agents (CAs) are essential for improving the detection and diagnostic confidence of MRI by altering the relaxation times of water protons in the tissue. The two main types of MRI CAs are T₁ and T₂ agents, which affect the longitudinal (T₁) and transverse (T₂) relaxation times, respectively. The effectiveness of these agents is quantified by their relaxivity, defined as the change in relaxation rate per unit concentration of the MRI CA.

Conventional T₁ MRI CAs are primarily synthesized as metal-ion chelate complexes, with gadolinium (Gd³⁺) and manganese (Mn²⁺) being the most common paramagnetic ions. Despite extensive research, the relaxivity of these agents is suboptimal, and there is a theoretical prediction that new contrast agents could achieve relaxivities up to fifty to a hundred times greater.

Recent advancements have focused on developing Gd³⁺-ion carbon nanostructure complexes, such as gadonanotubes and gadofullerenes, which show significantly higher relaxivities compared to traditional Gd³⁺-chelate complexes. However, the potential and efficacy of Mn²⁺-ion carbon nanostructure complexes as MRI CAs remain largely unexplored.

This invention addresses this gap by introducing Mn²⁺-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures that exhibit unprecedentedly high relaxivity. These structures are synthesized through a simple potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)-based oxidative chemical procedure, resulting in the confinement of Mn²⁺ ions within the graphene sheets. The resulting materials show diverse structural, chemical, and magnetic properties, with nuclear magnetic resonance dispersion (NMRD) profiles distinct from those of conventional paramagnetic chelates.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel Mn²⁺-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures that exhibit significantly enhanced relaxivity, making them highly effective MRI contrast agents. These structures are synthesized by oxidizing micro-graphite, graphene nanoplatelets, and graphene nanoribbons using a KMnO₄-based oxidative chemical procedure. The confinement of Mn²⁺ ions within the graphene sheets leads to a substantial increase in relaxivity, up to two orders of magnitude higher than that of paramagnetic chelate compounds.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **High Relaxivity**: The Mn²⁺-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures exhibit relaxivities up to 20 times higher than conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes at 0.47 T, with even greater enhancements at lower magnetic fields.
2. **Unique NMRD Profiles**: The NMRD profiles of these structures are distinct from those of other Mn²⁺-based complexes, indicating a novel relaxation mechanism.
3. **Versatile Synthesis**: The synthesis method is simple and scalable, allowing for the large-scale production of these high-relaxivity MRI CAs.
4. **Superior Magnetic Properties**: The structures display unique magnetic behaviors, including superparamagnetic and ferromagnetic properties, which contribute to their enhanced relaxivity.
5. **Potential for T₁ Contrast Agents**: The high r₁/r₂ ratio suggests that these structures are particularly suitable as T₁ contrast agents, although they can also be used for T₂ contrast at higher magnetic fields.

The invention also encompasses methods for synthesizing these Mn²⁺-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures and their use in MRI applications. The enhanced relaxivity and unique properties of these structures offer significant advantages over existing MRI CAs, potentially leading to improved diagnostic outcomes in medical imaging.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Synthesis of Mn²⁺-Intercalated Graphitic and Graphene Structures

The Mn²⁺-intercalated graphitic and graphene structures are synthesized using a simple potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)-based oxidative chemical procedure. The process involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Oxidized Micro-Graphite**:
   - Analytical grade micro-graphite is suspended in water and sonicated for 1 hour to form a uniform solution.
   - The solution is centrifuged, and the pellet is dried overnight to obtain oxidized micro-graphite.

2. **Synthesis of Oxidized Graphene Nanoplatelets**:
   - Dried oxidized micro-graphite is suspended in water and sonicated for 1 hour.
   - The suspension is centrifuged, and the pellet is dried to obtain oxidized graphene nanoplatelets.

3. **Reduction of Graphene Nanoplatelets**:
   - Oxidized graphene nanoplatelets are treated with hydrazine hydrate and heated at 100°C for 12 hours.
   - The product is isolated, washed with water and methanol, and dried to yield reduced graphene nanoplatelets.

4. **Synthesis of Graphene Nanoribbons**:
   - Multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) are suspended in concentrated sulfuric acid and stirred for 2 hours.
   - KMnO₄ is added, and the mixture is heated at 55-70°C for 1 hour.
   - The product is washed with water, ethanol, and ether, and isolated by centrifugation to obtain graphene nanoribbons.

### Characterization of Magnetic Behavior

The magnetic properties of the synthesized structures are characterized using a superconducting quantum interference device (SQUID) magnetometer. The samples are analyzed over a range of magnetic fields and temperatures to determine their magnetic behavior. Key findings include:

- **Oxidized Micro-Graphite and Oxidized Graphene Nanoplatelets**: Display paramagnetic behavior, attributed to the presence of Mn²⁺ ions.
- **Reduced Graphene Nanoplatelets**: Exhibit superparamagnetic behavior at room temperature, with a blocking temperature of 40 K.
- **Graphene Nanoribbons**: Show weak ferromagnetism at room temperature, with a coercive field of 250 Oe at 10 K.

### Electron Paramagnetic Resonance (EPR) Measurements

EPR spectroscopy is used to analyze the electronic structure and relaxation times of the Mn²⁺ ions in the synthesized structures. The EPR spectra reveal:

- **Broad Peaks**: Indicative of significant Mn²⁺-to-Mn²⁺ dipolar interactions.
- **Short Electron Relaxation Times**: Suggesting efficient relaxation processes.
- **Presence of Free Radicals**: Observed in graphene nanoribbons, which may contribute to the magnetic behavior.

### Proton Relaxivity Measurements

The relaxivity of the synthesized structures is measured using NMR spectroscopy. The longitudinal (r₁) and transverse (r₂) relaxivities are determined at various magnetic fields and concentrations. Key findings include:

- **High Relaxivity**: The Mn²⁺-intercalated structures exhibit relaxivities up to 20 times higher than conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes at 0.47 T.
- **Distinct NMRD Profiles**: The NMRD profiles are distinct from those of other Mn²⁺-based complexes, indicating a novel relaxation mechanism.

### Theoretical Analysis

The Solomon-Bloembergen-Morgan (SBM) equations are used to model the relaxation behavior of the Mn²⁺-intercalated structures. The parameters obtained from the fits include:

- **Zero-Field Splitting Energy (D)**: Within the accepted range of 10¹⁸–10²⁰ s⁻².
- **Correlation Time for Zero-Field Splitting (τ₀)**: In the range of 1–100 picoseconds.
- **Rotational Correlation Time (τ_R)**: In the nanosecond to microsecond range.
- **Number of Fast-Exchanging Water Molecules (q)**: Close to 8, consistent with theoretical predictions.
- **Water Residence Lifetime (τ_M)**: In the range of a few to hundreds of nanoseconds.
