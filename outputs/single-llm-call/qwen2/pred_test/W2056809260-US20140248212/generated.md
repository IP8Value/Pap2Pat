# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Positron emission tomography (PET) reporter gene (PRG) imaging is a powerful tool for non-invasively monitoring cell-based therapies. Therapeutic cells engineered to express a PET reporter gene (PRG) specifically accumulate a PET reporter probe (PRP) and can be detected by PET imaging. This technology is crucial for understanding the pharmacokinetics of therapeutic genes and cells, correlating this information with therapeutic outcomes, and predicting and evaluating treatment efficacy and side effects. However, the clinical adoption of this technology is hindered by the immunogenicity of viral PRGs, such as herpes simplex virus type 1 thymidine kinase (HSV1-tk), which can lead to immune-mediated elimination of therapeutic cells.

To address this issue, there is a need for non-immunogenic PRGs that do not alter the normal metabolism or function of therapeutic cells. Enzyme-encoding PRGs, particularly nucleoside kinases, offer high sensitivity due to the catalytic turnover of the enzymatic reaction that traps the probe. However, the ectopic expression of nucleoside kinases can perturb the normal regulation of nucleotide metabolism, leading to imbalanced nucleotide pools and increased risk of genotoxicity.

This invention describes a new PRG-PRP system that employs a mutated form of human thymidine kinase 2 (TK2) as the PRG and 2-deoxy-2-18F-5-methyl-1-β-L-arabinofuranosyluracil (L-18F-FMAU) as the PRP. The TK2 mutant, designated TK2-N93D/L109F, has been engineered to efficiently phosphorylate L-18F-FMAU while having reduced activity for endogenous nucleosides, thereby minimizing potential metabolic perturbations. This system offers a promising solution for the non-invasive monitoring of therapeutic cells in preclinical and clinical applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel PET reporter gene (PRG) and reporter probe (PRP) system for non-invasively monitoring therapeutic cells. The PRG is a mutated form of human thymidine kinase 2 (TK2), specifically the double mutant TK2-N93D/L109F, which has been engineered to efficiently phosphorylate the PET reporter probe L-18F-FMAU. The PRP, L-18F-FMAU, is a non-natural nucleoside analog that accumulates in cells expressing the TK2-N93D/L109F PRG.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Non-Immunogenic PRG**: The TK2-N93D/L109F mutant is derived from a human enzyme, reducing the risk of immune-mediated elimination of therapeutic cells.
2. **High Sensitivity**: The TK2-N93D/L109F mutant has comparable sensitivity to the widely used HSV1-sr39tk/18F-FHBG system, making it suitable for detecting small numbers of therapeutic cells.
3. **Reduced Metabolic Perturbation**: The TK2-N93D/L109F mutant has reduced activity for endogenous nucleosides, minimizing the risk of altering cellular metabolism and causing genotoxicity.
4. **Favorable Biodistribution**: L-18F-FMAU has a favorable biodistribution in both mice and humans, with minimal accumulation in non-target tissues.

The invention also includes methods for synthesizing L-18F-FMAU and using the TK2-N93D/L109F PRG-PRP system in PET imaging of therapeutic cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction

Positron emission tomography (PET) reporter gene (PRG) imaging is a valuable technique for non-invasively monitoring the distribution and function of therapeutic cells. Traditional PRGs, such as HSV1-tk, have been widely used but suffer from immunogenicity issues. This invention addresses these limitations by providing a new PRG-PRP system based on a mutated form of human thymidine kinase 2 (TK2) and the PET reporter probe L-18F-FMAU.

### Structure and Function of TK2-N93D/L109F

#### Molecular Modeling of TK2

The development of the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant began with the generation of a homology model of TK2 using the SWISS-MODEL server. The solved structures of human deoxycytidine kinase (dCK) in both its closed and open conformations served as templates. The homology model revealed that TK2 can adopt an open or closed conformation, with the closed conformation being active and the open conformation being inactive.

#### Engineering the TK2 Mutants

To reduce the susceptibility of TK2 to dTTP-mediated feedback inhibition, the N93D mutation was introduced. This mutation disrupts the hydrogen bonding between Asn-93 and Glu-200, favoring the open conformation of the enzyme and reducing the probability of dTTP binding and exerting its inhibitory effect. Kinase assays confirmed that the N93D mutant had reduced susceptibility to feedback inhibition compared to wild-type TK2.

To further improve the selectivity and affinity of TK2 for L-18F-FMAU, the L109F mutation was introduced. This mutation increased the overall speed of the enzyme for all tested substrates, with a greater effect on D-dT. However, it did not provide the desired increase in selectivity toward L nucleosides. Combining the N93D and L109F mutations resulted in the TK2-N93D/L109F double mutant, which had increased activity toward L-18F-FMAU and reduced activity toward endogenous nucleosides.

### Synthesis of L-18F-FMAU

The radiochemical synthesis of L-18F-FMAU involves several steps, including the preparation of the precursor and the labeling with 18F. The detailed synthesis protocol ensures the production of L-18F-FMAU with high radiochemical purity and yield.

### In Vitro and In Vivo Characterization

#### Kinetic Analyses

Kinetic analyses of the TK2 mutants were performed using an NADH-dependent enzyme-coupled assay. The results showed that the N93D mutation reduced the k_cat for endogenous nucleosides while increasing the k_cat for L-18F-FMAU. The L109F mutation increased the overall speed of the enzyme for all substrates. The TK2-N93D/L109F double mutant combined the beneficial properties of the single mutants, with a 14.9-fold higher k_cat (L-FMAU)/k_cat (D-dT) ratio compared to wild-type TK2.

#### Cell-Based Uptake Assays

Cell-based uptake assays using L1210 cells transduced with the TK2 mutants demonstrated that the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant had the highest L-18F-FMAU uptake, with a 1.5-fold increase over the N93D mutant and nearly a 4-fold increase over the L109F mutant. The presence of 5 μM D-dT significantly reduced L-18F-FMAU uptake in cells expressing the N93D and L109F mutants, but the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant remained less affected.

#### MicroPET/CT Imaging Studies in Mice

Mice implanted with L1210 cells expressing the TK2 mutants were scanned by microPET/CT using L-18F-FMAU. The results showed that the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant had the highest L-18F-FMAU uptake, with a 2.6-fold increase over the N93D mutant. The sensitivity of the TK2-N93D/L109F PRG was comparable to that of the widely used HSV1-sr39tk/18F-FHBG system.

### Biodistribution Studies

#### Biodistribution in Mice

The biodistribution of L-18F-FMAU in mice was compared to that of 18F-FHBG. Both probes had low retention in the thoracic cavity and minimal accumulation in the liver. L-18F-FMAU had lower accumulation in the gallbladder and abdominal cavity compared to 18F-FHBG, indicating a favorable biodistribution for cell tracking applications.

#### Biodistribution in Humans

The biodistribution of L-18F-FMAU in humans was determined in healthy volunteers. L-18F-FMAU accumulated in the liver, kidneys, gallbladder, bladder, and GI tract, with additional accumulation in the myocardium. The liver and myocardial accumulation were higher than that of 18F-FHBG, suggesting potential improvements in the PRP to reduce myocardial uptake.

### Advantages and Disadvantages

#### Advantages

1. **Non-Immunogenic PRG**: The human origin of the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant reduces the risk of immune-mediated elimination of therapeutic cells.
2. **High Sensitivity**: The TK2-N93D/L109F mutant has comparable sensitivity to the HSV1-sr39tk/18F-FHBG system.
3. **Reduced Metabolic Perturbation**: The TK2-N93D/L109F mutant has reduced activity for endogenous nucleosides, minimizing the risk of altering cellular metabolism and causing genotoxicity.
4. **Favorable Biodistribution**: L-18F-FMAU has a favorable biodistribution in both mice and humans, with minimal accumulation in non-target tissues.

#### Disadvantages

1. **Myocardial Accumulation**: L-18F-FMAU accumulates in the myocardium in humans, which may limit its utility for imaging therapeutic cells at this site.
2. **Liver Retention**: L-18F-FMAU is retained in the human liver, which may affect its utility for imaging therapeutic cells in the liver.

### Potential Improvements

1. **Modifying L-18F-FMAU**: Replacing the 5-methyl group with a larger substituent such as ethyl or propyl may reduce myocardial uptake.
2. **Further Mutagenesis**: Additional mutations in the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant may further improve its selectivity and affinity for L-18F-FMAU.
