# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of energy conversion and storage technologies, particularly to a novel perovskite triple conducting oxide (TCO) electrode for use in protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs). The invention specifically addresses the development of a highly active and durable oxygen electrode material, PrNi0.5Co0.5O3−δ (PNC), which facilitates efficient water electrolysis and oxygen reduction reactions at intermediate temperatures (400-600°C). This innovation is aimed at improving the performance and reliability of PCECs for applications in renewable energy systems, such as micro-grids and distributed power generation.

## BACKGROUND

The increasing reliance on renewable energy sources has led to a growing demand for efficient and cost-effective energy conversion and storage technologies. Protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs) are a promising technology for this purpose, as they can operate in a reversible manner to store renewable energy through water electrolysis and convert it back to electricity via fuel cell operation. However, the widespread adoption of PCECs has been hindered by several challenges, including the limited performance and durability of electrode materials, particularly at intermediate temperatures.

Conventional mixed ionic and electronic conducting (MIEC) electrodes in PCECs are typically limited by the slow kinetics of water oxidation and oxygen reduction reactions, which primarily occur at the triple phase boundaries (TPBs) where the electrolyte, electrode, and gas phases meet. This limitation restricts the overall efficiency and operational lifespan of PCECs. To address these issues, there has been a significant focus on developing triple conducting oxides (TCOs) that can extend the reactive sites throughout the entire electrode bulk, thereby enhancing the electrochemical performance.

Despite the potential benefits of TCOs, the development of suitable materials has been challenging due to the difficulty in creating sufficient defects for proton conduction while maintaining structural stability and high electronic conductivity. Recent advancements have led to the identification of a few candidate materials, but their performance and stability remain suboptimal for practical applications.

The present invention introduces a novel TCO electrode material, PrNi0.5Co0.5O3−δ (PNC), which exhibits superior electrochemical performance and durability at intermediate temperatures. This material is designed to overcome the limitations of existing electrode materials by facilitating efficient water electrolysis and oxygen reduction reactions, thereby enabling the self-sustainable and reversible operation of PCECs.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The invention provides a protonic ceramic electrochemical cell (PCEC) comprising a novel triple conducting oxide (TCO) electrode material, PrNi0.5Co0.5O3−δ (PNC). The PNC electrode is characterized by its high activity towards water oxidation and oxygen reduction reactions, excellent durability, and thermal cycling capability. The PCEC incorporating the PNC electrode demonstrates superior electrochemical performance at intermediate temperatures (400-600°C), making it suitable for applications in renewable energy systems.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Triple Conducting Oxide (TCO) Electrode**: The PNC electrode is a perovskite material that exhibits simultaneous proton, oxygen ion, and electron conduction. This unique property extends the reactive sites throughout the entire electrode bulk, enhancing the electrochemical performance.
2. **High Activity and Durability**: The PNC electrode shows excellent catalytic activity towards water oxidation and oxygen reduction reactions, even at intermediate temperatures. It also demonstrates remarkable durability and thermal cycling capability, ensuring long-term stable operation.
3. **Self-Sustainable Reversible Operation**: The PCEC with the PNC electrode can operate in a self-sustainable and reversible manner, converting electrical energy to hydrogen fuel and vice versa without external hydrogen feeding. This feature is crucial for energy storage and conversion applications.
4. **Optimized Microstructure**: The PNC electrode can be further optimized by incorporating a three-dimensional mesh-like structure, which enhances gas diffusion and improves the overall electrochemical performance.

The invention represents a significant advancement in the field of energy conversion and storage technologies, offering a robust and efficient solution for integrating renewable energy sources into micro-grids and distributed power generation systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Composition and Structure of the PNC Electrode

The PNC electrode material, PrNi0.5Co0.5O3−δ, is a perovskite oxide with a single orthorhombic structure (space group Pbnm). The material is synthesized by a wet chemistry method, such as the glycine-citrate combustion process, to ensure a homogeneous distribution of elements and a highly crystalline nature. The crystal structure parameters are as follows: a = 5.405 Å, b = 5.380 Å, and c = 7.617 Å. The PNC material is stable under high-temperature and humid conditions, as evidenced by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA).

### Proton Conductivity and Hydration Behavior

The PNC electrode exhibits significant proton conductivity, which is crucial for efficient water electrolysis and oxygen reduction reactions. The formation of proton defects in the PNC material is facilitated by the presence of intrinsic oxygen vacancies, as confirmed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and density functional theory (DFT) calculations. The hydration behavior of PNC is characterized by high-temperature XRD and TGA, showing a substantial weight increase due to the insertion of water molecules into the atomic lattice. The hydration content of PNC is higher than that of conventional MIEC electrodes, indicating a higher concentration of proton defects.

### Electrochemical Performance

The PCEC incorporating the PNC electrode demonstrates superior electrochemical performance in both fuel cell and electrolysis modes. In fuel cell mode, the PCEC achieves high peak power densities at intermediate temperatures, with values of 528 mW cm² at 600°C, 354 mW cm² at 550°C, and 230 mW cm² at 500°C. In electrolysis mode, the PCEC exhibits high current densities, reaching 1.31 A cm² at 600°C, 0.82 A cm² at 550°C, and 0.62 A cm² at 500°C at an applied voltage of 1.4 V. The cell performance is further enhanced by optimizing the microstructure of the PNC electrode, leading to improved gas diffusion and reduced electrode polarization resistance.

### Durability and Thermal Cycling Capability

The PCEC with the PNC electrode shows excellent long-term stability and thermal cycling capability. The cell performance remains stable over extended periods, with minor improvements observed after 120 hours of continuous operation. The cell also maintains its performance during thermal cycling between 400°C and 600°C, demonstrating the robustness of the PNC electrode material. The Faradaic efficiency of the PCEC is high, with values of 79.3% at 600°C, 83.4% at 550°C, and 86.2% at 500°C, indicating efficient hydrogen production and electricity generation.

### Self-Sustainable Reversible Operation

The PCEC with the PNC electrode can operate in a self-sustainable and reversible manner, alternating between electrolysis and fuel cell modes without external hydrogen feeding. This capability is demonstrated by cycling the cell between electrolysis and fuel cell operations at different current densities, showing stable performance and the ability to generate hydrogen and electricity efficiently. The hydrogen production rate and electricity generation yield are calculated based on current density and Faradaic efficiency, providing a clear indication of the cell's energy storage and conversion capabilities.

### Applications and Advantages

The PCEC with the PNC electrode offers several advantages for renewable energy systems:
1. **Efficient Energy Conversion**: The PNC electrode enables efficient conversion of electrical energy to hydrogen fuel and vice versa, making it suitable for energy storage and distributed power generation.
2. **High Performance at Intermediate Temperatures**: The PCEC operates effectively at intermediate temperatures (400-600°C), reducing the thermal stress on the system and extending its operational lifespan.
3. **Durability and Reliability**: The PNC electrode demonstrates excellent durability and thermal cycling capability, ensuring long-term stable operation.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness**: The use of less expensive and abundant materials in the PNC electrode reduces the overall cost of the PCEC system.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Synthesis and Characterization of PNC Electrode

**Synthesis**: The PNC electrode material was synthesized by the glycine-citrate combustion method. Stoichiometric amounts of Pr(NO3)3·6H2O, Ni(NO3)3·6H2O, and Co(NO3)3·6H2O were dissolved in a citrate-glycine aqueous solution. The viscous gel was heated and ignited to form the ash, which was then calcined at 1000°C in air for 5 hours to obtain the perovskite structure.

**Characterization**: The crystal structure and lattice parameters of the PNC material were confirmed by XRD, showing a single orthorhombic perovskite structure (space group Pbnm). TEM and EDX analysis confirmed the highly crystalline nature and homogeneous element distribution. The oxygen deficiency was determined by iodometric titration, and the hydration behavior was studied by TGA and high-temperature XRD.

#### Example 2: Electrochemical Performance of PCEC with PNC Electrode

**Cell Fabrication**: The PCEC was fabricated by screen-printing the PNC electrode slurry onto a BaCe0.4Zr0.4Y0.1Yb0.1O3 (BCZYYb4411) electrolyte substrate. The active area of the cell was approximately 3.1 cm².

**Fuel Cell Mode**: The PCEC was tested in fuel cell mode with pure hydrogen as the fuel. The cell achieved high peak power densities at intermediate temperatures, with values of 528 mW cm² at 600°C, 354 mW cm² at 550°C, and 230 mW cm² at 500°C.

**Electrolysis Mode**: The PCEC was tested in electrolysis mode with humidified air (~10% H2O) as the reactant gas. The cell exhibited high current densities, reaching 1.31 A cm² at 600°C, 0.82 A cm² at 550°C, and 0.62 A cm² at 500°C at an applied voltage of 1.4 V.

**Stability Testing**: The cell was operated at 1.4 V and 1.6 V for 120 hours, showing stable performance with minor improvements. The cell also maintained its performance during thermal cycling between 400°C and 600°C.

#### Example 3: Self-Sustainable Reversible Operation

**Cycling Experiment**: The PCEC was cycled between electrolysis and fuel cell modes at different current densities. The cell was first operated in electrolysis mode at a constant current density of 0.6 A cm² for three minutes to generate hydrogen, which was then consumed by switching to fuel cell mode at 0.2 A cm² for two minutes. The cell demonstrated stable performance and the ability to generate hydrogen and electricity efficiently without external hydrogen feeding.

**Hydrogen Production and Electricity Generation**: The hydrogen production rate and electricity generation yield were calculated based on current density and Faradaic efficiency. The average hydrogen production rate was 44.7 ml cm² h² at 500°C and 89.3 ml cm² h² at 550°C. The average electricity generation rate was 70 mWh cm² at 500°C and 200 mWh cm² at 550°C, indicating the potential for efficient energy storage and conversion.
