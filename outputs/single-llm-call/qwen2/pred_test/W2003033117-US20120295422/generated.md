# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of semiconductor technology, particularly in the development of light-emitting diodes (LEDs), has seen significant advancements over the past few decades. Gallium nitride (GaN)-based blue and green LEDs have found widespread commercial applications in various sectors, including color digital displays, liquid crystal display backlighting, traffic lamps, vehicle lamps, and general lighting. Despite these achievements, there remains a critical need to reduce costs and improve the efficiency of these devices to make them more competitive with traditional incandescent and fluorescent lamps.

One of the primary factors contributing to the high cost of GaN-based LEDs is the substrate material. Sapphire and silicon carbide (SiC) are commonly used substrates, but they are relatively expensive and introduce complexities in device fabrication processes. Silicon, on the other hand, offers a low-cost alternative due to its availability in large sizes, high surface quality, high conductivity, and well-established processing techniques. However, growing high-quality GaN layers on silicon substrates presents unique challenges, such as thermal mismatch and lattice mismatch, which can lead to defects and reduced performance.

Another critical aspect of improving the efficiency of GaN-based LEDs, especially green LEDs, is the optimization of the indium gallium nitride (InGaN) multiple quantum well (MQW) structures. High-indium content InGaN layers are essential for achieving high efficiency, but they are difficult to grow with good crystal quality. The quality of the InGaN QW material and the well/barrier interfaces significantly impact the performance of the LEDs.

This invention addresses these challenges by providing a method to improve the quality of InGaN QW materials and the well/barrier interfaces in GaN-based green LEDs grown on silicon substrates. Specifically, the invention focuses on the effect of the cap layer thickness on the quality of the InGaN QW material and the well/barrier interface.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for improving the quality of indium gallium nitride (InGaN) quantum well (QW) materials and the well/barrier interfaces in gallium nitride (GaN)-based green light-emitting diodes (LEDs) grown on silicon substrates. The method involves the use of a cap layer with a specific thickness to prevent the decomposition of the InGaN QW during the growth of the GaN barrier and magnesium-doped GaN (GaN:Mg) layer. By optimizing the cap layer thickness, the invention achieves more homogeneous indium distribution, improved well/barrier interfaces, and enhanced photoluminescence (PL) characteristics, leading to higher efficiency and better performance of the green LEDs.

The key aspects of the invention include:
1. **Cap Layer Thickness Optimization**: The invention discloses the use of a cap layer with a thickness of at least 15 Å to effectively prevent the decomposition of the InGaN QW during the high-temperature growth of the GaN barrier and GaN:Mg layer. This optimization ensures that the InGaN QW maintains its structural integrity and indium homogeneity.
2. **Improved Well/Barrier Interfaces**: The optimized cap layer thickness results in more abrupt and well-defined well/barrier interfaces, which are crucial for the efficient operation of the LEDs.
3. **Enhanced Photoluminescence**: The invention demonstrates that the optimized cap layer thickness leads to increased PL intensity, narrower full width at half maximum (FWHM), and a red-shift in the peak wavelength, indicating improved material quality and performance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a method for improving the quality of InGaN quantum well (QW) materials and the well/barrier interfaces in GaN-based green light-emitting diodes (LEDs) grown on silicon substrates. The method involves the use of a cap layer with a specific thickness to prevent the decomposition of the InGaN QW during the growth of the GaN barrier and GaN:Mg layer. By optimizing the cap layer thickness, the invention achieves more homogeneous indium distribution, improved well/barrier interfaces, and enhanced photoluminescence (PL) characteristics, leading to higher efficiency and better performance of the green LEDs.

### Method for Improving InGaN QW Material Quality and Well/Barrier Interfaces

The method according to the invention includes the following steps:

1. **Substrate Preparation**:
   - Silicon (111) substrates are degreased using a mixture of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) in a 3:1 ratio.
   - The substrates are then etched with a 5% hydrofluoric acid (HF) solution to remove surface contamination and oxide layers.

2. **Initial Growth of Buffer Layers**:
   - The substrates are heated in situ at 1100°C in a hydrogen (H2) atmosphere for 30 minutes.
   - Trimethylaluminum (TMAl) is introduced at 800°C to form a thin aluminum (Al) film on the silicon surface, which helps prevent silicon from reacting with metal gallium or ammonia (NH3).
   - An AlN/AlxGa(1−x)N stress-releasing layer and a 3 μm thick silicon-doped GaN (GaN:Si) layer are grown on the substrate.

3. **Growth of InGaN Strain Prerelief Layer and MQWs**:
   - A 200 nm thick InGaN strain prerelief layer is grown on the n-type GaN layer.
   - Six periods of InGaN/GaN MQWs are grown on the n-type GaN layer. Each period consists of an InGaN well layer, a GaN cap layer grown at the same temperature as the well, and a GaN barrier layer grown at a temperature 120°C higher than the well.

4. **Optimization of Cap Layer Thickness**:
   - The cap layer thickness is varied to determine the optimal thickness for preventing InGaN decomposition and improving the well/barrier interfaces.
   - Three different cap layer thicknesses are tested: 8 Å, 15 Å, and 30 Å. These samples are designated as Sample A, Sample B, and Sample C, respectively.

5. **Characterization of MQW Quality**:
   - Double crystal X-ray diffractometry (DCXRD) is used to analyze the structural quality of the MQWs.
   - Fluorescence microscopy (FLM) is used to obtain fluorescence images of the QWs under different excitation wavelengths.
   - Photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy is used to characterize the optical performance of the InGaN/GaN MQWs.

### Characterization Results

#### X-ray Diffraction (XRD) Analysis

XRD Omega/2theta rocking scans were performed to evaluate the quality of the MQWs. The scan range was from −5000 arcsec to +5000 arcsec, with the GaN (0002) diffraction peak set as the center of the rocking curve. The results showed that:

- **Sample A (8 Å Cap Layer)**: No clear satellite peaks were observed on the left side of the diffraction curve, indicating that the InGaN QW was damaged during the temperature ramping process. The 0 order peak from the well InxGa(1−x)N (0002) diffraction could not be resolved, suggesting heterogeneous indium distribution and poor QW quality.
- **Sample B (15 Å Cap Layer)**: Clear satellite peaks were observed, indicating the formation of a fine QW/QB periodic structure and abrupt QW/QB interfaces. The 0 order peak from the well InxGa(1−x)N (0002) diffraction was visible, although it was not as distinct due to the limited resolution of the X-ray diffractometer.
- **Sample C (30 Å Cap Layer)**: The satellite peaks were even more pronounced, and the 0 order peak from the well InxGa(1−x)N (0002) diffraction was more distinct. This indicates that the thicker cap layer provided better protection for the InGaN QW, resulting in improved material quality.

#### Fluorescence Microscopy (FLM) Analysis

FLM images were obtained to visualize the indium distribution in the InGaN QWs. The results showed that:

- **Sample A (8 Å Cap Layer)**: The FLM image was uneven, with extra-bright points distributed across the image. This indicates that the InGaN QW decomposed during the high-temperature growth of the GaN barrier and GaN:Mg layer.
- **Sample B (15 Å Cap Layer)**: The FLM image was more uniform, with fewer bright points. This suggests that the 15 Å cap layer provided some protection against InGaN decomposition.
- **Sample C (30 Å Cap Layer)**: The FLM image was the most uniform, with no significant bright points. This indicates that the 30 Å cap layer effectively prevented InGaN decomposition, leading to a more homogeneous indium distribution.

#### Photoluminescence (PL) Analysis

PL spectra were measured to evaluate the optical performance of the InGaN/GaN MQWs. The results showed that:

- **Sample A (8 Å Cap Layer)**: The PL spectrum had a peak wavelength around 500 nm, with a broad FWHM and low intensity. The spectrum also exhibited interference candy stripes due to optical cavity effects.
- **Sample B (15 Å Cap Layer)**: The PL spectrum had a peak wavelength around 515 nm, with a narrower FWHM and higher intensity compared to Sample A. The interference candy stripes were less pronounced.
- **Sample C (30 Å Cap Layer)**: The PL spectrum had a peak wavelength around 530 nm, with the narrowest FWHM and the highest intensity among the three samples. The interference candy stripes were minimal, indicating improved material quality and performance.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, the present invention provides a method for improving the quality of InGaN QW materials and the well/barrier interfaces in GaN-based green LEDs grown on silicon substrates. By optimizing the cap layer thickness to at least 15 Å, the invention prevents the decomposition of the InGaN QW during the high-temperature growth of the GaN barrier and GaN:Mg layer. This leads to more homogeneous indium distribution, improved well/barrier interfaces, and enhanced photoluminescence characteristics, resulting in higher efficiency and better performance of the green LEDs.
