# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel male sterility-fertility restoration system in plants. Specifically, the invention pertains to a method for achieving high-level, spatio-temporal, and stringent expression of a desired gene in the postmeiotic tapetum of plants, and a mechanism for the controlled abolition of this expression to restore male fertility. The system is particularly useful for hybrid seed production in agriculture.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION AND PRIOR ART

### LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ART

Male sterility and fertility restoration systems are crucial for hybrid seed production in agriculture. Various methods have been developed to induce male sterility, including the use of chemical treatments, temperature-sensitive mutations, and genetic engineering. However, these methods often suffer from limitations such as incomplete sterility, partial restoration of fertility, and the need for specific restorer genes. For instance, the barnase/barstar system, while effective, requires the identification of efficient restorer lines, which can be challenging. Other systems, such as those based on pathogenesis-related (PR) β-1,3-glucanase genes, have shown only partial restoration of fertility. Additionally, some systems involve the use of transgenes of foreign origin, which can raise biosafety concerns in certain regions.

### Objectives of the Present Invention

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a robust and efficient male sterility-fertility restoration system in plants. The system aims to achieve the following goals:
1. **High-Level and Stringent Expression**: Ensure high-level and spatio-temporal expression of a desired gene in the postmeiotic tapetum of plants.
2. **Complete Male Sterility**: Induce complete male sterility by expressing a male sterility gene, such as BECLIN1, in the tapetum.
3. **Controlled Abolition of Expression**: Develop a mechanism to abolish the expression of the male sterility gene in the F1 hybrid, thereby restoring male fertility.
4. **Generic Application**: Design a system that can be applied to various plant species and different male sterility genes.
5. **Commercial Viability**: Ensure that the system is suitable for large-scale hybrid seed production without significant yield penalties or biosafety concerns.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel male sterility-fertility restoration system in plants. The system comprises two main components:
1. **Female Component**: A construct for high-level, spatio-temporal, and stringent expression of a male sterility gene in the postmeiotic tapetum.
2. **Male Component**: A construct for the controlled abolition of the male sterility gene expression in the F1 hybrid, thereby restoring male fertility.

The female component includes a transcriptional control system based on the TGTA-TBPm3 complementation system, where the TATA box binding protein mutant (TBPm3) complements the TGTA mutation to drive high-level expression of the desired gene. The expression is further controlled by fusing TBPm3 with the N-terminal domain of HFR1 (HFR1NT131), which is targeted for degradation by COP1, thus limiting the availability of TBPm3.

The male component involves the expression of COP1 or a mutant form of COP1 (COP1L105A) under the control of a tapetum-specific promoter. COP1L105A, which has increased nuclear localization, effectively degrades the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 complex, leading to the complete abolition of the male sterility gene expression in the F1 hybrid.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### High-Level, Stringent, and Spatio-Temporal Expression in the Postmeiotic Tapetum

The invention utilizes the TGTA-TBPm3 complementation system to achieve high-level and stringent expression of a desired gene in the postmeiotic tapetum. The TATA box binding protein mutant (TBPm3) complements the TGTA mutation in the promoter, driving high-level expression. To further control the expression, TBPm3 is fused with the N-terminal domain of HFR1 (HFR1NT131), which is targeted for degradation by COP1. This fusion limits the availability of TBPm3, thereby controlling the expression of the desired gene.

### Transcriptional Abolition of the TGTA-TBPm3 System

To achieve transcriptional control over the TGTA-TBPm3 system, the invention employs COP1 or a mutant form of COP1 (COP1L105A). COP1 is a repressor of light-mediated photomorphogenesis that physically interacts with HFR1 (and HFR1NT131) and mediates its targeted degradation through the proteosome. By expressing COP1 under the control of a tapetum-specific promoter, the invention ensures that the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 complex is degraded, leading to the abolition of the desired gene expression.

### Complete Male Sterility by High-Level Expression of BECLIN1

The invention demonstrates the use of Arabidopsis BECLIN1/ATG6 as a male sterility gene. High-level expression of BECLIN1 in the tapetum under the control of the TGTA-TBPm3 complementation system leads to complete male sterility in tobacco. The expression of BECLIN1 results in abnormal tapetal growth and delayed degeneration, leading to the formation of non-viable pollen.

### Complete Restoration of Male Fertility in F1 Hybrid

To restore male fertility in the F1 hybrid, the male sterile female lines are crossed with male lines expressing either COP1 or COP1L105A. The expression of COP1L105A, which has increased nuclear localization, completely abolishes the expression of BECLIN1 in the F1 hybrid, leading to the restoration of male fertility. The F1 hybrid shows normal pollen viability, germination, and seed setting, comparable to control plants.

### Examples

#### Example 1: High-Level Expression of gusA in the Postmeiotic Tapetum

Transgenic tobacco lines harboring the expression cassette 1370, carrying the TGTA-driven gusA gene and HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion, were developed. The gusA expression was examined in the anthers of stages 2 and 3. The results showed high-level and stringent expression of gusA in the postmeiotic tapetum, with no expression detected in other plant organs.

#### Example 2: Transcriptional Abolition of the TGTA-TBPm3 System

Transgenic tobacco lines expressing COP1 under the control of the Arabidopsis tapetum-specific promoter A9 (PA9) were developed. The F1 progeny obtained by crossing female 1370 lines with male 1372 plants showed a 4-6-fold reduction in GUS activity in the anthers of stages 2 and 3, indicating partial control over the TGTA-TBPm3 system. When COP1L105A was used instead of COP1, the expression of gusA was completely abolished in the F1 hybrid.

#### Example 3: Complete Male Sterility by High-Level Expression of BECLIN1

Transgenic tobacco lines expressing BECLIN1 under the control of the TGTA-TBPm3 complementation system were developed. The transgenic lines showed complete male sterility, with no viable pollen and normal female reproductive organs. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analyses confirmed the abnormal tapetal growth and delayed degeneration in the transgenic lines.

#### Example 4: Complete Restoration of Male Fertility in F1 Hybrid

The male sterile 1371 transgenic female lines were crossed with male lines expressing either COP1 (1372) or COP1L105A (1373) to raise F1 and F'1 progeny, respectively. The F1 plants showed partial restoration of male fertility, with low pollen viability and germination but normal seed setting. The F'1 plants, obtained by crossing with COP1L105A lines, showed complete restoration of male fertility, with normal pollen viability, germination, and seed setting.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a robust and efficient male sterility-fertility restoration system in plants. The system achieves high-level, spatio-temporal, and stringent expression of a desired gene in the postmeiotic tapetum, induces complete male sterility by expressing a male sterility gene, and restores male fertility in the F1 hybrid through the controlled abolition of the male sterility gene expression. The system is generic and can be applied to various plant species and different male sterility genes, making it a valuable tool for hybrid seed production in agriculture.
