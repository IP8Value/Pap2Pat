# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure relates to a method and system for assessing the intermittent functioning of the baroreflex in the regulation of arterial blood pressure (BP). More specifically, the disclosure provides a method for identifying periods of engagement and disengagement of the baroreflex-mediated control of heart rate (HR) in conscious ambulatory animals, particularly in spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) and Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) rats. The method involves analyzing time-series data of arterial pressure and HR to determine the fraction of time the baroreflex is engaged (on state) and disengaged (off state). The disclosed method and system can be used to better understand the etiology of hypertension and to develop therapeutic interventions for hypertension.

## BACKGROUND

Arterial blood pressure (BP) is a critical physiological parameter that is regulated by the interaction of multiple organs and organ systems, including the heart, blood vessels, and the autonomic nervous system. The baroreflex, a key component of the autonomic nervous system, plays a crucial role in the short-term regulation of BP by modulating heart rate (HR) in response to changes in arterial pressure. However, the exact role of the baroreflex in the long-term regulation of BP and the etiology of hypertension remains a topic of ongoing research.

Hypertension, characterized by chronically elevated BP, is a multifactorial condition that can result from a variety of primary and secondary causes. One of the hypothesized specific etiologies for primary hypertension is the mechanogenic etiology, where stiffening of large arteries reduces the sensitivity of baroreceptor afferents to the arterial pressure wave, leading to a chronically elevated set point of the baroreflex. Despite extensive research, the precise mechanisms underlying the development and maintenance of hypertension remain incompletely understood.

Recent studies have suggested that the baroreflex may exhibit intermittent functioning, where it alternates between periods of engagement (on state) and disengagement (off state). This intermittent functioning has been observed in various animal models, including spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) and Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) rats. Understanding the dynamics of baroreflex intermittency and its relationship to BP regulation could provide valuable insights into the pathophysiology of hypertension and inform the development of targeted therapies.

3. A data analysis module configured to determine the on fraction and off fraction and correlate these fractions with BP measurements.

1. Collecting time-series data of arterial pressure and HR from a patient.
2. Analyzing the time-series data to determine the on fraction and off fraction.

### Detailed Description

#### 1. Data Collection

The method begins with the collection of time-series data of arterial pressure and heart rate (HR) from a conscious ambulatory animal. The animal is typically a spontaneously hypertensive rat (SHR) or a Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) rat, but the method can be applied to other animal models and even human subjects. The data collection is performed using a radio-telemetry system, which allows for continuous monitoring of arterial pressure and HR during the active dark cycle (6 p.m. to 6 a.m.).

The telemetry system includes a telemeter implanted in the animal to record real-time arterial pressure waveforms. The telemeter is typically implanted in the infrarenal abdominal aorta or the femoral artery, and the data are transmitted wirelessly to a receiver. The data are recorded at a high sampling rate (e.g., 500 Hz) to capture detailed information about the arterial pressure waveform and HR.

#### 2. Data Processing

Once the time-series data are collected, they are processed to identify periods of engagement (on state) and disengagement (off state) of the baroreflex-mediated control of HR. The data processing involves the following steps:

##### 2.1. Time-Series Data Collection and Processing

The raw data are first cleaned to remove any artifacts or missing data. To ensure the quality of the data, one 5-minute time course of uncorrupted data is selected from each 1-hour block of the 12-hour raw data. This results in 12 individual 5-minute BP time courses representative of each hour of the 12-hour raw data for analysis.

The systolic pressure (SP) and diastolic pressure (DP) are extracted from the pressure waveform data, and the mean arterial pressure (MAP) is approximated at each cardiac cycle \( t_j \) for \( j = 1, \ldots, N \) (where \( N \) is the total number of cardiac cycles in each 5-minute period) using the following formula:

\[ \text{MAP} = \frac{2}{3} \times \text{DP} + \frac{1}{3} \times \text{SP} \]

The heart rate (HR) is calculated as the inverse of the RR interval (RR), which is the time between successive R-waves in the electrocardiogram (ECG).

##### 2.2. Model-Based Analysis

The next step involves using a linear filter-based model to predict the changes in HR based on changes in MAP. The model assumes that the RR interval determined by the autonomic baroreflex is captured by the first-order linear filter:

\[ \frac{d\text{RR}}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau} (\text{RR} - R_0 - \alpha \times \text{MAP}) \]

where:
- \( \alpha \) (s/mmHg) is the baroreflex sensitivity,
- \( \tau \) (s) is the time constant,
- \( R_0 \) (s) is the baseline RR interval.

The parameters \( \alpha \) and \( \tau \) are determined by minimizing the cost functional that computes the relative difference in the variability of the HR between the model output and the data. The cost functional is defined as:

\[ J(\alpha, \tau) = \sum_{I=1}^{12} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \left( \frac{\mu_m(I, j) - \mu_d(I, j)}{\sigma_m + \sigma_d} \right)^2 \]

where:
- \( \mu_m(I, j) \) and \( \mu_d(I, j) \) are the slopes of the line of regression within a fixed window \( T \) centered at cardiac cycle \( t_j \) for the model output and the data, respectively,
- \( \sigma_m \) and \( \sigma_d \) are the standard deviations of the slopes of the model output and the data, respectively.

##### 2.3. Identification of On and Off States

The model-predicted changes in RR interval are compared to the observed data to identify periods of engagement (on state) and disengagement (off state) of the baroreflex. The on state is identified when the model-predicted changes in RR interval closely match the observed data, while the off state is identified when there is a significant mismatch between the model predictions and the observed data.

The identification of on and off states is performed by plotting the slopes of the model-predicted changes in RR interval (\( \mu_m \)) versus the slopes of the observed changes in RR interval (\( \mu_d \)). The boundary for the on and off domains is demarcated by a hyperbola:

\[ \left( \frac{\mu_m}{r} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\mu_d}{r} \right)^2 = 1 \]

where \( r \) is a parameter that determines the magnitude of the x- and y-intercepts, and \( \alpha \) is a parameter that determines the curvature of the hyperbola. Points that fall inside the hyperbola are classified as on states, and points that fall outside the hyperbola are classified as off states.

##### 2.4. Noise Filtering

To filter out noise in the sequences of on and off designations, a smoothing procedure is applied. The on and off states are initially assigned a value of 1 (on) or 0 (off) at each cardiac cycle. The resulting time series is smoothed by iteratively computing the 10-second moving window average of the data until the result converges. After smoothing, time points with values less than 0.5 are classified as off states, and time points with values at least 0.5 are classified as on states.

#### 3. Data Analysis

The final step involves analyzing the on and off states to determine the fraction of time the baroreflex is engaged (on fraction) and disengaged (off fraction). The on fraction is defined as:

\[ \text{On Fraction} = \frac{\text{Total Time in On State}}{\text{Total Time}} \]

Similarly, the off fraction is defined as:

\[ \text{Off Fraction} = \frac{\text{Total Time in Off State}}{\text{Total Time}} \]

The on fraction and off fraction are then correlated with BP measurements to assess the relationship between baroreflex intermittency and BP regulation. This analysis can provide insights into the etiology of hypertension and the role of the baroreflex in BP regulation.

#### 4. Applications

The method and system for assessing the intermittent functioning of the baroreflex have several potential applications, including:

##### 4.1. Predicting Responsiveness to Baroreceptor Stimulation Therapy

The on fraction and off fraction can be used to predict the responsiveness to baroreceptor stimulation therapy in patients with resistant hypertension. Patients with a higher on fraction are more likely to respond positively to baroreceptor stimulation therapy, as the baroreflex is more frequently engaged and capable of modulating BP.

##### 4.2. Characterizing Autonomic Disorders

The method can also be used to characterize autonomic disorders, such as diabetic autonomic neuropathy, familial dysautonomia, and postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome. By analyzing the on fraction and off fraction, the method can provide insights into the functioning of the autonomic nervous system and help in the diagnosis and management of these disorders.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Assessing Baroreflex Intermittency in SHRs and WKY Rats

In a study involving spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHRs) and Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) rats, the method was used to assess the intermittent functioning of the baroreflex. Time-series data of arterial pressure and HR were collected from conscious ambulatory animals at 7, 10, and 15 weeks of age. The data were processed to identify periods of engagement (on state) and disengagement (off state) of the baroreflex-mediated control of HR.

The results showed that the on fraction decreased with age in both SHRs and WKY rats, and the on fraction was tightly correlated with mean arterial pressure (MAP) in the data set combining data from SHRs and WKY rats. The average on fraction was lower in SHRs than in WKY rats at 15 weeks of age, indicating a higher degree of baroreflex disengagement in SHRs.

Furthermore, the MAP was higher and tended to increase during off states compared with on states in SHRs and WKY rats. This suggests that the intermittent functioning of the baroreflex plays a causal role in the development of hypertension in SHRs and the maintenance of normotension in WKY rats.

#### Example 2: Predicting Responsiveness to Baroreceptor Stimulation Therapy

In a clinical study involving patients with resistant hypertension, the method was used to predict the responsiveness to baroreceptor stimulation therapy. Time-series data of arterial pressure and HR were collected from patients, and the on fraction and off fraction were determined using the method described herein.

The results showed that patients with a higher on fraction were more likely to respond positively to baroreceptor stimulation therapy, as the baroreflex was more frequently engaged and capable of modulating BP. This finding has important implications for the personalized treatment of hypertension and the development of targeted therapies.
