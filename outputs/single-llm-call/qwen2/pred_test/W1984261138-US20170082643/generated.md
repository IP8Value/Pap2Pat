# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of metabolic disorders, particularly to methods and compositions for identifying and treating insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. More specifically, the invention provides a biomarker, 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA), and its use in assessing adipose tissue nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) expression and insulin resistance. The invention also encompasses methods for reducing adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels through interventions such as exercise and bariatric surgery.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention

Increased abdominal adiposity, especially the expansion of visceral adipose tissue, has been identified as a major risk factor for the development of insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. The mechanisms by which visceral adiposity contributes to the development of type 2 diabetes are not fully understood, but proposed mechanisms include macrophage deposition and activation, and the release of proinflammatory cytokines and non-esterified fatty acids (NEFA) that exacerbate systemic insulin resistance and loss of beta cell function.

Nicotinamide, the amide form of niacin or vitamin B3, is a precursor in the synthesis of NAD+ and NADP+, which play roles in energy production, regulation of cellular redox state, circadian rhythm, and longevity. Prolonged high intake of niacin or nicotinamide has been reported to reduce insulin sensitivity in individuals with normal or impaired glucose tolerance. Chronic treatment with a combination of extended-release niacin and laropiprant has been associated with a significant increase in new-onset diabetes and disturbed glucose control in patients with established type 2 diabetes. However, the mechanisms linking nicotinamide metabolism to insulin resistance are unknown.

Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) is the major nicotinamide-metabolizing enzyme, catalyzing the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the ring nitrogen of nicotinamide, yielding 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) and S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH). NNMT is primarily expressed in the liver but is also found in other organs, including adipose tissue. Increased NNMT catalytic activity has been observed in adipose tissue of mice fed a high-fat diet, and NNMT expression is upregulated in mouse models of obesity and diabetes. Reduction in liver and adipose tissue NNMT expression has been shown to protect against the development of diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance.

Despite these findings, it remains unclear whether plasma MNA concentrations are associated with adipose tissue NNMT expression, impaired insulin sensitivity, or type 2 diabetes in humans. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a method to assess adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance using plasma MNA levels as a biomarker. Additionally, the invention provides methods for reducing adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels through interventions such as exercise and bariatric surgery, thereby improving insulin sensitivity and reducing the risk of type 2 diabetes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Adipose tissue**: Connective tissue composed of adipocytes (fat cells) that store energy in the form of fat. Adipose tissue is found in various locations in the body, including subcutaneous and visceral regions.
- **Insulin resistance**: A condition in which cells fail to respond normally to the hormone insulin, leading to higher blood glucose levels and an increased risk of type 2 diabetes.
- **Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT)**: An enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the ring nitrogen of nicotinamide, producing 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) and S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH).
- **1-Methylnicotinamide (MNA)**: A metabolite of nicotinamide produced by the action of NNMT.
- **Biological sample**: Any sample obtained from a subject, including but not limited to blood, plasma, serum, urine, and tissue biopsy.
- **Exercise**: Physical activity designed to improve health and fitness, including but not limited to aerobic exercise, strength training, and flexibility exercises.
- **Bariatric surgery**: Surgical procedures performed on the stomach or intestines to induce weight loss, including but not limited to gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy, and adjustable gastric banding.

### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

#### Assessment of Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Insulin Resistance

The present invention provides a method for assessing adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance in a subject by measuring the concentration of MNA in a biological sample from the subject. The method comprises the following steps:

1. **Obtaining a Biological Sample**: A biological sample is obtained from the subject. Suitable biological samples include blood, plasma, serum, and urine. The sample should be collected under standardized conditions to ensure consistency and accuracy of the measurements.

2. **Measuring MNA Concentration**: The concentration of MNA in the biological sample is measured using a suitable analytical method. Preferred methods include liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The choice of method depends on the availability of equipment and the desired level of sensitivity and specificity.

3. **Comparing MNA Concentration to a Reference Value**: The measured MNA concentration is compared to a reference value. The reference value can be derived from a population of healthy individuals or from a predefined threshold value. If the measured MNA concentration is significantly higher than the reference value, it indicates increased adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance.

4. **Determining the Presence or Absence of Increased Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Insulin Resistance**: Based on the comparison, the presence or absence of increased adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance is determined. Subjects with increased MNA levels may benefit from interventions to reduce adipose tissue NNMT expression and improve insulin sensitivity.

#### Reducing Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Plasma MNA Levels

The present invention also provides methods for reducing adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels through interventions such as exercise and bariatric surgery. The methods comprise the following steps:

1. **Administering an Intervention**: An intervention is administered to the subject. Suitable interventions include exercise, bariatric surgery, and combinations thereof. Exercise can be tailored to the subject's fitness level and may include aerobic activities, strength training, and flexibility exercises. Bariatric surgery options include gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy, and adjustable gastric banding.

2. **Measuring MNA Concentration After the Intervention**: The concentration of MNA in a biological sample from the subject is measured after the intervention. The reduction in MNA concentration indicates a decrease in adipose tissue NNMT expression and an improvement in insulin sensitivity.

#### Treating Insulin Resistance and Type 2 Diabetes

The present invention further provides a method for treating insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes in a subject. The method comprises the following steps:

1. **Administering an Intervention**: An intervention is administered to the subject, as described above.

2. **Monitoring MNA Concentration**: The concentration of MNA in a biological sample from the subject is monitored over time to assess the improvement in insulin sensitivity and reduction in the risk of type 2 diabetes. Regular monitoring allows for the adjustment of the intervention as needed to optimize outcomes.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Assessment of Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Insulin Resistance

**Objective**: To assess adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance in a cohort of subjects with varying degrees of glucose tolerance.

**Methods**:
1. **Study Population**: A cross-sectional cohort of 199 individuals undergoing abdominal surgery for weight reduction, cholecystectomy, or explorative laparoscopy was included in the study. Participants were categorized into groups based on their glucose tolerance: normal glucose tolerance (NGT), impaired glucose tolerance (IGT), and type 2 diabetes (T2D).
2. **Sample Collection**: Paired samples of subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue were collected during surgery and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen.
3. **MNA Measurement**: Plasma MNA concentrations were measured using LC-MS/MS.
4. **NNMT Expression Analysis**: Adipose tissue NNMT mRNA expression was measured by quantitative real-time RT-PCR.
5. **Statistical Analysis**: Data were analyzed using Student's t-test, paired t-test, Spearman's correlation coefficients, and linear regression modeling.

**Results**:
- **NNMT Expression**: NNMT expression was significantly higher in both subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue of subjects with T2D compared to those with NGT.
- **MNA Concentration**: Plasma MNA concentrations were positively correlated with adipose tissue NNMT expression in subjects with T2D but not in those with NGT.
- **Insulin Sensitivity**: In a subgroup of subjects with hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic clamp data, NNMT expression in omental adipose tissue was significantly higher in insulin-resistant individuals compared to insulin-sensitive individuals.

**Conclusion**: The results demonstrate that increased adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels are associated with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. MNA can serve as a biomarker for assessing adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance.

#### Example 2: Effect of Exercise on Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Insulin Sensitivity

**Objective**: To evaluate the effect of a 12-week exercise intervention on adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin sensitivity in subjects with varying degrees of glucose tolerance.

**Methods**:
1. **Study Population**: A cohort of 60 individuals with NGT, IGT, and T2D participated in a 12-week exercise program consisting of three 1-hour sessions per week.
2. **Sample Collection**: Subcutaneous adipose tissue biopsies were collected before and after the exercise intervention.
3. **MNA Measurement**: Plasma MNA concentrations were measured using LC-MS/MS.
4. **NNMT Expression Analysis**: Adipose tissue NNMT mRNA expression was measured by quantitative real-time RT-PCR.
5. **Statistical Analysis**: Data were analyzed using paired t-tests and correlation coefficients.

**Results**:
- **NNMT Expression**: The exercise intervention led to a significant decrease in subcutaneous adipose tissue NNMT expression in subjects with IGT and T2D but not in those with NGT.
- **MNA Concentration**: Plasma MNA concentrations were negatively correlated with insulin sensitivity in subjects with IGT and T2D.
- **Insulin Sensitivity**: The exercise intervention significantly improved insulin sensitivity in all groups, as measured by the glucose infusion rate (GIR) during hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic clamp.

**Conclusion**: The results show that exercise can reduce adipose tissue NNMT expression and improve insulin sensitivity in subjects with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes.

#### Example 3: Effect of Bariatric Surgery on Adipose Tissue NNMT Expression and Insulin Sensitivity

**Objective**: To evaluate the effect of a two-step bariatric surgery procedure on adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin sensitivity in morbidly obese subjects.

**Methods**:
1. **Study Population**: A cohort of 55 morbidly obese individuals underwent a two-step bariatric surgery procedure: sleeve gastrectomy as the first step and Roux-en-Y gastric bypass as the second step 12 months later.
2. **Sample Collection**: Subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue biopsies and plasma samples were collected at the first and second surgeries.
3. **MNA Measurement**: Plasma MNA concentrations were measured using LC-MS/MS.
4. **NNMT Expression Analysis**: Adipose tissue NNMT mRNA expression was measured by quantitative real-time RT-PCR.
5. **Statistical Analysis**: Data were analyzed using paired t-tests and correlation coefficients.

**Results**:
- **NNMT Expression**: Both subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue NNMT expression were significantly reduced after the second surgery.
- **MNA Concentration**: Plasma MNA concentrations were significantly reduced after the second surgery.
- **Insulin Sensitivity**: The bariatric surgery procedure led to significant improvements in body weight, glucose homeostasis, and insulin sensitivity.

**Conclusion**: The results demonstrate that bariatric surgery can reduce adipose tissue NNMT expression and plasma MNA levels, leading to improved insulin sensitivity and reduced risk of type 2 diabetes.
