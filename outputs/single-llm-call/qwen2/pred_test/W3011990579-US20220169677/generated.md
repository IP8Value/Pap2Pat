## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure relates to novel peptidomimetic compounds that inhibit the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and neuropilin-1 (NRP-1). More specifically, the disclosure provides peptidomimetic compounds with enhanced affinity for NRP-1, methods for their preparation, pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds, and therapeutic uses thereof, particularly in the treatment of diseases associated with pathological angiogenesis, such as cancer.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) is a cell surface receptor that plays a critical role in the development of axon guidance and physiological and pathological angiogenesis processes, including cancer. NRP-1 acts as a co-receptor for class 3 semaphorins and multiple growth factors, including epidermal growth factor (EGF), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β). By enhancing the activity of the respective receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) in cell signaling systems, NRP-1 significantly influences the modulation of angiogenesis.

Vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) is a key modulator of angiogenesis, and NRP-1 enhances its binding to VEGFR-2, thereby promoting blood vessel formation. Increased expression of NRP-1 on the surface of cancerous cells is often associated with poor prognosis, highlighting the importance of developing NRP-1 inhibitors for therapeutic purposes.

The C-terminal Arg of the VEGF-A165 fragment, encoded by exons 7 and 8, is crucial for protein interaction with NRP-1. Screening of phage libraries has identified peptides with a R/KXXR/K consensus motif at their C-terminus, which exhibit high binding affinity to NRP-1. The strict requirements of this motif, known as the C-end rule (CendR), are essential for NRP-1 binding. However, recent studies have shown that the addition of cysteine (Cys) residues at the N-terminus of these peptides can further enhance their affinity for NRP-1, leading to the concept of the cysteine rule (CysR).

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)

The compounds of the present disclosure can be prepared using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) techniques. The synthesis typically involves the following steps:

1. **Resin Preparation**: Fmoc-protected amino acid resins, such as Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin, are used as the solid support.
2. **Coupling Reactions**: Amino acids are coupled to the resin using coupling reagents such as HATU and DIPEA in DMF. The coupling is repeated for each amino acid in the sequence.
3. **Deprotection**: Fmoc groups are removed using 20% piperidine in DMF.
4. **Final Cleavage**: The fully assembled peptide is cleaved from the resin using a TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS mixture.
5. **Purification**: The crude peptide is purified using reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC).
6. **Characterization**: The purity and identity of the final compounds are confirmed using analytical RP-HPLC, high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS), and tandem mass spectrometry (HRMS/MS).

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of Peptidomimetic Compounds

#### Materials

- Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.39 mmol/g)
- Amino acids (Fmoc-protected)
- HATU (2 eq)
- DIPEA (5 eq)
- DMF (solvent)
- TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (cleavage reagent)
- RP-HPLC system
- HRMS and HRMS/MS instruments

#### Procedure

1. **Resin Preparation**: Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin (0.5 g) was swollen in DMF.
2. **Coupling Reactions**: Amino acids were sequentially coupled to the resin using HATU and DIPEA in DMF. Each coupling was monitored using the Kaiser or chloranil test to ensure completion.
3. **Deprotection**: Fmoc groups were removed using 20% piperidine in DMF.
4. **Final Cleavage**: The fully assembled peptide was cleaved from the resin using a TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS mixture (88:5:5:2, v:w:v:v) for 3 hours.
5. **Purification**: The crude peptide was purified using RP-HPLC.
6. **Characterization**: The purity and identity of the final compounds were confirmed using analytical RP-HPLC, HRMS, and HRMS/MS.

### Example 2: Inhibition of VEGF-A165/NRP-1 Complex Formation

#### Materials

- Recombinant human NRP-1
- Biotinylated human VEGF-A165
- Streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase conjugate
- Chemiluminescent substrate
- Microplate reader

#### Procedure

1. **Coating**: A 96-well plate was coated with 100 μL (200 ng/well) of recombinant human NRP-1 and incubated overnight at 4°C.
2. **Blocking**: Non-specific binding sites were blocked with 0.5% BSA in PBS.
3. **Binding Assay**: Peptides in PBS (50 μL) at various concentrations and biotinylated VEGF-A165 (50 μL, 400 ng/mL) in PBS containing 4 μg/mL heparin were added to the wells and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature.
4. **Detection**: Wells were treated with streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase conjugate and incubated for 45 minutes. After washing, 100 μL of chemiluminescent substrate was added, and luminescence was measured using a microplate reader.
5. **Data Analysis**: Percentages of inhibition were calculated using the formula: \(100\% - \left[ \frac{(S - NS)}{(P - NS)} \times 100\% \right]\), where S is the signal intensity obtained from wells incubated with the peptide, P is the signal intensity from wells incubated with VEGF-A165 only, and NS is the signal intensity from non-coated wells.

### Example 3: Enzymatic Stability in Human Plasma

#### Materials

- Human blood plasma
- Ethanol
- RP-HPLC system
- LC-MS instrument

#### Procedure

1. **Preparation**: Human blood plasma was preactivated in an Eppendorf ThermoMixer for 20 minutes at 37°C (350 rpm).
2. **Incubation**: Peptides (6 mg/mL) were mixed with human plasma (~1 μmol/mL final concentration) and incubated at 37°C for up to 96 hours.
3. **Sample Collection**: At selected time intervals, 100 μL of the mixture was collected, and proteins were precipitated by adding 100 μL of 98% ethanol and vortexing for 1 minute.
4. **Analysis**: Samples were centrifuged, and the supernatant was lyophilized and reconstituted in water. The samples were then analyzed using RP-HPLC and LC-MS to determine the concentration of the peptides and their metabolites.
