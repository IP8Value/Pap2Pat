# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Pompe disease, also known as glycogen storage disease type II, is a rare genetic disorder characterized by the deficiency of acid α-glucosidase (GAA), an enzyme responsible for breaking down glycogen in the lysosomes of muscle cells. The accumulation of glycogen leads to progressive muscle weakness and, in severe cases, respiratory failure and death. Current treatments for Pompe disease primarily involve enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) with recombinant human GAA (rhGAA). However, the efficacy of this treatment is limited by the inefficient delivery of the enzyme to the lysosomes of affected cells. The present invention addresses this limitation by providing a novel enzyme replacement therapy that utilizes a glycosylation-independent lysosomal targeting (GILT) tag to enhance the delivery of GAA to the lysosomes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel enzyme replacement therapy for Pompe disease. The invention involves the fusion of a glycosylation-independent lysosomal targeting (GILT) tag to the N-terminus of human acid α-glucosidase (GAA). The GILT tag, derived from the insulin-like growth factor II (IGF-II), enables the GAA enzyme to bind to the cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor (CI-MPR) with high affinity, thereby enhancing its uptake and delivery to the lysosomes of target cells. The resulting chimeric enzyme, referred to as GILT-tagged GAA, demonstrates significantly improved glycogen clearance in Pompe mouse models compared to untagged rhGAA. The invention further includes methods for producing the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme and its use in treating Pompe disease.

## DEFINITIONS

- **Acid α-Glucosidase (GAA):** An enzyme responsible for breaking down glycogen in the lysosomes of muscle cells.
- **Cation-Independent Mannose 6-Phosphate Receptor (CI-MPR):** A receptor that facilitates the transport of lysosomal enzymes from the Golgi apparatus to the lysosomes.
- **Glycosylation-Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) Tag:** A peptide tag derived from IGF-II that enhances the binding of GAA to the CI-MPR, thereby improving its lysosomal delivery.
- **Enzyme Replacement Therapy (ERT):** A medical treatment for lysosomal storage disorders involving the administration of a functional enzyme to replace the deficient or dysfunctional enzyme in the patient's body.
- **Pompe Disease:** A rare genetic disorder characterized by the deficiency of GAA, leading to the accumulation of glycogen in muscle cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Lysosomal Enzymes

Lysosomal enzymes play a crucial role in the breakdown of macromolecules within the lysosomes of cells. Deficiencies in these enzymes can lead to the accumulation of undigested substrates, causing various lysosomal storage disorders. Acid α-glucosidase (GAA) is one such enzyme that is essential for the degradation of glycogen. In Pompe disease, the deficiency of GAA results in the accumulation of glycogen in the lysosomes of muscle cells, leading to muscle weakness and other symptoms.

### Pompe Disease

Pompe disease is a rare genetic disorder caused by mutations in the GAA gene, leading to a deficiency in the GAA enzyme. This deficiency results in the accumulation of glycogen in the lysosomes of muscle cells, particularly in the heart and skeletal muscles. The disease can manifest in infancy or later in life, with varying degrees of severity. Infants with Pompe disease often experience rapid progression of the disease, leading to respiratory failure and death within the first year of life. Late-onset Pompe disease, on the other hand, typically presents in childhood or adulthood with progressive muscle weakness and respiratory insufficiency.

### Enzyme Replacement Therapy

Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) is a treatment approach for lysosomal storage disorders, including Pompe disease. ERT involves the administration of a functional enzyme to replace the deficient or dysfunctional enzyme in the patient's body. For Pompe disease, recombinant human GAA (rhGAA) is used to supplement the deficient GAA enzyme. However, the efficacy of ERT is often limited by the inefficient delivery of the enzyme to the lysosomes of target cells. The present invention addresses this limitation by utilizing a glycosylation-independent lysosomal targeting (GILT) tag to enhance the delivery of GAA to the lysosomes.

### Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting

The glycosylation-independent lysosomal targeting (GILT) tag is a peptide derived from the insulin-like growth factor II (IGF-II). The GILT tag contains a portion of the IGF-II sequence that binds to the cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor (CI-MPR) with high affinity. By fusing the GILT tag to the N-terminus of GAA, the resulting chimeric enzyme, referred to as GILT-tagged GAA, can bind to the CI-MPR and be efficiently transported to the lysosomes of target cells. This enhanced lysosomal delivery significantly improves the therapeutic efficacy of the enzyme replacement therapy.

### Furin-Resistant GILT Tag

The GILT tag used in the present invention is designed to be furin-resistant, ensuring that the tag remains intact during the processing and transport of the GAA enzyme. Furin is a proprotein convertase that can cleave the GILT tag, potentially reducing its effectiveness. By incorporating specific amino acid substitutions or modifications, the GILT tag is made resistant to furin cleavage, thereby maintaining its high affinity for the CI-MPR and enhancing the lysosomal delivery of GAA.

### Binding Affinity for the Insulin Receptor

The GILT tag not only enhances the binding of GAA to the CI-MPR but also has a high binding affinity for the insulin receptor. This dual binding property of the GILT tag can potentially trigger the redistribution of the CI-MPR to the plasma membrane, further enhancing the uptake and delivery of GAA to the lysosomes. The binding of the GILT tag to the insulin receptor is mediated by the IGF-II portion of the tag, which activates the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) signaling pathway, leading to the redistribution of the CI-MPR.

### Mutagenesis

To optimize the GILT tag for use in the present invention, mutagenesis techniques were employed to identify and incorporate specific amino acid substitutions that enhance the binding affinity of the tag for the CI-MPR and the insulin receptor. These substitutions were selected based on their ability to improve the stability and functionality of the GILT tag while maintaining the enzymatic activity of GAA. The resulting GILT-tagged GAA enzyme demonstrates significantly improved lysosomal delivery and glycogen clearance in Pompe mouse models.

### Spacer

A spacer sequence was introduced between the GILT tag and the GAA enzyme to ensure proper folding and function of both the tag and the enzyme. The spacer sequence consists of a short, flexible peptide that allows the GAA enzyme to maintain its native conformation and enzymatic activity while the GILT tag binds to the CI-MPR and the insulin receptor. The length and composition of the spacer sequence were optimized to balance the stability of the chimeric enzyme and its ability to bind to the receptors.

### Cells

The GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was produced using both transiently transfected HEK293 cells and a stable CHO cell line. The HEK293 cells were transfected with a plasmid containing the coding sequence for the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme, and the resulting protein was purified from the culture supernatants. The stable CHO cell line was generated using the GPEx retrovector expression system, allowing for large-scale production of the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme. The CHO-produced GILT-tagged GAA enzyme demonstrated similar biochemical properties to the HEK293-produced enzyme, including enzymatic activity, binding affinity for the CI-MPR, and lysosomal delivery.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Production of GILT-Tagged GAA in HEK293 Cells

A plasmid containing the coding sequence for the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was constructed by fusing the GILT tag to the N-terminus of the GAA gene. The plasmid was transiently transfected into HEK293 cells using a 1:1:1 ratio of cells, DNA, and 293Fectin. The cells were grown in Opti-MEM I medium in polycarbonate shaker flasks on an orbital shaker at 37°C in 8% CO2. The culture supernatants were harvested 5-10 days post-transfection, filtered, and concentrated using a tangential flow device. The concentrated supernatants were adjusted to pH 4.6 with sodium acetate and stored at -80°C.

### Example 2

#### Purification of GILT-Tagged GAA

The concentrated supernatants from Example 1 were thawed and adjusted to 0.75 M ammonium sulfate. The material was centrifuged to remove precipitates and filtered. The filtered material was loaded onto a phenyl-Sepharose 6 Fast Flow column and washed with HIC wash buffer. The enzyme was eluted with HIC elution buffer and dialyzed into QXL load buffer. The dialyzed sample was loaded onto a Q Sepharose XL column, washed with QXL load buffer, and eluted with QXL elution buffer. The purified GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was concentrated by ultrafiltration, exchanged into phosphate-buffered saline, and stored at -80°C.

### Example 3

#### Enzymatic Activity Assay

The enzymatic activity of the purified GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was measured using a synthetic substrate, p-nitrophenol (PNP) α-glucoside. The enzyme was incubated in 50 μL reactions containing 100 mM sodium acetate, pH 4.2, and 10 mM PNP α-glucoside substrate. Reactions were incubated at 37°C for 20 minutes and stopped with 300 μL of 100 mM sodium carbonate. Absorbance at 405 nm was read in 96-well microtiter plates and compared with standard curves derived from p-nitrophenol. The specific activity of the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was determined by dividing the GAA PNP units by the protein concentration as determined by the Bradford assay.

### Example 4

#### Uptake Assay in Rat L6 Myoblasts

Rat L6 myoblast cells were seeded into 24-well tissue culture plates and grown to 90% confluency. The cells were washed with uptake medium and incubated with purified rhGAA or GILT-tagged GAA in the presence or absence of inhibitors (M6P or IGF-II). After 18 hours, the cells were washed, lysed, and the lysate GAA activity was measured using the GAA 4-methylumbelliferyl assay. The results showed that the GILT-tagged GAA enzyme was taken up by the cells 25-fold more efficiently than rhGAA and was not inhibited by excess M6P but was inhibited by excess IGF-II.

### Example 5

#### Glycogen Clearance in Pompe Mouse Model

The abilities of GILT-tagged GAA and rhGAA to reverse the storage pathology in Pompe mice were tested using a 4-week enzyme replacement protocol. Mice received four injections of GILT-tagged GAA or rhGAA at doses of 5 or 20 mg/kg. The reduction in glycogen storage was greater in both dosing groups receiving GILT-tagged GAA compared to those receiving rhGAA. Treatment with the 20 mg/kg dose of GILT-tagged GAA resulted in statistically significant lower levels of glycogen in all tissues compared to the 20 or 5 mg/kg dose of rhGAA. Treatment with the 5 mg/kg dose of GILT-tagged GAA resulted in significantly lower levels of glycogen in most muscle tissues compared to the 5 mg/kg dose of rhGAA.
