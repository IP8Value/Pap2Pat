## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmacology and medicine, specifically to the development and use of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) receptor antagonists. More particularly, the invention pertains to novel GHRH receptor antagonists and their use in treating lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer.

## BACKGROUND

Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) is a 44-amino acid peptide primarily secreted by the hypothalamus. However, it is also produced locally in various tissues, including the lungs. GHRH stimulates the release and secretion of growth hormone (GH) by the pituitary gland and plays a crucial role in the production of insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) through the GH/IGF-1 axis. The GHRH receptor (GHRH-R) is a G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) that mediates the effects of GHRH. Recent studies have revealed that GHRH and its receptor are expressed in lung tissues and play significant roles in lung physiology and pathophysiology.

Lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer are major health concerns with significant morbidity and mortality. Current treatments for these conditions often have limited efficacy and can cause severe side effects. There is a pressing need for novel therapeutic strategies to address these issues. GHRH receptor antagonists have emerged as promising candidates due to their ability to modulate key signaling pathways involved in lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel GHRH receptor antagonists and their use in treating lung inflammation, fibrosis, and cancer. Specifically, the invention encompasses the synthesis and use of GHRH receptor antagonists, such as MIA-602, which have been shown to effectively inhibit the GHRH receptor and modulate downstream signaling pathways.

The GHRH receptor antagonists of the present invention are characterized by their high binding affinity to the GHRH receptor and their ability to inhibit the proliferation of lung cancer cells, reduce inflammation, and prevent fibrosis. The antagonists are useful in the treatment of various lung diseases, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), sarcoidosis, and lung cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms and definitions apply:

- **Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone (GHRH):** A 44-amino acid peptide primarily secreted by the hypothalamus that stimulates the release of growth hormone (GH) from the pituitary gland.
- **GHRH Receptor (GHRH-R):** A G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR) that binds to GHRH and mediates its effects.
- **GHRH Receptor Antagonist:** A compound that binds to the GHRH receptor and inhibits its activity, thereby modulating downstream signaling pathways.
- **Lung Inflammation:** A condition characterized by the infiltration of inflammatory cells into lung tissue, leading to symptoms such as coughing, shortness of breath, and chest pain.
- **Fibrosis:** The formation of excess fibrous connective tissue in an organ or tissue, often as a reparative response to injury or inflammation.
- **Lung Cancer:** A malignant tumor that originates in the lung tissue, often leading to uncontrolled cell growth and metastasis.

### Compositions

The present invention provides novel compositions comprising GHRH receptor antagonists. These antagonists are synthetic peptides designed to bind to the GHRH receptor with high affinity and inhibit its activity. One exemplary GHRH receptor antagonist is MIA-602, which has the amino acid sequence:

PhAc–Ada–Tyr–DArg–Asp–Ala–Ile–5FPhe–Thr–Ala–Har–Tyr(Me)–His–Orn–Val–Leu–Abu–Gln–Leu–Ser–Ala–His–Orn–Leu–Leu–Gln–Asp–Ile–Nle–D–Arg–Ha–NH2

This sequence is synthesized by solid-phase methods and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The substitutions D-Arg2, Har9, Abu15, and Nle27 are conserved to enhance the stability and binding affinity of the antagonist. Additional modifications, such as the replacement of Lys12 and Lys21 with Orn, increase resistance to enzymatic degradation. The substitution of Arg at positions 11 and 20 by His is also conserved.

The GHRH receptor antagonists of the present invention can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and inhalation. The compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, and excipients. Examples of suitable carriers include water, saline, buffered solutions, and other solvents commonly used in pharmaceutical formulations.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists

The GHRH receptor antagonist MIA-602 was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) techniques. The synthesis was carried out on a 0.1 mmol scale using Fmoc-protected amino acids and a Wang resin. The coupling reactions were performed using HBTU/DIEA as the coupling reagent. After the completion of the synthesis, the peptide was cleaved from the resin using a cleavage cocktail containing TFA, water, and triisopropylsilane (TIS). The crude peptide was purified by reverse-phase HPLC using a C18 column and a gradient of acetonitrile in water. The purity and identity of the final product were confirmed by mass spectrometry and analytical HPLC.

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro

The inhibitory effects of MIA-602 on the proliferation of human lung cancer cell lines were evaluated using the MTT assay. A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells were seeded in 96-well plates and allowed to adhere overnight. The cells were then treated with varying concentrations of MIA-602 (0.1, 1, 5, and 10 μM) for 72 hours. After the treatment, the MTT reagent was added to each well, and the plates were incubated for 4 hours at 37°C. The formazan crystals formed were dissolved in DMSO, and the absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. The results showed that MIA-602 significantly inhibited the proliferation of A549 cells in a dose-dependent manner, with an IC50 value of approximately 5 μM.

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo

The in vivo antitumor activity of MIA-602 was evaluated using a xenograft model of human lung cancer. A549 cells were injected subcutaneously into the flanks of nude mice. Once the tumors reached a volume of approximately 100 mm³, the mice were randomized into two groups: a control group receiving vehicle (PBS) and a treatment group receiving MIA-602 (5 μg/day) via subcutaneous injection. The tumor volumes were measured every three days using calipers, and the body weights of the mice were recorded weekly. After 28 days, the mice were euthanized, and the tumors were excised and weighed. The results showed that MIA-602 significantly reduced tumor growth compared to the control group, with a mean tumor weight reduction of 60%.

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists

The binding affinities of MIA-602 and related GHRH receptor antagonists were determined using competitive binding assays. Rat pituitary membranes were incubated with a fixed concentration of radiolabeled GHRH and increasing concentrations of the antagonists. The bound radioactivity was measured using a scintillation counter, and the data were analyzed to determine the IC50 values. The results showed that MIA-602 and related antagonists had high binding affinities to the GHRH receptor, with IC50 values in the low nanomolar range.

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin

The effects of MIA-602 on lung inflammation and fibrosis were evaluated using a bleomycin-induced lung injury model. C57BL/6 mice were intratracheally instilled with bleomycin (1 mg/kg) to induce lung injury. One day after bleomycin administration, the mice were randomized into two groups: a control group receiving vehicle (PBS) and a treatment group receiving MIA-602 (5 μg/day) via subcutaneous injection. The treatment was continued for 28 days. At the end of the treatment period, the mice were euthanized, and the lungs were harvested for histopathological analysis and measurement of hydroxyproline content.

Histopathological examination of lung sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) showed a significant reduction in inflammation and fibrosis in the MIA-602-treated group compared to the control group. The hydroxyproline content, a marker of collagen deposition, was also significantly lower in the MIA-602-treated group, indicating a reduction in fibrosis. These results demonstrate that MIA-602 effectively modulates lung inflammation and fibrosis in a bleomycin-induced model of lung injury.
