# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of energy storage devices, particularly to lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) that are designed to be stretchable and flexible. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of kirigami patterns to create stretchable lithium-ion batteries that can maintain their electrochemical and mechanical performance under various states of deformation. These batteries are particularly suited for integration into wearable electronic devices, such as smart watches, bracelets, and headbands, where flexibility and stretchability are essential.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have become the dominant energy storage technology for portable electronic devices due to their high energy density, long cycle life, and low self-discharge rate. However, conventional LIBs are rigid and inflexible, which limits their application in wearable electronics and other devices that require flexibility and stretchability. The demand for flexible and stretchable energy sources has grown significantly with the increasing popularity of wearable devices, such as smart watches, fitness trackers, and health monitors.

Several approaches have been explored to create flexible and stretchable batteries, including the use of flexible substrates, serpentine patterns, and other geometric designs. However, these methods often suffer from limitations such as reduced energy density, poor mechanical stability, and degradation of electrochemical performance under repeated deformation.

Kirigami, a variation of origami that involves cutting and folding, has emerged as a promising technique for creating highly stretchable and flexible structures. Kirigami patterns can be designed to allow for significant stretchability without compromising the structural integrity of the material. This makes kirigami an attractive approach for developing stretchable energy storage devices.

Despite the potential of kirigami, there is a need for a comprehensive and practical method to integrate kirigami patterns into the fabrication of lithium-ion batteries. The present invention addresses this need by providing a detailed method for creating stretchable lithium-ion batteries using kirigami patterns, ensuring that the batteries maintain their electrochemical and mechanical performance under various states of deformation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a stretchable lithium-ion battery (LIB) that utilizes kirigami patterns to achieve high stretchability and flexibility while maintaining excellent electrochemical performance. The invention includes the following key features:

1. **Kirigami Patterns**: The invention employs three specific kirigami patterns—zigzag-cut, cut-N-twist, and cut-N-shear—to create the stretchable structure of the battery. Each pattern is designed to allow for significant stretchability without causing out-of-plane deformation or damage to the battery components.

2. **Material Selection**: The battery components, including the anode, cathode, current collectors, separator, and packaging materials, are all cut and aligned according to the selected kirigami pattern. This ensures uniformity and perfect alignment of all layers, which is crucial for achieving excellent packaging and mechanical stability.

3. **Fabrication Method**: The invention provides a detailed method for fabricating the stretchable LIBs. This includes the preparation of slurries for the anode and cathode, coating the current collectors, cutting and aligning all layers according to the kirigami pattern, and packaging the battery in a controlled environment.

4. **Electrochemical and Mechanical Performance**: The stretchable LIBs exhibit excellent electrochemical performance, including stable charge/discharge behavior, high capacity retention, and good rate performance. Additionally, the batteries demonstrate robust mechanical characteristics, with no significant changes in impedance or output power under repeated stretching cycles.

5. **Applications**: The stretchable LIBs are particularly suited for integration into wearable electronic devices, such as smart watches, bracelets, and headbands. The invention demonstrates the ability of the kirigami LIBs to power a commercial Samsung Gear 2 smart watch, showing the potential for these batteries to replace traditional rigid and bulky batteries in wearable devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Kirigami Patterns

The invention utilizes three specific kirigami patterns to create the stretchable structure of the lithium-ion battery (LIB):

1. **Zigzag-Cut Pattern**: This pattern is created by cutting a folded stack of foil asymmetrically between the neighboring creases, resulting in zigzag-like cuttings in the longitudinal direction. The zigzag pattern allows the battery to stretch beyond its length in the planar state, with out-of-plane deformation occurring at the vicinity of the cuts. The level of stretchability depends on the length of the cut and is a function of the buckling amplitude.

2. **Cut-N-Twist Pattern**: This pattern involves symmetrically cutting a folded stack of foil at all creases and then unfolding it to a planar state, followed by twisting at the two ends. The twisted structure is analogous to a twisted telephone cord and represents a locked structure where out-of-plane deformation is constrained, and rotation occurs at the cuts to accommodate stretching. The packing density of the cut-N-twist pattern is defined by the width of each face.

3. **Cut-N-Shear Pattern**: This pattern introduces folding after symmetric cutting and then subjects the folded structure to shear, doubling the packing density compared to the cut-N-twist pattern. Stretching is achieved by the rotations of the cuts, and no out-of-plane deformation is involved.

### Material Selection and Preparation

The battery components are carefully selected and prepared to ensure optimal performance and mechanical stability:

1. **Anode and Cathode Active Materials**: Graphite (Fisher Scientific Inc.) is used as the anode active material, and LiCoO2 (LCO, MTI Corp.) is used as the cathode active material.

2. **Current Collectors**: Copper (Cu) and aluminum (Al) serve as the anode and cathode current collectors, respectively.

3. **Slurries**: Conventional slurries of the active materials are prepared and used to coat the current collectors. The cathode slurry is prepared by mixing LCO, PVDF (MTI Corp.), Carbon black (Super C45), and N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone solvent with a ratio of 8:1:1:5 by weight. The anode slurry is prepared by mixing graphite (Fisher Scientific), carbon black (Super C45), Carboxymethyl cellulose (Fisher Scientific), Styrene Butadiene Rubber (Fisher Scientific), and DI water with a ratio of 95:2.5:1.25:1.25:200 by weight.

4. **Separator and Packaging Materials**: Polypropylene (Celgard 2500) is used as the separator, and aluminized polyethylene (PE) (Sigma-Aldrich) is used as the packaging material.

### Fabrication Method

The fabrication of the stretchable LIBs involves the following steps:

1. **Cutting and Aligning Layers**: All layers of the battery, including the anode, cathode, current collectors, separator, and packaging materials, are cut using the selected kirigami pattern. A customized puncher or laser cutting tool is used to ensure uniform cutting and perfect alignment of all layers.

2. **Coating and Laminating**: The current collectors are coated with the prepared slurries and dried on a hot plate at 130°C for 5 hours (cathode) and 120°C for 5 hours (anode) in ambient conditions. The multilayer structure is then laminated in the order of packaging material, cathode electrode, separator, anode electrode, and packaging material.

3. **Packaging and Sealing**: The laminated structure is delivered to an argon-filled glovebox for packaging. The sides of the cell are sealed using an impulse sealer, except for one side for electrolyte injection. The electrolyte (1 M LiPF6 in EC:DMC:DEC (1:1:1)) is injected, and the last side of the battery cell is sealed.

### Electrochemical and Mechanical Performance

The stretchable LIBs exhibit excellent electrochemical and mechanical performance:

1. **Stretchability**: The kirigami LIBs can be stretched beyond 100% of their original length without significant changes in thickness. The stretchability of the cut-N-shear pattern is up to 150%.

2. **Electrochemical Cycling**: The LIBs show well-defined plateaus at around 3.7 V and stable charge/discharge behaviors under both compact and stretched states. The capacity retention is over 85% after 100 cycles, and the Coulombic efficiency is 99.8%.

3. **Rate Performance**: The LIBs demonstrate good rate performance, with capacity recovery to 27.6 mAh when the discharge rate resumes to C/3 after 30 cycles at varying C-rates.

4. **Mechanical Stability**: The output voltage remains steady at 3.83 V under different stretchabilities, and the maximum output power is 4.1 W. The LIBs show no noticeable decay in output power over 3,000 stretching cycles and a stretchability of up to 90%.

### Applications

The stretchable LIBs have been successfully integrated into a commercial Samsung Gear 2 smart watch, demonstrating their potential for use in wearable devices:

1. **Smart Watch Integration**: The kirigami LIBs were sewn between two elastic bands and wrapped around the wrist, allowing the elastic bands to function as an elastic watch strap. The LIBs can be stretched and contracted, driven by the elasticity of the band.

2. **Performance Testing**: The kirigami LIBs powered the Samsung Gear 2 smart watch, maintaining normal functionality during dynamic deformation, including stretching from the wrist to the upper arm and bending and straightening of the elbow. The standby time was measured to be 24.5 hours, and the calling time was 90 minutes, which is consistent with the performance of the original battery.

3. **Thermal Performance**: The kirigami LIBs showed better thermal performance compared to the bulky battery used in the Samsung Gear 2, with no significant temperature rise during discharge.

### Numerical Calculation of Crack and Plastic Behavior

A theoretical analysis was conducted to understand the competing mechanisms between "crack growth" and "plastic rolling" in the kirigami LIBs:

1. **Crack Growth**: The critical moment for "crack growth" is given by the Griffith’s criterion, where the driving force (release of elastic energy) equates the resistance (surface energy). The critical moment is calculated based on the geometry and material properties of the battery components.

2. **Plastic Rolling**: The critical moment for "plastic rolling" is determined by the rate of energy dissipation due to plastic deformation and the rate of release of potential energy. Finite element simulations were conducted to analyze these deformation modes, confirming that the "safe mode" is activated for the present battery setup, contributing to the robust electrochemical and mechanical performance.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Fabrication and Characterization of Cut-N-Twist Pattern LIB

1. **Material Preparation**: Graphite and LiCoO2 were used as the anode and cathode active materials, respectively. Copper and aluminum served as the anode and cathode current collectors. Slurries were prepared and coated on the current collectors, followed by drying.

2. **Cutting and Alignment**: All layers of the battery were cut using the cut-N-twist pattern. A customized puncher was used to ensure uniform cutting and perfect alignment.

3. **Lamination and Packaging**: The layers were laminated in the order of packaging material, cathode electrode, separator, anode electrode, and packaging material. The structure was packaged in an argon-filled glovebox, and the electrolyte was injected.

4. **Electrochemical and Mechanical Testing**: The LIB was tested for electrochemical performance, including charge/discharge behavior, rate performance, and cyclic stability. Mechanical testing included stretchability, output power, and thermal performance.

5. **Results**: The cut-N-twist pattern LIB exhibited excellent electrochemical performance, with stable charge/discharge behavior, high capacity retention, and good rate performance. The mechanical testing showed no significant changes in impedance or output power under repeated stretching cycles.

#### Example 2: Integration of Cut-N-Twist Pattern LIB with Samsung Gear 2 Smart Watch

1. **Battery Preparation**: A kirigami LIB using the cut-N-twist pattern was fabricated and characterized as described in Example 1.

2. **Integration with Smart Watch**: The original battery of the Samsung Gear 2 smart watch was removed, and the kirigami LIB was connected to the device. The LIB was sewn between two elastic bands and wrapped around the wrist.

3. **Performance Testing**: The kirigami LIB powered the Samsung Gear 2 smart watch, maintaining normal functionality during dynamic deformation. The standby time was 24.5 hours, and the calling time was 90 minutes.

4. **Thermal Testing**: The temperature of the kirigami LIB was measured during discharge, showing no significant temperature rise compared to the bulky battery used in the Samsung Gear 2.

These examples demonstrate the feasibility and practicality of using kirigami patterns to create stretchable lithium-ion batteries that can be integrated into wearable electronic devices, offering significant advantages in terms of flexibility, stretchability, and performance.
