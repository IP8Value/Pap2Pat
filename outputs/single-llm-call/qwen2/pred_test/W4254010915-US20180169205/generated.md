# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to the design and use of hybrid flagellin (FliC) proteins as scaffolds for the creation of T cell-independent (TI) vaccines. The invention leverages the polymeric nature of FliC to enhance the presentation of vaccine antigens, thereby stimulating a robust and effective TI immune response.

### Related Art

Adaptive immune responses typically require the interaction between T helper cells and B cells to promote B cell activation, isotype switching, and the development of plasma cells that produce immunoglobulins. However, in settings of T cell deficiency, such as in AIDS, this T cell-dependent (TD) mode of antibody production is ineffective. Fortunately, "innate-like" B cells, including splenic marginal zone B cells and peritoneal B1 B cells, can be activated independently of T helper cells upon recognition of repetitive or polymeric antigenic epitopes. This T cell-independent (TI) response is crucial for generating protective immunity in individuals with compromised T cell function.

Bacterial flagellin (FliC) is a major antigen present on many commensal and pathogenic microbes. FliC is a four-domain protein that can form filaments hundreds of nanometers in length, a critical feature for stimulating a TI response. Previous studies have explored the use of FliC as a scaffold for vaccine development, but the focus has been on its TLR5 and NLRC4 binding sites rather than its potential as a TI antigen scaffold. The present invention addresses this gap by engineering FliC to incorporate vaccine antigens, thereby creating a novel platform for TI vaccines.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a hybrid flagellin (hFliC) protein designed to serve as a scaffold for T cell-independent (TI) vaccines. The hFliC protein is engineered by replacing the D3 domain of native FliC with a vaccine antigen domain, such as the envelope glycoprotein (E) from Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2 E). This replacement ensures that the antigen is fully exposed on the surface of the polymerized hFliC filaments, facilitating B cell receptor (BCR) crosslinking and subsequent TI stimulation.

The hFliC protein is produced using a baculovirus expression system and purified for use in vaccine formulations. The invention also includes methods for characterizing the hFliC filaments, confirming their ability to crosslink BCRs, and demonstrating their immunogenicity in both wild-type and T cell-restricted mouse models. The results show that hFliC filaments are capable of inducing a significant antibody response, even in the absence of T cell help, making them a promising platform for developing vaccines for use in immunodeficiency settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1

#### Design and Characterization of hFliC Protein

To create the hybrid flagellin (hFliC) protein, the D3 domain of native FliC was replaced with the full-length sequence of the envelope glycoprotein (E) from Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2 E). Flexible Gly-Ser linkers were added to the N- and C-termini of DENV2 E to ensure conformational independence. The resulting hFliC protein was cloned into a pENTR plasmid for baculovirus expression.

The hFliC protein was expressed using a baculovirus expression system and purified by His-tag affinity chromatography. The purity and molecular weight of the hFliC protein were confirmed using Coomassie staining and Western blot analysis. Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy was used to assess the secondary structure of the hFliC protein, revealing a hybrid spectrum of α-helical and β-sheet character, consistent with the retention of native secondary structures from both FliC and DENV2 E.

#### Characterization of hFliC Filaments

The hFliC protein was polymerized in high molar ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate, forming filaments with an increased diameter of 35 nm compared to native FliC filaments (20 nm). Negative stain transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed the formation of hFliC filaments with the expected diameter. The filaments exhibited a high degree of contrast in the inner core, suggesting a stable 11-protofilament core structure, while the outer regions displayed low contrast, indicating the flexibility of the DENV2 E domains.

Binding ELISA and immunogold labeling experiments confirmed that the DENV2 E domain retained its antigenicity and was accessible on the surface of the hFliC filaments. These results demonstrate that the hFliC filaments are capable of presenting the DENV2 E antigen in a conformationally intact and immunogenic manner.

### Example 2

#### In Vivo Activity of hFliC in Wild-Type Mouse Models

The immunogenicity of hFliC filaments was tested in wild-type C57BL/6J mice using an immunization protocol involving a priming dose followed by three booster doses. The priming dose was administered either intraperitoneally (IP) or intranasally (IN), and all booster doses were given IN. Serum IgA, IgG, and IgM responses were assessed using ELISA.

Mice immunized with hFliC filaments exhibited a strong serum IgA response when the priming dose was given IN, and a robust serum IgG and IgM response when the priming dose was given IP. The hFliC filaments induced a higher antibody response compared to monomeric hFliC, highlighting the importance of the polymeric structure in enhancing immunogenicity.

#### In Vivo Activity of hFliC in T Cell-Restricted Mouse Models

To test the potential for hFliC filaments to induce TI responses, the immunization protocol was repeated in T cell-restricted 6.5-TCR transgenic mice, which lack functional T helper cells. The results showed that hFliC filaments were capable of inducing a significant serum IgM response, even in the absence of T cell help. Additionally, a specific IgG3 response was observed, further supporting the TI nature of the immune response.

#### B Cell Crosslinking

The ability of hFliC filaments to crosslink BCRs was confirmed using a 4G2 B cell hybridoma cell line specific for DENV2 E. Confocal microscopy revealed that hFliC filaments induced the clustering of BCRs around the circumference of the cells, indicating successful BCR crosslinking.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention demonstrates the successful engineering of a hybrid flagellin (hFliC) protein as a scaffold for T cell-independent (TI) vaccines. The hFliC filaments are capable of presenting vaccine antigens in a conformationally intact and immunogenic manner, leading to a robust antibody response in both wild-type and T cell-restricted mouse models. The polymeric nature of hFliC enhances its ability to crosslink B cell receptors, making it a promising platform for developing vaccines for use in immunodeficiency settings. This approach holds significant potential for improving vaccine efficacy in populations with compromised immune systems, including those with HIV/AIDS, undergoing chemotherapy, or in the early perinatal period.
