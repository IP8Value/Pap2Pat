## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method and apparatus for treating conjunctivochalasis, a condition characterized by the presence of redundant conjunctival tissue that can cause eye irritation and visual disturbances. More specifically, the invention pertains to a near-infrared (NIR) laser therapy system for the precise and controlled shrinkage of redundant conjunctival tissue.

## BACKGROUND

Conjunctivochalasis is a degenerative condition of the eye characterized by the presence of loose, redundant conjunctival tissue. This condition can lead to various ocular symptoms, including chronic eye irritation, blurred vision, and disruption of the tear film. Traditional treatments for conjunctivochalasis, such as the use of artificial tears, punctal plugs, and anti-inflammatory drops, are often ineffective. Surgical interventions, such as thermocautery, electrocautery, and paste-pinch-cut techniques, can be painful, have prolonged healing times, and carry risks of complications such as scarring and pyogenic granulomas.

Laser therapy has emerged as a promising alternative for treating conjunctivochalasis. Argon lasers, which operate at a wavelength of 532 nm, have been used for this purpose. However, the high absorption of argon laser energy by blood can lead to vessel rupture and hemorrhage. Moreover, the thermal energy generated by these lasers can cause significant damage to the conjunctival epithelium and underlying stroma.

Water, which constitutes approximately 70% of the conjunctiva, is a more effective medium for heat absorption. Near-infrared (NIR) light, particularly at wavelengths of 1460 nm and 1940 nm, is strongly absorbed by water. While 1940 nm lasers are effective, they are expensive and inefficient. In contrast, diode lasers operating at 1460 nm offer a more practical solution due to their higher electro-optical efficiency and lower cost.

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing treatments by providing a miniature-probed-based laser therapy system that uses a 1460 nm diode laser to achieve precise and controlled shrinkage of redundant conjunctival tissue. This system is designed to minimize thermal damage, reduce pain, and expedite the healing process.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF DISCLOSED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for treating conjunctivochalasis using a near-infrared (NIR) laser therapy system. The system includes a programmable pulsed laser diode operating at a wavelength of 1460 nm, a handheld probe with a line-shaped focus spot, and angled forceps for precise tissue manipulation. The laser parameters are optimized to achieve effective tissue shrinkage while minimizing thermal damage and patient discomfort.

### System Overview

The NIR laser therapy system comprises a programmable pulsed laser diode, a handheld probe, and angled forceps. The laser diode is capable of delivering high-intensity NIR light in a pulsed mode, with adjustable parameters such as pulse duration, repetition rate, and output power. The handheld probe is designed to focus the laser beam into a line, ensuring that the energy is concentrated on the target tissue. The angled forceps are used to grasp and manipulate the redundant conjunctival tissue, ensuring that the laser energy is applied precisely and effectively.

### Laser Diode System

The laser diode system includes a fiber-coupled high-power laser diode module with a maximum continuous-wave (CW) power of 12 W. The light is output through a multimode fiber with a core diameter of 200 μm and a numerical aperture (NA) of 0.22. The other end of the fiber is connected to the handheld probe. A 650 nm laser diode is integrated into the source laser for aiming purposes, with a power of 200 μW.

Custom-built control circuits are used to drive the 1460 nm laser module. The pulse duty cycle, repetition rate, output power, and working duration are tunable through a LabVIEW-based control software. The duty cycle, repetition rate, and working duration are arbitrarily tunable. The maximum output power and the shortest pulse width are limited to 12 W and 100 μs, respectively. A foot pedal is used as a trigger for the laser output. Once the laser output is triggered, the 650 nm aiming light is turned off automatically, and the 1460 nm laser is delivered to the probe according to the preset parameters. If the foot pedal is released during the procedure, the infrared laser is turned off immediately.

### Handheld Probe

The handheld probe is designed to focus the SMA fiber output into a line, reducing power loss and keeping the probe compact. A cylindrical lens is used to focus the laser beam into a line with dimensions of 10 × 1 mm². The probe head and the forceps are connected through an adaptor, ensuring the right angle illumination of the NIR laser light on the conjunctival fold and a proper working distance. The line-shaped focal plane is just above the angled platform of the forceps, allowing the conjunctival folds grasped by the forceps to be effectively heated.

### Angled Forceps

The angled forceps are designed to grasp and manipulate the redundant conjunctival tissue. The forceps ensure that the tissue is held in place, allowing the laser energy to be applied precisely and effectively. The forceps are connected to the probe through an adaptor, ensuring the correct alignment and working distance.

### Laser Parameter Optimization

The laser parameters are optimized to achieve effective tissue shrinkage while minimizing thermal damage. The pulse duration is set to be shorter than the thermal relaxation time of the tissue, which is approximately 0.5 seconds for a conjunctival thickness of 0.5 mm. The repetition rate is set to 1 Hz, with a working duration of 4 seconds, corresponding to 4 pulses. The peak power and pulse duty cycle are adjusted to achieve the desired temperature range for collagen shrinkage, which is between 62 °C and 100 °C.

### Experimental Setup and Procedure

Ex vivo experiments were conducted on porcine eyes to validate the effectiveness of the NIR laser therapy system. The porcine eyes were thawed in phosphate-buffered saline before the experiment. A 3D-printed marker was used to create a specific, observable region of interest on the conjunctiva surface. The line-focused light from the LTC probe was targeted to shine between the parallel marker lines on the conjunctiva. A thermal camera was used to record the temporal temperature changes in the region during the LTC. OCT was used to monitor the tissue structural changes during the LTC. The laser-induced shrinkage was measured by comparing the photographs of the targeted area before and after the LTC.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Temperature Changes During Laser Heating

During the laser heating procedure, the temperature continues to increase until the laser is turned off. To optimize the shrinking, the temperature should be kept in a range to avoid burning and boiling. The collagen shrinkage temperature is around 62 °C, and to avoid boiling, the temperature should be kept below 100 °C. Pulse laser parameters were selected to achieve this temperature range. A pulse repetition rate of 1 Hz and a working duration of 4 seconds (a total of 4 pulses) were used.

Figure 1 shows the temperature changes during the LTC process using different laser parameters. The highest temperatures in each recorded thermal camera image sequence were obtained. The peak temperature increases very fast during the laser pulse illumination period and decreases relatively slowly during the laser-off period. The effects of peak laser power and pulse duty cycle were investigated. With a laser peak power of 1.51 W and a duty cycle of 10% (100 ms pulse duration), the temperature increase is very small and always below 40 °C. When the duty cycle was increased to 20% (200 ms pulse duration) and 30% (300 ms pulse duration), the cumulative effect of multiple pulses can be clearly seen. The temperature increases during the laser-illumination period and drops during the laser-off period. For a peak power of 3 W, the maximum temperature for the 30% duty cycle exceeds 100 °C, while the maximum temperature for the 10% duty cycle is around 65 °C. When the peak power was further increased to 5.77 W, the maximum temperature exceeds 100 °C for all three different duty cycles.

#### Example 2: OCT Imaging

Real-time OCT imaging was used to monitor the LTC-induced shrinkage process. This dynamic process can be clearly visualized in a video of the time-sequence OCT B-scans. Particle image velocimetry (PIV) was employed to further visualize and quantify the shrinkage from these images. The green boxes in the OCT B-frames were used to calculate the PIV using ImageJ. The results show that the red arrow groups at both sides point to the central region, where the laser was applied, indicating the shrinkage process.

#### Example 3: Shrinkage Using Different Laser Parameters

The influence of different laser parameters on the shrinkage was investigated. Microscope images of the porcine eyes before and after LTC with different laser powers and pulse duty cycles were analyzed. For a peak power of 2.33 W and a duty cycle of 20%, the shrinkage is 21%. As the peak power increases to 3 W and 3.66 W, the shrinkage increases to 36% and 45%, respectively. For a peak power of 3 W, the shrinkage and temperature were measured 6-8 times on different samples, and the averaged values were obtained. The optimal laser parameters were determined to be a peak power of 3 W and a duty cycle of 20%, achieving a shrinkage of 36% with a temperature of 88 °C.

#### Example 4: Shrinkage Using Angled Forceps

The probe is designed to work with the angled forceps to ensure the working distance is guaranteed, the shining angle is normal, and only the tissue fold held by the forceps is heated. The force from the forceps may introduce a false shrinkage. To eliminate this, the distance changes between the parallel marker lines due to only the force from the angled forceps were tested. A slight change (10%) between the marker lines was found. After the LTC, the shrinkage was measured to be 45%.

### Conclusion

The ex vivo porcine eye experiments of the NIR laser therapy for conjunctivochalasis (LTC) provide a proof-of-concept for this new technique. The texture and contents of the conjunctiva of the porcine eye are very close to those of the human eye, suggesting that similar shrinkage can be achieved in human subjects. However, further in vivo animal experiments are needed to calibrate the laser parameters for human use, assess conjunctival hyperemia, hemorrhage, and blanching, and evaluate the long-term maintenance of shrinkage.

In summary, the present invention provides a NIR LTC using a 1460 nm wavelength laser, which can be efficiently absorbed by water. A compact handheld probe with optimized features for LTC, including a line-shaped focus spot and angled forceps, was developed. The laser parameters were optimized, and ex vivo experiments on porcine eyes demonstrated up to 45% conjunctival shrinkage. This system offers a promising and minimally invasive solution for the treatment of conjunctivochalasis.
