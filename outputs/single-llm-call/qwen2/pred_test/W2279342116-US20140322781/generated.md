# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of catalysis, specifically to the synthesis and use of heterobimetallic zeolites for the conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. More particularly, the invention provides a novel heterobimetallic zeolite, InV-ZSM-5, which demonstrates superior performance in terms of selectivity and durability compared to monometallic zeolites and unmodified zeolites.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Zeolites are microporous aluminosilicate minerals commonly used as catalysts in various chemical processes due to their unique structural properties and high surface area. Among these, ZSM-5 is one of the most extensively studied and utilized zeolites, particularly in the conversion of biomass-derived ethanol to valuable hydrocarbons. The conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbons is a critical process in the production of renewable fuels and chemicals, and the efficiency of this process is heavily dependent on the catalyst used.

Monometallic zeolites, such as V-ZSM-5 and In-ZSM-5, have been widely investigated for their catalytic properties. Vanadium-exchanged ZSM-5 (V-ZSM-5) is known for its effectiveness in oxydehydrogenation reactions, while indium-exchanged ZSM-5 (In-ZSM-5) is recognized for its ability to promote alkane aromatization. However, the performance of these monometallic zeolites is often limited by issues such as low selectivity to higher hydrocarbons (C3+) and rapid deactivation due to coking.

To address these limitations, the development of heterobimetallic zeolites, which incorporate two different metal species, has gained significant attention. The combination of vanadium and indium in a single zeolite framework, referred to as InV-ZSM-5, has shown promise in enhancing the catalytic performance for ethanol conversion. The synergistic effects of the two metals can lead to improved selectivity towards valuable C3+ hydrocarbons and enhanced durability of the catalyst.

Despite the potential benefits, the synthesis and characterization of heterobimetallic zeolites pose significant challenges. Traditional characterization techniques such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), electron microscopy, and X-ray absorption near-edge spectroscopy (XANES) are often insufficient to discern the structure and properties of these complex materials. Elemental analysis and UV-Vis spectroscopy have proven to be more useful in confirming the presence of both metal species and their interactions within the zeolite framework.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a method for synthesizing InV-ZSM-5 and demonstrating its superior performance in the conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. The invention also includes detailed characterization of the catalyst and its performance in various reaction conditions, highlighting its advantages over existing monometallic and unmodified zeolites.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel heterobimetallic zeolite, InV-ZSM-5, and its use in the efficient conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. The invention encompasses the following aspects:

1. **Synthesis of InV-ZSM-5**: The invention provides a method for synthesizing InV-ZSM-5 by ion exchange, starting from a commercial NH4-ZSM-5 zeolite. The method involves sequential ion exchange with vanadium and indium precursors to achieve the desired metal loadings.

2. **Characterization of InV-ZSM-5**: The invention includes detailed characterization of InV-ZSM-5 using elemental analysis, UV-Vis spectroscopy, and Raman spectroscopy. These techniques confirm the presence of both vanadium and indium in the zeolite framework and provide insights into their interactions.

3. **Catalytic Performance**: The invention demonstrates the superior catalytic performance of InV-ZSM-5 in the conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. InV-ZSM-5 exhibits higher selectivity towards valuable C3+ hydrocarbons and enhanced durability compared to monometallic V-ZSM-5 and In-ZSM-5, as well as unmodified H-ZSM-5.

4. **Mechanism of Ethanol Conversion**: The invention provides evidence supporting the hydrocarbon pool mechanism for ethanol conversion over InV-ZSM-5. Deuterium labeling experiments and in-situ diffuse reflectance Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (DRIFTS) studies suggest that the initial formation of linear alkenes is followed by the rapid formation of aromatics, which is consistent with the hydrocarbon pool pathway.

5. **Durability and Regeneration**: The invention includes durability studies that show InV-ZSM-5 can function twice as long as V-ZSM-5 before requiring decoking. The catalyst can be fully regenerated by heating to 500°C, restoring its performance.

6. **Application in Fermentation Streams**: The invention demonstrates that InV-ZSM-5 can effectively convert partially concentrated ethanol streams, including those containing fermentation impurities, to hydrocarbon blend-stock without significant impact on catalyst performance or product quality.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Synthesis of InV-ZSM-5

The synthesis of InV-ZSM-5 involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of H-ZSM-5**: Commercial NH4-ZSM-5 (CBV2314) is thermally treated at 500°C for 4 hours to produce H-ZSM-5.

2. **Preparation of V-ZSM-5**: H-ZSM-5 is soaked in a 0.05 M solution of V(III)Cl3 at 80°C for 16 hours under reflux conditions. The mixture is then vacuum filtered, dried at 105°C overnight, and calcined at 500°C for 4 hours to obtain V-ZSM-5.

3. **Preparation of InV-ZSM-5**: V-ZSM-5 is ion-exchanged with a 0.015 M solution of indium(III) nitrate at 80°C for 16 hours under reflux conditions. The mixture is vacuum filtered, washed, dried at 105°C, and calcined at 500°C for 4 hours to obtain InV-ZSM-5.

### Characterization of InV-ZSM-5

#### Elemental Analysis

Elemental analysis confirms the presence of vanadium and indium in InV-ZSM-5. The vanadium loading is approximately 0.6 wt%, and the indium loading is approximately 1.6 wt%.

#### UV-Vis Spectroscopy

UV-Vis spectroscopy of InV-ZSM-5 shows an absorption near 300 nm, which can be attributed to both In3+ and V5+ species. This is in contrast to the mechanical mixture of V-ZSM-5 and In-ZSM-5, which shows bands at ~300 and ~400 nm. The absence of the 402 nm band in InV-ZSM-5 suggests that it is not a simple mechanical mixture of the two monometallic zeolites.

#### Raman Spectroscopy

Raman spectroscopy of InV-ZSM-5 reveals a spectrum similar to that of In-ZSM-5, with additional sharp peaks at 484 cm−1 and 919 cm−1. These peaks do not match with V2O5, In2O3, or VInO4, suggesting complex vanadium and indium interactions within the zeolite framework.

### Catalytic Performance

#### Ethanol Conversion to Hydrocarbon Blend-Stock

InV-ZSM-5 demonstrates superior performance in the conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. At temperatures ranging from 250°C to 450°C, InV-ZSM-5 achieves 100% ethanol conversion to hydrocarbons, with no oxygenates observed in the product stream. The selectivity to C2 hydrocarbons is significantly lower for InV-ZSM-5 compared to V-ZSM-5 in the 250-350°C range, while the selectivity to valuable C3+ hydrocarbons is higher. In contrast, H-ZSM-5 shows higher C2 selectivity and lower C3+ selectivity compared to InV-ZSM-5 in the 250-400°C range.

The product stream from ethanol conversion over InV-ZSM-5 contains 19% C3-C4 olefins, 35% C3-C4 paraffins, and 33% C5+ liquid fraction. The C5+ liquid fraction has an average molecular weight of 97.6, an average specific gravity of 0.81, and a Reid Vapor Pressure of 5.14. The calculated research and motor octane numbers are 105.7 and 90.6, respectively. The liquid product stream contains over 350 compounds, consisting of 3.8% paraffins, 24.0% iso-paraffins, 6.5% olefins, 5.4% naphthenes, 60.2% aromatics, and 4.2% unidentified by volume.

#### Catalyst Durability

Durability studies were conducted on InV-ZSM-5 and V-ZSM-5 at a weight hourly space velocity (WHSV) of 4.0 h−1 at 350°C. The performance of InV-ZSM-5 was monitored by the yield of C3+ hydrocarbons as a function of time. InV-ZSM-5 can function for about 12 hours before the C3+ yield drops below 80%, whereas V-ZSM-5 requires decoking after about 6 hours. After decoking at 500°C, InV-ZSM-5 fully recovers its performance, while V-ZSM-5 only recovers about 94% of its activity.

### Mechanism of Ethanol Conversion

#### Energy Balance and Deuteration Experiments

Ethanol conversion to hydrocarbons is exothermic (ΔHr, −1.0 MJ/kg) based on heat of formation data. Deuteration experiments using deuterated ethanol (C2H5OD) and a mixture of 70% C2H5OH and 30% D2O show that deuterium is incorporated into all hydrocarbons in the product stream except ethylene. This suggests that the hydrocarbon pool mechanism is a pathway for ethanol conversion to C2+ hydrocarbons, and the initial formation of ethylene via dehydration is not the sole step.

#### In-Situ DRIFTS Studies

In-situ diffuse reflectance Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (DRIFTS) studies on V-ZSM-5 reveal that linear alkenes form at the initiation of the reaction, followed by the rapid formation of aromatics. The presence of C-H and C=C stretches at 350°C is consistent with the formation of non-oxygenated hydrocarbon species. These findings support the hydrocarbon pool mechanism and indicate that the initial hydrocarbon pool forms from ethylene via dehydration or ethoxy groups, with subsequent propagation via the hydrocarbon pool pathway.

### Application in Fermentation Streams

InV-ZSM-5 can effectively convert partially concentrated ethanol streams, including those containing fermentation impurities, to hydrocarbon blend-stock without significant impact on catalyst performance or product quality. Gas chromatograms of the product streams show that the ratios of individual peaks remain unchanged regardless of the fraction of water in the input stream. This suggests that fermentation streams at any stage of purification can be employed for conversion to hydrocarbon blend-stock, eliminating the need for expensive complete dewatering.

### Example 2

**Catalytic Performance of InV-ZSM-5 in Ethanol Conversion**

1. **Catalyst Preparation**: InV-ZSM-5 was synthesized as described in Example 1.

2. **Catalytic Conversion Setup**: A quartz reactor (8 mm ID × 25 cm H) was loaded with 0.2 g of InV-ZSM-5 catalyst (250-500 μm) between two layers of quartz wool. The reactor was heated to 450°C and held for 0.5 hours under a 50 sccm dry helium flow. The temperature was then adjusted to the desired testing temperature, and pure ethanol was fed into the reactor using a syringe pump.

3. **Product Analysis**: Product analysis was performed using an on-line gas chromatograph (Agilent 6850) and mass spectrometer (5975C MS). The product stream contained 19% C3-C4 olefins, 35% C3-C4 paraffins, and 33% C5+ liquid fraction. The C5+ liquid fraction had an average molecular weight of 97.6, an average specific gravity of 0.81, and a Reid Vapor Pressure of 5.14. The calculated research and motor octane numbers were 105.7 and 90.6, respectively.

4. **Durability Test**: The durability of InV-ZSM-5 was tested at a WHSV of 4.0 h−1 at 350°C. The catalyst could function for about 12 hours before the C3+ yield dropped below 80%. After decoking at 500°C, the catalyst fully recovered its performance.

5. **Mechanism Study**: Deuteration experiments and in-situ DRIFTS studies supported the hydrocarbon pool mechanism for ethanol conversion over InV-ZSM-5. The initial formation of linear alkenes was followed by the rapid formation of aromatics, consistent with the hydrocarbon pool pathway.

6. **Fermentation Stream Conversion**: InV-ZSM-5 was tested with a 40% ethanol solution containing fermentation impurities. The catalyst effectively converted the ethanol stream to hydrocarbon blend-stock without significant impact on performance or product quality.

The present invention thus provides a novel heterobimetallic zeolite, InV-ZSM-5, which demonstrates superior performance in the conversion of ethanol to hydrocarbon blend-stock. The invention offers significant advantages in terms of selectivity, durability, and applicability to fermentation streams, making it a valuable catalyst for renewable fuel and chemical production.
