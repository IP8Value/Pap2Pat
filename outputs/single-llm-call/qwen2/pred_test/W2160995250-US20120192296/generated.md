# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic antibodies, specifically to agonist anti-EDAR (Ectodysplasin A Receptor) monoclonal antibodies and methods of using these antibodies for the treatment of ectodermal dysplasia. Ectodermal dysplasia is a group of genetic disorders characterized by abnormal development of ectodermal structures, including hair, teeth, nails, and sweat glands. The invention provides novel agonist anti-EDAR antibodies that can effectively rescue the developmental defects associated with ectodermal dysplasia in both murine and canine models.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ectodermal dysplasia (ED) is a group of hereditary disorders affecting the development and function of ectodermally derived structures such as hair, teeth, nails, and sweat glands. One of the most common forms of ED is X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED), which is caused by mutations in the EDA (Ectodysplasin A) gene. The EDA gene encodes a protein that binds to and activates the Ectodysplasin A Receptor (EDAR), a key component in the signaling pathway responsible for the development of ectodermal structures.

Current treatments for ED are limited and primarily symptomatic, focusing on managing symptoms such as dry skin, heat intolerance, and dental issues. There is a significant unmet medical need for effective therapies that can address the underlying causes of ED and promote the normal development of ectodermal structures.

Recent advances in the understanding of the EDAR signaling pathway have paved the way for the development of therapeutic agents that can activate this pathway. Agonist anti-EDAR antibodies represent a promising approach to treat ED by mimicking the natural ligand EDA and activating the EDAR receptor. However, the development of such antibodies has been challenging due to the need for high specificity, cross-reactivity across species, and the ability to induce robust signaling.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies and methods of using these antibodies for the treatment of ectodermal dysplasia. The invention addresses the limitations of current treatments by providing antibodies that can effectively activate the EDAR receptor and rescue the developmental defects associated with ED.

The invention includes the following aspects:

1. **Agonist Anti-EDAR Monoclonal Antibodies**: The invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to and activate the EDAR receptor. These antibodies are capable of rescuing the developmental defects associated with ED in both murine and canine models. The antibodies are characterized by their high specificity, cross-reactivity across species, and the ability to induce robust signaling.

2. **Methods of Using Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies**: The invention provides methods for treating ectodermal dysplasia using the agonist anti-EDAR antibodies. The methods involve administering the antibodies to subjects with ED, such as newborns with XLHED, to promote the normal development of ectodermal structures.

3. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the agonist anti-EDAR antibodies and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. These compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravenous, intraperitoneal, and subcutaneous.

4. **Therapeutic Applications**: The invention demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of the agonist anti-EDAR antibodies in treating various symptoms of ED, including the promotion of hair growth, the development of functional sweat glands, and the normalization of tooth morphology.

5. **Cross-Reactivity and Specificity**: The antibodies of the invention are characterized by their cross-reactivity with EDAR from different species, including human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken. This cross-reactivity makes the antibodies suitable for use in a wide range of therapeutic applications.

6. **Mechanism of Action**: The invention provides insights into the mechanism of action of the agonist anti-EDAR antibodies. The antibodies are capable of binding to specific epitopes on the EDAR receptor, leading to the activation of downstream signaling pathways that promote the development of ectodermal structures.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Generation and Screening of Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies

To obtain cross-reacting antibodies against conserved EDAR regions, EDAR-deficient mice were immunized with either human or mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins. The EDAR-deficient mouse strains Downless and Sleek have loss-of-function mutations in the extracellular or intracellular domains of EDAR, respectively, but still express the protein. Therefore, OVE1B mice, in which the Edar gene is completely deleted by random genomic integration of an unrelated transgene, were used for immunization.

Hybridoma supernatants with anti-EDAR reactivity were screened for agonist activity using two complementary tests. In the first test, surrogate reporter cell lines stably expressing hEDAR:Fas or mEDAR:Fas fusion proteins were used. EDAR activation in these reporter cells leads to apoptotic cell death by activation of the Fas pathway. In the second assay, hybridoma supernatants were administered intraperitoneally to newborn, EDA-deficient Tabby pups. Tabby mice completely lack tail hairs, and the agonist activity of antibodies can be measured by the induction of tail hair, provided that antibodies recognize and activate mouse EDAR.

Several hybridoma secreted agonist antibodies with in vivo activity, but only a few were also active in the cell-based in vitro assay. Selected hybridoma were subcloned and adapted for growth in serum-free medium from which antibodies were purified. The monoclonal nature of these antibodies was further confirmed by their sharp migration in native protein electrophoresis.

### Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies Have Varied but Relatively Limited Sequence Characteristics

Variable regions of heavy and light antibody chains were amplified by RT-PCR and sequenced for several hybridoma. A number of different variable region genes were identified for both the heavy and light chains, but some of them were shared by two or three hybridoma, usually with different somatic mutations. Interestingly, antibodies with identical heavy and light variable genes sharing greater than 90% sequence identity were obtained from different mice immunized with mouse (antibody 8) or human EDAR (antibodies 1 and 3).

### Agonist Antibodies Recognize at Least Three Different Epitopes in EDAR

EDAR contains three cysteine-rich domains (CRD1, CRD2, and CRD3) in its extracellular region, plus a stalk sequence. EDAR constructs containing these four regions alone or in combinations were used to roughly characterize the epitopes recognized by the antibodies. Some antibodies recognized CRD1 alone, others recognized CRD1 and CRD2 together, and one reacted with CRD1-3, but none bound to receptors lacking CRD1. All antibodies recognized recombinant EDAR by Western blot under nonreducing conditions, but only three reacted with reduced EDAR. These three antibodies had their epitopes localized in CRD1. It is possible that the presence of CRD1 is required for correct folding of EDAR. Abnormal disulfide bridge formation in the absence of CRD1 would explain why antibodies did not recognize constructs containing CRD2 and CRD3. Taken together, these results indicate that agonist anti-EDAR antibodies can recognize at least three different EDAR epitopes located in CRD1 and probably CRD2 and CRD3.

### Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies Cross-React with EDAR of Various Mammals and Birds

Most anti-EDAR antibodies cross-reacted with human, dog, rat, mouse, and chicken EDAR when these were expressed as glycosylphosphatidylinositol-anchored molecules in 293T cells. Antibody 5 only reacted minimally with chicken EDAR, whereas antibody 15 that had been selected on the basis of its specificity for human EDAR rather than for its agonist activity recognized human and dog EDAR, but not rat, mouse, and chicken EDAR.

### Binding Characteristics of Anti-EDAR Antibodies to EDAR

The affinity of 11 agonist antibodies to human EDAR was determined by surface plasmon resonance. For this purpose, monomeric Fab fragments were generated by ficin digestion and size exclusion chromatography. Affinities varied from 0.5 to 40 nM, and differences were also observed in the association and dissociation constants. The dissociation kinetics of two antibodies (7 and 14) were biphasic, showing first a rapid dissociation followed by a slower dissociation, but as these antibodies had no remarkable agonist activity, this was not analyzed further.

### Comparison of the Activity of Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies in Vitro and in Vivo

As a simple and quantitative assay to characterize EDAR agonists using EDAR's own signaling pathway in vitro has not yet been identified, a surrogate reporter assay was used. Fas-sensitive cells were transfected with the extracellular domain of human or mouse EDAR fused to the intracellular portion of Fas. Binding of an active recombinant EDA1 (Fc-EDA1) to these cells induces cell death by activation of the pro-apoptotic Fas pathway. Only some of the antibodies (1, 3, 8, 10, and 12) killed mEDAR:Fas-expressing cells, and even fewer killed hEDAR:Fas expressing cells (1, 3, 10, and 12). In all cases, antibodies were less active than Fc-EDA1 by 1-2 orders of magnitude.

In an in vivo assay, newborn Tabby mice were treated with antibodies on the day of birth. In this case, all antibodies rescued tail hair formation in a dose-dependent manner and with similar EC50 values of 0.1 to 0.7 mg/kg. Only one of the antibodies (antibody 11) seemed less active, with an EC50 value of about 3 mg/kg. Functional sweat glands were induced by the treatment with similar EC50 as for tail hair.

### Divalent Monomeric Agonist Antibodies Are Active in Vivo

It has been shown previously that cross-linked EDA1 containing more than one trimer in a single molecule are better agonists than trimeric EDA1. Therefore, the in vivo activity of agonist anti-EDAR antibodies was investigated to determine whether the activity was due to monomeric antibodies or to aggregates thereof. The activity of a monomeric (divalent) antibody purified by size exclusion chromatography was very similar to that of the total preparation and compared favorably (EC50 ≈ 0.1 mg/kg) with recombinant Fc-EDA1 (EC50 ≈ 0.05 mg/kg). In addition, the F(ab')2 fragment, but not the Fab fragment, was active in vivo when administered to newborn pups on the day of birth. When the F(ab')2 was administered later at day 3 post-birth, one of the latest time points where ventral tail hair can be induced, its action could be inhibited by an excess of the Fab fragment, ruling out that the lack of agonist activity of the Fab would be due only to a shorter half-life in vivo. These observations indicate that a divalent agonist anti-EDAR antibody is both sufficient and necessary to exert activity in Tabby mice.

### Effective Treatment of EDA-deficient Mice with Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies

Some patients with partially inactivating EDA mutations have teeth defects but otherwise normal skin appendages, suggesting that tooth formation may require more stringent EDAR signals than other skin appendages for proper development. To test the effect of agonist antibodies on tooth development, pregnant Tabby mothers were treated during pregnancy so that the antibody could be transferred to embryos by the trans-placental antibody transport system. Mice exposed to agonist anti-EDAR during development not only had tail hairs and functional sweat glands but also hair behind the ears, mucus-secreting glands in the trachea, and a normalized eye appearance. In addition, molars of treated mice were reverted and almost indistinguishable from those of wild-type animals. The effect was long-lasting, as a similarly treated mouse was still reverted after more than 2 years. These results indicate that the agonist anti-EDAR antibodies tested in this application (antibodies 1 and 3) revert the ectodermal dysplasia phenotypes, including tooth morphology.

### Activity of Agonist Anti-EDAR Antibodies in EDA-deficient Dogs

Agonist anti-EDAR antibodies recognize EDAR of different species. To test whether the observed cross-species reactivity also holds true for the agonist activity, anti-EDAR antibody 3 was administered intravenously to three EDA-deficient dogs at either 2 days of life (n = 2, 10 mg/kg) or at 14 days of life (n = 1, 7 mg/kg). None of these dogs showed adverse reactions upon injection. Dentition was corrected not only in EDA-deficient dogs that were treated at 2 days of life but also in those treated at 14 days of life, although the latter still lacked premolars, accounting for the decreased number of teeth. The premolars and molars in dogs treated at day 2 of life had a more normal appearance than in those dogs treated with Fc-EDA. Lacrimation was improved in treated dogs except in one treated at day 2 of age. The correction of glands in the trachea, bronchi, and esophagus appeared to be dependent on the age at which the dogs were treated, i.e., treatment administered earlier in life had a bigger impact on gland development. Notably, regardless of the extent of the phenotypic reversion, none of the treated dogs suffered from pneumonia or other airway diseases that are common in untreated EDA-deficient dogs or from dry eye condition (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) that affects all XLHED dogs.
