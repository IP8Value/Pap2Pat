## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION
The present invention relates generally to the field of virology and, more specifically, to the identification and characterization of a novel receptor for Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The invention provides methods and compositions for detecting, preventing, and treating SARS-CoV-2 infection, particularly in relation to coagulopathy and thrombosis associated with coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) is the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), a global pandemic that has resulted in millions of deaths worldwide. While the majority of infected individuals exhibit mild or asymptomatic symptoms, a significant proportion of patients develop severe complications, including viral pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and multiorgan injury affecting the heart, kidney, liver, and other organs. One of the most critical and life-threatening complications of COVID-19 is coagulopathy, characterized by increased plasma levels of D-dimer, von Willebrand factor (vWF), and coagulation factor VIII (FVIII). These markers are indicative of endothelial cell activation and damage, which contribute to the formation of microthrombi and thrombosis.

Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) is the primary receptor for SARS-CoV-2, facilitating the virus's entry into host cells. However, recent studies have suggested that other cellular receptors may also play a role in SARS-CoV-2 infection. Specifically, liver/lymph node-specific ICAM-3 grabbing nonintegrin (L-SIGN, also known as CD209L, CD299, or CLEC4M) has been identified as a potential receptor for SARS-CoV-2. L-SIGN is a C-type lectin receptor that mediates cell adhesion or pathogen recognition through calcium-dependent binding to glycans. The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 is heavily glycosylated, forming a "glycan shield" that helps the virus evade host immune surveillance. The interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN has been hypothesized to contribute to the virus's ability to infect endothelial cells, particularly liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs).

Despite these findings, the exact role of L-SIGN in SARS-CoV-2 infection and the mechanisms by which it contributes to coagulopathy and thrombosis remain poorly understood. There is a need for methods and compositions that can specifically target the interaction between SARS-CoV-2 and L-SIGN to prevent or treat these severe complications of COVID-19.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
The present invention addresses the aforementioned needs by providing methods and compositions for detecting, preventing, and treating SARS-CoV-2 infection, particularly in relation to coagulopathy and thrombosis. The invention is based on the discovery that L-SIGN is a receptor for SARS-CoV-2 and that the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN contributes to the virus's ability to infect endothelial cells, particularly LSECs and LECs.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for detecting SARS-CoV-2 infection in a sample. The method involves contacting the sample with an agent that specifically binds to L-SIGN and detecting the binding of the agent to L-SIGN, wherein the detection of binding indicates the presence of SARS-CoV-2.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for preventing or treating SARS-CoV-2 infection. The method involves administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an agent that specifically inhibits the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN. Suitable agents include, but are not limited to, antibodies against L-SIGN, mannan, and recombinant L-SIGN/Fc protein.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an effective amount of an agent that specifically inhibits the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for identifying compounds that inhibit the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN. The method involves contacting a test compound with a cell expressing L-SIGN and the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, and detecting the binding of the spike protein to L-SIGN, wherein a reduction in binding indicates that the test compound is a candidate inhibitor.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION
### Introduction
The present invention is directed to methods and compositions for detecting, preventing, and treating SARS-CoV-2 infection, particularly in relation to coagulopathy and thrombosis. The invention is based on the discovery that L-SIGN is a receptor for SARS-CoV-2 and that the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN contributes to the virus's ability to infect endothelial cells, particularly LSECs and LECs.

### Identification of L-SIGN as a Receptor for SARS-CoV-2
#### Interaction Between SARS-CoV-2 Spike Protein and L-SIGN
The extracellular domain of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein contains a receptor-binding domain (RBD) that is crucial for viral entry into host cells. The RBD sequence of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein includes an N-glycosylation site at Asn343 (N343). The inventors have demonstrated that the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein interacts with L-SIGN in a high-mannose-type N-glycan and calcium-dependent manner. Specifically, the high-mannose-type N-glycan at Asn343 on the spike protein is recognized by the EPN motif of L-SIGN, which is located in the extracellular domain of the receptor.

#### Expression of L-SIGN in Endothelial Cells
L-SIGN is highly expressed on LSECs and LECs in human biopsy or autopsy tissue samples. The inventors have shown that L-SIGN is not detected on other blood vessels, such as the central vein, portal vein, hepatic artery in the liver, or high endothelial venules and arterioles in the lymph nodes. Additionally, L-SIGN is not detected in airway epithelial cells and other cells in the lung or in LECs in other tissues, such as the intestine, heart, and kidney. Importantly, the inventors have demonstrated that L-SIGN, but not ACE2, is uniquely expressed on LSECs and LECs in the lymph node.

### Functional Assays and In Vivo Validation
#### Pseudo-typed Viral Infection Assays
The inventors have developed a lentivirus-based SARS-CoV-2 pseudo-typed virus (CoV-2–type virus) to measure L-SIGN-mediated viral entry. The CoV-2–type virus specifically infects cells expressing L-SIGN or ACE2, and the infection efficiency is enhanced in cells with higher levels of L-SIGN expression. The inventors have also shown that the infection can be inhibited by pretreatment with anti-L-SIGN antibodies, mannan, or recombinant L-SIGN/Fc protein.

#### Authentic SARS-CoV-2 Infection
The inventors have further validated the role of L-SIGN in authentic SARS-CoV-2 infection using primary LSECs. L-SIGN-expressing LSECs are susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 infection, and the infection can be blocked by mannan. The inventors have also detected SARS-CoV-2 in LSECs from liver autopsy samples from patients with COVID-19, confirming the clinical relevance of the interaction between SARS-CoV-2 and L-SIGN.

### Mechanisms of L-SIGN-Mediated Infection
#### High-Mannose-Type N-Glycan Recognition
The inventors have demonstrated that the high-mannose-type N-glycan on Asn343 of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein is critical for L-SIGN binding. Mutagenesis studies have shown that mutation of Asn343 to glutamine (Q) abolishes L-SIGN-mediated infection, while the mutation does not affect ACE2-mediated infection. The inventors have also shown that the EPN motif of L-SIGN is necessary for recognizing the high-mannose-type N-glycan on the spike protein.

#### Procoagulant Activities of Infected LSECs
The inventors have observed that LSECs from liver autopsy samples from patients with COVID-19 exhibit signs of activation and increased procoagulant activities. Specifically, the expression of vWF and FVIII, which are major procoagulant proteins, is significantly increased in infected LSECs. The loss of Lyve-1 expression, a marker of LSEC activation, further supports the notion that L-SIGN-mediated infection leads to endothelial cell dysfunction and coagulopathy.

### Therapeutic Applications
#### Detection of SARS-CoV-2 Infection
The invention provides a method for detecting SARS-CoV-2 infection in a sample by contacting the sample with an agent that specifically binds to L-SIGN and detecting the binding of the agent to L-SIGN. Suitable agents include antibodies against L-SIGN, mannan, and recombinant L-SIGN/Fc protein. The detection of binding indicates the presence of SARS-CoV-2.

#### Prevention and Treatment of SARS-CoV-2 Infection
The invention also provides a method for preventing or treating SARS-CoV-2 infection by administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an agent that specifically inhibits the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN. Suitable agents include, but are not limited to, antibodies against L-SIGN, mannan, and recombinant L-SIGN/Fc protein. These agents can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions comprising a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

#### Screening for Candidate Inhibitors
The invention further provides a method for identifying compounds that inhibit the interaction between the high-mannose-type N-glycans on the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and L-SIGN. The method involves contacting a test compound with a cell expressing L-SIGN and the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, and detecting the binding of the spike protein to L-SIGN. A reduction in binding indicates that the test compound is a candidate inhibitor.
