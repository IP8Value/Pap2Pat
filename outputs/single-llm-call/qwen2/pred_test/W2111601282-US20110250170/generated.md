# DESCRIPTION

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to a novel therapeutic approach for treating glioblastoma, a highly aggressive form of brain cancer. The invention involves the use of an immunocytokine, specifically the F16–IL2 fusion protein, in combination with the chemotherapeutic agent temozolomide. This combination therapy has demonstrated significant therapeutic benefits in both subcutaneous and intracranial mouse models of glioblastoma, leading to complete tumour eradication and prolonged survival without additional toxicity.

### VL CDR3 SEQ ID NO: 10

The variable light chain (VL) complementarity determining region 3 (CDR3) of the F16 antibody, which is crucial for its specificity to the A1 domain of tenascin-C, is defined by the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 10. This sequence is essential for the binding affinity and selectivity of the F16–IL2 fusion protein to its target antigen, thereby enabling its targeted delivery and therapeutic efficacy.

## TERMINOLOGY

### Antibody Molecule

An antibody molecule, also known as an immunoglobulin, is a large, Y-shaped protein produced by plasma cells (a type of white blood cell) that is used by the immune system to neutralize pathogens such as bacteria and viruses. Antibodies are composed of four polypeptide chains: two heavy chains and two light chains. Each heavy and light chain contains variable regions that are responsible for antigen binding and constant regions that mediate the biological effects of the antibody.

### Antigen-Binding Site

The antigen-binding site of an antibody is the region where the antibody binds to its specific antigen. This site is located at the tips of the Y-shaped antibody and is formed by the variable regions of the heavy and light chains. The antigen-binding site is highly specific and can recognize and bind to a particular epitope on the antigen, which is a small part of the antigen's surface.

## EXPERIMENTS

### Summary

The invention is supported by a series of experiments that demonstrate the therapeutic efficacy of the F16–IL2 fusion protein in combination with temozolomide. These experiments include biodistribution studies, therapeutic activity assessments in subcutaneous and intracranial glioblastoma xenografts, and microscopic analyses of immune cell infiltration, apoptosis, and proliferation.

### Immunohistochemistry on Human Glioblastoma Samples and on Glioblastoma Xenografts

Immunohistochemistry was performed to assess the expression of the A1 domain of tenascin-C and the extradomain B of fibronectin in human glioblastoma surgical specimens and U87MG xenografts in nude mice. The F16 antibody, specific to the A1 domain of tenascin-C, and the L19 antibody, specific to the extradomain B of fibronectin, were used at identical concentrations. Both antibodies strongly stained the experimental U87MG tumours and glioblastoma samples from patients, with patterns and intensities comparable to those of the L19 antibody, which had previously been reported to stain perivascular structures in high-grade gliomas.

### Cell Lines and Animals

Human subcutaneous and orthotopic glioblastoma xenografts were obtained by injecting U87MG human glioblastoma cells (ATCC Nr: HTB-14) into female BALB/c nude mice (Charles River Laboratories, Sulzfeld, Germany) and male BALB/c nude mice (Charles River Laboratories), respectively. The U87MG cell line was cultured in MEM (Invitrogen, Basel, Switzerland), supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (Invitrogen), 2 mM L-glutamine, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, and 100 U mL−1 ampicillin, and incubated at 37°C in 5% CO2. Animal experiments were conducted under project licences granted by the Veterinäramt des Kantons Zürich and the University of Milan animal facility rules.

### Antibodies and Therapeutic Agents

The L19 antibody, specific to the extradomain B of fibronectin, the F16 antibody, specific to the A1 domain of tenascin-C, and the F16–IL2 fusion protein with human interleukin-2 have been previously described. Temozolomide was dissolved in a saline solution (H2O 0.9% NaCl) containing 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).

### Biodistribution Experiments

The in vivo targeting performance of F16–IL2 was evaluated by quantitative biodistribution analysis. Female BALB/c nude mice bearing subcutaneous U87MG tumours were injected intravenously with radioiodinated F16–IL2. The immunoreactivity of the radiolabelled F16–IL2 was 84%. Mice were euthanized 24 hours after injection, and organs were weighed and counted for radioactivity. The F16–IL2 fusion protein displayed a preferential accumulation in the tumour, with a tumour-to-blood ratio of 11.5 and excellent tumour-to-organ ratios.

### Subcutaneous Glioma Mouse Models

Subcutaneous glioblastoma-bearing mice were obtained by injecting 5 × 10^6 U87MG cells subcutaneously into the flanks of female BALB/c nude mice. Twelve days after tumour cell implantation, when tumours had reached an average size of 300 mm³, mice were staged and divided into four therapeutic groups: (1) F16–IL2 (i.v.), (2) temozolomide (i.p.), (3) F16–IL2 (i.v.) plus temozolomide (i.p.), and (4) control (saline 10% DMSO). Five total administrations were performed on days 12, 15, 18, 21, and 24. The combination therapy of F16–IL2 and temozolomide led to complete tumour eradication and prolonged survival without additional toxicity.

### Immunofluorescence Assessment of Tumour-Infiltrating Cells and of Microvascular Density in Subcutaneous Glioma Xenografts

Immunofluorescence staining was performed to evaluate the infiltration of immune cells into subcutaneous and intracranial glioblastoma xenografts. Antibodies against F4/80 (macrophages), asialo GM1 (NK cells), and CD45 (leukocytes) were used, along with CD31 staining to identify vascular structures. The combination therapy of F16–IL2 and temozolomide resulted in the largest increase in infiltration of NK cells and macrophages in both subcutaneous and intracranial models.

### Statistical Analysis

Statistical comparisons were performed using a two-tailed Student's t-test for the efficacy study with intracranial xenografts and the infiltration and apoptosis/proliferation studies. Survival analysis was conducted using Kaplan–Meier curves, and their comparison was determined by the log-rank test. P-values of <0.05 were considered significant. The combination therapy of F16–IL2 and temozolomide showed significant therapeutic benefits in both subcutaneous and intracranial models.
