# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of plant biotechnology, specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the import of monosaccharides into plant vacuoles. More particularly, the invention provides genetically modified Arabidopsis plants overexpressing the TMT1 gene, which encodes a tonoplast monosaccharide transporter, and methods for improving plant growth, sugar metabolism, and seed yield through the overexpression of TMT1.

## BACKGROUND ART

Plants rely on the efficient transport of sugars for various metabolic and developmental processes. Monosaccharides, such as glucose (Glc) and fructose (Fru), are essential for energy production and the synthesis of storage compounds. The vacuole, a large organelle in plant cells, plays a crucial role in storing these sugars. The transport of monosaccharides into the vacuole is mediated by specific transport proteins located in the tonoplast, the membrane surrounding the vacuole.

The TMT (Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter) family of proteins has been identified as key players in the import of monosaccharides into plant vacuoles. Among these, TMT1 has been shown to be particularly important for Glc transport. However, the natural expression levels of TMT1 are often insufficient to maximize the potential benefits of enhanced vacuolar sugar storage. Previous studies have demonstrated that the overexpression of TMT1 can lead to increased Glc transport capacity, but the full extent of its impact on plant growth and development has not been thoroughly explored.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention addresses the need for improved methods to enhance the import of monosaccharides into plant vacuoles, thereby optimizing plant growth, sugar metabolism, and seed yield. The invention provides genetically modified Arabidopsis plants overexpressing the TMT1 gene and methods for generating and utilizing these plants.

### Summary of the Invention

The invention encompasses the following aspects:

1. **Genetically Modified Arabidopsis Plants**: Transgenic Arabidopsis plants overexpressing the TMT1 gene under the control of a constitutive promoter, such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter. These plants exhibit increased Glc transport capacity, leading to enhanced vacuolar Glc accumulation and improved plant performance.

2. **Methods for Generating Transgenic Plants**: Methods for generating transgenic Arabidopsis plants overexpressing the TMT1 gene, including transformation techniques and selection of high-expressing lines.

3. **Improved Plant Performance**: The overexpression of TMT1 in Arabidopsis plants results in several beneficial effects, including:
   - **Enhanced Growth**: Transgenic plants grow better than wild-type plants, especially under conditions of high Glc supplementation.
   - **Altered Sugar Metabolism**: Transgenic plants exhibit altered subcellular sugar partitioning, with increased Glc and Fru accumulation in the vacuole and reduced cytosolic sugar levels.
   - **Increased Seed Yield**: Transgenic plants produce seeds with higher 1,000 seed weight and increased total seed biomass, resulting from enhanced lipid and protein content.

4. **Applications**: The invention has broad applications in agriculture, including the development of crops with improved yield, stress tolerance, and nutritional quality.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Generation of TMT1 Overexpressor Lines

To create transgenic Arabidopsis plants overexpressing the TMT1 gene, the TMT1 coding sequence was cloned into a binary vector under the control of the constitutive cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter. The resulting construct was introduced into the homozygous Arabidopsis mutant tmt1-2::tDNA, which lacks isoforms TMT1 and TMT2, to prevent possible cosuppression. Transformed plants were selected based on high TMT1 mRNA levels, and three independent lines (lines 1, 4, and 10) were chosen for further analysis.

#### Patch-Clamp Analysis of Isolated Vacuoles

Whole-vacuole patch-clamp measurements were conducted to assess the Glc transport capacity of isolated vacuoles from wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressing plants. The results demonstrated that TMT1-overexpressing vacuoles exhibited a 2-fold higher response to Glc application compared to wild-type vacuoles, confirming the increased transport activity of TMT1.

#### Growth of TMT1 Overexpressors in Liquid Culture

Transgenic plants were grown in liquid culture medium with and without Glc supplementation. Under standard conditions, all plant lines developed rapidly. However, the presence of 5% Glc inhibited the growth of wild-type plants, while TMT1-overexpressing lines grew better than wild-type plants, with fresh weights ranging from 49 to 78 mg.

#### Sugar Metabolism in Arabidopsis Plants Grown in Liquid Culture

Total sugar levels in wild-type and TMT1-overexpressor lines were quantified. Despite similar total sugar levels, TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited altered subcellular sugar partitioning, with increased Glc and Fru accumulation in the vacuole and reduced cytosolic sugar levels.

#### Altered Gene Expression in TMT1 Overexpressor Plants

Northern-blot and quantitative reverse transcription (qRT)-PCR analyses revealed altered expression of sugar-responsive genes in TMT1-overexpressor plants. The chlorophyll a/b-binding protein1 (CAB1) gene was upregulated, while the nitrate reductase1 (NR1) gene was downregulated in TMT1-overexpressor lines compared to wild-type plants.

#### Respiration of Newly Imported Glc

Respiratory consumption of newly imported 14C-labeled Glc was measured in soil-grown plants. TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited reduced 14CO2 release, indicating lower respiratory consumption of Glc.

#### Growth Pattern of Arabidopsis TMT Mutants under Standard Conditions

Wild-type and TMT mutant plants were grown on soil for 15 and 34 days to monitor their development. TMT1-overexpressor lines grew faster than wild-type plants, while tmt1-2::tDNA plants developed slower.

#### Seed Properties and Composition of Arabidopsis Plants with Altered TMT1 Activity

Seeds from TMT1-overexpressor plants were analyzed for 1,000 seed weight, lipid content, and protein content. TMT1-overexpressor lines produced seeds with higher 1,000 seed weight and increased lipid and protein levels compared to wild-type seeds.

#### Rates of Photosynthesis and Nocturnal Respiration

Daily rates of net photosynthesis and nocturnal CO2 release were quantified in wild-type and TMT mutant plants. TMT1-overexpressor lines 4 and 10 exhibited slightly higher rates of CO2 fixation during the light phase and significantly lower rates of nocturnal CO2 release.

#### Sugar Export by Wild-Type and TMT Mutant Leaves

Phloem exudates were analyzed to determine sugar export capacity. TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited increased rates of sugar export from source leaves, while tmt1-2::tDNA plants showed slightly reduced export rates.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Generation of TMT1 Overexpressor Lines

1. **Cloning and Transformation**:
   - The TMT1 coding sequence was cloned into the binary vector pART27 under the control of the 35S promoter.
   - The construct was introduced into the tmt1-2::tDNA mutant using the floral dip method with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101.
   - Transformed plants were selected based on high TMT1 mRNA levels using RT-PCR.

2. **Selection of High-Expressing Lines**:
   - Three independent lines (lines 1, 4, and 10) were selected for further analysis based on their high TMT1 mRNA levels.

#### Example 2: Patch-Clamp Analysis of Isolated Vacuoles

1. **Isolation of Mesophyll Protoplasts**:
   - Arabidopsis wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressor line 4 plants were grown on soil for 5 to 8 weeks.
   - Mesophyll protoplasts were isolated and vacuoles were released using established protocols.

2. **Patch-Clamp Measurements**:
   - Whole-vacuole patch-clamp measurements were conducted to assess Glc transport capacity.
   - TMT1-overexpressing vacuoles exhibited a 2-fold higher response to Glc application compared to wild-type vacuoles.

#### Example 3: Growth of TMT1 Overexpressors in Liquid Culture

1. **Liquid Culture Setup**:
   - Wild-type, tmt1-2::tDNA, and TMT1-overexpressor lines were grown in liquid culture medium with and without 5% Glc supplementation for 12 days.
   - Fresh weights were measured to assess plant growth.

2. **Results**:
   - Under standard conditions, all plant lines developed rapidly.
   - The presence of 5% Glc inhibited the growth of wild-type plants, while TMT1-overexpressor lines grew better, with fresh weights ranging from 49 to 78 mg.

#### Example 4: Sugar Metabolism in Arabidopsis Plants Grown in Liquid Culture

1. **Quantification of Total Sugar Levels**:
   - Total sugar levels were quantified in wild-type and TMT1-overexpressor lines using HPLC.
   - Despite similar total sugar levels, TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited altered subcellular sugar partitioning.

2. **Nonaqueous Fractionation**:
   - Subcellular fractionation was performed to analyze sugar distribution in different compartments.
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines accumulated more Glc and Fru in the vacuole and less in the cytosol.

#### Example 5: Altered Gene Expression in TMT1 Overexpressor Plants

1. **Northern-Blot Analysis**:
   - RNA was isolated from wild-type and TMT1-overexpressor lines grown in liquid culture with and without Glc supplementation.
   - Northern-blot analysis was performed to quantify CAB1 and NR1 mRNA levels.

2. **qRT-PCR Analysis**:
   - qRT-PCR was used to evaluate the expression profiles of CAB1, NR1, and SUC2.
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited upregulated CAB1 and downregulated NR1 expression.

#### Example 6: Respiration of Newly Imported Glc

1. **Respiratory Consumption Measurement**:
   - Soil-grown seedlings were incubated in 100 mM 14C-labeled Glc.
   - 14CO2 release was measured to assess respiratory consumption.

2. **Results**:
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited reduced 14CO2 release, indicating lower respiratory consumption of Glc.

#### Example 7: Growth Pattern of Arabidopsis TMT Mutants under Standard Conditions

1. **Growth Monitoring**:
   - Wild-type and TMT mutant plants were grown on soil for 15 and 34 days.
   - Plant phenotypes were monitored to assess growth patterns.

2. **Results**:
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines grew faster than wild-type plants, while tmt1-2::tDNA plants developed slower.

#### Example 8: Seed Properties and Composition of Arabidopsis Plants with Altered TMT1 Activity

1. **Seed Analysis**:
   - Seeds from wild-type and TMT mutant plants were analyzed for 1,000 seed weight, lipid content, and protein content.
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines produced seeds with higher 1,000 seed weight and increased lipid and protein levels.

2. **Total Seed Biomass**:
   - The total seed harvest per plant was quantified.
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines produced a higher seed mass than wild-type plants.

#### Example 9: Rates of Photosynthesis and Nocturnal Respiration

1. **Gas-Exchange Measurements**:
   - Daily rates of net photosynthesis and nocturnal CO2 release were measured using an infrared gas analysis system.
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines 4 and 10 exhibited slightly higher rates of CO2 fixation during the light phase and significantly lower rates of nocturnal CO2 release.

2. **Results**:
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited improved carbon usage and reduced respiratory loss.

#### Example 10: Sugar Export by Wild-Type and TMT Mutant Leaves

1. **Phloem Exudate Analysis**:
   - Leaves from 6-week-old plants were cut and placed in reaction vessels filled with 15 mM EDTA solution.
   - Sugar export was allowed for 16 hours, and the amount of sugar released was quantified using HPLC.

2. **Results**:
   - TMT1-overexpressor lines exhibited increased rates of sugar export from source leaves, while tmt1-2::tDNA plants showed slightly reduced export rates.
