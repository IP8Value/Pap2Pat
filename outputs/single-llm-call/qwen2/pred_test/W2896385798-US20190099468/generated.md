# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and, more specifically, to methods and compositions for protecting the ocular surface against damage caused by oxidative stress. The invention provides a novel use of dynamin inhibitors, particularly Dynasore and its more potent analog Dyngo-4a, for preventing and treating ocular surface damage, including conditions such as dry eye disease and solution-induced corneal staining (SICS).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The ocular surface, comprising the cornea and conjunctiva, is a critical interface between the external environment and the internal structures of the eye. It is lined with a stratified squamous mucosal epithelium that is continually renewed through a process of cell division and differentiation. The apical cells of this epithelium express mucosal markers, including membrane-associated mucins such as MUC16, which form a glycocalyx that acts as a barrier to prevent intracellular penetration. Additionally, tight junctions between cells create a paracellular barrier, further enhancing the protective function of the ocular surface.

However, the ocular surface is vulnerable to various damaging agents and physical insults, such as ultraviolet light, microorganisms, and allergens. These factors can lead to ocular surface damage, barrier disruption, and increased desquamation, which are characteristic of conditions like dry eye disease (keratoconjunctivitis sicca). Dry eye disease affects a significant portion of the population in North America, Europe, and Asia, and is often diagnosed using vital dyes such as fluorescein and rose bengal. These dyes stain damaged cells, providing a visual indicator of ocular surface health.

Recent studies have shown that the uptake of vital dyes by ocular surface cells is an active process linked to sublethal cell damage. Specifically, oxidative stress has been identified as a major cause of ocular surface damage. Oxidative stress can stimulate vital dye uptake by inducing plasma membrane remodeling and increasing membrane permeability. However, the exact mechanisms underlying this process have not been fully elucidated.

Dynamin, a GTPase involved in endocytosis, has been shown to play a role in various cellular processes, including plasma membrane repair and mitochondrial dynamics. Dynasore, a potent inhibitor of dynamin, has been used to study endocytic processes and has been shown to protect cells against oxidative stress-induced damage. The present invention builds on these findings by demonstrating that Dynasore and its analog Dyngo-4a can protect the ocular surface against oxidative stress, thereby preventing vital dye uptake and reducing ocular surface damage.

#### Mechanisms of Ocular Surface Damage and Vital Dye Uptake

Ocular surface damage can be caused by various factors, including oxidative stress, which is a common cause of damage in conditions such as dry eye disease. Oxidative stress leads to lipid peroxidation of the plasma membrane, which can result in the formation of pores and increased membrane permeability. This increased permeability allows vital dyes such as fluorescein and rose bengal to penetrate the cells, leading to staining and a visual indication of damage.

Recent studies have shown that the uptake of vital dyes by ocular surface cells is an active process linked to sublethal cell damage. Specifically, oxidative stress can stimulate vital dye uptake by inducing plasma membrane remodeling and increasing membrane permeability. However, the exact mechanisms underlying this process have not been fully elucidated.

#### Role of Dynamin in Ocular Surface Protection

Dynamin, a GTPase involved in endocytosis, has been shown to play a role in various cellular processes, including plasma membrane repair and mitochondrial dynamics. Dynasore, a potent inhibitor of dynamin, has been used to study endocytic processes and has been shown to protect cells against oxidative stress-induced damage. The present invention builds on these findings by demonstrating that Dynasore and its analog Dyngo-4a can protect the ocular surface against oxidative stress, thereby preventing vital dye uptake and reducing ocular surface damage.

### Example 1

#### Experimental Model and Study Design

The purpose of this study was to investigate the protective effects of Dynasore and Dyngo-4a against oxidative stress-induced damage to the ocular surface. The study was conducted using human corneal epithelial cells in monolayer and stratified cultures, as well as ex vivo mouse eye organ cultures.

##### Cell Culture

A telomerase-immortalized line of human corneal limbal epithelial (HCLE) cells was used for all experiments. The cells were plated in 96-well plates and used at 90% confluence as monolayers or transferred to differentiation medium containing high calcium ion and bovine serum and left for 7 days to stratify and differentiate.

##### Mouse Eye Organ Culture

Wild type C57Bl/6J mice, 6–8 weeks of age, were purchased from Jackson Labs. Eyes were enucleated immediately after euthanasia and placed in Keratinocyte-SFM media.

##### Oxidative Stress Model

Oxidative stress was induced by adding tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBHP) to the culture medium. The final concentration of tBHP used was 3 mM for cell culture experiments and 10 mM for mouse eye organ culture experiments.

##### Vital Dye Staining Assay

Vital dye staining of cells in culture was performed using sodium fluorescein. Staining of mouse eyes was performed using a clinical fluorescein dye solution. The dyes were added to cultures for 10 minutes and then excess dye was removed by washing with PBS. Dye uptake was evaluated qualitatively by imaging and quantitatively using a plate reader.

##### Endocytosis Assay

Endocytosis was quantified by monitoring cell uptake of human Alexa Fluor 568-conjugated transferrin. Cells were probed with the conjugated transferrin and imaged using a fluorescence microscope. Uptake was quantified by image analysis.

##### Apoptosis Assays

Early apoptosis was assessed using an Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated Annexin-5 probe, and late apoptosis was assessed using the TUNEL assay. Cells were incubated with the respective probes and imaged using a fluorescence microscope.

##### Metabolic Assays for Cytotoxicity

Cytotoxicity was assessed using the MTT and WST-1 metabolic assays. Superoxide anions generated by NAD(P)H-dependent cellular oxidoreductase activity reduce MTT or WST to water-soluble formazans, which absorb visible light. Absorbance was measured at wavelengths of 590 nm (MTT) or 440 nm (WST-1).

##### Trypan Blue Exclusion Assay

The trypan blue exclusion assay was used to assess plasma membrane damage. Cells were stained with trypan blue and the extent of staining was measured qualitatively by imaging and quantitatively using a plate reader.

##### Lectin Binding

To determine the presence of mucin-type glycoconjugates on cell surfaces, a binding assay was performed with jacalin, a lectin that specifically recognizes the T-antigen present on O-glycans. Cells were fixed and incubated with fluorescein-conjugated jacalin, and images were taken using a fluorescence microscope.

##### Western Blotting

Proteins from cell culture media samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were probed with a primary antibody against LGALS3 and developed using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit.

#### Results

##### Oxidative Stress Stimulates Vital Dye Uptake Linked with Sublethal Cell Damage

Oxidative stress induced by tBHP significantly increased the uptake of fluorescein and rose bengal dyes by human corneal epithelial cells in monolayer and stratified cultures. The increase in dye uptake was associated with sublethal cell damage, as evidenced by increased phosphatidylserine exposure (ANXA5 binding) and minimal late-stage apoptosis (TUNEL assay).

##### Endocytosis Inhibitors Block Oxidative Stress-Stimulated Vital Dye Uptake in Monolayer Cells

Endocytosis was stimulated by oxidative stress in monolayer cell cultures, and this stimulation was blocked by the endocytosis inhibitors genistein, chlorpromazine, and Dynasore. Stress-stimulated vital dye uptake was also blocked by these inhibitors, indicating a link between endocytosis and vital dye uptake in monolayer cells.

##### Only Dynasore and Its Analogue Dyngo-4a Block Oxidative Stress-Stimulated Vital Dye Uptake in Stratified and Differentiated Cells

In stratified and differentiated cell cultures, oxidative stress did not stimulate endocytosis, and genistein and chlorpromazine had no effect on vital dye uptake. However, Dynasore and its analogue Dyngo-4a continued to block vital dye uptake, suggesting an alternative mechanism of action.

##### Dynasore Does Not Prevent Oxidative Stress-Stimulated Vital Dye Uptake After Stress Is Applied and Damage Has Occurred

In ex vivo mouse eye organ cultures, Dynasore prevented oxidative stress-stimulated vital dye uptake when applied simultaneously with the stress. However, Dynasore had no effect when applied after the stress was applied and the ocular surface was already damaged, indicating that Dynasore works by preventing damage rather than repairing it.

##### Dynasore Protects the Ocular Surface Subjected to Damaging Oxidative Stress

Dynasore was found to protect cells and their glycocalyx against oxidative stress-induced damage. Treatment with Dynasore reduced metabolic damage (MTT/WST-1 assays), prevented plasma membrane damage (trypan blue exclusion assay), and inhibited the shedding of LGALS3 from the cell surface (Western blotting).
